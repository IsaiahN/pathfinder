## 0. The residual in effect terms

R is the question's own uncertainty about the effect of inserting an instrument between a knower and a ground.

- **Arity.** At least four conditions, not two: (i) extension of perception, (ii) anchor relation, (iii) independence of readings, (iv) channel and habitat integrity. A two-condition phrasing ("increases objectivity" versus "creates dependence") is a bundle of these (Fig 9 :4-5, :54-57). A single lever clears the region where one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.
- **Symmetry.** "Increases objectivity" is ordered and comparative: it asserts `before < after` on a scale. "Creates uncertainty" is a different quality, not the negation of the first. The relation between the two branches is not a symmetric bond: swapping them does not give the same thing. What would settle the comparison is a ground that does not move when the instrument's owner changes their mind (Fig 2 :29, :33).
- **Scale.** First order inside one measurement (observer, instrument, target). Second order when instruments are compared across rooms, as in instrument intercomparison or replication. Fig 4 :19-20 governs the transform between the scales: the kernel is fixed, only actors, currency and anchor change.

The residual is indexed per measurement slot, never aggregated into "objectivity in science" as a single number (Fig 1 :19, :22). A global average of many instruments would throw away the only signal that says where to look.

**Bound shape written down before the run (Formula, "Direction"):** a per-slot separator that names actors, currency and anchor for each condition, states which branch each condition licenses, and gives a refuter. A universal "when" threshold is explicitly *not* part of the bound shape, because no figure line returns one.

**Union, not partition.** These six conditions overlap and can hold at once in one measurement. D1 and D2 share the target; D3 depends on D1; D4 can silently undo D2 even when D3 is satisfied; D5 governs cases where D1 and D2 both look satisfied but the reading underdetermines its source. This is Fig 9 :54-57: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** D1 perception must register something before D2 can range it against an anchor. D2 must be settled before D3 independence buys anything, because independence without an anchor is two mirrors. D4 channel integrity is a standing precondition on all three. D5 is asked only when a reading exists but its source is in question. D6 is asked only when no term in the current closure can represent the residual. Refusal precedes mint (Formula step 2); describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).

## 2. Findings

### F1. Objectivity is an anchor relation, not a property an instrument carries (decisive for D2)

Fig 2 :29, :30:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**
> **"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."**

Fig 2 :33, :34-35:

> **"The anchor must not update."**
> **"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."**

Fig 2 :36, :37-38:

> **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."**
> **"It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."**

This is the hinge of the whole answer. An instrument increases objectivity only while it is ranged against something that does not update on the instrument's own reading. It stops doing so the moment it is promoted into the anchor, that is, the moment the question "what is the temperature?" is answered by "what the thermometer says" with no independent reference. Then the instrument has become another frame and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices (Fig 2 :34). The second decisive clause is :37-38: an instrument is legitimate as an anchor only when the question is constitutively about what it measures. For a question constitutively about the instrument's own scale (is this reading on the defined scale?), it is the anchor. For a question about the world beyond that scale, it is an instrument that can be wrong on axes it does not measure.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 2 :29-38 and Fig 10 :47, two figures with low overlap: one defines the anchor, the other prices a convention that decides an outcome). **Refuter:** show a case where the reference against which an instrument is judged changes its answer when the instrument's owner changes their mind, and objectivity is nevertheless increased. That would break the anchor definition.

### F2. Independence between instruments is the actual mechanism of increase (D3)

Fig 2 :31-32:

> **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."**
> **"Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."**

Fig 2 :42:

> **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."**

Fig 8 :45, :48-49:

> **"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."**
> **"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus. Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."**

This is the most directly on-point figure text in the whole extract for instrumentation. Two instruments pointed at the same thing are two frames, whereas two people reading the same report are one. So reliance on instruments increases objectivity specifically when it multiplies independent readings of one ground, and it fails to increase objectivity when the added "instruments" share a calibration chain, a reduction pipeline, or a single latent assumption. The world-source literature on automation bias and the experimenter's regress corroborates this (World W3, W2 in `09`), but the verdict comes from the figures.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 2 :31-32, :42 and Fig 8 :45, :48-49; the first prices agreement, the second prices surplus). **Refuter:** demonstrate an objectivity gain from adding a reading that draws on exactly the same evidence as an existing reading, with no new ground contact. Under the figure that gain should be zero or negative.

### F3. Instruments extend representation and surface residuals that were unread (D1)

Fig 6 :26:

> **"The world does not gain primitives; a frame gains access. Composition explores what the operators reach; instruments extend what can be represented at all."**

Fig 6 :32, :34-35:

> **"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."**
> **"The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve."**

Fig 3 :27:

> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

This is the positive branch in its strongest form. An instrument adds no primitive to the world; it changes what a frame can represent. The question about a possible sensor is not whether one could be imagined but whether something at some resolution is already returning a signal that fails to resolve. That is the proto-instrument rule, and it explains why new instruments historically deepen objectivity: they take a residual that was silently present and make it readable. Fig 3 :27 says the same at the level of a chain: a link with no instrument is not a working link, just an unexamined one.

**Mode:** `specified`, with the historical claim `general` (no specific instrument is named). **Status:** triangulated (Fig 6 :26, :32-35 and Fig 3 :27; different figures, one on representation, one on dependency). **Refuter:** exhibit a case where an instrument extends what can be represented but leaves every per-slot residual unchanged and creates no new ground contact. That would break the claim that the gain is contact.

### F4. The channel decays while the ground does not: the core of new dependence (D4)

Fig 10 :24, :25-26, :27-28:

> **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**
> **"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"**
> **"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"**

Fig 1 :23:

> **"The ground enters as the only metric."**

This is the negative branch in its strongest form. Reliance creates new dependence because the instrument is a channel, and channels decay: calibration drifts, metrics saturate, readings get pooled. The ground does not decay, so the failure is invisible from inside the instrument's own output. The output is not a false verdict, it is *no verdict*: a report that you have lost touch. The correct response is to keep the anchor reachable (Fig 10 :29), not to read the instrument more carefully. Fig 1 :23 forbids scoring the instrument with a quantity the instrument itself produces.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 10 :24-28 and Fig 1 :23, low overlap). **Refuter:** show a channel that has lost contact with the ground while its own output still counts as a verdict on that ground. That would dissolve the channel and ground as separate quantities.

### F5. A convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention (D2, failure)

Fig 10 :47, :49:

> **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**
> **"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."**

Fig 2 :33-35 (quoted in F1):

> **"The anchor must not update."**

When a calibration convention, a standard scale, or a model output is allowed to settle the question it was built to measure, it has stopped being a convention and become a verdict. Fig 10 :49 adds that declaring the meaning of a reading does not ground it. The world-source experimenter's regress (World W2) is exactly this loop: apparatus reliability is certified by the results the apparatus produces. The figures do not need that source to reach the verdict; the source corroborates it.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 10 :47, :49 and Fig 2 :33-35). **Refuter:** produce a convention that fixes an outcome and still functions as a convention, in the sense that the ground can refuse it. If the ground can refuse it, it did not decide the outcome.

### F6. Shared evidence makes agreement worthless and makes a room a mirror (D3, failure)

Fig 2 :40, :39:

> **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**
> **"Correlation has valence."**

Fig 7 :35-36, :13:

> **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**
> **"agreement compounds, evidence does not"**

Fig 5 :48:

> **"Its own output is never a measure of success."**

Replication that reuses the same instrument, the same reduction, and the same prior is one long room (Fig 7 :36: *"A chain of mirrors is one long room."*). It creates an appearance of objectivity that is actually a weighted average of influence. This is why "many instruments agree" is not by itself an objectivity gain: the independence parameter decides whether it is triangulation or a single pool talking.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 2 :39-40, Fig 7 :13, :35-36, Fig 5 :48). **Refuter:** show agreement across frames that draw on the same evidence carrying the same verdict weight as agreement across independent frames. That would break the correlation rule.

### F7. A reading can underdetermine its source (D5)

From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened 2026-11-23, framework essay, high overlap, secondary): *"the instrument is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source"*; *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"*; *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it."* The essay's spectrometer example shows the case was resolved *"not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."*

Fig 6 :27:

> **"Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."**

This is the subtlest source of new uncertainty. Instrumental objectivity is often read as freedom from human judgment, but a reading that cannot discriminate among its possible sources has merely moved the ambiguity downstream. The figure-side contribution is Fig 6 :27: from inside the frame, a source that is merely unreachable at this budget and a source that does not exist look the same. The essay's metamerism case is world corroboration, not a figure line. What is owed is a discriminator, not a stronger claim.

**Mode:** `specified` on the structural claim, `general` on any particular metamerism case. **Status:** single-source on the figure side (Fig 6 :27), corroborated by one framework essay that shares the author's evidence pool, so weak. **Refuter:** exhibit a reading with several possible sources where no better instrument could in principle distinguish them and the ambiguity is nevertheless not uncertainty. That would break the discriminator requirement.

### F8. Isolation substitutes a habitat; a harness proves wiring, not capability (D4, D5)

Fig 11 :66, :68-69, :70:

> **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."**
> **"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"**
> **"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."**

Fig 11 :39-40:

> **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

Reliance on instruments can increase performance inside a test habitat while decreasing capability against the real ground. Two failure modes are silent: what you failed to reproduce, and what you unintentionally introduced. This is "new dependence" in its most concrete historical form: a measurement system that works perfectly on the standard and fails on the world.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 :66-70 and Fig 11 :39-40 are the same figure but different quantities, plus Fig 10 :24 on channel contact; low overlap between the habitat and channel claims). **Refuter:** show a substituted habitat that preserves all contact the goal required and introduces no new actor. Under Fig 11 :67 that would be removal rather than substitution, which the figure forbids.

### F9. A new representable is an import and needs provenance and a shadow test (D6)

Fig 6 :23, :24:

> **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**
> **"Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."**

Fig 8 :50-52:

> **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."**

When an instrument makes a new quantity representable, that quantity is an import from a frame whose closure differs (the instrument's construction physics, for example). It needs a source gate and a shadow test. Without the record, a convergent derivation and an adopted import are indistinguishable in the contents, so the apparent objectivity gain cannot be audited.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 6 :23-24 and Fig 8 :50-52). **Refuter:** show an imported primitive whose provenance and shadow test make no difference to whether the receiving frame can use it. That would break the import rule.

### F10. The question is a union of conditions, so there is no single "when" (structure of the answer)

Fig 9 :4-5:

> **"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."**

Fig 9 :54-55, :57:

> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
> **"A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."**

The historical literature reports instruments both increasing objectivity (mechanical objectivity, self-registering devices) and creating new dependence (automation bias, experimenter's regress). Those are not opposite answers to one question. They are two conditions of a union, each with its own anchor. Seeking one crossover point would be searching for a cut that does not exist. The honest answer is a per-condition separator with per-slot verdicts.

**Mode:** `general` on the structure, `specified` on the six domains. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 9 :4-5 and :54-58 are the same figure's two distinct claims, plus Fig 1 :19 on per-slot indexing). **Refuter:** exhibit a single variable whose value monotonically determines whether instrumentation helps across all six domains. That would collapse the union to a partition.

Fig 13 :134:

## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the seat-map rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. An anchor is exhibited that changes its answer when a frame pushes on it, and objectivity is still increased by ranging against it (breaks Fig 2 :29, :33).
2. An objectivity gain is demonstrated from adding a reading that draws on exactly the same evidence as an existing reading, with no new ground contact (breaks Fig 2 :31-32, :42).
3. A channel is exhibited that has lost contact with the ground while its own output still settles a verdict on that ground (breaks Fig 10 :24-28).
4. A convention that decides an outcome is exhibited that still functions as a convention, in the sense that the ground can refuse it (breaks Fig 10 :47).
5. A substituted habitat is exhibited that preserves all contact the goal required and introduces no new actor (breaks Fig 11 :66-69).
6. A figure line is produced that returns the universal threshold declared as `gap` in F11 (closes the gap).

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. State the mode on every sentence (F-mode rule). Do not let a per-slot condition become a universal verdict.
2. Lead with the anchor test (F1) and the independence test (F2), because those are the two figures that decide most cases.
3. Treat every apparent objectivity gain as a hypothesis about contact: ask what the instrument is ranged against, and whether the reference updates (F1, F4).
4. Ask what source the reading could be from, and whether an instrument exists that discriminates them (F7). If not, say `unreached`, not `objective`.
5. Keep the union split (F10). Do not answer "both, depending" without saying which conditions and which anchors.
6. Send the generator: the condition set, the per-slot verdicts and the refuter. Do not send a recording of one historical case as if it were the answer (Fig 4 :22-25: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*).
