# Structure, order and analogues: instrument, anchor and channel

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the analogues, not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The main audit (`01`) concludes that the question is a union of six conditions and that objectivity is an anchor relation rather than a property of a device. Two supporting questions are owed:

1. If the same physical device can be an anchor for one question and an instrument for another (Fig 2 :36-38), what is the difference? Ingredients or arrangement?
2. What is the order of the conditions, and which analogues from other frames actually carry over?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms available: a reading, a target, a knower, a reference, a channel, a habitat. Bonds available: the seven, plus negation. No new primitive is needed for this derivation. Anchor: the ground, which does not update (Fig 2 :29). Currency: whether the bond to the ground lets the ground refuse the reading.

## DERIVATION

### Q1. Is the difference between an instrument and an anchor a difference of atoms or of bonds?

- Fig 2 :37-38: *"It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."* The same device changes role with the question, not with its construction.
- Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
- Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."*

So the difference is a **bond**, not an atom. Let `R` denote a reading and `G` the ground. Then:

- `R → G` (sequence or ranging, the reading is corrected by the ground): instrument.
- `R ≡ G` (identity, two names for one referent): the device has been promoted to anchor for a question constitutively about it.
- `R ⋛ G` (comparison, a threshold relation between two quantities): the device is used as a standard against which another reading is judged.
- `R ≉ G` in the loose sense of a channel that has drifted: negation of the ranging relation, which is a refutation of the claim that contact holds, not an absence of the device.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a device's role as anchor or instrument were recoverable from the device alone, with no reference to the question asked, the bond reading would fail and the atom reading would stand. Fig 12 :46 says it is not: *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."*

### Q2. Are two measurement regimes isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Regime A: a self-registering device ranged against an external standard, with independent replication. Regime B: the same device treated as the reference, with replication inside one pipeline.
- Over the atom set `{reading, target, reference, channel, habitat}`, the two regimes share the atoms and differ in the bond between reading and reference (`→` versus `≡`). By Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance, and the difference is decisive in behaviour (one is triangulation, the other is coherent drift).
- **Falsifier:** if the two regimes could be written with the same bond and still differed only by degree of reliance, they would not be isomers but one quantity at two values, and the union reading in `01` F10 would be weakened. The world sources support the bond reading: Daston and Galison's mechanical objectivity and trained judgment are two practices over the same instruments, not two amounts of instrument use (World W1, carried via the opened Wikipedia summary).

### Q3. Is there a functional group?

- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- The substructure `reading → ground` (a reading ranged against something that does not update) fixes the outcome "objectivity increases", whatever instrument occupies the slot. The qualifier (thermometer, spectrometer, calibration standard, blinded assessor) says which member.
- This is why no figure line names the winning instrument: the group says what kind, the qualifier is a binding from the world. It is also why the gap in `01` F11 is a shape-not-a-name gap rather than an omission.
- **Falsifier:** if two instruments sharing `reading → ground` could diverge in whether objectivity increases with no other change, the functional-group reading would fail and each case would need its own loop.

### Q4. Which instruments are catalysts and which are spent parts?

- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**
- A calibration standard that is itself checked against a higher standard and survives the comparison is a catalyst: it can be used again without losing the bond. A standard that is promoted into the final arbiter becomes a spent part in the epistemic sense: its authority is consumed by the decision it fixes. Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."*
- Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome."** is already used in Q3; the catalyst case is a different quantity, the persistence of the part across uses, and is held separately.
- **Falsifier:** if a standard that decides outcomes could be reused with undiminished authority while the ground still refuses it, the spent/catalyst distinction would collapse here.

### Q5. What is the order of the conditions?

- Fig 3 :20-21: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces."*
- Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*
- Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

Order: perception (D1) before anchor (D2) before independence (D3); channel integrity (D4) as standing precondition; source discrimination (D5) when the reading exists but its cause is multiple; import (D6) only when the closure lacks a term. Refusal precedes mint (Formula step 2), and the residual is described before a frame is chosen (Fig 9 :27-28). A link with no instrument is unexamined, not working, so adding replication at D3 cannot rescue an unread residual at D1.

### Q6. Which analogues carry over, and which do not?

- **Daston and Galison, mechanical objectivity and trained judgment** (World W1, low overlap with the framework): carries as an analogue of two bonds over the same atoms. It does not carry as a claim about which instrument wins.
- **Bainbridge 1983, Ironies of Automation** (World W4, low overlap): carries as the catalyst/spent-part distinction in operator skill. Automating most work leaves the operator with unpracticed skills and heavier monitoring, so the human part is consumed rather than regenerated (Fig 12 :119). It does not carry as a universal reliability number.
- **Automation bias** (World W3, low overlap): carries as Fig 2 :13-15 mutual update, a frame converging on whoever had the most influence. It does not carry as a claim that instruments never help; the same source reports gains when raw information remains available.
- **GUM measurement uncertainty** (World W5, low overlap): carries as the requirement to report the residual alongside the reading. It does not carry as a threshold for when instruments help, because an uncertainty statement accompanies every measurement.
- **Fig 13 six-term substrate** does not carry as a mapping onto instruments. It is used in the main audit only for union and gap structure (Fig 13 :115, :134). To force instruments into the physical six would be the strain named and re-censused in `01` §1 and §6.

## CONCLUSION

The same device is an instrument or an anchor depending on its bond to the ground, not on its atoms (Fig 12 :5, :46; Fig 2 :37-38). Two measurement regimes over the same atoms are isomers when the bond differs (Fig 12 :117), and the general condition that fixes objectivity is a functional group, `reading → ground` (Fig 12 :124), with the specific instrument left as a provenanced binding. Standards that survive their own use are catalysts; standards that decide outcomes are spent (Fig 12 :119; Fig 10 :47). The order is dependency order (Fig 3), and the only analogues that carry are those that respect the union structure. This framing rescues the defensible part of the historical story (instruments can deepen contact) without smuggling in the part that failed (that the device itself is objectivity, or that one threshold governs all six conditions).
