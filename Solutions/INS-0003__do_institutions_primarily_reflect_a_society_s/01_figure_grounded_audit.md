## 0. The claim under audit and the residual in effect terms

The question offers two options for the relation between an institution I and a distribution of power P in the same society:

- **A (reflect):** I is a projection of P. The causal arrow runs P to I. Changing I without changing P is impossible; an institution is an artifact of who already wins.
- **B (reshape):** I is a generator over P. The arrow runs I to P. An institution can change who wins, independently of prior power.

Stated as a residual, what fails to resolve is **the direction and the scope of a relation that is observed as a correlation**: where a rule and a power distribution co-vary, which one is the reference and which one is the reading, and over which slots does the relation hold.

Two things are owed before any figure is chosen.

1. **Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term** (formula "Direction"; Fig 5 :65: "make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM"). The shape predicted here is: *a four-condition separator with per-slot verdicts, a stated refuter, and no universal percentage or arrow*. This term is allowed to accrue a residual in section 6 like anything else.
2. **Describe the residual before choosing a frame.** Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit". The figures are consulted only after the census below, not used to pick the domain.

The word "primarily" is itself a request for an aggregate. Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." The audit therefore refuses to average a verdict over institutions of different kinds.

## 1. Domain census (before any figure line is chosen)

**R, the residual.** Arity: two conditions, the power distribution at t and the rule structure at t+1, so a **union compound** rather than a single question with two opposite answers (Fig 9 :54, :57; section 2 F1). Symmetry: "reflect" is a symmetric, correlational reading (the operand swap test at Fig 12 :59, "swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No."), while "reshape" is an ordered production (Fig 12 :74, "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."; Fig 12 :75, "A produces B; B did not exist before"). A single correlation cannot choose between them. Scale: a single institution, a field of institutions, and a whole society are three different scales with different anchors.

One line per candidate domain. A domain needs an actor, a currency, and a nameable anchor; where there is no anchor it is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | partition or union |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1, the institutional frame (the room) | rule makers, gatekeepers, enforcement bodies, agenda setters, the seats that hold a convention | settled rules, compliance, precedence, what enters the library Γ | the ground that settles whether a rule still pays: an outcome that does not move when the rule's beneficiaries praise it (Fig 2 :29, :30, :45) | part of the compound; needs its own loop |
| D2, the power and habitat frame | organized interests, coalitions, capital, force, status, newcomers from outside the field | influence, resources, attention, who gets what | material outcomes and reproduced contact, not opinion (Fig 11 :39, :40, Fig 13 :24) | part of the compound; the habitat, not the ground |
| D3, the verification and outside frame | researchers, journals, associations, expert readers | residuals and agreement | the ground, never agreement (Fig 10 :42; Fig 2 :35, :40) | part of the compound; in this simulated run its independence is zero |
| D4, a universal "primarily reflect or primarily reshape" quantity | none nameable | none | none: no instrument returns a global institutional-direction coefficient | **not a domain**, declared `gap`, not silence (Fig 13 :134) |

**Dependency order (Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance.").**

1. Describe R in effect terms before naming a frame (Fig 9 :27-28). Done above.
2. Apply the anchor test (Fig 2 :29-30) before deciding whether the observed relation is reflection or triangulation. If the supposed anchor updates on what the powerful think, the whole relation is mutual update, not evidence.
3. Refuse before mint (formula step 2; Fig 12 :90: "A relation that fails all six is not a bond you are missing. It is a claim that has not been made yet."). The options "reflect" and "reshape" are not a bond; they are a union to be unbundled.
4. Determine contact and import (Fig 6 :23) before assessing whether any change is "independent".
5. Settle per slot (Fig 1 :19), then compose, then re-check the residual.

**Union, not partition.** D1, D2 and D3 overlap: the rule makers in D1 are also actors in D2, and the researchers in D3 are themselves an institution in D1. Fig 9 :57: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Each domain therefore gets its own loop and its own verdict; verdicts are never averaged (Fig 9 :60).

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The question is a union compound, not a two-valued partition (decisive)

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

The two offered options are not values of a single dial. "Reflect" describes a **reading relation** between a rule and a distribution; "reshape" describes a **production relation** between a rule and a later distribution. Those are different bonds (F7 below), in different domains (D1 and D2), ranging against different anchors (a rule's continued payment versus material who-gets-what). Asking which one is "primary" aggregates across the union and across slots, which is exactly the operation Fig 1 :22 disqualifies.

**Refuter:** show that a single observable, measured per institution and per slot, ranks all institutions on one reflect-reshape scale and predicts later power shifts better than the four-condition separator in section 5. If it does, the union reading is false.

### F2 - "Reflect" is a mirror, and a mirror cannot correct

Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :30: "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."

An institution that only reflects power is a mirror of the distribution. It has no purchase on the distribution by construction: a mirror reports, it does not act. So option A cannot be the mechanism of reshaping; it is a different *kind* of relation. Note also what this does to the usual story that institutions "embody the balance of power" and thereby stabilize it. A rule whose reference is the current balance updates when the balance moves. By Fig 2 :33 ("The anchor must not update.") such a rule is not an anchor for anything. It is a frame that has been handed the ground's job.

**Refuter:** exhibit an institution whose rule literally does not move when the underlying distribution moves, yet whose output still tracks that distribution at a non-updating instrument. Then reflection is a legitimate anchor relation and this finding is narrowed.

### F3 - "Independently reshape" is refused: only contact with a differing closure moves the wall

Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 6 :14 names import as "the only operator that moves the wall". Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."

An institution composed entirely from the interests, categories and remedies already present in its society cannot generate a new kind of actor or a new kind of claim. It can only recombine what it holds. Real reshaping, when it occurs, has the signature of **import**: a frame whose closure differs (a new technology, an external coalition, a migrated practice, a source-discriminating measurement) crosses the boundary with provenance and a shadow test (Fig 8 :50-52). The word *independently* in the question therefore fails the figure test. The honest form is *an institution can be the conduit of a change whose source lies outside its own closure*.

**Refuter:** show a case where an institution, with no contact whatsoever with a differing closure, introduced a genuinely new kind of actor or claim that later reordered power and paid at the ground. That would falsify the import requirement here.

### F4 - Institutional power is asymmetric, and capability is not the institution alone

Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." Fig 11 :63: "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state." Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

An institution can introduce an actor (a new franchise, a new office, a new franchise of voice) and cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult the rule book. So an institution is a maintained habitat, a garden, not an author of the world it orders. This cuts both ways: it refuses the strong "reshape independently" claim in B, and it also refuses the passive reading of A, because introduction is real work that changes contact. A managed institution is permanent work rather than a solved state, and a one-time constitutional settlement that stops maintaining contact becomes a recording (Fig 4 :25).

**Refuter:** identify a case where an institution removed an actor or a contact relation from its habitat by decree and the removal persisted without ongoing maintenance. That would break the asymmetry claim.

### F5 - The power distribution cannot serve as the anchor once it is occupied by its beneficiaries

Fig 10 :59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying". Fig 10 :61: "standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on". Fig 10 :93: "A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified." Fig 2 :47: "A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame."

This is the structural core of the institutional question. If "the existing balance of power" is offered as the thing institutions ought to reflect, and the people who hold that power keep the reading of it, the supposed ground has been promoted into a party. What follows is coherent drift, not a verdict: the rule, the reading of the rule, and the power that benefits from the reading all update together, and nobody notices because the institution can point at "the balance" as its supposed ground. This is precisely the collapse the seat map names: "An anchor someone benefits from is one whose keeper is thereby disqualified" (Seat Map :205).

**Refuter:** exhibit a society in which the balance of power is read by a keeper whose standing does not survive the outcome and whose reading cannot move with the powerful. Then the balance can be an anchor, and the drift diagnosis does not apply to that case.

### F6 - The mechanism by which "reflect" silently becomes "reshape"

Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one". Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."

A rule that begins as a shared meaning (a convention, which makes no claim about the world) can be promoted, without any new atom, into a verdict about who wins. Nothing flags the transition: the institution keeps the same vocabulary and the same procedural form. This dissolves a large part of the apparent A/B puzzle. The two options can describe the *same institution at two moments*: a convention that merely reflected power at t becomes an outcome-fixing verdict at t+1, and therefore reshapes the distribution at t+2, without anyone importing a new primitive. The change is in the bond and the seat, not in the ingredients.

**Refuter:** show an institution whose conventions decide outcomes while still being checkable by an outside frame that cannot reconstruct the decision. If such a convention remains a convention by the figures' own test, F6 is narrowed.

### F7 - The causal arrow is not carried by the correlation

Fig 12 :59 (symmetric operand swap): "swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No." Fig 12 :74 (ordered operand swap): "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes." Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

"Reflect" is symmetric (`+`): power and rule are both present, and swapping the descriptive order changes nothing about the co-occurrence. "Reshape" is ordered (`→`, and at the limit `⇒`: "A produces B; B did not exist before", Fig 12 :75). A symmetric observation cannot license an ordered claim. This is the same failure as reading causality off a single pooled data set: the two quantities were read from one evidence pool, so their agreement is the pool talking, not two frames ranging against an anchor. Effect sizes, correlations and historical narratives are filters for where to look and what budget to spend; they are not witnesses for the arrow.

**Refuter:** provide a source-discriminating instrument (an exogenous change in the rule with the power distribution held fixed, or the reverse) whose readings distinguish the two arrow directions at the same slot. That is the instrument owed in section 5.

### F8 - Verdicts are per slot, and no global winner is returned

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." Fig 10 :72: "A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom."

An electoral commission, a central bank, a university tenure board and a village land council are not four instances of one variable; they are four slots with different actors, currencies and anchors. Fig 11 :51 refuses any standing classification of which institutions are "reflective" or "transformative" independent of the residual at that slot. And a verdict that held for one historical question does not survive the question moving (Fig 10 :72). The answer is therefore a **separator applied per slot**, not a predicate attached to institution types.

**Refuter:** produce a classification of institutions into reflectors and reshapers that keeps its predictions across a change of residual and across slots without re-anchoring. If it does, per-slot indexing is unnecessary.

### F9 - Reflecting and reshaping institutions are isomers over a fixed atom set

Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :123: "A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." Fig 12 :119: "A part that survives its own use." Fig 12 :121: "The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."

Hold the atom set fixed (the same actors, resources, procedures and sanctions) and let only the bond change: one institution binds rule to power with `+` (mirror, both present), another with `⇒` (production, a new outcome appears), another with `⋛` (a threshold comparison that decides who wins). They are Fig 12 isomers. The ingredient list cannot tell them apart; the bond does. This is why type, not degree, is the variable. A part that survives its own use is a catalyst (Fig 12 :119): an institution can persist across many uses while its behavior flips with the bond. And when the rule's output re-enters as the input to the next power distribution, the relation becomes a cycle whose sign decides whether it closes (Fig 12 :121). See `06_isomers_and_order.md`, with its falsifier.

**Refuter:** show that a reflecting institution and a reshaping institution over the same atom set differ in their atoms, not in their bond. Then the isomer reading fails and the difference is a resource difference.

### F10 - A global "which is primary" answer is a `gap`, not a silence

Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 :133: "what it returns is a shape rather than a name". Fig 13 :121: "Different routes can arrive at the same substance."

No figure line returns a percentage, a universal arrow, or a named institution that "really reshapes". The figures return a kind of thing: a separator with conditions, a refuter, and an instrument owed. Declaring this a `gap` is the honest output, and it is of the **shape-not-a-name kind**, not unreachable in principle: with a per-slot ground reading and a differing closure, the separator can be applied case by case. What cannot be delivered from the figures alone is the one number the word "primarily" asks for.

**Refuter:** a figure passage that does state a universal direction or a global coefficient for institutions would fire this refuter and end the gap. None was found after a full census of Figures 1 to 13, the Operators and Symbols tables, and the formula.

### F11 - Provenance is the whole issue for claimed institutional change

Fig 8 :50: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents." Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." Fig 7 :12-13: "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"

A claim that an institution independently produced a new order and a claim that it merely imported an externally generated order look identical in the historical record. Only the lineage separates them (Fig 8 :50). Reporting a successful reform as a derivation, when it was an import, is a recording carried upward as if it were knowledge (Fig 4 :22). The round trip gap R_T is nonzero here and is enumerated in `11_audit.md`.

**Refuter:** a complete, dated lineage for a case of institutional change, showing source gate and shadow test, would close the provenance gap for that case. Until then the case is a candidate, not a derivation.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before outreach, per the seat map: "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device" (Seat Map :287). The shape claim of this folder is refuted if **any** of the following holds:

1. A single per-slot observable ranks institutions on a reflect/reshape scale and predicts later power shifts better than the four-condition separator (kills F1, F8).
2. An institution with no contact with any differing closure introduces a genuinely new kind of actor or claim that later pays at a non-updating ground (kills F3).
3. A rule that updates on the powerful still functions as an anchor for an independent verdict (kills F2, F5).
4. A symmetric co-observation of rule and power, without any external discriminator, selects the causal arrow by itself (kills F7).
5. Reflecting and reshaping institutions over a fixed atom set are found to differ in atoms rather than in bond (kills F9).
6. A convention that decides an outcome remains checkable by an outside frame that cannot reconstruct that decision (kills F6).
7. A figure passage states a universal institutional direction or a global coefficient (kills F10).

If any fires, the separator is withdrawn or narrowed and the retraction is recorded in section 6. Until one is run, no part of this folder is `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation: the four-condition separator

Applicable **per institutional slot**, in this dependency order (Fig 3 :20):

1. **Describe the residual first** (Fig 8 :53: "Residual first, frame second."). Name what failed to resolve at that slot before naming a theory of institutions.
2. **Anchor test** (Fig 2 :29-30, :47). Is there a reading of who-gets-what that does not update on what the institution or the powerful say about it? If no, the slot is a mutual-update mirror (Fig 2 :35) and no reflect/reshape verdict is available there. Report `gap` for that slot.
3. **Differing closure and import test** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50). Did any new kind of actor, claim or instrument enter from a frame whose closure differs, with provenance and a shadow test? If no, any observed "reshaping" is recombination inside the existing closure (Fig 6 :21) and the correct verdict is reflect-only at that slot.
4. **Seat and refusal test** (Fig 10 :42, :47, :59, :93). Is there a seat able to refuse a verdict, with no gain from a particular reading, and whose standing does not survive the outcome? If the rule fixes outcomes and its keeper benefits, it is a verdict in convention's clothing (Fig 10 :47) and the "reflect" verdict is the stronger one even if the rule looks transformative.
5. **Settle per slot** (Fig 1 :19), then compose, then re-check the residual. Verdicts stay separate and are never averaged into a "primarily".

The verdict at a slot is therefore one of: `mirror / reflect-only` (no independent anchor, no differing closure), `import-conduit / reshaping` (differing closure with provenance and a non-updating ground), `verdict-as-convention` (outcome-fixing rule with a benefiting keeper), or `gap` (no instrument resolves it). This is a shape, not a name, and it returns no institution type as the winner.
