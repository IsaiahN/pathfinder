## What the world sources bear on the question

1. **The arrow is not readable from a correlation.** Glaeser et al. 2004 report that human capital is a more basic source of growth than institutions, and that countries subsequently improve their political institutions. This is a low-overlap corroborator for F7: an observed rule-outcome association does not by itself choose the direction. It does not decide the institutional question; the figures do that. Note that the authors are studying growth, not institutional origins generally, so the corroboration is of the *mechanism shape*, not of a verdict in this domain.
2. **Those with power often lack the motive to change; those with the motive lack power.** Garud, Hardy and Maguire 2007 and Micelotta et al. 2017 (one evidence pool, not two witnesses) corroborate F3 and F5: the actor inside the dominant closure has the means but not the differing reading, while the peripheral actor has the differing reading but not the means. This is the institutional-theory form of "the bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs".
3. **Cognition supplies the mirror.** Jost, Sapolsky and Nam 2018 corroborate F2: people are motivated to defend and justify the institutions on which they depend, often nonconsciously. Their primate material (status maintenance through coalition sense, hierarchies that ritualize inequality, and protest when inequity is perceived) corroborates F4 and F9 from a genuinely low-overlap field: the same relation can stabilize or flip depending on the coalition and the residual.
4. **A balance of power is a moving coalition system, not a fixed stock.** de Waal 2010 and 2022 (same author, counted as one witness) corroborate F8 and F9: power is the ability to decide group processes, coalitions decide who rules, and even the most powerful player faces forces that erode its position. "Male alphas came and went during her long reign" is a direct case of an institutional position outliving the individuals who occupy it, which is the phenomenon F11 says needs a lineage.
5. **Agreement can be coherent drift.** The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap corroborates F5 and F7: "You may all agree and be wrong. You may set your clock to someone else's, who was setting your clock to yours." This is Fig 2's mutual-update collapse described in a different vocabulary. Same author as the figures, so maximal overlap and no independent witness weight.

No world source conflicts with the figure verdict. The conflict rule was not triggered. The institutional-theory sources share one evidence pool; the economics, cognition and primatology sources are genuinely lower-overlap corroborators.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

- "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." (Fig 4 :22). Sending the full framework before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the four conditions and the refuter; offer links after.
- "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." (Fig 10 :42). A recipient who has absorbed the framework before the verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit" (Fig 9 :27-28). Ask what they would expect to see if the separator were true before giving them the article.
- "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention" (Seat Map :163). A mass send selected for people who already agree produces a convention, not corroboration.
- "Prestige can fund an action... But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it" (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*). The ask gives the claim a budget to be tried, not an endorsement.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the four-condition separator and its refuter; (2) ask what they would count as a failure in their own field; (3) record that verdict; (4) only then the three links, with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority; (5) offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user write their own refuter and state their own mode.
