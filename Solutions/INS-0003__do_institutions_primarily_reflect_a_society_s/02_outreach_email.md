# Outreach draft - check the separator before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. No personal emails are used anywhere in this folder.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the live type questions in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 6 are visible to the reader as open questions. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Mode:** `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the universal "primarily" quantity. This is a **simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures**, not a ground reading.

**Subject:** One output to check before we argue about whether institutions reflect power or reshape it

> Hello,
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question as an effect.** We are told to choose: do institutions primarily reflect an existing balance of power, or can they independently reshape it? The output refuses the choice. It says the two are different relations with different anchors, and that a verdict requires four conditions to be checked at a specific institutional slot, not a global percentage.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):**
> 1. Describe what failed to resolve at that slot before naming any theory of institutions.
> 2. Ask whether there is a reading of who-gets-what that does **not** update on what the powerful or the institution say about it. If the only reading updates, the slot is a mirror and no reflect/reshape verdict is available there.
> 3. Ask whether any new kind of actor, claim or instrument entered from a frame whose closure differs, with a dated source. If nothing crossed, an observed "reshaping" is recombination inside the existing closure.
> 4. Ask whether the rule still leaves a seat able to refuse a verdict, with no gain from a particular reading. If the rule now decides who wins and its keeper benefits, it is an outcome-fixing verdict wearing a convention's clothes.
>
> Verdicts are per slot and are never averaged. The output returns mirror / import-conduit / verdict-as-convention / gap. It does not return a named institution type as the winner.
>
> **What it does not claim:** that institutions are mere epiphenomena (they introduce actors into a habitat they did not author), that any institution can reshape power from its own resources alone (change requires contact with a differing closure), or that the figures settle a question that needs measured institutional outcomes. Nothing here is `grounded`, and this is a simulated derivation, not a ground reading.
>
> **What would refute it, written before this message:**
> 1. A single per-slot observable that ranks institutions on a reflect/reshape scale and predicts later power shifts better than the four-condition separator.
> 2. An institution with no contact with any differing closure that introduces a genuinely new kind of actor which later pays at a non-updating ground.
> 3. A rule that updates on the powerful still functioning as an anchor for an independent verdict.
> 4. A symmetric co-observation of rule and power that selects the causal arrow by itself.
> 5. Reflecting and reshaping institutions over a fixed atom set differing in atoms rather than in bond.
>
> If any of these holds, I will narrow or withdraw the output. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **Before you answer,** what would you count as a failure of this separator in your own field? I would like your verdict on stage 1 recorded first.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send the three source links. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** I can point you to the formula. It is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer. Please write down what would refute your own result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get.
