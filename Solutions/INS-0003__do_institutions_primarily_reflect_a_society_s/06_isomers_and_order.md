# Bonds, isomers and order in the reflect / reshape question

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The question uses one word, "institutions", and two verbs, "reflect" and "reshape", as if they named one dial with two ends. Two derivations are owed: (1) which typed relation (bond) each verb names, and whether the two are isomers over a fixed atom set; (2) what dependency order the checks must run in. Both have falsifiers.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Which bond is "reflect" and which is "reshape"? (correction to my own first pass)

My first reading assigned `≡` (identity) to reflection. That is refused and preserved here as evidence of the correction.

- Operators table :24-25: identity is "two names, one referent", test: "substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No." A rule and a power distribution are not two names of one referent.
- Fig 12 :59: "swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No." This is the symmetric test. Reflection is co-presence: rule and distribution are both there, and swapping the descriptive order changes nothing about the observation. So reflection is `+` (conjunction).
- Fig 12 :74: "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes." This is the ordered test. Reshaping is a sequence or a production: the rule precedes and the new distribution did not exist before. Fig 12 :75: "A produces B; B did not exist before" is `⇒` (production), the stronger case; `→` (sequence) covers the weaker case where the later distribution was already waiting.
- Fig 12 :90: "A relation that fails all six is not a bond you are missing. It is a claim that has not been made yet." The offered "primary" relation is not a bond; it is a comparative preference over two bonds. The correct operator for the question itself is `⋛` (comparison, a threshold relation between two quantities), and it applies to the *researcher's* ranking of mechanisms, not to the institution's action.
- Operators :57: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate." Refusing the either/or requires `¬`, which is unary and not a bond. The question as posed has no negative operator, so it can only accumulate options; the derivation adds the refusal explicitly.

**Falsifier for Q1:** show that reflection is an ordered production (the rule literally creates the distribution it is said to reflect) or that reshaping is a symmetric co-presence. Then the bond mapping fails and the isomer claim below is moot.

### Q2 - Are a reflecting and a reshaping institution isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Hold the atom set fixed: same actors, same resources, same procedures, same sanctions. Institution R binds rule to power with `+` (mirror). Institution S binds rule to a later distribution with `⇒` (production). The ingredient lists are identical; the behavior differs categorically. By the figure's definition they are **isomers**.
- Fig 12 :123: "A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." "Institution" is the functional group; "reflecting" and "reshaping" are qualifiers. This is why a standing list of institution types that are "transformative" fails (Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."): the qualifier flips when the residual changes.
- Fig 13 :121: "Different routes can arrive at the same substance." A reflecting route and an importing route may converge on the same observed institution. Only the lineage separates them (Fig 8 :50: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents.").
- **Strain, named:** the isomer claim holds only over a fixed atom set. If the reshaping case imported a new actor, the atoms changed and the relation is composition plus import, not isomerism. This condition is live and is carried into `08` and `09`; it is not smoothed away.
- **Falsifier for Q2:** find a pair where the reflecting and reshaping institutions differ in their atom inventory rather than only in the bond between atoms. Then they are not isomers and type is not the variable.

### Q3 - Catalyst and cycle: why an institution can outlive its authors

- Fig 12 :119: "A part that survives its own use." An institution that persists across many contests while continuously changing which coalition it serves is a catalyst, not a spent part. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing in the ingredient list records which a term is.
- Fig 12 :121: "The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." When the rule's output (who won) becomes the input to the next distribution, the relation is a cycle. With a negative sign (an outside discriminator that can refuse the winner) it can close toward a ground; without one it is the coherent drift of Fig 2 :35. A chain of "reflect then reshape then reflect" cannot close by construction, which is why the loop needs a seat able to refuse.
- **Falsifier for Q3:** show an institutional cycle that closes to an independent ground with no refusal operator in the arrangement. That would break the requirement that closing needs a negative operator.

### Q4 - Two currencies: a rule is priced in bits and in actions

- Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."
- Describing an institution (its rules, offices, procedures) is a price in bits. Finding out whether it actually changed who-gets-what is a price in actions. A long, cheaply described rule can take one measurement to refute; a short, easily stated constitutional clause can take a whole budget to settle. The two readings come apart in both directions, and summing them into one "institutional strength" score is the error the figure names.
- **Falsifier for Q4:** a single measure that predicts both the descriptive complexity and the settlement cost of an institutional change, with the two moving together across cases. Then the currencies add and this finding is refused.

### Q5 - Dependency order: which check may not be skipped

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

1. **Perception / describe R.** Name what failed to resolve at this slot. Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit".
2. **Vocabulary / anchor test.** Is there a reading of outcomes that does not update on what the powerful say? Fig 2 :29-30. Without it there is no term for "reshape", only a mirror.
3. **Objective / state the refuter.** Fig 3 :9-10: the system "can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms". The separator is the objective, bound as an ordinary term before the run.
4. **Planning / import and seat checks.** Fig 6 :23 for the differing closure; Fig 10 :42 and :47 for the seat that can refuse and the convention that must not decide outcomes.
5. **Learn and carry / settle per slot.** Fig 1 :19; Fig 13 :111: "A settled composition enters the library as a term, and is then an operand like any other." A settled per-slot verdict becomes an atom at the next scale, which is the recursion, not a new primitive.
6. **Re-check R.** Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."

A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can (Fig 3 :22). Skipping the anchor test and going straight to "which institution is transformative" is effort above a break: it cannot rescue the missing reading below it.

**Falsifier for Q5:** a case where the import-and-seat checks succeed while the anchor test was never run, and the resulting verdict still pays at an instrument that discriminates sources. That would show the order is advisory rather than dependency-ordered.

## CONCLUSION

The two offered relations use different bonds: reflection is symmetric conjunction (`+`), reshaping is ordered sequence or production (`→`, `⇒`). Over a fixed atom set they are isomers, with "institution" as the functional group and the bond as the qualifier; the isomer claim is live only under that fixed-atom condition, and a genuine import breaks it into composition plus import. An institution can outlive its authors as a catalyst, and its output can re-enter as a cycle that closes only if a refusal operator is present. The checks must run in dependency order: describe the residual, test the anchor, bind the objective and refuter, test contact and the seat, settle per slot, then re-check the residual. No number of institutions or amount of agreement returns a global "primarily" direction.
