**What this is:** a claim in a form a scholar of institutions and power can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural separator, `gap` on the universal "primarily" quantity. Not `grounded`.** No institutional outcome was measured. ## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question's either/or is a union compound, not a partition. "Reflect" is a symmetric reading (rule and distribution co-present); "reshape" is an ordered production (a new distribution appears). They are different bonds, in different domains, ranging against different anchors, and a correlation cannot choose between them. What survives is a four-condition separator applied per institutional slot: describe the residual; test for a reading of who-gets-what that does not update on what the powerful say; test for a new kind of actor or claim entering from a differing closure with provenance and a shadow test; test for a seat able to refuse a verdict whose keeper gains from a particular reading. The verdict at a slot is one of mirror, import-conduit, verdict-as-convention, or gap. No global "primarily" direction is returned; that is a declared gap.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - The union and the mirror; checkable by reading Figures 9, 2 and 1

- **Statement:** the two offered options are not opposite values of one dial. Reflection is symmetric co-presence; reshaping is ordered production. An institution whose reference updates on the powerful is a mirror and cannot correct or ground anything, and averaging over institution types destroys the signal.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :57 - "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 12 :59 - "swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No." Fig 12 :74 - "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes." Fig 2 :35 - "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 1 :19 - "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
- **Refuter:** exhibit a single per-slot observable that ranks institutions on one reflect/reshape scale and predicts later power shifts better than the four-condition separator; or show an institution whose rule updates on the powerful while still serving as an anchor for an independent verdict.

### C2 - Change requires a differing closure; checkable by reading Figures 6 and 8, plus the institutional-theory literature

- **Statement:** an institution cannot generate a new kind of actor or claim from its own closure. Real reshaping has the signature of import: a new frame crosses the boundary, with a dated source gate and a shadow test. The word "independently" in the question therefore fails.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :21 - "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Fig 6 :23 - "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 8 :50 - "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents." Also Fig 11 :61 - "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." The world corroborator (secondary, not decisive): Garud, Hardy and Maguire 2007, "Dominant actors in a given field may have the power to force change but often lack the motivation; while peripheral players may have the incentive to create and champion new practices, but often lack the power to change institutions."
- **Refuter:** exhibit an institution with no contact whatever with a differing closure that introduced a genuinely new kind of actor which later paid at a non-updating ground; or show that the Garud et al. pattern does not hold in the case at hand (a world conflict returns to the census, it does not flip the figure verdict).

### C3 - The convention that decides an outcome; checkable by reading Figure 10 and the seat map

- **Statement:** the mechanism that turns "reflect" into apparent "reshape" without any new atom is the promotion of a shared convention into an outcome-fixing verdict. Because the keeper has a stake that survives the outcome, the promotion is silent. The institutional design consequence is that the balance of power cannot be the anchor for institutional design once its beneficiaries keep the reading.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :47 - "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one". Fig 10 :61 - "standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on". Fig 10 :93 - "A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified." Fig 2 :47 - "A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame."
- **Refuter:** exhibit a society where the balance of power is read by a keeper whose standing does not survive the outcome and whose reading cannot move with the powerful; or a convention that decides outcomes yet remains checkable by an outside frame that cannot reconstruct that decision. The latter is a live type question, not a settled point.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that institutions are mere epiphenomena; they introduce actors and change contact, with asymmetric power.
- Not that any institution can reshape power from its own resources alone.
- Not that an observed correlation identifies a causal arrow.
- Not that the figures name a specific institution as "the one that reshapes". That is a binding with provenance, not a derivation, and no ground reading was taken.
- Not `grounded`. Not a theory of everything, no final room.
- Not a settlement of the question this packet reports on. It is a simulated derivation.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes
