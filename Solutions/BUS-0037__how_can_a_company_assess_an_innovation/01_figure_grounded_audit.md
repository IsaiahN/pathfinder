## 0. The residual, in effect terms

**Question as put:** how can a company assess an innovation whose market, customers, and business model are all uncertain?

**Residual R:** a company holds a candidate term φ (the innovation) and must decide whether to commit an action budget. An assessment verdict is normally a comparison between a prediction Γ(b,a) and an observation o′ from a ground. Here the three candidate grounds are all unavailable or unreadable:

- no market that will pay (the anchor named in Fig 2 :29 is not reachable yet),
- no customers whose behaviour can be read (no instrument returning anything),
- no business model, which is the missing comparison term that would state what "success" even means (Operators table :52).

So the residual is not "how do we score the innovation better." It is: **how does a frame obtain a reachable anchor and a comparison term when the anchor it would normally use does not yet exist?** The three uncertainties are conditions of the residual, not three versions of one question.

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term (formula, "Direction").** Write down the sought-for shape as a term and let it accrue a residual: *a procedure that (1) splits the compound into separately checkable conditions, (2) constructs a proto-instrument already returning something at the ground, (3) runs inward and outward as two separate tracks, (4) arranges for refusal before the run, and (5) reports `unreachable` with the shape of what is owed rather than manufacturing a verdict.* This binding is pre-committed in §4 below, so it can be refused by the same test as everything else.

**What each option leads to.** Option A, score with internal instruments, produces a number the frame also produces, which Fig 1 :27 disqualifies as self-scoring. Option B, build contact and refuse on schedule, produces a checkable procedure that never claims to name the winning market, customer, or model before the ground settles it.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** three conditions (market uncertain, customers uncertain, business model uncertain). This is a **union compound**, not one hard question. Fig 9 :4-5: *"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* A single lever clears the region where one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.
- **Symmetry:** ordered, not symmetric. The relation runs action → observation → residual; the verdict is a threshold comparison (commit / refuse), i.e. a comparison bond ⋛, not a symmetric conjunction. Operators table :52: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one."* The missing business model is precisely a missing comparison term.
- **Scale:** three scales, with a lossy membrane between them. Slot scale (one customer contact, one experiment), venture scale (the composition of slots into a decision), market scale (the anchor that settles). Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

### One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 The company's prediction frame (Γ) | managers, analysts, the pitch, the TAM model, the scoring rubric | predictions Γ(b,a) and internal agreement | none of its own: its quantities are frame-internal | frame, not settleable locally |
| D2 Customer contact / proto-instrument | potential customers, their behaviour (what they do, not what they say) | willingness to pay, repeat use, time spent, pre-orders | customer behaviour, which is pinned: *"pinned: it does not update on A or B"* (Fig 2 :25) | admitted: nameable anchor |
| D3 Market habitat | customers, competitors, entrants, substitutes, complementors, regulators | survival, share, profit, payment | the market's payment, named as an anchor in Fig 2 :29 | admitted, reachable only through D2 instruments |
| D4 The business-model arrangement | atoms (value proposition, delivery, revenue mechanism) and their bonds | two incommensurate currencies: bits for the description, actions for finding out whether it holds | a comparison term that fixes what counts as success | compound, not yet a domain until the comparison is bound |
| X1 Founder conviction / pitch charisma | the maker | enthusiasm, credibility | none nameable | not a domain; worse, *"evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel, and the verifier is denied it"* (Fig 9 :51) |
| X2 Market-size estimate (TAM) as a verdict | the analyst | a number | none | filter for budget, not witness for verdict (Fig 9 :60) |

**Partition or union.** Union. D2 and D3 overlap (customers are in both), and D4 is a compound whose conditions bind across the others. So the whole is a union of overlapping conditions, and each condition keeps its own verdict. Verdicts are never averaged into one "innovation score." Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Perception → vocabulary → objective → planning → learn and carry. Mapped here: can the company register what the innovation affords (Fig 3 :5, :7) → can it say what would count as progress in its own terms (Fig 3 :10: *"it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms"*) → can it choose a route under incomplete information (Fig 3 :13) → can it name, verify and carry what worked (Fig 3 :15). The chain usually breaks between vocabulary and objective, exactly where "business model uncertain" sits. Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Effort spent polishing the pitch lands above the break: Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*

**Strain test applied during the census.** Fig 2 :29 names a market as an anchor. For an innovation whose market does not exist, that line strains if forced to anchor the assessment. Re-census: the market line governs D3 as a directional target for an established business; it does not supply a local anchor for D2 of a not-yet-existing market. The local anchor is a customer behaviour that is already returning something. Strain dissolved by separating domains, not by softening the reading. This matches the canonical rule that a reading making figures contradict one another has misidentified its domain.

## 3. Findings F1 to F10

### F1  -  Three uncertainties are a union compound, not one hard question (decisive)

Fig 9 :4-5: *"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*

Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*

Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

A single "innovation score" averages the three conditions into the mean that hides them. Each condition needs its own loop and its own ground. **Refuter:** an aggregate score over the three conditions predicts ground-verified outcomes as well as the per-condition procedure on a held-out set of innovations. If it does, the split is unnecessary.

### F2  -  Internal measures cannot be the verdict

Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*

Fig 9 :51: *"evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel, and the verifier is denied it"* (preceded in the same line by: *"evidence bearing on the residual is admissible"*).

The pitch quality, the TAM, the founder's confidence, and internal consensus are all produced by the frame that wants the verdict. The founder's conviction is a stake-inducing channel, explicitly inadmissible. **Refuter:** a frame-internal measure settles a live dispute about the innovation without any contact with customers, with the same accuracy as behavioural contact. **Status: triangulated** (Fig 1 and Fig 9, low overlap).

### F3  -  The market is an anchor in general, but not reachable here

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* and: *"A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."*

Fig 2 :25: *"pinned: it does not update on A or B"*

Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*

For an existing business, the market is the anchor. For this innovation, the market is the *target*, not yet an instrument. The available local anchor is a customer behaviour that will not move when the company argues with it (a pre-order, a repeat use, a payment). The re-census in §1 dissolves the apparent contradiction. **Refuter:** the company can show a market anchor that already returns a stable reading for this innovation before any contact experiment, and that reading is not produced by the same frame. **Status: triangulated** (Fig 2 anchor rule + Fig 6 instrument rule below).

### F4  -  An instrument is improved from a worse one already returning something

Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."*

Fig 6 :34: *"Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from"* and: *"the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there"*

Fig 11 :70: *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."*

This is the figure-grounded content of a minimum viable contact: not "build a smaller product," but "find the worst instrument already returning a signal at the ground, then sharpen it." A landing page, a manual concierge service, a pre-order ask, or a single repeated-use observation qualifies only if something already registers. An internal demo or a focus group is a substituted habitat: it proves wiring, never capability. **Refuter:** a company builds a valid assessment instrument from a pure description with no prior reading, or a synthetic solve transfers to market capability with no habitat-substitution loss. **Status: triangulated** (Fig 6 and Fig 11).

### F5  -  Capability is a property of innovation-and-habitat, not of the innovation alone

Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*

Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*

Fig 11 :44: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."*

Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*

Assessing the innovation in isolation (the pitch deck, the technology demo) grades the agent, not the agent-and-habitat system. The habitat must be enumerated: what acts on the innovation, what it acts on, conditions, and relations out to the cascade. Valence is relative: the same partner, price point, or channel helps one innovation and hurts another, so there is no standing list of good actors. **Refuter:** an invariant actor-valence list that survives a documented change of residual, or a capability demonstrated in isolation that transfers to the market without loss. **Status: single-source for the enumeration mechanism (Fig 11), corroborated on the anchor requirement by Fig 2.**

### F6  -  Inward and outward are two tracks, never read against each other

Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*

Fig 6 :29: *"inward: is our representation adequate to hold it? The instrument extends access. Fails by refining forever on an axis that cannot carry it. Sharpen in our own terms."*

Fig 6 :30: *"outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."*

Fig 6 :31: *"Neither answer tells you much about the other. Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other."*

Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*

**Inward:** sharpen the contact instrument in your own terms, from one already returning something. **Outward:** describe the residual in effect terms and look for a frame whose closure already predicts its shape (an adjacent market, a different segment, a peer domain that has already met this shape of uncertainty). Fig 8 :53: *"Residual first, frame second."* Outward imports carry a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :12 *"source gate: provenance"* and :13 *"shadow test: explains a gap"*). **Refuter:** one of the two tracks routinely returns the answer of the other, so independence between them is zero. **Status: triangulated** (Fig 6 and Fig 8/Fig 9).

### F7  -  Two currencies, and a business plan prices only one

Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*

Fig 12 :126: *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*

Fig 12 :127: *"It proposes arrangements it cannot afford to test, and the budget is what decides how much of the space is reached at all."*

Operators table :52: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*

Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

A business model is an arrangement, and it costs in bits (how cheaply you can state it) and in actions (what it costs to find out whether it holds). A polished plan prices the first and is silent on the second, so it proposes arrangements the company cannot afford to test. The missing business model is a missing comparison term: until a goal is bound, the vocabulary literally cannot state what would count as success. TAM, market-size estimates and scoring rubrics are filters for the budget, never witnesses for the verdict. **Refuter:** a plan's description cost predicts its action cost, or a filter is shown to settle a verdict without a witness. **Status: triangulated** (Fig 12, Operators table, Fig 9, distinct roles).

### F8  -  Refusal must be arranged before the run

Fig 10 :82: *"Three panels, one move: arrange for refusal."*

Seat Map :287: *"And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

Operators table :57: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*

Fig 9 :41: *"so keep every cut ranked and reversible"*

Fig 9 :42: *"narrowing gets you close, the ground decides"*

Each checkpoint must carry a pre-committed refusal criterion, written before the contact experiment. The founder's stake cannot be removed: Seat Map :171 says *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"* and *"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it."* Fig 10 :73: *"The residual selects the members, but only from what is available. A candidate set chosen by a participant is an authored room however well the selection is reasoned, so the pool must be unauthored first and examined second."* Fig 10 :78 offers the mechanism: *"a set assembled before the question existed"*. **Refuter:** a checkpoint whose refusal criterion was written after seeing the result, or a review panel hand-picked by the innovation's sponsor. **Status: triangulated** (Fig 10, Seat Map, Operators table).

### F9  -  A room of agreeing managers cannot corroborate

Fig 7 :12: *"same closure, handed along;"* and :13: *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*

Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*

Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*

Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

The usual innovation committee is a room sharing one evidence pool (the same deck, the same market report). Its agreement is the pool talking. Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong: a cheap reversible experiment needs one frame; an irreversible capital commitment needs several low-overlap frames ranging separately against the ground. **Refuter:** committee agreement with zero independence predicts outcomes as well as independent ranging on ground-verified cases. **Status: triangulated** (Fig 7 and Fig 2).

### F10  -  What survives, and what is owed but not held

Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*

Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*

Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

No figure line names which market, which customer, or which business model will win. After a genuine re-census that is a **`gap`**, not a hidden answer. The framework returns the shape of a procedure and the shape of the missing thing (a source-discriminating contact with a paying ground), in effect terms. It cannot return the name. **Refuter:** a figure line is produced that directly selects a specific market or revenue model for an arbitrary innovation, with a verbatim citation. **Status: single-source for the gap verdict across Fig 13, Fig 9, Fig 8, all with low overlap on the limit claim, so triangulated as a limit.**

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach, Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim (the eight-step procedure in §5) is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Aggregation fires.** A single aggregate score across market, customers and business model predicts ground-verified venture outcomes as well as or better than the per-condition procedure on a held-out set. (Breaks Fig 1 :19 and Fig 9 :55.)
2. **Maker evidence fires.** Founder conviction or pitch quality, with no behavioural contact, predicts outcomes as well as behavioural instruments. (Breaks Fig 9 :51 and Fig 1 :27.)
3. **No anchor fires.** A frame-internal measure, such as TAM or a business-plan score, settles a live dispute without any contact with customers. (Breaks Fig 2 :25 anchor rule.)
4. **Pure composition fires.** The winning business model is derivable by internal composition alone, with no import from a differing closure and no new instrument. (Breaks Fig 6 :23.)
5. **Substituted habitat fires.** A synthetic or internal solve transfers to market capability with no measurable habitat-substitution loss. (Breaks Fig 11 :70.)
6. **Room fires.** A committee sharing one evidence pool corroborates the verdict at the same accuracy as independent ranging. (Breaks Fig 7 :35 and Fig 2 :40.)

If any fires, the procedure is wrong and the claim is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation: the procedure the figures license

1. **Split before you search.** Name the three conditions separately and give each its own loop, its own instrument, and its own verdict. Do not issue one score. (F1)
2. **Disqualify frame-internal substitutes.** Pitch, TAM, founder conviction and internal consensus are either self-scoring or stake-inducing channels. (F2, F7)
3. **Find a proto-instrument already returning something, then sharpen it.** A minimum viable contact that registers behaviour (payment, repeat use, time spent) rather than opinion. (F4)
4. **Range it against a pinned anchor.** Customer behaviour does not update when the company argues with it. (F3)
5. **Run inward and outward as two tracks.** Sharpen your own instrument; separately, describe the residual in effect terms and find a frame whose closure already predicts its shape, with provenance and a shadow test. (F6)
6. **Enumerate the habitat.** List actors, conditions and relations out to the cascade; expect valence to change with the residual. (F5)
7. **Price both currencies.** Keep bits (description cost) and actions (cost of finding out) in separate columns. Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. (F7)
8. **Arrange for refusal.** Write the failure criterion before each experiment, keep cuts ranked and reversible, declare the sponsor's stake, and assemble reviewers from an unauthored pool. (F8, F9)
9. **Stop honestly.** If nothing in the closure closes the residual, report `unreachable` with the shape of what is owed. Do not name the market. (F10)

## 7. Honest stop

Unreached, not unreachable. The figures license a procedure and a shape for the missing instrument. They do not supply the market, the customers or the revenue model for any particular innovation. That remains a `gap` until a source-discriminating contact with a paying ground settles it (Fig 9 :42: *"narrowing gets you close, the ground decides"*). The record of which conditions were split, what was imported with provenance, and what would reverse the verdict is what makes the stop checkable.

**No measurement was taken.** All world sources are secondary corroboration, and most share an evidence pool with each other or with the framework. Their agreement is one pool talking, not corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
