## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E o T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :19 and :27 (per-slot residual, frame-internal disqualification), Fig 9 :4-5, :55 (union), Fig 6 :32 (instrument from a worse one), Fig 12 :125-127 (two currencies).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07` and `09`: split the compound; disqualify internal measures; find a proto-instrument; bind the comparison term; refuse on schedule; report a gap.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a business prescription: run an MVP, test assumptions at checkpoints, write a premortem, refuse at milestone gates.

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **per-slot / never-aggregated** rule is routinely dropped when the business layer converts conditions into a single stage-gate score;
- the **inward/outward separation** (Fig 6 :31) collapses when an analogy is treated as a substitute for a contact instrument;
- the **shape-not-a-name limit** (Fig 13 :135) is breached when a specific segment or revenue model is declared the answer.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a contact instrument already returning something at a pinned behavioural anchor, with the objective bound in advance. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the figures" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
