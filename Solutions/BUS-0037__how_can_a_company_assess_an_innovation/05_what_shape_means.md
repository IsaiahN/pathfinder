## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*; Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"This means that you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So for this question a **shape** is the narrowed form of an assessment procedure you can state before you can name the winning market, customer or revenue model. It constrains the procedure; it does not fill in the answer. "Split the compound, build a reachable instrument, refuse on schedule" is a shape. "The winning market is mid-market logistics firms in Southeast Asia" is a name, and the framework does not license it.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, then look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it. *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."* In business terms: start from the residual (three uncertainties, no anchor) and let the instruments you find sharpen the vocabulary, not the other way round.
- The same essay names the failure mode this question is unusually prone to: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"* That is F1 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, and it arrives from a low-overlap essay rather than from a figure line. It is corroboration only.
- Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"* The company must describe the residual in effect terms before choosing the analogous frame for the outward track.
- Fig 6 :30: *"outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."*
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* This is why the outreach email sends a checkable effect description rather than the architecture.

## 3. What licenses a name

Two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* The figures README: the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."* For this question, a customer pays, repeats, or does not. That is what names the customer.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs**, with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :23, :12, :13). A peer market that has already met this shape can supply a hypothesis, not a settlement.

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism for why a reading is not a naming: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"*, and *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* A landing-page signup reads "interest" but cannot distinguish curiosity from willingness to pay. That is a metamerism error in the business layer: the instrument reads one value for several sources. What is owed is a discriminator (a payment, a repeat), not a confident name.

*Decoding Tacit Knowledge* sharpens the same point: *"A measurement with no question is noise, and a reading with no encounter is projection."* A TAM number with no question is noise; a persona written from a workshop with no encounter is projection.

## 4. Why the "shape, not a name" rule is narrowed here, not applied bluntly

**Correction to my own first reading.** I initially wrote that the framework forbids naming anything in this question. That overstates the rule. The README's phrase governs a **missing** thing: *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Here no primitive is missing from the framework's six-term set. What is missing is (a) a local anchor at the ground and (b) a comparison term that would state the goal. Naming an instrument category (a payment test, a repeat-use observation) is a binding from the business frame onto a described effect, not the invention of a new primitive. The honest form is: the framework returns the **shape** of the instrument (registers behaviour at a pinned anchor, discriminates among sources, is improvable from a worse instrument already returning something), while the company supplies the specific instrument as a `specified`, ungrounded binding with provenance.

This correction is deliberate and matches the exemplar's own §5b retraction pattern. It does **not** soften F10: which market wins remains a `gap` until the ground settles it.

## 5. Prestige is not action

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."*

In this question the prestige trap has a specific business form: a well-known investor, a famous accelerator, or an influential analyst can give the innovation a budget to be tried. That is legitimate. What is not legitimate is letting their endorsement settle whether the innovation works. Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

## 6. My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

Same essay: *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."* And: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula"* while *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."*

So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, `06_isomers_and_order.md` and `09_watson_answer.md` are simulated derivations, checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are **not** a test of any innovation and must be labelled as such. They cannot settle the question they report on.

## 7. Answer to the only question this file owes

**What does "shape" mean for an innovation whose market, customers and business model are all uncertain?** It means a procedure-shaped answer: split the compound; treat the market as a directional target rather than an available anchor; find a proto-instrument already returning something at a pinned behavioural anchor; keep inward and outward separate; price description and action in different currencies; refuse on schedule; and report the missing discriminator in effect terms. It does **not** mean a named segment, a named revenue model, or a score. Those are names, and names are earned only by the ground.
