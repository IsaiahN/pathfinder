# Outreach draft: check the evidence rule before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter survive the check in `08_expert_verification_packet.md`. The first contact carries the evidence rule and the refuter, never the framework's interiors.

**Subject:** What would you count as proof that a change is a tipping point, not a trend?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one checkable output about tipping points, and I would like your verdict on it before I show you anything else. I am not asking you to agree with a theory, and I am not asking you to trust a framework.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A change qualifies as a tipping point, rather than a gradual trend or an abrupt external event, only when four kinds of evidence come together:
>
> 1. a **threshold comparison**: a control variable can be ranked against a critical value, and the change is localised at that crossing rather than proportional at every scale;
> 2. a **new regime**: the post-change state did not exist before in that regime, with hysteresis or alternative stable states when the forcing is relaxed;
> 3. a **closing internal loop**: after the trigger, self-amplifying feedbacks dominate the dynamics, so a chain that cannot close does not qualify;
> 4. a **persistent, non-trending residual** before the flip: the old regime's prediction stops improving, showing as slower recovery, rising autocorrelation, or rising variance.
>
> This is a conjunction, not a score. A gradual trend is an ordered sequence with no new regime and no closing loop. An abrupt event is a jump imposed from outside, with no internal threshold crossed. An early-warning statistic on its own is a filter for deciding what to measure, not a witness for the verdict.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that this rule gives a universal number for a critical temperature or a critical freshwater flux. It gives the *shape* of the evidence. The numeric threshold is settled for each system by an instrument, a palaeo reconstruction, or a model that can actually be pushed through the crossing. Where that has not been done, the honest report is "threshold unidentified", not a guessed value.
>
> **What would refute the rule:** (1) two cases with the same measured variables and the same arrangement, correctly called one tipping point and one trend with no further evidence; (2) a confirmed shift with no control variable rankable against a critical value and proportional at all scales; (3) a shift fully explained by external forcing that relaxes back when the forcing stops. I wrote these down before sending this. If one holds, I withdraw or narrow the rule. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send the three sources behind it: the formal tipping-element definition, the early-warning review and its limits, and the detection and attribution practice that shows how independent lines of evidence are combined. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the rule was derived from a composition framework whose loop is public. Run it on a system you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
