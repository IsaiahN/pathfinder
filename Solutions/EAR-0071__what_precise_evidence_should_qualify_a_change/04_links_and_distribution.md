## Two things in the wider literature that bear directly on the question

1. **Early warning is necessary-looking but not sufficient.** Scheffer et al. 2012 say slowing down is *"neither a universal warning signal for shifts nor specific to an approaching tipping point"* and *"Further diagnosis of what might be coming up requires additional information."* Dakos et al. 2012 show variance can fall. This is the world-side reason the answer is a **conjunction** of evidence, not a single statistic. It matches Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
2. **"Abrupt" is not the criterion.** Lenton et al. 2008 allow *"gradual or episodic tipping"* and *"slow or asymptotic tipping"*. A fast jump with an external cause and no internal loop is an abrupt event, not a tipping point. This matches Fig 12 :121 *"a chain cannot close by construction"*: the discriminator is the closing loop, not the slope.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22-25** — *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down. A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Send the evidence rule and its refuter, not a concluded label.
- **Fig 10 :42** — *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** — *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"* — ask what they would count as a failure before giving them any theory.
- **Fig 2 :40** — *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* — do not pool later agreement as corroboration.
- **Fig 2 :42** — *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* The cost here is mislabelling a real regime shift as a trend (or the reverse), so independent frames are worth buying.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the discriminating evidence rule and the four refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) record the verdict; (4) only then, if they are curious, the sources above, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

- **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52 *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* The recipient checks the evidence rule without holding the framework's interior.
- **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. Record the stage-1 verdict first.
- **Stage 3 sends a method**, which is permitted by Fig 4 :22. Each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode, because a frame cannot check the direction of its own question (THE_FORMULA, "Direction").

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 rule survives. The four refuters in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` are the test. Do not soften them to make the rule easier to accept.
