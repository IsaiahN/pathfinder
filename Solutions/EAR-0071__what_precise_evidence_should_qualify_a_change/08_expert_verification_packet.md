# Expert verification packet: evidence for a tipping point

**What this is:** a claim in a form a climate or Earth-system scientist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so the reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the evidence rule, `general` on its structural shape, not `grounded`.** No Earth-system measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Standing:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It cannot settle the question it reports on (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A change qualifies as a tipping point rather than a gradual trend or an abrupt event only when three things hold together: a threshold comparison (a control variable is ranked against a critical value, and the change is localised at the crossing), a new regime (the post-change state did not exist before, with hysteresis or alternative stable states), and a closing internal loop (self-amplifying feedbacks dominate after the trigger). A persistent, non-trending residual before the flip (slower recovery, rising autocorrelation, sometimes rising variance) is a filter that directs monitoring, not a witness for the verdict, because it can arise from other causes. The evidence must be read per system and per slot, checked by independent lines against a ground that does not move, and accompanied by the provenance of every threshold value. The framework supplies this shape and its refuters; it does not supply a universal number or declare any present-day element tipped.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 12

- **Statement:** the three options differ in bond, not in ingredients. The gradual trend is an ordered sequence (→), the tipping point is a threshold comparison (⋛) plus production (⇒) plus a closing cycle, and the abrupt event has no internal loop. A magnitude or speed test cannot decide.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :94 *"The arrangement is the substance."* Fig 12 :95-96 *"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."* Fig 12 :85-89 *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, test *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."* Fig 12 :121 *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Fig 12 :75-77 *"A produces B; B did not exist before"*.
- **Refuter:** exhibit two cases with the same measured variables and the same bond and arrangement, correctly classified one as a tipping point and one as a gradual trend with no further evidence. Or show a confirmed regime shift with no control variable rankable against a critical value and a proportional response at all observed scales.

### C2 - climate-science-facing; checkable against the published literature

- **Statement:** the three-condition conjunction (threshold crossing, new state, self-amplification) is what the tipping-points literature itself requires, and the early-warning signal is explicitly not sufficient on its own.
- **What to check:** Lenton et al. 2008 (PNAS, doi 10.1073/pnas.0705414105, opened via PMC 2026-11-22) define a tipping element through a critical control value ρcrit with *"any significant variation by δρ > 0 leads to a qualitative change (F̂) in a crucial system feature F"*, and define abrupt change as a transition *"to a new state at a rate determined by the climate system itself and faster than the cause"*. Lenton 2021 (*Weather*, doi 10.1002/wea.4058) states tipping points occur *"when reinforcing (positive) feedbacks within a system take over from stabilising (negative) feedbacks"*. Wunderling et al. 2024 (*Earth System Dynamics*, doi 10.5194/esd-15-41-2024) say *"its self-amplifying feedbacks dominate the dynamics of the tipping element during the tipping process."* Against that, Scheffer et al. 2012 (*Science*, doi 10.1126/science.1225244) say slowing down is *"neither a universal warning signal for shifts nor specific to an approaching tipping point"* and *"sharp change may simply result from a sudden big external impact"*; Dakos et al. 2012 (*Ecology*, doi 10.1890/11-0889.1) find *"variance may sometimes decrease close to a transition"*. Do these sources support the conjunction and the demotion of the early-warning statistic?
- **Refuter:** a documented regime shift that is fully accounted for by an external forcing with no internal reinforcing loop and that relaxes back when the forcing stops. That case is an abrupt event, and the packet's classification rule fails if it is insistently called a tipping point.

### C3 - verification and limits; checkable by reading Figures 1, 2, 8 and 13

- **Statement:** evidence is per slot; a global average hides the live signal. Verification is triangulation against a fixed ground, not agreement inside a shared evidence pool. Absence of early warning is not proof of absence. The framework returns the shape, not a universal number.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 2 :45 *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 8 :39 *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 13 :133 *"what it returns is a shape rather than a name."* Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
- **Refuter:** a confirmed tipping point whose discriminating evidence exists only in a global aggregate and vanishes in every physically meaningful slot; or a claim settled by one frame whose anchor moves when the frame changes; or a universal critical value derived from the figures alone and valid across all tipping elements.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a universal numeric tipping threshold. Critical values are ground-settled per element; Lenton et al. 2008 list several as *"Unidentified"*.
- Not a speed or magnitude criterion. Gradual and slow tipping are allowed in the literature.
- Not that rising autocorrelation or variance alone proves an approaching threshold.
- Not that any specific present-day element (AMOC, Greenland Ice Sheet, Amazon) has tipped. That needs the ground reading this packet does not contain.
- Not that agreement among studies sharing models, datasets or review pools is corroboration (Fig 2 :40 *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (essay, re-opened 2026-11-22): a green reading *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."* Concretely:

1. A pushed model or process experiment that locates ρcrit for one element and shows the response is non-proportional at the crossing.
2. Proxy evidence that discriminates alternative stable states and hysteresis, not merely a fast step.
3. A feedback diagnosis naming the loop that takes over after the trigger and its sign.
4. A recovery-rate record with controls for noise colour and background forcing, so the E4 filter is not a false positive.

Fig 6 :32 *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."*

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 12 :85-89, :94-96, :121, :75-77, four short passages, and say whether the bond distinction is faithful to the figure text.
2. Check C2 against your own knowledge: does the literature require a threshold, a new state and self-amplification, and deny sufficiency to early-warning statistics alone?
3. Name one instrument or proxy that would discriminate a threshold crossing from proportional forcing in the element you know best, or say why none exists.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
