## Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual line: "what evidence should qualify a change as a tipping point rather than a gradual trend or abrupt event?" I used the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors, currency, anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Three candidate descriptions compete for one observation. Arity: three conditions, so a **union compound** (Fig 9 :57 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*), not one hard question. Symmetry: the options are asymmetric, the trend is an ordered sequence, the tipping point needs an ordered comparison and a loop, the abrupt event is imposed. Scale: first order within a regime, second order at the boundary.
- **Domain 1, detection.** Actors: instruments, time series, slots, R⁺_s. Currency: per-slot residuals and their trend. Anchor: the ground. Question type: partition (which slot has live mass).
- **Domain 2, attribution.** Actors: control parameter, feature, feedbacks, structures, bonds, negation. Currency: bonds and settlement. Anchor: an experiment or proxy that can be pushed. Question type: union with Domain 1, detection and attribution are two conditions.
- **Domain 3, verification.** Actors: expert readers. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run its independence is zero.

Order by dependency: detect before attribute before decide (Fig 3); refuse before mint (formula step 2); describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).

My census matches the primary's three domains and its union reading. Where the primary derived from Fig 12's bond test and Fig 5's residual ranking, I derived from Fig 9's union/partition split and Fig 3's dependency rule.
