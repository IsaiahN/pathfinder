# Isomers, order, and cascades: the supporting derivation

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The three candidate descriptions of a change (tipping point, gradual trend, abrupt event) may share all their measured variables. We owe three supporting derivations: (1) what kind of structural relation holds among the three options; (2) what order the evidence must be gathered in; (3) how a regime boundary and a tipping cascade compose. Each has its own falsifier.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: a control variable ρ, a system feature F, the three states and three structures of Fig 13, and the eight operators of the Operators table. Bonds held: + ∥ ≡ → ⇒ − ⋛ and unary ¬. No new primitive is available without import. The three options are not three new atoms; they are three arrangements over the same observational atoms.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the three options isomers, or members of a functional group?

- Fig 12 :117 *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :123 *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- Hold the atom set fixed (same ρ and F measured in all three cases). Then:
  - gradual trend = ordered sequence → (Fig 12 :70-72 *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*); no new state, no closing loop;
  - tipping point = threshold comparison ⋛ plus production ⇒ plus closing cycle (Fig 12 :121);
  - abrupt event = an imposed discontinuity with no internal loop, externally sourced.
- **Therefore**, over the shared atom set, the three are **isomers** under Fig 12's definition: same atoms, different bond, different substance. If the atom sets differ (different variables, different systems), they are instead members of the same **functional group** of "changes in a state variable", with different qualifiers. This is the weaker but always-available claim.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two cases share every measured atom and the same bond and arrangement yet are correctly classified as different option types with no further evidence, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading also fails. Triangulated for the definition (Fig 12), single-source for the bond assignment until a ground settles each case.

### Q2 - What order must the evidence be gathered in?

- Fig 3 :20-21 *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*
- Fig 3 :22-23 *"A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can. Which is why a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing."*
- Formula step 2 orders refusal before mint; Fig 9 :27-28 *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"* orders residual description before frame choice.
- Fig 13 :4-5 *"The two kinds are in lockstep, a crossing is a change of role rather than a step upward, and no member of the pair is senior to the other."* So the order is dependency, never rank. Detection does not outrank attribution; it supplies its input.
- **Therefore** the evidence order is: detect the live per-slot residual (Fig 1 :19), refuse the option whose bond is absent (¬, not a bond: Operators table :57 *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*), then attribute the surviving option's bond, then verify against the ground.
- **Falsifier:** a confirmed threshold attribution with no prior per-slot detection, where the attribution survives without any residual measurement, breaks the dependency order. Mode `general`.

### Q3 - Is the regime boundary a second-order composition?

- Fig 12 :103-104 *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"*
- Each regime, settled against the ground by long observation or palaeo evidence, becomes an atom at the next level. The comparison ⋛ of the old and new regimes is therefore a second-order composition, not a new primitive. This is recursion, not import.
- Fig 12 :112 *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."* Within a regime, progress toward the threshold is a subtraction (distance in ρ to ρcrit); the crossing is a comparison. A vocabulary with only the sequence bond can state the trend and cannot state the goal.
- **Type caveat, still open and not hidden:** the Operators table defines ⋛ as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* (Operators table :49). At second order the operands are settled molecules now serving as atoms. Fig 12 :103 permits the demotion to atom; whether such an atom counts as a quantity at that level needs an instrument, not an argument. Recorded as a strain, as in the exemplar.
- **Falsifier:** show that ⋛'s operands must be bare first-order quantities at every level, so a comparison of two settled regimes is a type error. Then the second-order reading falls and the boundary must be stated at first order only.

### Q4 - Cascades are contact, not composition

- Fig 11 :43-44 *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* and *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."*
- Fig 11 :52-53 *"contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*
- A tipping cascade is therefore not a longer chain of the same bond. It is a contact cascade through an enumerated habitat: the residual that matters may be three steps out. Wunderling et al. 2024 (world source) find that *"tipping elements should not only be studied in isolation, but also more emphasis has to be put on potential interactions"* and that *"tipping cascades cannot be ruled out"* at warming levels between 1.5 and 2.0 degrees C. Low overlap: Fig 11's habitat rule and the Earth-system modelling literature arrive at the same verdict from different directions.
- **Falsifier:** a cascade fully predicted by composing the atoms with the seven bonds, with no enumeration of contact and no indirect residual. Then the habitat rule is redundant for this question.

## CONCLUSION

The three options are best read as **isomers over a shared observational atom set** (same variables, different bonds), with the always-available weaker reading that they are members of one functional group of changes. Their evidence order is **dependency order**, not rank: detect, refuse, attribute, verify. The regime boundary is a **second-order comparison** licensed by the settled-molecule rule, with one operand-type strain named. Cascades are **contact cascades** over an enumerated habitat, not longer chains. Every claim carries a falsifier, and none is `grounded`.
