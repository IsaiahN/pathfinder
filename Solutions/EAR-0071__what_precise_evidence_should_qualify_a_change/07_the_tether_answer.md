# The Tether's answer: what qualifies a change as a tipping point

**Mode: `specified` on the evidence rule, `general` on its structural shape. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## 1. The residual it answers

A change C is observed. Three descriptions compete: a tipping point (threshold crossing with internal reorganisation), a gradual trend (ordered proportional change in the same regime), and an abrupt event (externally imposed jump with no internal threshold). The decision changes what we do: monitor and avoid a threshold, project and adapt to a trend, or attribute and respond to a shock. The residual is which description the evidence licenses, per system and per slot.

## 2. The answer: a discriminating conjunction

Fig 12 :94 *"The arrangement is the substance."* The difference among the three options is not in the measured variables but in the bond. A magnitude test reads the ingredients and cannot decide. The qualifying evidence for a tipping point is therefore a conjunction of four conditions, plus a verification condition:

**E1, threshold comparison.** A control variable can be ranked against a critical value, and the change is localised at that crossing. Fig 12 :85-89 *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, test *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."* In a trend both driver and response are present throughout; the question is "both present", not "which is larger". World anchor: Lenton et al. 2008 define a tipping element by a critical control value ρcrit with *"any significant variation by δρ > 0 leads to a qualitative change (F̂) in a crucial system feature F"*.

**E2, new regime.** The post-change state *"did not exist before"* in that regime (Fig 12 :75-77), evidenced by a qualitative change in F, alternative stable states, or hysteresis. World anchors: Lenton et al. 2008 (a transition *"to a new state at a rate determined by the climate system itself and faster than the cause"*); Wunderling et al. 2024 (a transition to *"a completely different (often undesirable) state"*).

**E3, a closing internal loop.** After the trigger, self-amplifying feedbacks dominate. Fig 12 :121 *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* A gradual trend is a chain (Fig 12 :48 *"A chain is the degenerate case: one bond type, no branching"*) and cannot close. An abrupt event has no internal loop that takes over. World anchors: Lenton 2021 (*"reinforcing (positive) feedbacks within a system take over from stabilising (negative) feedbacks"*); Wunderling et al. 2024 (*"its self-amplifying feedbacks dominate the dynamics ... during the tipping process"*).

**E4, a persistent, non-trending residual before the flip.** The old regime's prediction stops improving. Fig 5 :58-60 *"a term whose residual PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN is the one to point the refusal at, which is the same shrinking test the selector already runs, read with the opposite sign."* World anchors: Scheffer et al. 2009 list *"slower recovery from perturbations, increased autocorrelation and increased variance"*; Lenton et al. 2008 note bifurcation tipping points are *"universally characterized by τ → ∞ at the threshold"*. **But** Scheffer et al. 2012 hold that slowing down is *"neither a universal warning signal for shifts nor specific to an approaching tipping point"*, and Dakos et al. 2012 find variance can decrease. Therefore E4 is a **filter, not a witness**, until E1 to E3 also hold: Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

**V, verification.** Read per slot, not as a global average (Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*). Range independent frames against a ground that does not move (Fig 2 :45 *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*; Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*). Enumerate the habitat and the cascade (Fig 11 :52-53 *"contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*). Record the lineage: Fig 8 :50-52 *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*.

### The three-way discriminator

| option | E1 threshold | E2 new regime | E3 closing loop | E4 persistent residual |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| tipping point | yes | yes | yes | usually, but not sufficient alone |
| gradual trend | no, ordered sequence → | no | no, chain cannot close | residual trends down (selector's improving case) |
| abrupt event | no crossing attributed internally | no new attractor, forced excursion | no, external impact dominates | absent before the jump (Scheffer et al. 2012) |

A change qualifies as a tipping point when E1, E2 and E3 all hold and V is satisfied. E4 raises the prior and directs the search; it does not by itself carry the verdict.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- **Not a speed criterion.** Lenton et al. 2008 allow *"gradual or episodic tipping"* and *"slow or asymptotic tipping"*. Fast is not the same as tipping; slow is not the same as not tipping.
- **Not a universal numeric threshold.** Fig 13 :133 *"what it returns is a shape rather than a name."* The number is ground-settled per element: experiment, palaeo proxy, or a model pushed through the crossing. Several critical values remain *"Unidentified"* in Lenton et al. 2008.
- **Not that absence of early warning disproves a tipping point.** Fig 8 :39 *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
- **Not a single-frame verdict.** Agreement among studies sharing a model, dataset, or review pool is one witness (Fig 2 :40 *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*).
- **Not ground.** No earth-system measurement was taken in this derivation. Nothing here is `grounded`.
- **Not a claim that the figures name any particular element as tipped.** That would require the ground reading this simulation cannot perform.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (essay, re-opened 2026-11-22): a green reading *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."* The spectrometer, not an argument, resolved the colour case. Here the corresponding instruments are:

1. A process experiment or pushed model that locates ρcrit for the element and shows the response is non-proportional at that crossing.
2. A palaeo or observational record with proxies that discriminate alternative stable states and hysteresis, not just a fast step.
3. A feedback diagnosis that shows which loop takes over after the trigger, with a sign that closes it.
4. A recovery-rate or autocorrelation record with a control for noise colour and background forcing, so E4 is not a false positive.

Fig 6 :32 *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* Where nothing is already returning a discriminating reading, the honest report is unreached at this budget, not a minted threshold.

## 5. Honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The rule stops at the limit of what the available instruments discriminate. It can say what evidence would have to look like, what would refute it, and which threshold values are still owed. It cannot say that the AMOC, the Greenland Ice Sheet, or the Amazon has crossed a threshold. Fig 13 :134 *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*. The canonical figures README adds *"Indiscernibility is not identity"* (same framework author, so high overlap).

## 6. Standing falsifiers

1. Same ingredients and same bond, different verdicts with no further evidence (breaks Fig 12 :94).
2. A confirmed shift with no rankable control variable and proportional response at all scales (breaks E1).
3. A shift fully explained by external forcing that relaxes back when forcing stops (breaks E2/E3; reclassify as abrupt event).
4. A slow-down signal with a non-threshold cause, no loop and no threshold (demotes E4 to a filter).
5. A single-frame verdict whose anchor moves with the frame (breaks V).

All five are written before any outreach, as required by the seat map's output-layer rule.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a universal number for a tipping point, and it does not call every fast change one. It says the distinction lives in the relation, not the variables: a tipping point carries a threshold comparison (a control variable crosses a critical value), produces a state that did not exist before, and is carried by a self-amplifying loop that closes after the trigger; a gradual trend is an ordered chain whose residual keeps shrinking; an abrupt event is a jump imposed from outside with no internal threshold crossed. Rising autocorrelation or slower recovery is a filter that tells you where to look, not a witness for the verdict, because it can arise from other causes. The evidence must be read per system and per slot, checked by independent lines against a fixed ground, and accompanied by the record of where each threshold value came from. What the framework returns is the shape of qualifying evidence and the refuters written in advance. The numeric threshold stays open until an instrument, a palaeo record, or a pushed model settles it.
