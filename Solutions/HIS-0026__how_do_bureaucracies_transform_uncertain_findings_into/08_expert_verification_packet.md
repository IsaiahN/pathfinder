# Expert verification packet: the Tether on findings becoming official artifacts

**What this is:** a claim in a form a knowledgeable reader can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the three sub-claims, `general` on the structural split, `gap` on any named case. Not `grounded`.** No measurement of a bureau is claimed. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim in one paragraph

An administrative frame meets a finding that is still contestable. It declares a meaning so the finding can be recorded; that first step is legitimate, because the convention makes the problem statable and does not fix it. The failure comes when the same convention is used to decide an outcome. At that point it stops being a convention and becomes an authoritative constant that nothing outside the frame can check. The aggregate of cases replaces the per-case record, removing the signal that said where to look; the producing frame scores itself with the count it produced; and downstream review compounds agreement without adding evidence, because the official number is treated as an anchor while updating on the frame's own reports. The three artifacts (rule, statistic, official category) use different bonds, so they are a union of conditions and must be checked separately. The framework returns this shape, not a named agency or a verdict about any particular institution.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1. Framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 10 and 2

- **Statement:** a convention used to make a finding statable is legitimate until it decides an outcome. At that moment it stops being a convention and becomes an authored constant carrying the seat's authority.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :16 *"the convention does not fix the bug"*, :17 *"it makes the bug statable"*, :18 *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"*, :47 *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*, :48 *"a convention nothing can check is a constant the seat authored, carrying the seat's authority"*, :49 *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."* Independently, Fig 2 :43 *"Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."*
- **Check:** do the two routes (seat-authority and anchor-collapse) actually converge on the same promotion event, from different quantities? If one route does not reach it, the triangulation mark is wrong.
- **Refuter:** a logged case in which an artifact decides an outcome yet remains checkable and is revised when the ground pushes back. That breaks the promotion claim.

### C2. Framework-internal, checkable by reading Figure 1

- **Statement:** aggregating cases into a statistic discards the per-slot residual, and the producing frame then scores itself with a quantity it also produces.

- **What to check:** the claim is about pre-settlement aggregation, not all aggregation. - **Refuter:** a published statistic that circulates per-slot residuals alongside the aggregate, uses them to aim the next probe, and is scored by a frame that does not draw on the bureau's records. That breaks both halves of C2.

### C3. Structural, checkable by reading Figures 2, 7, 12 and the Operators table

- **Statement:** an official category treated as a pinned anchor while updating on its frame's own reports is a frame, so review chains compound agreement without evidence. Rule, statistic and category are different bonds, not one object; over a fixed atom set rival schemes can be isomers.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*, :33 *"The anchor must not update."*, :34 *"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."*, :40 *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; Fig 7 :13 *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*, :35 *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict."*; Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*, :123 *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Operators table :15 gives the symmetric-bond test *"swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No."*: that is the statistic's bond.
- **What to check:** whether a rule's threshold ⋛ can operate on settled categories as quantities at that level. The Operators table :48 calls ⋛ *"a threshold relation between two quantities."* This type question is open and is named, not hidden (strain S4 in `01`).
- **Refuter:** a category used as a reference by several frames that is corrected separately by each against an unmoved ground, with logged refusals; or a demonstration that rule and statistic share the same bond after re-census. Either breaks C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that every bureaucracy does this, and not that any named agency, statute, statistic or historical episode is at fault. That is a declared `gap`.
- Not that statistics are lies or categories useless. Fig 2 :41 says agreement is *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."*
- Not that all aggregation is forbidden. Only pre-settlement aggregation that discards the live slot.
- Not that the statistic is the anchor. A published, revisable statistic is a channel report; Fig 10 :24 says *"the ground does not decay, the channel does."*
- Not `grounded`. No measurement of an actual bureau is asserted.
- Not a claim that a group of agreeable readers corroborates the shape. Fig 2 :40 disqualifies that.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement:

1. A case log with a refused and revised category (F1).
2. Per-slot residuals published beside an aggregate and used to aim a probe (F2).
3. An independent score paid by a ground the bureau cannot move (F3).
4. An outside verifier denied the classification interiors (Fig 10 :42).
5. An instrument that halts a process when the objective stops tracking the finding (F7).
