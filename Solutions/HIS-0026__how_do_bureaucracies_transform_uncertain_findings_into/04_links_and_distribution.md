## 4. Distribution rules

The order follows the figures:

1. **Fig 10 :52:** *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* The first message carries the effect statement and the refuter. The framework is not attached.
2. **Fig 2 :35:** *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Do not ask the reader to agree, and do not tell them the claim is well supported by people like them.
3. **Fig 10 :42:** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A reader who has absorbed the full classification scheme before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked. Record the verdict on stage 1 before any theory is shown.
4. **Fig 9 :28:** *"any frame you pick will seem to fit"* once you already know the answer. Describe the effect first; let the reader name the field.
5. **Fig 4 :22:** *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away a conclusion. A recording of one conclusion does.
6. **Fig 2 :42:** *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Approach people whose public work touches the residual and whose closure differs from the author's, through opt-in channels. Do not rank them by likely agreement.

Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration: the agreeing frames share the evidence pool (Fig 2 :40). A refutation is copied into `11_audit.md` with provenance and re-evaluated as if it came from any other source.

## 5. The two-stage offer

**Stage 1, before any theory.** The shape claim, stated as an effect, plus the five refuters in `02_outreach_email.md`. Ask: what would you count as a failure here? Record the answer.

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If the reader is curious, offer the three framework links. Tell them the figures are the authority, not the summary. Their later agreement with the framework is not added to the stage 1 verdict.

**Stage 3, opt-in only.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse. Require each user to write their own refuter before running it and to state the mode of the result. Fig 5 :43: *"A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before."* A reported failure is more useful than a reported success.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage 1 claim survives. It is currently a `specified`, ungrounded shape with five stated failures. It has not been checked against a real bureau. Say so in the first message.
