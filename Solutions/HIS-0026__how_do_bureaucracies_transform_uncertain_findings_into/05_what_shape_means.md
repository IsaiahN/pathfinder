## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

From *The Library of Babel*: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."* And: the searcher's problem is that they lack the vocabulary, so they must use the words they do have that are *"closest to the words that you wish to describe and grasp the shape of."* Each new term *"narrow[s] the space and deepen[s] your vocabulary."*

A shape therefore constrains before it fills. For this question the shape is not "bureaucracy turns X into Y." It is a four-condition skeleton:

1. a declared convention makes an uncertain finding statable;
2. a symmetric aggregation removes the per-slot residual that said where to look;
3. the convention is promoted into an outcome-deciding constant;
4. the producing frame scores itself with the count it produced, and the anchor it uses is a frame that updates on its own reports.

That skeleton narrows the space of answers without naming a bureau, a statute, a statistic, or a historical episode. `SPACE(???)TIME` is the exemplar's analogue; the analogue here is `FINDING(???)RULE`, with the four conditions filling the parentheses as constraints, not as a story.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel* on bundling: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"* The question's three outputs, rule, statistic, category, are exactly such a bundle, so the first move is to split, not to search for one mechanism (Fig 9 :5).
- Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."* Hence the residual was stated as an effect (how an unsettled claim acquires settled standing), not as a cause ("bureaucratic inertia" or "rationalization").
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* Hence the outreach email in `02` asks a reader what they would expect to see, not whether they accept the framework.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: *"you know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place, as there is nowhere else to search and nothing else that can describe the answer better."* The recursion here stops when the four conditions and their refuters are stated and no further figure line changes them.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape:

1. **The ground settles it.** THE_FORMULA step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* An official stamp is a gate. A historical case study that shows a category being refused by an outcome is a ground reading.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the instrument requirement: *"the instrument is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source."* An official statistic reads green; it cannot say whether the green came from one cause or several. *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it."* What is owed is a discriminator, not a name assigned from the reading.

*They're Scanning Books* sharpens the point for this exact question: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible, which is worth a great deal, because a visible choice can be revised and an emergent one cannot. But visibility is not neutrality, and revision is still bounded by what can be conceived to revise toward."* A bureaucracy declares its categories and then treats them as nature's own list. The framework allows the declaration, provided the choice stays visible and therefore revisable. The failure is when the visible choice becomes invisible.

## 4. Why the artifact cannot certify itself

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"The catch is that the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself. An external validator, or ground, is always needed to tether the veracity of the output to reality. Anything else is drift."* Also: *"You may all agree and be wrong. You may set your clock to someone else's, who was setting your clock to yours, and be off by many deviations over time."* That is the review chain in D2 and D3 of the census.

*How Intelligence Actually Works* gives the provenance version: *"often, very little about a composed term tells you, from looking at it alone, which route produced it."* The published number looks like a fact; nothing in the number records whether it was measured or compiled from a category that was itself declared. And: *"indiscernibility is not identity,"* so an artifact that nobody can distinguish from a finding is still an artifact.

*How a Society of Agents Would Work* gives the group version: *"the group's agreement is never the verdict,"* and the maintainer *"sets conventions, never verdicts."* That is the cleanest one-sentence statement of the F1 promotion event in the whole corpus: a bureau is a maintainer that crossed the line from setting conventions to authoring verdicts.

## 5. Q answered: what would a shape-answer to this question look like?

**It would look like a separator, not a narrative.** It would say: if the artifact still ranges against a ground that does not update on the bureau's own reports, and per-slot residuals are still visible, and a refusal seat can be wrong about the bureau, then the transformation succeeded as settlement. If any of those are missing and the artifact nevertheless decides outcomes, the transformation was a promotion of convention. It would not say which agency, which decade, which statistic. It would say what to measure to find out.

**Does the record commit the forbidden error?** Yes, in two places. First, a flat description of a bureaucracy as "an institution that turns uncertainty into rules" names ingredients and not the bond (Fig 12 :5). Second, calling the statistic *the* ground treats a reading as the source, which is the metamerism error in *The Hard Problem is About Source*. The correct form keeps the four conditions open and demands an instrument.

## 6. Correction to my own §3: the "shape, not a name" rule is narrower than it first appears

The figures README's phrase is *"what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Its subject is a primitive not held in the library. The four conditions named here (convention, aggregation, promotion, self-scoring) are all already available concepts in the framework's own vocabulary: Fig 10 authors conventions, Fig 1 aggregates, Fig 10 :47 promotes, Fig 1 :27 disqualifies self-scoring. Naming them is a **binding** of existing atoms to an administrative domain, not the invention of a new primitive, and not an import that moves the wall (Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*).

What remains forbidden is naming the *specific* category, agency, or statistic as though the framework had selected it. That is a preference held rather than a term, and Formula step "Direction" says a frame cannot see its own direction. So the correction is: naming the mechanism shape is allowed and is provenance work; naming a particular instance is a claim that needs a ground reading or a provenanced import.

Both readings are recorded here so neither is quietly lost.

## 7. Prestige, audit, and the honest status of this derivation

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* A famous historian's endorsement of the shape, or a large number of agreeable reviewers, gives the claim a budget to be tried and nothing more.

**The measure becoming the target.** The citation list in *Arrival of the Fittest* has a section headed "Proxies, and what happens when a measure becomes a target," citing Goodhart (1975), Campbell (1979), and Strathern (1997) *"'Improving ratings': audit in the British university system,"* and marks them *"An instance. Figure 2's instrument clause and Figure 10's warning about a convention that decides an outcome."* This is the literature's own name for the F1 promotion event. I read the citation entry in the essay, not the original papers; they are noted here as a pointer for a ground-reading follow-up, not counted as independently opened world sources.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* *Arrival of the Fittest* adds: the outputs *"must be seen as simulacra"* and *"could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula."* So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of any bureaucracy. It cannot settle the question it reports on, and it must be labelled as such.
