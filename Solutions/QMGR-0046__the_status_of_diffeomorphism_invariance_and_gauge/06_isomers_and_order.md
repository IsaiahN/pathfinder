# Supporting derivation: bond type, dependency order, and analogues

**Mode: `specified` on the physics-facing mapping, `general` on the framework-internal structure.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The shape term says gauge equivalence is an identity over descriptions. Two supporting questions are owed: (1) is identity ≡ the right bond type, or does the question need a different one from the seven; (2) what is the dependency order, so effort is not spent on observables before the symmetry is classified. A third question asks whether the classical quantum-gravity comparison is an isomer claim under Fig 12.

## DERIVATION

### Q1. Which bond type? Falsifier included

- Operators table (local): identity ≡ means "two names, one referent," symmetric, test "substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No."
- Fig 12 :92: "Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing. Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term."
- A gauge orbit is a set of representatives, all preserving invariants. Substituting one for another loses nothing. The relation is symmetric. This is identity ≡.
- Comparison ⋛ is refused: the Operators table defines it as "a threshold relation between two quantities" and it is ordered. A symmetric equivalence forced through an ordered bond strains. Re-census keeps ≡.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a gauge-related substitution changes a measurable quantity while invariants are held fixed, the bond is not identity and the mapping fails.

### Q2. Are diffeomorphism and gauge redundancy the same kind of thing?

- Both are local symmetries in the external sources: Weinstein and Rickles say the constraints of general relativity stem from diffeomorphism invariance; Brading and Castellani treat gauge symmetry as surplus structure with equivalence classes of observables.
- In the figure vocabulary both map to the same bond: identity ≡ over descriptions. Their difference is one of domain and currency, not of bond type. D1 (classical) has invariants as currency; D2 (quantum) has gauge-invariant observables and a physical state space.
- Fig 13 :103-104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world." The two descriptions can be settled at their own levels and demoted to atoms for the union without either being a new primitive.
- **Falsifier:** if diffeomorphism invariance is shown to be a global physical symmetry relating distinct states while gauge redundancy is a mere relabelling, they are different bond types and this equivalence must be split into two domains with separate loops.

### Q3. What is the order by dependency?

- Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces."
- Correct order: (1) classify the symmetry (identity or ordered comparison); (2) fix what survives quantization (constraints, equivalence classes); (3) ask which quantities are observable; (4) only then ask what the quantum-gravity contact selects.
- Spending effort at step 3 before step 1 is the named failure: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not" (Fig 3 :19). Most foundational debate starts at observables, below the break, which is why it looks like one hard question when it is a compound.
- **Falsifier:** if a result about observables can be obtained without first classifying the symmetry, the dependency order claimed here is wrong.

### Q4. Is the classical / quantum comparison an isomer claim?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- As the two domains are written, D1 `{metric, diffeomorphism, invariants}` and D2 `{state, constraint, gauge-invariant observable}` share almost no atoms. They are not isomers under Fig 12; they share at most a functional pattern, an identity bond over descriptions at different levels.
- The stronger version: if both are written over the full shared structural set, differing only in which state the identity bond mediates, they are isomers. That is the claim worth testing, and it needs an instrument to decide, not an argument.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either domain requires an atom the other does not even after the shared-set rewriting, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands.

### Q5. Why the union is second order and still open

- D1 and D2 are each settled against their own ground, so by Fig 13 :103-104 each becomes an atom for the next level. Their comparison is a second-order composition.
- Fig 8 :45-46: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone. Composition crosses the boundary between them, and that surplus is where imports live." The surplus here is the shared identity-bond pattern, not a new primitive.
- D3 has no anchor (Fig 2 :29), so no second-order verdict is available. The union stays a compound, unbundled before searching (Fig 9 :54).
- **Falsifier:** a measurement at the Planck scale that fixes the physical Hilbert space would close D3 and convert the second-order comparison from `candidate` to `grounded`.

## CONCLUSION

Identity ≡ is the right bond type; ⋛ is refused by strain. Diffeomorphism invariance and gauge redundancy are the same bond kind at two levels, not two different physical symmetries. The dependency order is symmetry, then quantization, then observables. The two domains are not isomers as currently written, but a shared-set isomer claim is available with a stated falsifier. The union is second order and stays open at the ground.
