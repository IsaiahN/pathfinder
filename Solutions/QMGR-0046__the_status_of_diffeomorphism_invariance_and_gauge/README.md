## What an answer would look like (bound as an ordinary term)

The sought-for shape is written down before the run so the loop can refuse it: **a gauge symmetry is an identity relation over descriptions, many names for one referent; the physical content is the referent, that is the invariant or relational quantity; a gauge choice is a convention and may not decide an outcome.** That sentence is a `specified` term, not a verdict. `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` writes three refuters for it before the conclusion, and `06_isomers_and_order.md` gives the supporting derivation with its own falsifier.

Option A in effect terms: treat diffeomorphism-related configurations as physically distinct states. This counts spellings as terms and lets a convention decide an outcome. Option B: treat them as one state under many descriptions. This keeps only invariant or relational quantities as admissible observables and stops at the instrument limit. What each leads to is recorded per domain, never averaged.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the framework's own words:

1. **A room is not an anchor.** Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." A list chosen for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation (Fig 2 :40).
2. **A verifier must not hold the interiors.** Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Sending the full architecture before asking for a verdict spends the reader's independence.
3. **Prestige is not action.** *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (essay, fetched 2026-11-23): "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one.
