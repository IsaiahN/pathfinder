**Subject:** One checkable claim about gauge redundancy, before any theory

> Hello,
>
> I have one specific output about the status of diffeomorphism invariance and gauge redundancy in a quantum setting. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive your check, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a gauge symmetry is an identity relation over descriptions, many names for one referent. The physical content is the referent, the invariant or relational quantity. A gauge choice is a convention and may not decide an outcome. In the framework's terms, this maps to its symmetric identity bond, its rule that two frames with complete overlap are one frame wearing two names, and its rule that a convention which decides an outcome has stopped being a convention. Diffeomorphism-related configurations are therefore not two physical states; counting them as two is counting spellings as terms.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the figures select a gauge group, a quantization procedure, or an observable algebra. They return a shape, not a name. Any such selection is a binding from the physics frame, provenanced as such, and carries zero evidentiary weight until a ground pays for it. I am also not claiming this settles quantum gravity. The quantum gravity contact has no instrument, so the specific question stops as unreached, not answered.
>
> **Three things written down before this message, any one of which refutes the output:**
> 1. diffeomorphism invariance or gauge redundancy is derived as one of the six primitives, or is neither derived nor an equivalence over descriptions;
> 2. a measurement whose outcome changes under a pure gauge transformation while all invariants are held fixed;
> 3. two diffeomorphism-related configurations shown to be physically distinct, that is, two names with two referents.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - The Hole Argument, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/spacetime-holearg/
> - Quantum Gravity, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/quantum-gravity/
> - Symmetry and Symmetry Breaking, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/symmetry-breaking/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the framework's public figures folder. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thank you for your time.
