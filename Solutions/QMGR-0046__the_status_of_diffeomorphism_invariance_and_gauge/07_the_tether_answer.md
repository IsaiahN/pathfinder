# The Tether's answer on diffeomorphism invariance and gauge redundancy

**Mode: `specified` on the shape and its per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural limits. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A transformation exists that leaves the physical situation unchanged: diffeomorphisms in classical gravity, local gauge transformations in quantum theory. The unresolved point is its **status**: a real symmetry between distinct states, or a redundancy in the description of one state? At the quantum level the same issue decides which states and operators are physical, and whether a gauge choice may enter a prediction.

It is a **union compound**, not one hard question (Fig 9 :54-58: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either). The classical frame and the quantum gauge frame have their own anchors; their union at quantum gravity has none. Verdicts are kept separate and never averaged.

## 2. The shape, per domain

1. **Symmetry is derived, not primitive.** Fig 13 :124-125: "Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are each what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member." Diffeomorphism invariance is a symmetry, hence a derived quantity. Status: derivative, `general`.
2. **Gauge redundancy is identity, not physical distinctness.** Fig 12 :65-69: "≡ identity two names, one referent yes substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No." And Fig 12 :92-93: "Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term." The orbit is the spellings; the physical state is the term. Counting orbit members as separate states overcharges by log₂(k!). Status: `specified`, triangulated across two passages of Fig 12.
3. **Overlapping descriptions are one frame.** Fig 8 :48-49: "Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names." Fig 2 :19-21: "they never talk, and still agree so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other." Diffeomorphism-related configurations share every invariant, so treating them as two witnesses double-counts one frame. Status: `specified`, triangulated across Fig 8 and Fig 2.
4. **A convention may not decide an outcome.** Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." Fig 10 :49: "Declaring a meaning does not ground it." Fig 9 :51 admits only residual-bearing evidence to the verifier. Status: `specified`, triangulated. Only invariant or relational quantities are admissible as observables.
5. **Irreducibility is level-relative.** Fig 13 :103-104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world." What counts as a physical degree of freedom can change between the classical and quantum levels. The figures do not fix a once-and-for-all list. Status: `general`, single-source.
6. **Quantization adds no atom.** Fig 6 :25: "An import adds no atom to the world." Passing to a quantum description changes access, not the world's primitives. Status: `general`, single-source.
7. **The framework returns a shape, not a name.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." It does not select a gauge group, a quantization, or an observable algebra. Status: `general`, triangulated with the local canonical README.
8. **The quantum-gravity contact has no ground.** Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." D3 is **unreached, not refuted**. Status: `gap`.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that gauge symmetry is physically unreal. The referent (the equivalence class, the invariant content) is physical; only the extra names are redundant.
- Not a derivation of a specific group, constraint algorithm, or physical Hilbert space. Those are bindings from the physics frame and carry zero figure weight until a ground pays for them.
- Not that diffeomorphism invariance is emergent or fundamental. The figures only say its status is derivative and level-relative; which level hosts it is a physics question.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement at the Planck scale settles `left(R,φ)`.
- Not that the three domains can be merged into one verdict. They have different anchors.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument or a proof, not more agreement. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (essay, fetched 2026-11-23): "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information," and the case was settled "not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." Here the corresponding questions are:

- **Referent test:** can an instrument distinguish two gauge-related descriptions by any invariant? If yes, the identity mapping breaks.
- **Convention test:** does any prediction change under a pure gauge change while invariants are held fixed? If yes, F4 breaks.
- **Primitive test:** can diffeomorphism invariance be derived as one of the six, or shown to be neither derived nor relational? If yes, F1 breaks.
- **D3 test:** a measurement that selects a physical Hilbert space and fixes the observable algebra would move the union from `candidate` to `grounded`.

Standing refuters are in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 5, written before the conclusion.

## 5. The honest stop

Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." The six-term set is a visible guess at compression axes, not nature's own inventory. Declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral. Indiscernibility is not identity, and silence at D3 is not a negative answer.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not pick a winner between quantum gravity programs. It says that symmetry is a derived quantity, not a primitive; that gauge equivalence has the shape of an identity bond, many names for one referent, so counting gauge-related configurations as distinct physical states is counting spellings as terms; that two descriptions with the same invariants are one frame wearing two names, not two witnesses; and that a gauge choice is a convention that may not decide an outcome, so only invariant or relational quantities are admissible as observables. What counts as irreducible can change between the classical and quantum levels, and quantization adds no new physical primitive. The framework returns this shape, not a name: it cannot select a gauge group, a quantization, or an observable algebra, and the quantum-gravity contact has no instrument, so that part stops as unreached rather than answered. What it offers is the shape a candidate answer must have, three refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it.
