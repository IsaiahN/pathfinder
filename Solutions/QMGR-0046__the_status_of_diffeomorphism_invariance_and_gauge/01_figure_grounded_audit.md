## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

The question asks for the **status** of a symmetry that maps distinct-looking descriptions into one. In effect terms: when a transformation leaves the physical situation unchanged, is the transformation a physical operation relating two different states, or is it a redundancy in the way one state is described? At the quantum level the same issue becomes: which states and which operators count as physical, and may a gauge choice enter a prediction?

Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term before running the loop (formula step "Direction"):

> **SHAPE TERM (bound before the run).** A gauge symmetry is an identity relation over descriptions, many names for one referent. The physical content is the referent, that is the invariant or relational quantity. A gauge choice is a convention and may not decide an outcome.

This is a candidate term with a stated refuter, not a verdict.

- **Option A:** diffeomorphism-related configurations are physically distinct; gauge symmetry is a real symmetry between different states. Leads to counting gauge orbit members as separate physical states and to allowing a convention to carry physical weight.
- **Option B:** diffeomorphism-related configurations are one state under many descriptions; only invariant or relational quantities are physical. Leads to a many-to-one map, an equivalence class as the referent, and a stopped slot at the instrument limit.

What an answer would look like: a per-domain verdict on which option holds, the figure lines that decide it, the refuters that would break it, and an honest statement of what no available instrument can settle.

## 3. Findings F1 to F8

### F1. Symmetry is derived, not primitive (single-source)

Fig 13 :124-125: "Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are each what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member."

Diffeomorphism invariance is a symmetry. Under this line it is not a member of the six, not a primitive, and not something declared at the bottom of the theory. Its status is derivative: it is what a particular composition leaves behind.

**Refuter:** derive diffeomorphism invariance (or gauge redundancy) as one of the six primitives, or show it is neither derived nor an equivalence over descriptions. If that holds, F1 fails.

**Status:** single-source (Fig 13 only). `general` structural mode.

### F2. Gauge redundancy is the identity bond (triangulated)

Fig 12 :65-69: "≡ identity two names, one referent yes substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No."

Fig 12 :92-93: "Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term, so a code that charges for the sequence overcharges by log₂(k!) while the bond term undercharges."

A gauge orbit is the set of spellings. The physical state is the one term. Substituting one representative for another loses nothing, which is exactly the identity bond's test. Counting orbit members as distinct states is the overcharge the figure names.

**Refuter:** exhibit a gauge-related pair for which substitution loses something measurable, that is, a gauge-dependent observable. Then the two names have two referents and the identity mapping is false.

**Status:** triangulated across two separated passages of Fig 12 (bond definition and pricing rule), low internal overlap. `specified`.

### F3. Two overlapping descriptions are one frame wearing two names (triangulated)

Fig 8 :48-49: "Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."

Fig 2 :19-21: "they never talk, and still agree so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other."

Two descriptions that draw on the same evidence are not two independent witnesses. Diffeomorphism-related configurations share every invariant (the external source in section 4 confirms this), so their agreement is the pool talking. Treating them as two physical states double-counts one frame.

**Refuter:** show that two diffeomorphism-related configurations disagree on an invariant, or that their agreement carries information beyond the shared structure. Then they are two frames, not one.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 8 (frame overlap) and Fig 2 (evidence pool), two different mechanisms. `specified`.

### F4. A convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one (triangulated)

Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."

Fig 10 :49: "Declaring a meaning does not ground it."

Fig 9 :51: "what the verifier may see: evidence bearing on the residual is admissible; evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel, and the verifier is denied it."

A gauge choice is a convention, not a physical fact. If a gauge-dependent quantity enters a prediction, the convention has decided an outcome and has ceased to be a convention. Only residual-bearing, that is invariant or relational, quantities are admissible as observables. The third line is an analogue, not a physics claim: it governs what a verifier may see, and the mapping to gauge invariance is a `specified` extension, marked as such.

**Refuter:** produce a measurement whose outcome changes under a pure gauge transformation while all invariants are held fixed. Then the convention does decide an outcome and F4 is false.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 10 and Fig 9. `specified`.

### F5. Irreducibility is level-relative (single-source)

Fig 13 :103-104: "Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible. A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."

What counts as a physical degree of freedom is a fact about a level. A quantity that is redundant at the classical level need not play the same role at the quantum level, and conversely. The framework does not fix a once-and-for-all answer.

**Refuter:** show that the set of physical degrees of freedom is invariant across the classical and quantum levels for every system, so that no demotion or promotion occurs. That would break the level rule.

**Status:** single-source (Fig 13). `general`.

### F6. Quantization adds no atom (single-source)

Fig 6 :25: "An import adds no atom to the world."

Passing to a quantum description changes the frame's access, not the world's primitives. Diffeomorphism invariance and gauge redundancy are not newly created physical ingredients at quantization; they are structural features of the description.

**Refuter:** exhibit a physical degree of freedom that comes into existence only upon quantization and is not a change of representational access. Then an atom was added.

**Status:** single-source (Fig 6). `general`.

### F7. The framework returns a shape, not a name (triangulated with local canonical rule)

The kind of thing here is an identity structure over descriptions. The framework does not name a specific gauge group, a specific quantization procedure, or a specific physical Hilbert space. Those are bindings from the physics frame, provenanced as such. The canonical figures README (local) states the limit: "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."

**Refuter:** show that the figures select a unique quantization or a unique set of relational observables from the shape alone. Nothing in the census does, so a claim of uniqueness would have to import an atom with no source gate.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 9 and the local canonical README (different documents). `general`.
