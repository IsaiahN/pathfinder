# The Tether's answer: improvement versus radical innovation

**Mode: `specified` on the firm-level claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** A shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A firm has one budget and two slots: the existing product with a known paying market, and a radical opportunity with no settled market. The question asks which to fund. The figures refuse the framing. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* This is a union compound with its own loop, not one hard allocation problem (Fig 9 :54). The verdicts for each loop stay separate and are never averaged.

## 2. One loop per domain (verdicts kept separate)

### D1, the product-improvement loop: licensed as inward mint

Fig 6 :29: *"inward: is our representation adequate to hold it? The instrument extends access. Fails by refining forever on an axis that cannot carry it. Sharpen in our own terms."*
Fig 6 :49 (partial): *"more search at the same reach: is it in here and unfound? The closure does not change. Same atoms, same wall, more of the space actually visited."*

Improving the product is the right operator when the live residual is expressible in the firm's current atoms and the instrument can resolve it. It fails by refining forever on an axis that cannot carry the signal, or by spending budget on a region already swept (Fig 6 :50). **Status: `general`, triangulated** (Fig 6 inward instrument and Fig 3 dependency order). It is not the whole answer and it cannot produce a radical atom (Fig 6 :21).

### D2, the radical-innovation loop: licensed as outward import

Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*
Fig 6 :30: *"outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."*
Fig 8 :53: *"Residual first, frame second."*

Radical innovation is the right operator when the live residual cannot be held by the current representation. The firm characterises R in effect terms first, then finds the frame whose closure predicts its shape. It fails by sharpening the description into terms only the firm can read (Fig 6 :30) or by picking the frame first and mapping afterwards (Fig 9 :27-28). It carries a **source gate and a shadow test** (Fig 8 :12-13) and a debit against the imported frame's independence (Fig 6 :24). **Status: `general`, triangulated** (Fig 6 outward instrument, Fig 8 union surplus, Fig 13 :115).

### D3, the allocation and review frame: authors conventions, not verdicts

Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*
Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

The senior team's legitimate output is a convention: a spend cap per track, a review cadence, a decay rule so failed bets can be retested, and independence bought in proportion to the cost of error. ROI survives only as a **filter for the budget**. The moment the convention decides which bet is good, it stops being a convention (Fig 10 :48). **Status: `specified`, triangulated** (Fig 10 seat rules, Fig 9 filter rule, Fig 2 independence rule).

### D4, the habitat and contact channel: the test for "improvement"

Fig 11 :40: *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*
Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*

The discriminator between real improvement and polishing is **contact with a paying ground**, not effort or spend. A dashboard can stay healthy while the channel decays, because the ground itself does not decay. A sandbox test is a substituted habitat and proves wiring, never capability (Fig 11 :70). **Status: `specified`, triangulated** (Fig 11 capability claim and Fig 10 channel rule).

### The name of the winner: `gap`

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*
Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

After a genuine re-census, no figure line yields a winning technology, a universal budget ratio, or a ranking of radical bets. The framework narrows the shape of the decision procedure and reports the destination as unreachable. Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence (Fig 13 :134). **Status: `general`, `gap`.**

## 3. The one rule that follows

Run the two tracks separately, never read one against the other (Fig 6 :31), and never sum them into one score (Fig 12 :125). Fund inward mint while the current slot's residual is resolvable in the firm's own terms. Fund outward import when a live slot residual persists and the current closure cannot hold it. Author the allocation convention at senior level, keep the per-slot signal visible, buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong, and let a paying ground settle each bet separately. What a radical unit teaches is carried back as a method, not as a recording (Fig 4 :22).

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a universal split. A fixed 70/20/10 ratio is a preference held as a term, predicting nothing (Fig 5 :62-63).
- Not a named winner. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not "more R&D spend means more exploration." Activity is not success (Fig 5 :48).
- Not that ROI is useless. It is a budget filter, not a witness (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that existing customers should choose the radical direction. They are the current frame and a mirror (Fig 2 :35).
- Not grounded. No firm has been measured in this derivation.

## 5. What would settle it, and the honest stop

A ground reading, not agreement. Instruments that would close the shape:

1. A per-slot residual log showing the radical signal is carried by one small slot and disappears from the portfolio average.
2. A paying experiment in which an imported atom from a differing closure wins where no amount of inward mint did, with provenance and a shadow test recorded.
3. A contact test: an improvement that pays only when it changes contact, and fails to pay when it does not (Fig 11 :40).
4. A decayed-verdict test: a bet previously refuted that pays when re-tested after the ground changed (Fig 6 :42-46).

The stop: the shape survives as `specified`; the name of the winner is `gap`. The firm's technology frame is *"a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes"* and declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral. The derivation stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a firm a number to split its R&D budget. It says the question is bundled: improving a product and pursuing a radical innovation are two different operators, not two points on one scale. Improvement is composition inside the firm's current frame, a new molecule from atoms it already holds, which can never add an atom. Radical innovation is an import, an atom added on contact with a frame whose closure differs. They must be run as two tracks and never read against one another, and no single ROI score can compare them because the two costs are in different units that do not add. ROI is a filter for the budget, never a witness for the verdict. The live radical signal sits in one small slot and vanishes into a portfolio average, and an improvement that changes no contact changes nothing. The senior team authors the allocation convention, buys independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong, and lets a paying ground settle each bet. Which technology wins is a declared gap, not a hidden answer. The shape, the refuters written in advance and the instruments that would close it are all this package offers.
