# Structure, order and analogues in the allocation question

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks for a choice rule, but a rule is a compound of structure (which operators exist and how they bond) and order (what depends on what, and what crosses a scale). Two questions are owed: (1) are improvement and radical innovation **isomers** under Fig 12, and (2) does the dependency order explain why a firm's improvement effort fails at the radical slot?

## DOMAIN CENSUS (inherited from `01`, not re-derived)

Atoms held: the firm's current product frame Γ, its budget, its existing market, the seven bonds and negation. Operators held: mint (composition inside closure) and import (atom from a differing closure). No third operator is available without import. D1 inward, D2 outward, D3 allocator, D4 habitat; union, not partition.

## Q1 - Structure: is this an isomer claim?

Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."*
Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."*
Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."*

A budget ratio is a state description. It names how much is spent, not the route by which a bet pays. Fig 12 :111-112 says a vocabulary holding only quantities can say what a situation contains and cannot say what would count as getting somewhere from it. So the answer cannot be a single number; it must be an arrangement of bonds.

Under Fig 12's definition, the two tracks are **not isomers** as usually written, because they do not share one atom set at the same level. Improvement bonds `{current frame, metric, existing market}`; radical innovation bonds `{external frame, imported atom, prospective market}`. They differ in atoms, not only in bond, so they are two different substances, which is what F1 concludes by a different route.

The stronger isomer reading is available only at a higher level: if both are written over the firm's full capability set `{frame, metric, market, budget, ground, habitat}` and differ only in which operator (mint vs import) and which market (existing vs prospective) occupies the slots, then they are *"same atoms, different bond, different substance"*. This is a checkable structural claim, not a metaphor. It is `specified`, ungrounded.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if either track can be written without an atom the other requires (for example, radical innovation with no external frame, or improvement with no paying market), the isomer reading fails and the weaker two-substances reading stands.

## Q2 - Order: what depends on what?

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*
Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*
Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
Fig 3 :29-30: *"Most improvement effort in practice lands above the break, because that is where the measurements already exist."*

The ordered chain for a firm is: perception of a live slot residual, vocabulary for what that residual affords, an objective stated in that track's own terms, a plan that routes inward or outward, and learn-and-carry. The firm's characteristic failure is at the middle link (vocabulary to objective), because that link has no instrument. Effort lands above the break where the metrics exist, which is why better product analytics cannot rescue a missing radical objective (Fig 3 :19). This is dependency order, not a ranking of importance. **Falsifier:** a firm whose radical failure is removed by adding more product-analytics capability while the vocabulary/objective link stays uninstrumented.

## Q3 - What crosses the membrane?

Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*
Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

When a radical bet succeeds, what may move back into the core business is the **method** (how to scout, how to source an external frame, how to shadow-test), never the recording of the particular win. Replaying the recording produces a firm that repeats one success and cannot produce a new one (Fig 4 :25). The lossy transform between the experimental unit and the core must be bracketed with a measured round trip R_T, otherwise the firm navigates by dead reckoning. **Falsifier:** a successful radical method transplanted wholesale into the core with zero loss and zero round-trip gap.

## Analogues (secondary, each with its own overlap declared)

### A1 - Mathematics / operations research: the multi-armed bandit

Daniel J. Russo, Benjamin Van Roy, Abbas Kazerouni, Ian Osband and Zheng Wen, *A Tutorial on Thompson Sampling*, Foundations and Trends in Machine Learning 11(1), 2018: *"actions are taken sequentially in a manner that must balance between exploiting what is known to maximize immediate performance and investing to accumulate new information that may improve future performance."*

This is the closest formal analogue and it **conflicts** if applied to the whole question. The bandit holds the arm set fixed: *"The success probabilities... are unknown, but fixed over time, and therefore can be learned by experimentation."* A firm's radical innovation changes the arm set by import, which the bandit frame cannot represent. Returned to census: the bandit governs D1 (inward search at a fixed reach, Fig 6 :49) and is silent on D2. It corroborates the inward half of F2 and conflicts with any claim that the two tracks are one optimisation. It is one witness from mathematics, not two.

### A2 - Animal behaviour and cognition: leave the patch, and occasionally make a large move

Thomas T. Hills and colleagues, *Exploration versus exploitation in space, mind, and society*, Trends in Cognitive Sciences 19(1), 2015: *"numerous species focus on finding resources in a cluster (or patch) until their acquisition rate falls below some threshold, as described by the marginal value theorem... At that point, organisms typically leave to seek a new patch."* And: *"gradient-following rules may then fail by becoming trapped on inferior local maxima far from global peaks of resource abundance. Making occasional large movements independent of recent experience may then be beneficial."*

This predicts a **switch rule by diminishing local return**, and a **large-move rule when local gradients saturate**. It corroborates the inward/outward split at the level of mechanism (low overlap: animal foraging and cognitive science, not business). **Conflict check:** it describes a balance within one search space, so like A1 it corroborates the shape, not the import operator. No verdict change.

### A3 - History and philosophy of science: normal and revolutionary phases

*Scientific Revolutions*, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, 2009, revised 2017: *"Normal science is convergent rather than divergent: it actively discourages revolutionary initiatives and essentially novel (unexpected) discoveries, for these threaten the paradigm."* And: *"If and when persistent efforts by the best researchers fail to resolve the anomalies, the community begins to lose confidence in the paradigm and a crisis period ensues in which serious alternatives can now be entertained."*

This corroborates Fig 3 :29-30 (effort lands where measurements exist) and Fig 7 :36 (a chain of mirrors is one long room). It also supplies the switch condition in effect terms: persistent anomaly that the current frame cannot resolve. Low overlap with business, so a genuine secondary witness. **Conflict check:** Kuhn's incommensurability thesis is stronger than the figures need, which require only that closures differ; the excess is not imported.

### A4 - Animal behaviour: specialisation reduces flexibility

Tanya Latty and Madeleine Beekman, *Keeping track of changes: the performance of ant colonies in dynamic environments*, Animal Behaviour 85(3), 2013: *"evidence for a trade-off between exploitation and flexibility such that species that tended to focus strongly on high-quality resources had relatively poor dynamic performance."* Corroborates F5 at the colony level. Abstract read via search index, full text not opened; weight is correspondingly low.

### A5 - Business: structural ambidexterity

Charles A. O'Reilly III and Michael L. Tushman, *The Ambidextrous Organization*, HBR 2004, and the HBS Online overview, 2024: *"By separating out the more exploratory ventures from the core businesses, the former can really investigate new technologies, new markets."* Also: *"If you don't have senior leaders who are visibly supportive of these businesses, then the short term will kill the long term."*

This corroborates the operational form of F2 and F6 (separate tracks with senior integration), but shares the business evidence pool with March and Christensen, so it is one witness, not three. It supplies a practical convention, not a figure-governed verdict.

## CONCLUSION

The two tracks are **two different operators, not a ratio and not isomers at the same level**. Their structure is mint versus import, their order follows the dependency chain in Fig 3, and what crosses back from the radical unit is a method, never a recording (Fig 4 :22). The bandit analogue governs only the inward half, because it fixes the arm set; this conflict was returned to census and did not change the verdict. The switch from inward to outward is licensed by a persistent live-slot residual that the current closure cannot resolve, corroborated by the foraging and Kuhnian analogues.

**Falsifier for the whole derivation:** a demonstration that an arm-set-fixed optimiser (A1) predicts a firm's radical breakthrough without any import from a differing closure. That would dissolve the mint/import distinction and collapse F1, F2 and F3 into one decision. Until it fires, the two-track shape stands as `specified`, ungrounded.
