# Outreach draft: check one output before deciding about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until a reader can see the residual and the refuter without first seeing the theory. The two-stage offer collapses on contact if the theory arrives first (Fig 10 :42).

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about R&D allocation, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about R&D strategy. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (in effect terms):** a firm has one budget and two slots. Slot I is the existing product, with a known market and existing instruments. Slot R is a radical opportunity, with an unknown market and no settled instrument. The claim concerns what each option does to the firm's reach, not which one is more profitable this quarter.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** these are two different operators and they must be run as two tracks that are never read against one another (Fig 6 :31). Improving the product is composition inside the firm's current closure: a new molecule from atoms it already holds, which can never add an atom (Fig 6 :21). A radical innovation is an import: an atom added on contact with a frame whose closure differs (Fig 6 :23). A single ROI score cannot compare them, because a composition costs in two currencies that do not add (Fig 12 :125). ROI is a filter for the budget, never a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).
>
> The live radical signal sits in one small slot. The global residual can be near zero with one slot live and that is a legal state, so a portfolio average hides the only signal that says where to look (Fig 1 :19, :22). And an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves (Fig 11 :40).
>
> **What I am not claiming:** I am not claiming a universal split such as 70/20/10, not naming a winning technology, and not claiming that more R&D spend produces more exploration. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Which bet wins is a declared gap, not a hidden answer.
>
> **What would refute the shape (written before this message):**
> 1. a radical atom derived entirely inside the current product closure, with no imported atom;
> 2. a single currency in which one score validly predicts both tracks on a paying ground;
> 3. the radical signal shown to be carried by the aggregate and to vanish per slot;
> 4. an agent-only improvement shown to raise capability while changing no contact;
> 5. a convention shown to settle an outcome while still checkable by an unmoved ground;
> 6. two low-overlap frames agreeing on a bet and predicting the market without either ranging against a paying ground.
>
> If any one holds, I retract the shape. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figures text with line numbers: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in the local repo extract (public GitHub branch `general-agent`, folder `figures/`).
> - Figures README, canonical usage rule: same repo, `figures/README.md`.
> - Worked architecture article: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> [your name]

| Residual and refuter before theory | Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* |

| ROI as filter, not witness | Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* |

| Offer the formula as a method to reuse | Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* |

## Two-stage rule for replies

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual, the shape claim, and the six refuters. Ask what the reader would count as a failure. Record their verdict **before** showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the three links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*).

One-to-one only. No bulk send, no opt-in assumed from silence, and an opt-out line in every message.
