## 0. The residual in effect terms

The firm faces one budget and two slots. Slot I is the existing product: a known frame, existing customers, existing instruments, near returns. Slot R is the radical opportunity: an unknown frame, maybe no market yet, distant and uncertain returns. The question asks which to fund.

Stated as R = |Γ(b,a) − o′| per slot s (Formula step 1): each R&D bet is an action a taken from the firm's library Γ against a belief b, and the market returns an observation o′. The gap is **indexed by slot and never aggregated**. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

**What an answer would look like (bound as an ordinary term before the run).** A rule with three parts: (i) what each option does to the firm's reach, (ii) the condition under which each is licensed, (iii) a refuter that would break the rule. Binding this shape as a term lets it accrue a residual like anything else, so the preference is refusable (Formula, "Direction"). The rule we will test is: **two loops with separate grounds, never one averaged score**. The allocation of budget between them is a *convention*, not a verdict.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.**

- **Arity.** The decision is a dyadic relation between a frame (the firm's Γ and its budget) and an action (fund slot I or slot R), indexed by slot. A third term, the allocator, authors conventions over both. There is no monadic "innovation" quantity to maximise.
- **Symmetry.** I and R are **not symmetric under option-swap**. Fundamental search inside the product's closure is repeatable and cheap; a radical atom is not reachable by that search at all (Fig 6 :21, Fig 8 :32). Their grounds differ: I ranges against an existing paying market; R ranges against a prospective ground the firm must find or build. Under a swap the currency and the anchor both change, so a single symmetric comparison is a type error.
- **Scale.** I operates within one frame (local, near-term, one crossing of a known membrane). R operates across frames (a new closure, distant, long-term). Fig 4 governs the lossy transform between them.

**One line per domain (actors / currency / anchor). No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.**

| id | domain | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | Product-improvement loop (inward) | engineers, existing customers, current metrics, routines | actions spent reducing R on the current slot; bits to describe the new design plus actions to verify | the existing paying market: customers, revenue, defect counts, benchmarks (Fig 2 :29 names "a counter, a market") | admitted |
| D2 | Radical-innovation loop (outward) | scouts, external frames (research, start-ups, adjacent fields, lead users, nonconsumers), importing seat | imported atoms, provenance, shadow test, independence | a prospective paying ground, nameable as a *kind* ("a market that pays or does not") but not yet instantiated until built | admitted with caveat |
| D3 | Allocation and review frame | senior management team acting as ground-maintainer seat, budget, review cadence, decay rules | budget as contact cost; conventions; independence bought in proportion to cost of being wrong | the market(s), never internal agreement; the seat authors conventions, not verdicts | admitted |
| D4 | Habitat and contact channel | customers, entrants, suppliers, regulators, adjacent disciplines; conditions and relations | contact and cascades | the ground, which does not decay; the channel, which does (Fig 10 :24) | admitted |
| X1 | "Visionary leader" as free-floating cause | the leader | talent | none nameable apart from the habitat | **not a domain yet** (Fig 11 :39-40) |
| X2 | A universal optimal R&D ratio (such as a fixed 70/20/10 split) | none | a number | none; no ground settles a universal ratio | **refused: a preference held as a term** (Fig 5 :62-63) |

**Partition or union?** Union. I and R share the budget and the senior team, so they overlap rather than partition. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* A single lever (a budget shift) clears the region where only one condition binds and leaves the intersection standing (Fig 9 :56-57). Each loop keeps its own verdict; they are never averaged.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** 1 perception: is a live slot residual arriving? 2 vocabulary: do we have terms for what it affords? 3 objective: can we say what counts as progress in each track? 4 planning: route inward or outward. 5 learn and carry: name what worked, verify, carry as a method. The chain is ordered by dependency, not importance (Fig 3 :20). Most firm effort lands above the break because that is where the measurements already exist (Fig 3 :29-30), which is exactly why the middle link, having terms for what a radical situation affords, has no instrument (Fig 3 :27).

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The two options are different operators, not two points on one scale

Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*
Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*
Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."*
Fig 13 :115: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."*

Improving an existing product is **mint inside closure(Γ)**: a new design is a molecule composed from atoms the firm already holds, and more of it visits more of the same space (Fig 6 :49, *"more search at the same reach: is it in here and unfound? The closure does not change. Same atoms, same wall, more of the space actually visited."*). A radical innovation is **import**: it adds an atom from a frame whose closure differs and moves the wall (Fig 6 :14, *"the only operator that moves the wall"*). No amount of the first produces the second.

**Verdict: `general`, triangulated** (Fig 6 closure rules, Fig 8 idempotence proof, Fig 13 minimum set: three figures with low conceptual overlap). **Refuter:** show a radical atom derived entirely inside the current product closure with no contact with a differing frame.

### F2 - The two tracks must be run separately and never read against one another

Fig 6 :29: *"inward: is our representation adequate to hold it? The instrument extends access. Fails by refining forever on an axis that cannot carry it. Sharpen in our own terms."*
Fig 6 :30: *"outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."*
Fig 6 :31: *"Neither answer tells you much about the other. Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other."*

This is the direct figure answer to "how should a firm choose": inward improvement and outward search are **opposed instruments**, not competing candidates under one criterion. Reading one against the other is the error the figure forbids. The practical form is structural separation with senior integration (corroborated in the world by O'Reilly and Tushman, see `09_watson_answer.md` WORLD), but the verdict comes from Fig 6.

**Verdict: `general`, triangulated** (Fig 6 :29-31 and Fig 4 :22, *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*). **Refuter:** a single measure that validly ranks both an inward improvement and an outward import on the same ground.

### F3 - A single ROI score is the named two-currency error

Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*
Fig 12 :126: *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them"*
Fig 12 :130: *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it. A price in bits and a price in actions are two readings, held apart."*
Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

Firms compare a known product improvement and a radical bet with the same discounted-cash-flow or ROI number. The figure says the two readings are in different units and do not add. The financial number is a **filter for the budget** and may allocate a cap on spend; it cannot be a **witness for the verdict** on whether the radical frame predicts a real ground.

**Verdict: `general`, triangulated** (Fig 12 pricing rule and Fig 9 :60 filter rule). **Refuter:** a demonstrated commensurable unit in which both tracks' residuals predict the same paying ground.

### F4 - The live signal is per slot; the aggregate hides the disruption

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
Fig 1 :20 (partial): *"Support is satisfied when some slot carries positive mass, so a global residual near zero with one live slot is a legal state"*

An incumbent's overall metrics look healthy while one small slot (a low-end or new-market foothold) carries the entire live signal. The global R near zero with one live slot is a **legal state**, not an inert one. Reporting only the portfolio average throws away the only signal that says where to look. March (1991) and Christensen, Raynor and McDonald (2015), read as secondary sources, independently describe the same asymmetry; they corroborate but do not decide.

**Verdict: `specified`, triangulated** (Fig 1 indexing and the dependency of support on one slot). **Refuter:** a case where the radical signal is carried by the aggregate and disappears under per-slot decomposition.

### F5 - Improvement that changes no contact changes nothing

Fig 11 :40: *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*
Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*
Fig 11 :70: *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."*

The test for whether "improving the existing product" counts is **contact**, not effort. An improvement that reaches a new customer, changes a use case, or alters the ground pays; a polish that only deepens the same frame does not, however much it improves the artifact. Fig 10 :24 shows why a healthy-looking internal dashboard can coexist with a dead channel: the ground does not decay, the channel does. A sandbox test of a radical idea proves wiring, never capability, because it is a substituted habitat (Fig 11 :66-70).

**Verdict: `specified`, triangulated** (Fig 11 capability claim and Fig 10 channel rule). **Refuter:** a firm made more capable by an agent-only improvement that demonstrably changed no contact with any paying ground.

### F6 - The senior team authors conventions, never verdicts

Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*
Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

The allocator's legitimate output is a **convention**: how much to cap each track, how often to review, how verdicts decay so failed ideas can be retested, and how much independence to buy for high-cost bets. The moment the allocation rule itself decides which bet is good, it has become a constant carrying the team's authority (Fig 10 :48). This is why the two-stage outreach in `02` sends the residual and refuter before any theory: a reader who has absorbed the frame cannot check it (Fig 10 :42, *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*).

**Verdict: `specified`, triangulated** (Fig 10 seat rules, Fig 9 filter rule, Fig 2 independence rule). **Refuter:** a convention that settles an outcome while remaining checkable by a ground that cannot be moved.

### F7 - Activity in exploration is not evidence of exploration succeeding

Fig 5 :48: *"Its own output is never a measure of success. A system can be enthusiastically curious and produce nothing."*
Fig 5 :41: *"Any one of them at zero stops the mint entirely. Which is why a system that is minting nothing may have nothing wrong with its minting."*
Fig 7 :13: *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*

Counting experiments, ideas generated, or innovation-lab headcount measures machinery firing, not a mint that paid. Fig 5 :41 is the mirror case: a firm improving nothing may have a perfectly healthy improvement process whose novelty guard is simply at zero (Fig 6 :22, *"NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world."*). Neither count is a verdict.

**Verdict: `general`, single-source for the guard structure (Fig 5), triangulated with Fig 7 on agreement versus evidence. Refuter:** an activity metric that predicts a ground payment without ranging against that ground.

### F8 - Describe the residual before naming the frame

Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*
Fig 8 :53: *"Residual first, frame second."*
Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*

A firm that first decides "we will bet on AI" and then maps its residual onto AI has performed echo without shadow: any frame will seem to fit once chosen. The discipline is to characterise R in effect terms (what is failing to resolve, for whom, at what scale) and then find the frame whose closure predicts its shape (Fig 9 :26). Asking existing customers what radical product to build is a mirror: they are the current frame. Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."*

**Verdict: `specified`, triangulated** (Fig 9, Fig 8, Fig 2). **Refuter:** a frame selected first that still predicts a novel ground with provenance and a shadow test.

### F9 - Which specific radical bet wins is a `gap`

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*
Fig 13 :134 (partial): *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*
Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

After a genuine re-census, no figure line names a winning technology, market, or allocation ratio. The figures narrow the **shape** of a good decision procedure and refuse the **name** of the winner. Fig 7 :42: *"the next room you book is the direction you travel, and booking a room like the last one is how a chain stops travelling at all."* The direction is legible; the destination is not. This is a declared `gap`, not a hidden answer, and unreachable is a verdict.

**Verdict: `general`, `gap`. Refuter:** a figure line that, after re-census, yields a specific winning technology or universal ratio without an import and a ground reading.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A radical atom is shown to be fully derivable by composition inside the firm's current product closure, with no imported atom and no contact with a differing frame (breaks F1, Fig 6 :21 and Fig 8 :32).
2. A single currency is found in which both tracks' residuals are commensurable and one score predicts both outcomes on a paying ground (breaks F3, Fig 12 :125-130 and Fig 9 :60).
3. The live radical signal is shown to be carried by the portfolio aggregate and to vanish under per-slot decomposition (breaks F4, Fig 1 :19 and :22).
4. An agent-only improvement is shown to raise capability while changing no contact with any paying ground (breaks F5, Fig 11 :40).
5. A convention is shown to settle an outcome while remaining checkable by an unmoved ground (breaks F6, Fig 10 :47).
6. Two low-overlap frames are shown to agree on a bet and predict the market without either ranging against a paying ground (breaks Fig 2 :35 and :40).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Strains, re-census, and canonical usage check

### Strain 1: two tracks versus one budget

Fig 6 :31 says never read the tracks against one another, yet a firm has one budget. Re-census: the two sentences govern different quantities. Fig 6 :31 governs the **verdict** (what each track predicts and what would settle it). The budget is a **filter for the budget** (Fig 9 :60) and a **convention** authored by the allocator (Fig 10 :47). One budget can be split by convention without ever comparing the two tracks in one currency. **No strain after re-census.**

### Strain 2: the novelty guard seems to forbid improvement

Fig 5's guards require novelty, which appears to ban incremental work. Re-census: Fig 6 :22 says novelty is relative to atoms(Γ), not to the world. A new design is a new molecule from held atoms, so it is novel relative to atoms and passes. The guard forbids a term already in the library, not an improvement. **No strain after re-census.**

### Strain 3: "improvement changes nothing" seems to forbid all improvement

Fig 11 :40 forbids improvement in isolation. Re-census: the object improved decides. An improvement that changes the product's contact with a paying ground is not in isolation. A polish that only refines the same frame is. **No strain after re-census; the caveat is carried in F5.**

### Recommendation

1. Stop treating this as one decision. Unbundle into two loops with separate grounds and keep the verdicts separate (F1, F2, F9).
2. Use financial metrics as a budget cap only. Never let one score witness the verdict (F3, F6).
3. Report per-slot residuals, not a portfolio average (F4).
4. Test every "improvement" for contact change; a sandbox prove is wiring only (F5).
5. Describe the residual in effect terms before selecting the frame or the technology (F8).
6. Author the allocation convention at senior level, with decay and independent review bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong (F6, Fig 2 :42).
7. Keep the shape claim and the six refuters in front of the links in any outreach (Fig 10 :42).

**After re-census: no hidden strain. One declared gap (F9). All substantive claims `specified`, with the structural rules `general`. Nothing `grounded`.**
