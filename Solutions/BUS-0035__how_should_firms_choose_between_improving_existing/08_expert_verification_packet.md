# Expert verification packet: the Tether on improvement versus radical innovation

**What this is:** a claim in a form an R&D strategy or technology-management researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the firm-level claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No measurement of a firm is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. **Status: simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.**

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Improving an existing product and pursuing a radical innovation are two different operators. Improvement is mint inside the firm's current closure: a new molecule from atoms it already holds, which can never add an atom. Radical innovation is import: an atom added on contact with a frame whose closure differs. They must be run as two tracks that are never read against one another, and they cannot be ranked in one currency, because a composition costs in two currencies that do not add. Financial metrics are filters for the budget, never witnesses for the verdict. The live radical signal is per slot and disappears into a portfolio average; an improvement that changes no contact changes nothing. The senior team authors the allocation convention and buys independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong; it never settles which bet is good. Which technology wins is a declared gap.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 6, 8 and 13

- **Statement:** mint and import are distinct operators, and inward search cannot manufacture a radical atom.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :21 *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* Fig 6 :23 *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 8 :32 *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."*
- **Refuter:** derive a radical atom entirely inside the current product closure, with no imported atom and no contact with a differing frame.

### C2 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 6, 9 and 12

- **Statement:** the two tracks must be run separately and cannot be compared in one score. Financial metrics are budget filters only.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :31 *"Neither answer tells you much about the other. Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other."* Fig 12 :125 *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
- **Refuter:** exhibit a single commensurable unit in which one score validly ranks both tracks and predicts both outcomes on a paying ground.

### C3 - framework-internal plus firm-facing; checkable by reading Figures 1, 10 and 11

- **Statement:** the live signal is per slot; a portfolio average hides it; the discriminator for improvement is contact, not effort; the senior team authors conventions and never verdicts.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 11 :40 *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Fig 10 :47 *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*
- **What to check in the field:** do incumbents in your sample report healthy aggregate metrics while a low-end or new-market slot carries the live signal? Does an improvement that changed contact pay differently from one that did not? Is the senior team's rule a convention (cap, cadence, decay) or a verdict (this bet is good)?
- **Refuter:** show that the radical signal is carried by the aggregate and vanishes per slot; or show an agent-only improvement that raises capability with no contact change; or show a convention that settles an outcome while remaining checkable by an unmoved ground.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a universal R&D split such as 70/20/10. That is a preference held as a term, predicting nothing.
- Not a named winning technology, market or allocation ratio. The framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32).
- Not that more R&D spend means more exploration. Activity is not success (Fig 5 :48).
- Not that ROI is useless. It is a budget filter, not a witness (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that existing customers should select the radical direction. They are a mirror (Fig 2 :35).
- Not that a sandbox test proves capability. It is a substituted habitat (Fig 11 :70).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by a firm measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. A per-slot residual log showing the radical signal in one small slot, hidden by the average.
2. A paying experiment where an imported atom from a differing closure wins where inward mint did not, with provenance and a shadow test.
3. A contact test separating improvements that changed contact from those that did not.
4. A decayed-verdict test: a refuted bet paying after the ground changed.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 6 :21, :23, :29-31 and Fig 12 :125. Four short passages.
2. Check C2 against your own knowledge of how firms actually rank projects. Is a single score in two units the real error the figures name?
3. Name one instrument that would show a per-slot radical signal before the aggregate shows it, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
