# WATSON answer: how should a firm choose between improvement and radical innovation?

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** Figures are primary and decide. World sources are secondary, corroborate but never overrule, and a conflict returns the derivation to the domain census. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. No firm was measured.

## QUESTION

How should firms choose between improving existing products and pursuing uncertain, radical innovations? Restated in effect terms: a firm has one action budget and two slots, the existing product with a known paying ground and a radical opportunity with no settled ground. What is being decided is which operator to fund, mint inside the current closure or import from a differing closure. Option A treats this as one allocation under one score. Option B treats it as a union of two loops with separate grounds, run separately and never averaged. A leads to a type error in which two currencies are summed and a live slot signal disappears into a portfolio average; B leads to a checkable shape with six refuters and a declared gap where the winner would be named. R is indexed per slot, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19).

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Arity: dyadic, a frame (firm Γ plus budget) acting on a slot, indexed by slot, with an allocator authoring conventions over both. Symmetry: the two options are not symmetric under swap, because fundamental search repeats cheaply inside a closure while a radical atom requires contact with a differing closure; the currency and the anchor both change under a swap. Scale: D1 is within one frame, D2 crosses frames. Fig 9 :55 names this a union compound: a bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones.
- **D1, product-improvement loop.** Actors: engineers, existing customers, current metrics, routines. Currency: actions reducing R on the current slot, priced in bits to describe and actions to verify. Anchor: the existing paying market (a counter, a market, Fig 2 :29). Admitted.
- **D2, radical-innovation loop.** Actors: scouts, external frames (research, start-ups, adjacent fields, nonconsumers), importing seat. Currency: imported atoms, provenance, shadow test, independence. Anchor: a prospective paying ground, nameable as a kind but not yet instantiated. Admitted with caveat.
- **D3, allocation and review frame.** Actors: senior management team as ground-maintainer seat, budget, review cadence, decay rules. Currency: budget as contact cost, conventions, independence bought in proportion to cost of error. Anchor: the market(s), never internal agreement; the seat authors conventions, not verdicts. Admitted.
- **D4, habitat and contact channel.** Actors: customers, entrants, suppliers, regulators, adjacent disciplines. Currency: contact and cascades. Anchor: the ground, which does not decay; the channel, which does (Fig 10 :24). Admitted.
- **X1, visionary leader as free-floating cause.** Actors: the leader. Currency: talent. Anchor: none nameable apart from the habitat. Not a domain yet (Fig 11 :39-40).
- **X2, a universal optimal R&D ratio.** Actors: none. Currency: a number. Anchor: none. Refused as a preference held as a term (Fig 5 :62-63).

**Union, not partition** (Fig 9 :54-57). Dependency order: perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry (Fig 3). The middle link, having terms for what a radical situation affords and saying what counts as progress, has no instrument (Fig 3 :27), so effort lands above the break (Fig 3 :29-30).

Strain test applied: one apparent strain (two tracks versus one budget) was re-censused. Fig 6 :31 governs the verdict; the budget is a filter for the budget (Fig 9 :60) and a convention (Fig 10 :47). Different quantities, so no strain. See `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §5.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

**The figure-derived verdict.** This is a union of two loops, not one decision. Improvement is inward mint inside the firm's current closure and is licensed while the live residual is resolvable in the firm's own terms. Radical innovation is outward import from a frame whose closure differs and is licensed when a live slot residual persists and the current closure cannot hold it. Run the two tracks separately, never read one against the other, and never sum them into one score. Financial metrics are filters for the budget, never witnesses for the verdict. The senior team authors conventions (cap, cadence, decay, independence bought in proportion to cost) and never settles which bet is good. The discriminator for improvement is contact with a paying ground. Which technology wins is a declared `gap`. All substantive claims are `specified`; the structural rules are `general`; nothing is `grounded`. The low-overlap world fields corroborate the two-instrument mechanism; the business pool corroborates the union shape but is one witness; the bandit conflict was returned to census and bounded to D1.

- **cost if wrong:** misdirected diagnosis. A firm failing to renew reach would be sold better product analytics, or a firm with a dead channel would be told to import more, and one lever would be applied to a union that needs two. The cost is a missed foothold and spent independence, and it is bounded because the shape is stated with six falsifiers in advance (Fig 2 :42 prices independence against the cost of being wrong).
- **undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails; a refutation is added and the reach falls because *"A refutation adds a term that refuses; it removes nothing, and the reach falls because something was added rather than because anything was deleted."* (Fig 6 :42). The shape and its refuters are recorded with provenance so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-64).
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. Any of the six pre-registered refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 would reverse it, in particular (1) a radical atom derived inside the current closure, (2) one commensurable currency predicting both tracks on a paying ground, (3) the live signal carried by the aggregate, (4) an agent-only improvement that raises capability with no contact change, or (5) a convention that settles an outcome while remaining checkable. A source with a genuinely different evidence pool showing one score predicts both tracks would also reverse it. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
