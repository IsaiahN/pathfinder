# Isomers, order and analogues in the digital money question

## RESIDUAL

1. Are a public digital claim and a commercial bank deposit isomers under Fig 12's definition, or merely members of a functional group?
2. What is the order of the effect: one step, two, or three?
3. Do the analogues the figures themselves use (ecosystem engineering, cross-scale cascade, network phase transition) carry over, and where do they strain?

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Isomer or functional group?

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

As the two objects are usually written, a retail deposit and a public digital claim share the full atom set: {a claim, a unit of account, a debtor, finality, par convertibility}. What differs is the bond, specifically:

- who is the debtor (a commercial bank versus the public issuer);
- where settlement is final (at the bank versus at the central bank);
- whether the claim can be changed by the issuer's discretion.

That difference is invisible in the ingredient list "a digital claim denominated in the unit" and decisive in behaviour, especially under stress. By Fig 12's definition they are **isomers**, not merely members of a functional group.

The weaker reading is also available and should be recorded. If one writes both objects only as "a claim in the unit of account held by a member of the public", they share a **functional group** (Fig 12 :124) with different qualifiers. The isomer claim is the stronger one and is the one worth testing, because it is the one that carries the behavioural difference.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if the new form requires an atom not in the existing set, for example a unit not convertible at par with the established unit, the isomer claim fails and the two objects are not even in the same substrate.

### Q2: What order is the effect?

- **First order, D1 to D2 direct.** Users move from a deposit claim to a public claim. This is a change in who holds what. Fig 11 :61: *"You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat"*: the banks and their funding relations remain.
- **Second order, D2 internal.** Deposits become a less stable funding source. This is the leg named by CPMI 174 (secondary corroboration): *"Commercial banks' reliance on customer deposits may become less stable, as deposits could more easily take flight to the central bank in times of stress."*
- **Third order, D2 to D3.** Credit supply and system-wide stability move. Fig 11 :52-54: *"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*

The order is **not** a sequence bond in the strict sense (Fig 12 :73: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*), because the three effects can co-occur and can be amplified by a stress event. It is better read as a **contact cascade** enumerated outward from the residual (Fig 11 :44: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."*). Treating it as a strict sequence imports an ordering the figure does not license, the same error the exemplar found in the `→` reading of the spacetime chain.

**Falsifier:** measured second- and third-order effects that occur without any first-order change in holdings, which would break the cascade reading and require a common-cause frame instead (Fig 2 :6 "collapse 2: common cause").

### Q3: Do the analogues carry?

**Ecosystem engineering (Fig 11 :65).** *"Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994. Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated."* This analogue is the figure's own, so it is primary, not an import. It carries directly: a public digital claim is introduced for its cascade, and the cascade is routinely unenumerated in policy documents. It strains at one point: an ecosystem engineer is usually not also the rule-maker for the habitat. A government is both. That extra role is exactly what F2 examines under the anchor rule.

**Pilot as substituted habitat (Fig 11 :70).** *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."* This carries directly to sandboxes and limited pilots. It says what a pilot can prove and what it cannot, without needing any monetary data.

**Network phase transition (world, low overlap).** Acemoglu, Ozdaglar and Tahbaz-Salehi (AER 2015) find that *"the same factors that contribute to resilience under certain conditions may function as significant sources of systemic risk under others."* This corroborates Fig 11 :49 (*"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*) at a different stratum. It does not corroborate any specific CBDC design. Using it to claim that a given holding limit is stabilising would be a cross-level import without a source gate.

**Rigidity trap (world, low overlap).** MathisonSlee et al. (2022) describe a state that is *"highly connected, rigid, and inflexible"* where *"System capacity for novelty is diminished"*. This corroborates the F5 warning that a design which maximises stability on one axis can reduce adaptive responses on another. Again, mechanism only, not verdict.

### Q4: The two prices do not add

Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* A digital claim arrangement is cheap to describe (a few rules) and expensive to test against a ground (a live issuance with stress). Conversely, a complex rulebook can be expensive to describe and cheap to refute on one axis. Summing "design complexity" and "testing cost" into one score is the named error. The pre-registered refuter in `01` §4 keeps the two readings separate.

## CONCLUSION

The public digital claim and the bank deposit are **isomers** over a shared atom set, with the debtor and finality as the decisive bond. The effect is a **contact cascade of three orders**, not a strict sequence, enumerated outward from the residual. The habitat and pilot analogues carry directly; the network and resilience analogues corroborate the valence-reversal mechanism at a different stratum and carry no monetary verdict. One strain is named: the comparison bond (par convertibility) at second order needs an instrument to decide whether a settled claim counts as a quantity at that level. `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` keep this strain visible.
