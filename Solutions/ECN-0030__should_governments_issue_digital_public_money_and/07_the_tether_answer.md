# The Tether's answer on digital public money

**Mode: `specified` on the shape and per-domain verdicts, `general` on the method, not `grounded`.** A simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6). No issuance data was used.

## 1. The residual it answers

A state actor proposes to add a new settlement object to a monetary habitat. The question bundles a normative limb and a two-part consequence limb. It is a union compound (Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*), not one hard yes or no. The ground, when one is read, will be deposit flows, funding costs and a stability metric, not the number of agencies that have published a view.

## 2. The shape, per domain, kept separate

1. **Settlement and anchor (D1).** A public settlement object is admissible only while it stays an anchor: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks"* (Fig 2 :29). If the rulebook lets the issuer change access, holdings or usability in response to what participants do, the money stops being a convention and becomes an outcome-deciding verdict (Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*). This is a constraint on design, not an objection to public money.
2. **Banking habitat (D2).** The effect is a contact cascade: direct change in holdings, second-order change in deposit funding, third-order change in credit and stability (Fig 11 :52-54). The figure gives the *shape* of the cascade; it gives no sign and no magnitude. Introduction is available and exclusion is not (Fig 11 :61). A pilot is a substituted habitat and proves wiring, not capability (Fig 11 :70).
3. **Systemic network (D3).** The same design feature can stabilise one residual and destabilise another (Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*). Holding limits spend one price in description and another in testing, and the two do not add (Fig 12 :125).
4. **Design configuration (D4).** A public digital claim and a commercial bank deposit are isomers (Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance"*), not a new primitive. The bond (debtor, finality, par convertibility) is what the ingredient list hides and what decides behaviour.
5. **The unconditional "should".** `gap`. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*; Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*. No figure line settles "governments should issue" or "should not issue" without a ground reading. The conditional rule is the reachable answer.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "issue" and not "do not issue". Those are verdicts on a gap.
- Not a number for deposit outflows, funding costs, or a stability effect. That requires measurement.
- Not that banks are harmed by public money. Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49, :51), and banks are intermediaries in D2, not a standing enemy class.
- Not that a public digital claim is a new monetary primitive, and not that it is merely cash in a new wrapper. It is an isomer: same atoms, different bond, different behaviour.
- Not that a pilot shows the live effect. A test harness is a substituted habitat.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by data.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating observation, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* says an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*, and that *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* For this question the discriminating observations are:

1. **Anchor test.** Does issuer discretion move settlement value, access or par convertibility, and is any outcome decided by the convention? A design plus measured behaviour answers this.
2. **Cascade test.** With holding limit and remuneration held constant, do deposit stability, funding costs and credit volumes move after introduction, and at which order?
3. **Isomer test.** Does the new form need any primitive absent from the current system, for example a unit not convertible at par?
4. **Reversal test.** Does a stress episode flip the sign of a design feature that was stabilising in calm conditions? This is the Acemoglu et al. mechanism applied to the monetary habitat (secondary corroboration, not a figure line).

The three refuters in `01` §4 were written before this conclusion. If any fires, the corresponding claim is withdrawn.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The ground is available in principle: monetary authorities publish deposit and funding data. It was not read in this run. The answer stops at the limit of the instruments actually used and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity"* (canonical README). Treating the conditional shape as if it were a verdict would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say whether governments should issue digital public money, and it does not give a number for the damage or benefit to banks. It says the question is a bundle of three conditions that no single yes or no can clear. A public settlement claim is admissible only while it stays an anchor, meaning its value and access do not change because the issuer or any other frame thinks otherwise; a rulebook that lets the issuer decide outcomes has stopped being a convention. Its effect on commercial banks is a contact cascade, not a one-step change: holdings move first, deposit funding second, credit and system stability third, and a pilot proves the wiring rather than the live behaviour. The new claim is an isomer of the bank deposit, not a new monetary primitive: same atoms, different bond, and the bond (who is the debtor, where settlement is final, whether conversion is at par) is what decides behaviour under stress. The unconditional "should" is recorded as a gap until a ground reading, not an agreement, settles it. What is offered is the shape any admissible answer must have, three refuters written in advance, and the observations that would close it.
