# Outreach draft: check the output before deciding about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the anchor constraint, the cascade enumeration and the pre-registered refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are stated in the material itself. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable shape claim about public digital money, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about money and banking. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with a position on whether governments should issue digital money. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The question bundles three conditions that a single yes or no cannot clear. My derivation returns a conditional shape, not a verdict:
>
> 1. A public settlement object is admissible only while it stays an anchor. By anchor I mean the thing that settles value and does not change its answer because any participant thinks otherwise. A design rule that lets the issuer decide who can hold, how much, or what the claim can be used for turns the money from a convention into an outcome-deciding verdict. That is the named failure.
> 2. The effect on banks is a contact cascade, not a first-order change. Direct: users hold the new claim. Second: bank deposits become a less stable funding source. Third: credit supply and system-wide stability move. A pilot proves the wiring, not the behaviour of the real habitat.
> 3. A public digital claim and a commercial bank deposit are isomers in the figure's sense: same atoms (a claim denominated in the unit of account), different bond (who is the debtor, settlement finality, convertibility at par). The ingredient list cannot tell them apart, and the difference decides the behaviour.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming that public digital money is good or bad. I am not claiming a number for deposit outflows or a direction for financial stability. I am not claiming that a government should or should not issue. No figure line settles that unconditionally, so I record it as a gap. I am not claiming a pilot result transfers to a live system.
>
> **What would refute the shape:**
> 1. A design and measurement showing that issuer discretion leaves settlement value, access and par convertibility unchanged, and that no outcome is decided by the convention.
> 2. Post-issuance data showing no measurable movement in deposit stability, funding costs or credit volumes, with holding limit and remuneration held constant.
> 3. A demonstration that the new form needs a primitive absent from the existing monetary system, for example a unit not convertible at par.
>
> I wrote these down before running the derivation. If any one holds, I retract the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures text extract with line numbers (local copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - BIS CPMI, Central bank digital currencies, Papers 174, 2018: https://www.bis.org/cpmi/publ/d174.htm
> - BIS Working Paper 948, Auer and Boehme, 2021: https://www.bis.org/publ/work948.htm
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the local `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`. Run it on a monetary problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [your name]

The first contact carries the shape claim and its refuter only. The framework's interiors stay out of it until the reader has recorded a verdict on stage 1, because a verifier who can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it (Fig 10 :42).
