## 0. The residual, in effect terms

The question is not "is digital public money good." Stated as an effect: a state actor proposes to introduce a new settlement object into a monetary habitat, and asks what changes in contact when it does. Three things are bundled into the one sentence.

1. **A normative limb.** "Should governments issue" presupposes an issuer. An issuer that can change the rules under which the money settles is not a neutral anchor. The limb is therefore an anchor question, not a preference question.
2. **A consequence limb.** "How might that affect commercial banks" is a question about an intermediary population whose funding is a contact relation to depositors.
3. **A systemic limb.** "And financial stability" is a property of the network those intermediaries form, three steps out from the initial change in who holds what.

Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* So the residual is a **union compound with its own loop**, not one hard yes or no.

The sought-for shape is bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"). Shape claim: *a new public settlement object is admissible only if it remains an anchor, its contact cascade is enumerated before introduction, and it is declared as a rearrangement of held atoms rather than a new primitive, with a named ground test.* Whether that shape is true is tested below. A conditional rule, not a verdict, is what the figures can return.

R is indexed per slot, not aggregated (Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*). The slots here are: the settlement asset, the bank depositor, the bank funding channel, and the systemic network. A global "digital money is good for stability" statement would throw away the only signal that says where to look.

## 1. Domain census

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity.** Multi-place: issuer, money form, intermediary, user, network. Two conditions in the normative limb (anchor held / cascade enumerated), binding a third (isomer declared).
- **Symmetry.** Asymmetric. The issuer can act on the rules; the depositor and the bank cannot act on the issuer in the same way. Fig 11 :61: *"You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat"*. The system is not a symmetric join of equal frames.
- **Scale.** Individual payment (micro), bank funding (meso), systemic stability (macro). The transform between scales is lossy; Fig 4 :30 requires the round trip gap to be measured.

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Settlement and anchor | issuer, unit of account, settlement asset, users | claims, settlement finality, convertibility at par | whatever settles value and does not move when a frame thinks (Fig 2 :29) | admitted |
| D2 Banking intermediary habitat | commercial banks, depositors, borrowers, payment service providers | deposits as bank liabilities, funding, credit supply | deposit flows, funding costs, balance sheet data | admitted |
| D3 Systemic stability network | interconnected institutions, payment systems, overseers | contagion, liquidity, trust | stress events and measured spreads or reserve movements | admitted |
| D4 Design configuration | rulebook authors, limits, remuneration rules, access rules | the bond between holder and debtor | a post-issuance measurement, not the design intent | admitted |
| X1 "Should" as free-floating verdict | a preference with no actor or instrument | none | none nameable | **not a domain yet** |

X1 fails the anchor test. A preference for or against state money has no instrument that would change it, so it is not a domain. It is converted into D1 to D4 by binding the conditional shape in §0.

### 1.3 Partition or union

**Union, not partition.** D1 to D4 overlap. A limit on holdings (D4) is simultaneously an anchor protection (D1), a funding intervention (D2), and a change in network connectivity (D3). Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds. The compound needs four loops, one per domain, kept separate and never averaged.

### 1.4 Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. perception: describe the change in contact (who holds what, who funds whom);
2. vocabulary: terms for what a new settlement object affords (claim, finality, convertibility, limit);
3. objective: say what would count as progress in measurable terms;
4. planning: choose the configuration;
5. learn and carry: measure, verify, carry to the next jurisdiction.

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."* The likely break is between 2 and 3. Most digital money proposals have a rich vocabulary (tokenisation, programmability, distributed ledgers) and no objective whose failure can be observed. Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* A reading taken below that break is a reading of nothing.

## 3. Findings

### F1: The question is a union compound, so no single yes/no is the right shape (decisive for the form of the answer)

Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
Fig 11 :52-54: *"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*

The first-order effect of a public digital money is a change in who holds a settlement claim. The second-order effect is a change in bank deposit funding. The residual that matters for stability is three steps out. Treating "should issue" and "effect on banks" as one question is the first-order scoping error the habitat figure names.

- **Mode:** `general` for the union claim, `specified` for its application here.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 9 union logic and Fig 11 contact cascade are low-overlap routes).
- **Refuter:** show that the anchor condition and the cascade condition are the same condition under one measure, so that one lever clears both without a categorically different residue.

### F2: The issuer cannot serve as the anchor if it updates (the structural core of "should")

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."*
Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."*
Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*
Fig 10 :18: *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"*

Money's unit of account is a convention: *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"* (Fig 2 :41). But a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one. A digital public money whose rulebook lets the issuer change access, holdings, or usability in response to what frames do converts the convention into an outcome-deciding verdict. The issuer becomes a frame, and the anchor is lost. This is not an objection to public money as such. It is a constraint on its design: the settlement property must stay fixed against issuer discretion, and the design must *"install what can be violated"* (Fig 10 :19) so a departure is detectable.

World corroboration only: the CPMI digital innovation page describes *"the pivotal role of central bank money as the anchor of the payment ecosystem"* and the 2026 CPMI report calls trust in money *"their cornerstone"*. These are secondary witnesses, not figure lines.

- **Mode:** `specified`. The figure constraint is general; its application to a programmable public money is a specification.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 2 anchor rule and Fig 10 convention/verdict rule, low overlap).
- **Refuter:** a design plus measurement showing that issuer discretion does not move the settlement value, access guarantee, or convertibility at par, and that no outcome is decided by the convention.

### F3: Bank effects are a habitat introduction with an unenumerated cascade

Fig 11 :61: *"You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat"*
Fig 11 :65: *"Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994. Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated."*
Fig 11 :70: *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."*

Issuing a public digital money introduces an actor. Exclusion of the existing habitat is not available: deposit-taking banks, their funding relations, and the payment rails stay. The figure gives the shape of the effect but not its magnitude or sign. A pilot or sandbox is a substituted habitat, so it proves that the wiring works, not that the system will behave that way when the habitat is the real one.

World corroboration only: CPMI Papers 174 (2018) states that *"Commercial banks' reliance on customer deposits may become less stable, as deposits could more easily take flight to the central bank in times of stress."* This is the second-order leg named by the figure, arrived at inside the central banking frame.

- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 habitat rule and Fig 2 :29 anchor/market rule: the bank's liability is a frame's claim while central bank money is the anchor; low overlap).
- **Refuter:** measured post-issuance data showing no change in deposit stability, funding costs, or credit volumes attributable to the introduction, after the holding limit and remuneration design are held constant.

### F4: Public digital money and bank deposits are isomers, not a new primitive

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
Fig 13 :115: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."*
Fig 6 :26: *"An import adds no atom to the world."*

Both a deposit and a public digital money are claims denominated in the unit of account. What differs is the bond: who is the debtor, whether settlement is final at the central bank, and whether convertibility is at par. The ingredient list cannot recover that bond, which is exactly Fig 12's isomer case. Calling the new form "a new kind of money" is a notation error, not a discovery. No atom is added to the world; the arrangement changes.

- **Mode:** `specified` for the monetary application, `general` for the isomer rule.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 12 bond definition, Fig 13 closure, Fig 6 import price; three distinct roles).
- **Refuter:** demonstrate that the new form requires a primitive absent from the existing monetary system, for example a unit not convertible at par with the established unit of account.

### F5: The design levers have valence relative to the residual, not fixed valence

Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*
Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*

Holding limits, non-remuneration, and intermediated custody are often presented as uniformly stabilising. The figures refuse that. A feature protects one residual and harms another. Adding a settlement claim to a payment network raises diversity for one purpose and connectivity for another, and the two prices (bits of description, actions to test) do not add. World corroboration from a low-overlap field: Acemoglu, Ozdaglar and Tahbaz-Salehi (AER 2015) find that *"the same factors that contribute to resilience under certain conditions may function as significant sources of systemic risk under others."*

- **Mode:** `general` for valence relativity, `specified` for these design levers.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 valence rule and Fig 12 two-currency price, low overlap).
- **Refuter:** exhibit a design feature with invariant valence across two documented residuals with opposite signs.

### F6: The unconditional "should" is a `gap`, and the conditional shape is the reachable answer

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*
Fig 13 :133: *"A system with a closure can answer that before it searches, and what it returns is a shape rather than a name."*
Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*
Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

After a genuine re-census, no figure line licenses "governments should issue" or "governments should not issue" as an unconditional verdict. What is licensed is the conditional rule in §0 and the four per-domain verdicts. The missing thing is a ground reading: data from an actual issuance that could distinguish "anchor preserved, cascade bounded" from "frame captured, cascade amplified". It can be characterised in effect terms but not named in advance. This is a `gap` for the unconditional normative, not a silence and not a refusal to answer.

- **Mode:** `general` for the gap, `specified` for the reachable shape.
- **Status:** `gap` for the unconditional verdict; triangulated for the conditional shape (Fig 9 and the canonical README, low overlap between the figure and the usage rule).
- **Refuter:** produce a figure line that directly settles the unconditional normative, or a ground reading that makes the conditional rule unnecessary.

### F7: The objective link is the break in most digital money proposals

Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*

Perception and vocabulary are strong in this field: payment instruments, tokenisation, settlement finality are all nameable. The objective is usually stated as "efficiency", "inclusion" or "resilience" without a measure whose failure is observable before issuance. Effort spent refining the architecture above that break cannot rescue a step that cannot use it. The pre-registered refuter in §4 is the instrument that repairs the link.

- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Status:** single-source (Fig 3), with a corroborating venue fact: the AOM-style registered-report logic is not present here, but the IJCB and Ecology and Society submission routes allow a design and its failure condition to be declared before results.
- **Refuter:** a proposal whose objective has a declared measure, a failure threshold, and a pre-registered refuter, all fixed before issuance.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds.

1. **Anchor test.** A design plus measurement shows that issuer discretion leaves settlement value, access, and convertibility at par unchanged, and that no outcome is decided by the convention. This kills F2.
2. **Cascade test.** After a controlled introduction with holding limit and remuneration held constant, deposit stability, funding costs, and credit volumes do not move measurably. This kills F3's cascade shape.
3. **Isomer test.** The new form requires a primitive absent from the existing monetary system, for example a unit not convertible at par. This kills F4.
4. **Normative test.** A figure line is produced that directly licenses an unconditional "should" or "should not", or a ground reading settles the conditional rule. This kills F6's gap verdict.

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

**Filters are not witnesses.** Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* The tier, the venue's prestige, and the number of agencies that have published on CBDC are budget filters. None of them decides the verdict.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Do not answer the question as a yes or no. Return the union split and the four per-domain verdicts (F1).
2. Make the anchor constraint explicit: the settlement property must not update on what any frame thinks, and any rule change that decides an outcome must be declared as such (F2).
3. Enumerate the cascade to three steps before introducing the actor, including the bank funding leg and the systemic leg, and say what a pilot cannot prove (F3).
4. Describe the new form as a rearrangement of a held claim, with the bond stated (who is debtor, finality, par convertibility), not as a new primitive (F4).
5. State the valence of every design lever relative to a named residual, and price description and testing separately (F5).
6. Keep the unconditional normative as a declared `gap`, with the missing ground characterised in effect terms (F6).
7. Attach the pre-registered refuter to any proposal before it leaves the machine (F7).
8. Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Send the conditional rule and its refuter, not a list of conclusions.

## 7. Strains and open items

- **The operand of "par" at second order.** Fig 12 :117 licenses the isomer reading, and the Operators table defines bonds as relations between two operands. Convertibility at par is a threshold relation between two quantities (comparison). Whether a settlement claim counts as a quantity at the second-order level where the comparison is made needs an instrument, not an argument. Named, not hidden.
- **The ground is available in principle but not read here.** Monetary data exists: deposit flows, funding spreads, reserve balances. This simulated derivation does not use it. That is a limitation of this run, not a claim that the question is unanswerable.
- **After re-census: one strain named, no hidden contradiction.**

**Bottom line.** The shape survives. The settlement asset must stay an anchor, the cascade must be enumerated before introduction, the new form is an isomer not a primitive, and the unconditional "should" is a `gap` until a ground reading settles it. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.
