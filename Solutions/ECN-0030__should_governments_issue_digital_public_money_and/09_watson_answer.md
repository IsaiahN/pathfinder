## QUESTION

Should governments issue digital public money, and how might that affect commercial banks and financial stability? Restated as an effect: a state actor proposes to add a settlement object to a monetary habitat; what changes in contact, and what would have to stay fixed for the introduction to be admissible? Option A: issue, accepting the cascade. Option B: withhold, accepting the current two-tier arrangement. The figure-derived answer is a conditional shape plus a declared `gap` for the unconditional normative, not a verdict.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and dependency order.

- **R (the residual).** Arity: multi-place (issuer, claim, intermediary, user, network); two conditions in the normative limb binding a third. Symmetry: asymmetric, the issuer can change the rules and the other actors cannot do so in the same way (Fig 11 :61 *"You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat"*). Scale: individual payment, bank funding, systemic stability, with a lossy transform between scales. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- **D1 Settlement and anchor.** Actors: issuer, unit of account, settlement asset, users. Currency: claims, finality, par convertibility. Anchor: whatever settles value and does not move when a frame thinks. Admitted.
- **D2 Banking habitat.** Actors: commercial banks, depositors, borrowers, payment providers. Currency: deposits as bank liabilities, funding, credit. Anchor: deposit flows, funding costs, balance sheet data. Admitted.
- **D3 Systemic network.** Actors: interconnected institutions, payment systems, overseers. Currency: contagion, liquidity, trust. Anchor: stress events and measured spreads or reserve movements. Admitted.
- **D4 Design configuration.** Actors: rulebook authors, limits, remuneration and access rules. Currency: the bond between holder and debtor. Anchor: a post-issuance measurement, not the design intent. Admitted.
- **X1 "Should" as free-floating verdict.** No actor, no currency, no nameable anchor. Not a domain yet. It is converted into D1 to D4 by binding the conditional shape as an ordinary term (Formula, "Direction").

**Union, not partition.** D1 to D4 overlap. A holding limit is at once an anchor protection, a funding intervention and a change in network connectivity. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* **Order by dependency:** describe the contact change, name the terms, state a measurable objective, choose the configuration, then verify (Fig 3). The likely break is between vocabulary and objective: Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

Strain test applied: the anchor rule (Fig 2) and the convention rule (Fig 10) were initially read as conflicting for a state-issued money. Re-census resolves it: Fig 2 governs what may serve as an anchor; Fig 10 governs when a convention stops being neutral. Different quantities, same habitat.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The figure-derived verdict is the conditional shape, kept separate per domain and never averaged: an anchor constraint on the settlement property, a three-order contact cascade that must be enumerated before introduction, an isomer reading of the public claim against the deposit, and a valence-relative treatment of design levers. The unconditional "should" is a `gap` until a ground reading settles it. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate. The world literature corroborates the mechanism shapes, with the central banking pages acting as one witness and two low-overlap fields corroborating valence reversal and cross-scale cascade.

- **Cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, refuters and provenance before the conclusion, so a wrong claim is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a conclusion instead of a generator, which Fig 2 :42 prices (*"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*). That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and its refuter. A second cost is a policy actor treating a `gap` as a licence to decide on prestige or agreement, which is the named collapse in *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*.
- **Undoable:** yes. No atom is deleted if a claim fails; a refutation is added and the reach falls because *"something was added rather than because anything was deleted"* (Fig 6 :42-43). The design configuration is reversible in law. The contact created is not subtractable from the habitat, so the lineage and the measurement must be kept (Fig 10 :62: *"A ruling carries provenance like an atom."*).
- **Reversed by:** any of the three pre-registered refuters in `01` §4. Specifically: a design and measurement showing issuer discretion does not move the settlement anchor and decides no outcome; post-issuance data showing no measurable cascade with limit and remuneration held constant; a demonstration that a new primitive is required; a figure line that settles the unconditional normative; or a ground reading that distinguishes source-driven deposit outflows from a common-cause outflow. Agreement among agreeing institutions is not such a reversal.
