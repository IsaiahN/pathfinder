## 0. The residual in effect terms

The loop defines R = |Γ(b,a) - o'| per slot s (Formula step 1; Symbols table, `R`). The question here
is meta-level: it asks which quantity may stand as o' for "business success," and who may select it.
A candidate measure (profit, growth, resilience, innovation, stakeholder outcomes, or a composite) is
a **proposed anchor**.

- **b** is the frame's belief about what success would look like, formed from its history and priors.
- **a** is the action taken on that belief: invest, report, disclose, reward, regulate, rate.
- **Γ(b,a)** is what the frame's library of measures predicts will follow.
- **o'** is what the ground actually returns. Fig 2 :29 names the candidates directly: *"A
  measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."*
- **s** is the firm or decision unit. R is indexed by slot, never by industry average.

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** (Fig 1 :19)
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** (Fig 1 :22)

The phenomenon to explain is not the level of any score but **which reading is entitled to settle the
residual and on what authority**. State it as: *which proposed o' actually settles the question about
a firm or decision unit, does not move when the measured party pushes on it, and remains admissible
when the question itself changes?* This is an effect description. It names no metric and no decider.
Those are candidate names, withheld until the census.

**Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term** (Formula, "Direction"), before the run: the answer
would be (i) a rule mapping a residual described in effect terms to the appropriate ground, (ii) a
discriminator that tells whether two candidate measures are independent witnesses or one evidence
pool wearing two names, and (iii) an account of the deciding seat that excludes anyone measured by
the reading or gaining from a particular value of it. Until bound, this predicts nothing and no loop
check can see it. It is now bound, so the pre-registered refuter in section 4 can fire on it.

**Anchor test.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its
answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :30: *"you push on it, it is not an anchor."* A score
that can be moved by accounting choice, disclosure choice, acquisition timing, or selection of what
to report is not an anchor. It is an instrument inside the measured frame. Fig 1 :23: *"The ground
enters as the only metric."*

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### 1.1 Arity, symmetry, scale of R

- **Arity.** R is a multi-place relation among frame Γ, action a, proposed measure M and deciding
  seat D, indexed by slot s. At minimum it carries an ordered bond (`→`, sequence: *"A then B; B needs
  A to have happened"*, Fig 12 :72), because measurement precedes the decision that uses it, and a
  comparison bond for "best" (`⋛`, *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, Fig 12 :87).
- **Symmetry.** Not symmetric. The ground does not update on what the frame thinks (Fig 2 :25, :33).
  "Best" is a threshold relative to alternatives, not an absolute quantity (Fig 12 :85-89; Operators
  table :46-51).
- **Scale.** Four scales are in view: the single firm's measurement loop, the population of firms
  with *"something that ranks what they produce"* (Fig 4 :5), the time channel across the life of a
  firm, and the stakeholder or habitat field. The transform between scales is lossy, so the
  round-trip gap R_T must be measured rather than assumed (Fig 4 :30).

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

A domain is admitted only if an anchor can be named. Where none can be named, it is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor, nameable? | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 Market ground** (profit, growth) | firms, customers, investors, competitors | profit, growth, share, return | yes: realized market return and survival; *"a market"* is named as an anchor in Fig 2 :29 | admitted, with the caveat that the metric is produced inside the firm's accounting frame |
| **D2 Habitat and time ground** (resilience, innovation) | firm, entrants, technologies, environment | continued contact, renewal of reach, survival across shocks | yes: the ground that persists while the channel decays (Fig 10 :24); capability is agent-and-habitat (Fig 11 :39) | admitted |
| **D3 Human or stakeholder ground** | workers, customers, owners, communities, future generations | human judgment, welfare, voice | yes: human judgment is legitimate when *"the question is constitutively about it"* (Fig 2 :36) | admitted |
| **D4 The deciding seat and convention** | standard setters, boards, regulators, raters, auditors | conventions, rulings, ratings | yes, as a rule: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one (Fig 10 :47); the position selects the occupant (Fig 10 :85) | admitted as a rule-governed meta-domain |
| **X1 A single universal success score** | a rating agency or index | one summed number | **no**: components are in incommensurable currencies (Fig 12 :125-130) and overlap (Fig 13 :38) | not a domain; refused as a compound |
| **X2 "The market decides everything"** | market | price | market return is real, but for people-questions it is only an instrument and can be wrong on axes it does not measure (Fig 2 :37-38) | absorbed into D1 and bounded by D3 |

X1 fails the anchor test before any figure line is applied to it. This is a structural exclusion, not a
claim that composite indices are useless. They are instruments with a declared purpose, not anchors
that settle a question about the world.

### 1.3 Partition or union

D1 and D2 overlap: a firm's market return and its survival through a shock both read the habitat, so
their overlap is a compound with its own loop. D1 and D3 overlap: customer and investor judgment are
both market readings and human judgments, a second compound. D2 and D4 overlap: a convention that
defines "resilience" decides what counts as surviving, so the measurement changes the thing measured.
The whole question is a **union**, not a partition.

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** (Fig 9 :54)
> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** (Fig 9 :55)
> **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."** (Fig 9 :57)

The unbundled conditions are: (C1) which ground is being ranged against, and (C2) who is entitled to
range it. A case where the metric is wrong and a case where the decider is disqualified are not two
degrees of one problem; they need different instruments. The essay *The Library of Babel* states the
same stop: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as
one?"*

### 1.4 Dependency order (Fig 3)

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."** (Fig 3 :20)
> **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** (Fig 3 :19)
> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** (Fig 3 :27)

Order for this question: 1 perception (can the frame read the market or stakeholder response at all?)
→ 2 vocabulary (does its Γ have terms for what changed, and can it say what would count as progress
there?) → 3 objective (can it state a comparison that would count as getting somewhere? Fig 12 :112:
*"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."*) → 4 planning → 5 learn
and carry. The middle link, deciding what counts as success, is exactly the one with no instrument,
so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments (Fig 3 :24-26). Most measurement effort
lands above the break, on metrics whose instruments already exist.

## 3. Findings

Mode tag on each: `general` if structural, `specified` if a falsifiable prediction about business
measurement, `grounded` only if settled by a measurement. None is `grounded`.

### F1: A candidate measure is not the anchor by itself

**Mode: `general`. Domains D1 to D4. Status: triangulated (Fig 2 and Fig 1, anchor rule vs metric rule,
low topical overlap).**

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."** (Fig 2 :29)
> **"you push on it, it is not an anchor."** (Fig 2 :30)
> **"The anchor must not update."** (Fig 2 :33)
> **"The ground enters as the only metric."** (Fig 1 :23)

A score that moves when the measured party reframes, reclassifies, times or discloses differently has
failed the anchor test. Profit can be moved by accounting choice; growth by acquisition timing; a
stakeholder score by which stakeholders are asked. Their legitimacy is relational: each may range
against a ground, but none is the ground by virtue of being a number. The figure explicitly permits
*"a market"* and *"a patient outcome"* as anchors, so the answer is not "no metric is ever an anchor."
It is "a metric is an anchor only where the question is constitutively about that metric's ground."

**Refuter:** a candidate measure whose value is invariant under every reframing and timing choice
available to the measured party while the underlying ground is held fixed, and which still settles
the question. That would pass the anchor test and F1 would fail for that measure.

### F2: Profit and growth are frame-internal instruments, disqualified as self-scores

**Mode: `general` for the disqualification, `specified` for its business application. Domains D1, D4.
Status: triangulated (Fig 1 and Fig 5, per-slot indexing vs settlement, low overlap).**

> **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."** (Fig 1 :27)
> **"A global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"** (Fig 1 :13)
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** (Fig 1 :22)
> **"then check it against the ground, then carry it up"** (Fig 5 :32)

A firm's accounting apparatus produces the profit figure with which the firm is then scored. That is
the named self-scoring failure. Fig 1 :13 and :22 add the second defect: a corporate average can be
near zero while one division is losing contact, so an aggregate "success" number hides exactly the
signal that would say where to look. The world pool corroborates the design difficulty: the balanced
scorecard literature records the question *"Of all the measures you could have chosen, why did you
choose these?"* (Wikipedia, Balanced scorecard, read 2026-11-22). That is the frame asking itself to
justify its own score, which Fig 1 :27 says cannot be self-scoring evidence.

**Refuter:** a measure generated wholly outside the measured frame, not adjustable by the measured
party, and used as the sole score; for example an independent survival count or a third-party
measured outcome that pays at the ground. If such a measure both settles the question and is produced
outside the frame, the disqualification does not apply to it.

### F3: "Best" is a threshold comparison, not a property of a number

**Mode: `general`. Domain all. Status: triangulated (Fig 12 and Operators table, same figure family but
different provisions, so one witness).**

> **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."** (Fig 12 :112)
> **"⋛ comparison"** / **"a threshold relation between two quantities"** (Fig 12 :87, Operators table :48-51)
> **"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"** (Operators table :52)

The question's word "best" is a comparison term. It requires two quantities and a threshold, and a
vocabulary that has only ingredients (a list of metrics) cannot state a goal at all. So "which
metric is best" is not answered by ranking numbers; it is answered by naming the comparison and the
ground against which it is made. A frame holding profit, growth and stakeholder scores but no
comparison operator can say what a situation contains and cannot say what would count as getting
somewhere (Fig 12 :111-113).

**Refuter:** a demonstration that "business success" can be stated and settled without any comparison
term, as an absolute stock whose value needs no threshold. If that works, F3's form claim is false.

### F4: A composite "combination" that sums the measures is the named error

**Mode: `general`. Domain X1, all. Status: triangulated (Fig 12 and Fig 9, composition pricing vs
filters/witnesses, low overlap).**

> **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."** (Fig 12 :125)
> **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."** (Fig 12 :126)
> **"Neither figure predicts the other, so both are read or the choice is made blind."** (Fig 12 :129)
> **"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."** (Fig 12 :130)
> **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."** (Fig 9 :60)

Profit is priced in money over an accounting period. Resilience is priced in continued contact across
a shock. Stakeholder outcomes are priced in human judgment. These are different currencies. Fig 12
:130 is explicit: summing them is the error, not the fix. The appropriate form is *both are read, or
the choice is made blind* (Fig 12 :129). So a combination is legitimate only as a **set of
measures held separately with a declared scope**, never as one blended score. A blended score is also
a pooled reading (Fig 10 :26), the very failure that hides the live slot (Fig 1 :22).

The business pool supplies a precise corroboration. The balanced scorecard is criticised for exactly
this: *"the balanced scorecard does not provide an overall score or a unified view of performance with
clear recommendations: it is simply a list of metrics that managers have to interpret"* (Jensen 2001,
quoted in Wikipedia, Balanced scorecard, read 2026-11-22). It is one evidence pool, so it corroborates
the shape as one witness, not as an independent test.

**Refuter:** a composite with commensurable units and a declared exchange rate that pays at the ground,
such that a one-unit increase in any component always buys the same settlement of the same residual.
If such a composite exists and survives a ground test, the "do not add" rule has been defeated for
that case.

### F5: Measures that draw on the same fact are one witness, not several

**Mode: `general`. Domains D1, D3. Status: triangulated (Fig 13 and Fig 2, independence applied to a
set vs independence between frames, low overlap after the re-census noted in S1).**

> **"If two members drew on the same underlying fact, their agreement would carry no information, and one of them would be redundant rather than minimal."** (Fig 13 :38)
> **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."** (Fig 2 :31)
> **"Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."** (Fig 2 :31)
> **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"** (Fig 2 :40)

Profit, share price growth and an equity-based ESG score often read the same underlying corporate
fact: expected future cash flows under current disclosure. Their agreement is the pool talking. By Fig
2 :31, two people reading the same report are one frame; the point of independence is that agreement
means something only when the evidence draw is low. The practical consequence: a dashboard with five
measures can still be one witness. This is why the scorecard literature's bias toward *"financial
stakeholders over others"* (Wikipedia, Balanced scorecard, read 2026-11-22) is not cured by adding a
stakeholder box if the new box reads the same data.

**Refuter:** a demonstrated low overlap between two candidate measures, for example one derived from
realized cash flows and one from an independently collected worker-well-being survey, with no shared
data lineage and independently maintained instruments. Their agreement would then count as
triangulation and F5 would not apply to that pair.

### F6: Stakeholder outcomes are the case where human judgment is the anchor, not an instrument

**Mode: `specified`. Domain D3. Status: triangulated (Fig 2 and Fig 10, anchor legitimacy vs seat
rules, low overlap).**

> **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."** (Fig 2 :36)
> **"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."** (Fig 2 :37)
> **"It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."** (Fig 2 :37-38)

This is the figure that breaks the apparent symmetry among the candidates. For "is this business a
good place for people to work, or a good neighbour to this community," the question is constitutively
about people, so human judgment is not a soft adjunct to the hard financial metric. It is the right
anchor. For "did the firm realize a return," human judgment is only an instrument and can be wrong on
axes it does not measure. Profit can be exactly right on the profit axis and silent on the harm axis:
that is what "instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure" means. Fig 10 :37-38 names the
same structure as two locally correct frames with no shared reading, a disagreement nothing can state
until a convention makes it statable.

**Refuter:** a people-question settled correctly on every axis the people themselves care about by a
non-human metric alone, with no appeal to human judgment at any point. If such a case exists, Fig 2
:36-38 does not govern it as F6 claims.

### F7: "Who gets to decide" is the anchor and the seat, not a negotiation or a reputation

**Mode: `general` for the seat rule, `specified` for its business application. Domain D4. Status:
triangulated (Fig 2, Fig 9 and Fig 10, three distinct provisions).**

> **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."** (Fig 2 :35)
> **"Nobody chooses. The gap does, and the gap is only as good as its description."** (Fig 9 :53)
> **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"** (Fig 10 :47)
> **"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."** (Fig 10 :49)
> **"Who holds the seat is not chosen. The position selects the occupant."** (Fig 10 :85)
> **"Outside the slice being graded. Whoever is measured by a reading cannot own it."** (Fig 10 :86)
> **"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"** (Fig 10 :59)
> **"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."** (Fig 10 :93)

The "who" in the question is answered structurally, not by naming an institution. The residual selects
the ground (Fig 9 :53), and the ground selects who may read it: someone outside the slice being
graded, with no gain from a particular reading. A board that sets its own success metric and is then
rewarded on it is inside the slice. A rating agency whose revenue depends on the rating is a ground
with a reputation to protect (Fig 10 :93). A convention that becomes the decision is no longer a
convention (Fig 10 :47). And an undeclared stake is modelled anyway (Fig 10 :60), so the honest move
is to declare it, not to assert neutrality.

**Refuter:** a case where a negotiated convention legitimately settles a factual question about the
world, with no ground outside the negotiation to range against, and where the result is subsequently
confirmed by an independent instrument. That would show that negotiation can supply an anchor where
Fig 2 :35 says it cannot.

### F8: No metric has a standing valence across all residuals

**Mode: `general`. Domains D1 to D3. Status: triangulated (Fig 11 and Fig 2, habitat relations vs
instrument axes, low overlap).**

> **"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** (Fig 11 :49)
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."** (Fig 11 :50)
> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."** (Fig 11 :51)
> **"instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."** (Fig 2 :37-38)

Read a metric as an actor in the habitat of a decision. Its valence depends on which residual the
decision is about. Growth is a benefactor to a residual about market expansion and a malfactor to a
residual about resource limits; profit is a benefactor to a private-return residual and silent or
misleading on a residual about externalized harm. This is not a relativist claim that all metrics are
equal. It is the structural claim that the question's residual is what fixes which metric counts, so
there is no context-free "best measure." The balanced scorecard's history corroborates it at one
stratum: the four original perspectives were designed for US commercial organizations and were found
not so relevant to public sector and nonprofit organizations (Wikipedia, Balanced scorecard, read
2026-11-22). Same tool, different residual, changed valence.

**Refuter:** a metric whose validity and valence are invariant across a documented change of the
residual it is asked to settle, including a change that reverses what the decision is for. That metric
would be the universal measure the question asks after.

### F9: The union must be unbundled before a measurement system is chosen

**Mode: `general` with a `specified` business consequence. Domains all. Status: triangulated (Fig 9 and
the Library of Babel essay, but the essay is framework-internal, so one external witness).**

Fig 9 :54, :55 and :57 are quoted in section 1.3. The two conditions are C1 (which ground) and C2 (who
may range it). Their instruments differ:

- A C1 failure shows up as a metric that moves when pushed, an aggregate that hides a live slot, or a
  measure that is wrong on an axis the question cares about (Fig 2 :30, Fig 1 :22, Fig 2 :37-38).
- A C2 failure shows up as an interested decider, a convention that has become an outcome, or a
  rating whose keeper benefits from the rating (Fig 10 :47, :86, :93).

A single lever, for example "adopt one integrated success index," clears the region where only C1
holds and leaves the intersection standing, because C2 still binds (Fig 9 :55). This is the reason the
verdicts in `09_watson_answer.md` are kept separate per domain and never averaged.

**Refuter:** a single instrument that settles both conditions in both failure types and leaves no
categorically different residue. That would collapse the union into a partition and F9 would fail.

### F10: A measure that becomes a target stops being a measure

**Mode: `general`. Domains D1, D4. Status: triangulated (Fig 2, Fig 10 and two independent world
fields, Goodhart and Campbell, which are themselves one closely related pool).**

> **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"** (Fig 2 :43, Fig 10 :47)
> **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"** (Fig 10 :47)

The figures' rule has two independent corroborations from fields far from management. Goodhart's law
is stated as *"When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure"* (Strathern 1997, quoted
in Wikipedia, Goodhart's law, read 2026-11-22), and Goodhart's original version is *"Any observed
statistical regularity will tend to collapse once pressure is placed upon it for control purposes"*
(Goodhart 1975, same page). Campbell's law states: *"The more any quantitative social indicator is used
for social decision-making, the more subject it will be to corruption pressures and the more apt it
will be to distort and corrupt the social processes it is intended to monitor"* (Campbell 1979, quoted
in Wikipedia, Campbell's law, read 2026-11-22). These corroborate the figure rule at a different
stratum. They also share an evidence pool with each other, so they count as one corroborating witness,
not two.

The specified business consequence: any candidate measure that is made the target of rewards or
ratings changes the behaviour it was meant to read. That is a second reason, beside F2, why a metric
cannot be its own anchor once it is installed as the decision rule.

**Refuter:** a targeted measure that remains unbiased after the target is installed, demonstrated by a
prospective comparison in which the measured party's behaviour and the ground both stay fixed. Absent
that, the rule stands.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the design-instrument rule: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only
an honesty device."* The shape claim is refuted if any of the following is observed:

1. A candidate measure passes the anchor test: its value is invariant under reframing and timing
   choices available to the measured party while the ground is held fixed, and it still settles the
   question (breaks F1).
2. A composite with commensurable units and a declared exchange rate pays at the ground, so that
   summing the components is not the error Fig 12 :130 names (breaks F4).
3. Two candidate measures drawn from disjoint evidence and independently maintained agree, and their
   agreement is shown to be triangulation rather than a shared pool (bounds F5 rather than breaking it).
4. A people-question is settled correctly on every cared-about axis by a non-human metric with no
   appeal to human judgment (breaks F6).
5. A negotiated convention settles a factual world question with no external ground and is later
   confirmed by an independent instrument (breaks F7).
6. A metric's validity and valence are invariant across a documented change of residual that reverses
   what the decision is for (breaks F8).
7. A single instrument restores both C1 and C2 in both failure types with no categorically different
   residue (breaks F9).
8. The figures themselves fail: independence between two frames is shown to increase rather than
   decrease with shared evidence, or a convention that decides an outcome is shown to remain a
   convention.

No part of this solution may be upgraded to `grounded` until at least one of these has been run and
has failed to fire, and until a real ground has paid for the shape.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the residual and the refuter, not with a theory of measurement (Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a
   characterised residual, never the source"*).
2. Keep the two conditions separate in every report. A blended success index is a pooled reading
   (Fig 10 :26) and destroys the diagnostic.
3. State for every metric which ground it ranges against and which residual it is for. If neither can
   be said, the metric is frame-internal (Fig 1 :27) and carries no verdict weight.

## 7. Recursive derivation summary

1. **Is this one question?** No. Fig 9 :54, :55, :57 plus the Library of Babel bundle passage: it is a
   union of C1 (which ground) and C2 (who may range it), each with its own instrument.
2. **What is the anchor?** It depends on the residual. Fig 2 :29 names markets and patient outcomes as
   anchors; Fig 2 :36 makes human judgment legitimate when the question is constitutionally about
   people. Profit is not self-authorizing (Fig 1 :27).
3. **Can the metrics be added?** No. Fig 12 :125-130: two currencies do not add, and summing them is
   the error. Both are read or the choice is made blind.
4. **Do several metrics corroborate each other?** Only if they draw on different evidence (Fig 2 :31,
   :40; Fig 13 :38). Otherwise they are one witness wearing several names.
5. **Who decides?** The gap selects the ground (Fig 9 :53) and the position selects the occupant
   (Fig 10 :85). Whoever is measured cannot own the reading (Fig 10 :86); a keeper with a reputation
   to protect is disqualified (Fig 10 :93).
6. **Why does a measure fail after adoption?** Because it becomes a convention that decides an
   outcome (Fig 2 :43; Fig 10 :47), corroborated by Goodhart and Campbell from other fields.
7. **What can be said at the stop?** A shape: per-slot, ground-selected, comparison-based,
   non-additive, independence-tested, seat-constrained. Not which single metric wins. Fig 13 :134:
   *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is
   never a proof of absence."*

## 8. Updated verdict

The shape survives. **Business success is not best measured by one metric, and it is not measured by a
single blended score either. A candidate measure is legitimate only as an instrument ranged against a
ground selected by the residual: realized market outcome for private-return questions, continued
contact and renewal for habitat and time questions, and human judgment for questions constitutively
about people. The measures are held in different currencies and must be read separately, because
summing them is the named error and overlapping measures are one witness. The "who" is not a chosen
decider but a seat: the residual selects the ground, the position selects the occupant, and whoever is
measured by the reading or benefits from its value is disqualified.** The two conditions are kept
separate and never averaged. Which single measure wins, and which instrument would discriminate a
protected resource from a paying habitat from luck when all produce the same reading, is a `gap`, not
a hidden answer. Mode `specified`. No world source flipped a verdict; the one genuine pool overlap
(Goodhart and Campbell) was narrowed to one witness, and the one genuine strain (Fig 13) was returned
to the census. No unresolved strain remains after re-census.
