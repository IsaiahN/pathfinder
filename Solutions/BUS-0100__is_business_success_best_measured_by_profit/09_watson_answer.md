## QUESTION

Is "business success" best measured by profit, growth, resilience, innovation, stakeholder outcomes, or
some combination, and who gets to decide? Restated as an effect: which proposed reading o' is entitled
to settle R = |Γ(b,a) - o'| per firm-slot, and who may select it? R is indexed per decision unit, never
averaged (Fig 1 :19). Option A: one metric is best and a decider picks it, which leads to a frame
scoring itself with a quantity it produces (Fig 1 :27) and to a convention that decides an outcome
(Fig 2 :43). Option B: the residual selects the ground per domain, the measures are held in separate
currencies, no composite is summed, and the seat is the position rather than the person. The verdict
recorded below is Option B in `specified` mode.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

**The figure-derived verdict:** "business success" is not best measured by one listed metric and not by
a single blended score either. A measure is legitimate only as an instrument ranged against a ground
selected by the residual: realized market outcome for private-return questions, continued contact and
renewal for habitat and time questions, human judgment for questions constitutively about people. The
measures are in incommensurable currencies and must be read separately, because summing them is the
named error (Fig 12 :130) and overlapping measures are one witness (Fig 2 :31). "Who decides" is not a
chosen decider but a seat: the residual selects the ground, the position selects the occupant, whoever
is measured by the reading or benefits from its value is disqualified, and a measure that becomes a
target stops being a measure. The verdict is the union kept separate per domain, never averaged. Mode
`specified`. Which single measure wins, and which instrument would discriminate sources behind one
score, is a declared `gap`.

- **Cost if wrong:** a firm or regulator adopts one headline score, the score becomes a target, and the
  behaviour it was meant to read is corrupted (Fig 2 :43). Alternatively, a people-question is decided
  by a financial instrument that is wrong on the very axes the question cares about (Fig 2 :37-38),
  or an interested rater owns the deciding seat (Fig 10 :93). The cost is real, and it is not easily
  visible from inside the frame that produced the score.
- **Undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails; a refutation is added and the reach falls
  because *"something was added rather than because anything was deleted"* (Fig 6 :42-43). The shape
  and its six falsifiers are recorded in a provenance trail so a later reader can locate the error
  (Fig 10 :62-64). A metric installed as a target is harder to undo, which is why the seat rule comes
  before adoption.
- **Reversed by:** any of the six pre-registered falsifiers, in particular (1) a reframing-invariant
  measure that still settles the question, (2) a commensurable composite that pays at the ground, (3)
  a people-question settled by a non-human metric alone, (4) a negotiated convention settling a factual
  world question and later confirmed, (5) a metric whose valence survives a residual reversal, or (6)
  one instrument restoring both conditions with no categorically different residue. A source with a
  genuinely different evidence pool showing that all three grounds reduce to one underlying fact with
  no loss would also reverse it.
