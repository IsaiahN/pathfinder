# Expert verification packet - the Tether on measuring business success

**What this is:** a claim in a form a performance-measurement researcher, management accountant or
empirically minded executive can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the
conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No prospective measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a
route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the
figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

**Boundary of the check.** The first three sections are the whole stage-1 ask. Provenance, links and
the method offer come later and only if a verdict on stage 1 is recorded first.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Business success best measured by profit, growth, resilience, innovation, stakeholder outcomes, or
some combination" is not a question with one metric as its answer. A measure is legitimate only when it
is ranged against a ground that the question itself selects: realized market outcome for
private-return questions, continued contact and renewal for habitat and time questions, and human
judgment for questions constitutively about people. The candidate measures are in different
currencies, so a single blended score is not a deeper measure but the error; they must be read
separately, and several that read the same underlying fact count as one witness. On "who decides," the
residual selects the ground rather than a person, the position selects the occupant of the deciding
seat, and whoever is measured by the reading or gains from a particular value of it is disqualified.
A measure that becomes a target stops being a measure. The claim offers a shape any candidate answer
must have, six falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. It does not name
which measure wins.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 2 and 12

- **Statement:** a proposed measure is not automatically the anchor; an anchor must not move when the
  measured party pushes on it; "best" is a threshold comparison, and a composite sum of
  incommensurable currencies is the named error.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 2 :29 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of
    what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."*
  - Fig 2 :30 - *"you push on it, it is not an anchor."*
  - Fig 1 :27 - *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also
    produces."*
  - Fig 12 :112 - *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."*
  - Fig 12 :125 - *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*
  - Fig 12 :130 - *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."*
- **Refuter:** a measure whose value is invariant under all reframing and timing choices available to
  the measured party while the ground is held fixed and which still settles the question; or a
  commensurable composite with a declared exchange rate that pays at the ground. Either fires against
  C1.

### C2 - Framework-internal plus one performance-measurement check

- **Statement:** where the question is constitutively about people, human judgment is the right anchor,
  not an instrument; two measures that draw on the same fact are one witness; a convention that
  decides an outcome has stopped being one.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 2 :36 - *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."*
  - Fig 2 :37 - *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because
    the question is about people."*
  - Fig 2 :37-38 - *"It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on
    axes they do not measure."*
  - Fig 2 :31 - *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* and
    *"Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing
    are."*
  - Fig 13 :38 - *"If two members drew on the same underlying fact, their agreement would carry no
    information, and one of them would be redundant rather than minimal."*
  - Fig 10 :47 - *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*
- **What a researcher can check against their own knowledge:** the balanced scorecard literature
  records the design question *"Of all the measures you could have chosen, why did you choose these?"*
  and criticises the framework for a *"lack of an overall score"* (Wikipedia, Balanced scorecard,
  quoting Jensen 2001, read 2026-11-22). Is that not exactly C1's complaint, and does adding a
  stakeholder perspective to the same data supply a second witness or only a second label? A "no" to
  both questions is a real refutation of the mapping.
- **Refuter:** a people-question settled correctly on every cared-about axis by a non-human metric
  alone with no appeal to human judgment; or a demonstrated low-overlap pair of measures with
  independent instruments whose agreement survives a ground test. The first breaks the anchor claim;
  the second bounds the one-witness claim.

### C3 - The deciding seat and the failure after adoption

- **Statement:** the residual selects the ground, the position selects the occupant, anyone measured by
  the reading cannot own it, and a keeper with a reputation to protect is disqualified. Once installed
  as a target, a measure changes what it was meant to read.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 9 :53 - *"Nobody chooses. The gap does, and the gap is only as good as its description."*
  - Fig 10 :85 - *"Who holds the seat is not chosen. The position selects the occupant."*
  - Fig 10 :86 - *"Outside the slice being graded. Whoever is measured by a reading cannot own it."*
  - Fig 10 :93 - *"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its
    keeper is thereby disqualified."*
  - Fig 2 :43 - *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*
  - Fig 11 :51 - *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*
- **What a researcher can check:** Goodhart's law, *"When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a
  good measure"* (Strathern 1997, Wikipedia, read 2026-11-22), and Campbell's law, *"The more any
  quantitative social indicator is used for social decision-making, the more subject it will be to
  corruption pressures..."* (Campbell 1979, Wikipedia, read 2026-11-22), are the same rule from two
  fields outside management. They corroborate the figure but share a measurement-gaming pool with each
  other, so they are one witness, not two.
- **Refuter:** a negotiated convention that settles a factual world question with no external ground
  and is later confirmed by an independent instrument; or a metric whose validity and valence survive
  a documented residual change that reverses the decision; or one instrument that restores both
  conditions in both failure types with no categorically different residue.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not `grounded`. No measurement has paid for any part of this.
- Not a theory of everything about business, and no final or unique theory claim.
- Not an average. No claim about the percentage of performance attributable to firm, industry, year or
  strategy.
- Not that profit, growth, resilience, innovation, stakeholder outcomes or any index are irrelevant.
  They are Γ, candidate instruments whose ground and scope must be declared.
- Not that a composite index is never useful. It is useful as a budget or communication filter
  (Fig 9 :60), not as a witness for the verdict.
- Not that stakeholder judgment is a tie-breaker. Where the question is about people it is the anchor;
  where it is not, it is an instrument that can be wrong on unmeasured axes.
- Not that the management literature independently corroborates this. Goodhart and Campbell share one
  pool; the scorecard and AOM material share another. Arrow's theorem and the bioindicator literature
  corroborate the shape from low-overlap fields, not the business fact.
- Not that this packet, produced with LLM assistance, is submittable or publishable as authored work.
  See section 6.

## 5. What would settle it

A prospective study that states the residual in effect terms first, names the ground for each measure,
records arrangements as well as readings, excludes interested parties from the deciding seat, and
reports which condition failed before a decline or a rating change. Concretely:

1. **Anchor test:** does the measure move when the measured party is free to reframe, reclassify, time
   or disclose differently while the ground is held fixed? If yes, it is an instrument, not an anchor.
2. **Currency test:** can the candidate measures be added without a declared exchange rate that pays
   at the ground? If not, they must be read separately (Fig 12 :125-130).
3. **Independence test:** do the measures draw on disjoint evidence with independently maintained
   instruments? If not, their agreement is one witness (Fig 2 :31).
4. **People test:** is the question constitutively about people? If yes, human judgment is the anchor
   (Fig 2 :36-38).
5. **Seat test:** is the decider outside the slice being graded, with no gain from a particular reading
   and no reputation to protect (Fig 10 :86, :93)?
6. **Source-discriminating instrument:** can the study distinguish genuine value creation from a
   paying habitat from an accounting choice from luck when all produce the same score? The source essay
   says a reading alone *"cannot comment on the source"*.

## 6. Submission and AI-policy caveat

The AOM Publishing page states: *"Artificial Intelligence: Work submitted to AOM must be created by the
authors and not the product of artificial intelligence tools unless appropriate to the research
question and properly cited."* The Journal of Business Ethics submission guidelines state: *"Large
Language Models (LLMs), such as ChatGPT, do not currently satisfy our authorship criteria."* This
packet is an LLM-assisted simulated derivation. Do not submit it as authored work. Use it privately as
a checking instrument; a human researcher who runs the check owns and writes the resulting article,
with disclosure as required by the venue's policy. The Registered Reports format is the best fit for a
human-authored version, because it evaluates the measurement plan without the results.

## 7. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29-30, :31, :36-38, :43; Fig 1 :27; Fig 12 :112, :125, :130; Fig 9 :53; Fig 10 :47,
   :85, :86, :93; Fig 11 :51 and Fig 13 :38. About ten short passages, roughly ten minutes.
2. Check C2 against your own knowledge of performance measurement: is there a documented case where
   adding a stakeholder perspective to the same data produced a genuinely independent witness rather
   than a second label? About four minutes.
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish value creation from rent extraction from luck in one
   profit reading, or say why none exists. About four minutes.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
