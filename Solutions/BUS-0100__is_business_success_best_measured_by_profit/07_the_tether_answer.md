# The Tether's answer on how business success should be measured

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation
checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading. No measurement
pays for any claim here.

## 1. The residual it answers

An answer would say which reading is entitled to settle the gap between what a firm's measures predict
and what the ground returns, and on what authority. R = |Γ(b,a) - o'| is indexed per firm or decision
unit, never averaged (Fig 1 :19, :22). The question is about **ground selection and authority**, not
about which number is biggest.

The union must be unbundled before it is searched (Fig 9 :54). It is two conditions, not one hard
question:

- **C1:** which ground is being ranged against (market, habitat across time, or human judgment about
  people).
- **C2:** who may range it (the seat that is outside the slice being graded and gains nothing from a
  particular reading).

## 2. The shape

1. **No listed metric is the anchor by itself.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the
   question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :30: *"you push on
   it, it is not an anchor."* A score that moves with accounting choice, disclosure choice or timing
   has failed the test. Fig 1 :27: *"A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*
2. **"Best" is a comparison, not a property of a number.** Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal
   is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* A vocabulary of metrics without a comparison term can
   say what a situation contains and cannot say what counts as getting somewhere (Fig 12 :111-113).
3. **Different grounds for different residuals.** Market return and survival for private-return
   questions (Fig 2 :29 names *"a market"*); continued contact and renewal for habitat and time
   questions (Fig 10 :24, Fig 11 :39); human judgment for questions constitutively about people
   (Fig 2 :36-38). This is not a ranking of the three. It is a selection rule driven by what the
   residual is about.
4. **The measures must be read separately, not added.** Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two
   currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 12 :130: *"Summing them into one figure is the error this
   names, not the fix for it."* A blended success score is also a pooled reading (Fig 10 :26) that
   hides the live slot (Fig 1 :22). A combination is legitimate as a set of measures with
   declared scopes, never as one number.
5. **Several measures are usually one witness.** Fig 13 :38: *"If two members drew on the same
   underlying fact, their agreement would carry no information, and one of them would be redundant
   rather than minimal."* Fig 2 :31: *"Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two
   instruments pointed at the same thing are."* A dashboard with five measures can still be one
   witness. Independence is bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42).
6. **The "who" is a seat, not a chosen decider.** Fig 9 :53: *"Nobody chooses. The gap does, and the gap
   is only as good as its description."* Fig 10 :85: *"Who holds the seat is not chosen. The position
   selects the occupant."* Fig 10 :86: *"Outside the slice being graded. Whoever is measured by a
   reading cannot own it."* A board that sets and is rewarded on its own metric is inside the slice;
   a rater paid by the rated is a ground with a reputation to protect (Fig 10 :93). Stakes that cannot
   be removed must be declared (Fig 10 :59-60).
7. **A measure fails after adoption because it becomes an outcome.** Fig 2 :43 and Fig 10 :47:
   *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* The Goodhart and Campbell literature
   corroborates this from economics and evaluation research, as one closely related witness pool.
8. **No metric has context-free valence.** Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a
   change of residual."* Growth is a benefactor to an expansion residual and a malfactor to a resource
   residual; profit is right on return and silent on harm (Fig 2 :37-38). The question's residual fixes
   which metric counts.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not `grounded`. No measurement has paid for this shape.
- Not a name. It does not say profit is best, or growth, or stakeholder outcomes, or any index. Fig 9
  :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- Not an average. It makes no claim about what percentage of performance variance is "firm effect" or
  "industry effect." Such numbers are filters for budget, not witnesses (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that the listed metrics are irrelevant. They are Γ, candidate instruments whose reach and
  ground must be declared. The claim is that Γ alone does not settle "best."
- Not that stakeholder judgment is a soft extra. Where the question is about people, it is the anchor
  (Fig 2 :36-38).
- Not corroboration by agreement. Goodhart and Campbell share a pool; the management and scorecard
  sources share another. The low-overlap sources (Arrow's theorem, bioindicators) corroborate the
  shape, not a business fact.
- Not a theory of everything about business. It stops where the instrument stops.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. The source essay's metamerism rule: a
reading of green *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"the answer is unreached until a better
instrument exists."* Here the readings are profit, growth, resilience scores and stakeholder scores;
the candidate sources are protected resources, paying habitats, accounting or disclosure choices and
luck.

The falsifiers, pre-registered in `01` and repeated in `08`:

1. a measure invariant under reframing and timing choices while the ground is held fixed, which still
   settles the question;
2. a commensurable composite with a declared exchange rate that pays at the ground;
3. a people-question settled on every cared-about axis by a non-human metric alone;
4. a negotiated convention settling a factual world question with no external ground, later confirmed
   by an independent instrument;
5. a metric whose validity and valence survive a residual change that reverses the decision;
6. one instrument that restores both C1 and C2 with no categorically different residue.

A prospective study that states the residual first, names the ground for each measure, records the
bonds as well as the readings, excludes interested parties from the deciding seat, and reports which
condition failed would turn this from `specified` toward `grounded`. The Registered Reports format is
the closest existing venue design, because it evaluates the measurement plan without the results.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and Fig 8
:39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The shape stops at the limit of the
available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. The missing thing is a
discriminator of sources, characterized in effect terms but not held. Naming a winner from a reading
would be the metamerism error the source essay forbids. The eight points in section 2 are a shape; the
ninth thing, which single metric actually wins and who is entitled to impose it, is not held and is not
pretended.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not rank the metrics and does not name a winner. It says that "best measured by" is a
> category error if it asks for one number. A measure is legitimate only when it is ranged against a
> ground that the question itself selects: the market for realized return, the habitat across time for
> continued contact and renewal, and human judgment for questions that are constitutively about
> people. Those measures are in different currencies, so a single blended score is not deeper, it is
> the error; they have to be read separately, and several of them usually read the same fact and count
> as one witness. On "who decides," the residual selects the ground rather than a person choosing it,
> and whoever is measured by the reading or benefits from its value is out of the deciding seat. A
> metric that becomes the target stops being a measure. What is offered is that shape, six falsifiers
> written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. Which particular measure occupies the
> slot stays open until a source-discriminating instrument exists, and the stop is recorded rather
> than filled.
