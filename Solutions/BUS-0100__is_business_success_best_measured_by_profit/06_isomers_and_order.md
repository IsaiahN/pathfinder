## Q1 - Are two firms with the same scores isomers?

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** (Fig 12 :117)
> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** (Fig 12 :124)
> **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."** (Fig 12 :5)

Two firms reporting identical profit and growth have the same readings. If the arrangements that
produced them differ (one composed as a repeated exploitation of a stable habitat, the other as a
reconfigurable set of capabilities renewed by contact with differing closures), they are **isomers**
under Fig 12 :117: same reported atoms, different bonds, different persistence, with the difference
invisible in the scorecard and decisive in the behaviour. This is the figure-grounded version of the
intuition that the same profit can mean different things, but it is stronger: the arrangement is
itself a substance and carries information the readings do not.

If the firms share only a pattern (both use `action → market reading → reward`) while differing in
which atoms occupy the slots, the weaker claim holds: they are members of the same **functional
group** (Fig 12 :124), the qualifier saying which member. The presence of the pattern fixes the kind,
not the outcome.

The distinction is testable. A scorecard comparison that finds no difference between two firms cannot
distinguish isomer from functional-group; the bond has to be recorded. This is the metamerism point of
`05` section 4 stated compositionally: a reading records ingredients, not the source or arrangement.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two firms with identical reported scores and identical
arrangements nevertheless diverge in persistence with no difference in ground contact or imported
atoms, the isomer framing is insufficient and the difference must be habitat (D2), which would move the
explanation out of composition.

**Status:** `specified`, triangulated for the legality of the reading (Fig 12 and Fig 11 :50, different
figures), single-source for the business application. The world corroboration is the balanced
scorecard's own observation that the four original perspectives were not so relevant to public sector
and nonprofit organizations, which is a change of arrangement under a change of residual, all from one
management evidence pool.

## Q2 - What order do the conditions bind in?

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."** (Fig 3 :20-21)
> **"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."** (Fig 3 :26)
> **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."** (Fig 12 :112)

Order when a firm or regulator installs a measurement system:

1. **Perception.** Can the system read the market or stakeholder response at all? Failure here is
   visible and has obvious tests.
2. **Vocabulary.** Does its Γ have terms for what the changed situation affords, and can it say what
   would count as progress there? This is the middle link. It is where "what counts as success" lives,
   and it has the fewest instruments.
3. **Objective.** Can it state a comparison that would count as getting somewhere? A vocabulary with
   only ingredient terms cannot state a goal (Fig 12 :111-113).
4. **Planning.** A route to that objective under incomplete information, including which measures to
   read separately.
5. **Learn and carry.** Name what worked, verify it against the ground, carry it to the next problem.

The two conditions map onto this chain: C1 (which ground) is mostly a failure of links 1 and 2; C2 (who
may range it) is mostly a failure of link 3, because a decider is chosen by prestige or convenience
and the comparison is never stated. That mapping is why a single lever cannot clear both (Fig 9 :55).

The order also decides which instrument to buy first. Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a
link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Links 1 and 4 have mature instruments: financial
reporting, surveys, dashboards. Link 2 and 3 have almost none: what the situation affords and what
would count as progress. So budget naturally piles up where the measurements already exist while the
break sits in the middle.

**Falsifier:** a measurement system whose perception, vocabulary and objective links all measure as
working and whose failure is located at planning. That would falsify the "usually middle" claim of F8
in the primary audit, though not the dependency ordering.

## Q3 - Competing explanations, and what each can and cannot reach

| candidate explanation | what it names | figure-line bearing | what it cannot reach |

No candidate is discarded by prestige or familiarity. Each is ranged against the same residual, in the
order Fig 8 :53 requires. The first six are largely one management evidence pool; Arrow and the
bioindicator result are low-overlap corroborations of the shape, not of any business fact.

## Q4 - Does the shape generate the literature's answers?

Partly. The shape can generate:

- the profit emphasis, as the D1 ground for a private-return residual, with the self-scoring caveat;
- the stakeholder emphasis, as the D3 ground where the question is constitutively about people;
- the multi-perspective scorecard, as Fig 12 :129 (read separately, no single overall score);
- the integrated-index impulse, as a desired composite that the figure refuses, which is itself an
  explanation of why those indexes keep failing to settle anything;
- the rating-industry critique, as Fig 10 :47 and :93;
- the Goodhart and Campbell failure modes, as the fate of any measure once installed as a target.

It cannot generate the *name* of the single measure that wins, nor the instrument that would separate
value creation from rent extraction from luck when all three produce the same score. That is the
`gap` recorded in F11 of `01`. It is a gap at the instrument boundary, not a silence: Fig 13 :134
(*"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*) and Fig 8 :39 apply. The essay's source rule states
why: an instrument that reads a result *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"the answer is unreached
until a better instrument exists"*.

## CONCLUSION

Two firms with the same reported scores but different arrangements are isomers under Fig 12 :117; two
firms sharing only an `action to reading to reward` pattern are members of one functional group under
Fig 12 :124. The conditions bind in dependency order, with the vocabulary/objective links and the
deciding-seat link the usual breaks, which is why C1 and C2 need different instruments. The standard
business explanations are mostly bindings of one evidence pool and are generated by the shape only up
to the point where a name would be required. The isomer claim has a written falsifier; the order claim
has one; the naming gap is declared rather than filled.
