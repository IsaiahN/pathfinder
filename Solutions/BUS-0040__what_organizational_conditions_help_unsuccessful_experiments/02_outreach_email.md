# Outreach draft - check the shape before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three conditions and the five refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are stated on the page exactly as written there. A recipient must be able to try to break the claim without first accepting a framework.

---

**Subject:** Three conditions for learning from a failed experiment, with the refuters written in advance

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific shape I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the shape does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a team makes a bet, the world returns an observation, and the gap between them belongs to that experiment slot, not to a portfolio average. The question is where that gap goes next. It can become a reusable method (learning), it can be aimed at a person so the report stops tracking the world (blame), or it can be dropped into an average so nothing is carried forward (waste).
>
> **The shape claim:** all three of the following have to hold for the first route, and they are a union, not a list of alternatives.
>
> 1. The channel from the experiment to the people who can act stays reachable. Punishment that changes what gets reported moves the anchor, and then the organization has lost touch rather than received a verdict.
> 2. The vocabulary can say that a held belief was wrong, not only that something was missing. And the refusal has to be aimed at the belief, not at the person or at the outcome nobody wanted. A postmortem that only adds items can accumulate without ever correcting.
> 3. The review frame differs from the frame that authored the failed bet. A room where everyone shares one evidence pool produces agreement, not corroboration.
>
> **What this is not claiming:** that a psychological safety score is the cause or the measure of learning. Such a score is a reading produced inside the frame it grades, so it is a prompt for inquiry, not a verdict. It is one factor in a product. Nor is this an argument for "no accountability at all": a rule nobody can satisfy is stated wrong. The claim is that the channel must stay reachable and that any stake which cannot be removed must be declared.
>
> **What would refute the shape (written before this message):**
> 1. Reliable learning with a demonstrably closed reporting channel, where the recorded incident rate moves with the punishment policy and independent measurement of the same events does not.
> 2. A team that records no held belief as false, only missing or added items, whose failure residual still declines on novel experiments judged by a ground the team cannot move.
> 3. A homogeneous closure with no imports and no new contact that closes genuinely novel failure residuals over multiple cycles.
> 4. One intervention that restores all three conditions with no categorically different residue after it is removed.
> 5. A self-report safety score that predicts learning at maximum independence from two frames with zero shared evidence.
>
> If any one holds, I will narrow or withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`. Read Fig 1 :19, Fig 5 :53, Fig 5 :62-64, Fig 10 :24-29, Fig 11 :39-40.
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`.
> - The eight-step loop (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a failed experiment you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [your name]

## Distribution discipline

- Send to one person at a time, or through a public submission portal. No lists, no automation, no misleading subject line.
- Record the stage-1 verdict before showing any theory. Once a recipient has absorbed the framework, their agreement is no longer evidence (Fig 2 :40, Fig 10 :42).
- Do not pool later agreement as corroboration. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
- Practical constraint: the Academy of Management states that submitted work must be created by the authors and not be a product of AI tools unless appropriate to the research question and properly cited. This package is a simulated derivation. Check the current policy of any venue before submitting, and treat the constraint as a reason to offer the packet for checking, not as a claim to publication.
