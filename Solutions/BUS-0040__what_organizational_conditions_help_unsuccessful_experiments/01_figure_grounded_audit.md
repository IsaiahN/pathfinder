## 0. The claim under audit, stated as a residual

The question asks for organizational conditions. The first discipline is to refuse the noun phrase "a learning culture" as a free-floating property and restate the effect.

An experiment is a bet Γ(b,a). The world returns o′. R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| is the gap between prediction and observation, and it is indexed per experiment slot (Fig 1 :11, Fig 1 :19). A failed experiment is simply a slot where R has live mass. The question is not about R1 (the experiment's own error) but about a second residual R2: the difference between the term the failure could have produced and the term actually carried into Γ. Three routes are possible in effect terms:

- **Route L (learning):** a term φ is minted, settles against the ground, and becomes composable at the next experiment (Formula steps 3 to 5).
- **Route B (blame):** the refusal is aimed at a person or at an unwanted outcome instead of at the belief that was wrong (Fig 5 :62-64). The anchor moves when pushed, so the signal stops arriving (Fig 10 :24-29).
- **Route W (waste):** the residual is dropped as a transient or pooled into an average; nothing is minted and Γ is unchanged (Fig 5 :41).

The sought-for shape, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): *the three conditions that must all hold for R2 to fall, plus an instrument that discriminates a channel failure from a refusal failure from a closure failure.* Until it is bound it predicts nothing, and no check in the loop can see it. The question's direction is not itself a term the loop holds, so this binding is the discipline that makes the claim refusable.

What an answer would NOT look like: a named best practice (a particular postmortem template, a particular survey, a particular "no-blame" policy). Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*

## 1. Domain census, before any figure line is chosen

**R by arity, symmetry and scale.** Arity: three-way route (L, B, W), which is a **union of conditions**, not a single hard question (Fig 9 :54-57). Symmetry: asymmetric. Minting, refusing and punishing are ordered operations; Fig 12 :70 separates ordered bonds from symmetric ones, and Fig 5 :53 gives refusal its own sign. Scale: per experiment slot (Fig 1 :19), then the team / population membrane (Fig 4), then the time channel across incidents (Fig 10 :24).

**One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor.** A domain is admitted only if it has a nameable anchor that does not move when a frame pushes on it (Fig 2 :29-30).

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 The experiment loop | the bet, the observation o′, the residual R1 | prediction error per slot | the measurement, the experiment (Fig 2 :29) | admitted |
| D2 The reporting channel | the reporter, the leader, the reporting system, the listener | a signal about R1 reaching the seat | the ground must stay reachable (Fig 10 :29) | admitted |
| D3 The sensemaking loop | Γ, atoms, bonds, the team vocabulary, the postmortem | whether a term is minted, settled, composable | settlement against the ground (Formula step 5) | admitted |
| D4 The habitat | cross-functional frames, outside closures, incentives, time, leadership | contact and independence between frames | the ground via contact (Fig 11 :39-40) | admitted |
| D5 The verification frame | expert readers, this second seat | agreement and residual | the ground, never agreement | admitted, independence zero in this simulation |
| X1 "a learning culture" as a property of the organization alone | the organization | a survey score | none that does not move when pushed | not a domain yet, absorbed into D4 |
| X2 "blame is always bad" | the manager | stated policy | none; an absolute prohibition nobody can meet | absorbed into D2 after re-census (Fig 10 :53-56) |

**Partition or union: union.** Route L requires at least three conditions that overlap: C1 channel to ground reachable; C2 the vocabulary can refuse a held belief and bind the sought-for shape; C3 contact with a differing closure. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* A single lever (for example, declaring "failure is fine") clears the region where only C1 binds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :57).

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Perception (the failure is visible) → vocabulary (terms for what it affords and what would count as learning) → objective (what counts as progress) → planning (a route) → learn and carry. The break is usually between perception and objective: Fig 3 :25-27 says this link has neither obvious failures nor obvious tests, and *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

## 3. Findings F1 to F10

### F1 - Keep the experiment residual per slot and as an anchor (decisive for D1)

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."** Fig 2 :30: **"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."**

A failed experiment is a slot with positive mass. Two common organizational moves destroy it: pooling it into a portfolio average (waste) and punishing the reporter so the measured value changes with what the frame thinks (blame). The ground here is the experiment outcome, and it is legitimate because the question is constitutively about it (Fig 2 :36).

**Refuter:** a team whose experiment residuals are aggregated into a single annual average, and whose reported incident count changes when the reporting policy changes, yet whose next-experiment prediction error declines at maximum independence. If that holds, per-slot anchoring is not necessary.

### F2 - Blame is a moved anchor and a decaying channel (D2)

Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**; Fig 10 :26: **"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"**; Fig 10 :28: **"you have lost touch"**; Fig 10 :29: **"keep the anchor reachable."** Fig 10 :59: **"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."** Fig 10 :60: **"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it."**

Blame is not a moral category here, it is a structural one. Punishing a reporter gives them an interest in the reading, which disqualifies their report. It also moves the apparent anchor, which turns panel 3 of Fig 2 into panel 1: *"the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices"* (Fig 2 :34). The world literature's "just culture" and "psychological safety" are bindings onto this condition, not the condition itself.

**Refuter:** a unit where reporters are demonstrably punished for disclosures, where the recorded incident rate therefore falls, and where independent measurement of the same underlying events shows a matching fall in real failures. If the ground and the report move together, the anchor was not moved by punishment.

### F3 - A failed experiment owes a refusal, not just an explanation (D3)

Fig 5 :53: **"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"**; Fig 5 :55: **"What it removes is not a term but a term's standing to be composed over. Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach."** Operators table :58: **"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."** Operators :73-74: **"A vocabulary with − and without ¬ can say what is missing from an arrangement and cannot say that an arrangement is wrong, so it can only ever accumulate."**

Most "lessons learned" artifacts carry only subtraction: what was missing, what should have been done, what to add. A lesson that the team's governing belief was wrong requires negation. This is the sharpest figure-grounded distinction between learning and waste, and it is *not* the same as psychological safety. A team can be safe to speak and still lack the operator to say a held belief is false.

**Refuter:** a postmortem archive in which no held belief is ever recorded as false, only items recorded as missing or added, while the team's failure residual declines on independent novel experiments over two budget cycles. If it declines, negation is not necessary for learning here.

### F4 - Blame is aiming the refusal at the wrong operand (D3, the mechanism of route B)

Fig 5 :62-64: **"Every check above runs on the MECHANISM and none runs on the DIRECTION OF THE QUESTION - so a frame can refuse the right kind of thing and still aim the refusal at the outcome it did not want rather than at the belief it holds."** Fig 5 :65-66: **"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."**

This is the framework's own definition of the blame failure, and it is more precise than the everyday one. A team that refuses the *person* or the *unwanted result* has performed a refusal, satisfied the mechanism, and still learned nothing, because the wrong term lost its standing. The cure is not a feeling of safety, it is a discipline: write down in advance what would count as an error in the governing belief, so that the target of the refusal is bound before the failure arrives. Formula, "Direction": committing a prediction before a run is this, already working.

**Refuter:** a team that, after a failed experiment, reliably refuses the responsible individual's competence and never records a changed belief, yet whose subsequent experiments show a declining residual against a ground the team cannot move. If so, the direction of the refusal is irrelevant to learning.

### F5 - A postmortem that carries up a recording is not learning (D4, cross-scale)

Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** Fig 4 :25: **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."** Fig 4 :30: **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."**

An after-action review that produces a vivid account of one occasion has produced a recording. Learning requires a method that can be reapplied at the next scale, and a round-trip check that measures what the compression dropped. The artifact to test is not whether a report was written but whether a successor team, given only the carried-up method, can avoid the same class of failure without replaying the story.

**Refuter:** an organization whose postmortems are purely narrative, with no reusable method and no round-trip measurement, yet whose successors avoid the same failure class at a rate that beats a control with independent ground contact. If so, recording is sufficient.

### F6 - Guards are a product, and safety alone is not sufficient (D2 and D3)

Fig 5 :33: **"The three guards are a product, not a checklist."** Fig 5 :41: **"Any one of them at zero stops the mint entirely. Which is why a system that is minting nothing may have nothing wrong with its minting."** Fig 5 :20-26 names the guards: *"is there anything to explain?"*, *"can we even build this term?"*, *"is it actually new?"*

Mapping the world literature honestly: psychological safety corresponds to the first guard's precondition, live mass reaching the seat. It is necessary and not sufficient. A team can have high safety and still fail the second guard (no terms for what the failure affords, Fig 3 :7) or the third guard (the "lesson" is already in the library, Fig 5 :24-25). This is where the dominant single-factor story overreaches.

**Refuter:** a team with very high measured safety that repeatedly mints nothing from large, novel residuals while an equally safe team with a richer vocabulary and outside contact does. That confirms the product reading. The refusal would be the opposite: a team with one guard measurably at zero that still learns reliably.

### F7 - A room's agreement is not a verdict (D4, verification)

Fig 7 :13: **"agreement compounds, evidence does not"**; Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."** Fig 7 :36: **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."** Fig 2 :40: **"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."**

A postmortem meeting whose participants share the same department, the same training and the same incident report is one frame wearing several names. Its unanimous conclusion is a convention, not corroboration. The next "room" (the next review, the next auditor, the next discipline) must differ from the last and be selected against the residual the last one surfaced.

**Refuter:** a single homogeneous group, with no outside frame at any step, that reliably produces correct refusals on novel failures judged by an independent ground. If so, differing closures are not necessary.

### F8 - Novel failures need a differing closure and an enumerated habitat (D4)

Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."** Fig 8 :45: **"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."** Fig 11 :39: **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."** Fig 11 :40: **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."** Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**

When the failed experiment's residual needs a primitive the team does not hold, cross-functional or outside frames are not a diversity slogan, they are the import operator (Fig 6 :14, *"the only operator that moves the wall"*). And the conditions around the experiment (incentives, workload, status gradients, time to analyze) are read off the world, not composed from a list of virtues. This is why a "blame the person" intervention is an agent-only fix: it leaves contact unchanged.

**Refuter:** a homogeneous closure with no imports and no new contact that reliably closes residuals from genuinely novel failures over multiple budget cycles. If so, closure is not a barrier here.

### F9 - The three conditions are a union, so one lever cannot clear it (method)

Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."** Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

A survey score, a postmortem template and a cross-functional review are each a lever for one region. Applying the same lever to all three conditions is the single-lever error. Scores and survey results are filters for where to spend effort; they are not witnesses for whether learning occurred.

**Refuter:** one intervention that restores all three conditions with no categorically different residue after it is removed. If so, the union reading is wrong and it is one condition after all.

### F10 - Which specific practice wins is a `gap`, not a hidden answer (scope)

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."** Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."** Fig 1 :24-27: **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."**

After a genuine census, no figure line selects among blameless postmortems, just culture, after-action reviews, registered reports or psychological-safety training. That is a declared `gap`. The missing thing is a source-discriminating instrument, characterized in effect terms (does the report track the ground? does the lesson carry negation? does a successor avoid the class?) but not held. The same rule disqualifies a self-report safety score as the *verdict*: it is a channel reading, frame-internal, and a frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces.

**Refuter:** a genuinely different evidence pool showing that one named practice, measured by a ground that cannot be moved by the people measured, fully predicts learning across all three conditions. That would close the gap.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach, per the seat rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is C1 + C2 + C3 (channel reachable, refusal available and aimed at the belief, contact with a differing closure). It is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A unit reliably converts failed experiments into reusable methods with a declining residual while its reporting channel is demonstrably closed (reporters punished, ground moved) → C1 not necessary.
2. A unit whose vocabulary has no negation-like operator, recording only what is missing and never that a belief was false, still shows a declining residual on novel experiments → C2 not necessary.
3. A homogeneous closure with no imports and no new contact reliably closes residuals from genuinely novel failures over multiple cycles → C3 not necessary.
4. One intervention restores all three conditions with no categorically different residue → union reading false.
5. A self-reported safety score predicts the learning outcome at maximum independence from two frames with zero shared evidence, so the self-report is the ground rather than a channel reading → the re-map in F10 fails.

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

**Conflict rule applied before the verdict.** The business evidence pool largely converges on psychological safety as *the* condition. A direct conflict exists inside that pool: Eldor, Hodor and Cappelli (2023) claim high safety can harm routine-task performance, and the Field Guide carries a point-by-point critique showing the downturn is not established. Per the WATSON rule, a conflict sends us back to the census, it never flips the verdict. Re-census result: psychological safety is re-mapped from "the ground / the verdict" to "the channel condition C1, read by a fallible instrument." That re-map is recorded in the retraction log below. The claim that safety is *sufficient* is refused; the claim that the channel must stay reachable survives.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the residual and its three routes, not with a theory of culture. The failed experiment is the anchor (F1).
2. Treat blame as a structural failure of the channel and of the direction of refusal, not as a moral failing (F2, F4). Ask who can be hurt by a disclosure before asking how much safety is felt.
3. Require that a lesson record at least one held belief as false, and that the artifact carried forward is a method with a measured round-trip gap (F3, F5).
4. Do not stop at a survey. The three guards are a product; safety is one factor (F6, F9).
5. Build the next review from a differing closure, selected against the residual the last one surfaced (F7, F8).
6. Say `gap` where no figure line governs, and name the instrument that would close it (F10).

Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Send the generator (the residual, the route conditions and the refuter), not a success story.

## 7. Recursive derivation summary (search, inward, outward)

1. **SEARCHED (cheapest, asked first).** Is the winning practice already in the closure? The business pool contains many named practices but no figure line selects among them. Fig 9 :44: *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room."* Result: gap, not yet a proof of absence.
2. **INWARD (extend the instrument from one already returning something).** The failed experiment already returns R per slot. Sharpen it: compare reported incidents against an independent log (Fig 6 :32, *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."*) and test whether a lesson carries negation. No new primitive required.
3. **OUTWARD (import from a frame whose closure differs).** Characterize R in effect terms first (F1), then look for frames predicting its shape: aviation safety listening (Noort et al. 2021), publication bias and registered reports (Curry et al. 2025; Nature Neuroscience 2024), high-reliability organizations (Silla et al. 2025), healthcare prosecution cases (Field Guide, "Learning from Error"). Each predicts one of the three conditions and none supplies a name for the missing practice.
4. **Keep INWARD and OUTWARD opposed.** Sharpening the internal instrument does not tell us whether an import exists; finding an outside frame does not tell us whether our representation can hold it. Read them as two tracks (Fig 6 :31).

## 8. Updated verdict

The shape survives; the single-factor story does not. A failed experiment reaches learning when three conditions hold together: the channel to the ground stays reachable (C1), the team can refuse a held belief and aims the refusal at that belief rather than at a person or an outcome (C2), and the sensemaking frame has contact with a closure that differs from the one that authored the bet (C3). Blame is the compound where C1 fails through a moved anchor and C2 aims the refusal at the wrong term; waste is the compound where the residual is pooled or dropped and no term is minted. Verdicts are kept per domain, never averaged: D1 triangulated (Fig 1 + Fig 2), D2 triangulated (Fig 10 + Fig 2), D3 triangulated (Fig 5 + Fig 4 + Operators), D4 triangulated (Fig 6 + Fig 8 + Fig 11), D5 single-source and internally consistent only. Naming the specific practice that wins is a `gap`. Strains after re-census: one live (whether an organizational belief is a term in Γ), named, not hidden. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.
