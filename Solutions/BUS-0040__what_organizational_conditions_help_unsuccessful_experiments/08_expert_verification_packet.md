# Expert verification packet - the Tether on learning from a failed experiment

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher of organizational learning from failure can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed to have settled which route a given organization took. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A failed experiment produces a prediction gap that belongs to that slot and must stay anchored to the experiment, not pooled into an average. From this the Tether derives three conditions that must hold together for the gap to become learning: the reporting channel must stay reachable, so punishment cannot move the anchor; the vocabulary must be able to say that a held belief was wrong and must aim that refusal at the belief rather than at a person or an unwanted outcome; and the review must include a frame different from the one that authored the failed bet, so a mirror chain cannot pass itself off as corroboration. Blame is the compound where the first two fail, waste is the compound where the gap is dropped and nothing is minted. The framework does not name a winning practice and does not treat a self-report safety score as the ground.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 10 and 2

- **Statement:** the channel to the ground must stay reachable. Punishment that changes what gets reported moves the anchor; a high self-report score is a channel reading, not the ground.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :24 - *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; Fig 10 :29 - *"keep the anchor reachable"*; Fig 10 :59 - *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* Also Fig 2 :30 - *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."*
- **Refuter:** a unit where reporters are demonstrably punished, the recorded incident rate falls as a result, and an independent measurement of the same underlying events shows a matching fall in real failures. If the ground and the report move together, the anchor was not moved. A second refuter: a self-report score that predicts learning at maximum independence from two frames with zero shared evidence.

### C2 - framework-internal plus a linguistic check; checkable by reading Figure 5 and the Operators table

- **Statement:** learning from failure owes a refusal (negation), not only an addition; and the refusal must be aimed at the held belief. A "lessons learned" list that records only what was missing can accumulate without correcting.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :53 - *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"*; Fig 5 :62-64 - *"a frame can refuse the right kind of thing and still aim the refusal at the outcome it did not want rather than at the belief it holds."* Operators table :58 - *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."*
- **What to check:** inspect a real postmortem artifact. Does it record any governing belief as false, or only missing items and additions? Is the target of the refusal a person, an outcome, or a belief?
- **Refuter:** a team whose postmortems record no held belief as false, only missing or added items, whose failure residual nevertheless declines on novel experiments judged by a ground the team cannot move over two cycles. If so, negation and target are not necessary.

### C3 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 7, 6 and 11

- **Statement:** the next review must differ from the frame that authored the failed bet, and must be selected against the residual the last one surfaced. A homogeneous room's agreement is a convention, not corroboration.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 7 :36 - *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."* Fig 6 :23 - *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 11 :39 - *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*
- **Refuter:** a single homogeneous group, with no outside frame and no new contact at any step, that reliably produces correct refusals on novel failures judged by an independent ground over multiple cycles. If so, differing closures are not necessary.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that psychological safety is *the* cause or the measure of learning. It is a frame-internal channel reading (Fig 1 :24-27) and one factor in a product (Fig 5 :33). The business evidence pool, including Frazier et al. and Edmondson, is one witness with several names, not four.
- Not that accountability should be abolished. Fig 10 :53 - *"A prohibition nothing can satisfy is a prohibition stated wrong."* The claim is keep the anchor reachable and declare the stake (Fig 10 :60).
- Not that one practice wins. Naming a specific practice is a declared `gap`: Fig 9 :32 - *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- Not a union-free single lever. Fig 9 :55 - *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not another argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a reading *"is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source"* and *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."*

1. **Channel test:** reports matched against an independent log of the same events, across a leadership or policy change. Does the report track the log only while the channel is open?
2. **Negation / catalyst test:** does the artifact record a false belief, and does a successor team given only the carried-up method avoid the same failure class? A part that survives its own use is a catalyst (Fig 12 :119).
3. **Closure test:** was the next review drawn from a closure different from the last, selected against the residual it surfaced?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 10 :24, :29, :59; Fig 5 :53 and :62-64; Operators table :58; Fig 7 :36. Seven short passages.
2. Check C2 against one real postmortem you know: does it carry a refusal or only an addition?
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish an open channel from a high safety score, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
