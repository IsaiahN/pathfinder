## What the wider literature bears on the question

**One evidence pool, not four witnesses.** Edmondson 1999 and 2011, Cannon and Edmondson 2005 and 2012, Carmeli and Gittell 2009 and the Frazier meta-analysis all come from the organizational-behavior pool and cite one another heavily. Their convergence on psychological safety is one witness with several names. By Fig 2 :40, *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* It is useful as a convention about what to measure next, not as a verdict.

**Low-overlap corroboration of the mechanism, at different strata.** Aviation cockpit recordings (voice without listening is wasted signal), nuclear-plant safety research (voice works through team learning, not directly), healthcare prosecution cases (an interest in the reading is disqualifying), and the science publication-bias literature (null results go into the file drawer; pre-registration changes the incentive) each predict one of the three conditions from a different evidence base. They corroborate the *shape*, not the organizational claim as a firm-level fact.

**One genuine conflict, kept visible.** Eldor et al. 2023 says too much safety can hurt routine performance. The Field Guide's critique says the evidence does not establish the downturn. Per the WATSON conflict rule this returned us to the census. Result: psychological safety was re-mapped from "the ground" to "the channel condition C1, read by a fallible instrument," and the sufficiency claim was dropped. The figure verdict did not flip.
