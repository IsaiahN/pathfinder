## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the three conditions the same kind of thing? NO

- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."**
- Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Operators table :73-74: *"A vocabulary with − and without ¬ can say what is missing from an arrangement and cannot say that an arrangement is wrong, so it can only ever accumulate."*

C1 is a **structure**: a network-like relation between the event, the report and the people who can act (Fig 13 :23, *"that entities relate, and influence travels"*). C2 is an **operator**: negation, unary, outside the seven bonds (Operators table :70-74). C3 is a **transform**: an import across a closure boundary, the only operator that moves the wall (Fig 6 :14).

They are not three members of one list. They are one relation, one operator and one transform. That is the structural reason a single lever fails: a lever is a bond or a policy, and it cannot substitute for an operator it does not contain. Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if one intervention can be shown to restore all three conditions simultaneously with no categorically different residue after removal, then they are one condition after all and this structural claim fails.

### Q2 - Are two postmortems with the same lessons isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :112-113: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."** *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."*
- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**

Two postmortem reports that list the same items (atoms: the missed signal, the late escalation, the missing check) are **not necessarily variants of one artifact**. If one joins them with `−` (what was missing and must be added) and the other joins them with `¬` (which held belief was false), then by Fig 12 they are different substances whose ingredient lists cannot tell them apart. The first can accumulate; the second can correct. This is a real isomer claim under Fig 12's definition, and it is checkable by looking at the bonds in the text, not the topics.

The **catalyst** test is the sharper one for practice. A report is a catalyst if it survives its own use and regenerates at the next experiment. A report that is spent on the occasion it describes is a recording (Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*). Nothing in the ingredient list records which one you have; the lineage does.

**Falsifier:** if two reports with identical atom sets but different bond types produce indistinguishable behavior in successor teams against an independent ground, the isomer reading fails and the difference is cosmetic.

### Q3 - What is the dependency order?

- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
- Fig 3 :20-21: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**
- Fig 3 :25-27: the middle link, *"having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."* And: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

Order by dependency, not by popularity:

1. **Perception:** can the failure be seen as a slot with live mass? (C1, instrument: compare reports against an independent log.)
2. **Vocabulary and objective:** are there terms for what the failure affords, and for what would count as learning? This is the usual break. It needs the negation operator and the bound refuter (C2).
3. **Planning / route:** is there a differing closure to import from when the needed term is absent? (C3.)
4. **Learn and carry:** name what worked, verify it, and carry a method upward with a measured round-trip gap (Fig 4 :30).

Effort spent on step 4 (more postmortems, more templates) cannot rescue a break at step 2. Fig 3 :22-23: *"A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can. Which is why a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing."*

### Q4 - Are the routes cycles or chains, and what decides the sign?

- Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**
- Fig 7 :12-13: **"same closure, handed along;"** *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"* (a mirror chain, motion without progress).

All three routes are cycles, because each changes the next experiment. Their signs differ:

- **Learning** is a loop whose output (a settled method) re-enters as a head start for the next search. It closes against the ground (Fig 4 :11-12, *"a head start, never a recording"*).
- **Blame** is a loop whose output (a punished reporter) re-enters as a smaller signal. It closes the channel; Fig 10 :24 says the ground does not decay, the channel does. At the limit the loop runs in place and *"you have lost touch"* (Fig 10 :28).
- **Waste** is a chain masquerading as a loop: the residual is pooled into an average and the next experiment begins from the same place. Fig 5 :41: *"a system that is minting nothing may have nothing wrong with its minting."*

**Falsifier:** if blame-reporting units show an undiminished flow of independent-event-matched residual over multiple cycles, the "blame closes the channel" loop model is wrong.

### Q5 - Functional group: why the practices rhyme without being identical

- Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

Blameless postmortems, just culture, after-action reviews, aviation crew-resource-management and registered reports all share a functional group: each keeps a report attached to a ground and makes a refusal sayable. They differ in the qualifier, which is the member. Fig 12's rule says the group fixes *what kind* and the qualifier fixes *which member*. So the framework can predict the family (the shape) without selecting a member (the `gap`). That is exactly what F10 of the audit records.

## CONCLUSION

The three conditions are not one list: they are a relation (C1), an operator (C2) and a transform (C3), which is why the bundle needs three instruments and no single lever clears it. Two postmortems with identical ingredient lists are isomers if their bonds differ (`−` versus `¬`), and a report is a catalyst only if it survives its own use. The dependency order is perception, then vocabulary and objective (the usual break), then route, then carry; more templates cannot rescue a break upstream. The three routes are cycles with different signs, and the family of successful practices is a functional group whose member is not named by the framework. All falsifiers were written before the run.
