## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not whether an experiment "failed" but where its residual goes. In effect terms: a team makes a bet Γ(b,a), the world returns o′, and R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| is measured per experiment slot, never averaged across the portfolio (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22). The residual R is the primary signal. The question asks what routes it:

- **Route L (learning):** a term φ is admitted to Γ, settled against the ground, and composable at the next experiment. Reachable when the reporting channel stays open, the organization can refuse a held belief, and the sensemaking frame differs from the one that authored the failed bet.
- **Route B (blame):** the residual is aimed at a person rather than at the belief that was wrong. The anchor (the experiment outcome) gets moved by punishment, so the channel decays and the signal stops arriving (Fig 10 :24, Fig 10 :59).
- **Route W (waste):** the residual is dropped as a transient or pooled into an average, nothing is minted, and Γ is unchanged. The machinery may run perfectly while returning nothing new (Fig 5 :41).

- Option A, "learning is a property of a good culture the organization owns": leads to the prediction that installing a stated value or a survey score is sufficient.
- Option B, "learning is a maintained three-condition route from an experiment residual to a settled term": leads to the prediction that the route fails when any one of the three conditions fails, and that a single lever clears only the region where one condition binds (Fig 9 :55-57).

What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a *shape* naming the conditions that must all hold for a failed experiment to reach route L, plus an instrument that would discriminate channel failure from refusal failure from closure failure. That shape is the sought-for term; until it is bound it predicts nothing and no check in the loop can see it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B in `specified` mode, kept per domain and never averaged.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the business question, no claim that "psychological safety" is the single cause, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of learning from failure.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
