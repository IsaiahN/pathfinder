# Isomers and order in the correspondence claim

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 8).

## RESIDUAL

`01` unbundles REA-0046 into three conditions (information, causal role, structure) but leaves two things owed: (1) are two representations with the same component features the same representation, and (2) in what order must the three conditions be settled? This file derives both. Question 1 is the isomer question; question 2 is the dependency-order question.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Are two representations with the same features the same representation?

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
Fig 12 :92: "Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."

Take two internal states X1 and X2 with the same activated feature set F = {f1, f2, f3}. A feature list records F. It does not record whether f1 → f2 → f3 or f1 + f2 + f3 or f1 − f3, and those are different molecules under Fig 12. By the figure's definition X1 and X2 are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance, with the difference invisible in the feature list and decisive in behaviour.

This is not only an analogy. The superposition result (Elhage et al., 2022) makes the ingredient list actively misleading: many features can be packed into one unit, so a unit-level feature list undercounts the atoms and misrecords the bond. And the place-cell case supplies a biological instance: the Nobel press release records that O'Keefe's hippocampus "generates numerous maps, represented by the collective activity of place cells that are activated in different environments." Same cell types, different arrangement, different map. That is the isomer relation in a system whose correspondence to the world is established by tracking the environment.

**Conclusion Q1:** no. Feature identity does not settle representation identity. The relation (bond) is load-bearing, and a correspondence claim that only lists features has recorded half of itself.

### Q2: What is the order of the three conditions?

Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."

Map the three conditions onto Fig 3's five links:

| Fig 3 link | in REA-0046 | can fail? |
|---|---|---|
| 1 perception | can we read X and W at all? | yes, an instrument failure |
| 2 vocabulary | do we have terms for what X affords and what would count as progress? | yes, the "correspondence" link: Fig 3 :25-27 says it has neither obvious failures nor obvious tests |
| 3 objective | what counts as correspondence in our own terms? | yes, this is where the three-condition shape is bound |
| 4 planning | design the intervention and the independent frames | yes |
| 5 learn and carry | record the lineage, reduce the next round-trip gap | yes |

Within the three conditions the dependency order is:

> information → causal role → structure → record

- **Information first** because it is cheapest and the other two assume its answer. It mirrors formula step 7's SEARCHED-first rule, asked first because it is cheapest and the other two assume its answer. If no signal about W can be read from X at any resolution, the causal and structural questions have nothing to attach to. This is the same discipline, not the same step.
- **Causal role second** because it discriminates among the possible sources of the decodable signal (the metamerism problem in `05` section 4). Hewitt and Liang's control tasks and Meng et al.'s causal intervention both operate here.
- **Structure third** because it asks whether the bond survives rearrangements, which presupposes that the signal and its causal role are established.
- **Record last and throughout** because Fig 12 :129 says "the order is not recoverable from the artefact. It is recoverable from the lineage."

**Conclusion Q2:** the order is a dependency order, not a ranking of importance. Skipping information to assert structure repeats the link-below-the-break error of Fig 3 :22-23: "A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can. Which is why a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing."

### Q3: Is the union a partition, and can a single lever clear it?

Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
Fig 9 :56: "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."

The three conditions overlap: a good selective probe is both an information reading and a step toward causal discrimination; a causal intervention is both a world test and a structural probe. But they do not reduce. A lever that clears information leaves causal role and structure standing. Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

So the union must be reported as three separate verdicts. There is no single "representation quality" number. This is the same refusal as Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."

### Q4: Do the three conditions compose into something at the next level?

Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."

When a correspondence claim is settled against the ground for one representation, that settled claim becomes an atom. A second-order claim, for example that two settled representations stand as isomers over a shared feature set, is legal only because both first-order claims were settled. This is recursion, not import: no new primitive is needed. It is also where the cross-model convergence question lives, under the record condition of Fig 8 :51.

## FALSIFIER, written before the run

The isomer and order derivation is refuted if either of the following holds:

1. Two representations with identical feature sets but different relational organization produce identical behaviour under every intervention designed to discriminate them, and no instrument can be improved from a returning reading to separate them. Then the bond carries no information and the isomer claim fails for that system.
2. A causal-role or structure verdict is demonstrably settleable when no decodable information about W exists at any resolution, or the conditions can be settled in an order that makes information unnecessary. Then the dependency order in Q2 is wrong and the chain must be re-censused.

Both are cheap to state and expensive to run, which is the right order for a refuter (Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong.").
