# Solution REA-0046

**Question:** How can researchers tell whether a system's internal representations correspond to meaningful structures in the world?

**Source:** reasoning.txt line 57

**Section:** 5. Memory, representation, and world models

**Mode:** `specified` on the substantive methodological claims (the shape of the test), `general` on the structural frame. Nothing here is `grounded`: no measurement in this simulated derivation settles any particular mapping from an internal state to a world structure. Every substantive claim is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

**Residual in effect terms.** A researcher observes internal states of a system and wants to decide the claim "representation X corresponds to world structure W." The gap is between what the system's own account delivers (fluent output, internal coherence, decodable activation patterns) and what the world returns when the researcher intervenes on it. The system cannot close this gap from inside, because those self-scores are quantities the frame itself produces. The question is therefore an anchoring question, not a vocabulary question: what would have to be true of an instrument for the correspondence claim to be settled by something that does not move when the frame is pushed?

**What an answer would look like (bound as an ordinary term).** A *correspondence test* is a protocol with three separable conditions, each with its own failure: (1) information, the representation carries a decodable signal about the world structure; (2) causal role, intervening on the representation changes the system's world-tracking behavior with specificity, and intervening on the world changes the representation in the predicted way; (3) structure, the representation preserves the arrangement (the bond) and not merely the ingredient list. A correspondence claim is settled only when an anchor (the world, which does not update on the frame's opinion) pays for all three, and when independent frames converge with a recorded lineage. This is the sought-for shape written down and bound before the derivation, so it can accrue a residual and be refused like any other term.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of researchers by how likely they are to agree with the framework.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons in the figures' own words: Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."; Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"; Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation, and sending the full architecture before asking for a verdict spends the only independence the outside frame has.

Legally: unsolicited bulk email to scraped personal addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in and submission channels avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
