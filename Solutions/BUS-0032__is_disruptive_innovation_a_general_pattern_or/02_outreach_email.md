# Outreach draft: check the separator before you decide about the label

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the refuter below has been written down and dated in your own record. The two-stage offer collapses on contact if the first stage is a theory pitch.

---

**Subject:** What would distinguish a real disruption pattern from a label applied afterwards?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific check I would like you to make against what you already know about innovation cases. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the check does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The check (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The question "is disruptive innovation a general pattern or a retrospective label?" bundles four different things: is there a mechanism, can it be identified before the outcome, does the term pay for itself across new cases, and is researcher agreement a verdict or a shared convention. Those have different grounds and cannot be averaged. My current reading is that the either/or is a **union**, not a partition, and that the ordinary label is a **convention** that is efficient for shared meaning and carries no verdict weight about the world. This is a shape claim, not a settled result.
>
> **What I claim is licensed:** where nothing maintains the entrant's path, a label is applied afterwards to a lineage that met the market; the recurring shape is an entrant from a differing closure entering where the incumbent's channel to paying customers has decayed, with the entrant's value relative to the residual rather than carried by the technology. The figures return that shape. They do not return a name for the winner, and they do not return an ex ante discriminator. I record the discriminator as unreached, not as silence and not as a number to invent.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that disruption theory is false, that Christensen was wrong about disk drives, that no mechanism exists, or that hindsight bias explains everything. A generative shape is licensed; what is refused is the name as a settled ex ante term and the promotion of a convention into a verdict.
>
> **What would refute my reading (written down before this message):**
> 1. a blinded classification from the term, on cases not used to define it, that discriminates genuine displacement from merely successful innovation above baseline against an independent ground that does not update on the classification;
> 2. evidence that the term composes into a candidate that pays for itself on held-out cases rather than explaining one occasion perfectly;
> 3. evidence that the label's valence stays the same across a documented change of residual;
> 4. two genuinely low-overlap frames, with different data and no shared case list, that independently reach the same ex ante classification and range it separately against the market.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the corresponding part. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical figures README (usage rule, "a shape, not a name"): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The working audit with all refuters: `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`
>
> **If you want to use the method:** write down what would refute your own result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A frame cannot check the direction of its own question because the shape it seeks is a preference, not a term it holds. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
