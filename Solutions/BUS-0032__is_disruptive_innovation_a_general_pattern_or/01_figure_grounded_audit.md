## 0. The claim under audit

> Is disruptive innovation a general pattern, or a useful label applied selectively after the fact?

The ordinary term **"disruptive innovation"** is treated here as a candidate φ in the research library Γ. Two readings:

- **Reading A (general pattern):** φ is a law-like structure that predicts, before the outcome, which innovations will displace incumbents. It has been settled at a ground, is composable over, and pays for itself.
- **Reading B (selective retrospective label):** φ is minted after `o′` arrives. It re-describes a completed history. It is efficient as a shared meaning (a convention) and carries no verdict weight.

The framework's own vocabulary gives a third possibility that the either/or suppresses: a **shape** that constrains any candidate answer without naming the winner, plus an **instrument gap** that must be reported rather than filled.

---

## 1. Residual, in effect terms

`R = |Γ(b,a) − o′|`, indexed per innovation-slot and per incumbent-slot, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19, :22).

- **What failed:** a research frame predicts (implicitly, through its classifications) that a set of cases shares a generative structure. The market outcome arrives. The label is then assigned to the cases that fit, so the prediction is fitted to the observation rather than ranged against it. `R` is driven to zero by construction, and a zero residual obtained by fitting the prediction to the outcome is not settlement.
- **Arity:** a multi-place relation (innovation, incumbent, market, time), not a two-place correlation.
- **Symmetry:** "disruptive" is a **comparison** relative to incumbents, carrying an ordered displacement over time. The Operators table defines ⋛ as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* and warns *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*. It is not a symmetric join. The 1995-to-2026 history of the term is a sequence (→), not an identity (≡).
- **Scale:** the entrant's own loop, the incumbent's loop, the market habitat, the research room chain, and the lineage of cases across decades. The membrane between these scales is lossy (Fig 4 :27-29).
- **Anchor:** the market's payment. Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* The label does not move the market, so the market is an anchor. Research agreement does move when researchers are shown the label, so it is not.

## 4. Findings F1 to F9

### F1. The governing line: a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground (decisive for Reading B)

Fig 11 :36:

> **"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."**

Re-censused onto D3/D1: where no seat maintains the entrant's path, "disruptive" is a name applied afterwards to a lineage that met the market. The market selected; the label did no work in the selection. This is the figure's own statement of the retrospective-label reading. It is supported, on a very different route, by the world: King and Baatartogtokh 2015 report that of 77 cases chosen as examples of disruption, *"only seven of the cases (9%) contained all four elements of the theory that we asked about"*, and Lepore 2014 writes *"Disruptive innovation can reliably be seen only after the fact."*

**Mode:** `general` for the structural line; `specified` for the disruption application. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 habitat and Fig 9 leave/arrive, low overlap). **Refuter:** a blinded, pre-registered classification from the term, on cases not used to define it, that discriminates genuine displacement from merely successful innovation above baseline against an independent non-updating ground. If it fires, the label did work before the outcome and F1 is withdrawn.

### F2. Describe the residual before choosing the frame: why the label looks general after the fact

Fig 9 :27-28:

> **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**

Fig 9 :30-31:

> **"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"**

The ordinary practice fits the term to cases selected by outcome. Select the cases first and any explanation will seem to fit. *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* calls this exact inversion **Projection** and points to Fig 9 :27-28 as its source. From the world, apophenia is *"the tendency to perceive meaningful connections between unrelated things"* (Wikipedia, summarizing Conrad 1958 and Shermer's "patternicity": *"the tendency to find meaningful patterns in meaningless noise"*); in statistics it is described as *"the false identification of patterns in data."* Hindsight bias is *"the common tendency for people to perceive past events as having been more predictable than they were"* (Fischhoff's "creeping determinism", 1975, via Wikipedia).

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 9 plus Fig 8 :53 *"Residual first, frame second."*; plus three low-overlap cognition sources). **Refuter:** cases selected on an effect description written before the outcomes were known, with the selection record dated.

### F3. The term fails the MDL bargain as an ex ante generator

Fig 5 :26:

> **"does the new term cost less to state than the confusion it removes?"**

Fig 5 :43-44:

> **"A term that explains everything by saying nothing fails this, and so does a term that explains one occasion perfectly."**

A label fitted to a completed history explains each occasion perfectly and predicts none. Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* The research literature's own defensive moves support this: Christensen is quoted as saying he *"never thought... that the word disruption has so many connotations... that people would then flexibly take an idea, twist it, and use it to justify whatever they wanted to do in the first place"* (King & Baatartogtokh 2015, quoting an HBR interview), and the same paper concludes that disruption theory *"is a good reminder of potential pitfalls... but in no way does it predict what most companies will do."*

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 5 bargain and Fig 4 membrane). **Refuter:** show the term's cost plus leftover residual falls below the unlabelled description on held-out cases. Guo et al. 2019 (*Technological Forecasting and Social Change* 139, 250 to 265) is a world attempt at exactly this instrument; its abstract states *"Assessing potential disruptiveness of innovations is an important but challenging task for incumbents... most of the documented methods tend to be case specific."* That is a published attempt, not a figure settlement, and the figure gap remains (§5).

### F4. The convention/verdict distinction is the whole answer to the either/or

Fig 2 :40:

> **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**

Fig 2 :41:

> **"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"**

These are two quantities read from one parameter (overlap). "Disruptive innovation" is a legitimate and efficient convention: it lets scholars and managers coordinate on a meaning. It is disqualified as a verdict about the world when the agreeing frames share the same case pool, the same book, and the same author's definitions. Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 7 :36: *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."*

Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* So the failure is not using the label. It is promoting the convention into a verdict, then letting that verdict allocate capital, define a field, or decide which firms get studied.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 2, Fig 7, Fig 10, three distinct roles, low overlap). **Refuter:** two low-overlap frames (different data, different instruments, no shared case list) that independently arrive at the same ex ante classification and range it against a market outcome neither can move.

### F5. The general pattern is a shape, not a name

Fig 13 :134-135:

> **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."**

The canonical figures README: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"* and stops *"at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*

What survives as a shape (all `specified`, ungrounded):

1. An entrant from a **differing closure** enters where the incumbent's channel to the paying ground is weak. Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 8 :45: *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."*
2. The incumbent's failure is **channel decay**, not ground change. Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; Fig 10 :26: *"saturated metrics, pooled readings"*; Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Fig 11 :69: *"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts."*
3. Valence is **relative to the residual**, not carried by the technology. Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* This is why the same technology can be sustaining in one habitat and disruptive in another, and why Christensen's own late correction (Uber was *"correlated with a business model that is unattractive to its competitor"*, not caused by being low-end) fits the figure rather than contradicting it.
4. The entrant's **arrangement**, not its ingredient list, is the substance. Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."* The framework's own history records this: Christensen replaced "disruptive technology" with "disruptive innovation" because *"most technologies are not intrinsically disruptive or sustaining in character; rather, it is the business model that identifies the crucial idea"* (Wikipedia, summarizing *The Innovator's Solution*).

**Mode:** `general` for the shape, `specified` for the four claims. **Status:** D1 and D2 triangulated (Fig 6/8 channel/closure and Fig 10/11 habitat/channel); D3 triangulated (Fig 11 plus Fig 2). **Refuter:** a documented case where the technology alone, with the same arrangement and the same habitat, flips from disruptive to sustaining with no change of residual; or an incumbent failing while its channel to the paying ground measures high and non-declining.

### F6. The name is mistyped as an identity and as a standing list

The Operators table defines ≡ as *"two names, one referent"* with the test *"substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No."* "Disruptive" is not ≡ to "successful entrant": substitute and the meaning changes, because the label is a threshold comparison (⋛) against an incumbent and an ordered displacement (→) through time. When the label is used as a standing category (a list of "disruptive companies"), it becomes the standing list Fig 11 :51 refuses.

Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Disruption can name a **functional group** (a kind of relation) with the particular case as qualifier. It cannot name a permanent property of a firm.

**Mode:** `general` for the typing, `specified` for the application. **Status:** single-source on the figure typing (Operators table with Fig 12), corroborated by the world's boundary disputes (Uber, the 9% result). **Refuter:** an operator test in which substituting "disruptive" for "successful new-market entrant" loses nothing, or a standing list whose members keep their valence across a documented residual change.

### F7. What is shared between disruption accounts is arrangement, not atoms

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Two accounts that list the same ingredients (cheaper product, low-end foothold, incumbent overshoot) but bind them differently (foothold as cause vs foothold as correlate) are isomers, not variants of one claim. Fig 12 :124: *"Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are each what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member."*

The 9% result and the Uber correction are evidence that the same ingredient set carries different bonds. This is the supporting derivation in `06_isomers_and_order.md`, with its own falsifier.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** single-source for the figure definition of isomer; the world supplies the ingredient/bond distinction. **Refuter:** show a single bonding of the ingredients that covers every case without a second arrangement.

### F8. The ex ante discriminator is a `gap`, not a silence

No figure line returns which specific innovation will be disruptive before the outcome. After re-census across all domains:

- Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*
- Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
- Fig 13 :134-135: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."*

Ask the three questions in order (formula step 7): **SEARCHED** first (is a discriminator already in the closure and unfound?); **INWARD** second (extend an instrument already returning something, such as the Guo framework's ten indicators); **OUTWARD** last (find a frame whose closure predicts the shape, such as survivorship bias or ecological niche construction). These are distinct tracks and are never read against one another. Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts with different instructions (formula step 7).

**Mode:** `general` for the gap, `specified` for the naming of candidate instruments. **Status:** `gap`. **Refuter:** the blinded ex-ante test of F1 would close it at the ground, not by further argument.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written BEFORE the conclusion)

Per Fig 11 :36's own demand for ground contact, and the seat rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim and the convention verdict are refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Ex ante prediction fires.** A blinded classification from the term, on cases not used to define it, discriminates disruptive from merely successful or sustaining innovation above baseline, against an independent ground that does not update on the classification. This closes F8.
2. **Generator fires.** The term is shown to compose inside `closure(Γ)` into a candidate that pays the MDL bargain on held-out cases (Fig 5 :26, :43-44), rather than explaining one occasion perfectly.
3. **Invariant valence fires.** The label keeps the same valence across a documented change of residual (breaks Fig 11 :49-51).
4. **Independence fires.** Two genuinely low-overlap frames, with different data and instruments and no shared case list, independently produce the same ex ante classification and range it separately against the market (breaks Fig 2 :40).
5. **Same-closure displacement fires.** An incumbent displaces itself by the same route from the same closure with no decayed channel (breaks Fig 6 :23, Fig 10 :24).
6. **Anchor moves.** Withholding the label changes the market outcome. Then the label has moved the ground and is not merely descriptive.

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the union is re-censused. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Use "disruptive innovation" as a **convention**, explicitly labelled, and refuse to let it decide an outcome (F4, Fig 10 :47).
2. Lead with the **shape**, not the name: differing closure plus decayed channel plus residual-relative valence, indexed per case (F5, Fig 1 :19).
3. Describe the **residual** before selecting frames or cases; date the description (F2, Fig 9 :27-28).
4. Ask the ground, not the room: what would an independent, non-updating market test show? (F1, Fig 7 :35.)
5. Report the ex ante discriminator as **unreached**, not as silence and not as a number to be invented (F8, Fig 13 :134-135).
6. Send the generator (shape plus refuter), not the recording of one case history (Fig 4 :25).
