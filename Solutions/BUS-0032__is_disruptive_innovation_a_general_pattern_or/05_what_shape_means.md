## 1. A shape is a constraint you can state before you can name the winner

The canonical README: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134-135: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

For this question a **shape** is the narrowed form of a pattern that constrains any candidate account without picking the winner or the label. The shape is not ".disruptive innovation is the pattern" and not "disruption is hindsight bias". It is: **an entrant from a differing closure enters where the incumbent's channel to a non-updating ground has decayed, and the entrant's valence is relative to the residual, indexed per case.**

## 2. How the shape is obtained: describe the effect first

- *Library of Babel*: *"If you don't know the right terms to search for, you will have to use the existing vocabulary you do know that is closest to the words that you wish to describe and grasp the shape of."* Then *"With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"*; Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."*
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."*
- *Library of Babel* on a bundled question: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."* This is Fig 9 :54-57 in prose and is the direct warrant for refusing the either/or in BUS-0032.

The failure mode has a name in the essay. *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* calls the inversion in Fig 9 :27-28 (**"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**) **Projection**. Selecting the disruption cases first and then describing the pattern is exactly Projection. The world has two low-overlap names for it: hindsight bias (*"the common tendency for people to perceive past events as having been more predictable than they were"*) and apophenia (*"the tendency to perceive meaningful connections between unrelated things"*).

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* The README: the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."*
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the reason a reading cannot license a name by itself. With metamerism, an instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source"*. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* It was *"proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."* Applied here: observing that a firm was displaced by a cheaper entrant does not tell you which source produced the displacement (channel decay, scale economies, legacy costs, regulatory change). King and Baatartogtokh 2015 found legacy costs, changing scale economies and the laws of probability doing work in at least 40%, 40% and 30% of the 77 cases respectively. Naming "disruption" as the source from the single reading is the metamerism error. What is owed is a discriminating instrument, not a better story.

## 4. The "shape, not a name" limit, narrowed correctly

The exemplar records a correction that applies here too. The README's phrase is *"what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* The subject is a primitive **not held** in Γ. "Disruptive" is not a missing primitive; it is a name for a relation among held atoms (an entrant, a differing closure, a decayed channel, a non-updating ground). So the correct statement is not "the framework forbids naming disruption". It is:

- The framework does not **derive** the name from the six-term set. The name is a binding from the business frame onto a shape.
- The framework cannot tell you **which case** instantiates the shape before the ground settles it (Fig 9 :32; Fig 13 :134-135).
- Naming the binding is legitimate **provenance**; promoting it to a settled verdict is the failure (Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*).

I overstated this rule in an earlier pass by writing as if any naming were forbidden. The narrowed form is kept here and in `06_isomers_and_order.md`, so neither reading is silently lost.

## 5. Prestige is not action

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Someone let prestige stand in for proof... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."*

The disruption literature is unusually prestige-loaded: a single book, a famous author, a consulting industry, a stream of conference talks. That prestige can fund the theory's trials, which is good. It cannot settle whether the label predicts. A package that gathers famous names to agree with the theory is the named collapse, not corroboration (Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict"*).
