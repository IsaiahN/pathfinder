## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is whether the term **disruptive innovation** is a candidate term φ that predicts ahead of the outcome (a general pattern with ground payment), or a name fitted to the outcome after it arrives. In effect terms: does `Γ(b,a)` predict `o′`, or is the label minted from `o′` after the fact? If the latter, the residual `R = |Γ(b,a) − o′|` is zero by construction, the term is a recording of one occasion, and it carries no verdict weight. Option A (general pattern) leads to a predictive claim that must be tested at a ground. Option B (selective label) leads to a convention that is efficient for shared meaning and disqualified as evidence about the world.

The answer derived below is: **the either/or is a union, not a partition.** The figures license a general **shape** and refuse the **name** as a settled term. In its current ordinary use the label is applied after the fact and is a convention, not a verdict. A structural pattern is real, but it is per-case, relative to a residual, and not available as an ex ante predictor. That predictor is a declared `gap`, reported as unreachable, not silence.
