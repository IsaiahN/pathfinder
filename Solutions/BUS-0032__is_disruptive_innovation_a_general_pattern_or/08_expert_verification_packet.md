# Expert verification packet: the Tether on disruptive innovation

**What this is:** a claim in a form an innovation scholar or strategy researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the sub-claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the ex ante discriminator, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "general pattern or retrospective label?" is a union of four conditions, not a partition. The ordinary label is applied after the market ground has settled, so it is an efficient convention and carries no verdict weight about the world. The figures license a general **shape** but not a **name**: an entrant from a differing closure enters where the incumbent's channel to a non-updating ground has decayed, with residual-relative valence, indexed per case. No figure line returns an ex ante discriminator, so that item is reported as unreached rather than filled. World sources (King and Baatartogtokh 2015, Lepore 2014, Budd and Mann 2018) corroborate the shape; they do not decide it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1. Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 11, 9 and 2

- **Statement:** a label fitted to a lineage after the ground settled does no work in the selection; agreement about a world from a shared case pool is disqualified as a verdict; agreement about a shared meaning is efficient as a convention. Therefore "disruptive" is a convention until a ground settles it.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 11 :36 *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."*
  - Fig 9 :27-28 *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*
  - Fig 2 :40 *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*
  - Fig 2 :41 *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*
  - Fig 10 :47 *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*
- **Refuter:** two genuinely low-overlap frames, with different data and instruments and no shared case list, that independently reach the same ex ante classification and range it separately against a market outcome neither can move.

### C2. Framework-internal plus market-facing; checkable by an innovation scholar against their own cases

- **Statement:** the licensed shape is a differing-closure import into a habitat where the incumbent's channel to the paying ground has decayed; valence is relative to the residual, so no standing list of disruptive actors survives a residual change. The label names a functional group, not a stock.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 6 :23 *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*
  - Fig 10 :24 *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; Fig 10 :26 *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*
  - Fig 11 :66 *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."*
  - Fig 11 :49 *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*; Fig 11 :51 *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*
  - Fig 12 :123 *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- **What to check:** in cases you know well, does the incumbent's channel to paying customers actually decay while the ground stays put? Or are scale economies, legacy costs, regulation or entrant count doing the work? Does a firm called "disruptive" keep that valence when the residual changes?
- **Refuter:** an incumbent failing while its channel to the paying ground measures high and non-declining; or a standing list whose members keep their valence across a documented residual change; or a same-closure entrant displacing the incumbent by the same route.

### C3. Framework-internal derivation; checkable by reading Figures 5, 9 and 13

- **Statement:** the term fails the MDL bargain as an ex ante generator, and the ex ante discriminator is a `gap`, reported as unreached.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 5 :26 *"does the new term cost less to state than the confusion it removes?"*
  - Fig 5 :43-44 *"A term that explains everything by saying nothing fails this, and so does a term that explains one occasion perfectly."*
  - Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*
  - Fig 13 :134-135 *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."*
- **Refuter:** a blinded classification rule, written before the outcomes, that scores held-out cases above baseline against an independent non-updating ground. That closes the gap at the ground and is the strongest possible failure of C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that disruption theory is false, that Christensen was wrong about disk drives, or that no mechanism exists. The generative shape is licensed.
- Not that all apparent disruption is hindsight bias or apophenia. Those are world corroborations of the retrospective reading of the *name*, not figure verdicts over every mechanism.
- Not a universal percentage, a standing list of disruptive firms, or a named winner for any future case.
- Not a derivation of disruption from the six-term substrate, and no new primitive.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a market measurement, and the ex ante test was not run.
- Not an equivalence between "disruptive technology" (1995) and "disruptive innovation" (2003). The Operators table's substitution test fails for that pair; the history is a sequence, not an identity.

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Blinded ex ante test** on held-out cases against a market ground that does not update on the classification.
2. **Source discriminator** separating channel decay from scale economies, legacy costs, regulatory barriers and entrant count.
3. **Per-slot channel measurement** rather than reputation or case-list membership (Fig 10 :24-26).
4. **Substitution test** on the 1995 and 2003 definitions, to see whether the terms share a referent.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :36, Fig 9 :27-28, Fig 2 :40-41 and Fig 10 :47. Four short passages.
2. Check C2 against two cases you know: did the incumbent's channel to paying customers decay, or did something else do the work?
3. Name one instrument that would discriminate channel decay from scale economies in a single case, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
