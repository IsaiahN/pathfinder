# The Tether's answer: general pattern or retrospective label?

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the ex ante discriminator, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The either/or asks whether the term **disruptive innovation** predicts ahead of the market outcome or is fitted to it afterwards. In effect terms, `R = |Γ(b,a) − o′|` per innovation-slot and per incumbent-slot, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*; Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*).

The question is a **union** of four conditions with different anchors, not one question with opposite answers. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* A single lever, "it is a pattern" or "it is a label", clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :54-57). Verdicts below are kept separate per domain and never averaged.

## 2. The shape, per domain

**D1, the entrant frame. `specified`, triangulated (Fig 6 plus Fig 8).** The entrant enters from a closure that differs from the incumbent's. Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 8 :45: *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."* This is a licensed structural shape, not a prediction of which entrant wins. Refuter: an incumbent displaced by a same-closure entrant through the same route with no decayed channel.

**D2, the incumbent frame. `specified`, triangulated (Fig 10 plus Fig 11).** The incumbent's failure is a channel failure, not a change in the ground. Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; Fig 10 :26: *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*; Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Fig 11 :69: *"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts."* Refuter: an incumbent failing while its channel to the paying ground measures high and non-declining.

**D3, the market ground. `general` for the anchor rule, `specified` for the valence claim, triangulated (Fig 2 plus Fig 11).** The market settles and does not update on anyone's classification. Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Valence is relative to the residual: Fig 11 :49 *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* and Fig 11 :51 *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* Hence the same technology can be sustaining in one habitat and disruptive in another; "disruptive" is a relation, not a stock.

**D4, the research frame. `specified`, triangulated (Fig 9 plus Fig 2 plus Fig 7).** Selecting cases by outcome and then fitting the term is the inversion Fig 9 forbids. Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*. Agreement inside the frame is one evidence pool: Fig 2 :40 *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* and Fig 7 :35 *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* This is the governing line for **"a useful label applied selectively after the fact"**. Refuter: two genuinely low-overlap frames, with different data and no shared case list, reaching the same ex ante classification.

**D5, the name. `general` on the convention rule, `specified` on its application, triangulated (Fig 2 plus Fig 5).** The label is efficient as a convention and disqualified as a verdict. Fig 2 :41: *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*. A label that explains one occasion perfectly fails the acceptance test: Fig 5 :43-44 *"A term that explains everything by saying nothing fails this, and so does a term that explains one occasion perfectly."* A case history carried upward is a recording, not a generator: Fig 4 :25 *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."*

**D6, the discriminator. `gap`.** No figure line returns an ex ante discriminator. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*; Fig 13 :134-135: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."* This is unreached, not silence and not a licence to invent a number.

## 3. The answer to the either/or, stated plainly

**Both halves are true of different objects, and the either/or is refused.** In its ordinary use "disruptive innovation" is a **label applied after the fact**: the ground settles first, the name is fitted to the survivors, and the word does no work in the selection (Fig 11 :36 *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."*, re-censused onto the unmaintained firm lineage; D4 is the maintained classification seat). As a **convention** it is legitimate and efficient, provided nobody promotes it into a verdict (Fig 10 :47).

A **general pattern** is licensed, but only as a **shape**: an entrant from a differing closure entering where the incumbent's channel to a non-updating ground has decayed, with residual-relative valence, per case. The figures do not name a winner, do not return a standing list, and do not provide an ex ante discriminator. That gap is the honest stop. The world corroborates the shape of the answer: King and Baatartogtokh 2015 found only 9% of the 77 claimed cases met all four theoretical conditions and concluded that the theory *"in no way does it predict what most companies will do"*; Lepore 2014 wrote *"Disruptive innovation can reliably be seen only after the fact"*; Budd and Mann 2018 show, in a completely different field, that conditioning a sample on survival manufactures an apparent general pattern. World sources corroborate; they do not decide.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that disruption theory is false, or that Christensen was wrong about disk drives. The shape is licensed.
- Not that all apparent disruption is hindsight bias. The retrospective reading governs the *name* and the *research frame*, not every underlying mechanism.
- Not a universal percentage, a standing list of disruptive firms, or a named winner for any future case. Fig 11 :51 refuses the standing list; Fig 9 :32 refuses the answer itself.
- Not a derivation of one theory from the other, and no new primitive.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement at the market ground was taken here, and the ex ante discriminator was not run.

## 5. What would settle it (the ground)

A source-discriminating instrument, not more agreement among readers of the same cases. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a reading of green *"cannot comment on the source"*; *"It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."* Here:

1. **Blinded ex ante test.** Write the classification rule before the outcomes, score held-out cases, and range the score against a market outcome that does not update on the score. This closes the `gap`.
2. **Source discriminator.** Separate channel decay from scale economies, legacy costs, regulatory barriers and sheer entrant count in individual cases. King and Baatartogtokh report those alternatives operating in 40%, 40%, under 40% and 30% of their sample respectively; the single reading cannot assign the source.
3. **Per-slot measurement.** Measure the incumbent's channel to paying customers, not the firm's reputation or the researcher's label (Fig 10 :24-26).
4. **Standing falsifiers.** A same-closure displacement with no decayed channel; invariant label valence across a documented residual change; a substitution test equating "disruptive technology" with "disruptive innovation"; a term that pays the MDL bargain on held-out cases; a case where withholding the label changes the market outcome.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The market cases are real and the pattern shape survives a re-census, but the ex ante instrument was not run and no figure line returns one. The 1995-to-2026 literature is a lineage of remakings, not a settled atom. "Indiscernibility is not identity": a label fitted to a completed history is not the generative term, even when the fit is perfect. The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that disruptive innovation is a law, and it does not say the theory is nonsense. It says the either/or is a union of four different questions: mechanism, ex ante instrument, name and convention. The ordinary label is applied after the ground has settled, so it is efficient for shared meaning and carries no verdict weight about the world. What the figures license is a shape, per case: an entrant from a differing closure enters where the incumbent's channel to paying customers has decayed, and the entrant's value is relative to the residual rather than carried by its technology. The figures refuse a standing list of disruptive firms, refuse to name a winner in advance, and return no ex ante discriminator. That last item is reported as unreached, not as silence. What would settle it is an instrument that discriminates sources and a blinded prediction tested on held-out cases, not more agreement among people who read the same cases.
