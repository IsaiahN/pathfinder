# Outreach draft - check the separator before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` have been checked against the verbatim figure lines. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One check on the crowding-out question, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** "Does government spending raise output or crowd out private activity?" is not one question with two answers. It is a union of overlapping conditions with different anchors. The two outcomes use the same atoms (spending, savings, labour, capital, customers) but a different bond: spending can *produce* activity that did not exist before, or it can *subtract* activity that private actors would have undertaken with the same atom. A pooled multiplier cannot choose between those two bonds, because it is a ratio and the bond is ordered. The live signal is per sector and per period, not in the national total.
>
> **What follows from that (`specified`, unmeasured here).** A spending action is a candidate for raising output only when all three conditions hold together: some slot carries positive residual (idle capacity, unmet demand, hoarded saving); the resulting activity composes from what the economy already holds and is not already in the private library; and the action reaches that slot while the channel to a non-updating outcome stays open. Any one at zero forces inertness, and the action can only reallocate. Where the atoms are already held by private actors, the bond is subtraction and the spending displaces. Where the private channel is broken (a liquidity trap, for example) and the spending reaches idle resources, the bond can be production. I am not claiming a number for the multiplier or a standing list of sectors that always work.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** Not that more spending is always better. Not that a deficit is automatically inflationary or automatically harmless. Not that the framework names the sector; it returns a shape, not a name. Not that any of this is settled by measurement here. It is a separator with its failure conditions written down in advance, not a result.
>
> **What would refute it (written before this email):**
> 1. A controlled episode where every slot is at capacity, no new atom is imported, the private channel is intact, and total output still rises persistently one for one.
> 2. A case where public and private activity use identical atoms yet both expand without constraint, or use disjoint atoms yet output is unchanged.
> 3. A multiplier estimate stable across slack regimes and countries to within measurement error, read against an outcome that does not move when the forecast changes.
> 4. A realized outcome series that moves when the authority or the researchers change their forecast, so the anchor updates.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - The figures and formula, public repository branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - The canonical usage rule for how they may be cited: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - The architecture article, which includes the figures and their citation form: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so a reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks for your time.
> [name / affiliation]
