## How the wider literature bears on the question

The economics sources are one broad evidence pool and count as one witness, not five. That is recorded honestly in `10_source_ledger.csv`. What they contribute:

1. **Regime dependence.** Auerbach and Gorodnichenko find multipliers larger in recessions. Ramey gives a range for one specific experiment, a temporary deficit-financed increase. Both are ensemble statements, not per-slot verdicts.
2. **Capacity.** The crowding-out, fiscal-multiplier and MMT references all make spare capacity the pivot. They differ in policy conclusion but agree on the conditional, so the agreement is partly a shared evidence pool and is not counted twice.
3. **Measurement.** The VoxEU column is the most useful for this package because it reads what governments actually did rather than inferring discretion from outcomes. Its stated limitation (policies are classified by direction, instrument, motivation, implementation and size) is exactly the source gate Fig 8 :12 requires for an import claim.
4. **Low-overlap analogue.** The overjustification effect supports the shape only: the sign of an external input depends on the initial state of the slot. It does not support any economic mechanism.

No world source conflicts with the figure verdict. The conflict rule was therefore not triggered. If an outside reader produces a conflict, it returns to the domain census; it does not flip the verdict.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** Sending the whole framework before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the separator and the refuter.
- Fig 10 :42: **"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."** A recipient who has absorbed the architecture before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- Fig 9 :27-28: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**. Ask what they would expect to see if the separator were true before showing the article.
- Fig 2 :40: **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**. A send list ranked by receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation.
- Fig 2 :42: **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."** The cost here is a wrong policy conclusion, so the independence is worth buying.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the separator and its four refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and what anchor they would use; (3) record that verdict; (4) only then the three links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1:** residual, shape claim and pre-registered refuter. No framework material.
2. **Stage 2, opt in only:** the three source links. The recipient's stage-1 verdict is already recorded and is never pooled as corroboration if they later agree (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter, names their own ground and states their own mode.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives an outside check. The output here is a separator with declared gaps, not a multiplier. If a reader shows that a pooled multiplier can decide a per-slot verdict, stage 1 fails and nothing further is sent.
