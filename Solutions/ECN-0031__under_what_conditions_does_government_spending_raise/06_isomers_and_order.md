# Addition and displacement: isomers, union order, and the bond that decides

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the bond map. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The main audit left three structural questions open: (1) are addition and displacement two substances over one atom set, or two different atom sets; (2) what is the order of the union of conditions, and where does the chain break; (3) which bond type does the question's own grammar force us to use. This file answers those, and states a falsifier for each.

## DOMAIN CENSUS (recap, from `01` section 1)

Atoms held: public spending `G`, private spending `C` and `I`, saving `S`, labour `L`, capital `K`, materials `M`, time `t`, and the outcome reading `Y`. Bonds held: `+` conjunction, `∥` disjunction, `⇒` production, `−` subtraction, `⋛` comparison, `¬` negation. No new primitive is available without an import (Fig 13 :115: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."*).

Actors and anchors per domain are in `01` section 1. The order of dependency is perception of a live slot, vocabulary for the bonds, a stated objective in a declared currency, direction of the spend, then carrying what settled.

## Q1 - Are addition and displacement isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance."**
- Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**
- Operators table :37: **"A produces B; B did not exist before"** (`⇒`, production, ordered).
- Operators table :43: **"A with B removed or absent"** (`−`, subtraction, ordered).
- Fig 12 :40: **"same two atoms, three substances"** (`A+B`, `A→B`, `A−B`).

Write the two outcomes over the same atom set `{G, S, L, K, M, C, I}`:

- Addition: `G ⇒ (L,K,M) ⇒ Y_new`, where the intermediate activity was not in the private closure at that moment.
- Displacement: `I − G_share`, where `G` and `I` draw on the same `S` or the same `L,K,M`.

Same atoms, different bond, different substance. So **they are isomers over the fixed atom set**. This is not an analogy: Fig 12 quotes the chemistry case (silver fulminate and silver cyanate, same composition, different properties) as the reason structure entered notation.

**But the isomer claim has a condition.** If the spending imports an atom the private closure did not hold (a demand floor created by the authority's currency power, a public good no private seller can provide, a coordination rule), the atom set changes and the relation is no longer isomerism but composition plus import (Fig 6 :14). So the precise statement is:

> Over a **fixed** atom set, addition and displacement are isomers. Where an atom is imported, they are not comparable as isomers at all; the wall has moved (Fig 6 :23).

- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either outcome can be written without an atom the other requires while the atom set is fixed and no import occurred, the isomer claim fails. A case where addition needs no `L,K,M` that displacement also needs would break it.

**Mode:** `specified`. Triangulated (Fig 12 and the Operators table, plus the canonical README's import rule).

## Q2 - What fixes the functional group?

- Fig 12 :123: **"A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Fig 5 :21: **"the gap has real mass somewhere"**.
- Fig 1 :13: **"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"**.

The functional group here is **a slot with positive residual that the spending can reach**. Its presence fixes which bond is available: with a live slot, production is composable; without one, only reallocation is composable. The qualifier (which sector, which region, which household class) says which member of the group.

This is why "under what conditions" cannot be answered with a list of sectors. The functional group is a relation between an action and a slot, and Fig 11 :49 says **"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** A sector can be a benefactor in one residual and a malfactor in another (Fig 11 :50).

- **Falsifier:** a sector that fixes the outcome sign in every residual regime under a non-updating anchor. That would turn the functional group into a standing category and break the claim.

**Mode:** `general`. Triangulated (Fig 12, Fig 5, Fig 11).

## Q3 - What is the order of the union?

- Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
- Fig 9 :57: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**
- Fig 9 :56: **"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the"** intersections standing.
- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**
- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**

The conditions are: live residual (D3), composable activity (D2), import where needed (D1), open channel (D4), non-updating outcome and independent reading (D5). They overlap. A single lever, "more spending", clears the region where only the support condition is missing and leaves intersections standing. For example, adding spending where there is slack but no importable atom produces reallocation; adding an importable atom where there is no slack produces inflation rather than extra real output.

Order by dependency, not by policy attractiveness:

1. **Perceive** the slot. Is there a live residual? Without this the rest is unread (Fig 3 :5).
2. **Have terms** for the bonds. Can the frame say production versus subtraction, or only "more versus less spending"? Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**
3. **State the objective** in a declared currency. Is the target GDP, employment, tax receipts, or private capacity? A goal is a comparison (Operators :51). The reader must know which quantity `⋛` compares.
4. **Plan** the direction of the spend. Fig 9 :27-28: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**.
5. **Learn and carry** only settled terms. THE_FORMULA step 5: *"A gate is not the ground."*

The chain usually breaks at link 3, not at link 1 or 4. That is why a literature rich in multiplier estimates (link 2, a vocabulary of ratios) can still lack a term for the bond (Fig 3 :25-27: perception and planning have obvious tests, the middle link has neither).

- **Falsifier:** a demonstration that the five conditions form a partition with an assignable shared fraction, so one lever and one multiplier decide every case. Figure 9 :55-58 rules this out structurally; a real per-slot accounting that contradicts it would break the union claim.

**Mode:** `general`. Triangulated (Fig 9 and Fig 3, with Fig 12 for the vocabulary link).

## Q4 - Catalyst or spent part? Why the duration of the effect matters

- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."**
- Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input."**
- Fig 12 :117 already gives the isomer frame.

Public spending divides into two kinds by this test:

- A **catalyst** leaves a part that survives: infrastructure, a standing institution, a trained cohort, a payment system. Its effect can continue after the outlay stops, because the part is still in the composition.
- A **spent part** is gone after use: a one-off transfer consumed in the period. Its effect is confined to the period unless it changed a slot's state (for example by paying down debt that was blocking consumption).

Fig 12 :120 says *"Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."* Multiplier studies that pool all spending categories mix the two. That is another reason the pooled estimate is a filter. The Zandi estimates in the fiscal multiplier reference (food stamps 1.73, general state aid 1.36) are in different parts of this divide, though the source does not use these terms.

- **Falsifier:** a spent part whose output effect persists at the same rate indefinitely with no remaining part in the composition, or a catalyst that leaves no trace yet raises output later. Either breaks the catalyst reading.

**Mode:** `specified`. Single-source on the figure side (Fig 12's catalyst definition), with the world estimate as corroboration only.

## Q5 - The two currencies: describing the separator versus settling an episode

- Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**
- Fig 12 :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**
- Fig 12 :128: **"A long chain of cheap parts is expensive to describe and may cost one action to refute."**
- Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict."**

The separator is cheap in bits (a few conditions) and expensive in actions (a per-slot outcome record with a discriminator). The multiplier is relatively cheap in actions to estimate from national accounts and expensive in bits if you try to make it carry the bond. Confusing the two is the error Fig 12 names: a system holding one accounting *"will not notice the shortfall"* and *"will propose arrangements it cannot afford to test"*.

This is also why the outreach packet asks the reader for a 20-minute check, not a literature review: it prices the action correctly.

**Mode:** `general`. Triangulated (Fig 12 and Fig 9).

## Q6 - Refusal is a unary operator, and it has to be used here

- Fig 5 :53 (amendment): **"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"**.
- Operators table :58: **"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"**.
- THE_FORMULA step 3: *"NOTHING IS EVER DELETED, and the reach falls because something was ADDED."*

Two terms in this domain are candidates for refusal:

1. **"The multiplier decides the direction of the bond."** Refused in F6. A ratio cannot choose an ordered relation. Nothing is removed from the literature; the term loses its standing to be composed over as a verdict.
2. **"More spending is the single lever."** Refused in F2 and F7. With a union of overlapping conditions, one lever leaves the intersections standing.

A refusal is `¬`, not `−`. Subtracting the multiplier from the literature would be wrong; refusing its standing as a verdict is right. This matters for the outreach: the packet does not ask the reader to forget anything, it asks them to check whether a term has the standing they give it.

- **Falsifier:** a vocabulary that can say addition and displacement but cannot say either is false, with a demonstrated case where accumulation alone corrects the claim. The Operators table says such a vocabulary can only accumulate; a counterexample would break the operator claim.

**Mode:** `general`. Triangulated (Fig 5 amendment, Operators table, formula).

## Q7 - The question's direction cannot be checked from inside; it was bound in the README

- Fig 5 :61-63: **"BUT THE TERM MOST WORTH REFUSING MAY NOT BE IN THE LIBRARY AT ALL."** and **"A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term."**
- THE_FORMULA "Direction": *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM - write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."*

The question asks "under what conditions", which silently prefers an answer that is a list of conditions. The README binds that preference as an ordinary term: a condition is a slot-level relation deciding the bond. Once bound, it can be refused, and section 4 of `01` refuses it as a universal list while keeping it as a per-slot separator. That is the discipline the figure asks for, applied before the run.

## CONCLUSION

Over a fixed atom set, addition and displacement are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance. Where a spending imports a new atom, the relation is composition plus import and the wall moves. The union of conditions is a **union**, not a partition, ordered by dependency with the middle link (stating the objective in a declared currency) the likely break. A positive-residual slot is the **functional group**; sector names are qualifiers. Some public outlays are **catalysts** whose part survives, others are spent parts. Description cost and settlement cost are two currencies and do not add. Two refusals (`¬`, not `−`) are owed before any synthesis.

The falsifiers are written above for Q1 to Q4 and Q6. This derivation rescues the defensible part of the fiscal-policy debate (a bond distinction over a shared atom set) without pretending to return a number or a standing sector list.
