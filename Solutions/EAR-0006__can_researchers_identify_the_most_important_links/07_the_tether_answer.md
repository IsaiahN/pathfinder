# The Tether's answer: can researchers identify the most important links without oversimplifying the rest?

**Mode: `specified` on the four constraints, `general` on the structural refusals. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading. No Earth system measurement was taken by this agent.

## 1. The residual it answers

A researcher has finite attention and wants a shortlist of links on which to spend instruments and model runs. The named obstacle is that selecting a shortlist normally compresses everything else into background. Stated as an effect, R is: how to rank link slots by contribution to a stated residual while keeping the unranked slots legible rather than averaged away.

The question bundles two conditions that do not share a lever. Fig 9 :5: "not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." Seat Map :211: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either; it stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound." The compound is handled in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

## 2. The answer, in two parts

**Weak reading, yes, under four conditions.**

1. **Name the residual first.** Importance is relative to R, not carried by a link. Fig 11 :49: "An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." Without a stated R, "most important" is a preference, not a term, and Fig 5 :62-64 says nothing in the loop can see a preference. *Centrality* (Wikipedia, network science) independently finds that "The word 'importance' has a wide number of meanings, leading to many different definitions of centrality," and that a measure "appropriate for one category will often 'get it wrong' when applied to a different category."
2. **Keep the residual per slot.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." This is the direct answer to "without oversimplifying the rest." The shortlist may be small; the unranked slots must remain individually addressable, because a global R near zero with one live slot is a legal state.
3. **Enumerate outward past first order.** Fig 11 :44: "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." Fig 11 :52: "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." A first-order shortlist is not a smaller correct shortlist, it is a different object. The climate tipping literature corroborates: "Crossing a threshold in one part of the climate system may trigger another tipping element to tip into a new state." A current Earth system example is the 2026 *Environmental Research Letters* result that "Warming-induced emissions (WIE) of greenhouse gases from natural sources are a large amplifying feedback that most climate models do not yet represent." A link absent from the models is not unimportant; by Fig 3 :27 it is "a link nobody has looked at."
4. **Spend the ranking; do not believe it.** Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." The score decides where to point the next instrument. The ground decides whether the link matters. Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."

**Strong reading, no.** There is no canonical final list. Fig 7 :6 names "the limit: no last room." Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence." The deliverable is a shape with a recorded lineage and a refuter, stopping at the limit of what the available instruments distinguish. *How Intelligence Actually Works*: "The recursion converges toward indiscernibility, and indiscernibility is not identity."

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any particular link is important. This folder contains no shortlist of Earth system links, because that would be a name where the framework returns a shape.
- Not that an importance metric is an anchor. A metric that moves when the framing moves is a frame (Fig 2 :29-30).
- Not that links absent from a model are negligible. They are unassessed (Fig 3 :27, Fig 8 :39).
- Not that averaging is a harmless compression. It is the named failure (Fig 1 :22).
- Not that agreement among researchers settles anything. Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Seat Map :283: "A room does not upgrade the label."
- Not a new primitive. "Network" and "gradient" are already in the six-term set (Fig 13 :22, :24).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here has been settled by an Earth system measurement.

## 4. What would settle it

A discriminator, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*, an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the case "was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." Here the corresponding tests are:

- **Sensitivity of the ranking to R:** does the ranking change when the residual changes in the way the field recognizes as a different question? If never, F1 fails.
- **Per-slot preservation:** can one aggregated score preserve every live slot and predict where to measure next as well as the per-slot field? If yes, F2 fails.
- **Cascade reach:** does first-order enumeration find the third-order residual on model-intercomparison cases? If reliably yes, F3 fails.
- **Ground check without a filter:** does the ranking predict the observed outcome with no separate ground reading? If yes, F4 fails.
- **Instrument check:** does a model that omits a link because no instrument exists for it still predict the residual? If yes, F5 fails.

The five standing falsifiers are written before this conclusion in `01` §3. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** *They're Scanning Books* warns: "A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six." Declaring the axes makes the choice visible, not neutral. We stop at the limit of the available instruments and record the lineage so the stop is checkable. One strain remains live: a continuous Earth system residual field may not be finitely enumerable at a fixed budget. That is unreachable-at-this-budget, for which the remedy is more independent search, not a new atom (Fig 8 :40-43, Formula step 7). It is recorded in `11_audit.md`, not smoothed over.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not hand over a list of the most important links in the Earth system, and it does not claim a final one. It says that "important" is a relation to a stated residual, not a property a link carries; that a single global score averages away the very slot signal that tells you where to look; that the link carrying the residual is often two or three steps out, so a first-order shortlist is a different object rather than a smaller one; and that a ranking is a way to spend a measurement budget, not a verdict, which is still owed by observations, reanalysis or experiment. What it offers is the shape any acceptable shortlist must have, five falsifiers written in advance, and an instrument-shaped import to look for. The rest stays enumerable until the ground settles it.
