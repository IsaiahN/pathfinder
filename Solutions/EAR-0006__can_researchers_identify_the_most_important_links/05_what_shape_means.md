## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."

From *The Library of Babel*: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question." And the crucial condition: "The problem you have determines what makes a book relevant to you or not."

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question:

- "There is a ranked shortlist" is not a shape, it is the question restated.
- "The shortlist must be residual-relative, per-slot, cascade-aware, spent as a budget filter and ground-checked" is a shape. It says what any acceptable answer must look like without saying which links are in it.

The distinction matters because the question asks for names ("the most important links") while the framework can only return the form those names must have. *How Intelligence Actually Works* states the stop plainly: "The recursion converges toward indiscernibility, and indiscernibility is not identity." A shortlist that is indiscernible from the answer is still a projection, which is why the lineage has to be kept.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap with it, recurse. "With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary." The stop condition: "the finish line will also be the starting place." And the warning for this question: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?" That is §0's union split in the essay's own words.
- Fig 6 :30: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." This is the exact reason the outreach email leads with what a failure would look like.
- *How Intelligence Actually Works* adds the horizon term: "Being 'below the union horizon' is never a settled verdict, only a report that nobody has reached it yet." A shortlist that no available instrument discriminates among is unreached, not proven final.

## 3. What licenses a name

Two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays." The README: the framework "stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test. *They're Scanning Books*: "The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space." For this question the import would be a measurement channel from Earth observation or a model intercomparison that can discriminate among candidate links, not a cleverer ranking formula.

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. An instrument reads green and "cannot comment on the source." "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information (it cannot define consciousness in full)." The case "was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." Applied here: several different link structures can produce the same aggregated diagnostic. The aggregate cannot tell you which link did the work. What is owed is a discriminator with per-link resolution, not a better argument about importance.

*They're Scanning Books* adds the honest caveat about the six-term set: "A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six." And: "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible." So calling a flux a "network" or a "gradient" (Fig 13 :22, :24) is a visible choice of compression axes, not a discovery of nature's own list. This is why `01` F7 is `general` and not `grounded`.

## 4. The shape, specifically for "the most important links"

Putting §1 and §2 together, and reading them against Fig 1, Fig 9 and Fig 11, the shape has four load-bearing constraints. These are the same four stated in `01` F10 and `07` §2, deliberately repeated so a reader can check them without reading the whole folder:

1. **Residual-relative.** Importance is a relation between a link and R, not a property of the link (Fig 11 :49-51). Without a named R the ranking is an unbound preference (Fig 5 :62-64).
2. **Per-slot.** R is indexed per object slot (Fig 1 :19). The unranked rest remains addressable, so no global average (Fig 1 :22). *How a Society of Agents Would Work* carries the same rule into a different domain: "A method that is brilliant in one setting must not be drowned by an average across all of them." Its reference list quotes Fig 1 exactly: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
3. **Cascade-aware.** Enumerate outward until the cascade stops mattering (Fig 11 :44, :52). First-order enumeration is a different object, not a smaller one. The *Arrival of the Fittest* citation list names the source directly: "Carson, R. (1962). Silent Spring. ... An instance. Figure 11's cascade warning."
4. **Filter, not verdict.** Rank for budget; let the ground settle (Fig 9 :60, Fig 2 :45). *How a Society of Agents Would Work* restates it as a design rule for groups: "Deadlines are filters, never verdicts."

A name, when it comes, is licensed only by a ground check or a provenanced import. Until then the shortlist is a candidate shape with a lineage.

## 5. Q answered: what licenses calling a link "most important"?

Nothing in the framework. The framework returns a shape. A particular link is licensed only by the ground settling a term or by a provenanced import with a shadow test. Until one of those, "most important" is the searcher's own contribution and must be recorded as such.

Does the question commit the forbidden error? **As asked, yes, in one respect.** It asks for names without naming the residual, so the shape is held as a preference. *How Intelligence Actually Works* describes the cure: "At some point the recursion of the 'shape' will have carved away at so many aspects, that it will fall out of perceptible range and be indistinguishable, in your frame, from the answer." The carved-away aspects here are the four constraints, and they are not optional polish. Removing any one returns a ranking that can mislead.

## 5b. Correction to my own §1 and §3

I overstated the "shape, not a name" rule in the first draft of this file. The README's phrase is "what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." It governs a primitive not held in Γ. "Network" and "gradient" are **both already among the six** (Fig 13 :22, :24), so using them to describe Earth system links is a binding onto held atoms, not the invention of a new primitive. What remains wrong is treating that binding as if it were a derivation or as if it moved the wall (Fig 6 : "IMPORT adds an atom" would add none here). Both the narrower and the wider reading are now recorded, so neither is quietly lost. This is the same correction the exemplar makes at `05` §5b, arrived at independently from Fig 13.

A second narrowing: I initially wrote that the framework "cannot name the most important links at all." That is too strong. It cannot name them *from inside the frame*; a ground-visible expert can name them after a measurement, and that name arrives as a provenanced import, which is exactly why the outreach packet asks an expert rather than asserting a list.

## 6. Two consequences for this question

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige." A campaign that gathers famous names to agree with a shortlist is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask gives the claim a budget to be tried and lets an instrument, a reanalysis or an experiment settle it. This is why `03_public_contact_points.csv` contains venues and channels, never a ranked list of people.

**This audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post." The *Arrival of the Fittest* article adds the caveat: "could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula." So `01`, `07`, `09` and `11` are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are **not** a test of the shape claim and must be labelled as such. They cannot settle anything they report on.

**A group cannot substitute for a ground.** *How a Society of Agents Would Work*: "Ten copies watching the same thing are one opinion said ten times." The article's four rules for keeping a shared store honest are the same laws in a different vocabulary: outcomes not popularity, independent discoveries count most, things fade so they can be tried again, and bad news travels in the same lane as good news. For the Earth system question this says: do not treat the number of model groups that report a link as evidence of its importance if they all ingest the same reanalysis.
