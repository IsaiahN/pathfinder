## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A shortlist of "the most important links" in the Earth system is only well defined after the residual is named. Importance is a relation between a link and that residual, not a property the link carries. A global importance score averages away the per-link signal that says where to look next, so the unranked links must remain individually addressable. The link carrying the residual is often two or three steps out, so a first-order enumeration is not a smaller version of the right shortlist but a different object. A ranking is a way to spend a measurement budget, not a verdict; the verdict is owed by observations, reanalysis, experiments or paleo constraints. This is offered as a shape with falsifiers written in advance, not as a list of links.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - earth-system-facing; checkable by a modeler against their own work

- **Statement:** importance is residual-relative. A ranking that stays the same under two residuals the field treats as different questions contradicts the claim.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49 "An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."
- **World corroboration:** *Centrality* (Wikipedia, network science), "a centrality which is appropriate for one category will often 'get it wrong' when applied to a different category." *Sensitivity analysis* (Wikipedia), "Different statistical tests and measures are applied to the problem and different factors rankings are obtained."
- **Refuter:** exhibit one ranking invariant under a change of residual across two residuals the field treats as different questions. That makes importance intrinsic and breaks C1.

### C2 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 1 and Figure 11

- **Statement:** residuals are indexed per link slot, never aggregated, and the habitat is enumerated outward past first order.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 11 :52 "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." Symbols table :109-110 "Everything in contact with the residual: actors, conditions, relations. Enumerated, never composed."
- **What to check:** does the shortlist keep unranked links individually addressable, and does the enumeration reach beyond direct exchanges? A current test case is the 2026 *Environmental Research Letters* report that "Warming-induced emissions (WIE) of greenhouse gases from natural sources are a large amplifying feedback that most climate models do not yet represent."
- **Refuter:** a single aggregated importance statistic that preserves every per-slot live residual and predicts the next measurement location as well as the per-slot field, or published cases where first-order enumeration reliably finds the third-order residual. Either breaks C2.

### C3 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 9 and Figure 2

- **Statement:** the ranking is a budget filter and the verdict belongs to the ground. No final list is claimable.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Fig 2 :45 "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." Fig 7 :6 "the limit: no last room." Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
- **Refuter:** a ranking that predicts the observed outcome with no separate ground check and survives a change of residual, or a procedure that no further observation could change. Either breaks C3.

## 5. What would settle it

The five falsifiers in `01` §3. In short: invariance of importance under a change of residual; a lossless aggregate; first-order sufficiency for third-order residuals; a filter that hands over a verdict; a final list. Any one firing withdraws the claim. A search that finds none of them does not confirm it (Fig 8 :39).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :49-51 and Fig 1 :19-22, four short passages. Do those two rules match how importance actually behaves in your models?
2. Name one residual you work with and say whether a different residual would reorder your links. If it would not, C1 is in trouble.
3. Look at your own shortlist or parameter-ranking exercise. Does it keep unlisted links individually addressable, or does it fold them into a residual background term?
4. Name one case where the effect you care about was carried by a link more than one step out, or one link missing from the models altogether. The DDT case and the warming-induced-emissions paper are provided examples.
5. Say whether your importance score is used to decide where to measure next or treated as a result. If the latter, C3 is in trouble.
6. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
