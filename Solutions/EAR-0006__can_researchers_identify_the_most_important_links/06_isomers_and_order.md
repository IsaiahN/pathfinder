## DERIVATION

### Q1 - What is a link?

- Fig 13 :24 names a structure "network": "that entities relate, and influence travels." Fig 13 :22 names "gradient": "a difference that can be spent."
- Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
- Operators table :43: "progress is a subtraction, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot say how far along it is." Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."

A link is therefore not a component and not a flux magnitude. It is a **typed relation between two operands**, with direction and a cost. Two components plus an undirected "connected" label has recorded the ingredients and lost the arrangement. In Earth system terms, "the ocean and the atmosphere exchange carbon" is an ingredient list; "atmosphere ⇒ ocean carbon uptake, with the flux declining under warming" names a bond. Under the seven tests the flux is `⇒` production or `→` sequence, and the change in flux is `−` subtraction or `⋛` comparison, not a symmetric `+`. That is precisely why reversing the residual reverses the ranking: an ordered bond does not commute.

- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a shortlist can be written entirely as a symmetric `+` conjunction of components with no loss of predictive content, the bond claim fails and the list really is a bag.

### Q2 - Are two shortlists isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Take two shortlists built over the same component set {atmosphere, ocean, cryosphere, land, biosphere} and the same held structure set. If they differ only in which structure mediates which pair, they are **isomers** under Fig 12: same atoms, different arrangement, different behaviour. If they share only a pattern such as "one state, one structure, one state" while using different components, they are members of the same **functional group**, not isomers. This is the exact distinction the exemplar uses for GR and QM, carried into a different field; the overlap is structural (Fig 12 governs both), so this is internal consistency rather than independent corroboration.

**Falsifier:** if two shortlists with identical atoms and different arrangements produce identical behaviour on a ground-checked case, the isomer claim fails and the difference is notation only.

### Q3 - What order does the shortlist have?

- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."
- Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
- Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
- Fig 3 :29-30: "Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."

The shortlist has **two orders that must not be confused**. The first is dependency order, which belongs to the system: a link whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, and effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it. The second is attention order, which belongs to the budget: which link to measure next. Fig 9 :60 assigns the second to filters. Confusing them produces the error the question is at risk of: treating the most-fundable link as the most-load-bearing one.

Fig 3 :24-26 adds why the middle is where chains usually break: perception and planning have obvious tests, but "having terms for what a situation affords and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments." In the Earth system this is the link between a flux and the diagnostic that would say whether the flux matters for R. A link with no instrument is not negligible; it is unassessed.

- **Falsifier:** if a ground-checked case shows that attention order and dependency order coincide across all reported shortlists, with no counter-case where the most-measured link was downstream of the break, the distinction collapses.

### Q4 - Is the union a compound, and what does that buy?

- Fig 9 :5: "not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."
- Seat Map :211: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either; it stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound." And: "the residue is finally describable."
- *The Library of Babel*: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"

The two conditions are: rank a shortlist, and keep the rest legible. They overlap in the same residual field, so they are a union. Unbundling does not just shrink the pile; it changes the object from a vague request into a compound with describable residue, which is the four constraints in `01` F10.

- **Falsifier:** if both conditions can be met by one scalar without residual loss or a change of object, the union reading fails and the question was a partition after all.

### Q5 - Why no new primitive is owed, and what would have to be imported

- Fig 13 :3: "No system functions without any of them, none is built from the others, and everything else is a composition across the two kinds."
- Fig 13 :115: "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."
- Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

A "most important link" is not a seventh structure. It is an arrangement over held structures, ranked against a residual. What may be missing is not a term but an instrument: a measurement channel that resolves contributions among candidate links. Fig 6 :32: "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something." This is a budget question (unreachable at this budget) rather than an underivability question, and importing a new primitive against a budget limit "adds an atom that was not needed" (Formula step 7). *They're Scanning Books* puts the real import in different words: "importing an axis extends what can be perceived at all." A per-link observational discriminator is an axis, not a term.

- **Falsifier:** exhibit a link contribution that cannot be represented by any composition over the six terms, so that a genuinely new structure is required. That breaks Fig 13 :3 and licenses an import.

## CONCLUSION

A link is a typed, directed bond, not an item in a list. Two shortlists over the same atoms with different arrangements are isomers in the strict sense; those sharing only a pattern are a functional group. The shortlist carries two orders that must be kept apart: dependency order belongs to the system, attention order belongs to the budget. The question's two halves form a union compound, and unbundling is what makes its residue describable. No seventh primitive is owed; what may be owed is a source-discriminating instrument, which is an import of an axis rather than a new term.

This rescues what is defensible in the everyday phrase "most important links" without pretending the framework can hand over a canonical list. What remains `specified` and unmeasured is every claim about which actual links carry which residual in the Earth system.
