## Two findings in the wider literature that bear directly

1. **Unrepresented links are not unimportant links.** The ERL paper on warming-induced emissions reports that a large amplifying feedback is "not yet" represented in most climate models, with consequences for projected warming and carbon budgets. This is Fig 3 :27 in a current research context: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." A shortlist built only from represented links would omit it and still look complete.
2. **The importance list is revisionary.** The tipping-points article documents a list that changed across assessment cycles: ENSO and the Indian summer monsoon were formerly included and later dropped, while other elements were added. That is Fig 11 :51, "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual," written in the vocabulary of climate science. Do not present any shortlist as settled.

## The two-stage offer

**Stage 1.** Residual, shape claim, four conditions, refuter. No framework, no links, no request for agreement or trust. The recipient's verdict is recorded here.

**Stage 2, opt-in.** If they are curious, offer the linked figures and formula. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because they now hold the interior (Fig 2 :40).

**Stage 3, opt-in.** Offer the eight-step loop as a method. The framework's own limit applies: a frame cannot check the direction of its own question when the sought-for shape is held as a preference (Fig 5 :62-64), so each user writes down the shape and the refuter before running it.

**Hard precondition.** All three stages depend on stage 1 surviving. This agent has not checked the shape claim against an Earth system observation. No such check is claimed here. Hosting and sending the packet is yours to do; this folder does not send anything.
