# Outreach draft: check the shape before you decide which side to take

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until a ground reading exists for at least one domain. The stage-1 output is a shape, not a verdict. If the recipient reads it as a verdict, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** A gig-work question that may be two questions, not one

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the form, `specified` on the case, not `grounded`):** the question "is gig work more efficient, or does it shift costs onto workers?" is phrased as an exclusive-or. The framework's residual is indexed per party, never aggregated. When I describe the parties, "efficiency" has an anchor in matching data (fill rates, search time, finding rates) and "cost and insecurity" has an anchor in worker net outcome and in statute. Those are two different quantities with two different anchors, and they can both move at once. So the either/or is a union compound, not a partition. The answer should be given once per party, in two columns, with no net score. This is a shape, not a measurement.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that gig work is efficient, that it is exploitative, that either side of the slogan wins, or that one study settles it. I am not claiming that a platform's own efficiency metric is the ground. The framework returns a kind of thing, never the answer itself, so the claim stops at the unbundling.
>
> **What would refute this shape claim, written before sending:**
> 1. a single common measure on which efficiency and cost transfer are the same quantity in the same units for the same party, so the question really is a partition;
> 2. a measured case in which raising matching efficiency necessarily lowers worker net outcome, or vice versa, across parties and settings;
> 3. a demonstration that the firm's own efficiency metric is constitutively what the question is about, so worker outcome is only an instrument.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed. If you answer, I will record your verdict on this step before showing anything else.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`

> - Kanayama and Otani, matching efficiency on a spot work platform, arXiv: https://arxiv.org/html/2412.19024v3
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the formula is in the figures. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Two-stage offer, made explicit

1. **Stage 1:** send the union shape and the three refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record the answer before anything else is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if curious, send the four links, with the note that the figures are the authority and this summary is not.
3. **Stage 3, opt-in only:** offer the formula as a method to reuse. Each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration: Fig 2 :40 *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* and Fig 7 :13 *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*.
