# Expert verification packet: the Tether on gig work

**What this is:** a claim in a form a labour economist, economic sociologist or labour lawyer can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the domain claims, not `grounded`.** No measurement was taken by this run. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "is gig work more efficient, or does it shift costs onto workers?" is a union compound, not a partition. Efficiency registers against matching data; cost and insecurity register against worker net outcome and statute. Both can be positive at once, because the same classification lowers cost for the firm and raises risk for the worker. The answer should therefore be given once per party, in two columns, with no aggregated score and no final room. The Tether offers the shape, three pre-registered refuters, and the ground readings that would close each domain. It does not take a side.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 9, 1 and 12

- **Statement:** the either/or is a union compound and must be unbundled; R is per slot; the two quantities are two currencies that must not be summed.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54 *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :57 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 12 :125 *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 12 :130 *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."*
- **Refuter:** exhibit one common measure in which efficiency and cost transfer are the same quantity in the same units for the same party, or a measured necessary tradeoff between them across settings. Then the partition reading wins and this claim fails.

### C2 - world-facing; checkable against the cited studies

- **Statement:** empirical work already registers the two quantities separately, and an intervention can move both at once.
- **What to check:** Johnston, Ergun, Schor and Chen (*Socio-Economic Review* 22(1), 2024; open record https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10560167-employment-status-demand-economy-natural-experiment-reclassification) report that after reclassification *"flexibility with respect to how work schedules were determined was maintained"* and *"the switch to employee status increased the firm's ability to match scheduled and actual working time, indicating greater operational efficiency."* Habans (UCLA IRLE, 2015) reports *"independent contractor status saves businesses between 29 and 39 cents for every dollar of pay"* and raises the question of where those costs land. Kanayama and Otani (arXiv:2412.19024v3, 2026) report *"80% of the available jobs are successfully filled"* on a spot platform. Are these faithful characterisations? Do the sources draw on one another, and if so are they one witness rather than three?
- **Refuter:** show that the studies share a single evidence pool or author lineage such that their convergence is one witness; or show that the operational-efficiency measure and the cost-shift measure are the same variable. Either collapses the two-currency claim.

### C3 - framework-internal plus law; checkable by reading Figure 11 and the classification rule

- **Statement:** the classification actor has opposite valence in the firm domain and the worker domain; "insecurity" is a statutory anchor, not a sentiment.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49 *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :50 *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* Fig 2 :36 *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."*
- **What to check:** Mollie Pinion Harper (*Wake Forest Law Review Online*, 2025) writes that *"the main benefit of operating as an independent contractor is the classification's namesake: independence, in exchange for foregoing employment rights."* Does the jurisdiction in question treat status as a statute-backed anchor, and does that anchor move when a platform's stated position changes?
- **Refuter:** exhibit an actor whose valence is fixed across all domains, or a jurisdiction where status is determined solely by platform opinion so that no statutory anchor exists. Then the relation claim fails in that jurisdiction.

## 5. What would settle it

Ground readings per domain, not more agreement. (1) Finding rates and search time against a public comparator. (2) Net worker income after equipment, insurance and unpaid waiting, against a standard-employment comparator. (3) Audited firm costs avoided and liability retained. (4) The applicable statute and ruling. (5) Observed prices and service quality. A common measure across these, or a measured necessary tradeoff, would refute the union shape.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :54-57, Fig 1 :19, Fig 12 :125 and :130, Fig 11 :49-50, Fig 2 :36 (six short passages).
2. Check C2 against the open NSF record and the arXiv paper. Are the quoted findings faithful? Are the sources independent?
3. Name one instrument that would register worker net outcome in the same platform where matching efficiency is measured, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation if there is one. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. Recorded verdicts are kept before any framework material is shown.
