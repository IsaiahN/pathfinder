## 0. The claim under audit

> Is gig work a more efficient form of flexible employment, or does it transfer costs and insecurity onto workers?

**Bound shape, written before the run.** An answer is checkable when it names, for each party (worker, platform firm, consumer, regulator), the quantity at stake, the anchor that settles it, and the net position on that anchor; and when it says whether the two named effects are one quantity or two. If they are two, an exclusive-or is a malformed question and the answer must be given per slot. This binds the sought-for shape as an ordinary term so it can accrue a residual and be refused (Formula "Direction"; Fig 5 :65-66).

**Residual in effect terms.** Something changed when work was reorganised from standard employment into platform-mediated gig work. One reading says the change removed waste (better matching, flexibility, lower search cost). The other says the change moved costs (equipment, insurance, unpaid waiting, income variance, legal protection) from the firm to the worker. In effect terms: `R_s = |Γ(b,a) − o′|` per party `s`. The question as written aggregates over `s` and asks for one global answer. That aggregation is the defect the figures bear on first.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.** Arity: two named conditions (efficiency gain, cost transfer), phrased as exclusive-or. Symmetry: not symmetric. "More efficient" is a threshold comparison of two quantities; "transfer costs" is a movement of an operand across parties. They are different relations, not two spellings of one. Scale: individual worker slot, firm balance sheet, market matching, legal classification. Four scales, so no single global reading is licensed.

**One line per domain. A domain needs a nameable anchor.**

| domain | actors | currency | anchor (nameable?) |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 matching and allocation | platform algorithm, workers, consumers | fill rate, search time, utilisation, vacancy elasticity | yes: measured hires and finding rates |
| D2 worker cost and risk | worker, vehicle/equipment, household | net pay after costs, unpaid waiting, accident rate, income variance | yes: worker outcome records and surveys |
| D3 firm balance sheet | platform firm, shareholders, insurers | labour cost avoided, capex avoided, liability | yes: firm accounts and classification law |
| D4 legal classification and protection | legislator, court, regulator, worker | employee vs contractor status, benefits owed | yes: statute and ruling |
| D5 consumer and market | consumers, rival platforms | price, convenience, service quality | yes: consumer surplus, observed prices |

All five have nameable anchors, so all five are domains. No domain is admitted on a metaphor alone.

**Partition or union.** Union, not partition. D1 and D2 can both register positive residuals at the same time: a platform can fill more vacancies faster while the worker keeps the vehicle cost and the income variance. D3 is not a third side of the same argument, it is the same event seen from the firm's slot. D4 is where "insecurity" becomes nameable at all (a statute, not a sentiment). Under Fig 9 :54 *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* The exclusive-or must be dissolved into one loop per domain.

**Dependency order (Fig 3).** Perception (what is being measured) before vocabulary (terms for what each party affords) before objective (what counts as progress, for whom) before planning before learn and carry. The question's objective link is the break: it never says whose efficiency or whose cost counts, so effort spent measuring aggregate matching efficiency arrives at a step that cannot use it (Fig 3 :20 *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*).

**Strain test applied while censusing.** My first pass tried to place "cost transfer" under Fig 12's subtraction bond (Operators :46 *"progress is a subtraction, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot say how far along it is"*). That strained: subtraction removes an operand from an arrangement, while a transfer moves an operand between parties. Recensus: Operators :52 *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"* governs "more efficient", and the movement itself is a cross-slot composition, not a within-slot bond. The first mapping is retracted and kept visible in §6.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The exclusive-or is a union compound, not a partition (decisive for the shape)

Fig 9 :54 *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
Fig 9 :57 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
Fig 9 :5 *"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*

"Efficiency" is well formed in D1 (fill rates). "Cost transfer" is well formed in D2 and D4 (net worker outcome, statute). Each rule works well on its own subset; the disagreement does not shrink with effort because the two subsets are not the same question. So this is not one hard question to be answered by choosing A or B. It is a union with its own loop per domain.

The world corroborates without overruling: Johnston, Ergun, Schor and Chen (*Socio-Economic Review* 22(1), 2024) found that when a delivery platform reclassified contractors as employees, *"flexibility with respect to how work schedules were determined was maintained"* and *"the switch to employee status increased the firm's ability to match scheduled and actual working time, indicating greater operational efficiency"* (NSF PAR record, opened 2026-11-23). One intervention moved both quantities at once. That is the union shape at the ground.

**Refuter:** if a single common measure existed on which "efficiency" and "cost transfer" were the same quantity in the same units for the same party, so that raising one necessarily lowered the other, the union reading fails and the exclusive-or is a genuine partition. Also refuted if the world shows a case where one condition cannot be registered at all without the other being false.

### F2 - R is per slot; the question aggregates and loses the signal

Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
Fig 1 :28 *"The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."*

"Is gig work more efficient?" asks for a global number over workers, firms and consumers. "Does it transfer costs onto workers?" asks one slot. Averaging the two produces a number that says where to look nowhere. The fix is not to weigh the options; it is to keep the slots separate. This is `general` because it follows from the residual form, not from any labour-market fact.

**Refuter:** display a measured quantity that is invariant to how parties are grouped, such that per-party and pooled readings coincide on it. Then the aggregation objection is empty for that quantity.

### F3 - Two currencies, and summing them is the named error

Fig 12 :125 *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*
Fig 12 :126 *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*
Fig 12 :130 *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."*
Operators :52 *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*

"More efficient" is a comparison (⋛): a threshold relation between two quantities. "Transfers costs" is a movement of an operand between parties. They are in different units (fill rate versus net dollars and risk). Adding them into one "net efficiency" score, as both campaign slogans and some policy summaries do, is the exact error Fig 12 names. The right output is two readings held apart.

World corroboration at low overlap: Robert Habans (UCLA Institute for Research on Labor and Employment, 2015) found that *"independent contractor status saves businesses between 29 and 39 cents for every dollar of pay"* and added that *"These estimates raise deeper questions regarding how these costs are shi[ft]ed onto workers and onto the public sector."* The saving and the shift are two readings of one arrangement, and the paper itself refuses to add them. Hayato Kanayama and Suguru Otani (arXiv:2412.19024v3, 2026) find *"80% of the available jobs are successfully filled"* in a spot platform, a matching measure that says nothing about who paid for the vehicle. Formal theory corroborates from a third closure: Hagiu and Wright (2019) state *"The effect of worker misclassification on worker welfare can go in either direction in our model."*

**Refuter:** produce a common unit in which a marginal gain in matching efficiency and a marginal dollar of cost shifted to the worker are commensurable, with an instrument that measures both. Until then this finding stands as `general` on the form and `specified` on the gig case.

### F4 - Each domain has its own legitimate anchor; the question is constitutively about people

Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
Fig 2 :36 *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."*
Fig 2 :37 *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."*

D1's anchor is measured matching data; D2's is the worker's net outcome; D4's is the statute. None of these moves when a platform's public relations frame changes its answer. Because the question is constitutively about workers' outcomes, a platform's own efficiency score is an instrument on an axis it does not measure, not the anchor. Fig 1 :27 *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*

**Refuter:** show that the firm's own efficiency metric is constitutively the thing the question is about, so that workers' net outcomes are merely an instrument for it. That would invert the anchor and flip this finding.

### F5 - The same actor is benefactor to one residual and malfactor to another

Fig 11 :49 *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
Fig 11 :50 *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."*
Fig 11 :40 *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

Independent-contractor classification is one actor. Relative to D3 (firm cost) it is a benefactor; relative to D2 (worker risk) a malfactor. Calling it "efficient" or "insecure" as a property of the arrangement is the error the figure names. Valence is a relation to a residual. This is why both options in the question can be true at once without contradiction.

World corroboration from law: Mollie Pinion Harper (*Wake Forest Law Review Online*, 2025) writes that *"the main benefit of operating as an independent contractor is the classification's namesake: independence, in exchange for foregoing employment rights."* One classification, two valences, in the same sentence.

**Refuter:** exhibit an actor whose valence is fixed across every residual in the habitat, so that the relation reading collapses into a kind. No such actor was found, but the claim is falsifiable.

### F6 - The dependency chain breaks at the objective link

Fig 3 :20 *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*
Fig 3 :27 *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
Fig 3 :29 *"Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."*

The question can perceive gig work (it is visible). It has vocabulary (flexibility, cost, security). It has no stated objective, because "efficient for whom" is never said. That is the middle link the figure says attracts the least attention. Work done above it (more efficiency statistics) cannot arrive at a step that has no objective to consume it. The remedy is to state the objective per domain before measuring, not to measure harder.

**Refuter:** show an instrument that already registers "progress" on this question without stating whose progress it is. If one exists, the link is not broken and this finding is withdrawn.

### F7 - No new primitive is owed; a compound is owed

Fig 5 :33 *"The three guards are a product, not a checklist."*
Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*
Fig 4 :22 *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*

A mint (a new term) would need support, reachability and novelty (Formula step 3). All the vocabulary needed here, matching, cost, risk, status, is already in the library. What is missing is not an atom but an unbundling. Figure 9 gives a kind of thing: a union compound with one loop per domain and an anchor per loop. It does not give the answer. The honest stop is a shape plus refuters, not a verdict.

**Refuter:** derive a genuinely new construct that is neither matching nor cost nor status and that closes R in every domain at lower description cost. Then a mint is owed and this finding is narrowed.

### F8 - Filters are not witnesses

Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
Fig 2 :42 *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

A question's tier, the prestige of a source, and the number of people who already agree are budget filters. They decide what gets tried first and how much independence to buy. They never decide whether the per-domain verdicts are true. This is why `03_public_contact_points.csv` lists venues, not believers.

**Refuter:** none in principle, this is a rule this framework imposes on itself. It can be shown inconsistent if a filter is used as evidence and the resulting verdict survives a ground reading, which would mean the rule was too strong.

### F9 - No final either/or verdict: there is no last room

Fig 7 :13 *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*
Fig 7 :35 *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*
Fig 8 :39 *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

A final answer that declared one side of the either/or and closed the question would be a terminal room. The figures forbid a last room. The compound stays open at the limit of the instruments, with the lineage recorded. "No answer yet" is not silence and is not a failure.

**Refuter:** a ground reading that settles every domain at once, so that no further handoff could change any verdict. That would be a last room and would break the framework's own rule.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A single common anchor is exhibited on which "efficiency" and "cost transfer" are the same quantity in the same units for the same party, so the question is a genuine partition (breaks F1 and F3).
2. A measured case shows that raising matching efficiency necessarily lowers worker net outcome, or vice versa, across all parties and all institutional settings (breaks the union reading).
3. The platform's own efficiency metric is shown to be constitutively what the question is about, so worker outcome is only an instrument (breaks F4).
4. An actor is found whose valence is fixed across every residual in the habitat (breaks F5).
5. Every domain is settled by one ground reading that leaves no further handoff possible (breaks F9).

If any fires, the union/unbundling verdict is withdrawn. Until one is run, no row in F10 may be labelled `grounded`. As Fig 6 :32 says from the world-search side, *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* The instruments exist here; they have not been run by this simulated derivation.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Do not answer the either/or. State the union, then answer one loop per domain with its own anchor (F1, F10).
2. Keep the two currencies in two columns. Do not publish a single "net efficiency" score (F3).
3. State whose efficiency and whose cost before measuring anything (F6).
4. Withhold any claim of `grounded` until a ground reading is run per domain. Report `specified` with the refuter attached (F4, F7).
5. Offer the sub-claims and the refuter, not the framework, through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv` (Fig 10 :52, Fig 4 :22).
6. Treat agreement and source prestige as budget filters only (F8).

## 7. Bottom line

`general`: the question is malformed as an exclusive-or. It is a union compound and must be unbundled. `specified`: efficiency and cost transfer can both be true, in different domains, against different anchors, and neither is settled by this run. `grounded`: unreached. The pre-registered refuter is above and has not fired; a failure from an outside frame is more useful than agreement.
