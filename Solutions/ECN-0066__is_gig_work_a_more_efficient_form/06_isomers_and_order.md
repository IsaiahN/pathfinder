# Union, order and analogues: are the two options isomers?

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question offers two readings of one arrangement. Are they isomers under Fig 12 (same atoms, different bond, different substance), or are they a union of two conditions with different anchors? And in what order must they be handled?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: worker, firm, consumer, regulator; matching data, net outcome, statute. Bonds held: comparison ⋛ ("more efficient"), movement of an operand across parties ("transfer"), status assignment (contractor or employee). No new primitive is available without import.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are "efficient" and "cost-transfer" isomers? NO

- Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :124 *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- The two readings do not share an atom set. The efficiency reading uses `{worker, firm, matching data}`; the cost reading uses `{worker, net outcome, statute}`. They share only the worker. Under Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**; they are two members of a larger platform-work family, distinguished by which anchor is used.
- A stronger isomer claim is available but is **not** made here: if both readings were written over the full set `{worker, firm, consumer, regulator, matching data, net outcome, statute}`, differing only in which anchor binds which party, they would be a real isomer claim. That would require showing the anchors are commensurable at one level, which F3 of `01` refuses.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if both readings can be written over one shared atom set with only the bond differing, and a measurement shows the same substance (same net outcome distribution) under both bonds, the isomer claim is confirmed and the union claim is unnecessary. No such measurement was read.

### Q2 - What is the order of operations?

- Fig 3 :20 *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* Perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry.
- Fig 9 :53 *"Nobody chooses. The gap does, and the gap is only as good as its description."*
- Order: (1) describe R per party; (2) decide partition or union (Fig 9 :54); (3) name an anchor per domain (Fig 2 :36); (4) keep the two currencies apart (Fig 12 :125); (5) refuse the net-score move (Fig 12 :130); (6) hand each domain to its ground. Steps 3 and 4 assume step 2, so doing them in the other order is how the question gets stuck.
- Formula step 2 sorts R before minting: a union compound needs a split, not a new term. Fig 9 :56 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*

### Q3 - Analogues as the question needs them

- **Fig 9 union / partition.** The closest analogue and the governing one: high-overlap conditions are one frame with two names; low-overlap conditions are a union with a surplus. D1 (matching) and D2 (net outcome) are low overlap.
- **Fig 11 mutualism and parasitism.** Fig 11 :50 *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* Independent-contractor status is the actor. This is the mechanism that lets both options be true, and it is not a biological import: the figure states the structural rule directly.
- **Fig 7 room chain.** A sequence of single-issue studies is a chain only if the next study is chosen against the residual the last one surfaced (Fig 7 :36 *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."*). Repeated surveys of "flexibility" are one long room.
- **Fig 4 round trip.** A policy slogan is a recording carried upward from one study; applying it to another jurisdiction is a replay, not a method. Fig 4 :25 *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*

### Q4 - Is anything here a catalyst, cycle or functional group?

- Fig 12 :119 *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."* Classification law survives each individual contract and shapes the next one; it is a catalyst in D4. It is not an atom that can be spent.
- Fig 12 :121 *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* A platform's efficiency gain can be reinvested in more matching, which is a positive loop in D1; the same gain can feed lower pay in D2. Whether the loop closes depends on a rule in D4, so the cycle is not decidable inside D1.
- The functional-group reading (Q1) is the one that survives.

## CONCLUSION

The two options are **not isomers**. They are two members of a platform-work family with different anchors, and they form a union rather than a partition. The order is split first, then anchor, then currency, then ground. The analogues that bear are the union/partition rule (Fig 9), valence relative to residual (Fig 11), and the round-trip warning (Fig 4). The isomer claim is demoted to a stated falsifier, not a finding.
