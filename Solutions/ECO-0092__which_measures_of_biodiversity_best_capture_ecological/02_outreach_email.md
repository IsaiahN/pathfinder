# Outreach draft: check one output before deciding what to measure

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the union split in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F1 and the overlap test in F2 are stated plainly in the recipient's own terms. The output must survive a refuter check or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** A shape claim about biodiversity measures, with the refuter written first

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a question that asks for the measure that best captures ecological difference, evolutionary history and functional importance is a bundle of three questions over one species list, not one hard question. The figures this work is derived from say to split a union before searching it, to keep the readout indexed per slot rather than averaging across slots, and to count two measures as one witness when they draw on the same evidence. So the shape any good answer must have is: three readouts, one per target, each with its own anchor, reported per species or lineage or function, with the overlap between them measured rather than assumed. The framework returns a shape, not a name, so it does not tell you which index wins.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that Shannon, Simpson, Hill numbers, Faith's phylogenetic diversity or Rao's quadratic entropy is *the* answer. Those are bindings from the ecology frame onto the shape. A binding is licensed by provenance and a ground reading, not by the framework. I am also not claiming that three readouts are automatically independent. Different names are not independence; if two columns correlate near perfectly on your communities, they are one witness and I will count them as one.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. A representative set of communities in which a change in one target never co-occurs with an opposing change in another. Then the three targets are one quantity and the split is unnecessary.
> 2. A single aggregate score that is injective from the joint slot vector for all three targets, so no live slot can be hidden.
> 3. A case where a species' functional importance ranking is invariant across habitats and residual definitions with no cascade effect.
> 4. A ground reading that names one index settling all three targets with no residual left on any one.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the affected claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
