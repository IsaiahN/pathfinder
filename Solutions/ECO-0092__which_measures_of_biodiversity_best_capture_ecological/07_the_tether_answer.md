# The Tether's answer on biodiversity measures

**Mode: `specified` on the per-slot and independence predictions, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A practitioner must choose a readout for a community and is asked for the one that best captures ecological difference, evolutionary history and functional importance. The residual is that a readout chosen for one target systematically fails to register a change in another, and the failure is invisible when the three are folded into one score. It is a **union compound** over one species list, not one hard question. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* It has its own loop and its own ground.

## 2. The shape

1. **Split before you measure.** Fig 9 :5: *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* Name the residual for each target separately, in effect terms, before selecting an index. The three targets overlap at the atoms but bind differently, so a single lever clears only the region where one condition holds.

2. **Keep three low-overlap readouts, one per target.** Fig 13 :36-38: *"If two members drew on the same underlying fact, their agreement would carry no information, and one of them would be redundant rather than minimal."* Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Different names are not independence. Measure the overlap; if two readouts are near-invertible on the communities in question, count them as one witness, not two. Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

3. **Report per slot, never a single aggregate.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Publish which species, lineage or function moved, not only the scalar. A global score near zero with one live slot is a legal state and not an inert one.

4. **Functional importance is a relation, not an intrinsic property.** Fig 11 :49: *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Price a species' importance against a named process in a named habitat, enumerate the habitat by contact, and follow the cascade outward. Fig 11 :44: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."*

5. **The framework returns a shape, not the name of an index.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* A Hill number, Faith's PD or Rao's Q is a binding from the ecology frame onto the shape, licensed by provenance and a ground reading, not derived by the figures. Over the shared species set the facets stand as Fig 12 :117's isomers: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

6. **Cost and convenience are filters, never witnesses.** Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* Sampling cost and ease of computation decide what gets tried first. Only a measurement settles a binding.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any named index is the winner. That slot is a `gap` owed to the ground, reported as a reach verdict rather than a silence (Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."*).
- Not that three readouts are independent because they have three names. Correlated readouts are one witness.
- Not that a diversity profile's order q is a fourth biodiversity facet. It is an ordering over sensitivity within one readout, a spectrum term, not a new member (Fig 13 :19-20).
- Not that the three-target list is nature's own list. It is a human framing made visible by being declared.
- Not `grounded`. No field measurement in this derivation settles `left(R,φ)` for any index.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument reading green *"cannot comment on the source,"* and *"It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."* Here the corresponding questions are:

- **Union test:** on a representative set of communities, does a change in one target ever co-occur with an opposing change in another? If never, the split is unnecessary.
- **Overlap test:** what is the measured redundancy among the three readouts on those communities? If two are invertible transformations of one another, drop one. Mouchet et al. 2010 and Lyashevska and Farnsworth 2012 report substantial redundancy among widely used indices, which is the world-side form of this test.
- **Per-slot test:** can any aggregate score preserve every slot residual without collision? An injective map from the joint slot vector to one number would refute the per-slot requirement.
- **Context test:** does a species' functional importance ranking stay invariant across habitats and residual definitions with no cascade effect? If yes, importance is intrinsic and the valence rule fails.
- **Ground test for a name:** does a monitoring outcome or experiment show one index settling all three targets with no residual left on any one? That would fill the name slot and end the `gap`.

The standing falsifiers written before the run are in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 5 and are repeated in `08_expert_verification_packet.md`.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The three-target framing is a declared framing, not discovered nature. Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* The ground here is a field measurement of the target process. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Indiscernibility is not identity.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name a winning biodiversity index. It says the question is a bundle of three questions over one species list, so it must be split before an instrument is chosen; that the three readouts must be kept per species, lineage or function rather than averaged into one score; that they count as separate witnesses only to the degree they draw on different evidence, so redundancy has to be measured rather than assumed; and that functional importance is a relation between a species and a named process in a named habitat, not a property carried by the species. Over the shared species set the facets behave as isomers: same atoms, different bonds, different behaviour. What the framework returns is that shape, plus falsifiers written in advance and the instruments that would close it. Which index wins stays open until the ground settles it.
