## Q1: Are two indices over one species set isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :95-96: **"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."**
- Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**

A species list with counts is an ingredient list. A richness readout bonds species to presence; a Hill number of order q bonds species to frequency raised to q; a phylogenetic readout bonds species to branch length; a functional readout bonds species to a trait and a process. Same atoms, different bonds, different behaviour. That is the isomer claim.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if one readout requires an atom the others do not (for example if functional importance cannot be written over the same species set without importing a new primitive), the isomer claim fails and the weaker functional-group reading stands: Fig 12 :123, *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* The three readouts would then be members of one family, not isomers.

**Status:** triangulated for the figure definition (Fig 12 plus the canonical figures README's isomer discussion); the shared-atom premise is an unmeasured ecology-facing condition. Kept as a live condition, not hidden.

## Q2: Why "order" has two senses here

**Sense A, bond order (arrangement).** Fig 12 :70: sequence is *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"* and is ordered: *"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."* Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."* A profile is not a list of numbers in arbitrary order; the relation between q values carries meaning.

**Sense B, statistical order q.** Fig 13 :19-20 names **spectrum** as a structure: *"ordering, so more and less can be said."* Jost 2006 gives the ecology instance: *"The order of a diversity indicates its sensitivity to common and rare species."* At q = 0 the readout is richness, indifferent to frequencies; q = 1 weights each species by frequency; q > 1 favours the common species. The Hill-number family is therefore a **spectrum**, not a single point, and the figure's structure term licenses treating it as an ordering over which more and less can be said.

Derivation:

1. The six-term set includes spectrum, a structure whose deciding question is *"it names what is being measured"* for the states side and *"it decides what the measurement would mean"* for the structures side (Fig 13 :16, :26).
2. A diversity profile with parameter q is an ordering over sensitivity, so it composes as a spectrum term over the state "community composition."
3. A single index is one slice of that spectrum. Choosing q = 0 and reporting only richness is a projection, which is the metamerism error of `05` section 4.
4. Therefore "which measure is best" is not well posed until the objective names the region of the spectrum that matters. The framework returns a shape: a profile with a stated order and a stated objective, not a winning scalar.

**Falsifier:** if two different values of q on the same community give identical readouts for all communities, the spectrum collapses to a point and Sense B has no content. Jost 2006 refutes this for real communities, but the ground reading here is from the literature, not from this derivation.

**Status:** single-source for the figure licence (Fig 13 :19-20), corroborated from a low-overlap field by Jost 2006 (mathematics and information theory). Not triangulated inside the figure set.

## Q3: The three targets as members of a frame set, not readings of one number

- Fig 13 :35: **"Space, time and energy are not three readings of one quantity."**
- Fig 13 :36: **"cannot: adjacency is not duration, an ordering is not a pathway, a pathway is not a direction."**
- Fig 13 :36-38: **"If two members drew on the same underlying fact, their agreement would carry no information, and one of them would be redundant rather than minimal."**

Applied by structural analogy, not by name: ecological difference, evolutionary history and functional importance are three members of a measurement frame set. Each answers a question the other two cannot. If two of them draw on the same underlying fact (measured as near-perfect correlation across communities), they are the same witness twice and one should be dropped from the verdict count.

**Falsifier:** an invertible map between two readouts on all communities. That would make them one member with two names and break the three-frame shape.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 13 and Fig 2 :31 (independence is how little two frames draw on the same evidence). The binding of the three ecological targets onto the structural rule is a provenanced import, recorded as such in `01` F10 where the stronger six-term mapping was withdrawn.

## Q4: What order composes the union?

- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."**
- Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**

Each first-order readout (one target, one community) is settled separately against the ground. Only then can it serve as an atom for the second-order decision about which combination of readouts to report. A combination chosen before the first-order readouts are settled is a step whose input never arrives, which Fig 3 :22 calls *"a reading of nothing."*

**Falsifier:** show that a settled first-order readout cannot become an atom for a second-order reporting decision. That would make the union uncomposable and force a scalar index by structure rather than by convenience.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 12 :104 and Fig 3 :20-22; both are figure-internal but they start from different objects (level relativity versus dependency order).

## CONCLUSION

Two indices over the same species set are isomers, or at minimum members of one functional group, under Fig 12. The statistical "order q" of Hill numbers is a spectrum term under Fig 13 :19-20, so a diversity profile is a family of ordered readouts and a single index is one projection of it. The three targets behave as three members of a frame set whose independence must be measured, not assumed. The union is second order and composes only over settled first-order readouts.

This rescues what is defensible in the intuition that there is a "best" biodiversity measure without forcing a name: what is best is a **protocol shape** (per slot, three low-overlap readouts, a stated order on the profile, provenance, ground reading), and the name of the index is owed by the ground. The shared-atom premise remains a live empirical condition with a stated falsifier.
