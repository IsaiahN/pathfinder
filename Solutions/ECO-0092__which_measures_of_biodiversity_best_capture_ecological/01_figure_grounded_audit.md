## 0. The question under audit, stated as an effect

> Which measures of biodiversity best capture ecological difference, evolutionary history, and functional importance?

Restated in effect terms: a practitioner must choose a readout for a biological community. Three candidate targets are named in the question: **ecological difference** (who is present and how they differ), **evolutionary history** (what lineage depth and divergence are represented), and **functional importance** (what the species do and what happens if they go). The question asks which *measure* best captures all three.

What each option leads to:

- **Option A, one best scalar index.** A single number is convenient for reporting and comparison. It collapses three targets into one ordering, so a change in one target can be masked by an opposing change in another. The per-slot signal is lost (Fig 1 :19, :22). Two practitioners using the same number can agree while drawing on one evidence pool, which is disqualifying for a verdict (Fig 2 :40).
- **Option B, three low-overlap readouts kept separate.** Each target gets its own frame with its own anchor and its own ground reading. Agreement between them means something only if they draw on different evidence (Fig 2 :31; Fig 13 :35-39). The cost is three numbers instead of one and a provenance record for each binding.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term (formula step "Direction"): a **shape** with four properties, (i) per slot and not aggregated, (ii) one frame per target with low overlap between frames, (iii) ground-anchored rather than self-scored, and (iv) provenance recorded for every binding of a named index onto a figure term. The sought-for shape is written down here before the run so it can accrue a residual and be refused by the same test as everything else (Fig 5 :62-66; formula "Direction").

The honest boundary: the figures return a shape, not a name. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* They cannot return "the best measure is Shannon" or "the best measure is Faith's PD." Naming an index is a binding from the ecology frame, provenanced as such, and is owed a ground reading before it can be called settled.

## 1. Residual, arity, symmetry, scale

**R (the residual).** A readout chosen to capture one biodiversity target systematically fails to register a change in another target, and the failure is invisible when the three are averaged or ranked into one score.

- **Arity:** three conditions, not one. Ecological difference, evolutionary history and functional importance are three different questions about the same community. Fig 9 :5: *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*
- **Symmetry:** the three targets are not readings of one quantity. Fig 13 :35: *"Space, time and energy are not three readings of one quantity."* The figure's point generalises: where two measures draw on the same underlying fact, their agreement carries no information (Fig 13 :36-38). There is no symmetric conversion that trades one target for another without loss.
- **Scale:** first order within one community and one target, second order at the comparison across targets. A site-level richness count and a site-level phylogenetic distance are first-order readouts over the same species slots; the decision about which combination to report is second order, composed over settled first-order molecules (Fig 12 :104).

**Union or partition.** Union. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* The same species list feeds all three readouts, so the conditions overlap at the atoms. That overlap is why a scalar index can appear to resolve all three while actually reporting the intersection. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*

**Dependency order (Fig 3).** Perception of which slots exist (species, lineages, functions) precedes vocabulary for what each slot affords; vocabulary precedes an objective stated per target; the objective precedes choice of index; choice of index precedes comparison. Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* An index chosen before the objective is a step whose input never arrives, which Fig 3 :22 calls *"a reading of nothing."*

## 4. Findings

### F1: The question is a union of three questions, and must be split before an index is chosen (decisive at the structural level)

Fig 9 :5: **"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."**
Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**

The three targets overlap at the species list but bind differently: ecological difference is a comparison among present entities, evolutionary history is an ordering over lineage depth, functional importance is a relation between a trait and a process under a condition. A single lever (one index) clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55-56).

**Mode:** `general` on the structural claim that the question is a union; `specified` on the prediction that a scalar index will mask at least one target in at least one community.

**Refuter:** show that the three targets are a partition, that is, that a measured change in one never co-occurs with an opposing change in another over a representative set of communities. If no such co-occurrence can be found, splitting is unnecessary and F1 fails.

**Triangulation:** triangulated across Fig 9 and Fig 13 (low overlap: one governs question splitting, the other governs member independence). World corroboration is independent of the figure evidence pool: Purvis and Hector 2000 write that *"although biodiversity can never be fully captured by a single number"*; Lyashevska and Farnsworth 2012 report *"Biodiversity seems irreducibly three-dimensional."* These are corroboration only, not the verdict.

### F2: The three targets must be independent frames; if two draw on the same fact, one is redundant

Fig 13 :35: **"Space, time and energy are not three readings of one quantity."**
Fig 13 :36-37: **"Each answers a question the other two cannot: adjacency is not duration, an ordering is not a pathway, a pathway is not a direction."**
Fig 13 :36-38: **"If two members drew on the same underlying fact, their agreement would carry no information, and one of them would be redundant rather than minimal."**
Fig 13 :38-39: **"Six members that overlapped would be fewer members wearing six names."**
Fig 2 :31: **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."**
Fig 2 :40: **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**

Applied to D2: taxonomic richness, phylogenetic diversity and functional diversity are useful *together* only to the degree that they do not reduce to one another. If a proposed "three-target" index reports three columns that are near-perfect correlations of one abundance gradient, it is one frame wearing three names and its agreement is worthless. Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

**Mode:** `specified`, with the operational test stated (measure pairwise correlation or redundancy among the three readouts on the same communities; high correlation reduces the count of independent witnesses rather than increasing it).

**Refuter:** exhibit a community dataset in which the three readouts are mathematically independent yet one is derivable from the other two without loss. If derivation succeeds, the redundancy claim fails for that pair.

**Triangulation:** triangulated across Fig 13 and Fig 2 (different figures, different starting points, low textual overlap). World corroboration: Mouchet et al. 2010 found most functional indices *"highly correlated"* while FEve and FDiv were *"rather independent to all the others"*; Lyashevska and Farnsworth 2012 report *"mathematical redundancy among these indices."*

### F3: Report per slot, never a single aggregate

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**
Fig 1 :23: **"The ground enters as the only metric."**
Fig 1 :27: **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."**

A species, a lineage and a function are slots. An aggregate diversity score is an average over them; a global score near zero with one live slot is a legal state and not an inert one (Fig 1 :13, :20). So the readout must carry which slot changed, not only how much the total moved. D2's own index values are frame-internal quantities: an index defined from abundance data and scored on the same abundance data is a frame scoring itself with a quantity it also produces.

**Mode:** `specified` (a global score can hide a live slot; this is falsifiable with a constructed community).

**Refuter:** produce a case in which an aggregate score preserves every slot-level residual without loss for all three targets. That requires an invertible map from slots to one number across all possible communities, which fails if two different slot vectors give the same score. A single counterexample of that collision refutes the aggregate claim.

**Triangulation:** single-source for the figure line (Fig 1), with independent world corroboration: Santini et al. 2017 recommend that *"separate metrics of species composition, richness, and abundance should be reported instead of (or in addition to) composite metrics like the Shannon index."*

### F4: The framework returns a shape, not the name of a winning index

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."**
Fig 9 :27-28: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**
Fig 5 :62-63: a frame searching for an answer of a given shape holds that shape *"as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term. It is not bound, it predicts nothing, and it accrues no residual, so nothing here can see it."*

This is why section 0 binds the sought-for shape as an ordinary term before the census. The shape is not the name. Naming Hill numbers of order q, Faith's PD or Rao's Q is a **binding** from the ecology frame onto a held term, provenanced as such. It is not a derivation of a new primitive and it does not move the wall (Fig 6 :25: *"An import adds no atom to the world."*).

**Mode:** `general` for the limit, `specified` for the claim that any particular index name requires a provenance record.

**Refuter:** name an index that the figures themselves derive as the unique winner for all three targets without a ground reading. If such a line exists, F4 fails. No such line was found after a full census of Figures 1 to 13 and the two tables.

**Triangulation:** triangulated across Fig 9 :32 and the canonical figures README, which states the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* The README shares the figures' evidence pool, so this is internal confirmation of the limit, not an independent witness.

### F5: The three facets are isomers over a shared species set, not three names for one thing

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
Fig 12 :95-96: **"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."**
Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**

A species list (ingredients) is not a biodiversity measure. The measure is the bonded arrangement: presence bonded to abundance, or species bonded by phylogenetic distance, or species bonded to a trait and a process. Two indices over the same species set with different bonds are isomers, and the difference is decisive in behaviour exactly where the world sources report it: corridors, forests and farmlands rank the same assemblages differently depending on which facet is bonded (see `06_isomers_and_order.md`).

**Mode:** `specified`; the shared-atom premise is an empirical condition, not a figure line.

**Refuter:** show that one facet requires an atom the others do not (for example that functional importance cannot be written over the same species set without importing a new primitive). If so, the isomer claim fails and the weaker functional-group reading stands instead.

**Triangulation:** single-source for the figure definition (Fig 12), with the ecology-facing shared-atom premise unmeasured. Kept as a live condition in section 8 and in `06`.

### F6: Functional importance is relative to the residual and to the habitat, not an intrinsic species property

Fig 11 :49: **"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
Fig 11 :39: **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**
Fig 11 :40: **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**
Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**
Fig 11 :44: **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**

A species' "functional importance" cannot be read off its traits alone. It is a relation between the species and the residual of a named process in a named habitat, and it changes when the residual changes. Second-order contact matters: a species three links out can carry the effect that matters, the way DDT reached the eagles (Fig 11 :52-54). Enumerate the habitat by contact before pricing function; do not compose the list from a trait database.

**Mode:** `specified` on the context dependence; `general` on the enumeration rule.

**Refuter:** exhibit a species whose functional importance ranking over a fixed set of processes is invariant across habitats and residual definitions, with no second-order contact effect. That would make importance an intrinsic property and refute the valence rule.

**Triangulation:** triangulated for enumeration and valence within Fig 11, and corroborated from a separate evidence pool: Le Bagousse-Pinguet et al. 2019 report that the three biodiversity attributes *"do not necessarily relate to each other and may have contrasting effects on ecosystem functioning,"* and a 2018 Ecological Indicators study found that associations among diversity metrics *"change across environments."*

### F7: A measure cannot score itself; the ground settles the binding

Fig 1 :27: **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."**
Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."**
Fig 5 :42-44: **"The acceptance test is a bargain, not a threshold. A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before."**
Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

Cost per sample, ease of computation and citation count are **filters for the budget**, not witnesses for the verdict. They decide what gets tried first. A measure is settled only when a ground reading pays for it: a monitoring outcome, an experiment, a before and after measurement on the target process. Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."*

**Mode:** `general` on the filter/witness rule; `specified` on the claim that any index choice lacking a ground reading stays `candidate`.

**Refuter:** identify a figure line in which computational cost or ease of collection decides which measure is correct. None exists in the census. If one is produced, F7 is withdrawn.

**Triangulation:** single-source for the figure rule, with world corroboration from Santini et al. 2017, who found classic indices *"insensitive to certain changes or showed misleading responses"* and therefore unsuitable for comparison even though they are cheap and widely used.

### F8: Naming a slot needs an instrument, not another analogy

Fig 6 :30: **"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."**
Fig 6 :32: **"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."**
Fig 6 :34-35: **"Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."**
Fig 10 :52: **"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."**

The effect-term description of the residual is: *a readout that moves when lineage depth changes but not when species count changes, and a readout that moves when a functional process changes but not when either of the first two change.* A field method that already returns a partial version of that discrimination is the edge to extend. Jost 2006 provides the mathematics of one such partial instrument: the order q of a diversity profile determines sensitivity to rare versus common species, which turns a single index into a *"spectrum"* of readouts. That is an instrument improvement from a worse instrument, not a new atom.

**Mode:** `specified`; the instrument claim is about what would close the residual, not about a device that exists in the framework.

**Refuter:** show a description-only route that names the correct index for all three targets with no ground reading. The figures' source-discrimination argument (*The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument reading green *"cannot comment on the source"*) predicts such a route does not exist.

**Triangulation:** triangulated across Fig 6 and Fig 9 (instrument versus lookup, opposed tracks never read against one another). World corroboration: Jost 2006 and Morris et al. 2014.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the recommendation and before the conclusion, per Fig 5 :65-66 (*"Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else"*). The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Union refuter.** A representative set of communities shows no case where a change in one target co-occurs with an opposing change in another. Then the three targets form a partition or a single quantity, and splitting is unnecessary (F1).
2. **Independence refuter.** Two of the three readouts are provably invertible transformations of one another on all communities, so their agreement carries full information and one is not redundant. F2 fails for that pair.
3. **Per-slot refuter.** An aggregate score is found that is injective from the joint slot vector for all three targets, so no live slot can be hidden. F3 fails (Fig 1 :19, :22).
4. **Context refuter.** A species' functional importance ranking is invariant across habitats and residual definitions with no cascade effect. F6 fails (Fig 11 :49).
5. **Isomer refuter.** One facet requires an atom the others do not, so the shared-atom premise is false. The isomer claim falls to the functional-group claim (Fig 12 :117, :123).
6. **Ground refuter.** A ground reading names a single index that settles all three targets on the same communities with no residual left on any one. Then the slot is filled and the "shape only" limit is over for that question.

If any fires, the affected finding is withdrawn and the recommendation is re-run from the census. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. **Split before you measure.** State the residual for each target separately, in effect terms, before selecting an index (Fig 9 :27-28).
2. **Report at least three low-overlap readouts and check their overlap.** If two columns correlate near perfectly on the communities in question, count them as one witness, not two (Fig 2 :31, :40).
3. **Keep the per-slot record.** Publish which species, lineage or function moved, not only the scalar (Fig 1 :19, :22).
4. **Bind names with provenance.** Say "Hill number of order q chosen for this monitoring objective, binding recorded, ground reading owed," not "the best measure" (Fig 8 :50-52).
5. **Enumerate function by contact.** Price functional importance against a named process in a named habitat, following the cascade outward (Fig 11 :43-48, :52-54).
6. **Use cost as a filter only.** Ease of collection ranks what gets tried, never what is true (Fig 9 :60).
7. **Send the generator, not the recording.** Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Lead with the shape claim and the refuter; offer the links after.

### Round trip

The condensation carried into `07` and `09` is: split the question, keep three low-overlap readouts, report per slot, bind names with provenance, let the ground decide. What does not survive the round trip back down to a concrete monitoring programme is: (i) which three indices to use, (ii) the threshold at which two readouts count as "low overlap", (iii) which process defines functional importance, and (iv) what instrument would settle a binding. These four drops are the nonzero `R_T` measured in `11_audit.md`. Treating the formula as identical to the figure set would set `R_T` to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives: a union split into three low-overlap, per-slot, ground-anchored readouts with recorded bindings. The specific name of a winning index does not, and stays a `gap` owed to the ground. No strain remains after re-census; two earlier overstatements and one unlicensed import are withdrawn above rather than erased.
