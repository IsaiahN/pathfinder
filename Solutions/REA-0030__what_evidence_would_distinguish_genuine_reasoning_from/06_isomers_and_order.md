# Isomers and order: two systems, one output, different routes

**Mode: `specified` on the isomer and order claims, `general` on the structural relation. This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading. The live type question is named and not hidden.**

## RESIDUAL

A process G (a loop that ranges against a ground and settles terms by what pays) and a process I (a replay or a pattern completion that produces the same surface) yield an observationally identical output O. We need to say what relation G and I stand in, in the figures' own vocabulary, and in what order a check would distinguish them. Two questions are owed: (1) are G and I isomers under Fig 12's definition, and (2) where does the chain from O to a verdict actually break.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: a per-slot residual R, the belief b, the action a, the observation o′, the library Γ, the eight operators including unary negation, the anchor, the habitat, and the record with origin stamps. Bonds held: the seven, including → sequence, ⇒ production, ⋛ comparison, and − subtraction; plus ¬ as a non-bond unary operator. No new primitive is available without an import with a source gate.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 : Are G and I isomers?

- Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**
- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**

Write the two systems over the full shared atom set `{R, b, a, o′, Γ, ground, habitat, record}`:

- G composes `b → a → o′ ⇒ R_s ⋛ 0 ⇒ mint ⇒ settle ⇒ record` with the ground as the only metric. The residual persists per slot and can be refused.
- I composes `b → a → o′ ⇒ replay of a stored surface` with no live `R_s` term and no settle step. Its output is the same formula, but the bonds differ.

Over that shared atom set they are **isomers**: same ingredients, different bond, an invisible difference in the formula that is decisive in behaviour under intervention. This is the structural claim that F9 of `01` makes. It is `specified`: the figure gives the definition, and whether a given pair of systems occupies it is an empirical question.

Weaker form. If I shares only the output atoms with G and holds no ground or record atom, the pair shares an **isomer's ingredients minus the route atoms**. Then they are not isomers but members of one **functional group** in the sense of Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** They are both "answer producers"; the qualifier (ground-settled versus replayed) is not in the output formula. The distinction matters because a functional-group claim is weaker and easier to establish; the isomer claim requires the full shared atom set.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two systems with identical output and different internal routes behave identically under every intervention that changes the cue channel, the habitat, or the anchor, the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group reading stands.

### Q2 : The order: where the chain breaks

Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**

The five links for this question:

1. **Perception.** We can read O. This link works: a fluent answer is perceptible.
2. **Vocabulary.** We have terms for what O affords: coherence, confidence, length, step count. This link works, and it is the trap. Fig 3 :25-26: "The link between them, having terms for what a situation affords and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."
3. **Objective.** Can we say what would count as progress toward distinguishing the route? Only if we name an anchor that does not update. This is the break.
4. **Planning.** A route to that objective under incomplete information: break the cue channel, demand the record, vary the habitat.
5. **Learn and carry.** Name what worked, verify it, carry it to the next problem (Fig 3 :15). A method crosses; a recording of one success does not (Fig 4 :22-26).

Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** A reasoning score instruments link 2, not link 3. Effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it. This is why a large evaluation literature can accumulate without moving the verdict.

**Falsifier:** exhibit an instrument at link 3 that reads the route from O alone. That would rescue the chain below the break and refute F1/F11 of `01`.

### Q3 : Convergent derivation versus adopted import at second order

Fig 8 :50: **"But shared atoms are not the signature. Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."** Fig 8 :51-52: **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."**

So two systems giving the same answer might be two independent witnesses (maximum independence, corroboration) or one system replaying the other's output (one evidence pool wearing two names). The contents cannot decide it. This is a second-order claim: at first order we compare answer with ground; at second order we compare the *provenance of the answer* with the *provenance of the other answer*. Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"**. A settled answer becomes an atom at the level of the comparison. Its route becomes part of what it is, but only in the record, not in the artefact.

**Falsifier:** show a case where two answers with identical contents and differing provenance records have the same evidentiary weight without consulting the record. That would collapse Fig 8's surplus.

### Q4 : Habitat substitution and why a harness solve is the wrong object

Fig 11 :66: **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."** Fig 11 :70: **"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."** Fig 11 :68-69: "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails" and "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts."

A benchmark is a new habitat. A system moved into it must reproduce everything the goal required, and it inherits actors it did not choose. Output identity across the two habitats is not evidence of route identity. The practical separator follows: enumerate the habitat (Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**), find the cue that the new habitat supplies, and remove it. If performance collapses, the output was reading the habitat rather than ranging against a ground. This is the Clever Hans intervention, and it is a structural prediction, not a rhetorical analogy.

**Falsifier:** an agent-only improvement that moves the ground (Fig 11 :72 names this as the standing refutation of the capability claim).

### Q5 : The order of the probe, and the three questions at step 7

Fig 9 :43: **"The verdict tells you to leave. The lookup tells you where to go. Elimination is what you do after you arrive."** Formula step 7 gives three questions, and the first is cheapest: SEARCHED ("is it in here and unfound?"), then INWARD (sharpen an instrument already returning something), then OUTWARD (import a frame with a differing closure). They are opposed and must not be read against each other.

For REA-0030 the probe order is therefore:

1. **SEARCHED.** Is the discriminator already in the output evaluation we hold? Fig 1 :24-27 says no: coverage, compression, minted predicates and credibility are frame-internal. This is asked first because it is cheapest.
2. **INWARD.** Find an existing reading that already fails to resolve, and sharpen it by cutting the cue channel. Fig 6 :34: **"Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."** Clever Hans's blinkers are the edge; the double-blind protocol is the sharpening.
3. **OUTWARD.** If no local instrument exists, import a frame whose closure already predicts the shape: the animal-cue literature, the shortcut-learning literature, the source-discrimination literature. Each import needs a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 8), and it is debited against independence.
4. **Elimination at the ground.** Only after arrival. Fig 9 :40: **"a wrong cut removes the answer and speeds up so keep every cut ranked and reversible"**.

### Q6 : The threshold on "genuine"

Fig 12 :87 (also Operators :51): **"a threshold relation between two quantities"**. The word "genuine" functions as a threshold, not a substance: at some point a route has enough contact and record to count. The figures do not give the numeric threshold. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." So a declared `gap` is recorded: no figure line sets the point at which a system crosses from imitation to reasoning, and any number offered would be a convention (Fig 2 :41), not a verdict (Fig 2 :40). Refusal of the numeric threshold is not refusal of the distinction.

## CONCLUSION

G and I are **isomers over the full shared atom set** (same output ingredients, different route bonds, invisible in the artefact and decisive under intervention), or, when I holds no ground or record atom, members of one **functional group** with a qualifier the output cannot carry. The chain from output to verdict breaks at the objective link, because there is no instrument at that link; the probe order is SEARCHED, then channel-break INWARD, then provenanced OUTWARD import, then ground elimination. The isomer claim, the functional-group fallback, and the order claim each carry a written falsifier. The numeric threshold is a `gap`, recorded as a shape-not-a-name limit, not an omission.
