# Outreach draft: check the separator before you decide about the claim

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until C1 to C3 in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` survive your own check. The offer collapses on contact if the output cannot survive validation.

---

**Subject:** One output to check: what could separate reasoning from a fluent imitation

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (`specified`, not `grounded`):** we are shown an answer, a chain of steps, a confident explanation. The same surface could come from a process that bet, observed a per-slot residual and settled its terms against a ground, or from a replay that learned the surface without that contact. The question I am asking is what evidence separates those two.
>
> **The output:** the evidence is not in the answer. Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents; only the record of where each came from separates them. Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it alone, which route produced it. A video frame and a photograph taken from it can be pixel-identical while carrying different warrants, and nothing in the image records which. So a fluent answer, a confidence score, a chain of steps that looks like inference, and a panel of readers who agree after reading the same transcript are all disqualified as witnesses. They are quantities the frame produces, or one evidence pool talking.
>
> **What survives instead, as a five-part separator:** a per-slot residual with the ground as the only metric; a record from which the route is recoverable; independent checkers ranging against an anchor that does not update; a cue channel that can be broken to see whether performance collapses; and a trajectory in which failure accumulates traction rather than repeating a confident guess. Over a shared atom set, a reasoning process and an imitation producing the same output are isomers: same atoms, different bond, invisible in the formula and decisive under intervention.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any named current system reasons, or that any named current system merely imitates. I have taken no measurement. I am not claiming a reasoning detector, a threshold on confidence, a benchmark rank, or a standing list of surface tells. The figures return a shape and a probe order, and I report the detector as unreached rather than invent one. A failed search is not a proof that no detector exists, and it is not a proof that one does.
>
> **What would refute the separator, written before this message:** (1) an instrument that reads the route or the ground contact off the artefact alone, with no record and no anchor; (2) a system that gains capability while contact is held fixed; (3) a verifier that can reconstruct the claim and still verifies it soundly; (4) agreement inside one evidence pool shown to add evidence by agreement alone; (5) two systems with identical output and different routes shown to behave identically under every contact-changing intervention. If any one holds, I will retract the claim.
>
> A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figures with line numbers: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Architecture and figure set: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether
> - Outputs versus generators: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/theyre-scanning-books-when-they-should
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state whether your claim is `general`, `specified` or `grounded`. The architecture essay warns that simulated outputs "could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula," so a reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> : [name]

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Output and residual first, theory second | Fig 10 :52, "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Frames range against the ground separately (Fig 2 :45). |
| No request for agreement or trust | Fig 2 :35, "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." |
| Pre-registered refuter before the conclusion | Seat Map: "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." |
| Mode stated on every substantive claim | `THE_FORMULA.md`: "Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which." |
| No detector, threshold or ranking asserted | Fig 9 :32, "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"; Fig 13 :134, "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,". |
| Agreement withheld as corroboration | Fig 2 :40, "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". |
| The maker does not hold the criterion | Fig 10 :42, "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Do not train against the test and then cite the test. |
| Prestige is not action | *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it." |
| Offer the formula as a method, not an answer | Fig 4 :22, "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." |
| Ask each user for their own ground and refuter | Formula, "Direction": a frame cannot check the direction of its own question. |

## The two-stage offer, stated explicitly

1. **Stage 1:** send the residual, the five-part separator and the refuter. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record that verdict before showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the three source links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

Stage 1 before stage 2 is Fig 10 :52 and Fig 2 :45. Stage 2 is opt-in by construction. Stage 3 sends a generator, which is permitted by Fig 4 :22, and still needs a ground per problem.
