# Bottom-up and top-down: isomers, functional groups and the order of the union

## RESIDUAL

`01` established that the question is a union of four conditions and that the "which" decision is a per-slot kind-sort. Two further questions are owed:

1. Are the two routing sources (current residual mass versus belief weighting) different **kinds** of thing, or two strengths of one thing?
2. In what **order** do the four union conditions compose, and where does the chain actually stop?

The ordinary term "bottom-up versus top-down" is treated as a question to be re-grounded, not as a pair already understood.

## DERIVATION

### Q1. Are the two routing sources isomers? CORRECTION TO MY OWN FIRST READING

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**
- First reading: bottom-up and top-down are two quantities of one system, so they are two strengths of one term. **Retracted as applied to this derivation.** They share an atom set (mass, weighting, slot, residual, objective) but differ in which quantity is compared and which anchor settles the comparison. Under Fig 12 :117 that makes them a real isomer claim, not two dial positions.
- However, they are **not** isomers over the full six-term set as the physiologist would write it: bottom-up needs no inherited-prior term, and top-down needs no novel-gap term. Fig 8 :50: *"But shared atoms are not the signature."* Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence. So the honest weaker claim is: **the two routing sources are members of the same functional group, differing in the bond and the anchor.**
- Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** The group here is `slot -> comparison -> routing`. The qualifier is which quantity is compared.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either source can be written without an atom the other requires, and the measurement shows a single mechanism with no separable parameters, the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim survives.
- Status: `specified`, triangulated for the group (Fig 12 and Symbols table), single-source for the isomer reading over a fixed neural atom set.

### Q2. Does the bond vocabulary actually describe a route?

- Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**
- Fig 12 :112: **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."**
- The routing decision is a sequence: perceive, compute residual, sort, aim the probe. The current objective is a comparison (⋛ against threshold). The change in residual after the probe is a subtraction (Fig 12 :112). So the figure vocabulary can write the route, not just the ingredients. This is why "which signals deserve attention" cannot be answered by a list of ingredient-like features.
- Fig 13 :75: **"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."** Applied at the neural scale, a routing decision cannot go straight from a signal state to an action state without a structure between them. The candidate structures held are the gradient (spendable difference) and the network (influence travels). Which one occupies the slot is a binding from the neuroscience frame, not a derivation (see §5 correction and `05` §6).

### Q3. What is the order of the union?

Fig 9 :56: **"intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**
Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific"**

The four conditions of `01` §1.3 compose in dependency order, not all at once:

1. **C4 resolvability** first, because nothing can be routed to a slot the instrument cannot register (Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."*).
2. **C1 per-slot mass** next, because the boundary diff sorts only what is already registered (Fig 1 :15: *"where to look: broken vs novel"*).
3. **C3 kind** next, because a novel gap and a broken mechanism owe different responses (Fig 5 :12: *"new: extend perception, aim the probe here"*; Fig 5 :14: *"mechanism broken: a new term is owed"*).
4. **C2 goal weighting** last, because belief and prior can only weight candidates that already exist (Symbols table :24).

A case in C1 and C2 is categorically different from either alone: a salient signal that also matches the goal is not "more salient" and not "more wanted". It is a compound with its own loop (Fig 9 :57-58, Fig 8 :53: *"Residual first, frame second."*).

### Q4. Where does the chain stop, and what is the second-order composition?

- Fig 3 :25: **"Perception has obvious failures and obvious tests. Planning has obvious failures and obvious tests. The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."**
- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
- Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**
- The chain stops between vocabulary and objective (links 2 and 3 of `01` §1.4). The world corroborates: the predictive-coding source says there is no consensus on neural implementation. A routing molecule cannot be settled at the second order until link 3 has an instrument.
- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."** Once a first-order routing molecule is settled against the ground, it can become an atom for the next stratum. That second-order composition is legal by the recursion rule, not by an import that adds a primitive. Until settlement, the second order is a candidate only.

### Q5. Two currencies, not one score

- Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**
- Fig 12 :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**
- A per-slot routing rule with many parameters is expensive to describe and may be cheap to test with one cueing experiment. A short rule over a costly operand (for example, a single "attend to the largest residual" rule over a high-bandwidth neural record) is cheap to state and may take a whole budget to settle. Neither figure predicts the other, so both are read or the choice is made blind. This is why the public packet asks for a refuter that is checkable in twenty minutes rather than a full theory comparison.

## CONCLUSION

The two routing sources are not two strengths of one thing and not a flat list of features. They are members of one **functional group** (`slot -> comparison -> routing`) over a shared atom set, with a real but unmeasured **isomer** reading if a fixed neural atom set can be agreed. Their union is a **union of overlapping conditions**, not a partition, and its order is resolvability, mass, kind, then weighting. The chain currently stops at the objective link, where there is no instrument. A second-order routing molecule is licensed by Fig 12 :104 only after settlement; before that it is a candidate.

The classic early versus late selection debate is dissolved by this derivation rather than answered: it asks C2 and C3 at once. That is a re-census, not a verdict flip.
