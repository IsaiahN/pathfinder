# Outreach draft: check one routing output before you decide whether the frame is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` survive your own check. The offer collapses on contact if stage 1 does not hold up.

**Subject:** Check one per-slot prediction before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about selective attention. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the routing claims, not `grounded`):** the framework indexes the residual between prediction and observation by object slot, not by a global total. From its boundary-diff rule it follows that a system aiming its next probe reads which slots carry positive mass, and that the gap is sorted into kinds: new (extend perception, aim the probe here), mis-attached (refit what you have), mechanism broken (a new term is owed), and held (nothing owed). It also says that the valence of a signal is relative to the current residual, so no ranking of signals survives a change of task. This is a prediction of a **shape**, not a measurement, and it names no nucleus, no neurotransmitter and no frequency band as the seat of attention.
>
> Two routing sources are predicted, not one. One reads the current per-slot residual mass, which is the large compressible gap that can be spent to aim a probe. The other reads how far belief leans on private history versus inherited prior. They have different anchors and cannot be averaged into one "attention" number without losing which one moved.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the pulvinar, V1, the superior colliculus or a prediction-error unit *is* the mechanism the framework derives. Those are bindings from the neuroscience frame onto candidate atoms, and until an experiment pays for one they carry zero evidentiary weight. I am not claiming that attention is precisely the precision weighting of prediction errors either; that binding covers only one of the two routing sources in the literature.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a measurement in which allocation is a single global scalar with no per-slot structure, or in which aggregating across slots loses nothing about where the next probe goes;
> 2. a residual-independent fixed ranking of signals that predicts allocation across changed tasks better than a residual-relative rule;
> 3. one uniform response that cannot be separated into new, mis-attached and broken with distinct grounds;
> 4. a single variable that clears bottom-up capture, goal-directed selection, kind-routing and instrument resolvability together;
> 5. a validated instrument showing that the middle link, what counts as progress per slot, already works;
> 6. a figure line naming a specific nucleus, or a result in which the routing shape predicts no allocation.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will narrow or retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the links are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them. I would be especially interested in what you would count as a failure at the objective link, the one where a system says what would count as progress here in its own terms.
>
> - Figure text extract with line numbers (local canonical copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local canonical copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop and mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground, and write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and the author's essay warns that outputs can be simulated rather than tested. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks for your time,
> [name]

## Two-stage offer, stated plainly

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual, the shape and the refuter. Ask what they would count as a failure. Record their verdict *before* showing any framework material.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
3. **Stage 3, optional.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

Stage 1 before stage 2 is Fig 10 :52 and Fig 2 :45. Stage 2 is opt-in by construction because a recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked. Stage 3 sends a generator, which Fig 4 :22 permits. Nothing here asks anyone to trust anything.
