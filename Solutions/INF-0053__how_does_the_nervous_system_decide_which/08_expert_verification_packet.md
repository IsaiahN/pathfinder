# Expert verification packet — the Tether on selective attention

**What this is:** a claim in a form an attention researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the routing claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on a standing list. Not `grounded`.** No neural measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The nervous system's "which" is a per-slot read of the residual between prediction and observation, not a global allocation. The gap is sorted by kind (new, mis-attached, broken, held), the valence of a signal is relative to the current residual and objective, and two routing sources (current per-slot mass and belief weighting) have different anchors and cannot be averaged into one attention scalar. These are members of one functional group over a shared atom set, with a real but unmeasured isomer reading if a fixed neural atom set can be agreed. The chain currently lacks an instrument at the objective link, the point where a system says what counts as progress in its own terms. The framework returns this shape and its refuters; it does not name a nucleus, a neurotransmitter or a frequency band as the seat of attention.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1. Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 1 and the Symbols table

- **Statement:** the decision is indexed per slot and a global total is not the signal.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19, *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22, *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 1 :28, *"The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."*
- **What to check:** do these lines actually say what is claimed, and does the per-slot form match what a multi-slot attention experiment measures? Note that Fig 1 :20 says a global residual near zero with one live slot is a legal state, so the two are not interchangeable.
- **Refuter:** a measurement in which allocation is a single global scalar with no per-slot structure, or in which aggregating across slots loses nothing about where the next probe goes.

### C2. Framework-internal and neuroscience-facing; checkable by reading Figures 5 and 11 and by comparing with cueing paradigms

- **Statement:** "deserve" is residual-relative valence, and the gap is sorted into kinds rather than strengths.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49, *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :51, *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* Fig 5 :12, *"new: extend perception, aim the probe here"* and Fig 5 :14, *"mechanism broken: a new term is owed"*.
- **What to check:** is the mapping of "deserve" onto residual-relative valence faithful? Does the experimental literature distinguish novel orienting from violation-driven re-weighting, or does one response account for both?
- **Refuter:** a residual-independent fixed ranking that predicts allocation across changed tasks better than a residual-relative rule, or one uniform response that cannot be separated into new, mis-attached and broken with distinct grounds.

### C3. Framework-internal derivation with a stated live strain; checkable by reading Figures 3, 6, 12 and the Symbols table

- **Statement:** two routing sources sit in one functional group over a shared atom set, their union is a union of overlapping conditions in dependency order, and the chain currently stops at the objective link; a first-order molecule becomes an atom at the next level only after settlement.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 3 :25, *"Perception has obvious failures and obvious tests. Planning has obvious failures and obvious tests. The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."* Fig 6 :32, *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* Fig 12 :104, *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."* Fig 9 :56, *"intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*
- **What to check:** is the functional-group reading (Fig 12 :123, *"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member"*) a better description than the isomer reading over a fixed neural atom set? Is the dependency order resolvability, mass, kind, weighting defensible given the literature?
- **Refuter:** a single variable clearing C1 to C4 and their intersections; a validated instrument showing the objective link already works; or a measurement showing routing to a slot the instrument never registered at any resolution.

**Live strain, named not hidden:** the isomer reading requires a fixed neural atom set that is not yet agreed. Under Fig 8 :50, *"But shared atoms are not the signature."* Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence. The weaker functional-group claim does not depend on that agreement and is the one offered for checking.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a standing list of features that deserve attention. Fig 11 :51 refuses one.
- Not a single scalar resource divided among tasks. Fig 1 :22 says aggregation throws away the where-to-look signal.
- Not that the early versus late selection debate has a winner. It is C2 plus C3, a union.
- Not that attention is precision weighting in general. The opened world source limits that binding to endogenous spatial attention; it is a `specified` binding with a refuter.
- Not that the pulvinar, V1, the superior colliculus or gamma-band synchronization is the figure-derived seat of attention. All are bindings from the neuroscience frame, provenanced as such and unproven here.
- Not grounded. No measurement was taken by this agent and no instrument was run.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A per-slot test: eye tracking and single-unit gain at multiple simultaneous slots, to see whether allocation has per-slot structure.
2. A standing-list test: swap tasks and see whether the same signals win.
3. A kind test: separate new, mis-attached and broken gaps by distinct signatures.
4. A union test: try one variable across C1 to C4 and their intersections.
5. An objective-link instrument: measure what counts as progress per slot.
6. A source-discriminating instrument: separate per-slot residual sorting from a pooled-resource artifact and from a late-selection artifact.

Agreement is not a settlement. Fig 2 :35, *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, :22, Fig 11 :49, :51 and Fig 5 :12, :14. Six short passages.
2. Check C2 against cueing and inattentional-blindness work: does "deserve" track the current task residual rather than a fixed feature list?
3. Check C3's functional-group reading against whether bottom-up and top-down can be separated into distinct anchors in your data.
4. Name one instrument that would measure what counts as progress per slot, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
