# The Tether's answer on why a structure fails at the next growth stage

**Mode: `specified` on substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading. No company was measured.

## 1. The residual it answers

`R = |Γ(b,a) − o'|` per slot: the gap between what the organization's structure predicts and what the ground returns. "Worked well" means per-slot `R` was paid by an anchor that did not move when the company reported a success. "Fails at the next stage" means it is no longer paid there. Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** The question is a union of conditions (Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*), so the answer is a set of per-domain verdicts, never one averaged cause.

## 2. The shape

1. **The room follows the question.** Fig 10 :71-72: *"The room composition is a function of the question."* / **"When the question changes, the room should change. A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom."** A structure works because its decision rights, meeting cadence and information edges formed a room for the earlier question. Growth changes the question; a copied room satisfies neither half of Fig 7 :36 (*"The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced"*).

2. **A method must cross; a recording cannot.** Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** Fig 4 :25-26: **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion. Replayed downward, it produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one."** The early structure is often a compressed record of one occasion. Carried up it looks like knowledge; replayed at scale it repeats the old occasion.

3. **The channel decays, not the ground.** Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**. Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."** The customer, regulator or patient did not change because the chart changed. The reading of them went stale, saturated or pooled across segments, and internal instruments read green because they read through the same channel.

4. **Capability is agent-and-habitat; the habitat is substituted.** Fig 11 :39-40: **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."** / **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."** Fig 11 :66-67: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* / *"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."* Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49, :51), so a structure that was a benefactor can become a malfactor without changing its own behaviour.

5. **Agreement is not a verdict.** Fig 2 :40: **"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**. Alignment, shared dashboards and common mental models are conventions about meaning (Fig 2 :41: *"efficient for a convention"*), not evidence that the structure still fits the ground. Fig 7 :13: *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*.

6. **A prior standard can harden into a verdict.** Fig 10 :47: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**. The hiring bar, review cadence or margin threshold that encoded an early success becomes an authored verdict when hitting it substitutes for reading the ground.

7. **The next chart is withheld.** Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."** The shape constrains: method not recording, reachable non-updating ground, a seat able to refuse (Fig 10 :42: **"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."**), an enumerated habitat (Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*), and reversible cuts (Fig 9 :41). It does not name the chart.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that flat is better than tall, or that hierarchy is better than flat, at any stage. Those are bond arrangements, and each must be checked against its own slot and habitat.
- Not that the old structure was stupid, badly designed, or the fault of any person. It was a room for an earlier question.
- Not that one big reorganization fixes the transition. Fig 4 :30 requires a measured round trip, and Fig 9 :41 requires cuts to stay ranked and reversible.
- Not that "culture" is a single cause. It is absorbed into the habitat and verification domains (X2 in `01` §2).
- Not a derivation of a specific structure, and no new primitive (Fig 13 :116: *"Six members, and no amount of arranging produces a seventh"*).
- Not `grounded`. No company, stage transition or outcome was measured. The whole folder is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency.

## 4. What would settle it : the ground

A discriminator, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding tests are:

- **Question-change test:** hold question, habitat and channel strictly constant across a transition and see whether the identical structure's per-slot `R` still rises. If it does not, the room-follows-question claim survives; if it does, F1 fails.
- **Method/recording test:** find a pure recording of one occasion that produces new ground-paid successes at the next stage. If found, F2 fails.
- **Channel test:** measure channel fidelity to a non-updating anchor independently of internal sentiment. If per-slot `R` rises while fidelity stays high and non-declining, F3 fails.
- **Habitat test:** hold the habitat strictly constant and see whether the same structure's valence still flips. If it does, F4 fails.
- **Discriminator for "the structure failed":** an instrument that separates question change, channel decay, habitat substitution and convention drift as sources of the same outcome reading. Without it, the cause is not named (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134).

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The six-term set is *"a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes"* (*They're Scanning Books*); declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral. Another frame's minimum set need not be these six. The answer stops where the available instruments can no longer separate the sources of one outcome reading, and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity"* (*How Intelligence Actually Works*). Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name the org chart for the next stage and does not say the old chart was bad. It says the room follows the question, so a structure fails when the question moves and the room does not; that what crosses into a new scale must be a re-applicable method, because a recording of one occasion replayed produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one; that the ground does not decay, the channel does; that capability is a property of company and habitat, so a structure's value flips when the habitat is substituted; and that internal agreement is a convention, not a verdict. The effect is per slot, the conditions form a union, and the verdicts are kept separate. What the framework offers is the shape any working next structure must have, five refuters written in advance, and the discriminators that would name the actual cause. The chart stays open until a ground settles it.
