# Expert verification packet : the Tether on stage-transition structure failure

**What this is:** a claim in a form an organization scholar or growth-stage practitioner can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No company or transition is claimed to have been measured. This is a shape and a route, not a result. The whole packet is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Why the structure fails" is a union of conditions, not one problem with opposite answers. A structure stops working when its room no longer matches the changed question; when what crossed into the new scale was a recording of one occasion rather than a re-applicable method; when the channel to a non-updating ground decayed while the ground itself did not; when the habitat was substituted and the structure's valence flipped with the residual; or when a standard that was a shared meaning was promoted into a verdict and now decides the outcome. The effect is indexed per decision and team, never as an org-wide average. The verdicts are kept separate per domain and never averaged. No figure line selects the next org chart; it returns a shape, five refuters, and the discriminators that would name the cause.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 : Framework-internal and organization-facing; checkable against the figures and against your own cases

- **Statement:** the room follows the question, and a fixed room is the failure. A stage transition changes the room; a copied room is motion without progress.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :71-72 : *"The room composition is a function of the question."* / **"When the question changes, the room should change. A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom."** Also Fig 7 :36 : **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."** Fig 1 :19 : **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
- **What to check:** in a transition you know, did the question actually change (new slots, new anchor, new habitat) while the decision rights and meeting cadence stayed the same? Are the reported symptoms only correlations of that mismatch?
- **Refuter:** a transition with question, habitat and channel held strictly constant in which the identical structure's per-slot residual still rises. That falsifies the claim.

### C2 : Transfer claim; checkable by anyone who has scaled an organization

- **Statement:** the admissible crossing object is a re-applicable method; the inadmissible one is the old arrangement replayed as a rule.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :22 : **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** Fig 4 :25-26 : **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion. Replayed downward, it produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one."** Fig 12 :107 : *"Settling compresses the description and not what it cost to establish."*
- **What to check:** when the early structure was carried into the next stage, was a generator transferred (something that could be reapplied in a new habitat) or a record of one occasion (a chart that encoded how a particular crisis was once solved)? Does the record of the transition show which?
- **Refuter:** a pure recording of one occasion, with no re-applicable method, that reliably produces new ground-paid successes at the next stage. That falsifies the claim.

### C3 : Framework-internal derivation about the anchor and the seat

- **Statement:** the ground does not decay, the channel does; capability belongs to company and habitat; and a verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :24 : **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**. Fig 11 :39-40 : **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."** / **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."** Fig 10 :42 : **"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."** Fig 2 :29 : **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**
- **What to check:** in a case where "the structure stopped working," was there an independent reading of the paying ground, or only internal reports read through the same channel? Did anyone positioned to refuse a verdict still have access to the ground, or had the change removed every outside seat?
- **Refuter:** per-slot residual that rises while independently measured channel fidelity stays high and non-declining, or an agent-only change that improves the residual with the habitat strictly held constant. Either falsifies the claim.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that flat, tall, centralized or decentralized is the right answer at any stage. The figures return a shape, not a chart (Fig 9 :32).
- Not that the old structure was badly designed or anyone is to blame. It was a room for an earlier question.
- Not that a single reorganization restores fit. Fig 4 :30 requires the round trip to be measured; Fig 9 :41 requires cuts to be ranked and reversible.
- Not that "culture" is a single cause. It is inside the habitat and verification domains, not a separate domain with a nameable anchor.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement at any company is asserted.
- Not a derivation of a specific structure, and no new primitive (Fig 13 :116).

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminator, not another argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. Hold question, habitat and channel constant across a transition and measure the same slots. Fires F1 either way.
2. Find a pure recording that produces new ground-paid successes at the next stage, or fail to find one after a wide search. Fires F2.
3. Measure channel fidelity to an independent anchor while per-slot residual is rising. Fires F3.
4. Name an instrument that separates question change, channel decay, habitat substitution and convention drift as sources of one outcome reading. Until one exists, the cause of a given failure is not settled by this packet.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 10 :71-72, Fig 4 :22 and :25-26, Fig 10 :24 and :42, Fig 11 :39-40, and Fig 1 :19. Eight short passages.
2. Take one stage transition you know. Say which of the five conditions was present, and which anchor would have settled it.
3. Try to break C1 or C2 with a counter-case. A single good counter-case is worth more than an endorsement.
4. Name one instrument that would discriminate among the five sources of "the structure failed," or say why none is available.
5. Send the refutation. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
