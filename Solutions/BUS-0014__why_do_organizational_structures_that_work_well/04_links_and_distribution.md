## What bears on this question in the wider literature

1. **Greiner (business, direct hit).** Each growth phase has a dominant management approach that eventually becomes ineffective and provokes a crisis. The PDF excerpts include: *"Managerial problems and practices are rooted in time. They do not last throughout the life of an organization."* and *"Too often, it is tempting to choose solutions that were tried before but that actually make it impossible for the new phase to emerge."* This corroborates the room-follows-question finding (Fig 10 :71-72) and the method-versus-recording finding (Fig 4 :25-26). It is a business-source witness, so it overlaps the question's own pool.
2. **Structural inertia (business/sociology, ecology school).** The Schwarz et al. article and the Hannan and Freeman material it cites give the selection-side version: reliable and accountable organizations reproduce their structures, and reproducibility generates resistance to change; reorganization carries a liability of newness. This corroborates Fig 11 :66-69 and Fig 9 :40-41. Same broad pool as Greiner, so one witness, not two.
3. **Ashby / requisite variety (cybernetics and mathematics, genuinely low overlap).** *"only variety can destroy variety"* and the good regulator result give an independent reason a control structure of fixed complexity fails as the system it regulates becomes more various. Corroborates the scale claim (Fig 4) and the habitat claim (Fig 11) from a different field. It does not select a chart, which matches the F8 gap.
4. **Punctuated equilibrium (paleontology and evolutionary biology, genuinely low overlap).** Long stasis interrupted by geologically rapid change is a shape match to Greiner's evolution and revolution and to Fig 7's room chain. It is an analogue, not a figure line, and the criticism recorded on the Wikipedia page (Dawkins, Dennett) is noted: the analogy carries no verdict weight.
