## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

`R = |Γ(b,a) − o'|`, the gap between what the organization's library and structure predict and what the ground returns. Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** The slots here are individual decisions, teams, product lines, customer segments and time periods. "Worked well" is not an org-wide mood; it is per-slot `R` paid by an anchor that does not move when the organization reports a success. Fig 1 :23: **"The ground enters as the only metric."**

The question asks what happens across a stage transition. Two readings are possible, and they are not two answers to one question. Option A treats the structure as an object that became bad, so a better chart would fix it. Option B treats the transition as a change of question, scale, habitat and channel, with the structure held fixed, so the remedy is a rebuilt room and a re-checked contact. Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** The transition bundles several conditions; a case in several is categorically different from a case in one (Fig 9 :57).

**Shape bound before the run.** Per Fig 5 :65, *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."* An acceptable answer must, for each slot: name the non-updating anchor that paid; say whether a method or a recording crossed the scale boundary; name the room the structure formed for its question; name the habitat and channel; name the seat able to refuse; state what would reverse the call. A claim that cannot be checked on these six points is vocabulary, not a derivation.

## 1. Census of R: arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity.** Relational, at least three places: the library `Γ`, the belief and action `(b,a)`, and the observation `o'`. A fourth place, the edge between two slots, carries coordination. A fifth, the time index, carries the stage. `R` is always a slice, and what cannot be perceived yet is simply unread (Symbols table :38-40: *"it is always a slice: what cannot be perceived yet is residual and simply cannot be read."*).
- **Symmetry.** "Stage" and "growth" are orderings, a spectrum in the six-term sense (Fig 13 :20: *"ordering, so more and less can be said"*). "Fails" is a threshold comparison, an ordered bond, not a symmetric join (Operators table :51: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*). "Structure" is a network arrangement (Fig 13 :24: **"that entities relate, and influence travels"**). The bonds that carry the transition, sequence, production, subtraction and comparison, are ordered. Rearranging them changes the substance (Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**). So a stage is not a bigger version of the same term; it is a different arrangement.
- **Scale.** The single decision loop (Fig 1), the membrane between one agent and many (Fig 4), the room chain across stages (Fig 7), and the habitat contact cascade (Fig 11 :52). The transform between scales is lossy (Fig 4 :27: **"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."**), so a structural claim made at one scale does not transfer to another without a measured round trip.

## 4. Findings F1 to F10

### F1 : The question moves; a room that stays fixed is the failure (decisive for framing)

Fig 10 :71: *"The room composition is a function of the question."*
Fig 10 :72: **"When the question changes, the room should change. A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom."**
Fig 7 :36: **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."**
Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**

A structure "works well" because the people in it formed a room for the question that stage actually asked: who decides, what counts as a win, which ground pays. Growth changes that question. The same reporting lines, the same meeting cadence and the same decision rights are now a room composed for an older question. The observed symptom, slow decisions, duplicated work, missed customer signals, is the correlation; the failure is the fixed room. Fig 7 :36 requires the next room to differ from the last *and* match the residual the last one surfaced. Copying the last room satisfies neither half.

- **Mode:** `general` for the room-follows-question rule, `specified` for its application to growth stages.
- **Refuter:** a stage transition in which the question, the habitat and the channel are held strictly constant (same slots, same non-updating anchor, same contact edges) yet the identical structure's per-slot `R` rises. That would falsify the claim. A mere report of "it stopped working" does not fire it, because such reports are usually pooled with a changed question.

### F2 : What crossed the scale boundary was a recording, not a method

Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**
Fig 4 :23: *"upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."*
Fig 4 :24: *"downward: something that makes the next search cheaper. Never a replay of what worked once."*
Fig 4 :25-26: **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion. Replayed downward, it produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one."**

This is the sharpest figure-grounded answer to "why the thing that worked fails." A startup structure is often a compressed record of one occasion: the way the founders happened to split work, the hierarchy that resolved one crisis, the process that shipped one product. Carried up it looks like a method; replayed at the next scale it repeats the old occasion and cannot produce the new one. Fig 12 :107: *"Settling compresses the description and not what it cost to establish."* The cost of re-deriving the arrangement was paid once, in a habitat that no longer exists. Fig 12 :110: *"everything is."*

- **Mode:** `specified`, ungrounded.
- **Refuter:** a structure that is demonstrably a recording of one occasion (no re-applicable method, only a past arrangement) that still produces new successes at the next stage against a non-updating anchor. If that is measured, the method/recording boundary is not the mechanism here.

### F3 : The channel decays, not the ground

Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**
Fig 10 :25-26: *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*
Fig 10 :27-28: *"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"*
Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**

Growth adds slots, layers and pooled readings. The customer who pays, the regulator who rules and the patient who recovers do not change because the org chart changed, but the organization's reading of them goes stale, saturates, or gets pooled across segments. The structure that worked was a high-fidelity channel at small scale. It fails when the channel loses contact while every internal instrument still reads green, because those instruments read *through* the same channel. The seat-map failure genus is explicit: *"Every instrument points inward unless one is built to point out"* (Seat Map :285).

- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Refuter:** a stage in which per-slot `R` rises while channel fidelity to a non-updating ground is independently measured high and non-declining. That would show contact is not the mechanism.

### F4 : Capability is agent-and-habitat; the habitat is substituted, not removed

Fig 11 :39: **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**
Fig 11 :40: **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**
Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
Fig 11 :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**
Fig 11 :66-67: **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."** / **"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."**
Fig 11 :68-69: *"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"* / *"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"*

Growth moves the residual into a new habitat: new customers, new regulators, new competitors, new internal actors. The old structure's value was relative to the old residual; an actor that was a benefactor then can be a malfactor now, without changing its behaviour (Fig 11 :50). Moving the operation into the new stage is isolation by substitution: the firm fails to reproduce some condition its goal required and inherits actors it did not choose. Both failure modes are silent until the goal fails. Redrawing the chart does not touch the habitat if contact is unchanged.

- **Mode:** `specified` for organizations, `general` for the capability rule.
- **Refuter:** a stage transition in which the habitat is strictly unchanged (same actors, conditions and contact edges) and the same structure's valence still flips. That would falsify the habitat claim.

### F5 : Internal agreement is not a verdict about the stage

Fig 2 :31: **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."**
Fig 2 :40: **"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**
Fig 2 :41: *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*
Fig 7 :12-13: *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"*

Structures "work" partly because they manufacture alignment: shared vocabulary, common dashboards, a uniform way of seeing the customer. Fig 2 :41 permits that as a convention about meaning. It is disqualifying as a verdict about whether the structure still fits the ground. As the organization grows, the same alignment mechanism compounds correlation (Fig 7 :13), so the structure's own success reports lose evidentiary value exactly when they are most needed. Fig 2 :42: **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."**

- **Mode:** `general` for the correlation rule, `specified` for organizations.
- **Refuter:** a case in which agreement across one evidence pool is independently anchored at the ground and reliably predicts which structures survive the next transition. That would break the disqualification here.

### F6 : A prior success hardens into a convention that decides an outcome

Fig 10 :47: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**
Fig 10 :49: **"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."**
Fig 2 :43: *"Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."*

What worked at stage N is encoded as a standard: a hiring bar, a review cadence, a margin threshold, a span of control. A standard is a convention, a shared meaning, and Fig 10 :46 says a convention makes no claim about the world. When it is treated as the outcome, when hitting the standard substitutes for reading the ground, it has become an authored verdict. At the next stage it fixes the wrong value and nothing flags the transition, because conventions that decide outcomes look like disciplined execution.

- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Refuter:** a standard that improves per-slot `R` at the next stage *while* demonstrably deciding the outcome (no independent readout exists). That would falsify the convention-to-verdict claim.

### F7 : Reorganization is not a free lever; the gap is real and must be measured

Fig 4 :30: **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."**
Fig 4 :31-32: *"The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold, and it is the only honest report of how much was given up."*
Fig 9 :41: **"so keep every cut ranked and reversible"**
Fig 7 :37-39: *"Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place: the bracket's fidelity across the handoff / the ground's pressure on the room"*

Growth-stage failure is not cured by one big re-org, because the transform between scales is lossy. A method carried up and a head start brought down will not match; treating the new chart as identical to the old method is dead reckoning. Every cut (which roles to merge, which layer to remove, which decision to push down) removes something and speeds up a search, so cuts must be ranked and reversible. A handoff with no bracket fidelity to what the old room surfaced, or with no ground pressure on the new room, is motion without progress (Fig 7 :32).

- **Mode:** `general` for the round trip, `specified` for organizations.
- **Refuter:** a stage transition in which a one-shot reorganization restores per-slot `R` with `R_T` measured zero, all dropped constraints recovered, and no reversible cut needed. That would falsify the lossy-transform claim.

### F8 : No figure line selects the next org chart: a declared `gap`

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**
Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"**
Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**
Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

No figure derives a specific structure for the next stage. The framework returns a shape: the next arrangement must be a method and not a recording (Fig 4 :23), keep a channel to a non-updating ground (Fig 10 :24), keep a seat able to refuse (Fig 10 :42), enumerate the new habitat (Fig 11 :43), and keep cuts ranked and reversible (Fig 9 :41). Which concrete chart does that in a given habitat is a ground question requiring an instrument. Searching harder inside the same frame will not manufacture it: closure is idempotent (Fig 8 :32: **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."**). This is `unreachable` as a shape, not silence, and not an excuse to invent a number.

- **Mode:** `general` for the gap.
- **Refuter:** a figure line deriving the unique next structure, or a ground measurement that settles the chart across habitats with the same per-slot residual. Until one fires, the slot stays open.

### F9 : Strain recorded and dissolved: growth stages are not the six-term set

My first pass tried to map growth stages onto Fig 13's six terms, one term per stage. That only fits with force and strains Fig 13 :74: **"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."** By the strain test, the line governs another quantity, so I re-censused. Fig 13 governs the minimum substrate of *any* composed system. A "stage" is a parameter of the room chain (Fig 7) and the time channel (Fig 10 :24), not a seventh term. The strain dissolves. The earlier reading is kept here as evidence, not erased. See §7 and `11_audit.md`.

### F10 : Reflexive check: this solution's room must also change with its question

Fig 7 :6 names *"the limit: no last room"* and Fig 13 :118: *"still not arrive: infinite effort inside a finite reach."* A package that freezes its own six-domain census as the final room for all growth questions would repeat the failure it describes. This derivation is a room composed for this residual; a different question (which specific chart, which specific stage) summons a different room and owes a new census. This is a self-check, not an extra finding about companies.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required by the derive-first discipline: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument (Seat Map :287). The shape claim is:

> The transition is a union of conditions. A structure fails at the next stage when its room no longer matches the changed question, when it crossed the scale membrane as a recording rather than a method, when the channel to a non-updating ground decayed, when the habitat was substituted and its valence flipped, or when a prior convention was promoted into a verdict. Verdicts are per domain and never averaged. No figure line selects the next org chart.

It is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A transition with question, habitat and channel strictly held constant in which the identical structure's per-slot `R` still rises (against F1 and F4).
2. A demonstrable recording of one occasion that reliably produces new successes at the next stage against a non-updating anchor (against F2).
3. A measured rise in per-slot `R` while channel fidelity to an independent anchor stays high and non-declining (against F3).
4. Agreement from a single evidence pool that independently predicts which structures survive the transition (against F5).
5. A figure line deriving a unique next structure, or a ground measurement settling the chart across habitats (against F8).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Do not ask "is the structure good". Ask which link stops the chain (Fig 3 :29) and which room the structure formed for its question (Fig 10 :71-72).
2. Treat the old chart as a possible recording, not a method, until a re-applicable generator is named (Fig 4 :23-26).
3. Measure the channel to a non-updating ground separately from internal sentiment (Fig 10 :24, Fig 2 :29).
4. Enumerate the new habitat before redesigning; valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :43, :49, :51).
5. Keep any new standard a convention by pairing it with an independent readout (Fig 10 :47).
6. Rank and reverse every cut; measure the round trip rather than assuming identity (Fig 9 :41, Fig 4 :30).
7. Publish the F8 gap rather than filling it with a chart. Offer the refuter before the theory (Fig 10 :52: **"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."**).

## 8. Verdict of this audit

The figures do not say "flat wins" or "hierarchy wins" at any stage, and they do not say the old structure was stupid. They say the effect is per slot (Fig 1 :19), the room follows the question (Fig 10 :71-72), a method must cross the membrane and a recording cannot (Fig 4 :23-26), the channel decays while the ground does not (Fig 10 :24), capability is agent-and-habitat (Fig 11 :39-40), agreement from one pool is not a verdict (Fig 2 :40), and a standard that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention (Fig 10 :47). Those are distinct quantities in a union, reported separately and never averaged. The next org chart is a declared gap, not an invented answer. Mode `specified` on substantive claims, `general` on structural shape, not `grounded`; the whole folder is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency, not a ground reading. Strains after re-census: none live; one retracted strain (F9) preserved in §4 and §7.
