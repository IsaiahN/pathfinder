## DERIVATION

### Q1 : What crosses when the company grows?

- Fig 4 :19-20: *"The kernel is the part that does not change with size"*; *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* The per-slot loop is invariant. A stage transition changes the cast that runs it and the ground it ranges against.
- Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**
- Fig 4 :23-24: *"upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."* / *"downward: something that makes the next search cheaper. Never a replay of what worked once."*
- Fig 4 :25-26: **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion. Replayed downward, it produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one."**

So the admissible crossing object is a generator: a method that can be reapplied in a new habitat. The inadmissible object is the old arrangement replayed as a rule. This answers the "why" at the level of what transfers, and it is the first half of the shape. Fig 12 :107-110 gives the economic reason: where the arrangement is recorded, re-deriving it is a lookup and the actions were spent once; where it is not, *"the result carries forward and the deriving does not, so every lineage pays again."*

- **Falsifier, wrote first:** a documented transition in which a pure recording of one occasion, with no re-applicable method, reliably produces new ground-paid successes at the next stage. That kills the method/recording boundary here.

### Q2 : Are the two stage structures isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**

An early structure and a later structure built over the same held atoms, differing in which decision right is bonded to which information edge, are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance. The two org charts can list the same roles and still behave differently, and nothing in the ingredient list records which (Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*).

The stronger version requires a fixed atom set. If the transition adds a role, a layer, or a new coordination instrument that was not in the earlier structure, the two are not isomers; the later one has an added atom and is a **composition over a changed library**. For organizations the fixed-atom condition is an empirical question, not something this derivation can assert. So the honest claim is conditional: over a fixed atom set, stage structures are isomers; when roles are added or removed, the relation is composition with an import or a subtraction, and Fig 6 :21 applies (*"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*). This condition is a live strain, recorded in `11_audit.md`, not hidden here.

- **Falsifier, wrote first:** a transition in which the two structures share no fixed atom set and still cannot be described as a composition over a changed library. If the roles, links and ground all change with no invariant, the isomer framing fails and the union framing of `01` stands alone.

### Q3 : What is the order of the transition?

Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**
Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*
Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

The dependency order for a stage transition is:

1. **Perceive** the new stage: which slots now carry live residual. Fig 1 :19 (per-slot residual) and Fig 1 :15 (*"where to look: broken vs novel"*).
2. **Vocabulary**: does the organization have terms for what the new situation affords? Fig 3 :7: *"it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked"*.
3. **Objective**: can it say what would count as progress in the new stage? Fig 3 :10: *"it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms"*.
4. **Plan**: a route under incomplete information. Fig 3 :13.
5. **Learn and carry**: name what worked, verify it, carry it. Fig 3 :15.

The room follows the question (Fig 10 :71-72), so the question is upstream of the chart. A re-org that starts at step 4 (new reporting lines) while step 2 or 3 is missing pushes decisions down to people who cannot say what would count as progress. Fig 3 :22-23: a reading taken below the break is *"a reading of nothing."* This is the order claim, and it explains why the same structural change helps one company and harms another: the break is in a different link.

- **Falsifier, wrote first:** a measured improvement in per-slot `R` from a pure step-4 intervention while steps 2 and 3 are demonstrably absent and unmeasured. That would break the dependency order.

### Q4 : How does the room chain advance?

Fig 7 :26: *"progress per handoff: R = h² × S"*
Fig 7 :38-39: *"the bracket's fidelity across the handoff"* / *"the ground's pressure on the room"*
Fig 7 :36: **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."**
Fig 7 :32: *"→ one long room: motion, no progress"*

A stage transition is a handoff between rooms. Both factors must be non-zero. If the new room is a copy of the old one, bracket fidelity is high in a trivial sense but the next room does not differ, so there is no selection and no progress. If the new room differs but nothing carries the surfaced residual across (a re-org that loses the customer signal), the bracket fails. Fig 9 :41: **"so keep every cut ranked and reversible"** is the corresponding operating rule: a handoff is not a one-way bet.

### Q5 : What do genuinely low-overlap fields corroborate?

World sources here are secondary and corroborate shape only. They are not figure lines and they never decide the verdict.

- **Cybernetics and mathematics.** Ashby's law, *"only variety can destroy variety"* (Wikipedia, *Variety (cybernetics)*, quoting Ashby 1956), plus the good regulator result that a regulator needs at least as many distinguishable states as the system it regulates. Prediction: a control structure of fixed variety fails as the firm's variety grows, independently of which chart is chosen. Corroborates the scale and habitat claims. It does not select a chart, which matches the F8 gap. Field overlap with the business sources is low.
- **Evolutionary biology and paleontology.** Punctuated equilibrium (Eldredge and Gould 1972, via Wikipedia): *"the population will become stable, showing little evolutionary change for most of its geological history"*, with significant change *"restricted to rare and geologically rapid events of branching speciation"*. Prediction: long stable phases punctuated by rapid reorganization. Shape match to Greiner's evolution and revolution and to Fig 7's room chain. The criticism recorded on the same page (Dawkins calls it a minor wrinkle) is noted; the analogy carries no verdict weight.
- **Structural inertia (business/sociology).** Schwarz et al. 2020: *"Structurally inert firms suffer elevated failure risks in the face of environmental change because inertia makes it difficult to keep pace with the speed of such change."* Corroborates the habitat and channel claims from inside the same broad pool as Greiner, so one witness, not two.

## CONCLUSION

A growth transition is a membrane crossing whose admissible object is a method, not a recording (Fig 4 :22-26). Over a fixed atom set the before and after structures are isomers, same roles, different bonds, different behaviour (Fig 12 :117, :94); when the atom set changes, the relation is composition plus import or subtraction. The transition is ordered by dependency (Fig 3 :20), and it advances as a room handoff with two factors that can each be zero (Fig 7 :26, :36). Low-overlap corroboration from cybernetics and evolutionary biology supports the shape but selects nothing. This framing rescues what is checkable in the ordinary claim ("the old structure stopped fitting") without asserting a chart the figures withhold.
