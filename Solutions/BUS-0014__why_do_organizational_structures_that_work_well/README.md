# Solution BUS-0014 : figure-grounded package

**Question:** Why do organizational structures that work well at one stage of a company's growth often fail at the next?

**Source:** business.txt line 21

**Section:** 2. Organization design and decision-making

**Mode:** `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`. Every claim is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. No company was measured.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

**Question restated as an effect.** `R = |Γ(b,a) − o'|`, the gap between what an organization's structure makes its people predict and what the ground returns (a customer choice, a market payment, a regulator ruling). "Worked well at one stage" means per-slot `R` was paid by an anchor that did not move when the organization reported success. "Fails at the next" means `R` is no longer paid there. Option A: the structure itself became bad, so the remedy is to replace it with a better design. Option B: the question, the scale, the habitat, or the channel moved while the structure stayed fixed, so the remedy is to rebuild the room and re-check contact. Each option leads to a different action, and the figures decide between them.

**Bound shape (formula step "Direction", written before the run).** An acceptable answer must, for each slot: (a) name the non-updating anchor that paid at the earlier stage, (b) say what crossed the membrane between scales, a method or a recording, (c) name the room the structure formed for the question it faced, (d) name the habitat and channel at each stage, (e) name the seat that could refuse a verdict, and (f) state what would reverse the call. This is Fig 5 :65: *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."*
