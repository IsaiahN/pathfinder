**Subject:** One checkable claim about why a structure stops working after a growth stage

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about organizations. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms:** `R` is the gap between what an organization's structure predicts will happen and what the ground returns, a customer choice, a market payment, a regulator ruling. It is measured per slot (per decision, team, segment, time period), never as one org-wide average. "Worked well at one stage" means that gap was paid by an anchor that did not move when the company reported a success. "Fails at the next" means it is no longer paid there.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the transition is a union of conditions, not one problem with opposite answers. A structure stops working when one or more of these hold:
> 1. The question changed and the room did not. The structure was composed for the earlier stage's question; a room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure, and the slow decisions or missed signals are only its symptom.
> 2. What crossed into the new scale was a recording of one occasion, not a re-applicable method. Replayed at the next stage it repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one.
> 3. The channel to the ground decayed. The customer or regulator did not change; the organization's reading of them went stale, saturated or pooled across segments.
> 4. The habitat was substituted. Actors, conditions and relations changed, and a structure whose value was relative to the old residual flips without its own behaviour changing.
> 5. A standard that was a shared meaning got promoted into a verdict and now decides the outcome instead of describing it.
>
> These are reported separately, per domain. I am not averaging them into a percentage, and I am not claiming a figure line returns the next org chart. It returns a shape, not a name.
>
> **What would refute this:**
> 1. A transition with question, habitat and channel held strictly constant in which the identical structure's per-slot gap still rises.
> 2. A demonstrable recording of one occasion that reliably produces new successes at the next stage against a non-updating anchor.
> 3. A measured rise in the gap while independently measured channel fidelity stays high and non-declining.
> 4. A figure line that derives a unique next structure, or a ground measurement that settles the chart across habitats.
>
> I wrote these down before this email. If any holds, the output is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figure text with line numbers (local extract in this folder's framework): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`, cited as `Fig N :line`.
> - The eight-step loop and mode rule: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`.
> - The architecture essay with the full figure set: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** run it on a stage transition you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and the author's write-up warns that outputs can be contrived to fit the formula. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
