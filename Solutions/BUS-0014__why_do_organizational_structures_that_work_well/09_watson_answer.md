## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, why a structure that paid at one growth stage stops paying at the next. Option A: the structure became a bad object, so a better design is the remedy. Option B: the question, the scale boundary, the habitat and the channel moved while the structure stayed fixed, so a rebuilt room and a re-checked contact are the remedy. What each leads to: A produces an ungrounded chart prescription and treats symptoms as the cause; B produces a per-slot shape, five refuters, and an honest open slot where a discriminator is owed.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The gap between what the structure predicts and what the ground returns. Arity: at least three places, library, belief and action, observation, plus a network edge between slots and a time index. It is always a per-slot slice. Symmetry: stage and growth are orderings, failure is a threshold comparison, structure is a network arrangement; the governing bonds are ordered, so rearrangement changes the substance. Scale: the decision loop, the membrane between scales, the room chain across stages, and the habitat contact cascade. The transform between scales is lossy.
- **Domain 1, stage decision loop.** Actors: decision makers, library, actions, observations. Currency: per-slot residual reduction. Anchor: the outcome the market, customer or patient pays, which does not update on a report. Admitted.
- **Domain 2, membrane between scales.** Actors: the cast carrying the loop, methods and head starts. Currency: method versus recording. Anchor: whether a generator crosses. Admitted.
- **Domain 3, room chain across stages.** Actors: the rooms, the residual each surfaces, the next room. Currency: progress per handoff. Anchor: the ground's pressure on the room. Admitted.
- **Domain 4, habitat.** Actors: customers, competitors, regulators, suppliers, conditions, relations. Currency: payment, survival, contact. Anchor: the ground that settles, which does not update. Admitted.
- **Domain 5, verification seat and channel.** Actors: the seat that keeps the ground reachable, verifiers, conventions. Currency: verdicts versus conventions, channel fidelity. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Admitted.
- **Excluded 1, "the structure" as a free-floating object.** No nameable anchor. It is an arrangement inside domains 2 and 3, not a domain.
- **Excluded 2, "culture" as a sole cause.** Absorbed into domains 4 and 5.
- **Partition or union.** Union. A transition changes the room, the crossing, the habitat and the channel at once; a case in several conditions is categorically different from a case in one. Verdicts are kept separate and never averaged.
- **Order by dependency.** Perceive the new slots, acquire vocabulary for what the situation affords, state what counts as progress, plan, then learn and carry. The room follows the question, so the question is upstream of the chart. Diagnose which link stopped before crediting the structure.

Strain test applied: a growth stage is not one of the six substrate terms. It is a parameter of the room chain and the time channel, so no line strains after re-census (F9 in `01`).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

A structure fails at the next growth stage because the conditions that paid at the earlier stage are not invariant under the transition. The room no longer matches the question (Fig 10 :72); what crossed was a recording rather than a method (Fig 4 :25-26); the channel to a non-updating ground decayed while the ground did not (Fig 10 :24); the habitat was substituted and valence flipped with the residual (Fig 11 :40, :67); or a convention was promoted into a verdict (Fig 10 :47). The effect is per slot (Fig 1 :19), the conditions form a union with separate verdicts, and no figure selects the next chart (Fig 9 :32). Over a fixed atom set the before and after structures are isomers, same roles arranged differently (Fig 12 :117); when the set changes, the relation is composition plus import or subtraction. This is a `specified`, ungrounded result. The strongest single figure for outreach is Fig 10 :72, because it names the failure as a relation between question and room rather than a property of the chart.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode, three checkable sub-claims, five refuters, and the provenance of every figure line, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real avoidable cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending a recording or a chart prescription instead of the generator (Fig 10 :42). Fig 2 :42 prices the remedy: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."
- **undoable:** yes. The five conditions are separable, and a wrong mapping can be dropped without withdrawing the shape claim. `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove the chart prescription and the single-cause reading. No primitive has to be withdrawn, and a sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A transition with question, habitat and channel held constant that still raises per-slot residual breaks Fig 10 :72; a pure recording that produces new ground-paid successes at the next stage breaks Fig 4 :25-26; independently measured high channel fidelity alongside rising residual breaks Fig 10 :24; a habitat held constant with a still-flipping valence breaks Fig 11 :40; a figure line deriving the next chart closes the F8 gap. An instrument that separates these sources of one outcome reading would move the source question from `candidate` toward `grounded`. Prestige or agreement is not such an instrument.
