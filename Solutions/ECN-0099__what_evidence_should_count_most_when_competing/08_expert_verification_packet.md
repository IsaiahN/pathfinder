# Expert verification packet - what evidence should count most in economics

**What this is:** a claim in a form an economist, econometrician or methodologist can check. Mode,
derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what
would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-case verdicts, `general` on the shape. Not `grounded`.** No ground
reading of an actual economic disagreement was taken. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

When economic theories disagree, the evidence that should count most is whichever item is an anchor
for that specific disagreement: it does not change its answer when either theory pushes on it, it is
independent of the two theories rather than drawn from their shared evidence pool, and it registers
contact with what the question is constitutively about. Mathematical consistency is frame-internal
and cannot be a verdict; predictive accuracy counts only out of sample and from an independent
source; agreement from a shared pool is the pool talking; experiments and historical cases count
when they measure the axis actually in dispute. A universal ranking of the five kinds is a gap the
framework declines to fill, while the ranking for a named disagreement is determinate.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 2 and 7

- **Statement:** verdict weight belongs to an independent anchor, not to frame-internal coherence or
  to pooled agreement.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of
    what any frame thinks."
  - Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."
  - Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also
    produces."
  - Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence
    pool"
  - Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the
    ground votes."
- **Refuter:** show one evidence kind is an anchor for every economic disagreement regardless of the
  question; or show mathematical consistency changing contact with an independent ground; or show
  agreement from a single shared evidence pool carrying verdict weight.

### C2 - economics-facing; checkable against the methodology literature and a real design

- **Statement:** predictive accuracy counts only when out of sample and independent; experiments and
  historical cases count on the axis they actually measure, and the five kinds do not form one
  ladder.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 1 :23: "The ground enters as the only metric."
  - Fig 1 :25: "coverage, and compression achieved"
  - Fig 2 :37: "It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on
    axes they do not measure."
  - Fig 6 :32: "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument
    already returning something."
  - Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you
    a verdict."
- **What to check:** AEA 2010 (fetched) records that "the primary engine driving improvement has
  been a focus on the quality of empirical research designs" and also the worry that design-driven
  work seeks "good answers instead of good questions." Does that worry not match Fig 2 :37 exactly?
  The Nobel 2019 press release (fetched) describes experiments answering small precise questions,
  not a universal hierarchy. Is in-sample fit anywhere treated as ground contact in the literature,
  or only as "coverage, and compression achieved"?
- **Refuter:** exhibit an economic disagreement settled by internal consistency alone with the
  verdict unchanged by an independent ground; or show in-sample fit carrying verdict weight across
  two low-overlap instruments; or show a trial whose randomized axis is not the disputed axis still
  settling the disagreement without import.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation; checkable by reading Figures 9 and 12

- **Statement:** the five kinds are members of a functional group answering a dependency chain of
  questions, not isomers and not a dependency chain themselves. The universal ranking is a `gap`.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says
    what kind; the qualifier says which member."
  - Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the
    one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
  - Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you
    search again."
  - Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"
- **Refuter:** write all five over one shared atom set so they are genuine isomers (Fig 12 :117);
  or exhibit a real dependency relation between two evidence kinds so that one consumes the other as
  input; or produce a figure line licensing a fixed ordering of all five for all questions.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a universal evidence hierarchy. That is a `gap` after a genuine re-census.
- Not that randomized trials always win, or that prediction is truth.
- Not that mathematical consistency is useless. It is a convention and a validity constraint.
- Not that the framework settles any actual economic disagreement. No measurement was taken.
- Not `grounded`. Every substantive verdict is a candidate.
- Not that the corroborating web sources are independent witnesses where they share a pool. AEA 2010
  and Nobel 2019 both belong to the design-driven economics pool and are recorded as one witness on
  that point.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading on a named disagreement. Concretely:

1. Name what the disagreement is constitutively about and identify which candidate does not move
   when either theory pushes on it.
2. Check independence: re-run the comparison with the shared evidence pool removed.
3. Check ground contact: out-of-sample prediction against data unavailable to the tested theory.
4. Discriminating-source test: commit to the prediction before examining a historical case, then
   check with an independent instrument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (fetched): "If
   several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29, :36, :40 and Fig 1 :27 (four short passages).
2. Check C2 against a design you know: was the randomized axis the axis in dispute, and was the
   prediction registered before the data were seen?
3. Name one case where agreement from a shared pool was treated as corroboration in your field, or
   say why none exists.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
