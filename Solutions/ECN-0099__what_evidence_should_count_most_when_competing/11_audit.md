## Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual line alone, before relying on the primary's conclusions, using
the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors / currency /
anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Two or more economic theories disagreeing about a claim, with five
  candidate evidence kinds. Arity: two frames plus a meta-question about ranking. Symmetry: the kinds
  are not interchangeable, and which bears depends on the question. Scale: theory choice is second
  order. Question type: possibly several well-formed questions (Fig 9 :4) or one compound; decided by
  asking whether one ground settles all parts.
- **Domain 1, ground and anchor.** Actors: the world, measurements, experiments, markets. Currency:
  residual against a non-updating ground. Anchor: the ground. Partition: ground versus frame-internal.
- **Domain 2, independence.** Actors: the competing frames and their evidence pools. Currency:
  independence. Anchor: independence, never agreement. Union collapse when one pool is shared.
- **Domain 3, prediction.** Actors: predictions and observations. Currency: out-of-sample residual.
  Anchor: the ground via contact. Partition: contact versus internal coherence.
- **Domain 4, instruments.** Actors: interventions, assignment, records. Currency: identification and
  comparison conditions. Anchor: the world's response. Union: different instruments, not a ladder.

Order by dependency: anchor legitimacy (Fig 2 :36), then independence (Fig 2 :31), then ground contact
(Fig 1 :23), then instrument (Fig 6 :32). Describe before choosing (Fig 9 :27).

My census matches the primary's four domains and the union/partition handling. Where the primary
derived from Fig 1 and Fig 2, I derived from Fig 9 :4 and Fig 3 :20. Different starting figures, same
domain boundaries: low overlap between routes, so the agreement is weak but not circular within the
framework. It is still one evidence pool.

## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down,
`gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it
to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually
uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 2 :29-30, :36, :40 and Fig 1 :23, :27.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07`: independent anchor, ground contact, no
   universal ranking, consistency disqualified.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a practical evidence rule for a named economics disagreement.

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **question-constitutive condition**, which a practical rule tends to drop, turning the shape
  into a universal ladder;
- the **independence check**, which in practice collapses into counting publications or citations
  from one pool;
- the **out-of-sample restriction** on predictive accuracy, which drops into in-sample fit when a
  convenient data set is at hand.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: verdict weight belongs to what does not move when a theory
pushes on it and is independent of the frames. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not**
measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the
enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the formula as identical to the figures would set
R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy
condensation with a measured gap, which `09_watson_answer.md` states.
