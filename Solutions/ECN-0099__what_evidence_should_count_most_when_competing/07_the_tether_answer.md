# The Tether's answer on what evidence should count most

**Mode: `specified` on the per-case verdicts, `general` on the shape. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape
and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures
(`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Two or more economic theories disagree about a claim. Five evidence kinds are candidates for verdict
weight. The question asks which should count most. Restated in effect terms: which candidate settles
the disagreement without moving when a theory pushes on it, is independent of the competing frames,
and registers contact with what the question is about? The answer keeps the five verdicts separate;
it never averages them into one "evidence strength."

## 2. The shape

1. **The anchor test decides.** Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not
   change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :30: "If it moves when you push on it,
   it is not an anchor." Decisive evidence is an anchor for that specific disagreement, and Fig 2
   :36 says an anchor "is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."
2. **Mathematical consistency cannot be a verdict.** Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame
   cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." Consistency is a validity constraint and a
   convention, not ground contact.
3. **Predictive accuracy counts only out of sample and from an independent source.** Fig 1 :23:
   "The ground enters as the only metric." In-sample fit is "coverage, and compression achieved"
   (Fig 1 :25) and is frame-internal. Confirmation is not verification (SEP, fetched 2026-11-23).
4. **Agreement is not corroboration when the pool is shared.** Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world
   is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is
   variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
5. **Experiments and historical cases count when they are the anchor for the disputed axis.** Fig 2
   :37: "It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they
   do not measure." Randomized trials, natural experiments and historical cases are different
   instruments, not points on one ladder.
6. **No universal ranking is licensed.** Anchor legitimacy is question-constitutive (Fig 2 :36), so
   a ranking valid for every economic disagreement is a `gap`. The per-disagreement ranking is
   determinate. Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a
   filter hand you a verdict."

## 3. The per-domain verdicts

| domain | verdict | status |
|---|---|---|
| D1 Ground / anchor | anchor test; question-constitutive legitimacy | triangulated (Fig 1, Fig 2, Nobel, SEP) |
| D2 Independence | shared-pool agreement disqualified; triangulation required | triangulated (Fig 2, Fig 7, Fig 8, AEA) |
| D3 Prediction | out-of-sample accuracy counts; in-sample fit and consistency do not | triangulated (Fig 1, Fig 5, SEP, Box) |
| D4 Instrument | RCT, natural experiment, historical case each count on their own axis | triangulated for the shape, single-source per instance |
| Universal ranking | no figure line licenses one ordering for all questions | `gap`, plainly stated |

Verdicts are kept separate. Nothing here adds them into a composite score.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that randomized trials always win. They win when the question is constitutively about the
  randomized effect and the measured axis is the disputed one.
- Not that predictive accuracy is truth. It is corroboration against a ground, not verification.
- Not that mathematical consistency is useless. It is a convention and a validity constraint, and
  Fig 2 :41 calls such agreement "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is
  the product, not a corruption of it."
- Not that the framework settles any actual economic disagreement. No ground reading was taken.
- Not a universal evidence hierarchy. That claim is a gap after a genuine re-census.

## 5. What would settle it - the ground

A ground reading, not a further argument. For a named disagreement:

- State what the question is constitutively about, and which candidate does not move when either
  theory pushes on it.
- Check that the candidate's source is independent of both theory pools. Re-run the comparison with
  the pool removed.
- Check out-of-sample prediction against data unavailable to the theory being tested.
- **Discriminating-source test:** for a proposed historical or observational case, commit to the
  prediction before examining the case, then check whether an independent instrument confirms it.
  *The Hard Problem is About Source* (fetched): "If several paths create the same behavior, then
  behavior is a projection with lossy information."
- **Standing falsifiers from `01` §4:** one evidence kind shown to be an anchor for every question;
  consistency shown to change ground contact; shared-pool agreement shown to carry verdict weight;
  an out-of-sample loser beating an internally consistent rival; the five kinds reducing to one
  compensating scale.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The universal ranking is unreachable because anchor legitimacy is
question-constitutive; that is a verdict, not a silence. The per-case ranking is reached as a shape
and stops at the limit of the available ground reading. Indiscernibility is not identity. The
lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not rank historical cases, natural experiments, randomized trials, mathematical
> consistency and predictive accuracy on one ladder, and it does not claim such a ladder exists. It
> says that the evidence which should count most is whichever item is an anchor for the specific
> disagreement: something that does not move when either theory pushes on it, that is independent of
> the two theories rather than drawn from their shared data, and that registers contact with what
> the question is about. Mathematical consistency is frame-internal and cannot be a verdict;
> predictive accuracy counts only out of sample; agreement from one evidence pool is the pool
> talking; experiments and historical cases count when they measure the axis actually in dispute. A
> universal ranking is a gap the framework declines to fill, while the ranking for a named
> disagreement is determinate. What it offers is the shape decisive evidence must have, falsifiers
> written in advance, and the ground reading that would close each case.
