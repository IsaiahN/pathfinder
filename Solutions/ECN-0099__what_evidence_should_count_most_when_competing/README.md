## Question and source

**Question:** What evidence should count most when competing economic theories disagree: historical cases, natural experiments, randomized trials, mathematical consistency, or predictive accuracy?

**Source:** economics questions.txt line 130.

**Section:** 10. Economic methods, welfare, and alternative frameworks.

## Restating the question as an effect

Two or more economic theories (frames with different closures) disagree about a claim. Five kinds of
evidence are offered as candidates for verdict weight. Restated in effect terms: which candidate
(1) settles the disagreement without moving when a theory pushes on it, (2) is independent of the
competing frames rather than drawn from their shared evidence pool, and (3) registers contact with
the thing the question is about rather than with the internal coherence of a theory? Each option
leads somewhere different: choosing consistency or pooled agreement produces a coherent drift that
never touches the ground; choosing an independent, question-constitutive anchor produces a checkable
verdict.

The sought-for shape is bound as an ordinary term before derivation, per `THE_FORMULA.md`
"Direction": **decisive evidence** means evidence whose standing does not depend on which theory
holds it, whose source is independent of the competing frames, and which registers contact with the
ground for the specific question. Binding it makes the preference refusable by the same test as
everything else.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the answer.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons in the figures' own words:

1. **A room does not upgrade the label.** Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Adding recipients raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor.
2. **Selecting for agreement manufactures the disqualifying correlation.** Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."
3. **Asking for trust before checking spends independence.** Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." The outside frame must not hold the interiors.

Legally: unsolicited bulk outreach to personal addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in
many jurisdictions. Public opt-in channels avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
