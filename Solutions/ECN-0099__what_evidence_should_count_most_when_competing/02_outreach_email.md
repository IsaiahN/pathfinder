# Outreach draft: check the shape claim before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in
`03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the claim in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` has been
checked for the three sub-claims. The stage-1 output must survive validation or the two-stage offer
collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** What would you count as a failure here? One shape claim, no theory required

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about economic
> evidence and methodology. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you
> to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` shape, `specified` per case):** when two economic theories disagree, the
> evidence that should count most is whichever item is an anchor for that specific disagreement,
> meaning it does not change its answer when either theory pushes on it, it is independent of the two
> theories rather than drawn from their shared evidence pool, and it registers contact with the
> thing the question is actually about. The figures put it this way: "An anchor is whatever settles
> the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." (Fig 2 :29). And
> "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." (Fig 2 :36).
>
> What follows for the five candidates you might have in mind:
>
> - **Mathematical consistency** cannot be the verdict. It is a property the producing theory also
>   scores, and the framework says frame-internal quantities "do not count" (Fig 1 :24). It is a
>   validity constraint, not a ground.
> - **Predictive accuracy** counts only out of sample, against data the theory being tested did not
>   have and from a source independent of that theory. In-sample accuracy is the same genus as
>   "coverage, and compression achieved" (Fig 1 :25).
> - **Agreement between theories, panels, or data sets that share one evidence pool** does not count.
>   "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." (Fig 2 :35).
> - **Randomized trials, natural experiments, and historical cases** are instruments. Each counts
>   most when the question is constitutively about the axis that instrument measures. An instrument
>   "can be wrong on axes they do not measure" (Fig 2 :37).
>
> I am **not** claiming there is one fixed ranking that works for every economic disagreement.
> Anchor legitimacy is question-constitutive, so a universal ranking is a gap in what the framework
> can return. The ranking for a named disagreement is determinate; the ranking for economics in
> general is not something I am asserting.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. Show one evidence kind is an anchor for every economic disagreement regardless of the question.
> 2. Show mathematical consistency changing contact with an independent ground.
> 3. Show agreement from a single shared evidence pool carrying verdict weight.
> 4. Show a theory judged by out-of-sample prediction losing to a rival judged only by internal
>    consistency.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line
> "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no
> reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my
> summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical figures README: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Credibility revolution in empirical economics: https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/jep.24.2.3
> - Nobel 2019, experimental approach to poverty: https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/economic-sciences/2019/press-release/
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on an economics
> problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result
> *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me
> than a reported success.

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Shape claim and refuter first, theory last | Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Frames range against the ground separately (Fig 2 :45). |
| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." |
| Pre-registered refuter | A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device. |
| Mode stated on every substantive claim | `THE_FORMULA.md` "Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which." |
| No universal ranking asserted | Fig 2 :36 anchor legitimacy is question-constitutive; Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict." |
| Predictive accuracy narrowed to out of sample | Fig 1 :25 "coverage, and compression achieved"; SEP on confirmation not verifying a hypothesis. |
| Consistency given as convention, not verdict | Fig 1 :27 "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." |
| No request for trust or prestige | The figures' own rule: the ground settles, agreement from a shared pool does not (Fig 2 :40). |
| Offer the formula as a method to reuse | Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." |
| Ask recipients to write their own refuter | `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": a frame cannot check the direction of its own question. |
