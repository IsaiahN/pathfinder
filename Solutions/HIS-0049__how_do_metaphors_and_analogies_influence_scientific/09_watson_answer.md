## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the influence of metaphors and analogies on scientific classification and explanation. Option A: treat the influence as decoration or persuasion, so that what matters is how memorable the comparison is and how many people repeat it. Option B: treat it as a frame import with a source gate and a shadow test, settled by the ground. What each leads to: A makes agreement and prestige the currency, which the figures disqualify as a verdict (Fig 2 :29-32, :40); B gives a checkable mechanism, four refuters written in advance, and an open question until a ground discriminates.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** How a structure carried from a familiar domain changes what counts as a kind and what counts as an explanation. Arity: binary and ordered, a source frame mapped onto a target residual, with two outputs affected (classification, explanation). Symmetry: asymmetric. "The atom is like the solar system" is not an identity, so `→` is the right glyph and `≡` ("two names, one referent", Fig 12 :65-67) is the wrong one. Scale: two, one scientist's mapping and a discipline's paradigm, with a lossy transform between (Fig 4 :19-20).
- **Domain 1, the analogy operation.** Actors: a source frame with a closure, a target residual described in effect terms. Currency: provenance and shadow test. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Question type: partition (the wall either moves or it does not). This is an import, not a mint.
- **Domain 2, classification.** Actors: specimens, atoms, bonds, categories. Currency: which arrangement is recorded as the term. Anchor: experiment, which decides whether two things are the same kind. Question type: union where two conditions overlap, so unbundle before searching (Fig 9 :54-58).
- **Domain 3, explanation.** Actors: theories, models, mapped relations, predictions. Currency: which relations transfer and whether they predict. Anchor: experiment or measurement. Question type: partition (a shape or a name).
- **Domain 4, the social record.** Actors: communities, paradigms, prestige, reviewers. Currency: agreement. Anchor: **none of its own**. Agreement is not an anchor (Fig 2 :29-32). Question type: union compound, not one question. Its verdict weight is zero.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Describe R in effect terms (Fig 9 :27-28) -> find the frame whose closure predicts its shape (Fig 8 :27-28) -> import with source gate and shadow test (Fig 6 :11-14) -> settle against the ground (Fig 2 :45) -> re-sorted kinds become operands at the next level (Fig 12 :104).

Strain test applied. Fig 11 :38 "That is not a metaphor for intelligence. It is a description of the practice" would forbid metaphor if read globally, but that reading contradicts the import machinery in D1, so by the self-consistency rule the domain was misidentified. Re-census puts it in D3, where it governs the habitat description only. Likewise Fig 12 :97 "This is not an analogy" governs the isomer case, not analogy as an operation. No strain survives re-census; one gap is declared (the psychological mechanism is world-side only) and one weak link is named (the Fig 3 middle link has no instrument).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives, and the four domain verdicts stay separate. An analogy is a gated import from a frame whose closure differs, licensed by provenance and a shadow test, with the residual described before the frame is chosen. It changes classification through the recorded bond rather than the ingredient list. It changes explanation through the transfer of relations, returning a kind of thing and never the answer, and a comparison that changes no contact has improved pedagogy rather than explanation. The social effect of a memorable metaphor is real but carries no verdict weight, because agreement has no anchor. Two global readings were retracted: Fig 11 :38 does not ban metaphor, and Fig 12 :97 does not ban analogy. One sub-claim (relations-not-attributes) is world-side single-source, not figure-derived. This is a `general`/`specified`, ungrounded, candidate result.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states four refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a recording instead of a generator, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and the refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The two retracted global readings are withdrawn without touching the import derivation. The relations-not-attributes sub-claim can be removed without changing D1, D2 or D4. A sent claim can be superseded by the corrected packet, and no primitive has to be withdrawn.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A case where the ground discriminates an arrangement change from a relabelling would settle D2; a case where relation transfer predicts and attribute transfer does not would settle D3; a case where an import adds a primitive, or moves a wall without contact with a different frame, would break D1; a case where agreement about a metaphor, with no new measurement and no new source gate, changes the ground's answer would break D4 and the anchor definition. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
