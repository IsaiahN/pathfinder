## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."

*The Library of Babel* makes the same point in search terms: "You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

For **this** question a shape is: *a gated import that selects the recorded bond and maps relations, returning a kind of thing and never the answer*. That constrains. It does not fill. "Metaphor is the mechanism" is a name, not a shape. "The analogy changes which arrangement is recorded" is a shape.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel* on bootstrapping vocabulary: "If you don't know the right terms to search for, you will have to use the existing vocabulary you do know that is closest to the words that you wish to describe and grasp the shape of." And: "With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."
- The same essay says the thing that makes analogy work is exactly structural transfer: "The reason why analogies work so well to explain concepts: they are translating structural relationships and problems through language and scenarios that you are already familiar with, so that the knowledge transfers." This is the essay-side counterpart of Fig 12 :111, "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."
- *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* says the author "used analogy as a bridge across domains that i did not have vocabulary or jargon to understand." That is the same claim from the practitioner's side, and it is a useful admission: an analogy is a *bridge*, which is Fig 6 :23, "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." This is the method of `02_outreach_email.md`: send the effect description, not the source.
- Fig 6 :30, quoted in that same essay: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause." "your words are borrowed too" is the precise hazard for a question about metaphor: the vocabulary of the source frame travels with the analogy and will decide in advance what looks like a match.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." The README: the framework "stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the discriminator argument: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information" and "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy." Applied here: two metaphors can produce the same classification while arriving from different structures (one from a real bond, one from a repeated label). A reading of the classification cannot tell which. The spectrometer, not an argument, resolved the colour case; in this question the corresponding need is a case where the ground discriminates an arrangement change from a label change.

*They're Scanning Books* sharpens the classification point: "Change the sensors, and not just the data changes, the shape of the compression changes: the categories, the primitives, what counts as similar to what." A metaphor is a compression scheme imported from another sensorium. It changes categories because it changes what counts as similar. The same essay warns: "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible." So borrow a metaphor with its provenance written on it, not as a transparent lens.

## 4. Correction to my own reading of "shape, not a name"

I initially read the rule as "never name the structure a metaphor imports." That overstates it. The README's phrase is "what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." The subject is a primitive not held in Γ. Most scientific metaphors bind onto atoms the receiving science already holds (energy, flow, network, kind, boundary). Naming such a binding is not inventing a new primitive. What is forbidden is claiming the binding moves the wall when it adds no atom: Fig 6 :28 says "An import adds no atom to the world," and Fig 6 :21 says "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."

Both claims are kept here: (a) a binding onto held atoms is allowed if its provenance is recorded, (b) calling that binding a new primitive is refused. The correction does not erase the earlier caution; it narrows it to the right object.

## 5. Q answered: what licenses a particular influence?

**Nothing in the framework licenses a particular metaphor as true.** A metaphor licenses a *shape* of influence (a bond is recorded, relations transfer, the social record has no anchor). A particular metaphor is licensed only when the ground settles a minted term, or when it arrives as a provenanced import with a shadow test. Until one of those, the influence is the searcher's own contribution and must be recorded as such.

This is why `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` keeps the historical isomer case as the strongest witness: there the arrangement was forced by experiment, not by a comparison. Fig 12 :97-98: "This is not an analogy. Chemistry hit it first: silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different properties, which is what forced structure into the notation."

## 6. Prestige is not action

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige." And: "You may all agree and be wrong."

A memorable metaphor that a community repeats is a prestige loop: a term is believed because it is familiar, and the ground is never consulted. The legitimate ask is the opposite: give the claim a budget to be tried, then let an instrument, an experiment or a board settle it. Fig 2 :40, quoted in *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." A list of recipients chosen because they are likely to like the claim is one pool wearing many names.

## 7. My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

Same essay: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."

So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, `06_isomers_and_order.md` and `09_watson_answer.md` are simulated derivations, checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are **not** tests of the claim and must be labelled as such. They cannot settle anything they report on. The only thing that can settle it is a case where the ground discriminates: an arrangement change against a label change, a relation transfer against an attribute transfer, or a source gate that actually explains a gap.
