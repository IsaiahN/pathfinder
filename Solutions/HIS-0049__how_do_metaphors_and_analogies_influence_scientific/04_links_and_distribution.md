## Wider literature that bears on the derivation

1. **Hesse's neutral analogy.** Both the SEP analogy entry and the SEP models entry report Hesse's distinction. The neutral part of an analogy, the part not yet known to be similar or different, is what "gives rise to questions and suggest[s] new hypotheses." This is the world-side counterpart of Fig 6 :11-14: the import earns its keep by explaining a gap (the shadow test), not by being vivid.
2. **Gentner's systematicity.** Relations, not attributes, transfer, and a predicate embedded in a connected system is more likely to be imported than an isolated one. This is the world-side counterpart of Fig 12 :111, "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." Low overlap with the figures: cognitive science rather than a metatheory.
3. **Priestley's claim** in the SEP analogy entry, "analogy is our best guide in all philosophical investigations," is a useful historical witness to the heuristic role, and a good example of a claim the figures would *not* accept as a verdict: a heuristic is a filter for the budget, never a witness (Fig 9 :60).
4. **The acoustical analogy** in SEP §1, which "guide[d] and limit[ed] discovery" before quantum theory overturned it, is a documented case of an import whose wall moved but whose settlement failed. It is exactly the case Fig 5 :69-72 warns about: settling is necessary for composition and not sufficient, and an import against the wrong budget adds a term that was not needed.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 10 :52:** "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Sending the whole framework before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and the four refuters.
- **Fig 2 :35:** "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Do not ask whether the recipient agrees. Ask what they would count as a failure.
- **Fig 8 :50-52:** convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents; only the record separates them. So the first contact must not depend on the reader trusting the source.
- **Fig 4 :22:** "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." The formula is a generator, so offering it in stage 3 gives away a method, not an answer.
- **Fig 2 :40:** agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool. A reply is a conversation, never corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the residual and the shape claim; (2) the four refuters; (3) ask what the recipient would count as a failure and record the answer; (4) only then the figure text and the three links, with the note that the figure text is the authority; (5) only if asked, the formula as a method with a user-written refuter and a stated mode.
