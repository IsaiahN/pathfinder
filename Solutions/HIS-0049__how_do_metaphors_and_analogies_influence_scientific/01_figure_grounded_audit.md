## 0. The residual, before any figure line is chosen

The question asks how metaphors and analogies *influence* two things: scientific classification (what counts as a kind) and scientific explanation (what counts as an account of why). Both effects are observable. The unstated question is the mechanism and its limit.

State R in effect terms:

- **What is being decided:** whether the influence is (A) decoration or social persuasion, or (B) a frame import that moves a closure, with a source gate, a shadow test, and a ground that settles it.
- **What each option leads to:** A makes agreement and prestige the currency, which the figures disqualify (Fig 2 :29-32, :40). B makes the residual and the ground the currency, and leaves a checkable record (Fig 6 :11-14, Fig 8 :50-52).
- **What an answer would look like (bind it as an ordinary term, Formula step "Direction"):** a three-part shape, (i) the operation by which a source frame is carried onto a target residual, (ii) the condition under which it changes a classification, (iii) the condition under which it changes an explanation, plus a refuter for each. This is the sought-for shape written down before the search so it can accrue a residual like anything else.

R by arity, symmetry and scale:

- **Arity:** binary and ordered. A source frame is mapped onto a target residual. Gentner's structure-mapping result (world source, cognitive science) is that relations, not attributes, are mapped, and the mapping is asymmetric. Under the figures, an ordered bond (`→` sequence, Fig 12 :30-32) is the right notation, not a symmetric one (`+`, `≡`, Fig 12 :55-69).
- **Symmetry:** asymmetric. "The atom is like the solar system" is not reversible in the way an identity is. The analogy maps a system of relations; it does not assert identity. The figures' identity bond `≡` is "two names, one referent" (Fig 12 :65-67), which is exactly what an analogy is not.
- **Scale:** two, with a lossy transform between. One scientist's mapping (one agent, Fig 4 :16-17) and a discipline's paradigm (many agents, Fig 4 :4-5). "The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change" (Fig 4 :19-20). So the operation is the same at both scales; only the cast changes.

## 3. Findings, with verbatim figure lines before conclusion

### F1 - An analogy is an import, and import is the only operator that moves the wall (D1, decisive)

Fig 6 :11-14:

> **"IMPORT"**
> **"source gate: provenance"**
> **"shadow test: explains a gap"**
> **"the only operator that moves the wall"**

Fig 8 :32:

> **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."**

So a metaphor or analogy cannot change a science by rearranging what that science already holds. That is mint inside a closure, and it adds no atom. What it *can* do is bring a frame whose closure differs into contact with the residual, and that is an import. The two gates are not optional: provenance asks where the structure came from, and the shadow test asks whether it explains the gap it was brought in for. A metaphor that fails both is a blend, which Fig 8 :6-7 names "collapse 5: the undirected union" and "→ interdisciplinarity, no mint." The surplus exists but nothing was aimed, so nothing is owed and nothing lands.

Fig 6 :28:

> **"An import adds no atom to the world."**

So the influence is on access, not on the world. This is the first half of the answer and it is `general` (the operation is the same at every scale and in every field).

**Refuter:** show a case where a metaphor adds a primitive to the world rather than to a frame's access, or where a frame's wall moves without contact with a frame whose closure differs. Either breaks F1.

### F2 - Residual first, frame second; otherwise apophenia (D1)

Fig 9 :27-28:

> **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**

Fig 8 :27-28:

> **"1: describe R, then find the frame that already predicts its shape"**

This is the exact order question. If a scientist picks the source frame first and then reads the target off it, any frame will seem to fit, because the fit was manufactured by the choice. The figures call the right order a one-pass check, not a search: "matching is a one-pass check, not a search" (Fig 9 :29) and "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause" (Fig 9 :30-31). "your words are borrowed too" is unusually apt here, because the vocabulary of the source frame travels with the analogy and will silently define what counts as a match.

**Refuter:** a documented case where describing the residual first made the correct frame *less* findable than picking the frame first, with the ground settling it. Not expected, but written down before the run.

### F3 - Classification changes when the bond changes, not when the ingredients change (D2, decisive)

Fig 12 :5:

> **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**

Fig 12 :94:

> **"The arrangement is the substance."**

Fig 12 :95-96:

> **"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."**

Fig 12 :117:

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

This is the mechanism by which an analogy changes classification. A metaphor imports a *way of seeing the bond*. Under a flat ingredient list, two things look like one kind; once the arrangement is recorded, they split. The figure's own worked case is historical: Fig 12 :97-98 says

> **"This is not an analogy. Chemistry hit it first: silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different properties, which is what forced structure into the notation."**

and Fig 13 :121-123:

> **"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."**
> **"In chemistry this has been established since Liebig and Wöhler found two substances with the same composition and different properties, 1824 to 1826, and Berzelius named it isomerism in 1830."**

Note the careful wording, "This is not an analogy." The figure is not saying analogy is forbidden. It is saying the chemical case is a *literal* structural fact, and that is exactly why it is the stronger witness: the arrangement, not the metaphor, made the classification. The world source on isomer history (Wikipedia, "Isomer", 2026, opened 2026-11-23) independently confirms the Wöhler 1827 / Berzelius 1830 sequence and adds that the finding "challenged the prevailing chemical understanding of the time, which held that chemical compounds could be distinct only when their elemental compositions differ." This corroborates F3 from a different field.

**Refuter:** a documented case where a community re-sorted a kind with no change in the recorded arrangement, only a change of label, and the ground paid for the new sort. That would mean the bond is not the substance and Fig 12 is the wrong domain for classification.

**Strain declined (see §7):** reading "This is not an analogy" as a global ban on analogy in science strains the line. It governs a specific claim (the isomer case is literal, not figurative), so by the canonical re-census rule it governs another quantity. Re-census puts it in D2, and F1 stands.

### F4 - Explanation maps relations, not attributes, and returns a shape (D3, decisive)

Fig 8 :27-28, already quoted in F2, is the explanation rule too: find the frame that already predicts the shape of R. What crosses is the relation set. Fig 11 :36 is the figure's own guard against mistaking the description for the thing:

> **"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."**

and the line that governs this question directly, Fig 11 :38:

> **"That is not a metaphor for intelligence. It is a description of the practice."**

This line is the most quoted-relevant line in the whole figure set for this question, and it is also the most dangerous to over-read. It governs the *habitat description*: the account of enumeration, instruments and introduction is a description of what a maintainer does, not a figurative comparison with intelligence. It does **not** forbid metaphor as a cognitive operation. Read globally it would contradict F1 and the whole import machinery, so by the self-consistency rule the global reading is wrong and the domain must be re-censused. It belongs to D3 and says: an explanation earns its standing by describing the practice that contacts the ground, not by being a vivid comparison.

Fig 9 :32 gives the limit of what is returned:

> **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**

and Fig 11 :40 gives the test of whether an explanatory improvement is real:

> **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

A metaphor that makes a theory easier to teach but changes no contact has not improved the explanation in the figures' sense. It may have improved the *pedagogy*, which is a different domain with a different currency, and must be recorded as such. The hydraulic analogy world source (Wikipedia, 2026) makes the same point in engineering language: "If taken too far, the water analogy can create misconceptions. Negative transfer can occur when there is a mismatch between phenomena in the source (hydraulics) and the corresponding phenomena in the target (electronics)." That is F4's contact test stated from the classroom.

**Refuter:** an explanation whose influence on the ground is unchanged in *kind* (only in vividness or retellability) but which the relevant community and the ground both count as a new explanation, with the ground voting. That would break the contact test.

### F5 - The social record has no anchor of its own (D4)

Fig 2 :29-30:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."**

Fig 7 :12-13:

> **"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"**

Fig 2 :35:

> **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

A metaphor can become accepted because it is memorable, because a prestigious group adopts it, or because it is repeated. All three are the social record, and none is an anchor. Fig 4 :25:

> **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."**

A successful analogy repeated down through a community is a recording, not a generator. It makes the next search cheaper (Fig 4 :22, "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down.") but it does not settle what the new classification or explanation means. D4 therefore gets a verdict of *no verdict*: it is a contact field to be enumerated, not a source of evidence. "The habitat is enumerated, never composed" (Fig 11 :43); "You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual" (Fig 11 :48).

**Refuter:** a case where community agreement about a metaphor, with no new measurement and no new source gate, changed the ground's answer to a classification question. By Fig 2 :29 that cannot happen; if it did, the anchor definition is wrong.

### F6 - The mechanism is ordered, and vocabulary without bonds cannot say a route (notation check)

Fig 12 :30-32 defines `→` as "A then B; B needs A to have happened," with the test "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes." Fig 12 :111:

> **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**

Fig 12 :115:

> **"having terms for what a situation affords was taken for a dictionary and it is a grammar."**

An analogy supplies the grammar (which relations may hold), not just a dictionary (which objects are present). That is why it can change explanation: it changes what can be said to follow from what. It is also why it can mislead: a grammar borrowed from hydraulics will happily generate sentences about electronics that have no referent there. The hydraulic analogy source records exactly this as "negative transfer" and notes that "in order for the model to be useful, the reader or student must have a substantial understanding of the model (hydraulic) system's principles." That is the shadow test, stated by an educator.

The mapping is asymmetric, so `→` is the right glyph and `≡` ("two names, one referent", Fig 12 :65-67) is the wrong one. An analogy that is promoted to `≡` has stopped being an analogy and has become a claim of identity, which must then pay the ground's price or be refused by ¬ (Operators table: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate").

**Refuter:** a documented analogy where the source and target relations are interchangeable with no loss and the ground pays for the identity. Then `≡` would be the correct glyph and this finding falls.

### F7 - Filters, not witnesses (budget discipline)

Fig 9 :60:

> **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

Three filters are tempting here and all three are excluded: the number of textbooks that repeat the metaphor, the prestige of the scientist who coined it, and its pedagogical success. Each is a real quantity for deciding *where to look* and *how much to spend*. None can decide whether the classification or explanation is right. Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." A widely repeated metaphor is one evidence pool wearing many names (Fig 8 :49: "Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names").

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach, per the seat-map rule: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. These are written here, before §5 and before `07_the_tether_answer.md`:

1. **Import refuter.** Show a metaphor or analogy adding a primitive to the world, or moving a frame's wall without contact with a frame whose closure differs. Breaks F1.
2. **Bond refuter.** Show a community re-sorting a kind with no change in the recorded bond or arrangement, only a change of label, with the ground paying for the new sort. Breaks F3.
3. **Relations refuter.** Show an analogy's explanatory influence surviving with zero shared relational structure, only attribute overlap, and the ground settling it. Breaks F4 and the Gentner corroboration.
4. **Anchor refuter.** Show community agreement about a metaphor, with no new measurement and no new source gate, changing the ground's answer to a classification question. Breaks F5 and the anchor definition at Fig 2 :29.

If any fires, the corresponding domain verdict is withdrawn, not softened. Until one is run, nothing in this folder is labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with F1 and F2: the operation is an import with two gates, and the residual must be described before the frame is chosen. This is the part that is `general` and most likely to survive a re-census.
2. Use F3 for the classification half: the analogy changes what is recorded as the bond, and the isomer case is the clean historical witness.
3. Use F4 for the explanation half: relations transfer, attributes do not, and an explanation that changes no contact has not improved.
4. Keep F5 as a refusal, not a finding about people: the social record has no anchor, so agreement is reported as contact, never as verdict.
5. Send the generator (the derivation and the four refuters), not the conclusion. Fig 4 :25 again: a recording is a description of one occasion.
6. State the mode on every claim. `general` for the operation, `specified` for the per-domain predictions, `candidate` throughout until a ground settles them.

## 7. Strains, gaps and honest stop

- **The middle link of Fig 3 has no instrument.** The figures say a link with no instrument is a link nobody has looked at (Fig 3 :24-27). Here the middle link is exactly "having terms for what a situation affords," which is what the analogy supplies. No measurement in this derivation closes it. Named, not hidden.
- **Fig 8 :49 versus F5.** "Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names" governs independence between frames, not the social record. Kept in D1 as a warning about repeated metaphors, not used as a D4 verdict.
- **No figure line governs the *psychological* mechanism** of why a relation is easier to map than an attribute. That is a `gap` in the figures, filled only by a secondary world source (Gentner, structure-mapping). It is not promoted to a figure verdict. "Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence."
- **Round trip R_T.** The condensation from the full figure set down to the shorthand "analogy = import with two gates" drops three constraints: the bond mechanism (F3), the relations-not-attributes point (F4, which is not even in the figures), and the no-anchor rule for the social record (F5). Measured qualitatively in `11_audit.md`, not set to zero.

**Verdict of this audit.** The shape survives: an analogy is a gated import that works by selecting the recorded bond and by mapping relations, returning a kind of thing and never the answer, with the ground settling both classification and explanation and the social record carrying no verdict weight. The specific global readings of Fig 11 :38 and Fig 12 :97 were retracted. Strains after re-census: one live (the Fig 3 middle link has no instrument), one gap declared (the psychological mechanism is world-side only). Mode `general` on the operation, `specified` on the domains, `candidate` throughout.
