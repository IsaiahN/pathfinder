## Residual in effect terms

Scientists visibly reorganize what counts as a kind and change what counts as an explanation when they carry a structure from a familiar domain onto an unfamiliar one. The residual is not *whether* metaphors and analogies matter. It is **what the operation is, what it is owed before it lands, and what settles whether the new classification or explanation is right**. Option A: treat the influence as decoration or persuasion. Option B: treat it as a frame import with a source gate and a shadow test, settled by the ground. A leads to crediting agreement and prestige, which the figures disqualify as a verdict. B leads to a checkable mechanism with a stated refuter.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks" (Fig 2 :29); "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you" (Fig 2 :35); and "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it" (Fig 10 :42). Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that makes their agreement worthless. Asking them to trust before they check spends the only thing they contribute.

## Verdict in one line

The figures decide that an analogy is the import of a frame whose closure differs, and it is licensed only after the residual is described in effect terms, with a source gate and a shadow test; it changes classification by selecting the bond that is recorded, and it changes explanation by mapping relations rather than attributes, but it returns a kind of thing and never the answer, and the ground, not agreement, settles it.
