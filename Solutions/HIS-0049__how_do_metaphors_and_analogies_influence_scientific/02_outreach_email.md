**Subject:** One checkable claim about how scientific metaphors work

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question in the history and philosophy of science: what a metaphor or analogy actually *does* when it changes a scientific classification or an explanation. I have one output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the operation, `specified` on the domain predictions):**
>
> 1. An analogy is not a new fact about the world. It is the import of a frame whose closure differs from the one the scientist is working in. It moves the wall of what can be composed, and it is licensed only by two gates: provenance (where did this structure come from?) and a shadow test (does it explain the gap it was brought in for?). Without those, the analogy is an undirected union that adds nothing.
> 2. Residual before frame. If the scientist picks the familiar source first and then reads the target off it, any frame will seem to fit. The effect on classification is real but manufactured by the choice.
> 3. For classification, the analogy changes which *arrangement* is recorded, not which ingredients are listed. Same atoms, different bond, different kind. That is why the 1820s isomer cases changed chemistry: the arrangement, not the comparison, forced structure into the notation.
> 4. For explanation, what transfers is the system of relations, not the surface attributes. The hydraulic analogy for circuits works where the relations line up and produces misconceptions where they do not. And an analogy that only makes a theory easier to retell changes no contact, so by this account it has not improved the explanation.
> 5. The social side has no anchor of its own. A memorable metaphor that a community repeats is agreement, not evidence. I am reporting that as a refusal, not as a claim about anyone's motives.
>
> **What this account explicitly does not claim:** that the metaphor is the mechanism; that the framework names the structure in the slot; that any particular historical case is settled by this derivation; or that agreement among experts counts as corroboration. This is a shape and a route, not a result, and nothing here is `grounded`.
>
> **What would refute it. I wrote these before sending this:**
> 1. A case where a metaphor adds a primitive to the world, or moves a frame's wall without contact with a different frame.
> 2. A case where a community re-sorts a kind with no change in recorded arrangement, only a change of label, and the ground pays for the new sort.
> 3. A case where an analogy's explanatory influence survives with zero shared relational structure, only attribute overlap, and the ground settles it.
> 4. A case where agreement about a metaphor, with no new measurement and no new source gate, changes the ground's answer to a classification question.
>
> If any one holds, the corresponding part of the account is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
