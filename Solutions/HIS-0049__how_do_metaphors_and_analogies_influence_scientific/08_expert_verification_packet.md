# Expert verification packet - the Tether on metaphor and analogy in science

**What this is:** a claim in a form a historian, philosopher or cognitive scientist of science can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion.

**Mode: `general` on the operation, `specified` on the domain predictions, not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Standing:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A metaphor or analogy is not a new fact about the world. It is the import of a frame whose closure differs, licensed by two gates: provenance (where did the structure come from?) and a shadow test (does it explain the gap it was brought in for?). The residual must be described before the frame is chosen; otherwise any frame will seem to fit. On classification the influence runs through the recorded bond rather than the ingredient list, so a new arrangement makes a new kind while a new label alone does not. On explanation what transfers is a system of relations, not surface attributes, and a comparison that changes no contact has improved pedagogy rather than explanation. The social effect of a memorable metaphor is real but carries no verdict weight, because agreement has no anchor. The framework returns a kind of thing, never the answer, and four refuters are written in advance.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 6, 8 and 9
- **Statement:** an analogy is an import with two gates, and the residual precedes the frame. Order is not optional.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :11-14: "IMPORT"; "source gate: provenance"; "shadow test: explains a gap"; "the only operator that moves the wall". Fig 8 :27-28: "1: describe R, then find the frame that already predicts its shape". Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit". Fig 6 :28: "An import adds no atom to the world."
- **Refuter:** show a case where a metaphor adds a primitive to the world, or moves a frame's wall without contact with a frame whose closure differs, or where describing the residual first made the correct frame less findable and the ground settled it.

### C2 - history of science facing; checkable by a historian or philosopher of science
- **Statement:** a genuine reclassification changes the recorded arrangement and the behaviour together; a relabelling changes neither. The isomer cases are the clean witness.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance." Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :97-98: "This is not an analogy. Chemistry hit it first: silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different properties, which is what forced structure into the notation." Fig 13 :121-123 names Berzelius 1830.
- **What to check:** is the historical account faithful? Wöhler 1827, silver cyanate versus silver fulminate with identical AgCNO composition and distinct properties, Berzelius coining "isomerism" in 1830 (Wikipedia "Isomer", history section, opened 2026-11-23). Is there a documented case where a community re-sorted a kind with no bond change and no behaviour change, only a new label, and the ground paid for it?
- **Refuter:** such a case breaks the bond test and puts Fig 12 in the wrong domain for classification.

### C3 - cognitive science facing; checkable by a researcher in analogy or conceptual change
- **Statement:** relations transfer, attributes do not, and an analogy's fertile part is its neutral part, the not-yet-known that suggests a hypothesis. A comparison that changes no contact has improved pedagogy, not explanation.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
- **What to check:** Gentner's structure-mapping theory (Cognitive Science 7(2), 1983, as reported by Wikipedia, opened 2026-11-23) claims that relations rather than attributes are mapped, with the systematicity principle. Hesse's positive, negative and neutral analogy, reported in both "Analogy and Analogical Reasoning" and "Models in Science" (SEP, opened 2026-11-23), holds that neutral analogies "give rise to questions and suggest new hypotheses." Does that fit the claim, or does it conflict? Note that the relations-not-attributes claim is **world-side only**; the figures license the import and the shape limit but do not themselves assert it.
- **Refuter:** an analogy whose explanatory influence survives with zero shared relational structure, only attribute overlap, and the ground settles it. Also: a documented case where a metaphor changed no contact yet the ground counted the explanation as new.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that a metaphor is the mechanism of scientific change. An import adds no atom to the world (Fig 6 :28).
- Not that Fig 11 :38, "That is not a metaphor for intelligence. It is a description of the practice," bans metaphor in science. It governs the habitat description in one domain; the global reading was retracted in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §6.
- Not that Fig 12 :97, "This is not an analogy," bans analogy. It governs the isomer case, which is a literal structural fact.
- Not that any particular metaphor is true, or that the framework names the structure an analogy imports.
- Not that agreement among experts, or the number of textbooks repeating a metaphor, is corroboration. Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."
- Not grounded. No measurement is asserted as settling this.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. Concretely: a case where the ground discriminates an arrangement change from a label change; a case where a relation transfer predicts and an attribute transfer does not; a source gate whose shadow test actually explains a gap. The four refuter tests in §2 are the operational forms. Until one fires, the verdict stays `candidate`.
