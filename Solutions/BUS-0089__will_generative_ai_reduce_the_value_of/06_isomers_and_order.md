# Union, bond and order: supporting derivation for BUS-0089

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question offers two outcomes joined by "or": (A) reduced value of specialized expertise, (B) more productive and influential expert workers. Three questions are owed before any answer is given: (1) are A and B a partition or a union, (2) what bond actually joins the composer to the worker, and (3) in what order do the links of the chain have to close.

## DOMAIN CENSUS (carried from `01`, not re-derived here)

Atoms held for this derivation: a composer `C`, a worker `W`, a ground `G`, an artifact `Art`, a settlement `S`. Bonds available: the seven typed bonds plus negation. No new atom is introduced. The missing thing is a discriminator instrument, not a new primitive. The four domains are D1 generator frame, D2 expert frame, D3 habitat, D4 channel; X1 (free-floating "value") and X2 (output count) were refused as domains in `01` §2.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Partition or union?

- Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
- A and B can both hold in one task slot: the artifact-production component of a worker's term loses standing while the worker's settlement term becomes more load-bearing and faster to reach. There is no contradiction, so no partition.
- The world record has the same shape. The jagged-frontier experiment reports gains inside the frontier and a 19% drop in correct solutions outside it (Dell'Acqua et al., HBS WP 24-013, opened 2026-11-22). The medical deskilling review reports both deskilling and a transformation of competencies (Natali et al., 2025).
- ⇒ **Union**, with two overlapping conditions and one compound residue. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Triangulated across Fig 9 and two low-overlap fields.

### Q2: Are the two outcomes isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- To be isomers, A and B would have to share an atom set and differ only in a typed bond. They do not. A and B are not two molecules built from the same atoms; they are two different **readings** of one change, taken from different frames (D3 payment versus D4 contact).
- The retracted reading from `01` §7 tried to call "human plus AI" and "AI alone" isomers. Re-census: substituting an actor in a habitat is not changing a bond inside one library. Fig 12 governs composition within `closure(Γ)`, not habitat replacement. The line strains, so it is **not** used.
- ⇒ **Not isomers.** The correct structural analogue is a union compound whose two conditions have opposite valence by residual (Fig 11 :50). A weaker analogue does survive: two task slots with the same atoms but different arrangements (one where the ground is reached by generation, one where it needs settlement) are *"same atoms, different bond, different substance"*. That is a checkable structural claim at the task level, not at the level of the whole question.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if both readings of the change can be written over the same atom set and differ only in one typed bond, the isomer claim would be restored and the union reading would need re-census. It was tested against the census and failed.

### Q3: What bond joins the composer to the worker?

- The composer's output precedes the worker's settlement. The bond is ordered, `C → Art → S`, where `→` is *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"* (Fig 12 :70) or, where the artifact did not exist before, `⇒` production: *"A produces B; B did not exist before"* (Fig 12 :76).
- It is **not** `+` conjunction. Operators table :15: *"swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No."* Swapping worker and composer changes the claim completely: a worker who settles a generated artifact is not a generator who settles a worker.
- It is also not `≡` identity. Operators table :27: *"substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No."* Substituting a composer for a worker loses the ground contact that D2 supplies, which is precisely the scarce term identified in F5.
- The relevant "influence" claim is a `⋛` comparison: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* (Fig 12 :86). It compares workers on an anchored axis; it is not a substance added to one.
- ⇒ The bond is ordered and asymmetric. Mode `general` for the bond typology, `specified` for the worker application. **Refuter:** show that swapping composer and worker leaves the claim unchanged against a ground.

### Q4: What is the order of the chain?

- Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*
- Ordered links for this residual: **perception** of the artifact, **vocabulary** to say what it affords, **objective** saying what counts as progress, **planning** the use or refusal, **learn and carry** (Fig 3 :4-15).
- Fig 3 :25: *"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."*
- The predicted break is therefore at links 2 to 3, not at generation. More generation is effort above the break. Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*
- Cross-scale order: Fig 4 :11-12 sends down *"a head start, never a recording"*. The observed novice gain (Brynjolfsson et al., 34%) is a head start delivered to a learner earlier in the chain; it is not evidence that the expert's wall moved.
- ⇒ Order is dependency order, and the scarce instrument sits in the middle. Mode `specified`. **Refuter:** an instrument at link 2 that discriminates correct from plausible output with residual trending to zero, plus evidence that gains are then largest at link 4 rather than at link 2.

### Q5: Does the substrate need a seventh term?

No. Fig 13 :115: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."* Fig 13 :117: *"So a frame can search without end and still not arrive: infinite effort inside a finite reach."* A generative model is exactly an engine for searching arrangements inside a finite reach. It needs no new substrate term, and claiming one would be a mint without support (Formula step 3). Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* The missing discriminator is an **instrument**, which extends what can be represented, not a new member of the six (Fig 6 :32).

## CONCLUSION

The two offered outcomes are neither a partition nor isomers. They are a **union compound** with two conditions of opposite valence, joined to the worker by an ordered, asymmetric bond, and gated by a missing discriminator in the middle of the dependency chain. The composer adds search inside the same closure; the head start is real, the wall move is not. The substrate is closed and needs no seventh term. The occupation-level sign is a `gap` closed only by an instrument, not by another analogy.

What survives from the question is the useful part: something real changes in both directions at once, and the change is per task slot, never a workforce average. What does not survive is the "or".
