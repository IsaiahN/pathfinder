# Outreach: check the shape claim before you decide about the frame

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the refuter below is stated in your own words and you are willing to retract on it. The two-stage offer collapses on contact if stage 1 cannot survive a one-line failure.

---

**Subject:** One claim about expertise and AI to check before you read any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I'd like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking
> you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not
> survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (what failed to resolve).** For a task slot, an expert predicts what the work
> needs, acts, and compares the prediction with what the ground returns (issues resolved, code
> that runs, a client who pays). A generative model is a second frame that can produce artifacts
> cheaply from the same corpus. I want to know which term becomes load-bearing after it arrives,
> not whether the workforce as a whole is "helped" or "hurt". Averaging across slots throws away
> the only signal that says where to look (Fig 1 :19).
>
> **The claim, in `specified` mode, not `grounded`:** the question's "or" is a false partition.
> The effect is a union of two conditions. First, the composer composes inside the same closure,
> so it makes search cheaper without adding an atom (Fig 6 :49, Fig 4 :12). That is the real core
> of "more productive". Second, because recordings become cheap, the scarce term shifts toward
> settling the artifact against a ground (Fig 4 :25, Fig 10 :42). That is the real core of
> "value of expertise". For any particular worker the sign depends on the residual of their task,
> because an actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries (Fig 11 :49).
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that AI makes experts obsolete, that it only helps them, that a
> benchmark gain is a market gain, or which occupations win. The frame returns a kind of thing,
> never a name (Fig 9 :32). Which tasks gain is an open instrument, not a filled slot.
>
> **What would refute it. I wrote these before writing the claim:**
> 1. One lever clears artifact production and settlement standing together, with no
>    categorically different residue measured on a ground.
> 2. A generative system adds a settled atom to a worker's closure, so the wall moves rather
>    than only the search cost.
> 3. The sign of the effect is invariant across task slots whose residuals demonstrably differ.
> 4. Artifact volume, controlling for verification, predicts ground outcomes better than
>    verification does.
>
> If any holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..."
> is more useful to me than agreement. If you'd rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not
> my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - The canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Jagged frontier, HBS Working Paper 24-013: https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=64700
> - Generative AI at Work, NBER WP 31161: https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the loop is in the formula. Run it on a problem you know
> well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run
> it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported
> success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Residual and refuter before the claim | Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* The recipient checks the output without holding the frame's interior. |
| No request for agreement or trust | Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* A list selected for receptiveness is a list selected to agree. |
| Refuter written before the conclusion | The seat rule: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device. |
| Mode stated on the claim | `THE_FORMULA.md`: *"Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which. Accepted means the ground settled it; otherwise candidate."* |
| "Union" instead of "or" | Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* |
| Cheap search, not new atoms | Fig 6 :49: *"more search at the same reach: is it in here and unfound? The closure does not change. Same atoms, same wall, more of the space actually visited."* |

| Output counts withheld as evidence | Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* |

## Hard rules for sending

- One recipient at a time, with a real subject line and a real opt-out line.
- Record their verdict on stage 1 *before* any theory is shown. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
- If they return a refutation, copy it into `11_audit.md` with provenance and have the auditor seat re-evaluate it as if it came from any other source.
- Do not use prestige, title, or follower count as a reason to include or weight a reply. Prestige can fund a trial and cannot settle one.
