# Expert verification packet: the Tether on generative AI and expertise

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher of AI and knowledge work can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** No wage or employment measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question's "or" is a false partition. A generative model trained on a corpus is a cheap composer inside the same closure as its users, so it makes search less expensive without adding an atom; that is the defensible core of "more productive". Because artifacts become cheap recordings, the load-bearing term moves toward settling them against a ground; that is the defensible core of "value of expertise". For any particular worker the sign depends on the residual of their task slot, not on the tool, and the missing instrument is a discriminator that says what counts as progress. The frame returns that shape, four refuters written in advance, and the kind of ground that would close it. It does not name an occupation.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 9, 6 and 4

- **Statement:** the two offered outcomes form a union compound, and the composer changes search cost, not the closure.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Fig 6 :49: *"more search at the same reach: is it in here and unfound? The closure does not change. Same atoms, same wall, more of the space actually visited."* Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*
- **Refuter:** one lever clears artifact production and settlement standing with no categorically different residue measured on a ground; or a generative system adds a settled atom with provenance and a shadow test, so the wall moves rather than only the search cost.

### C2: world-facing, checkable against the AI-and-work literature

- **Statement:** the observed effect is uneven by task and by worker in the way the shape predicts: large gains where search was cheap to extend and the ground was already reachable, near zero or negative where the artifact must be discriminated against a ground.
- **What to check:** the Brynjolfsson, Li and Raymond result (NBER WP 31161, opened 2026-11-22) reports 14% average, 34% for novices, minimal for experienced workers. The Dell'Acqua et al. jagged-frontier experiment (HBS WP 24-013, opened 2026-11-22) reports gains inside the frontier and 19% less likely to be correct outside it. Lee et al. (CHI 2025, opened 2026-11-22) report that *"GenAI shifts the nature of critical thinking toward information verification, response integration, and task stewardship."* Are those three findings faithful, and do they share an evidence pool or three distinct ones? They come from customer support, consulting and knowledge-worker surveys respectively, so overlap is partial, not zero.
- **Supporting figure line:** Fig 11 :49: *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
- **Refuter:** a ground-outcome study showing invariant sign across task slots with demonstrably different residuals; or artifact volume, controlling for verification, predicting outcomes better than verification.

### C3: framework-internal derivation about the bottleneck

- **Statement:** the scarce link after cheap generation is vocabulary-to-objective (what the artifact affords, what counts as progress), not generation itself.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 3 :25: *"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."* Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*
- **Refuter:** an instrument at that link that discriminates correct from plausible output with residual trending to zero, plus evidence that the next gains appear downstream rather than at discrimination.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that AI makes experts obsolete, and not that it only helps them. Both would be verdicts without a ground.
- Not that a benchmark, a token count, or a task-laboratory rating is a market outcome. Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*
- Not that the wall moved. Fig 6 :25: *"An import adds no atom to the world."*
- Not which occupations, sectors or countries gain. That is a named occupation where the frame returns a shape (Fig 9 :32).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a wage series or a task census.
- Not a claim that expertise is tacit to the point of being untransferable. A plain description of effects travels; the history does not, and contact rebuilds it.

## 5. What would settle it

A task-level census with a real anchor. Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."*

1. **Source discrimination:** does the measured speed-up come from cheaper search at the same wall, a genuine import that moved the wall, or a change in who pays? The reading cannot comment on its source; an instrument is required.
2. **Anchor test:** does the gain survive contact with payment or outcome, not just a benchmark or a rating? Fig 10 :26: *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*.
3. **Valence and standing tests:** does the sign flip with the residual, and after controlling for artifact volume does verification predict ground outcomes better than volume?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :55, :57; Fig 6 :21, :49; Fig 11 :49; Fig 3 :19, :25, :27. Eight short passages.
2. Check C2 against your own reading of the three studies: are the reported numbers faithful, and is the overlap between their evidence pools stated honestly?
3. Name one instrument that would discriminate cheaper search from a moved wall at the task level, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement, and agreement after seeing the frame is not corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
