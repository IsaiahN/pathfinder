## 0. The residual in effect terms

Let `Γ` be the frame that generates the forecast: the model family, its parameterisations, its training or assimilation data, its discretization, and the rule that turns a belief `b` and an action (a member run) `a` into a prediction `Γ(b,a)`. Let `o′` be what the ground actually returned: the verifying observation. The residual is

```
R_s = |Γ(b,a) − o′|   per slot s
```

where `s` is a location, a lead time, a variable, an event threshold, or a basin. It is indexed per slot, never averaged into one number for the whole ensemble.

The question asks about the **status of spread**, not the size of spread. Spread is the dispersion of `Γ(b,a)` across `a`. Two readings compete:

- **Reading A (genuine):** the members are low-overlap frames, each ranging separately against `o′`; their disagreement registers the shape of `R_s` and would shrink if the ground were better known.
- **Reading B (repetition):** the members are perturbations inside one closure; they share atoms, data, and assumptions; their disagreement is generated by `Γ` itself and cannot certify `Γ`.

What each leads to: A licenses a probability statement tied to verification and to a described residual; B licenses only a display of the generator's own sensitivity, which can look like uncertainty while carrying no new reach.

Binding the sought-for shape as an ordinary term (Formula, "Direction"): *an ensemble captures genuine uncertainty when the variation among members is attributable to variation in the ground-contacting assumptions, each member can be ranged against a ground that does not update on the ensemble's own output, and the member list is not a single closure respelled.* Written down and bound this way, the shape accrues a residual and is refusable like any other term. Without this binding, the search would pick a favourite member-count or spread metric first and any result would seem to fit (Fig 9 :27-28).

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** the residual is a union of at least four overlapping conditions, not one binary property. The conditions are (i) member independence, (ii) structural reach, (iii) ground contact and calibration, (iv) the use to which the spread is put. One lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :54-56).
- **Symmetry:** spread is a symmetric measure. Swapping members leaves it unchanged, so it cannot tell you which member carried the information or which direction the error ran. The relation that matters, correlation, is a typed bond between members, and it is not recoverable from the member list (Fig 12 :5).
- **Scale:** inside one model, ensemble members are a search at one level (parameter and initial-condition space). Across models, they are a composition between frames, and the reach is different (Fig 6 :21, :23). The two scales must not be averaged.

### One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

**D1, the ensemble-generation frame.** Actors: the members, each a run of a model with a given initial condition, parameter set, or physics scheme. Currency: prediction disagreement, that is spread. Anchor: the generating model family and its data, which do not change when a member changes. This is a domain because the anchor is nameable: a specific closure with a specific atom set. Question type: partition at first order (what composes inside the closure) and union at second order (which closures are present at all).

**D2, the structural frame.** Actors: model structures, parameterisations, discretization choices, physical assumptions, and the imports between them. Currency: reach, that is what can be represented at all. Anchor: the closure boundary, which only an import from a differing frame moves. Question type: imported atom versus rearranged atom. A perturbation of a parameter rearranges; a new process added from another model class imports.

**D3, the verification frame.** Actors: observations, hindcasts, reforecasts, verification scores. Currency: settlement, that is whether the ground pays for the term. Anchor: the observation, which must not update on what the ensemble thinks. Question type: triangulation. Each member is ranged against the same non-updating anchor separately; that is what makes their agreement or disagreement mean anything (Fig 2 :45-46).

**D4, the decision/use frame.** Actors: forecasters, users, decision makers, and the conventions they adopt. Currency: cost of being wrong and the value of independence. Anchor: the ground for the decision (an outcome, a loss, a threshold). Question type: whether a convention has quietly become a verdict (Fig 10 :47). Spread used as a decision input has to be checked here as well as in D3, or sharpness becomes an end in itself.

No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet. D4 has one only where a real outcome and cost exist; where they do not, the use frame is recorded as `gap`, not smuggled in as a domain.

### Partition or union

D1 and D2 form a **union of overlapping conditions**: a multi-model ensemble can share ancestry and data while differing in structure; a perturbed-parameter ensemble can share structure while differing in reach within a bounded range. The overlap has its own loop and must not be folded into either side (Fig 9 :54-58). D3 is a **partition boundary** between a frame and its ground: the ground is not one more member. D4 is a **union** of cost and convention, with the convention forbidden to decide the outcome.

### Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. Describe the residual per slot before choosing any frame (Fig 9 :27-28).
2. Refuse the claim that spread certifies itself before minting a new metric (Formula step 2: a refused term pays the same bargain).
3. Settle member generation (D1) and structural reach (D2) before the comparison (D3), because nothing composes over a term that was never admitted as a part (Fig 12 :104).
4. Range each member against the ground before using spread in a decision (D4). Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not (Fig 3 :19).

### Strain test applied during the census

Fig 13's six-term set is a physical substrate. Applying it literally to statistical model ensembles strains the reading. Re-census: the governing claim is not *which six physical states exist* but *a closure is finite, the arrangements over it are not, and only import moves the wall* (Fig 13 :115-118; Fig 6 :23). That claim is domain-agnostic by the figures' own rule (Fig 4 :20: *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."*). The strain is dissolved by assigning Fig 13 to closure structure and not to meteorological variables. It is recorded, not hidden, in §6.

## 3. Findings

### F1 — Spread cannot certify itself (decisive against Reading B)

Fig 1 :23: **"The ground enters as the only metric."** Fig 1 :24-27:

> **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."**

Spread is produced by `Γ`. Using spread as the sole evidence that `Γ` has captured genuine uncertainty is exactly the frame-internal scoring the figure disqualifies. The field's own guidance agrees: Wikipedia's *Ensemble forecasting* notes that *"It is common for the ensemble spread to be too small, such that the observed atmospheric state falls outside of the ensemble forecast. This can lead the forecaster to be overconfident in their forecast."* Small spread can accompany a wrong forecast. The question "is the spread genuine?" is therefore not decidable from the spread.

Fig 1 :19 and :22 add the indexing rule:

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

A global dispersion number can be small while a per-slot residual is live. This is the forecast analogue of the exemplar's per-object warning.

**Refuter:** exhibit a case where spread is computed without any contribution from the generating frame, or a verification showing that spread alone predicts settlement at the ground with no access to observations. If spread were an instrument rather than a product of the frame, F1 falls.

### F2 — Independence, not member count, is the variable

Fig 2 :31-32: **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."** Fig 2 :40:

> **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**

Fig 2 :44 makes the arithmetic explicit:

> **"The effective-sample-size correction for correlated observations is standard in survey statistics and meta-analysis; the collapse is the same arithmetic read as a warning."**

So a hundred members that share a parameterisation are not a hundred witnesses. Fig 8 :48-49:

> **"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."**
> **"Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."**

The climate field states the same thing in its own words. SimClim's AR6-based guidance says *"The spread of a multiple model ensemble is therefore rarely a direct measure of uncertainty, particularly given that models are unlikely to be independent, but the spread can help to characterise uncertainty."* And: *"Ensemble members may not represent estimates of the climate system behaviour (trajectory) entirely independent of one another. This is likely true of members that simply represent different versions of the same model or use the same initial conditions. But even different models may share components and choices of parameterixations of processes and may have been calibrated using the same data sets."*

**Refuter:** show that member count predicts verification skill after conditioning on a measured dependence or shared-ancestry metric. If adding near-duplicate members improves ground settlement at the same rate as adding independent frames, independence is not the variable and F2 falls.

### F3 — Parameter perturbation is search at the same reach; it repeats the structural assumptions

Fig 6 :49:

> **"more search at the same reach: is it in here and unfound? The closure does not change. Same atoms, same wall, more of the space actually visited."**

Fig 6 :21: **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."** Fig 8 :32: **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."** Fig 13 :116-118:

> **"Six members, and no amount of arranging produces a seventh: a new member would have to be a new kind, and composition only ever rearranges the kinds already held."**
> **"a frame can search without end and still not arrive: infinite effort inside a finite reach."**

A perturbed-parameter ensemble varies values inside an held parameterisation. It visits more of the same space; it does not add a process. That is the figure's definition of *simply repeating the same structural assumptions*. Fig 5 :29-30 names the output state: **"the machinery worked; the answer was already known."**

**Refuter:** exhibit a parameter perturbation that changes the set of representable outcomes in a way the unperturbed model cannot reproduce by reweighting, and show at the ground that this new outcome was paid for. If perturbation adds an atom, F3 is wrong for that case.

### F4 — Genuine structural uncertainty requires an import, with a source gate and a shadow test

Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."** Fig 8 :45: **"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."** Fig 6 :12-13 names the gates as **"source gate: provenance"** and **"shadow test: explains a gap."**

So structural uncertainty is captured when a member brings an atom the others lack from a frame whose closure differs, with a recorded source and a demonstrated explanation of a gap the first frame could not close. Without provenance, convergent derivation and adopted import are indistinguishable in the contents (Fig 8 :50-52). A multi-model ensemble whose members descend from a common ancestor may have many names and one closure.

**Refuter:** show a structural difference between members that is neither imported from a differing closure nor a rearrangement of held atoms, and that changes ground-visible predictions. If a third operator moves the wall, Fig 6 is incomplete and F4 falls.

### F5 — The question is a union, so it must be split rather than averaged

Fig 9 :4-5:

> **"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."**

Fig 9 :56-57: **"A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."** The four conditions in the census overlap. A multi-model ensemble can fail on independence (shared ancestry), on reach (same physics despite different names), on verification (poor calibration), or on use (a convention that fixes a decision). One lever does not clear the intersections. Fig 9 :60:

> **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

A spread threshold, a member count, and a sharpness score are budget filters. They are not witnesses for genuineness.

**Refuter:** show that the four conditions collapse to one measurable quantity with a single anchor and that a single lever clears all intersections. F5 then fails and an averaged verdict becomes legitimate.

### F6 — Many similar members are one long room

Fig 7 :12-13: **"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not."** Fig 7 :32: **"one long room: motion, no progress."** Fig 7 :35-36:

> **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**
> **"The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."**

An ensemble built by adding near-identical members is a mirror chain. It moves (new runs, new plots, new members) without travelling. A genuine ensemble is a selection between differing rooms, where the next member is chosen against the residual the last one surfaced, not against the member list.

**Refuter:** show a sequence of members that does not differ from its predecessor yet reduces the per-slot residual at the ground. If motion without difference is progress, Fig 7's selection rule is wrong and F6 falls.

### F7 — Self-consistency is not correctness; the anchor must not update

Fig 2 :29-30: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."** Fig 2 :33-35:

> **"The anchor must not update."**
> **"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."**
> **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

In forecasting, the anchor is the verifying observation, not the ensemble consensus and not a climatology that has been refitted to the ensemble's own output. Calibration performed on the same data that generated the members, with no held-out truth, is mutual update: the frames converge and the convergence is coherent. The field's own reliability criterion is a ground criterion: Wikipedia's *Ensemble forecasting* states that *"If the forecast is reliable, the observed state will behave as if it is drawn from the forecast probability distribution."* Reliability is a statement about observations, not about internal agreement.

Fig 10 :24 adds: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**, and :26 names **"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"**. A calibration that silently stops reaching observations does not announce itself.

**Refuter:** exhibit a case where the verification target was changed by the ensemble's own reported probabilities and the resulting agreement still settled the question. If a frame can serve as its own anchor without drift, F7 falls.

### F8 — The bond carries the information the member list does not

Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."** Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."** Fig 12 :117:

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Two ensembles with the identical member list can be different substances: in one the errors are independent, in the other they share a cause. The member list is the ingredient set; the correlation structure is the bond. It is not recoverable from the list. Reliability engineering has a named operator for this. The ECSS common-cause-failure handbook defines the beta factor as **"the ratio of the failure rate due to common cause to the total failure rate"**, and states that *"The use of different design principles, diverse hardware, diverse software or distinctly different design groups during the development reduces the susceptibility of a system to CCF resulting from a lack of diversity."* The Reliafy guide puts the consequence plainly: *"Any shared cause puts a floor under the system's failure probability that redundancy can't lower"*, and *"the closer β gets to 1, the more the redundant units behave like one."* That is Fig 8 :49 in engineering notation.

`06_isomers_and_order.md` develops the isomer reading and its falsifier.

**Refuter:** show that for a fixed member list and a fixed correlation structure the ground-visible behaviour is always identical, so the bond adds nothing. If correlation is a property of the ingredients rather than an unrecorded relation, F8 falls.

### F9 — Verdicts are per slot, never averaged into a global spread

Fig 1 :19, :22 (quoted in F1). Symbols table :43-44: *"R is measured per slot, never as a single global number."* A forecast can be well dispersed at one lead and underdispersed at another, reliable for temperature and unreliable for precipitation. Averaging these into one spread-skill coefficient throws away the only signal that says where to look (Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**).

**Refuter:** exhibit a decision that is genuinely global across all slots and a ground that settles it globally. F9 then narrows to that domain rather than falling entirely.

### F10 — A convention that fixes an outcome has stopped being a convention

Fig 10 :47: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."** Fig 10 :48: **"a convention nothing can check is a constant the seat authored, carrying the seat's authority."** In D4, a decision threshold or a standard ensemble size is a convention only while it can be refused when the ground turns against it. A forecast product that reports spread as if it were a verified probability, with no route to refusal, has promoted a convention into a verdict. Fig 10 :72: **"A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom."**

**Refuter:** show a fixed threshold that is checkable, refusable, and still settles the outcome at the ground across a change of question. F10 then narrows to cases where the threshold is unrefusable.

### F11 — Sharpness and reliability are in different currencies

Fig 12 :125-126:

> **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**
> **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**

The forecast literature's maxim, quoted in Wikipedia's *Ensemble forecasting*, is *"The key aim of a forecaster should be to maximise sharpness, while maintaining reliability."* That is a budget rule, not a verdict rule. Narrowing the ensemble improves the description; checking it against observations costs actions. Summing them into one score is the error Fig 12 names. Fig 9 :60 forbids letting the filter hand you the verdict.

**Refuter:** produce a single score in one currency that is proved to price both the description and the settlement actions and whose optimum coincides with ground settlement. F11 then falls for that score.

### F12 — The gap: no figure returns a threshold or names the missing atom

After the census, no figure line governs either of these:

- **What numerical spread, correlation, or member count is enough?** Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."** Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."** The canonical README says the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*. A universal number is outside what the figures return.
- **Which specific structural assumption is missing in this forecast problem?** Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** Naming the missing primitive would mean already holding it (Fig 13 :134-135). What can be reported is a shape: an atom that changes the representable outcome set and is owed from a frame with a different closure.

This is a `gap` of the **shape-not-a-name** kind for the threshold, and of the **unreachable** kind for the specific missing atom in an instance. It is a verdict, not silence. It is not an invitation to invent a number.

**Refuter:** derive a universal threshold from the figures, or exhibit a figure line that names a specific missing meteorological primitive. Until one is produced, F12 stands.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Self-certification survives.** A demonstration that ensemble spread predicts ground settlement without any access to observations, so that a frame can score itself with the quantity it produces (breaks F1, Fig 1 :27).
2. **Independence is inert.** Member count predicts verification skill after conditioning on measured dependence or shared ancestry (breaks F2, Fig 2 :40).
3. **Perturbation adds an atom.** A parameter perturbation changes the representable outcome set rather than reweighting it, and the ground pays for the change (breaks F3, Fig 6 :49).
4. **The wall moves without import.** A structural difference arises inside one closure and changes ground-visible predictions, with no source gate or shadow test owed (breaks F4, Fig 6 :23).
5. **Averaging is legitimate.** The four conditions collapse to one anchor and one lever clears all intersections (breaks F5, Fig 9 :54-57).
6. **The bond is redundant.** For a fixed member list, correlation structure makes no difference to ground-visible behaviour (breaks F8, Fig 12 :5).
7. **A frame can anchor itself.** Verification changes under the ensemble's own reported probabilities and the agreement still settles the question (breaks F7, Fig 2 :33-35).

If any fires, the separator is withdrawn in the form written and the census is reopened. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`. This refuter was written before the synthesis in `07` and the verdict in `09`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. **Lead with the separator, not a metric.** The checkable claim is about member independence, structural reach, ground contact, and refusal, not about a spread value (F1, F2, F12).
2. **Report per slot.** Diversity, spread, and calibration are indexed by location, lead, variable, and threshold (F9).
3. **Name the bond.** Record the dependence structure and ancestry of members alongside the member list; otherwise the artifact cannot tell you which ensemble you built (F8, Fig 13 :128-129).
4. **Split the union before searching.** Diagnose independence, reach, verification, and use separately, and keep the intersections as specific compounds (F5).
5. **Gate structural differences.** Require a source and a shadow test for any member claimed to bring a new assumption (F4).
6. **Keep the gap visible.** Report `gap` where no threshold is derivable, and state what a discriminating instrument would have to do (F12). Do not fill the slot with a number.
7. **Send the generator.** Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** Send the separator and its refuter, not a conclusion.

### Round trip

The condensation used in `07` and `09` is lossy. What survives: per-slot residual, independence as the variable, import as the only wall-moving operator, ground settlement. What drops: the dependence structure of members (Fig 12 :5), the requirement that the next member match the surfaced residual (Fig 7 :36), and the prohibition on a frame certifying itself (Fig 1 :27). `11_audit.md` records this as a nonzero `R_T`. Treating a formula summary as identical to the figures would set `R_T` to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
