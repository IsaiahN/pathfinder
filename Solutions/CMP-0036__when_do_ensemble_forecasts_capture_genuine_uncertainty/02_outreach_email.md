**Subject:** A four-condition check for when ensemble spread is genuine uncertainty

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one checkable claim about ensemble forecasts and would like your verdict on it before I show you any framework material. I am not asking you to agree with anything, and I am not asking you to trust a source. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`).** An ensemble captures genuine uncertainty only when four conditions hold together:
>
> 1. The members differ in what they draw on. Independence is how little two frames draw on the same evidence. Members that share a parameterisation, a training set, or a model ancestry are one frame wearing several names, no matter how many runs there are.
> 2. The variation changes reach, not just position inside one closure. Perturbing a parameter visits more of the same space. Bringing in a process another model does not have is a different operation, and it needs a recorded source and a demonstrated explanation of a gap the first model could not close.
> 3. Each member is ranged separately against a verifying observation that does not update on what the ensemble reported. A spread that is narrow because the members agree with each other is not evidence of reliability. Reliability is a statement about observations.
> 4. The spread is used under a rule that can be refused when the ground turns against it. A fixed threshold or a fixed ensemble size is a convention until it decides the outcome.
>
> These are conditions, not a score. The claim is that the question is a union of overlapping conditions with separate anchors, so a single spread number or member count cannot settle it.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming that a particular dispersion value, member count, or correlation coefficient is the right one. I am not claiming that a named physics scheme *is* the missing atom. I am not claiming that a large ensemble is useless, or that multi-model ensembles are always better. The framework I am working with returns a shape, not a name, and the specific number or missing assumption would have to be paid for at the ground.
>
> **What would refute the check, written before this email:**
> 1. spread predicts verification skill without any access to observations;
> 2. member count predicts skill after conditioning on measured dependence or shared ancestry;
> 3. a parameter perturbation changes the set of representable outcomes rather than reweighting it, and the ground pays for it;
> 4. a structural difference changes predictions with no provenance or gap explanation owed;
> 5. for a fixed member list, correlation structure makes no difference to ground-visible behaviour;
> 6. the verification target changes under the ensemble's own probabilities and agreement still settles the question.
>
> If any of these holds I will withdraw the check in this form. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Ensemble forecasting, the field's baseline account of spread, reliability and underdispersion: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ensemble_forecasting
> - The use of ensembles, AR6-based practitioner guidance on ensembles of opportunity and non-independence: https://help.climsystems.com/docs/simclim-ar6/TheuseofEnsembles.html
> - Design effect and effective sample size, the arithmetic of correlated observations: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Design_effect
> - Common-cause failure and the beta-factor model, the engineering treatment of shared causes in redundant systems: https://handbook.reliability.space/en/latest/system/handbook/reliability_prediction/modelling_common_cause_failure.html
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the formula is in the figures link I can send on request. Run it on a forecast problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state whether your outcome is `general`, `specified`, or `grounded`. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Contact discipline

- One recipient at a time. No distribution lists, no forwarding chains, no bulk send.
- No ranking by likely agreement. Fig 2 :40 disqualifies a verdict drawn from a shared evidence pool; selecting for receptiveness manufactures exactly that pool.
- Do not pool later agreement as corroboration. A reply on stage 1 is a verdict; a later endorsement of the framework is a conversation.
- Use the institutional or opt-in routes in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. ECMWF's own contact page notes that it *"cannot answer general meteorological questions"*, so route through the support or publication channels instead of the switchboard.
