## The question restated in effect terms

An ensemble produces many predictions. Two outcomes are at issue. In one, the members range around a non-updating ground (an observation, a verification, a measurement), and their disagreement carries information about what the world might do. In the other, the members are perturbations of one model family, trained on one data set and sharing one parameterisation, so their disagreement is a re-spelling of one closure and carries no information the single model did not already hold. What is being decided is what licenses the word *genuine*: what has to be true of the member set and its relation to the ground for spread to count as uncertainty rather than as repetition. An answer would look like a separator, a set of conditions that can be checked before the forecast is issued, each with its own refuter.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **Agreement is not a verdict.** Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* A room selected for receptiveness is a mirror with a different name.
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* The central finding of this solution is that correlation among ensemble members is the failure being named; it would be self-refuting to manufacture correlation among the people checking it.
3. **Asking for trust spends the checker's independence.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* The first contact carries the residual and its refuter only.

Legally: unsolicited bulk email breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one.
