## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about testing whether a cognitive pattern is caused by culture, ecology, institutions, or their interaction. Option A: treat the four options as four alternative causes and compare their effect sizes in one design. Option B: treat them as a union of overlapping habitat conditions and constrain the shape of any valid test. What each leads to: A clears the regions where one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, and it treats a fallible instrument as an anchor; B unbundles first, anchors the test in an intervention, buys independence, keeps the residual per person and withholds the cause name until the ground pays.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between observing that P varies and knowing which contact produces it. Arity: four named candidates plus a compound, so a **union**, not a partition. Symmetry: attribution is ordered (cause produces pattern), so an ordered bond governs, not a symmetric conjunction. Scale: individual response to population pattern, so a lossy cross-scale transform is in play. Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*.
- **Domain 1, measurement.** Actors: participants, tasks, operationalisations. Currency: responses, invariance checks. Anchor: performance as it pays, a measurement that does not update on the theorist's frame (Fig 2 :29). The specific test is an instrument, not the anchor (Fig 2 :37-38).
- **Domain 2, attribution.** Actors: culture, ecology, institutions as habitat conditions and interventions. Currency: causal credit and per-slot residual. Anchor: an experiment or natural experiment that moves one candidate while holding another (Fig 2 :29).
- **Domain 3, verification.** Actors: research teams and reviewers. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement (Fig 2 :40). In this simulated run all seats draw on one evidence pool, so this domain's independence is effectively zero.
- **Domain 4, compound and habitat.** Actors: intersections, cascades, substituted habitats. Currency: interaction terms and second-order effects. Anchor: an intervention that separates the intersections, or none if the compound cannot be resolved at this budget (Fig 9 :54).
- **Order by dependency.** Perceive the pattern, build effect-term vocabulary, state the objective, then plan, then carry the method (Fig 3). The vocabulary/objective link is the least instrumented and cannot be rescued by effort at the planning link.

Strain test applied: Fig 2 :36-38 (what is legitimately the ground) and Fig 10 :24 (decay of the channel to it) govern different objects, so there is no strain after re-census. Fig 13's six-term substrate does not bear on a methods question and was not forced.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the cause names are withheld. The four options are a union compound, so unbundle single-condition regions from intersections before testing. Anchor the test in an intervention that does not move with the frame, with performance as the ground and each task as a fallible instrument. Buy independence because the candidates share an evidence pool. Keep the residual per person and enumerate the habitat, treating person plus habitat as the unit. Measure the cross-scale round trip gap. A cause name is licensed only by an intervention that pays or by a provenanced import with a shadow test. This is a `specified`, simulated, ungrounded result, not a measurement and not a protocol.

- **cost if wrong:** an incorrect single-cause attribution changes the intervention that is applied to a population, which is a real cost borne by people, not just a classification error. The framework prices the shortfall at Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The second cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a conclusion instead of a generator and a refuter. Both are avoidable by leading with the shape claim and the six refuters.
- **undoable:** the design constraints are revisable without withdrawing any primitive, and a superseded packet can replace an earlier one. A wrong intervention already applied in a habitat is not fully undoable: contact cascades and introduced actors are not subtractable (Fig 11 :61-62: introduction is available, exclusion is not). This is why the cost is bounded only if the refuters fire before deployment.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A single lever that clears the intersections would break the union constraint; a frame-invariant instrument would break the instrument-versus-ground constraint; demonstrated independence of the three candidates would break the independence constraint; a mean carrying all per-person structure would break the per-slot constraint; an attribution that works with the habitat removed would break the habitat constraint; a measured round trip gap of zero would break the membrane constraint. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
