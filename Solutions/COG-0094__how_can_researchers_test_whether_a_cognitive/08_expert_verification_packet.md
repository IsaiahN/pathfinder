# Expert verification packet — the Tether on culture, ecology, institutions and interaction

**What this is:** a claim in a form a cross-cultural methods researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the design constraints, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** No intervention or dataset is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status of this packet:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The four candidates in the question are a union of overlapping conditions, not four alternative causes. A valid test must therefore split single-condition regions from intersections before collecting data; it must range against an intervention that does not change its answer when a theory pushes on it, with people's performance as the ground and each test as a fallible instrument; it must buy independence because culture, ecology, and institutions historically co-vary; it must keep the residual per person rather than in a population mean; and it must enumerate the habitat and treat person plus habitat as the unit of analysis. The framework does not name the cause and does not supply a protocol. It offers the shape a test must have, six refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 9 and 12

- **Statement:** the attribution is a union compound; "interaction" is not a fourth cause, and a single lever leaves the intersections standing.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54 — *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :55-56 — *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."* Fig 12 :117 — *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- **Refuter:** show a single intervention that clears the full attribution, including every culture-by-ecology-by-institution intersection, with the same instrument and no separate lever.
- **What to check:** is the union reading faithful to how these three conditions actually overlap in the field? Does the factorial implementation detect the interactions that a one-factor design misses?

### C2 — methods-facing, checkable by a researcher against measurement practice

- **Statement:** performance is the legitimate ground here, but any particular test is an instrument that can fail on axes it does not measure; invariance and pooling are channel checks, not cultural findings.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 — *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :37-38 — *"It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."* Fig 10 :24 — *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*, followed at Fig 10 :26 by *"saturated metrics, pooled readings."*
- **Refuter:** show one instrument that returns the same attribution under every framing, translation, and population, with no invariance failure and no independent check needed.
- **What to check:** does the field already enforce this distinction, and under what name (equivalence, invariance, differential item functioning)? Does a passed invariance test say anything about which habitat condition produced the pattern, or only that the channel is open?

### C3 — framework-internal derivation with a direct empirical consequence

- **Statement:** independence is the parameter, the residual is per person, and the habitat is enumerated; a laboratory task is a substituted habitat that proves wiring and not capability in the original habitat.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :31 — *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Fig 1 :22 — *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 11 :43 — *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 11 :70 — *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."*
- **Refuter:** show a valid attribution that excludes all habitat conditions from the unit of analysis and still selects the intervention that works; or show that a population mean carries all per-person structure with zero live residual on every slot.
- **What to check:** are the candidate causes genuinely independent in the settings proposed, or do they draw on one evidence pool? Where would a third-order habitat effect be missed by direct-contact scoping?

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that culture, ecology, or institutions caused any particular pattern. The slots stay open.
- Not a specific experimental protocol. Factorial designs, natural experiments, ethnography and population data are world implementations, corroboration only.
- Not that measurement invariance establishes a culture. It is a channel check at most.
- Not that a population mean is a person. It is a lossy derivative.
- Not `grounded`, and not validated by agreement among researchers.
- Not a claim that the framework can certify its own completeness: Fig 8 :39 — *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating intervention, not further argument. The full list of six is in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §3 and `07_the_tether_answer.md` §4. In brief: a single lever that clears the intersections; a frame-invariant instrument; demonstrated independence of the three candidates; a mean that carries all variance; an attribution that works with the habitat removed; a measured round trip gap of zero. Until one fires, the verdict is `candidate`.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :54-56, Fig 2 :29 and :37-38, Fig 1 :22, and Fig 11 :43 and :70. Six short passages.
2. Check C1 against how culture, ecology, and institution effects actually overlap in your work. Is the compound real, or is the flat list adequate?
3. Check C2 against your field's invariance practice. Does a passed test license a causal attribution, or only a comparison?
4. Name one setting in which the three candidates move separately, or say why none exists.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement, and no reply is needed if you would rather not engage. Record the verdict before reading any framework material.
