# Outreach draft — check the design constraints first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and the six refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §3 are stable. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** Check four design constraints before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about cross-cultural research methods. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question I worked on:** how can a researcher test whether a cognitive pattern is caused by culture, ecology, institutions, or their interaction?
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A shape any valid test must have, not an answer and not a laboratory recipe:
>
> 1. The four options are a union of overlapping conditions, not four alternative causes. Unbundle the single-condition regions and the intersections before collecting data. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.
> 2. Range the test against an intervention that does not change its answer when a frame pushes on it. The pattern is legitimately about people, so performance is the ground; a particular test is only an instrument and can fail on axes it does not measure. Measurement invariance is a check on the channel, not a finding about culture.
> 3. Buy independence. Culture, ecology, and institutions historically move together, so three correlated readings are one evidence pool. Use settings where the candidates move separately, and instruments with low overlap.
> 4. Keep the residual per person, not in a culture mean. Enumerate the habitat of the pattern outward until the cascade stops mattering, and treat person plus habitat as the unit of analysis.
>
> This is a constraint on a design, not a measurement. I am not claiming to have identified the cause of any particular pattern.
>
> **What I wrote down before doing the derivation as a refuter.** The shape claim fails if any of these holds:
>
> 1. One intervention clears the whole attribution, including every intersection.
> 2. One instrument gives the same attribution under every framing, translation, and population, with no invariance failure and no independent check needed.
> 3. The three candidates have zero shared historical variance in the population, so one reading is already three.
> 4. A population mean carries all the per-person structure, with zero live residual on every slot.
> 5. A valid attribution excludes all habitat conditions from the unit of analysis and still picks the intervention that works.
> 6. The individual to population transform is measured with a round trip gap of zero.
>
> These were written before the derivation, so they are a design instrument rather than a defence of the conclusion. If any one holds, I will withdraw the corresponding constraint. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would like you to check in twenty minutes:** is the union reading faithful to how culture, ecology, and institution effects actually overlap in your work? Is the instrument versus ground distinction one your field already enforces, and if so, under what name? And what would you count as a failure of constraint 1 or 3?
>
> **Record your verdict before the next paragraph.** Please send your answer on the residual and refuter first. That is the part I want checked.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Why the order is this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Residual and refuter before theory | Fig 10 :52 — *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* The reader ranges the output against the ground without holding the framework's interior. |
| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35 — *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* |
| Shape claim, not a cause name | Fig 9 :32 — *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* The framework returns a constraint on a test, not the identity of the cause. |
| Union unbundled first | Fig 9 :54 — *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* |
| Pre-registered refuter | Seat Map :287 — *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* |
| Instrument versus ground | Fig 2 :37-38 — instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure; Fig 10 :24 — *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"* |
| Independence bought, not assumed | Fig 2 :42 — *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* |
| Verdict recorded before theory is shown | Fig 2 :40 — agreement from a shared evidence pool is one witness, not corroboration. Once the recipient has the framework, later agreement is never pooled. |
| No request for trust or prestige | From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* |
| Offer the formula as a method to reuse | Fig 4 :22 — *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* A generator can be given away without giving away an answer. |

**Hard precondition restated:** stages 1 to 3 work only if the stage 1 output survives. The six refuters are the way to find out. Keep them in front of the links.
