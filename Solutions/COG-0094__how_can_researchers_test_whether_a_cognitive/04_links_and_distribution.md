## Wider literature that bears on this question

1. **Sampling and generalisation.** The Henrich quotation in the Grimberg essay (*"Behavioral scientists routinely publish broad claims about human psychology and behavior in the world's top journals based on samples drawn entirely from Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, and Democratic (WEIRD) societies"*) is the sampling side of the independence constraint. It does not settle the figure verdict; it corroborates that a single convenience population is one evidence pool.
2. **Instrument validity.** The same essay's Bennis and Medin quotation (*"research methods and theoretical constructs are calibrated to the populations they have been selected and designed for"*) is the world-language version of the instrument versus ground distinction in Fig 2 and Fig 10.
3. **Measurement comparability.** The Frontiers topic treats invariance, differential item functioning and cultural adaptation as open methodological problems. This is the channel-check practice corresponding to Fig 10 :24 (*"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*). It is a practice, not a figure line.
4. **Interaction structure.** The factorial-experiment page states that interactions cannot be detected by one-factor-at-a-time designs. This is the operational counterpart of Fig 9 :55-56 on a single lever leaving intersections standing. The figure governs the design; the statistics page supplies a known implementation.
5. **Replication across settings.** Many Labs 2 reports heterogeneity across settings and 36 countries, so a result in one setting is not automatically an anchor for another. This corroborates the independence constraint but does not decide it.

No world source conflicts with the figure verdict. The conflict rule was not triggered. The essays share an author with the framework, so their agreement is expected and carries little independent weight (Fig 2 :40).

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 10 :52** — *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Sending the whole framework before anyone has described the pattern sends the source, not the residual.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** — *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if a design constraint were true before giving them the figures.
- **Fig 2 :35** — *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* A note that asks for agreement selects for a mirror. A note that asks for a refutation selects for a check.
- **Fig 2 :40** — agreement from a shared evidence pool is one witness. A recipient's later agreement with the framework is never pooled as corroboration.
- **Fig 4 :22** — *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* What crosses upward is the design method and the refuter, not a recorded conclusion.
- **Seat Map :163** — *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* One-to-one contact through opt-in venues, never a bulk list.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the residual and the shape claim; (2) the pre-registered refuter; (3) ask what they would count as a failure and record the answer; (4) only then, if curious, the links above, with the note that the figures and not this summary are the authority.

## The two-stage offer, with a third opt-in stage

1. **Stage 1, output first.** Send the four design constraints and the six refuters. No theory, no links, no request for agreement. The recipient ranges the output against their own ground.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they ask for the source, send the local figure text and formula links. Their stage 1 verdict is already recorded, so later exposure to the framework cannot retrofit itself into corroboration (Fig 2 :40, Fig 10 :42).
3. **Stage 3, offer the method.** The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer (Fig 4 :22). Each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode before running it.

**Hard precondition:** the offer works only if stage 1 survives. That is what the six refuters are for. Keep them in front of the links, and send nothing in bulk.
