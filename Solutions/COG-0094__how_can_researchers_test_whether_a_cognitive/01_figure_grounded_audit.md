## 0. The claim under audit, stated as a residual

> How can researchers test whether a cognitive pattern is caused by culture, ecology, institutions, or their interaction?

**Residual in effect terms.** A cognitive pattern P is observed to vary across populations. The observation is not in dispute; the **attribution** is. The question decides which intervention follows: attribute P to culture and you change transmitted beliefs and practices; to ecology and you change the material environment; to institutions and you change rules, schooling, and incentives; to their interaction and you must change a compound, not a single lever. The residual is the gap between *P is observed* and *which contact with P actually produces it*. Nothing here names a cause.

**What an answer would look like (the sought shape, bound as an ordinary term before the run).** An answer is a test design with four properties: it splits the four-way bundle before searching; it ranges against an anchor that does not move when a frame pushes on it; it buys independence between candidate causes and between instruments; and it keeps variation per person rather than averaging it into a population mean. This bound is written down now so it can accrue a residual and be refused (Formula, "Direction"; Fig 5 :65-66: *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."*).

### 0b. Domain census, before choosing any figure line

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.** Arity: four candidate causes plus a compound, so R is **four-way and conjunctive**, not a list of four independent hypotheses. Symmetry: attribution is **asymmetric** (cause produces pattern; the reverse does not hold), so the governing bond is ordered rather than symmetric. Scale: the pattern is read at the individual level and claimed at the population level, so a **lossy transform between scales** is in play.

One line per domain. A domain requires an actor, a currency, and a nameable anchor.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor (nameable?) |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Measurement | participants, tasks, test instruments, operationalisations | observed responses, scores, invariance tests | yes: the pattern as it pays against performance, a measurement that does not update on the theorist's frame (Fig 2 :29) |
| D2 Attribution | culture, ecology, institutions as candidate causes; interventions | causal credit, residual per slot | yes: an experiment or natural experiment that fixes one candidate and varies another (Fig 2 :29) |
| D3 Verification | research teams, reviewers, replications | agreement and residual | yes: the ground, never agreement; independence is the parameter (Fig 2 :40) |
| D4 Compound / habitat | the intersections, cascades, and conditions in contact with P | interaction terms, second-order effects | yes: an intervention that separates the intersections, or none if the compound cannot be resolved at this budget (Fig 9 :54) |

**Partition or union.** D1 to D3 are a **partition** of roles (instrument, cause, checker), each with its own loop. D4 is a **union**: culture, ecology, and institutions overlap and historically co-vary, so this is a compound with its own loop, not a fourth cause beside the others. The question's phrase "or their interaction" is the union made explicit.

**Dependency order (Fig 3).** Perception of the pattern, then vocabulary for what the habitat affords, then an objective (what counts as a successful attribution), then the factorial or natural-experiment plan, then carrying the result. The middle link is the least instrumented: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* (Fig 3 :27). *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* (Fig 3 :19). The order is dependency, not importance (Fig 3 :20).

## 2. Findings

### F1 — The question is a union compound, not four alternative causes (decisive, `general`)

Fig 9 :54:

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**

Fig 9 :55:

> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**

Fig 9 :55-56:

> **"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**

Fig 9 :57:

> **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"**

Culture, ecology, and institutions are overlapping conditions. A design that changes only schooling clears the region where institutions alone bind and leaves the culture-by-ecology intersection standing. Treating "culture / ecology / institutions / interaction" as four columns of a table is the flat formula error: it records ingredients and not the bond (Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*).

**Refuter:** demonstrate a single intervention that clears the full attribution, including every intersection, with the same instrument and no separate lever. **Mode: `general`. Triangulated:** Fig 9 :54-58 and Fig 12 :46-47 (bond carries information the operands do not), two figures with low overlap.

World check (secondary): the Wikipedia factorial-experiment article states that *"When the effect of one factor is different for different levels of another factor, it cannot be detected by an OFAT experiment design. Factorial designs are required to detect such interactions."* Corroborates, does not decide.

### F2 — The anchor is an intervention, and a test is an instrument that can fail (decisive for D1, `specified`)

Fig 2 :29:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**

Fig 2 :30:

> **"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."**

Fig 2 :36:

> **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."**

Fig 2 :37-38:

> **"It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."**

This resolves the central tension in cross-cultural work. The question is constitutively about people's cognition, so **performance is the legitimate ground** (Fig 2 :36-37). But a particular test, scale, or task is only an **instrument**, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure. A test imported from one population and scored in another changes its answer with the frame, so it is a frame, not an anchor. Measurement-invariance testing is a **channel check**, not a new verdict about culture.

Fig 10 :24:

> **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**

Fig 10 :26:

> **"saturated metrics, pooled readings"**

The Seat Map (line 20) names the specific failure: the channel decays through *"stale readings, dropped signals, a metric that saturated, populations pooled across a change."* Pooling populations across a change is a channel failure, not a cultural finding. Fig 10 :18-19 requires: *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"* and *"install what can be violated."*

**Refuter:** show a single instrument that returns the same attribution under every framing, every translation, and every population, with no invariance failure and no independent check needed. **Mode: `specified`. Triangulated:** Fig 2 :29-38 and Fig 10 :18-26, different figures and different failure modes.

World check: the Frontiers Research Topic on cross-cultural measurement says *"careful validation and adaptation are crucial for producing data that genuinely reflect psychological traits rather than methodological artifacts."* Corroborates the channel reading. The anthrometronom essay (opened 2026-11-22) quotes Bennis and Medin (2010): *"research methods and theoretical constructs are calibrated to the populations they have been selected and designed for."* Same point, different field.

### F3 — Independence must be bought, because the three causes share an evidence pool (`specified`)

Fig 2 :31:

> **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."**

Fig 2 :31 continues: *"Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."*

Fig 2 :40:

> **"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**

Fig 2 :42:

> **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."**

Culture, ecology, and institutions are historically correlated: institutions embody culture and ecology shapes both. A design in which they move together is one evidence pool. Agreement among three such measures is the pool talking, not three causes being triangulated. Independence is purchased by settings where the candidates move separately (migration, schooling reform, ecological shock) and by low-overlap instruments (experiment plus ethnography plus historical or population data).

Fig 8 :50:

> **"But shared atoms are not the signature. Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."**

So shared vocabulary is not itself a collapse. What matters is the record of where each reading came from (Fig 8 :51-52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."*).

**Refuter:** show that culture, ecology, and institution measures have zero shared historical variance in the population studied, so one reading is already three independent ones. **Mode: `specified`. Triangulated:** Fig 2 :31-42 and Fig 8 :47-52.

World check: Klein et al. (Many Labs 2, 2018) report *"Across settings, the Q statistic indicated significant heterogeneity in 11 (39%) of the replication effects"*, with data from 36 countries. This is a finding about variation across settings, and it corroborates the need to range an attribution against more than one setting. It does not decide the figure verdict.

### F4 — The residual is per person, not a culture mean (`specified`)

Fig 1 :19:

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**

Fig 1 :22:

> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

A culture-level mean destroys the live signal when variation within the population carries the causal structure. Neighbouring question 95 in the same source section (*"What methods best capture variation within a culture rather than treating each society as internally uniform?"*) is a related question, not a source; it is mentioned only to locate this one in the source. The figure line governs the unit of measurement directly.

**Refuter:** demonstrate a case where the population mean carries all the per-person structure, with the within-person residual exactly zero on every slot. **Mode: `specified`. Single-source:** Fig 1 only. The cross-scale loss claim is separately triangulated with Fig 4 :30 (see F6).

### F5 — Describe the pattern in effect terms before choosing a cause frame (decisive for method order, `general`)

Fig 9 :27-28:

> **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**

Fig 9 :30-31:

> **"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"**

Fig 9 :32:

> **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**

If "intelligence" or "rationality" is defined inside the culture being studied, then measuring it there is circular. The pattern must be characterised in effect terms (what the person does under which contact) before a culture, ecology, or institution frame is selected to explain it. Formula step 7: *"OUTWARD IMPORT. Characterize R first, in effect terms, then find the frame whose closure predicts its shape."*

Fig 5 :65-66 makes the discipline explicit: *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."* This is why the refuter in §3 is written before this conclusion.

**Refuter:** show a case where the cause frame is chosen first and still survives an effect-term characterisation by an independent team that did not know the frame. **Mode: `general`. Triangulated:** Fig 9 :27-32 and Fig 5 :65-66 plus the Formula "Direction" paragraph.

World check: the anthrometronom essay quotes Baumard and Sperber (2010) on economic games: outside a WEIRD context a participant may ask *"Who owns the money? ... Who is the other participant? Is he or she someone I know? Does he or she have rights over the money?"* Different task interpretation, different answer, so an effect-term description is owed before a causal frame is named.

### F6 — Culture, ecology, and institutions are the habitat, not the ground (`specified`)

Fig 11 :43:

> **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**

Fig 11 :44:

> **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**

Fig 11 :49:

> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**

Fig 11 :39-40:

> **"And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**
> **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

Fig 11 :73:

> **"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."**

The candidates in the question are habitat elements: conditions (ecology), relations and transmitted practices (culture), and introduced structures (institutions). They are **enumerated, not composed**, and their valence is relative to P, not fixed. Treating "culture" as the anchor is a category error: culture updates, and a thing that updates is a frame or a habitat condition, not the ground. The unit of test is person-plus-habitat.

Fig 11 :52-53: *"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* An interaction that is three steps out is invisible if the habitat is scoped to direct contact.

**Refuter:** show a valid attribution that excludes all habitat conditions from the unit of analysis and still changes the intervention that works. **Mode: `specified`. Single-source:** Fig 11 only. This is the largest single-figure dependency in the solution and is marked as such in `09_watson_answer.md`.

### F7 — The individual to population step is a lossy transform; measure `R_T` (`specified`)

Fig 4 :21-22:

> **"The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction."**
> **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**

Fig 4 :30:

> **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."**

A method that finds a pattern in one person can be carried up only as a reusable method, not as that person's recording. Casting it down as a head start for the next population is legitimate; replaying it as if the pattern were identical is dead reckoning. The round trip gap `R_T = gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))` must be measured, not assumed zero. In this simulated run `R_T` is recorded qualitatively in `11_audit.md`; no numeric value is claimed.

**Refuter:** show that the transform from individual response to population pattern loses nothing, with `R_T = 0` measured rather than assumed. **Mode: `specified`. Single-source:** Fig 4.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Required before outreach: the Seat Map, line 287, says *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* Written before this conclusion was drawn.

The shape claim is refused if any of the following holds:

1. **Union refuter.** A single lever is demonstrated to clear the whole attribution, including every culture-by-ecology-by-institution intersection (breaks Fig 9 :54-58, F1).
2. **Anchor refuter.** One instrument returns the same attribution under every framing, translation, and population, with no invariance failure and no independent instrument needed (breaks Fig 2 :29-38 and Fig 10 :24, F2).
3. **Independence refuter.** The three candidate causes are demonstrated to have zero shared historical variance in the population, so one reading is already three (breaks Fig 2 :31 and Fig 9 :55, F3).
4. **Per-slot refuter.** The population mean carries all the per-person structure, with zero live residual on every slot (breaks Fig 1 :19 and :22, F4).
5. **Habitat refuter.** A valid attribution is produced with every habitat condition excluded from the unit of analysis and still selects the intervention that works (breaks Fig 11 :39-43, F6).
6. **Round-trip refuter.** The individual to population transform is measured with `R_T = 0` (breaks Fig 4 :30, F7).

If any fires, the corresponding constraint is withdrawn and the audit is rewritten around the surviving residue. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Split the question into its single-condition regions and its intersections before collecting data (F1). Treat "interaction" as a compound with its own loop, not a fourth column.
2. Characterise the cognitive pattern in effect terms first, and bind the sought-for design shape as an ordinary term with the refuter above attached (F5).
3. Range every candidate cause against an intervention that does not update on the researcher's frame. Treat each test as an instrument and run the channel checks (F2).
4. Buy independence with settings where the candidates move separately and with low-overlap instruments, and record which reading came from which frame (F3).
5. Keep the residual per person and measure the cross-scale gap (F4, F7).
6. Enumerate the habitat outward until the cascade stops mattering, and treat person-plus-habitat as the unit (F6).
7. Figure 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Offer the design constraints and the refuter, not a finished conclusion.
