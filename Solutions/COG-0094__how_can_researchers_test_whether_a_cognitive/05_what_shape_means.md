## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13, on the closed set: *"It can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands... It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*, opened this session: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

For this question the **missing thing is the attribution**: which contact with a person produces the observed pattern. A shape here is the set of properties a valid test must have. It is not the name of a cause and not a specific protocol:

- **A shape:** the attribution is a union compound, so unbundle before testing; the anchor must not update on a frame; the instruments must be independent; the residual is per person; the habitat is enumerated.
- **A name:** "culture caused it," or "this exact factorial design is the answer." Neither is returned by the framework.

The essay on the Library of Babel also names the structural error this question invites: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"* That is the figure's union rule in world language, and it is why the four options in the question are not four columns.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary already held that is closest to the thing, then look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it. *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."*
- The stopping rule in *Library of Babel*: *"you will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place, as there is nowhere else to search and nothing else that can describe the answer better."* The paradox there is that by then *"you have already composed the answer through the process of locating it."*
- Formula step 7: *"OUTWARD IMPORT. Characterize R first, in effect terms, then find the frame whose closure predicts its shape."*

Applied here: describe the cognitive pattern as what a person does under specified contact, before selecting culture, ecology, or institutions as the explaining frame. Otherwise, per Fig 9 :27-28, any frame will seem to fit.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays."* An intervention that changes one candidate while holding the others and moves the pattern accordingly licenses a name.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test. A causal theory imported from another field licenses a name only with its provenance recorded.

The frameworks' own limit applies to both: *"A gate is not the ground."*

## 4. Correction to my own §1 and §3

I initially wrote the "shape, not a name" rule as if it forbade naming culture, ecology, or institutions at all. That overstates it. The README's phrase governs a **missing** primitive, one not held in the frame. Culture, ecology, and institutions are already nameable habitat elements (Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed"*). They are read off the world, not invented.

What the framework actually withholds here is the **ranking and the attribution**, not the vocabulary. It can state the shape a valid test must have; it cannot say which of three already-named habitat conditions produced this pattern without a ground reading. Calling "culture" the answer from an observed correlation is the metamerism error in world language: a source named from a reading that several sources could have produced. The remedy is an instrument that discriminates sources, not a ban on using the word.

Both readings are kept. The restriction is on the verdict, not on the lexicon.

## 5. Prestige is not action

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, opened this session: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* And: *"You may all agree and be wrong. You may set your clock to someone else's, who was setting your clock to yours, and be off by many deviations over time. ## 7. The one-paragraph shape

> For this question, a shape is the constraint set any valid causal-attribution test must satisfy: split the overlapping causes before testing, anchor the test in an intervention that does not move with the frame, buy independence between causes and instruments, keep the residual per person, and enumerate the habitat rather than composing it. The framework does not name the cause and does not supply a protocol; a name is licensed only by an intervention that pays or by a provenanced import. What is offered is the shape, the refuter written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. Everything here is `specified`, simulated, and ungrounded until the ground settles it.
