## 0. The residual, in effect terms

The question asks about the effect of a relation (components are not interchangeable) on two outcomes (remaining in contact with the ground under perturbation, and composing a coherent collective). Stated as a failure: a system can hold a great deal of component difference and still fail in either of two ways. It can fail to survive a perturbation that a more uniform set would absorb, or it can fail to coordinate at all because no shared reading exists. A single scalar answer ("diversity helps" or "diversity hurts") is therefore a category error before it is a wrong number.

The residual is a **union compound**, not a partition. Two conditions bind at once:

- C-independence: do the components draw on different evidence?
- C-aim: has the residual been described so that the difference has something to be aimed at?

"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds" (Fig 9 :55). "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either" (Fig 9 :57). Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged. Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

The sought-for shape, bound as an ordinary term before the search per `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": *a rule that assigns a valence to a component difference relative to a named residual and a non-updating ground, and that reports a coordination cost separately from a resilience benefit.* This is the preference that the rest of the audit allows to be refused.

## 1. DOMAIN CENSUS (before any figure line was chosen)

### 1a. R by arity, symmetry, scale (Fig 9 :22, "arity · symmetry · scale")

- **Arity.** Diversity is a relation among at least two components and one collective outcome, evaluated against a ground. It is one field with two readers whenever two components draw on the same evidence (Fig 10 :13, "two frames, one field, no shared reading"), so it is a seam before it is a quantity.
- **Symmetry.** Diversity is precisely the claim that the operands are *not* interchangeable. Where components are symmetric, "k operands have k! spellings and one term" (Fig 12 :92) and diversity is zero by construction: many spellings, one collective. Where they are asymmetric, different arrangements are different terms and the bond carries information the operands do not (Fig 12 :5).
- **Scale.** Step (one component's bet and per-slot residual), population (many components, one membrane: Fig 4), and lineage (selection between rooms: Fig 7). The same loop at each size, with different actors, currency and anchor (Fig 4 :20, "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change").

### 1b. One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor

No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet. Each line below has one.

- **Domain 1, the single component and its library.** Actors: an agent with Γ_i, belief b_i, weighting w_i, per-slot residual. Currency: prediction minus observation, per slot. Anchor: the ground as the only metric, "The ground enters as the only metric" (Fig 1 :23). Diversity has no meaning at this scale. One component has one library; there is no relation among components to measure. This domain is upstream and we do not force the question onto it.
- **Domain 2, the population across the membrane.** Actors: many components, a shared library, and something that ranks what they produce. Currency: methods carried up and head starts sent down; agreement; distributed search. Anchor: the ground via selection, Fig 7 :35 on the ground vote. The framework-author's essay *How a Society of Agents Would Work* describes the same domain in its own vocabulary, but the figure line that governs it is Fig 8 :49.
- **Domain 3, independence and overlap (the hinge domain).** Actors: frames with closures Γ_A and Γ_B. Currency: independence, defined as "how little they draw on the same evidence" (Fig 2 :31). Anchor: a ground that does not update on either frame, "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks" (Fig 2 :29). This domain governs both outcomes. It is the one domain where a figure line decides the question directly.
- **Domain 4, the union and the aim.** Actors: a residual R and the frames whose closures could predict its shape. Currency: described residual, imports, conventions. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. "nothing was aimed; the union is mute" (Fig 8 :21) names the failure when this domain is skipped.
- **Domain 5, the habitat and selection.** Actors: components, conditions, relations, the maintainer if one exists. Currency: contact and cascade. Anchor: the ground, which settles and does not update; "The habitat is not the ground" (Fig 11 :73). Valence is assigned here: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries" (Fig 11 :49).

### 1c. Partition or union?

Union, not partition. The overlap of Domains 3 and 4 is a compound with its own loop: independence without aim gives unaimed variety; aim without independence gives a single frame with several names. Domain 5 overlaps both because the habitat supplies the components whose independence is measured in Domain 3. Following Fig 9 :54, "unbundle before you search again," so each domain gets its own loop and its own verdict.

### 1d. Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. Domain 1 first: a per-slot residual must exist somewhere. "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated" (Fig 1 :19).
2. Domain 4 next: describe R in effect terms before picking a frame, "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit" (Fig 9 :27-28).
3. Domain 3 next: measure independence of the frames that R selects, "Residual first, frame second" (Fig 8 :53).
4. Domain 5 next: enumerate the habitat and let the ground vote, "variation inside, selection between" (Fig 7 :33).
5. Domain 2 last: only now ask what the population can compose, because "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down" (Fig 4 :22) and a method collected before step 4 is a recording, not a generator.

Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." A coordination fix applied before the residual is described arranges a room nobody summoned.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - There is no single "diversity" quantity to measure (decisive for the framing)

Fig 8 :47: "Independence is one quantity read twice."
Fig 8 :48: "How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."

The question treats "diversity among components" as one input with two outputs. The figure says the operative quantity is independence, and that one quantity is read twice: once as the validity condition for agreement (verdict) and once as the source of surplus (import). A trait-variance score over components is not that quantity, because it can be large while independence is zero.

Fig 8 :49: "Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."
Fig 13 :39: "Six members that overlapped would be fewer members wearing six names."

Refuter for F1: show a case where a high trait-variance score and zero evidence-independence produce a genuine surplus of collective reach against the ground, with the contribution attributed per slot and not to an average. Until that is shown, "diversity" as a scalar is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground (Fig 11 :36), which does no work in the derivation.

**Mode:** `general` structural finding. Triangulated across Fig 2 :31, Fig 8 :47-49 and Fig 13 :38-39 (three readings with low overlap: definition, consequence, set-level restatement).

### F2 - Independence promotes resilience, by the mirror-chain and anchor rules

Fig 7 :13: "agreement compounds, evidence does not."
Fig 7 :36: "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."
Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

A population whose members draw on the same evidence is one frame with many names. It cannot correct itself, because "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool" (Fig 2 :40). Resilience under a perturbation that changes the ground's reading requires at least some frames that were not correlated at origin, because the anchor "does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks" (Fig 2 :29) and each frame is corrected separately. Fig 7 :24 gives the operational form: the next room is picked "against the residual the last one surfaced," with "low ρ between rooms." Fig 7 :26: "progress per handoff: R = h² × S" (bracket fidelity times ground pressure). Zero either factor and the population runs in place.

Refuter for F2: a population whose members are provably correlated at origin (same report, same dataset, same benchmark) retains a real resilience advantage against a ground that changed, with the advantage located per slot and measured after the change. That would break the mirror-chain rule at the ground.

**Mode:** `specified` on the operational form (low ρ, residual-matched selection, non-zero h² and S), `general` on the shape. Triangulated across Fig 2, Fig 7 and Fig 8.

### F3 - The same independence impedes coordination, by the seam and lossy-transform rules

Fig 10 :13: "two frames, one field, no shared reading."
Fig 10 :17: "it makes the bug statable."
Fig 10 :18: "a declared meaning is not a grounded one."
Fig 4 :27: "The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."

Coordination is not the absence of difference. It is the authoring of a convention across a seam: a shared meaning that "makes no claim about the world" and whose whole value is that nothing can check it (Fig 10 :45-46 in the Seat Map; Fig 10 :18 in the figures). The more independent the frames, the more information the bridging transform must discard, so the cost of coordination rises with the very property that buys resilience. Fig 2 :43: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." A coordination rule that starts deciding which frame is right has collapsed into the mutual-update failure.

Fig 8 :19-21: "closures differ, so a surplus exists / but no residual is described / nothing was aimed; the union is mute." The cost is not merely friction. Without a described residual, the union has no direction and cannot be searched at all.

Refuter for F3: a set of frames with maximally different evidence coordinates a joint action with no shared convention, no described residual, and no lossy transform, and still improves its ground reading. That would break the seam rule and the undirected-union rule at once.

**Mode:** `specified`. Single-source for the convention role (Fig 10), triangulated for the mute-union failure (Fig 8 + Fig 9 :27-28), because coordination is a convention and conventions are not grounded claims.

### F4 - Component difference that is only spelling is not diversity at all

Fig 12 :92: "Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term"
Fig 12 :27: "a bigger space, not a bigger vocabulary"
Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."

If two components differ only in how they are named, or in the order of operands under a symmetric relation, they compose one term. Charging each spelling as a separate frame inflates the apparent diversity and the search space at the same time. This is the flat-formula collapse: the ingredient list is recorded and the arrangement is not. It is also the exact reason a roster of "different" components can fail to triangulate: they are the same frame under permutations.

Refuter for F4: an arrangement under a symmetric bond whose k! spellings demonstrably change the collective's ground contact, with the change attributed to the bond rather than to the operands. Under Fig 12 :92 that cannot happen, so firing this refuter would break the bond definition.

**Mode:** `general`. Single-source for the bond mechanics (Fig 12), corroborated by the Operators table :59-62 ("Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term").

### F5 - Valence is residual-relative: the same difference can help one goal and hurt another

Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
Fig 11 :50: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."
Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

This is the engine of the question's "when." A component difference has no standing valence. Holding a fixed residual and ground, independence buys resilience. Change the residual to one where the task requires a shared convention, and the same independence becomes the coordination cost. The answer is therefore conditional on which residual the collective is ranged against, and any unconditional "diversity promotes" or "diversity impedes" is a standing list of good actors, which the figure forbids.

Refuter for F5: an actor or trait whose valence is the same for two residuals that differ only in their aim, with contact measured in both cases. That would restore a standing list of good actors and break Fig 11 :49-51.

**Mode:** `general`. Triangulated with Fig 9 :27-28 (describe the residual before choosing a frame) and Seat Map section THE HABITAT, which repeats the valence rule.

### F6 - Diversity of closures moves the wall; diversity of names does not

Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."
Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."
Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."

The resilience benefit is not "more search." It is contact with a closure that holds a primitive yours lacks. Fig 6 :30 warns that the description must travel "in effect terms," or the frame you find "will be sharpened into terms only we can read." This is also the budget rule: more searchers at the same reach buy spelling, not atoms. Fig 6 :53: "searchers drawing on the same evidence are one searcher wearing many names."

Refuter for F6: a homogeneous closure adding a genuinely new atom to its reach by internal composition alone, with the atom not derivable from its primitives. That would break idempotence and the mint/import distinction together.

**Mode:** `specified` (the wall moves only on import), `general` (idempotence). Triangulated across Fig 6 and Fig 8.

### F7 - Coordination is authored as a convention and must stay revisable

Fig 10 :16: "the convention does not fix the bug"
Fig 2 :43: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."
Fig 9 :41: "so keep every cut ranked and reversible"

A convention is the mechanism by which independent frames can act as one. It is allowed exactly because it makes no world claim (Fig 10 :46). It becomes corrupt the moment it decides which frame was right, because it then "has stopped being one." Operationally, this means a coordination rule must be revisable and ranked, with its reversal condition written down, or it silently becomes a verdict. The maintainer may author conventions and nothing that has a truth value (Fig 10 :45-46).

Refuter for F7: a coordination convention that decides an outcome and remains a convention under the figure's own test, or a convention nothing can check that is not "a constant the seat authored, carrying the seat's authority" (Fig 10 :48).

**Mode:** `general` (structural rule), `specified` on the operational requirement of reversibility. Single-source for the convention/verdict split (Fig 10), corroborated by Fig 9 :40-41.

### F8 - The two currencies do not add, so "optimal diversity" is not a single number

Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."
Fig 12 :127: "They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them"
Fig 12 :130: "Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."

A diverse roster is expensive to describe in bits and may be cheap to refute in actions; a small coordinated roster is cheap to state and may take a whole budget to settle. The resilience benefit and the coordination cost are read in different currencies. Any answer that returns one optimal diversity level has already performed the forbidden sum. The honest form is two curves with the residual and ground named, and a budget that decides how much of the space is reached.

Refuter for F8: a single additive payoff function over component heterogeneity that predicts both the resilience benefit and the coordination cost across two different residuals, with the units declared and no residual-specific term. That would dissolve the two-currency rule.

**Mode:** `general`. Single-source (Fig 12 :125-130), corroborated by Fig 9 :60 ("Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict").

### F9 - The specific mixture that is optimal is an honest `gap`

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"
Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"
Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

After a genuine census, no figure line names which particular mixture of components is best for a given residual. The figures give the shape: low evidence overlap, a described residual, a non-updating ground, selection between rooms, a revisable convention, per-slot reporting. They do not give the name of the mixture. Reporting that as `gap` is a verdict, not a silence, and it is the `gap` kind that is *unreachable at this budget with this instrument* rather than *underivable in principle* (Fig 8 :40-41). The two issue different instructions, so importing a named mixture from a frame that has not been source-gated would be a wrong atom, priced above starting from primitives (Fig 6 :24).

Refuter for F9: a figure line that names the optimal component mixture for a stated residual, or a measurement that settles it at the ground. Until one fires, the name is the searcher's own contribution and must be recorded as such.

**Mode:** `general` on the stop, `specified` on the gap kind. Triangulated across Fig 9, Fig 13 and Fig 8.

### F10 - Aggregation hides the very signal the question asks for

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
Symbols table :43-44: "R is measured per slot, never as a single global number."

A collective can have a global performance near zero with one live slot residual (Fig 1 :13). An averaged report of the form "diversity improved performance by X percent" throws away the signal that says where the improvement occurred. This is not a statistical nicety. It is the difference between SUPPORT being satisfied ("|R⁺_s| > 0 for some s") and the system being inert, and it is the reason the outreach packet asks for a per-slot failure, not a global score.

Refuter for F10: a global aggregate that predicts a per-slot intervention as well as the per-slot census on a held-out perturbation. By Fig 1 :22 that cannot happen without discarding the live slot.

**Mode:** `general`. Triangulated across Fig 1 and the Symbols table.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach: "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device" (Seat Map :287).

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **R1.** A high trait-variance score with zero evidence-independence produces a real, per-slot surplus of collective reach against a changed ground (breaks F1 and F2).
2. **R2.** Frames correlated at origin retain a genuine resilience advantage after the ground changes, with the advantage located per slot (breaks the mirror-chain rule, Fig 7 :13, :36).
3. **R3.** A maximally independent set coordinates a joint action with no convention and no described residual and still improves its ground reading (breaks F3 and the mute-union rule).
4. **R4.** A composition whose k! spellings under a symmetric bond change ground contact, attributed to the bond (breaks Fig 12 :92).
5. **R5.** An actor or trait has the same valence for two residuals differing only in aim, with contact measured in both (breaks Fig 11 :49-50).
6. **R6.** A homogeneous closure adds a genuinely new atom by internal composition alone (breaks Fig 6 :21, Fig 8 :32).
7. **R7.** A single additive payoff over heterogeneity predicts both currencies across two residuals with units declared (breaks Fig 12 :126-130).
8. **R8.** A figure line names the optimal mixture for a stated residual, or a ground measurement settles it (closes the `gap` in F9).
9. **R9.** A global diversity/performance aggregate predicts per-slot intervention as well as the per-slot census on a held-out perturbation (breaks Fig 1 :19, :22).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed at that point and the change is logged. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`. The refuter was fixed before the recommendation below and is carried unchanged into `02`, `07`, `08`, `09` and `11`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. **Never answer the question with one number.** Split the residual into independence and aim, keep the two effects in separate domains, report the coordination cost in its own currency (F1, F3, F8).
2. **Describe the residual before selecting components.** "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit" (Fig 9 :27-28). This is the step that turns undirected variety into a searchable union.
3. **Measure independence over evidence, not roster variety.** Fig 2 :31 defines it; Fig 8 :49 and Fig 6 :53 say what high overlap costs. Two components reading the same report are one component with two names.
4. **Treat coordination conventions as authored, revisable, and groundless.** Keep them ranked and reversible (Fig 9 :41); the moment one decides an outcome it has stopped being a convention (Fig 2 :43).
5. **Report per slot.** Fig 1 :19, :22. A global diversity index is a filter for the budget, never a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).
6. **Name the `gap` honestly.** The specific mixture is not given. "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" (Fig 13 :134).
7. **Send the generator, not the conclusion.** Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." The two-stage offer in `02` and the packet in `08` lead with the refuter.

## 7. Strains and re-census

- **S1, Fig 13 six-term substrate applied to social or ecological components.** Re-census: the six-term set governs the physical substrate; applying it to a team, a food web or an agent population is a binding by analogy, licensed by Fig 4 :19-20 (the kernel travels, the actors change) but not a derivation of team membership. The answer uses the *rules* (closure, independence, valence, selection, membrane), not the six physical atoms, as governing. Marked `specified` where a binding is used and `general` where a rule is used.
- **S2, "resilience" as return to equilibrium versus maintenance of contact.** Re-census: Fig 11 :73 separates habitat from ground and Fig 10 :24 says "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Resilience in this derivation is continued contact and channel integrity, not a return to a prior state. This matters because a system can return to equilibrium and still have lost the channel, which would look stable from inside.
- **S3, the "moderate diversity" reading in the world literature.** Several world sources report an inverted-U (moderately diverse groups outperform both homogeneous and maximally heterogeneous ones). This is not taken as a figure line. Re-census: the figures give two mechanisms with opposite signs and a union condition (Fig 8 :47, Fig 9 :55); the inverted-U is a *world-shaped curve* that may result from their interaction, so it is corroboration of the two-effect shape, not a replacement for it. A conflict sends us back to the census; here there is none, because the world claim is about outcome while the figure claim is about mechanism.
- **S4, calling a coordination convention a "ground."** Refused by Fig 10 :18, :47 and Fig 2 :43. The ground is what settles; a convention is what lets frames act together. Confusing them is named as the collapse in which a convention decides an outcome.

After re-census: four strains, all named, none hidden, no figure line forced.
