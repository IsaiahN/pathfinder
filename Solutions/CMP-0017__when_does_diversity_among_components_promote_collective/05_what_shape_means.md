## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

From *The Library of Babel*: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question." The same essay gives the method: use the vocabulary closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, and recurse. "With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."

For this question, the shape is not "diversity is good up to a point." That is a curve, and a curve is not a mechanism. The shape is:

- a **union of two conditions** with separate loops, never averaged (Fig 9 :55, :57);
- a **governing quantity**, independence over evidence, with two readings (Fig 8 :47-48);
- a **residual-relative valence**, so the same component difference can promote one outcome and impede the other (Fig 11 :49-50);
- a **per-slot report**, because a global average hides the live signal (Fig 1 :19, :22);
- an **honest stop** at the name of the particular mixture (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134).

A shape constrains a candidate answer: any viable account of diversity and collective performance must say where the independence comes from, what residual it is ranged against, what convention carries coordination, and in which currency each cost is paid. An account that gives a single optimal number satisfies none of those constraints.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." Here, a measured per-slot change in collective contact after a controlled change in evidence-overlap would settle part of it.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

The essays sharpen this. *The Hard Problem is About Source* gives metamerism: an instrument reads green and "cannot comment on the source." The reading does not determine the source, and "if several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved it. Applied here: a collective performance curve does not tell you whether independence or mere roster variety produced it. Several different component arrangements can give the same aggregate reading. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument, not a name assigned from the aggregate.

*They're Scanning Books When They Should* (carried from the exemplar mapping) adds that "The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space." A diversity metric imported from one field into another is only an import if it adds a dimension to the receiving closure; a new column called "diversity" copied over from a roster is not.

## 4. Why the world sources cannot hand us the name

Hong and Page 2004 find that randomly selected problem solvers outperform the individually best ones, because the best become similar as the pool grows. That is a result about *functional* difference in perspective and heuristic, which is a difference of closure and search rule. It is a low-overlap corroboration of Fig 6 :23, not a figure line. The same paper reports that "identity-diverse groups often have more conflict, more problems with communication, and less mutual respect and trust among members," and that agents with diverse perspectives "may have trouble understanding solutions identified by other agents." That is the coordination cost, corroborating Fig 4 :27 and Fig 10 :13.

May 1972 finds instability beyond a critical connectance in randomly assembled systems; McCann 2000 and Arese Lucini et al. 2020 find conditional stability, with the latter showing that the paradox "is a conclusion driven solely by the linearity of the model." Those are three world results with opposite-looking verdicts. They do not conflict with the figures, because the figures' verdict is about mechanism and domain: a union with no described residual is mute (Fig 8 :21), and the model you bring decides what you see. The apparent contradiction sends the reader back to the census, which is exactly what happened in section 1.

The Wikipedia page on collective intelligence reports a moderate-cognitive-diversity result: groups "moderately diverse in cognitive style have higher collective intelligence than those who are very similar in cognitive style or very different." That is a world-shaped inverted-U. It corroborates the existence of two opposing effects, but it is not the framework's answer and is not used as one. The framework's answer is the pair of mechanisms and the union condition that can produce such a curve.
