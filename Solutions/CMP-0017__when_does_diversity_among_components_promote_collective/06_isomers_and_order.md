# Independence and overlap: one quantity, two readings, and the invertibility test

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The main audit names two effects of component difference with opposite signs. Two questions are owed before they can be held together: (1) how can one quantity govern both the resilience benefit and the coordination cost without contradiction, and (2) what observation separates the case where two frames independently reached the same term from the case where one copied the other? The second is the provenance question, and if it cannot be answered the first cannot be settled empirically, because a correlated population can look like a diverse one in its contents.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - How can one quantity have two readings?

- Fig 8 :47: "Independence is one quantity read twice."
- Fig 8 :48: "How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."
- Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."

The two readings are not two variables and they are not averaged. Reading one is *validity*: if A and B agree while drawing on one pool, "the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other" (Fig 2 :20-21). Reading two is *reach*: two closures that differ hold a surplus where one holds none (Fig 8 :19). Both are decided by the same parameter, ρ, the amount of shared evidence. What differs is the operation applied to the frames: comparing their verdicts (reading one) or composing across their closures (reading two).

So the answer to the user's "when" is not a threshold on diversity. It is a switch on which operation is running. When the collective is comparing verdicts against a ground, low overlap is a benefit. When the collective is composing a joint action, the same low overlap is a cost that must be paid for with a convention. This is the union condition from Fig 9 :55 in another form: the two conditions bind in different operations, so the compound needs its own loop.

- **Status:** triangulated across Fig 2 :31, Fig 8 :47-48 and Fig 13 :38-39. Low overlap between definition, consequence and set-level restatement.

### Q2 - The invertibility test: convergent derivation versus adopted import

- Fig 8 :50: "But shared atoms are not the signature."
- Fig 8 :51: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents."
- Fig 8 :52: "Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."

This is the key observation for measuring the effect. A population can hold the same term in every member either because each reached it separately from different evidence (maximum independence, corroboration) or because one copied it (minimum independence, one frame wearing many names). From the *contents* the two cases are indistinguishable. Therefore any study that measures only the distribution of answers, traits or heuristics cannot tell whether it has independence or a single propagating frame. It must carry the lineage.

- Fig 8 :53: "Residual first, frame second."
- Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."

The operational test: first describe R in effect terms, then record for each component whether its contribution was derived against the ground from its own evidence or adopted from another component. Only the second column measures independence. Fig 6 :24 prices the mistake: "Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence." A population with a high fraction of adopted imports has lower independence than its roster suggests, even when its answers are spread.

- **Status:** single-source for the figure rule (Fig 8), corroborated at low overlap by the opened Society of Agents essay, which says "Independent discoveries count most" and quotes the same figure line, and by Lorenz et al. 2011 as reported on the opened Wikipedia page: when people could see each other's guesses, "their answers narrowed without getting any more accurate." The essay and Wikipedia are world sources, so this remains figure-governed.

### Q3 - The seam is a compound, not a missing third frame

- Fig 10 :13: "two frames, one field, no shared reading"
- Fig 10 :14: "a disagreement nothing can state"
- Fig 10 :15: "is not yet a defect"
- Fig 10 :16: "the convention does not fix the bug"
- Fig 10 :17: "it makes the bug statable"

The coordination problem is not solved by adding a third component that agrees with both. That would be another frame, so the population would still have no shared reading and would now also have an authored anchor (Fig 2 :47: "A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame"). The correct move is to author a *convention*: a shared meaning with no truth value, whose job is to make the disagreement statable. It "makes the bug statable" rather than fixing it, and "a declared meaning is not a grounded one" (Fig 10 :18).

This is why coordination cost is not a defect to be eliminated. It is the price of the transform across the seam, and Fig 4 :27 says the transform "loses information, and that is the point." The cost rises with independence because a coarser shared description discards more of what made the frames different. The round trip must be measured: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning" (Fig 4 :30). Here the gap `R_T` is the part of each frame's evidence that the convention could not carry into joint action.

- **Status:** single-source for the seam mechanics (Fig 10), triangulated for the lossy transform (Fig 4 :27-30) and the prohibition on a framed ground (Fig 2 :47).

### Q4 - The two currencies and the shape of the trade-off

- Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."
- Fig 12 :127: "They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them"
- Fig 12 :130: "Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."

Write the two costs in their own units. Let D be the description cost of the component arrangement (bits: how many distinct closure types must be named and tracked). Let A be the action cost of settling whether it works (actions: how many ground readings are needed to distinguish independent contributions from copied ones). A diverse roster can have high D and low A, or low D and high A, and neither predicts the other (Fig 12 :128-129). The budget decides how much of the space is reached.

The world analogues have the same shape. On the opened Society of Agents page: Kish's n_eff = k/(1 + (k-1)ρ) says k observers with correlation ρ count as at most 1/ρ independent observers, and the Universal Scalability Law adds a coherence term κN(N-1) so that past a peak "adding more makes the group slower." The first is the resilience side (correlation caps effective independence); the second is the coordination side (coherence caps usable size). They are not added; they are two curves with a residual named. These are corroboration from a framework-author essay, not figure lines.

- **Status:** single-source for the two-currency rule (Fig 12), corroborated by low-overlap world analogues (Kish, Amdahl, Gunther) as reported in a world source.

## FALSIFIER, written before the run

If either of the following holds, this supporting derivation fails and the main answer must be narrowed:

1. **F-A.** A population whose contributions are all adopted imports, with ρ near one, shows the same per-slot resilience gain against a changed ground as a population of independent discoveries, with lineage recorded in both cases. That breaks Q2 and the invertibility test.
2. **F-B.** A convention across a seam can be shown to fix the underlying disagreement itself, so that it carries a truth value and still functions as a convention, and no ground reading is required to restore contact. That breaks Q3.

A third check applies to Q4: a single additive payoff over heterogeneity that predicts both curves with declared units and no residual-specific term would break Fig 12 :126-130. That is R7 in the main refuter list.

## CONCLUSION

Independence and overlap are one parameter read in two operations, not two quantities to trade off on a single scale. The resilience benefit comes from composition across differing closures; the coordination cost comes from the lossy transform and the authored convention needed to act as one. The two costs are in different currencies and cannot be summed. The empirical handle is lineage: convergent derivation and adopted import are identical in contents, so only the record of origin separates a genuinely diverse population from one frame wearing many names.

This rescues the part of the folk question that survives: component difference helps when it is difference of evidence ranged against a described residual, and hurts when it is difference of name without a shared reading or when the convention that supplies that reading is allowed to decide the verdict. It does not produce the name of an optimal mixture. That remains the `gap` from F9.
