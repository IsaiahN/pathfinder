# Outreach draft - check the shape before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape and its refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` have survived a second-seat read. The stage-1 claim must be checkable on its own or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about diversity and collective performance, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the conditions, not `grounded`):** "diversity among components" is not one quantity. The quantity that governs the outcome is *independence*, defined as how little the components draw on the same evidence. One quantity does two jobs. Low overlap between the frames lets them be ranged separately against a ground that does not move when they push on it, which is what makes a population more than one frame. The same low overlap removes the shared reading a joint action needs, so coordination has to be supplied by an authored, revisable convention across the seam, and that convention must never be allowed to decide which frame was right.
>
> So the answer to "when does diversity help?" is conditional, not scalar. Against a stated residual and a non-updating ground:
>
> 1. Difference of closure and evidence buys resilience, but only if the next room differs from the last and is picked against the residual the last one surfaced. Correlated frames are one frame wearing several names.
> 2. Difference with no described residual is an undirected union and is mute. There is nothing to aim it at.
> 3. Coordination is a convention, not a grounded claim. It is revisable and carries no verdict weight. The moment it decides an outcome it has stopped being a convention.
> 4. The resilience benefit and the coordination cost are in different currencies and must not be added into one optimal-diversity number.
>
> **What would refute it.** I wrote these down before sending this:
> 1. A high trait-variance score with zero evidence-independence produces a real, per-slot surplus of collective reach after the ground changes.
> 2. Frames correlated at origin retain a per-slot resilience advantage after the ground changes.
> 3. A maximally independent set coordinates a joint action with no convention and no described residual and still improves its ground reading.
> 4. A homogeneous closure adds a genuinely new atom by internal composition alone.
> 5. A single additive payoff over heterogeneity predicts both the resilience benefit and the coordination cost across two different residuals.
>
> If any holds, the shape is wrong at that point and I will narrow or withdraw it. A one-line "this fails because..." is far more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I can name the optimal mixture for a given system. The framework returns a shape, not a name. I am not claiming a scalar optimal-diversity value, not claiming that coordination costs are mere friction, and not claiming any of this is settled by experiment. It is a `specified`, ungrounded result. I am also not asking you to trust the source below.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the public sources. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Hong and Page, "Groups of diverse problem solvers can outperform groups of high-ability problem solvers," PNAS 101(46), 2004, full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC528939
> - May, "Will a Large Complex System be Stable?", Nature 238, 1972, abstract: https://www.nature.com/articles/238413a0
> - ACM Collective Intelligence conference series (public submission and proceedings): https://ci.acm.org/
>
> **If you want to use the method:** the formula is public at the figures repository. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Recording the verdict

Before any link is shown, record in the packet copy:

- the date and the venue through which the claim was sent;
- the recipient's stated failure condition, verbatim;
- whether they fired any of R1 to R9 from `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`;
- the stage-1 verdict, before any framework material was disclosed.

Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration. Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." A Stage 3 reply is a conversation, not a witness.
