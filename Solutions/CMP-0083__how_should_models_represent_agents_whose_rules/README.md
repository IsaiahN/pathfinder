# Tether outreach : figure-grounded package

**Question:** How should models represent agents whose rules, goals, or access to information differ across time and context?

**Source:** complexity science.txt line 102 (question 83, section 9: Modeling, measurement, and reproducibility).

**Mode:** `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on which specific rule occupies a slot at a step. Nothing here is `grounded`; no measurement of a model or its target system was taken.

**Status:** done.

**Registry status:** pending.

**Boundary.** Nothing in this folder is in any other repository. No framework file was edited. Sources used were the canonical local figure text at `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`, the canonical figures README, `THE_FORMULA.md`, the seat map, the eight essays, and the public web pages listed in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

**Simulated derivation notice.** Every substantive file below is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. Per *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, LLM output is "simulacra" and "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." This package cannot settle the question it reports on.

## Restating the question as an effect

The decision is what a model writes down when its agents differ and keep changing. Option A writes one fixed agent type, or a representative agent, and pools the results. Option B keeps a per-agent, per-step index and encodes each rule, goal and information set as a typed relation over a shared library. What each leads to: A throws away the only signal that says where to look when an agent changes (Fig 1 :19, :22, :28) and cannot say which rule governs at a step. B preserves the live slot residual, costs more to state, and still cannot name the governing rule without a ground discriminator (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134). The answer returned here is a **shape plus a separator**, not a modeling language and not a fitted model.

The sought-for shape is bound as an ordinary term before the run, per the formula's Direction rule: *an answer* here means (i) a per-slot indexing discipline, (ii) a typed encoding of rule/goal/information, (iii) a re-grounding rule at each context shift, and (iv) an honest `gap` on rule identity. That binding is what the pre-registered refuter in `01` tests.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with this shape.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **A room does not upgrade the label.** Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Adding modelers raises the vocabulary, not the anchor.
2. **Selecting for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation.** Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". A list ranked by likely agreement is a list selected to agree.
3. **Asking for trust before checking spends the only thing a reader contributes.** Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, never the framework.

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one.

## Suggested next step

Take `01` and `08` to the public venues in `03`. The people who can help are those with a public, checkable record of contact with agent heterogeneity and an opt-in channel, positioned to refute the shape rather than endorse it.
