# Outreach draft: check the shape claim before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are stable. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one claim about constitutional checks before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual.** A constitution has to do two things at once. It must stop a government from drifting into self-serving abuse, and it must leave the government able to act. Those two conditions overlap in the same institutions, so a lever that fixes one can break the other. That is the whole problem I am asking about.
>
> **The shape claim (`specified`, not `grounded`).** An effective constitutional check is a relation in which the checker ranges against a ground the checked party cannot move, draws on evidence the checked party does not author, and is satisfiable by a real office without zeroing the government's capacity to act. Three consequences follow. A check that updates on the government's own account of its conduct is a mirror, not a check. A check that no real office can satisfy produces paralysis, not accountability. A checking chain with perfect procedure and no pressure from the result of government action runs in place.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming the framework names the best constitution. It returns a shape, not a name. Whether judicial review, a constitutional court, parliamentary sovereignty, federalism, or some combination achieves that shape in a given polity is a question for evidence I do not have here. I am also not claiming that more checks are always better; past a point they buy gridlock.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written down before this message):**
> 1. An effective check whose anchor changes its answer because the checked party thinks differently, with no loss of corrective force.
> 2. Two checkers that correct a government while drawing on exactly the government's own evidence and no independent source.
> 3. A check no real office could satisfy that nevertheless prevents abuse while the government still governs.
> 4. A checking chain with zero pressure from the result that nevertheless makes progress against the residual.
>
> If any of these holds, I withdraw or re-census the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the public sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figures with line numbers (local canonical file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: local `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight essays that ground "shape": see `05_what_shape_means.md` for the list and quotes
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the framework files. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> [your name]

## Recording the verdict before theory is shown

Stage 1 asks what the recipient would count as a failure, and records their answer. Stage 2, opt-in only, offers the links. Their later agreement with the framework is never pooled as corroboration, because by then they hold the frame's interior (Fig 2 :40, Fig 10 :42). A disagreement is copied into `11_audit.md` with provenance and re-evaluated by the auditor seat as if it came from any other source.
