## 0. The question under audit

> What kinds of constitutional checks prevent the abuse of power without allowing governments to become paralyzed?

Stated as an effect: the constitution is a checking arrangement that must satisfy two constraints at once. The first is that the governing frame cannot correct itself out of an anchor (abuse). The second is that the checking arrangement cannot remove the government's capacity to act (paralysis). The bound ordinary term is stated in `README.md` and repeated in §2.

**What an answer would look like (bound before the run):** a check that ranges against a ground the checked party cannot move, draws on evidence the checked party does not author, and is satisfiable by a real office without zeroing the capacity to act. If this term is wrong, the refuter in §4 fires.

## 2. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity: two.** The residual has two conditions (check strength, action capacity) that must hold at once. By Fig 9 :55, *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* A single lever clears the region where only one condition binds and leaves the intersection standing (Fig 9 :56). This is a **union compound**, not one hard question and not two independent questions.
- **Symmetry: mixed.** A check can be a symmetric relation (two offices mutually veto each other, which is collapse 1 mutual update under another name) or an ordered relation (a checker ranges against a ground it cannot move). Fig 12 :59 (Operators table :15) gives the test for the symmetric case: *"swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No."* The ordered case fails that test. Symmetry is therefore the diagnostic, not a decoration.
- **Scale: individual office to institution to polity.** The membrane (Fig 4) carries a method up and a head start down, never a recording. What works for one office must be re-derived for a branch, not replayed.

### One line per domain (actors / currency / anchor)

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | nameable? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 Constitutional structure (separation of powers, judicial review, independent audit)** | legislature, executive, judiciary, constitutional court, auditor | legal authority, jurisdiction, rulings | a constitution treated as a ground the branches cannot update at will | yes, the Constitution, a court, an audit record |
| **D2 Electoral and democratic accountability** | citizens, representatives, press, candidates | votes, mandates, public record | the election outcome as a counter that does not move because the government thinks otherwise | yes, the count, the published record |
| **D3 Institutional design and anti-paralysis engineering** | office holders, designers, veto players | veto points, action cost, transaction cost | the capacity to act (the ground's pressure on the chain) | yes, a government that can still pass and execute a decision |
| **D4 Naming a specific constitution (the stop)** | constitution drafters, comparativists | institutional names | none available inside the figures | **no nameable anchor inside the figures** |

D4 is not yet a domain in the strong sense: with no nameable anchor it cannot be settled here. It is recorded as the honest stop (Fig 9 :32, Figures README 'a shape, not a name').

### Partition or union

D1, D2 and D3 are **a union of overlapping conditions**, not a partition. They share actors (a court is also an accountable office) and they share the anchor requirement. The compound is D1 and D2 and D3 together, which is why it needs its own loop (Fig 9 :54). D4 is the residue after the union is described.

### Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. Describe R in effect terms (Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*).
2. Settle the anchor question (D1, D2) before the satisfiability question (D3): a check that does not range against a real anchor is not a check, so tuning its veto power is effort above the break (Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*).
3. Refuse before mint (Formula step 2): the terms "absolute veto" and "check that decides the outcome" are refused before any new institution is proposed.
4. Stop at D4 and report shape, not a name.

## 3. Findings

### F1: An effective check must be an anchor that does not update (decisive for the anti-abuse half)

Fig 2 :29:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**

Fig 2 :33 and :35:

> **"The anchor must not update."**
> **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

Fig 2 :34 names the failure:

> **"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."**

Applied to D1: a court, auditor or legislature that updates its view of its own power on the government's view of that power has become another frame. The result is not a check but coherent drift, the abuse the question asks to prevent, executed by the checking apparatus itself. The constitutional anchor (a written constitution, a published ruling, an audit record) plays the role of Fig 2 :24-27's pinned anchor: it does not update on the branches and it is not a third party to be voted on.

**Refuter:** show an effective check whose ruling changes because the checked party thinks differently about the ruling, with no loss of corrective power. Fig 2 :33 makes this impossible by definition; a real case would break the figure, not the reading.

**Status: `triangulated`.** Fig 2 (alignment collapses) and Fig 10 :42-43 (verifier non-access) are two figures with different offices and low overlap:

> **"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."** (Fig 10 :42)
> **"Soundness requires the checker to be unable to produce what it is checking."** (Fig 10 :43)

A checker that can produce the government's own account cannot check it. Madison's *Federalist No. 51* (1788) corroborates from political theory: *"the great difficulty lies in this: you must first enable the government to control the governed; and in the next place oblige it to control itself."* He proposes *"its several constituent parts may, by their mutual relations, be the means of keeping each other in their proper places."* Note the strain handle: Madison's "mutual relations" reads like collapse 1 if taken as mutual update; re-census below (§7) resolves it as mutual **range against a fixed constitution**, which is why the same sentence survives.

### F2: Checks must draw on independent evidence, not the government's own report

Fig 2 :31:

> **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."**

Fig 2 :40:

> **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**

Fig 8 :47:

> **"Independence is one quantity read twice."**

Applied to D1 and D2: a check is real only to the degree its evidence does not come from the checked frame. This is why an independent audit record, a free press, an opposition with its own information, and an electorate that sees more than the government's account matter structurally, not morally. The separation-of-powers literature states the same condition in its own terms: a system of checks and balances *"requires a balance of power between the institutions, so that the goals and actions of one are not completely determined by the other (independence); if both institutions were always in agreement by dint of one dominating the other, they would never challenge each other."* (Wikipedia, *Separation of powers*, fetched 2026-11-23). In security engineering the same rule appears as separation of duties: *"Separation of duties (SoD), also known as segregation of duties, is the concept of having more than one person required to complete a task."*

**Refuter:** show two checkers that reach a corrective verdict while drawing on exactly the checked party's evidence and no other source. Under Fig 2 :31 their independence is zero, so their agreement is the pool talking.

**Status: `triangulated`.** Fig 2 (independence) and Fig 8 (surplus) share a definition but govern different quantities (verdict validity vs union surplus); corroborated independently by the political-science and security sources above. Note the overlap warning: the three political sources (Federalist, separation of powers, veto players) are one broad evidence pool, not three witnesses. The genuinely low-overlap witnesses are separation of duties (accounting and information security) and the Swiss cheese model (safety engineering), both below.

### F3: A check must be satisfiable by a real office; an absolute prohibition is paralysis

Fig 10 :53 and :54:

> **"And a prohibition nothing can satisfy is a prohibition stated wrong."**
> **"A rule no real occupant could meet describes an idealisation, not a position. Before enforcing an absolute, ask what would have to be true for a real occupant to meet it."**

Fig 10 :58-59 on the seat's actual stake:

> **"A seat with no stake at all does not exist."**
> **"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"**

Applied to D3: a check that requires a real office to govern without ever exercising judgement is a prohibition nothing can satisfy. It produces paralysis, not accountability. This is the structural reason why an absolute negative over every action fails where a qualified, reviewable power works: the absolute version demands an occupant who can act and never act. The seat-reasoning rule (Seat Map :171) requires declaring the unavoidable stake instead of pretending it away: *"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it, an undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."*

**Refuter:** exhibit a working check that no real occupant could satisfy, where "working" includes the government still governing. If such a check works, Fig 10 :53 is wrong.

**Status: `single-source` for the satisfiability rule (Fig 10 only).** It is strengthened, but not corroborated, by Fig 7 below, which governs a different quantity (chain progress). I keep the verdicts separate per instruction and do not average them.

### F4: Paralysis is the ground's pressure going to zero

Fig 7 :37-39:

> **"Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:"**
> **"the bracket's fidelity across the handoff"**
> **"the ground's pressure on the room"**

Fig 7 :12-13 on the mirror chain:

> **"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"**

Applied to D3: a constitution can have perfect bracket fidelity (every check is procedurally correct, every veto is properly filed) and still run in place if the ground's pressure is zero, that is, if no check is answerable to the result of government action. Conversely a government with strong ground pressure and no fidelity is arbitrary. Paralysis is the first factor without the second, exactly as abuse is the second without the first (collapse 2). Fig 2 :42 prices the balance:

> **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."**

Madison states the same proportionality in 1788: *"The provision for defense must in this, as in all other cases, be made commensurate to the danger of attack."* The security-engineering source states it again: *"Increased protection from fraud and errors must be balanced with the increased cost/effort required."* (Wikipedia, *Separation of duties*). The political-science source supplies the failure mode: *"Some literature claims that any change (in policies or institutional designs) will become more slow and difficult with increases in the number of veto players and/or the distance between them."* (Wikipedia, *Veto Players*, summarising Tsebelis 2002).

**Refuter:** show a checking chain with zero ground pressure that nevertheless makes progress against the residual, or a chain with perfect fidelity and zero pressure that does not run in place. Either breaks Fig 7 :37.

**Status: `triangulated`.** Fig 7 (chain progress) and Fig 2 :42 (proportionality) are different figures with low overlap, one about handoffs, one about buying independence. Corroborated from political science, constitutional theory and security engineering.

### F5: A convention that decides the outcome has stopped being a check

Fig 2 :43, Fig 10 :47 and :48:

> **"Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."**
> **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**
> **"a convention nothing can check is a constant the seat authored, carrying the seat's authority"**

Fig 10 :49:

> **"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."**

Applied to D1: a procedural convention (the ruling is binding, the audit is published) is legitimate because it has no truth value about the world. But if the convention is used to predetermine the substantive result, it has become a verdict and an authored constant. That is the disguised-policy check: it looks like a check and is an election result wrote large. Fig 10 :62-63 adds what a legitimate ruling must carry:

> **"A ruling carries provenance like an atom."**

Constitutional rulings need the residual they answered, the reasoning and the date recorded, so a later reader can locate the error. Provenance does not make a ruling right (Fig 10 :65: *"Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right"*), but without it the check is uncheckable.

**Refuter:** show a convention that determines the substantive outcome and still functions as a convention under Fig 10 :46. The figure denies this by definition.

**Status: `triangulated`.** Fig 2 :43 and Fig 10 :47-48 are two figures with the same rule stated in different offices (guard vs seat prohibition).

### F6: The abuse/paralysis pair is a union compound (correction to my first reading)

Fig 9 :54-56:

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
> **"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**

*The Library of Babel* (essay, fetched 2026-11-23) names the same error: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."*

**Correction recorded visibly.** My first census treated "prevent abuse" and "avoid paralysis" as two separable domains whose verdicts would be kept apart and then summed into a recommendation. That was wrong. They overlap: the same office can be both a check and a veto point. By Fig 9 :55 they form a union compound with its own loop. The corrected census in §2 keeps three domains under the compound and refuses to average their verdicts. This retraction is not erased; it is the reason the union loop exists in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

**Refuter:** show that the two conditions do not overlap anywhere (no institution is ever both a check and a veto point). Then a partition is correct and this correction is withdrawn.

**Status: `triangulated`** (Fig 9 union rule plus the essay's bundle warning; low overlap between a figure about leaving a frame and an essay about search vocabulary).

### F7: The anchor is not a vote among the branches (strain tested, then dissolved)

Fig 2 :46:

> **"The anchor is not a third party to the disagreement and it is not the tie-breaker in a vote."**

At first reading this strains against democratic accountability, where voting is central. **Strain test:** if the line only fits by ignoring elections, it governs another quantity. **Re-census:** in D1 the anchor is the constitution against which branches range, and the branches do not vote on whether it binds them; in D2 the election is itself the anchor, the counter that settles, not a party to a disagreement among frames (Fig 2 :29 names *"a counter"* as an anchor). The line governs the role of an anchor, not the existence of voting. It is not strained after re-census.

This matters because it blocks a common bad answer: "make the checks democratic by letting the checked government choose them." A pool chosen by a participant is an authored room (Fig 10 :73: *"A candidate set chosen by a participant is an authored room however well the selection is reasoned"*). Fig 10 :75-79 requires an unauthored mechanism: a lottery, everyone who qualifies, a set assembled before the question existed, or the enumerated habitat.

**Refuter:** exhibit an anchor that is simultaneously a tie-breaker in a vote among the frames it is supposed to correct and still satisfies Fig 2 :29.

**Status: `single-source`** (Fig 2 :46 for the role, Fig 10 :73-79 for the unauthored pool; same evidence pool, so one witness).

### F8: Checks are habitat actors, not a standing list of good institutions

Fig 11 :43-44 and :48-50:

> **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**
> **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**
> **"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."**
> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**

Applied to D2: a free press, an opposition, an independent court and an election commission are not good in themselves. Their valence is relative to the residual (abuse vs paralysis). A check that is a benefactor to the anti-abuse residual can be a malfactor to the anti-paralysis residual when it becomes an absolute veto. The Swiss cheese model (safety engineering, fetched 2026-11-23) corroborates from an unconnected field: *"lapses and weaknesses in one defense (e.g. a hole in one slice of cheese) do not allow a risk to materialize, since other defenses also exist (e.g. other slices of cheese), to prevent a single point of failure."* It also gives the failure mode: *"The system produces failures when holes in each slice momentarily align."* That alignment is Fig 2's common cause. The source itself warns that the model *"has been subject to criticism that it is used too broadly, and without enough other models or support"*, so I record it as a limit-case analogue, not a proof.

**Refuter:** exhibit a check whose valence is fixed across residuals, that is, a check that is good for anti-abuse and never contributes to paralysis. Fig 11 :50 denies this.

**Status: `triangulated`** (Fig 11 habitat rule plus the safety-engineering analogue, genuinely low overlap).

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* The shape claim in `README.md` is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. An effective check is demonstrated whose anchor changes its answer because the checked party thinks differently, with no loss of corrective force. (Breaks Fig 2 :29, :33.)
2. Two checkers are demonstrated to correct a government while drawing on exactly the government's own evidence and no independent source. (Breaks Fig 2 :31, :40.)
3. A check no real office could satisfy is shown to prevent abuse while the government still governs. (Breaks Fig 10 :53-54.)
4. A checking chain with zero ground pressure is shown to make progress against the residual, or a chain with perfect fidelity and zero pressure is shown not to run in place. (Breaks Fig 7 :37-39.)
5. The anti-abuse and anti-paralysis conditions are shown never to overlap in any institution. (Breaks the union reading, Fig 9 :55.)

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or re-censused. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Build checks as **anchors**, not mirrors: a record the checked party cannot move (Fig 2 :29, :33), with verifier non-access (Fig 10 :42).
2. Buy **independence** where the cost of being wrong is high, not everywhere (Fig 2 :42). Saturating vetoes buys gridlock (veto players source).
3. Make every check **satisfiable** by a real office (Fig 10 :53-54), and keep the **ground's pressure** nonzero (Fig 7 :37-39). Qualified and reviewable powers beat absolute negatives.
4. Treat the pair as a **union compound** (Fig 9 :54-56), so a lever that clears abuse is tested against paralysis before it is called a solution.
5. Enumerate the **habitat** of the specific polity (Fig 11 :43-44); do not import a standing list of good institutions. An actor's valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :50).
6. Report the **stop** honestly: properties, not a name (Fig 9 :32). Which precise constitution to adopt is a ground question for the venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`.

Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Send the generator (the residual, the shape, the refuter), not a copied constitution.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives: effective constitutional checks are anchors with independent evidence, satisfiable by real offices and proportional to the cost of being wrong, arranged as a union compound so that stopping abuse is never bought by zeroing the government's capacity to act. The specific name of a constitution is not given by any figure and is reported as `gap`. No claim here is `grounded`; the derivation is a simulation checked for internal consistency against the verbatim figure text.
