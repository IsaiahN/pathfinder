# The bond, the order, and the union: supporting derivation for constitutional checks

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The main audit establishes that an effective check must be an anchor with independent evidence, satisfiable by real offices and proportional to the cost of being wrong. Two questions remain, and both are about structure rather than properties:

1. What is the **order/bond** difference between a check that corrects and a check that paralyses? The two can share the same actors (a court, a veto, an election), so the difference cannot be in the ingredients.
2. Is the abuse/paralysis pair a **partition** (two separate questions) or a **union compound** (overlapping conditions), and what follows for the search?

Atoms held from the question's own vocabulary: a checking office, a governed office, a rule, a record, an election count, a veto, a review, a sanction. Operators held: the seven bonds plus negation (Fig 12, Operators table). No new primitive is available without import.

Bond candidates already in the framework:

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Why a mutual veto is not a check, in the bond notation

Fig 12 :94:

> **"The arrangement is the substance."**

A mutual absolute veto between two offices is written `A + B` under a symmetric bond, or more accurately `A ≡ B`: the two names are interchangeable, and each office can stop the other. Under the symmetric test (Fig 12 :59), swapping operands changes nothing. That is exactly the failure Fig 2 names as collapse 1:

> **"collapse 1: mutual update"** (Fig 2 :4)

and collapse 2:

> **"collapse 2: common cause"** (Fig 2 :6)

When the two offices range only on each other, they converge on whoever had the most influence and the drift is coherent. The veto arrangement *looks* like two checks and is one frame with two names. The "check" has no anchor, so it satisfies the letter of the checking requirement and none of its substance.

An anchor check is written with an **ordered** bond: `governed → checker ⋛ record`. The checker comes after the action (sequence), and its ruling is a comparison of the action against a record that it does not author. Swapping the operands produces a different thing: the record does not review the checker. The arrangement, not the ingredients, is what makes it a check.

**Result:** the difference between correcting and paralysing checks is a bond difference, not an actor difference. Two constitutions can hold the same offices and differ only in which bond joins them.

### Q2: Are they isomers, and what would that mean here?

Fig 12 :117:

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fig 12 :124:

> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

- If a correcting constitution and a paralysing constitution are written over the **same atom set** (courts, legislature, executive, electorate, record, veto, review) and differ only in the bonds (ordered anchor check vs symmetric mutual veto), then they are **isomers** under Fig 12's definition. Same atoms, different bond, different behaviour. The formula that lists the offices cannot tell them apart, which is exactly why a list of institutions is not an answer.
- If they are written over different atom sets (one has a constitutional court, the other has none), they are not isomers. They are two members of the same **functional group**, the group "a checking arrangement with some independent office." The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member.
- The stronger claim worth testing is the isomer one, because it is checkable: hold the institutions fixed and vary only whether the check ranges against an unmovable record or updates on the government.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a correcting and a paralysing constitution cannot be written over the same atom set, or if the only difference in their behaviour comes from adding or removing an actor rather than from changing the bond, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands.

### Q3: The union is not a partition

Fig 9 :54-56:

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
> **"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**

The abuse and paralysis conditions overlap: the very office that anchors the government (a court with review power) can be the veto point that stops it governing. A lever that only increases check strength clears the anti-abuse region and leaves the intersection standing, where the second condition still binds. That is why the main audit refuses to average the domain verdicts and instead keeps three domains under one compound.

Order by dependency (Fig 3 :19):

> **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**

Settling the anchor question (does the check range against a ground it cannot move?) comes before tuning veto power. Tuning an absolute veto on a mirror check is effort spent above a break: the mirror cannot use it.

### Q4: Analogues as corrections, not proofs

Two low-overlap analogues were opened. They are **not** figure lines and cannot overrule them. Both were used in `01` as corroboration; here I record exactly what they can and cannot carry.

1. **Separation of duties (accounting and information security).** *"Separation of duties (SoD), also known as segregation of duties, is the concept of having more than one person required to complete a task."* This is the atom set: more than one office. Its own sentence *"Increased protection from fraud and errors must be balanced with the increased cost/effort required"* is the proportionality rule of Fig 2 :42 in another currency. What it does **not** supply is the bond: SoD can be implemented as a symmetric mutual veto and paralyse, so the analogue alone is insufficient. That is a genuine limit, recorded rather than smoothed.
2. **Swiss cheese model (safety engineering).** *"lapses and weaknesses in one defense (e.g. a hole in one slice of cheese) do not allow a risk to materialize, since other defenses also exist (e.g. other slices of cheese), to prevent a single point of failure."* This corroborates the value of multiple independent imperfect barriers. But the same source says failures occur when *"holes in each slice momentarily align"*, which is Fig 2's common cause, and warns the model *"has been subject to criticism that it is used too broadly"*. It is an analogue with a stated limit, not a proof.

### Q5: Refusal operator is required

Operators table :57-58:

> **"¬"**, negation, *"this does not hold"*, and *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*.

A checking vocabulary that only adds procedures (more committees, more signatures, more review stages) can correct and accumulate, and it cannot carry the correction that a particular check is wrong for this residual. The design needs `¬`: the standing to refuse a check that has become a mirror or an absolute veto. In `01` F3 and F5 this is the refusal of the absolute veto and of the convention that decides the outcome. Nothing is deleted from the constitutional record; what falls is the standing of the refused check to be composed over (Fig 6 :42-43).

## CONCLUSION

The correcting check and the paralysing check are distinguished by **bond, not by atom**. A symmetric bond (`A ≡ B`, mutual veto, two names for one frame) produces coherent drift or gridlock; an ordered bond (`governed → checker ⋛ record`) produces a check that ranges against a ground the checked party cannot move. Over a shared atom set they are a real **isomer** claim with a stated falsifier; over different atom sets the weaker **functional group** claim stands. The abuse/paralysis pair is a **union compound**, so a single lever cannot clear both. Refusal (`¬`) must be available, because a vocabulary of procedures can only accumulate.
