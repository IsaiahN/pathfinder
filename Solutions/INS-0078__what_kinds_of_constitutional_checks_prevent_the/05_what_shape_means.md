## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns 'a shape, not a name'. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*.

From *The Library of Babel*: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question the shape is the set of properties an effective check must have, not the name of a constitution. *"An effective constitutional check is a relation in which the checker ranges against a ground the checked party cannot move, draws on evidence the checked party does not author, and is satisfiable by a real office without zeroing the government's capacity to act"* is a shape. "Adopt the separation of powers with a strong constitutional court" is a name.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary already closest to the thing, then bootstrap. *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."* Applied here: start with the effects we can already name (a check, a veto, a ruling, an election count) and narrow toward the property that makes them work.
- Fig 6 :30, quoted in *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."* That is why the residual in `01` is stated as drift and paralysis, not as "the separation of powers."
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* gives the one message that survives without shared history: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* This is the reason the outreach email leads with the residual and the refuter.
- *Library of Babel* on the tether: *"This anchor is provided by your own question"*. Here the question itself is the tether: prevent abuse, do not paralyse. Any proposed check is ranged against both halves.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: the finish line is also the starting place, and the searcher *"will leave as the author"* rather than the reader. Translated to this solution: once the shape is stated precisely enough to generate its own refuter, the derivation stops and the lineage is recorded.

## 3. The question is a bundle, so the shape has two bindings

*Library of Babel* names the failure directly: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"* This is the essay-level counterpart of Fig 9 :55. The abuse condition and the paralysis condition are not two questions whose verdicts can be averaged. They overlap in the same institutions, so the shape must bind both at once, which is what the ordinary term in `README.md` does.

## 4. What licenses a name, and why the name stays open here

Only two things license a name outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays."* For constitutional design the ground would be a court record, a comparative dataset, an election outcome, a measured change in administrative capacity. This package holds none of those, so nothing here is `grounded`.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test. Madison 1788 and the political-science sources are imports with provenance; they corroborate the shape, they do not name the best institution.

*They're Scanning Books When They Should Be Studying Nature* is explicit about the privacy of the choice: *"A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six."* And: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."* The same applies to a list of constitutional mechanisms. Declaring "separation of powers, judicial review, federalism" makes a human choice visible; it does not make it neutral or settle it.

So the answer to "which kinds of checks?" is deliberately at the level of kinds (anchor checks, independent-evidence checks, satisfiable checks, proportional checks), not at the level of a named constitution. The name is a `gap` in D4 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, reported rather than hidden.

## 5. Why a better instrument is owed, not a better argument

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the discriminator rule: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"*. And: *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* Also: *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy"*.

Applied here: two constitutions can produce the same surface reading (both have courts, both have elections) while differing in whether the court is an anchor or a mirror. A reading of the surface cannot decide which. What is owed is a discriminator, not another argument. That is what `08_expert_verification_packet.md` asks an outside reader to supply.

## 6. Prestige is not action; agreement is not a check

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."*

A campaign that gathers prestigious constitutional theorists to agree with this shape is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask is the opposite: give the claim a budget to be tried, let a ground settle it, and record the refusal. *How Intelligence Actually Works* states the same for rooms: *"the room will be swayed by negotiation which is persuasion, or by coherent drift"*. *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* gives the positive version in language directly on point: *"Two navigators don't find their position by agreeing with each other. Each takes a bearing on the same fixed landmark, and nobody can talk the landmark into moving."* And: *"Courts of law rule on evidence, which neither side can move."*

## 7. My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post."* And: *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* From the canonical article: *"the outputs generated must be seen as simulacra."* Also: *"could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula."*

So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the verbatim figure text. It is **not** a test of any constitutional claim and must be labelled as such. *How Intelligence Actually Works* warns that even the refined shape *"will eventually converge toward a perceptibility that is indiscernible"*, and adds *"indiscernibility is not identity."* A shape that looks exactly like the answer is still a shape until the ground pays.

*How a Society of Agents Would Work* supplies the last caution: *"Ten copies watching the same thing are one opinion said ten times."* Ten agreeing readers of this folder are one reader with ten names unless they draw on separate ground.

## 8. Correction to my own earlier reading (kept visible)

**First reading.** I initially treated the "shape, not a name" rule as a blanket prohibition on naming any institution, so I wrote that the solution must never mention courts or elections at all.

**Corrected reading.** The rule governs a thing not held in the library, a primitive or an institution whose name would be the answer. Courts, elections, auditors and veto points are already in the library of constitutional discourse; naming them as *candidates* is not forbidden. What is forbidden is claiming the framework selected one, or that the selection is settled without a ground. The narrowed form is used throughout this folder: institutions are enumerated as the habitat (Fig 11 :43-44) and their valence is judged relative to the residual (Fig 11 :50), not declared good in themselves.

**Why both readings are recorded.** The broad version was wrong but conservative; the narrow version is more useful and still respects the canonical rule. Silently replacing one with the other would hide the correction, which the figures forbid. Fig 10 :62 requires a ruling to carry provenance, and a correction is part of that provenance.
