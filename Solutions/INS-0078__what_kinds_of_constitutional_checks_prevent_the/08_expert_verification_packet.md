# Expert verification packet: the Tether on constitutional checks and paralysis

**What this is:** a claim in a form a constitutional scholar, political scientist or institutional designer can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** No court record, comparative dataset or election outcome is claimed as a measurement. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

An effective constitutional check is a relation in which the checker ranges against a ground the checked party cannot move, draws on evidence the checked party does not author, and is satisfiable by a real office without zeroing the government's capacity to act. A check that updates on the government's own account of its conduct is a mirror, not a check. A check that no real office can satisfy produces paralysis. A chain with perfect procedure and no pressure from results runs in place. The anti-abuse and anti-paralysis conditions overlap, so they form a union compound that must be tested together, never averaged. The framework returns these properties, not the name of a constitution.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 2 and Figure 10

- **Statement:** a check whose anchor updates on the checked party is not a check.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29, *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :33, *"The anchor must not update."* Fig 2 :35, *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 10 :42, *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*
- **What to check:** is the constitutional court, auditor or review body positioned so that its ruling is a comparison against a record it does not author, or does its view of its own power move with the government's? The political-theory corroboration is Madison, *Federalist No. 51*: *"you must first enable the government to control the governed; and in the next place oblige it to control itself."*
- **Refuter:** exhibit a check that remains effective while its anchor changes its answer because the checked party thinks differently, with no loss of corrective force. If one exists, Fig 2 :29 and :33 are wrong for this domain.

### C2: independence and satisfiability; checkable by reading Figure 2 and Figure 10

- **Statement:** checks must draw on evidence the checked party does not author, and must be satisfiable by a real office.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :31, *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Fig 2 :40, *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Fig 10 :53, *"And a prohibition nothing can satisfy is a prohibition stated wrong."* Fig 10 :54, *"A rule no real occupant could meet describes an idealisation, not a position."*
- **What to check:** do the checking offices have at least one source of information the government does not control? Can each check be executed by an actual person without that person needing to act and refrain from acting at once? The security-engineering corroboration is separation of duties: *"Increased protection from fraud and errors must be balanced with the increased cost/effort required."*
- **Refuter:** two checkers that correct a government while drawing on exactly the government's own evidence and no other source; or a check no real office could satisfy that still prevents abuse while the government governs. Either breaks this sub-claim.

### C3: the union compound and the anti-paralysis condition

- **Statement:** abuse and paralysis are overlapping conditions, and paralysis is the ground's pressure going to zero while procedural fidelity stays intact.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55, *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 7 :37, *"Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:"* Fig 7 :38, *"the bracket's fidelity across the handoff"* and Fig 7 :39, *"the ground's pressure on the room"*. Fig 2 :42, *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*
- **What to check:** is the same institution ever both the anchor against abuse and a veto point against action? If so, the two conditions overlap and cannot be averaged. Does the checking chain remain answerable to the result of government action? The political-science corroboration is Tsebelis's veto players: *"any change (in policies or institutional designs) will become more slow and difficult with increases in the number of veto players and/or the distance between them."*
- **Refuter:** show the two conditions never overlap in any institution (a partition is then correct), or show a checking chain with zero ground pressure that still makes progress against the residual. Either breaks this sub-claim.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that any particular constitution is best, and no name of an institution is licensed by the figures.
- Not that more checks are better, or that all vetoes are defects.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement is claimed; this is a `specified` shape.
- Not that agreement among scholars would corroborate it. Shared evidence pools disqualify agreement as a verdict (Fig 2 :40).
- Not that the political sources decide anything. They are secondary witnesses; the figures decide.
- Not a claim about any real polity. Whether a given country's checks are anchors or mirrors is a ground question requiring evidence this packet does not hold.

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminator, not an argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy"*, and *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* Here:

1. **Anchor test:** does the reviewing office's ruling change when the government's own account changes?
2. **Independence test:** does at least one checker hold evidence the government does not author?
3. **Satisfiability test:** can a real office perform the check without ceasing to be able to act?
4. **Pressure test:** is any check answerable to the result of government action?

A clean failure on any of the four refutes the corresponding sub-claim.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29, :31, :33, :35, :40, :42 and Fig 10 :42, :53-54. Eight short passages.
2. Check C1 to C3 against one constitution or one institutional design you know well. Name the office, its record and its source of evidence.
3. Apply the four tests in §5 to that design and say which pass and which fail.
4. Send the failure. A refutation is more useful than an endorsement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
