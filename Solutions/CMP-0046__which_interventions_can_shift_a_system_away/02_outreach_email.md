# Outreach draft: check the shape before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the refuter below is stated in the recipient's own terms. If the first contact carries the framework interior instead of the residual, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one claim about interventions near a tipping point before you decide about the theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the predictions, not `grounded`):** there is no standing list of interventions that are safe near a tipping point. An intervention's valence is relative to the residual it is applied to. So the only defensible answer to "which interventions" is a shape any acceptable intervention must have:
>
> 1. Its habitat has been enumerated outward until the cascade stops mattering, not just to first-order contact. The figure's own example is DDT reaching the eagles three steps out.
> 2. Its effect is measured per slot, because a global system indicator can read near zero while one downstream slot carries a live residual. An average is a budget filter, not a verdict.
> 3. The two requirements, move the system and do no harm elsewhere, are treated as a union that must be unbundled. One lever clears the first condition and leaves the intersection standing.
> 4. Because you can add an actor but cannot subtract the habitat, it is permanent monitoring work, not a solved state. Moving the system elsewhere is habitat substitution, and you inherit actors you did not choose.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that this tells you the name of an intervention in your system. It does not. The framework returns a shape, not a name. A system-specific intervention has to be imported from the ground, a measurement, an experiment, a dataset, with its provenance shown. "We found no candidate" is not "no candidate exists".
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. A global metric is shown to preserve every slot-level residual relevant to harm at all stated horizons.
> 2. An intervention is demonstrated against an independent ground to move a system away from a threshold with zero residual in every enumerated downstream slot and no ongoing maintenance.
> 3. An introduced actor's harm can be subtracted from the habitat without substituting a habitat, verified at the ground.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the figures and the derivation are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - The formula and mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - The canonical figures README (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Public figure set: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** run the loop on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - [name]

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Shape claim and refuter first, theory second | `Fig 10 :52` "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Each frame is corrected by the ground separately (`Fig 2 :45` "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."). |
| Do not ask for agreement | `Fig 2 :35` "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." |
| Pre-registered refuter | A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device. |

| No named intervention | The framework returns "a shape, not a name" (canonical figures README); `Fig 9 :32` "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". |
| Enumeration to cascade horizon | `Fig 11 :44` "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." |

| No request for trust or prestige | *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (2026-11-19): "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it." |

## Stage discipline

- **Stage 1:** recipient checks the shape claim and its refuter, records a verdict, and names what they would count as a failure.
- **Stage 2, opt-in only:** after that verdict is recorded, offer the theory. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because shared evidence makes agreement worthless as a verdict (`Fig 2 :40`).
- **Stage 3, also opt-in:** offer the formula as a method to reuse. Each new user must name their own ground and write their own refuter before running it.

All three stages are conversations, not a campaign. One recipient at a time.
