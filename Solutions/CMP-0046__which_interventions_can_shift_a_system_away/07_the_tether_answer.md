# The Tether's answer: which interventions can shift a system away from a tipping point without harmful side effects elsewhere?

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the per-domain predictions, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a validation procedure, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A system sits near a critical threshold. Two conditions bind at once: move it away from the threshold, and leave no harm in another slot or at a later time. This is a **union compound**, not one optimization with a side constraint. `Fig 9 :55` "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." A single lever clears the region where only the first condition holds and leaves the intersection standing.

## 2. The shape

1. **No standing list of safe interventions.** An intervention's valence is relative to the residual, not carried by the intervention (`Fig 11 :49-51`). So the answer to "which interventions" is a rule for validating a candidate, not a roster.
2. **Enumerate the habitat outward.** `Fig 11 :44` "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." The DDT example (`Fig 11 :52`) shows the harm that mattered was three steps out. State the horizon.
3. **Measure per slot, not in aggregate.** `Fig 1 :22` "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A global indicator can read near zero with one live slot residual (`Fig 1 :13`). Aggregates are budget filters, never witnesses (`Fig 9 :60`).
4. **Treat the two conditions as a union and unbundle before searching** (`Fig 9 :54`). Then buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong (`Fig 2 :42`), since a tipping-point intervention is high-cost and often irreversible.
5. **Expect permanent work.** `Fig 11 :61` "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." `Fig 11 :63` "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state." Harm cannot be recalled by removing the lever, because the contact has already propagated.
6. **The figures do not name the intervention.** They return a shape and a test. A system-specific name requires an import from the ground, with provenance and a shadow test. `Fig 13 :134` "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a list of named interventions, and not "reduce X by Y percent". That is owed from the ground.
- Not that any intervention is safe because experts agree. `Fig 2 :35` "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
- Not that a global resilience or stability indicator is sufficient. It is a filter.
- Not that the system can be returned to a prior state by undoing the action. Exclusion is not available (`Fig 11 :62`).
- Not that the monitoring task ends when the threshold is crossed away from. The channel degrades even though the ground does not (`Fig 10 :24`).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. **Slot-resolving observation:** an instrument that reports the residual per slot and per horizon, so a third-order effect cannot hide in a global average. Without it, condition 2 of the question is unmeasurable.
2. **Intervention test:** a measured case in which a candidate moves the system away from a threshold and leaves zero residual in every enumerated downstream slot over a stated horizon, with independent frames ranged against the same ground. This is the test that would move a candidate from `candidate` to `grounded`.
3. **Asymmetry test:** a measured demonstration that an introduced actor's harm can be removed without substituting a habitat. This would reverse the cost claim in §2.5.
4. **Standing refuters from the audit:** a global metric preserving every slot residual; a system-specific intervention derived from the figures alone; agreement from one evidence pool surviving an independent instrument. All five are in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §3.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The shape is derived; the name is not. The figures give the form an acceptable intervention must have and the test it must pass, then stop at the limit of what the available instruments distinguish. `Fig 9 :44` "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing." The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. *Indiscernibility is not identity.*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give you a list of safe interventions, and it does not claim one. It says that an intervention's value depends on the residual it is applied to, so no standing list can survive a change of system. What it gives is the shape any acceptable intervention must have: enumerate the habitat outward until the cascade stops mattering, because the harm that counts may be three steps away; measure the result per slot, because an average hides a live downstream signal; treat "move the system" and "do no harm elsewhere" as a union and unbundle them before choosing a lever; buy independent measurement in proportion to the cost of being wrong; and expect permanent monitoring, because you can add an actor but cannot subtract the habitat. The figures do not name the intervention. A system-specific name has to come from the ground, with its provenance shown. What is offered is the shape, the refuter written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The slot stays open until the ground settles it.
