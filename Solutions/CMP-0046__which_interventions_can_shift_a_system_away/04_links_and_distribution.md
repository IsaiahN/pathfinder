## 2. What the wider literature contributes

All of the following are secondary corroboration. None overrules a figure line.

1. **Why a single lever produces side effects.** Meadows' leverage-point hierarchy and *Unintended consequences* both say that the intuitive point of intervention is often the wrong one. Meadows: "Counterintuitive. That's Forrester's word to describe complex systems." This corroborates `Fig 3 :19` (optimising a working link cannot rescue a broken one) and `Fig 9 :55` (a single lever leaves the intersection standing).
2. **Why first-order scoping fails.** The DDT example in `Fig 11 :52` is mirrored by *Cascading failure* (load shifts to neighbours until they fail) and by climate tipping cascades. These are three low-overlap fields giving the same structural prediction.
3. **Why aggregate indicators cannot be the verdict.** Earth System Dynamics' "information loss inherent in commonly used aggregation approaches" corroborates `Fig 1 :22` at a completely different scale.
4. **Why naming a source from a reading is unsafe.** *The Hard Problem is About Source* gives metamerism: a reading does not determine the source. Applied here, a system-level improvement reading does not determine which slot improved or what caused it. The remedy is a better instrument, not a name.
5. **Why the first contact must be an effect description.** *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: a plain description of what you would expect to see can be checked by anyone against the world. This is the world-side reason for leading with the residual and the refuter.

No world source conflicts with the figure verdict. The conflict rule was therefore not triggered. Most of the essay sources share an author with the figures, so their agreement carries little independent weight; the Wikipedia and journal sources are the genuine low-overlap witnesses.

## 3. Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded rules:

- `Fig 10 :52` "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator.
- `Fig 9 :27-28` "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them anything else.
- `Fig 2 :35` "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Never ask for agreement.
- `Fig 4 :22` "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." The formula is a method, so giving it away does not give away an answer.
- `Fig 2 :42` "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." One-to-one contact with people whose public work touches intervention side effects, not a list selected for receptiveness.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its three refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) record their verdict; (4) only then offer the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## 4. The two-stage offer

- **Stage 1:** the recipient checks the shape claim and refuter against their own ground. Their verdict is recorded before any theory is shown.
- **Stage 2, opt-in:** theory offered only after that verdict. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (`Fig 2 :40` "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool").
- **Stage 3, opt-in:** the formula offered as a method to reuse. Each user writes down their own ground and their own refuter before running it, because a frame "cannot check the direction of its own question" (`THE_FORMULA.md`, "Direction").

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 claim is checkable. As written it is: the three refuters are stated in effect terms, and no named intervention is asserted.
