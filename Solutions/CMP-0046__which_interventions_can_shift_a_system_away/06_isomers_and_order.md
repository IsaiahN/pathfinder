# Order, cascade order, and intervention isomers

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The main audit says an intervention must have its habitat enumerated outward and its effects measured per slot. Two supporting questions are owed: (1) in what **order** does an intervention act, and (2) are two candidate interventions that use the same atoms but different arrangements genuinely different things, or just spellings of one?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - What is the order of an intervention?

Two different orders are in play and must not be confused.

1. **Dependency order of the response chain (Fig 3).** `Fig 3 :20` "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it." An intervention applied at the planning link cannot repair a missing objective link.
2. **Cascade order in the habitat (Fig 11).** `Fig 11 :52` "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." Third order is not a harder version of first order; it is a different slot.

`Fig 12 :111` "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." A plan is a sequence (`Fig 12 :112` "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."). So an intervention description that lists only ingredients (target variable, dose, timing) has recorded half of itself. It owes the bond: which contact the action follows and which it produces.

**Re-census note:** the apparent tension between "chain order" and "cascade order" is resolved by noting that Fig 3 orders the *links of a response chain*, while Fig 11 orders the *spread of contact through a habitat*. Different objects, no strain.

### Q2 - Are two interventions isomers?

`Fig 12 :117` "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

Take two candidate interventions over the same atom set: same target variable, same magnitude, same budget. If one is applied by changing a parameter (a subsidy, a harvest quota) and the other by changing the feedback structure (who observes the stock, who can act on it), then by Fig 12 they are isomers, not variants: same atoms, different bond, different substance. The flat formula that lists only the two magnitudes cannot tell them apart, and nothing signals that anything was lost.

This maps onto Meadows' leverage hierarchy: her level 12 (parameters) and level 6 (information flows) can share the same stated goal and budget while being different substances. The world source is corroboration only; the figure line decides.

`Fig 12 :124` "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." Two interventions that both "introduce a monitored feedback loop" share a functional group; which actor carries it decides the member and therefore the side effects. This is why naming the group ("payment for ecosystem services") is not naming the intervention.

### Q3 - Catalyst, cycle and the monitoring institution

- `Fig 12 :119` "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is." A monitoring institution is a catalyst in this sense: it survives its own use and keeps the ground reachable. A one-off survey is a spent part. Treating them as the same thing is the error the figure names.
- `Fig 12 :121` "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." A reinforcing loop (more monitoring leads to more detected harm leads to more intervention leads to more monitoring) closes differently from a balancing one. Whether it closes is decided by the sign, not by the number of parts.
- `Fig 12 :104` "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world." A validated intervention becomes an atom at the next level of management, which is why a wrong validation propagates far beyond its original slot.

### Q4 - Union order before search

`Fig 9 :54` "when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." So the order of operations is: unbundle first, then enumerate each condition's habitat, then recombine. Doing it the other way (pick a lever, then check harm) is exactly the single-lever failure at `Fig 9 :55`.

## FALSIFIER (written before the run)

The isomer and order claims are refuted if:

1. Two interventions with the same atoms but different bond or order produce identical per-slot residual vectors at every enumerated horizon, verified at the ground (breaks the isomer claim at Fig 12 :117).
2. A response chain is shown to be order-insensitive, so an action at the planning link succeeds while the objective link is missing (breaks Fig 3 :20).
3. The third-order residual in a DDT-type case is shown to be a re-description of the first-order residual rather than a new slot (breaks the cascade-order claim at Fig 11 :52).

## CONCLUSION

Intervention description has two orders: dependency order in the response chain and cascade order in the habitat. Same-atoms-different-bond interventions are isomers under Fig 12, so "same target and same magnitude" does not mean "same intervention". Monitoring is a catalyst when it survives its own use. The union must be unbundled before search. These are structural claims (`specified`), not measurements.
