## 2. Wider literature that bears on the question

- **Mathematics / information theory.** Shannon 1948 separates the engineering problem (reproducing a message from a set of possible messages) from semantic questions. This is the reason a channel-capacity number cannot by itself settle which biological information matters. It corroborates F9.
- **Molecular biology.** Cobb 2017 and Haseltine and Patarca 2024 are a productive pair: the first establishes that the dogma was about sequence information (not a general "DNA makes RNA makes protein" slogan); the second documents multidirectional flow, extracellular RNA transfer and epigenetic inheritance. Together they corroborate F3's point that sequence information and selection/heritable state are distinct quantities. They share a molecular-biology evidence pool to a degree, so they are one witness on the direction question, not two.
- **Systems biology / statistical physics.** Lan and Tu 2016 and Ito and Sagawa 2015 give the per-channel machinery: mutual information, working memory, crosstalk, transfer entropy bounds. They corroborate F5 and F9. They also share the E. coli chemotaxis literature, so count as one pool on the mechanism shape.
- **Ecology / evolution.** Laland, Matthews and Feldman 2016 corroborate F10's habitat enumeration and F3's feedback loop. This is the lowest-overlap witness relative to the molecular sources.

**Overlap declaration.** The molecular trio (Cobb, Haseltine/Patarca, and the Pukkila central-dogma entry in the ledger, which was read only through the search extract) is one evidence pool. The two biophysics papers are one pool. The ecology source is a genuinely different pool. The framework essays and the figures share the framework author's evidence pool completely, so their agreement with the verdict is internal consistency, not independent corroboration.
