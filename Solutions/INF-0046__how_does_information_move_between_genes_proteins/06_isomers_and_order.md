## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Is a five-stop chain the right structure? NO (correction to the ordinary reading)

Fig 12 :48:

> **"A molecule is a bonded arrangement. A chain is the degenerate case: one bond type, no branching. Nothing about a chain is a different kind of object."**

Fig 12 :111:

> **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**

A chain is permitted but degenerate. The biological crossings are not one bond type: transcription and translation are **production** (⇒: the product did not exist before the first fires; Operators :39, *"is there a B before A firing? No."*), sensory transduction is a **sequence with feedback** (→ plus a loop), selection is a **comparison** (⋛: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, Operators :50), and rejection of a signal is **negation** (¬, not a bond). A flat chain using only → commits the error the Operators table names at :34: *"the only bond a flat chain can express, so every term today is a sequence whether or not it should be."* So the correct structure is a bonded arrangement with several bond types, not a five-stop sequence.

**Falsifier:** show that all four crossings are faithfully typed by a single ordered bond → with no branching, no loop and no threshold. Then the chain reading is restored and this correction is withdrawn.

### Q2 - What order is licensed?

Fig 3 :20:

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**

Fig 3 :19:

> **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**

Dependency order for this question:

1. **Bond/correspondence first** (D1). Without a typed relation between sequence and product there is no term to carry up. Settled against the sequence correspondence (Cobb 2017).
2. **Cellular input-output second** (D2). The product must be bonded to a receptor and a response before it can count as a signal; the anchor is the measured input-output relation (Lan and Tu 2016).
3. **Multicellular coordination third** (D3). A settled molecule becomes an atom for the organism: Fig 12 :104, *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."*
4. **Environmental and generational loop last** (D4/D5). Selection and niche construction compose over settled organismal terms, not over raw molecules.

This is a dependency order, not a claim that information "arrives" at the environment only after a fixed delay. In real biology the loops run concurrently. The figure licenses the order of composition, not a temporal schedule.

### Q3 - Are the crossings isomers?

Fig 12 :117:

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fig 12 :123:

> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

Take two sensory crossings over the same held atoms `{receptor, ligand, kinase, response}`:

- D2a: a nutrient cue bonded by production and feedback ⇒/→, producing a metabolic response.
- D2b: the same atoms bonded by a threshold comparison ⋛ against a predator cue, producing an avoidance response.

Same atoms, different bond, different behaviour. Under Fig 12 :117 these are **isomers**, and the distinction is invisible in an ingredient list. If instead we remove the atoms and keep only the substructure `state → structure → state`, they belong to the same **functional group**, with the qualifier naming which member (Fig 12 :123).

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if both crossings can be written with the same bond and the same atoms and still produce different settled behaviour, they are not isomers by the figure's definition, and the difference must lie in the habitat or the residual rather than in the bond. The isomer reading then fails and the functional-group reading stands.

### Q4 - What about the cycle?

Fig 12 :121:

> **"cycle. The output re-enters as input."**

The figure adds: *"The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* The environment-to-lineage arrow is selected for by survival: Fig 11 :36, *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran."* The organism-to-environment arrow is niche construction (Laland et al. 2016): organisms modify selective environments, and the modification can persist as ecological inheritance.

These two arrows are not sequence information running protein → nucleic acid. Cobb 2017: *"there is no evidence in any organism that the information in a DNA sequence can be rewritten from information in a protein."* The apparent conflict dissolves by re-census: sequence order and selection pressure are different quantities with different anchors (see `01` F3).

Fig 12 :119:

> **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**

The methylated receptor in chemotaxis (Lan and Tu 2016) is a catalyst-like part in the sense that it persists across uses as working memory, while the transient phosphorylation is spent. A model that records only concentrations cannot tell which is which. This is a structural analogue at the cellular boundary, marked `specified` and not claimed as a figure derivation of any particular molecule.

### Q5 - A live type question, named not hidden

Fig 12 :125-126:

> **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."** **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**

The Operators table defines ⋛ as *"a threshold relation between two quantities."* At the population boundary the "quantities" are frequencies or fitness values, so ⋛ is well typed there. At the cellular boundary the comparison is between a concentration and a receptor threshold, also well typed. At the cross-scale boundary, however, composing a settled molecular term with a settled organismal term requires treating both as quantities at the new level; Fig 12 :104 permits the demotion to atom, but whether the atom counts as a quantity needs an instrument, not an argument. This is recorded as a live strain, not smoothed over.

## CONCLUSION

The four crossings are **bonded arrangements with several bond types**, not one degenerate chain. Their dependency order is bond/correspondence, then cellular input-output, then multicellular coordination, then the environmental and generational loop. Over a shared atom set, two sensory crossings differing only in bond are **isomers** under Fig 12 :117; over the shared functional group `state → structure → state` they are members of one **functional group**. The topology is cyclic, with selection and niche construction as distinct feedback arrows that are not sequence information flowing backwards. One type question remains open (⋛'s operand type when settled molecules serve as quantities at a new level), and one falsifier is registered for the isomer claim.
