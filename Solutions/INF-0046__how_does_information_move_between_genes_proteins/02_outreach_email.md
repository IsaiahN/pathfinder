**Subject:** Check one claim about cross-scale biological information before deciding whether the framework matters

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the phrase "information moves between genes, proteins, cells, organisms and environments" treats information as a substance travelling along a line. What actually crosses a boundary is a physical carrier joined to a receiving process by a typed relation. The relation, not the carrier's ingredient list, is where the correspondence lives. Each crossing is a lossy transform, so what arrives is a method or a head start, never a replay of the occasion that produced it. The topology is a cycle, not a line: selection carries environment to lineage, and niche construction carries organism back to environment, while sequence-order information itself does not run protein to nucleic acid. The anchor that settles whether any of this was functional is the ground (a measured input-output relation, or fitness), which does not move when a description of it changes.
>
> **What the claim explicitly does not say:** that there is a single quantity called "information flow" shared by all five stops; that channel capacity tells an organism which information matters; that the framework derives a list of molecular pathways (transcription, translation, phosphorylation, and so on). Those are bindings from the biology frame onto atoms the framework already holds. They are legitimate provenance, but they are not figure results and they do not "move the wall." No figure line returns them, so I record that as an unreached gap rather than invent one.
>
> **What would refute the claim:** I wrote these before this message.
> 1. Recovering the full message at the receiving scale after the typed relation has been destroyed and no new relation supplied.
> 2. Measuring a cross-scale round trip with zero loss in both directions.
> 3. Showing the four crossings form an acyclic chain with no feedback in any regime.
> 4. Showing the supposed ground changes its answer when the observing frame's description changes.
> 5. Showing all four boundaries share one residual that trends down under a single intervention.
>
> If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the figure text and the worked answer are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Full derivation and the retraction log: `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`
> - The synthesized answer and its non-claims: `07_the_tether_answer.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the eight-step loop is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
