**Subject:** One checkable claim about what the CMB pattern can and cannot say

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** A model of the early universe predicts a sky; an instrument returns a sky. Call the gap between them, at one angular scale and one field, the residual. The monopole temperature is a pooled reading and carries none of the pattern. The information is in the roughly one-part-in-100,000 variations and their statistics. An answer has to say what those variations constrain, what they cannot select, and what instrument would close what is left open.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):**
> 1. The pattern is a per-slot signal against an anchor that does not move when a model is believed. It says the early universe was hot and dense, near-flat, and uniform to about one part in 100,000.
> 2. The perturbation statistics are nearly scale-invariant, Gaussian, and adiabatic. The acoustic peak ratios carry that information; individual pixels do not.
> 3. This is a shape and a set of constraints, not a mechanism name. The pattern does not select an inflaton potential, a reheating history, or a first instant. I leave that slot open rather than fill it.
> 4. More sky modes in the same channel shrink error bars but add no new discriminating ingredient. Separating models needs a channel whose evidence is actually different, such as polarization B-modes, 21 cm, or primordial non-Gaussianity.
> 5. Agreement counts only under independence. Two pipelines reducing the same public map are one witness; cross-instrument agreement with shown provenance is more.
> 6. The path from last scattering to the detector is part of the problem. Foregrounds, lensing, and reionization are in contact with the pattern and cannot be scoped away by assuming a pristine photograph.
>
> This is a shape and a route, not a result. I am explicitly not claiming it is `grounded`, and I am not claiming the framework settles whether inflation happened.
>
> **What would refute it (written before this message was sent):**
> 1. the monopole alone carrying all pattern information with no loss of prediction;
> 2. search inside one channel adding a genuinely new discriminating ingredient, not a tighter estimate;
> 3. an instrument specified for a signal that has never registered at any resolution, with no prior failing reading to improve from;
> 4. two pipelines sharing one evidence pool shown to supply independent corroboration;
> 5. a checkable rule that names the specific early-universe mechanism from the shape alone.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures text verbatim with line numbers: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`.
> - Figures and formula: `FRAMEWORK/figures/`, with the usage rule at `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`.
> - Literature I actually read: the ledger in `10_source_ledger.csv`, including the COBE and Planck instrument records, the Planck 2018 parameter paper, and the survey-statistics and underdetermination records.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
