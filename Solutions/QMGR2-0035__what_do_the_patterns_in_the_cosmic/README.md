## Restating the question in effect terms

An instrument returns a sky of temperature and polarization readings. A set of early-universe models predicts what that sky should look like. The question asks what the observed pattern licenses us to say about the earliest expansion. Option A answers with a name: a particular inflaton field, a particular potential, a first instant. Option B answers with a shape plus the constraints plus an honest open slot: a hot, near-flat, statistically uniform expansion that laid down nearly scale-invariant, Gaussian, adiabatic perturbations, with the specific mechanism left to a ground that can discriminate. The figures decide between A and B: they return a kind of thing, never the answer itself (Fig 9 :32). This package takes B.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree that inflation happened.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons in the framework's own words: the sky is an anchor and models range against it separately (Fig 2 :29, Fig 2 :45); a room of agreeing cosmologists is not an anchor (Fig 2 :35, "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."); selecting for likely agreement manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict (Fig 2 :40, "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"). Legally, scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions, a practical failure on top of the structural one.
