## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Is a pixel map a pattern?

- Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
- Fig 12 :46: *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."*
- A temperature map gives pixels (ingredients). The angular power spectrum C_ℓ, the two-point correlation, is a bond between pixels. The acoustic peaks live in that bond, not in any pixel. A study that records only the pixel histogram has recorded half the pattern.
- ⇒ The unit of analysis for this question is a **bonded arrangement**, not a pixel list. A "picture of the early universe" answer commits the flat-formula collapse Fig 12 names.
- **Falsifier:** two skies with identical power spectra but different pixel-level statistics that yield different cosmological conclusions which the power spectrum cannot recover. If that were shown and reproduced, the bond would not be carrying the information and this derivation fails. (The existing anomaly literature on non-Gaussianity is a live test of exactly this: it asks whether higher-order correlations carry information the two-point function misses. A positive detection would narrow this claim, not automatically refute it, because higher-order correlations are themselves bonds.)

### Q2 - Does the dependency order matter?

- Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*
- Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*
- Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
- In this question the middle link is "what would count as a discriminating prediction rather than a retrodiction." A model that is fitted to the same spectrum it was designed to explain has run the chain backwards: the objective was bound after the planning. The literature's warning about the low-multipole anomaly and the psychological desire to find unusual things is what a reverse-order chain looks like from inside.
- Formula step 2 orders refusal before mint: a held claim that a feature is primordial must be refused (foreground, systematics) before a new primordial term is sought. The history of the BICEP2 claim, later attributed to dust, is the case where the refusal should have come first.
- **Falsifier:** a demonstration that the five links can be reordered without loss of out-of-sample prediction, with the discriminating objective bound after model fitting. That would break the dependency claim.

### Q3 - Are adiabatic and isocurvature perturbations isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- Adiabatic perturbations: the fractional overdensity is the same across photons, baryons and neutrinos. Isocurvature perturbations: the fractional variations sum to zero across species. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is decisive in the behaviour: adiabatic peak locations run 1:2:3, isocurvature roughly 1:3:5. The world CMB record states that observations are consistent with entirely adiabatic perturbations, which supports inflation and rules out many defect models.
- **Status:** triangulated for the isomer mapping (Fig 12 definition plus a world source whose evidence pool differs from the figure text in kind, though it reports the same experiments). Single-source for the specific peak-ratio claim, which is reported by one encyclopedia record opened this session.
- **Falsifier:** show that the two perturbation types can be written with the same bond over the same atoms and yet produce distinguishable peak series. Then the difference is not the bond and the isomer claim fails.

### Q4 - Are E-modes and B-modes isomers?

- Fig 12 :96: *"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing."*
- Fig 12 :97: *"They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."*
- E-modes and B-modes are both polarization patterns on the sky and share the same polarization atoms. The bond differs: E is gradient-like (even parity), B is curl-like (odd parity). A map that records only total polarization intensity cannot tell them apart. The difference is decisive: E-modes are produced by Thomson scattering in a heterogeneous plasma and are a factor of ten weaker than temperature; B-modes are not produced by standard scalar perturbations, and are expected from tensor perturbations during inflation or from gravitational lensing of E-modes.
- This is a second instance of Fig 12's isomer rule, and it is the one that matters most for F10: the B-mode channel is a different bond over the same atoms, so it is a candidate for the new discriminating channel that SEARCHED cannot supply.
- **Falsifier:** show that E and B are related by an invertible transform that preserves all cosmological information and that no experiment can separate their sources. Then they are one term, not two substances, and the B-mode program loses its special status.

### Q5 - What order is the union of model and transfer?

- Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."*
- A measurement of the acoustic scale and the spectral index is settled against the sky (as a reading, not as a mechanism). It can therefore serve as an atom for the next level: a model comparison. Fig 13 :110: *"And each composition becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."*
- The primordial signal and the transfer history are each first-order compositions; their convolution is second-order, and is legal only because both are settled readings. This is recursion, not a new primitive, and it is why F7 (the habitat) and F3 (the shape) belong to one loop rather than two unrelated literatures.
- **Strain, named rather than hidden:** Fig 9 :54 says *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* The compound in this question (signal times transfer) is a union. Unbundling is done physically, by component separation and cross-frequency comparison, not by averaging. That is a method from the world frame, provenanced as such, not a figure derivation.
- **Falsifier:** show that the compound cannot be unbundled by any channel and that the two conditions are genuinely one. Then the union reading is wrong and F7 must be re-censused.

## CONCLUSION

The pattern is a bonded arrangement, not a pixel list. Its dependency order runs from perceiving to objective to planning, and the middle link is where the chain breaks when a model is fitted to the data it was built to explain. Adiabatic versus isocurvature perturbations, and E-modes versus B-modes, are two Fig 12 isomer pairs: same atoms, different bond, different substance, with the difference decisive in the observable behaviour. The union of primordial signal and transfer history is second-order and is unbundled by physical channel separation, not by averaging. The derivation survives its stated falsifiers only if a bond, not an ingredient, is shown to carry the difference.
