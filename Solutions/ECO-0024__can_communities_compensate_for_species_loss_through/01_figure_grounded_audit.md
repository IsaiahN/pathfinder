## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

A species disappears from a community. Before the loss, some function or set of functions was being performed at a rate r. After the loss, the observed rate is o. The residual is R_s = |r_s minus o_s| for each function slot s. The question asks whether changing the abundance or behavior of the species that remain can drive R_s back toward zero.

Three properties of R must be fixed before any figure is chosen:

- **Arity.** R is indexed by function slot, not by community total. More than one function can fail at once, and the same remaining species can be a compensator for one slot and a malfactor for another. This is a union compound when the failed functions overlap in the actors that could perform them (Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*).
- **Symmetry.** Compensation is not symmetric. The relation *actor A compensates function f* is relative to f, not a property A carries. Swapping the function changes the verdict.
- **Scale.** A behavioral or abundance change is first-order at the actor, but contact cascades. The effect that matters can be two or three steps out (Fig 11 :52: *"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*).

The question as posed already names a mechanism, abundance or behavior change, before naming which function failed. That ordering is a known hazard: Fig 9 :27 says *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* So the audit describes the residual first and only then asks whether abundance or behavior can compose over it.

An answer would look like this, bound as an ordinary term before the run: **a per-function verdict of the form** *for slot s, the change in remaining species closed R_s, and the ground measurement agrees* **or** *for slot s, R_s persists and the claim fails*. This is the "Direction" discipline from `THE_FORMULA.md`: make the sought-for shape an ordinary term so it can accrue a residual and be refused.

## 1. Domain census, before choosing any figure line

R by arity, symmetry, scale is stated above. One line per domain giving actors / currency / anchor follows. A domain without a nameable anchor is not yet a domain.

### Domain 1: the community as a composition (framework-internal)

- **Actors:** the remaining species as atoms, their interactions as typed bonds, and functional groups as substructures.
- **Currency:** composition inside closure(Γ). A function is a molecule, not an ingredient list; which species performs it and how it is bonded to the states of the habitat both matter.
- **Anchor:** the figures as metalaws for legality of composition, and the ground for settlement. Both are nameable, so this is a domain.
- **Question type:** partition with a union overlap. Which compositions cross from a state to a structure and back is a partition question (Fig 13 :74). When two failed functions compete for the same remaining species, the case is a union compound and must be unbundled before search (Fig 9 :54).

### Domain 2: the habitat and contact

- **Actors:** remaining species, conditions (light, water, temperature, season, terrain), and relations (predation, pollination, competition, dependency).
- **Currency:** contact with the residual. An actor counts only by what it does to R_s.
- **Anchor:** the ground, meaning the measured function. The habitat updates; the ground does not (Fig 11 :73).
- **Question type:** enumeration, not composition. The habitat is read off the world by contact cascades, not assembled from a theory.

### Domain 3: measurement and verification

- **Actors:** field experiments, long-term datasets, instruments, and independent review frames.
- **Currency:** residual versus agreement. Agreement is a budget signal; residual is the verdict signal (Fig 9 :60).
- **Anchor:** a measurement of the specific function per slot. A measurement moves only if the world changes, so it qualifies as an anchor (Fig 2 :29-30).
- **Question type:** triangulation. Two frames with low overlap may both range against the same measurement. Two models using the same dataset are one frame wearing two names.

### Partition or union overall

The three domains form a **union**, not a partition. Compensation is simultaneously a composition question (can the remaining atoms produce the function), a contact question (does the change touch the failed slot), and a settlement question (does the ground agree). A single lever that clears one condition leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*). So each domain gets its own loop and its own verdict, and the verdicts are never averaged.

### Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. **Perception:** index R by function slot. Without this, a live slot residual hides inside a global average.
2. **Vocabulary:** have a term for the function and for the functional group that could perform it. This is the middle link that usually breaks, and a link with no instrument is not a link that works (Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*).
3. **Objective:** say what would count as progress in the community's own terms, per slot, not as a total.
4. **Planning:** enumerate the habitat outward by contact from the failed function.
5. **Learn and carry:** settle each slot against measurement and record the lineage so a later reader can locate the error.

Effort spent above a break cannot rescue a link below it (Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*). A more elaborate abundance model is effort above the break if the slot-level function was never measured.

## 3. Findings F1 to F10

### F1: aggregate compensation hides a live slot residual (decisive for the question's form)

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

A claim that "the community compensated" is normally tested against a total: total biomass, total primary production, total nutrient uptake. That is an aggregate. Fig 1 says the only decision-relevant quantity is per function slot. A community can hold its total biomass steady while pollination, seed dispersal, or nitrogen fixation stays unperformed, because a different species increased in abundance in a different slot. The world literature agrees from a low-overlap direction: the insurance model of Yachi and Loreau (1999) distinguishes a buffering effect on temporal variance from a performance-enhancing effect on the temporal mean, and explicitly ties the result to the degree of asynchronicity of species responses. A stable mean is not the same claim as a restored slot.

**Verdict:** single figure for the structural rule (Fig 1), triangulated with the world insurance model on the distinction between mean and variance. Mode `general`. Refuter: show a case where a slot residual persists after the change while aggregate function is statistically unchanged, and the aggregate claim is refuted for that slot; or show a figure re-census in which R is legitimately aggregated across functions.

### F2: compensation is composition inside the closure, not an import

Fig 6 :21: **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."**
Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**
Fig 13 :115: **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."**
Fig 13 :109: **"The array does not close."**

When a species is lost, the remaining species are the atoms still held. Any abundance or behavior change rearranges those atoms and their bonds. That can close a slot if another actor can take over the function, because the arrangements do not run out. It cannot create a function that requires an actor or a condition not present. That would be an import (a new species arriving, a new condition supplied), which is a different operator and has to be recorded as such (Fig 8 :50-52: **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"**).

The common phrase "the community compensated" blurs this. If a pollinator disappears and a different pollinator that was already present increases its visits, that is composition. If an entirely new pollinator arrives from outside, that is import, and it is not compensation by the community that suffered the loss.

**Verdict:** triangulated (Fig 6 and Fig 13, low overlap between the wall rule and the atom-set rule). Mode `specified`. Refuter: exhibit a compensation in which a function is performed by an actor that was absent before the loss and was not introduced by any seat. That is an import and the compensation label fails.

### F3: the figure already has the concept the field calls functional redundancy

Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

A functional group is the substructure that fixes which function appears; which species occupies it is the qualifier, not the kind. Compensation is therefore a shift of qualifier within a functional group, while the group itself persists. That is exactly what the ecological literature calls functional redundancy. The Wikipedia article on functional groups states it in the field's own words: *"Redundant species can easily do the job of a similar species from the same functional niche."* Laliberté et al. (2010) define functional redundancy as *"the number of species contributing similarly to an ecosystem function"* and pair it with response diversity.

If two communities hold the same species but arrange the interactions differently, they are Fig 12's isomers, not variants of one community. A change in behavior changes the bond, so it can change the substance without changing the ingredient list. This is why behavior change is not a weaker form of compensation than abundance change; it is a different bond change and can be more decisive.

**Verdict:** triangulated (Fig 12 definition plus two independent world sources with no shared author with the figures). Mode `specified`. Refuter: show that the group does not fix the outcome in a given community, so that swapping the qualifier changes which function appears. That demotes the case from functional group to isomer and removes the compensation claim.

### F4: abundance or behavior change counts only when it changes contact with the failed slot

Fig 11 :39: **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**
Fig 11 :40: **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

This is the sharpest test the figures give for the question as worded. Increasing the abundance of a remaining species is an agent-only change. It matters only if it changes that species' contact with the function that failed. A species that becomes more numerous but does not visit the plant whose pollinator was lost has not compensated. A predator that changes its hunting behavior and thereby releases a lower trophic level has changed contact and can compensate. The world source on trophic cascades describes the mechanism directly: a top-down cascade occurs when predators reduce the abundance *or alter the behavior* of prey, releasing the next lower level. Behavior change is a contact change, and the figure permits it.

The converse is equally important. The habitat is enumerated, never composed (Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**), so which changes count cannot be read off a model of the species alone. It has to be read off the world: Fig 11 :44 says **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**

**Verdict:** triangulated (Fig 11 contact rule plus the trophic-cascade world source, which is a different evidence pool). Mode `specified`. Refuter: measure a change in abundance or behavior, show that contact with the failed slot did not change, and show that R_s did not move. If R_s moved anyway, the figure's contact rule is wrong for this domain and the claim must be re-censused.

### F5: valence is relative to the residual, so there is no standing list of good compensators

Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
Fig 11 :50: **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**
Fig 11 :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

A species that compensates for seed dispersal may suppress recruitment of the same plant by herbivory. The question asks about *the* remaining species as if they had a fixed compensatory value. They do not. The verdict is a relation between an actor and a specific failed slot. This is also why a "compensation verdict" cannot be transferred from one ecosystem to another, or from one function to another in the same ecosystem.

Fetzer et al. (2015) corroborate from microbial model systems: *"Species that are functionally redundant in one environment may become pivotal in another."* They add *"Thus, caution is advised in drawing conclusions about functional redundancy based on a single environmental situation."* The biological concept of degeneracy states the same relational property in a different field: structurally dissimilar components perform similar functions under some conditions and distinct functions under others.

**Verdict:** triangulated (Fig 11 plus two low-overlap world sources). Mode `specified`. Refuter: exhibit an actor whose valence for a fixed residual flips with no change in that residual and no change in context, which would break the relational claim.

### F6: a direct-contact assessment misses the residual that matters

Fig 11 :52: **"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**
Fig 11 :65: **"Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994."**

Compensation is often asserted from the first ring of interactors: if another species eats the same prey, function is assumed restored. The figure warns that the decision-relevant residual can be three steps out. The world literature agrees that the knock-on effects of removing a species can reorganize a whole ecosystem, and that a system may fail to return to its prior state after the perturbing species returns. The Wikipedia article on trophic cascades calls this hysteresis: *"Such lack of return to a prior state is referred to as hysteresis."*

So a first-order compensation verdict is a weak verdict. It must be carried outward until the cascade stops mattering, and the enumeration is a reading of the world, not a derivation from the species list.

**Verdict:** triangulated (Fig 11 plus the trophic-cascade world source). Mode `specified`. Refuter: show a case where every actor in the first ring is accounted for and a second- or third-order residual still closes the gap, which would mean the cascade rule was applied too late; or show a cascade enumeration that is complete and still leaves an unassigned residual.

### F7: managed compensation is permanent asymmetric work

Fig 11 :61: **"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."**
Fig 11 :63: **"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."**

Read carefully, exclusion here is the subtraction of habitat, not the removal of an individual organism. Contact is a fact about the world and does not consult the manager. You can add an actor for its cascade (Fig 11 :65) and you cannot subtract the conditions it lands in. So an assisted compensation, such as reintroducing a functional equivalent, is not a solved state. It is an introduction into a habitat the manager did not author, with two silent failure modes: what was failed to reproduce, and what was unintentionally introduced.

This matters for the question because a "yes, communities compensate" answer is often used to argue that intervention is unnecessary. The figures say the opposite about managed cases: the work is permanent, and a substituted habitat is not the original one (Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."*).

**Verdict:** single-source (Fig 11 only; the world sources do not speak to the asymmetry rule directly). Mode `specified`. Refuter: exhibit a managed compensation that reaches a stable solved state with no further maintenance. That would break the asymmetry claim for this domain.

### F8: settlement belongs to the measured function, not to model agreement

Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**
Fig 2 :30: **"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."**
Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**
Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**

An insurance model, a redundancy index, or a stable time series is a frame. It can be internally perfect and still wrong about the failed slot. Only a measurement of that slot settles it, and the measurement must not move when a model disagrees with it. If it does move, it has become another frame, and the chain has collapsed into a single long room.

The averaging effect and negative covariance effect in the biodiversity literature are statistical results: with more species, summed random variables vary less. That is real, but it is a frame-internal result about an aggregate. Using it to declare a slot compensated repeats F1's error. Doak et al. (1998) are cited in the literature as showing the statistical inevitability of stability-diversity relationships; the article on ecological effects of biodiversity records the field's own caution: *"there is no one overarching effect of diversity on productivity"* and *"First, it is imperative that scientists stop looking for a single relationship."*

Operators table :51 says **"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one."** To assess compensation, "restored function" must be bound as a comparison between a target and a measurement, not as a feeling that the community looks healthy.

**Verdict:** triangulated (Fig 2 and Fig 7, plus the world caution about single relationships). Mode `general` for the anchor rule, `specified` for the slot measurement. Refuter: show a settlement in which the measured value was adjusted until models agreed, and the verdict is void by Fig 2 :30.

### F9: the question as worded risks frame-first apophenia

Fig 9 :27: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."**
Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**

The question supplies the mechanism (abundance or behavior change) before supplying the failed function. Any community will show some abundance change after a species loss, so if the frame is chosen first, every community will appear to compensate. The correct order is to describe R_s in effect terms, then ask which changes could predict its shape. The returned object is a kind of thing: a rearrangement within a functional group, bounded by held atoms. It is not the answer for a particular community. That answer requires the ground.

The figures also forbid turning this shape into a name for a specific species. The canonical usage rule says the framework reports *"a shape, not a name"*; it cannot say which species is the compensator, because naming it would mean already holding it.

**Verdict:** single-source for the ordering rule (Fig 9), triangulated with the canonical usage rule. Mode `general`. Refuter: produce a compensation verdict reached by first selecting a frame and only then describing the function, and treat it as a candidate, not a result.

### F10: habitat is not ground

Fig 11 :73: **"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."**
Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**

"Community function" is a habitat property. It changes with season, abundance, and management. It is not the anchor. If a verdict is taken from a change in habitat alone, the anchor has been replaced by a frame and the correction loop has lost its reference. The observable that can settle the question is a specific measurement chosen in advance of the comparison (for example, fruit set per plant for a pollinator loss), not the general condition of the habitat.

Fig 10 :59 adds the required declaration: **"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."** A conservation manager who wants compensation to be true has a stake in the outcome, which is allowed; a stake in which reading of the data gets recorded is not. The packet declares this.

**Verdict:** triangulated (Fig 11 and Fig 10). Mode `general`. Refuter: identify a verdict that was settled by a habitat description with no independent measurement, and mark it `candidate` rather than `grounded`.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the seat rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The claim under test is: **remaining species can compensate for a lost species through changes in abundance or behavior.** It is refuted if any of the following holds.

1. **Slot refuter.** A function slot s that was performing before the loss stays unperformed after the change, while an aggregate community metric is unchanged (Fig 1 :19, :22).
2. **Import refuter.** Closing R_s requires an actor or condition that was not present before the loss and was not introduced by a seat. That is an import, not compensation (Fig 6 :21, Fig 13 :115).
3. **Contact refuter.** The abundance or behavior change demonstrably does not alter contact with the failed slot, and R_s does not move (Fig 11 :40).
4. **Ground refuter.** A pre-registered measurement of the specific function shows no restoration while models or indices predict restoration (Fig 2 :29-30, Fig 7 :35).
5. **Relocation refuter.** One compensator measurably improves slot f while degrading slot g, so the residual was relocated rather than closed (Fig 11 :49-51).
6. **Cascade refuter.** A first-order verdict is followed by a second- or third-order failure that restores the original residual (Fig 11 :52).

If any fires, the specific compensation claim fails. None of these refute the framework's shape; they refute the empirical or binding claim. Until one is run, no part of this document may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. **Restate the question per function before answering it.** Name R_s and the measurement that will read it. Do not answer for "the community" as a single slot.
2. **Answer conditionally, with mode.** The defensible form is: *compensation is reachable as a rearrangement inside a functional group when the change alters contact with the failed slot, and it is not reachable when a new atom or a new condition would be required.* Mode `specified`, not `grounded`.
3. **Keep compensation and import in separate columns.** Record which species changed, which bond changed, and whether any actor was introduced. Convergence and import look identical in the contents (Fig 8 :50-52).
4. **Enumerate the habitat outward.** Direct interactors first, then their contacts, until the cascade stops mattering (Fig 11 :44, :52).
5. **Measure, do not average.** A stable total is not a restored slot (Fig 1 :22). Bind "restored" as a comparison against a pre-declared measurement.
6. **Treat managed compensation as permanent work.** Record the introduction and its provenance; never present a substituted habitat as the original (Fig 11 :61-66).
7. **Offer the generator, not the conclusion.** Fig 10 :52: **"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."** Lead with the slot residual and its refuter. Offer the links after a verdict is recorded.

## 7. Verdict on the figure lines, per domain

Verdicts are kept separate and are never averaged into a single score.

- **Domain 1, composition:** `specified`, triangulated. Compensation is reachable as a rearrangement within a functional group and bounded by the atoms held. It cannot manufacture a function needing a new atom.
- **Domain 2, habitat and contact:** `specified`, triangulated. It counts only when contact with the failed slot changes, valence is relative to that slot, and the enumeration must extend to the cascade. Managed cases are permanent work.
- **Domain 3, measurement:** `general` on the anchor rule, `specified` on slot measurement, triangulated. No verdict is settled without a measurement of the specific function that does not move when frames disagree.

**Overall:** the figures return a shape with refuters, not a yes or no. The empirical yes or no is `unreachable` from these figures and is owed to the ground. As Fig 13 :134 puts it: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"**, and Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**
