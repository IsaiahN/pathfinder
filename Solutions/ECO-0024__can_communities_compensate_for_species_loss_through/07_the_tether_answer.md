# The Tether's answer: can communities compensate for species loss?

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a field result. It is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §5), not a ground reading. No community was measured.

## 1. The residual it answers

A species disappears. A function or set of functions it performed is now under-supplied. The question asks whether abundance or behavior changes in the remaining species can close that gap. The figures say the residual is indexed by function slot, not by community total (Fig 1 :19), and that aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average (Fig 1 :22). So the question cannot be answered for "the community" as one object. It has to be answered once per function, with a measurement for each.

## 2. The shape

1. **Compensation is bounded rearrangement, not creation.** The remaining species are the atoms still held. Mint composes inside the closure and can never add an atom (Fig 6 :21). The arrangements do not run out (Fig 13 :109), so a held species can often take over a function. A function that needs an actor or a condition not present requires an import, which is a different operator and must be recorded as such (Fig 13 :115, Fig 8 :50-52).

2. **The functional group fixes the kind; the species is the qualifier.** Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* This is the figure's own version of functional redundancy. Behavior change rewires the bond rather than changing the species count, which can make an isomer of the community: same atoms, different bond, different substance (Fig 12 :117). Behavior change is therefore not a weaker mechanism than abundance change.

3. **A change counts only if it changes contact with the failed function.** Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* A more abundant species that does not visit the plant whose pollinator was lost has not compensated. A species whose behavior changes and thereby releases a lower trophic level has changed contact and may compensate.

4. **Valence is relative to the function, not a property of the species.** Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* The same species can compensate one function and damage another (Fig 11 :50). There is no standing list of good compensators, so a verdict from one ecosystem or one function does not transfer (Fig 11 :51). Fetzer et al. (2015) corroborate from a different field: *"Species that are functionally redundant in one environment may become pivotal in another."*

5. **Contact cascades beyond the first ring.** Fig 11 :52 gives the DDT and eagles case, where *"the residual that mattered was three steps out."* A first-order enumeration is a weak verdict. The habitat is enumerated, never composed (Fig 11 :43), outward until the cascade stops mattering (Fig 11 :44).

6. **Managed compensation is permanent work.** Introduction is available and exclusion of habitat is not (Fig 11 :61); a managed ecosystem is *"permanent work rather than a solved state"* (Fig 11 :63). A reintroduction is an introduction into a habitat the manager did not author, with two silent failure modes: what was failed to reproduce and what was unintentionally introduced.

7. **The verdict belongs to the ground, not to agreement.** An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks (Fig 2 :29). A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict (Fig 7 :35). An insurance model, a redundancy index, or a stable total is a frame; only a measurement of the specific function can settle the verdict. Bind "restored" as a comparison between a target and that measurement before the run (Operators table :51: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*).

8. **The habitat is not the ground.** Fig 11 :73: *"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."* A verdict taken from a general description of community condition has no anchor.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a general yes. No measurement was taken, so no community is claimed to have compensated.
- Not a general no. A failed search from these figures is not a proof of absence (Fig 8 :39), and the arrangements over held atoms are unbounded (Fig 13 :118).
- Not that functional redundancy guarantees compensation. Redundancy is conditional on context and on the response diversity of the species involved (Laliberté et al. 2010; Fetzer et al. 2015).
- Not that a stable aggregate is evidence of compensation. That is the exact aggregation error Fig 1 :22 names.
- Not a name for the compensating species. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not a claim that managed reintroduction restores an original state. It substitutes a habitat (Fig 11 :66).
- Not `grounded`. Every claim above is a candidate until a measurement pays for it.

## 4. What would settle it

The ground, not further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* describes an instrument that reads green but *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved the case, not an argument. Here the corresponding questions are:

1. **Slot measurement.** For each function lost, does a pre-declared measurement of that function return to its prior rate after the change? A total biomass series does not answer this.
2. **Contact test.** Did the abundance or behavior change alter contact with the failed function? If not, Fig 11 :40 predicts no compensation.
3. **Union test.** Did improving slot f measurably degrade slot g through the same compensator? If so, the residual was relocated, not closed.
4. **Cascade test.** Does a second- or third-order effect restore the original residual after the first-order verdict?
5. **Import test.** Did closing the gap require an actor or condition absent before the loss? If so, the event is an arrival, not compensation by the community that lost the species.
6. **Standing refuters.** Derive a function slot that stays live while the aggregate is flat, or a case needing a new atom, or a change with no contact effect and no function response, and the specific compensation claim fails.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The six-term substrate is a visible guess at reality's compression axes, not nature's own list; declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral. The ecological vocabulary of functional groups and redundancy is another frame, borrowed and recorded as such. This derivation stops at the limit of what the available instruments in this folder can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity"* (figures README). A community that looks compensated at the level of a total is not thereby shown to have compensated at the level of the function that failed.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer yes or no to whether communities compensate for species loss, because that is a measurement question and no measurement is reported here. It gives the shape a checkable answer must have. Compensation is a bounded rearrangement of the species that remain: a functional group persists while a different species occupies it, or the interaction bonds rewire and the community becomes a different substance. It works only when the abundance or behavior change alters contact with the function that actually failed, it is relative to that function rather than a standing virtue of a species, and it can be undone by effects two or three steps out. It cannot create a function that needs an actor that was not there; that is an arrival from outside and must be recorded as an import. A stable community total is not evidence, because the residual is indexed per function and aggregates hide live slots. What settles a real case is a pre-declared measurement of the failed function, not a model, an index, or an agreement among experts. The framework names no compensating species. It offers the shape, the refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The slot stays open until the ground settles it.
