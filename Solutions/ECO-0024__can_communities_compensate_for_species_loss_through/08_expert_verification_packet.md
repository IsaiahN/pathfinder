# Expert verification packet: the Tether on ecological compensation

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ecologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the shape, not `grounded`.** No field measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Compensation after species loss is a bounded rearrangement of the species that remain, not the creation of a new function. It takes one of two forms: a change of qualifier inside a functional group, or a rewiring of interaction bonds that makes an isomer of the community. It holds only for the specific function that failed, only when the abundance or behavior change alters contact with that function, and only if a measurement of that function agrees. It cannot close a gap that requires an actor or condition absent before the loss; that is an import and must be recorded as such. A stable community total is not evidence, because the residual is indexed per function and an aggregate hides a live slot. The framework returns this shape with refuters written in advance, and it names no compensating species.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 1, 6, 12 and 13

- **Statement:** a compensation verdict is per function slot and is bounded by the atoms held. It can swap a qualifier inside a functional group or change a bond to make an isomer, but it cannot add a function that needs a new atom.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19, *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 6 :21, *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* Fig 12 :123, *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Fig 13 :115, *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."*
- **What to check:** is the distinction between a functional group and an isomer well defined in the figure text, and does the per-slot rule actually forbid using a community total as the verdict variable?
- **Refuter:** derive one of the six substrate terms from the other five; or show the framework must admit a new function from rearrangement alone; or name a slot residual that the figures permit to be aggregated away.

### C2: ecology-facing, checkable by an ecologist against their own system

- **Statement:** an abundance or behavior change counts as compensation only if it changes contact with the failed function, its valence is relative to that function, and first-order enumeration can miss a third-order residual.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :40, *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Fig 11 :49, *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :52, *"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*
- **What to check:** take a loss you know well. Did the compensating change alter contact with the function that failed, or did it only alter the compensator's own abundance? Did the same actor help one function and harm another? Did the decision-relevant effect sit more than one step out?
- **Refuter:** exhibit a case where contact with the failed function did not change, yet the function was restored, with the measurement and the contact record both available; or a case where a first-order enumeration was complete and a third-order residual still closed the gap without any missing actor.

### C3: verification-facing, checkable against the anchor rule

- **Statement:** the verdict is settled by a measurement of the specific function, not by model agreement, a redundancy index, or a stable aggregate. Habitat condition is not the ground.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29, *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 7 :35, *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 11 :73, *"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."*
- **What to check:** in a published compensation claim you know, was the outcome variable a pre-declared measurement of the lost function, or was it a community-level aggregate chosen after the fact? Was the conclusion adjusted when a model disagreed?
- **Refuter:** show a settlement in which the measurement was moved until the frames agreed, which voids the verdict under Fig 2 :30; or show that a habitat description alone can settle a compensation question without an independent measurement.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a general yes or no. No community was measured in this packet.
- Not that functional redundancy guarantees compensation. Redundancy is conditional on context and response diversity (Laliberté et al. 2010; Fetzer et al. 2015).
- Not that a stable aggregate is evidence. Fig 1 :22 says aggregation is how a live signal disappears.
- Not a name for the compensating species. The canonical rule returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that a managed reintroduction restores an original state. It substitutes a habitat (Fig 11 :66).
- Not `grounded`. Everything here is a candidate until a measurement pays for it.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A pre-declared measurement of the failed function before and after the change, per slot.
2. A contact record showing whether the abundance or behavior change touched that function.
3. A union check on other functions sharing the same compensator.
4. A cascade enumeration carried beyond the first ring.
5. An import test: was any actor or condition required that was absent before the loss?

If these are run and the slot residual closes with no relocation and no import, the claim holds for that case. If any refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 fires, it fails there.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, Fig 11 :40, :49, Fig 12 :123, Fig 13 :115 and Fig 2 :29, six short passages.
2. Take one species loss you know. Write down the function that actually failed, not the total that changed.
3. Say whether the compensating change altered contact with that function. One sentence.
4. Name one second- or third-order effect that a direct-contact assessment would have missed, or say why none exists.
5. Say what measurement you would have trusted before seeing the result.
6. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
