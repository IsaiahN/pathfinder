**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the per-function slot residual is named and the refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 are written down. The offer collapses on contact if the first-stage object cannot be checked.

**Field:** ecological compensation research. No personal addresses. No lists of likely believers.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about compensation after species loss

> Hello,
>
> I have one specific, checkable claim about compensation after species loss. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with me. If the claim does not survive your check, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a community can compensate for a lost species through changes in abundance or behavior of the species that remain, but only conditionally. The change has to touch the function that actually failed, measured per function rather than as a community total. If another species already in the same functional group increases its contribution, that is a rearrangement of what is held. If the function needs an actor or a condition that was not there, that is an arrival from outside and is not compensation by the community that lost the species.
>
> **Why I say that.** The framework I am using indexes the residual by function slot, so a stable total can hide a live gap in one function. It also says an actor's value is relative to the function in question, so a species that helps one function can harm another. Neither of these is a measurement; they are the shape a checkable answer has to have.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that communities always compensate, that functional redundancy guarantees compensation, that a stable aggregate biomass is evidence of compensation, or that any particular species is the compensator. The framework returns a shape, not a name. It cannot tell you which species did the work in your system.
>
> **What would refute this, written down before your reply:**
> 1. a function that stayed unperformed after the change while the community total was unchanged;
> 2. a case where closing the gap required an actor that was absent before the loss;
> 3. a change in abundance or behavior that demonstrably did not alter contact with the failed function, with no change in that function;
> 4. a pre-declared measurement of the function showing no restoration while an index or model predicted restoration;
> 5. one species improving function A while measurably degrading function B.
>
> If any of these holds in a system you know, the claim fails there and I will record it as a failure. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send three public sources. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority. I am also happy to send the formula as a method to reuse, with the request that you write your own refuter and state your own mode before you run it.
>
> Thank you for your time.

## Distribution rules for this email

1. Send to one person at a time through an institutional or opt-in channel. No purchased lists, no scraped addresses, no mail merge.
2. Do not attach the framework before stage 1 is answered. A recipient who has absorbed the interior can no longer serve as an outside frame.
3. Record the stage 1 verdict with a date and a quote. Keep it separate from any later agreement about the framework itself.
4. If the reply is a refutation, copy it into `11_audit.md` with provenance and have the auditor seat re-evaluate it as it would any other source.
5. Never pool later agreement as corroboration. The agreement of people selected for likely agreement is a convention, not a verdict.
