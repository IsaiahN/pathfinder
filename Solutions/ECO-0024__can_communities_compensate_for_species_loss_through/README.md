# Solution ECO-0024

**Question:** Can communities compensate for species loss through changes in the abundance or behavior of remaining species?

**Source:** ecology.txt line 31 (question 24 in section 3, Species loss, extinction, and community change).

**Section:** 3. Species loss, extinction, and community change.

**Mode:** `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the per-domain claims, nothing `grounded`. Every substantive claim carries its mode at the point of use. This package is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, NOT a ground reading, so it cannot settle the question it reports on.

**Status:** done.

**Registry status:** pending (set by the registry owner, not by this folder).

**Contact field:** ecological compensation research (public institutional and opt-in venues only, no personal emails, no bulk send).

## What this folder answers, in effect terms

A species is lost. A function or set of functions it performed is now under-supplied. The question asks whether changes in abundance or behavior of the remaining species can close that gap. Option A: yes, compensation is a general property of communities with redundant species. Option B: only conditionally, because compensation is a bounded rearrangement of what remains, it must change contact with the specific function that failed, its valence is relative to that function, and its verdict belongs to a measurement rather than to a model. This folder derives Option B as a shape and refuses Option A as a `general` verdict.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals. The venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv` are institutional pages, public submission portals, and public contact forms.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to believe the claim. Selecting for likely agreement manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict (Fig 2 :40).
- No bulk-send plan. A mass send is a room, not an anchor (Fig 7 :35).
- No claim that this derivation is grounded. It is a shape, a route, and a set of refuters.
