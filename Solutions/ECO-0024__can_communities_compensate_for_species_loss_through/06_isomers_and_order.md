## DERIVATION

### Q1: Is compensation a functional-group change or an isomer change?

- Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- A compensation in which species B takes over the function of lost species A, with the same interaction structure and the same functional group, is a **change of qualifier**. The kind of function does not change. That is the ordinary case described as functional redundancy. The Wikipedia entry on functional groups puts it in field terms: *"Redundant species can easily do the job of a similar species from the same functional niche."*
- A compensation in which the species set is unchanged but the interaction bonds are rewired, so that a different function appears, is an **isomer change**: same atoms, different bond, different substance. Behavior change is the typical route here. Fig 12 :117 says the difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour, which is exactly why a species count cannot detect it.
- The two are not rival descriptions of one event. They are different objects. A qualifier swap keeps the group; an isomer change swaps the bond. A real community can do both at once, in which case it is a union compound and must be unbundled (Fig 9 :54).
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a case can be shown in which the functional group is preserved, the bonds are preserved, and the function nonetheless changes, both readings fail and the vocabulary must be re-censused.

### Q2: What is the dependency order of the slots?

- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
- Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**
- The chain for a compensation claim is: perceive the failed slot, have a term for the function and the group that could perform it, state what would count as restored, plan the habitat enumeration, then settle and carry.
- The middle link is the vocabulary link (Fig 3 :25-26). In ecology it is often assumed rather than measured: a functional group is assigned from traits, and the assignment is treated as if it were an instrument. If the group assignment has no independent check, the link breaks there, and any elaborate abundance model is effort above the break.
- Slots are not independent. A species that can restore function f may be the one that suppresses function g. So the order is not simply one slot after another; it is **slot, then union check, then next slot** (Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*).
- **Falsifier:** if a compensation verdict for slot f is later shown to have been invalidated by an unmeasured effect on slot g that shared the same compensator, the order was wrong and the union check must be moved earlier.

### Q3: What do the low-overlap analogues predict?

Two sources outside ecology bear on the same shape without sharing the figures' evidence pool.

1. **Engineering redundancy.** Wikipedia's article on redundancy in engineering defines it as *"the intentional duplication of critical components or functions of a system with the goal of increasing reliability of the system"* and distinguishes dissimilar redundancy, where different things are unlikely to contain identical flaws. It also warns that *"This formula assumes independence of failure events"* and that *"sometimes redundancies backfire and produce less, not more reliability."* Prediction: two seemingly redundant species that fail under the same perturbation are not redundant in the decision-relevant sense. This corroborates Fig 11 :49 on valence being relative to the residual and Fig 2 :31 on independence. Where it conflicts is in its design stance: engineering treats redundancy as something a designer deliberately installs, while Fig 11 :61 says introduction is available and exclusion is not, and the habitat is not authored. The conflict sends us back to the census, not to a compromise: the analogue governs designed systems, Fig 11 governs contact systems, and they are different domains.

2. **Biological degeneracy.** Wikipedia's article on degeneracy states that *"degeneracy occurs when structurally dissimilar components/pathways can perform similar functions ... under certain conditions, but perform distinct functions in other conditions"* and that *"Degenerate components compensate for one another under conditions where they are functionally redundant, thus providing robustness against component or pathway failure."* Prediction: compensation is conditional and relational, not a standing property. This corroborates Fig 12 :123, where the group says what kind and the qualifier says which member, and Fig 11 :49-51, where valence is relative to the residual. There is no conflict; there is also no independence to claim, because both figures and this analogue are describing the same structural fact at different strata. It is recorded as corroboration, not as a second witness.

The ecological literature itself adds a temporal qualification. Laliberté et al. (2010) define *"Ecosystem resilience depends on functional redundancy (the number of species contributing similarly to an ecosystem function) and response diversity (how functionally similar species respond differently to disturbance)."* Fetzer et al. (2015) found that *"Species that are functionally redundant in one environment may become pivotal in another."* Both predict that a redundancy verdict from one place or year does not transfer.

### Q4: What is the order of the habitat enumeration?

- Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**
- Fig 11 :44: **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**
- Fig 11 :52: **"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**
- The operational order is therefore: name the failed function; list direct contacts; list the contacts of those contacts; continue until a further step no longer changes R_s. A compensation claim that stops at the first ring is a first-order claim and must be labelled as such.
- Fig 11 :50 says the same actor can be *"a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another,"* so each ring must be re-scored for each slot. A single habitat list scored once is not enough.
- **Falsifier:** if a cascade enumeration terminates at the first ring and a later intervention reveals a third-order change that recreates the original residual, the termination rule was applied prematurely and the claim is downgraded to `candidate`.

## CONCLUSION

Compensation in this framework is either a **qualifier swap** inside a functional group (Fig 12 :123) or a **bond change** that makes an isomer of the community (Fig 12 :117). It is not a new atom; that would be import. The correct processing order is slot first, union check second, habitat enumeration outward third, and settlement last. The low-overlap analogues corroborate the conditional and relational character of the verdict, with one genuine conflict about design stance that is resolved by assigning the analogue to its own domain rather than by softening Fig 11.

This framing keeps what is defensible without forcing a general answer: it gives a checkable distinction between two kinds of compensation and a stated falsifier for each. It does not name a compensating species, and it does not claim any real community passed the test.
