## Two things in the wider literature that bear directly on this question

1. **The insurance result is not a field result about a particular slot.** Yachi and Loreau distinguish a buffering effect (lower temporal variance) from a performance-enhancing effect (higher temporal mean) and trace both to the asynchronicity of species responses. That is a model inside a frame. The figures require a measurement of the failed function that does not move when a model disagrees (Fig 2 :29-30). Cite the insurance paper to explain the mechanism, not to settle the verdict.

2. **Functional redundancy is conditional on context.** Fetzer et al. found that shapes suggesting redundancy in one environment can change, suggesting functional differences in another, and that *"Species that are functionally redundant in one environment may become pivotal in another."* This is the world-facing twin of Fig 11 :49, *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* It is a genuine low-overlap corroboration, not a restatement from the same evidence pool.
