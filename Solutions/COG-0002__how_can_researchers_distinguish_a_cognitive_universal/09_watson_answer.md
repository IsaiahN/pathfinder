## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what method can separate two hypotheses that produce the same observation. Option U: a pattern P widespread across societies is produced by a cognitive architecture shared by all humans. Option E: P is produced by similar environments or institutions, so the agreeing societies are duplicate copies of one cause. Option M: P is an artifact of the measuring instrument. What each leads to: treating E or M as U names a local regularity as a law of mind and exports it; refusing U because E is possible leaves no way to say anything is shared. The residual is that the reading is identical under all three options. Several causes create the same reading, so what is owed is an instrument that discriminates sources, not a further count of societies.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

A cognitive universal is supported only by agreement that survives decorrelation of the common cause, with a provenance record that distinguishes convergence from copying, with the instrument shown to measure the same construct in each group, with per-slot residuals reported rather than averaged, and with a habitat check that admits it is a substitution rather than a removal. Prevalence itself is not evidence. A null environmental result is `unreached at this budget`, not `universal`. This is a `general` shape plus a `specified`, ungrounded prediction, not a measurement and not a claim about any particular cognitive capacity.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode, writes refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spent independence: asking a field to endorse a universality claim without a provenance record turns potential outside frames into members of the same pool (Fig 2 :40, Fig 10 :42). That cost is avoidable by leading with the residual and the refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The method can be narrowed without withdrawing the shape. If a mechanism-discriminating instrument is found, the independence requirement is relaxed (refuter 1 in `01` §4); if invariance fails, the universality claim is ended for that construct; if a small set of patterns survives all tests, those become `candidate` universals. Nothing has to be deleted; a refutation adds a term that refuses and the reach falls (Formula step 3).
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A direct mechanism instrument that shows invariance across habitats would fire refuter 1; a pattern that survives documented decorrelation and independent convergence would fire refuter 2; a demonstration that independence is unnecessary in a closed domain would reverse the C1 sub-claim; a successful agent-only improvement across substituted habitats would reverse the Fig 11 capability claim. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
