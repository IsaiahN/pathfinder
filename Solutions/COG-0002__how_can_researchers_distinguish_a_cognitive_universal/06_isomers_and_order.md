# Isomers of explanation, partition versus union, and dependency order

**Mode: `specified` on the structural derivation, `general` on the bond and order rules. Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).**

## RESIDUAL

The same observation P is compatible with U (shared architecture), E (common environmental cause) and M (instrument artifact). Fig 8 :50-52 says these "look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them." Two questions are owed: (1) is the residual one question or a union of conditions, and (2) under Fig 12's definition, are U, E and M **isomers**, that is, same ingredients arranged differently?

## DOMAIN CENSUS (supporting derivation)

Atoms held: the observation P, the set of societies S, the set of environmental conditions E, the instrument M. Bonds held: the seven (three symmetric, four ordered) and negation. No new primitive is available without import. The anchor is the ground (behavior under varied conditions), never agreement. Order is by dependency: instrument first, decorrelation second, habitat substitution third.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Union or partition?

Fig 9 :54-57: "when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again" and "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."

The observation is a **union** of at least three conditions: the mechanism contributes, the habitat contributes, and the instrument contributes. Searching for "the cause of P" across the union is the error. Each condition needs its own loop and its own ground check:

- U loop: does the pattern persist when the cause is decorrelated?
- E loop: does the magnitude covary with the environmental variable after decorrelation?
- M loop: does scalar invariance hold?

A single lever (for example, adding more societies) clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds. "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different" (Fig 9 :57).

### Q2 — Are U, E and M isomers?

Fig 12 :117, verbatim:

> **Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Read literally, the three explanations share the same ingredients (P, S, E, M) and differ only in which relation is load-bearing:

| case | load-bearing bond | written form | consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| U, convergent derivation | independence plus conjunction of independent bearers | independent bearer A + independent bearer B | agreement means something; the pattern is corroborated |
| E, common cause | production from the shared condition | E ⇒ P | agreement is the pool talking; one witness |
| M, instrument artifact | identity misapplied across groups | P ≡ P' (same score, different construct) | the comparison is void before it begins |

These are three different substances built from the same observations. That is Fig 12's isomer definition applied to explanations: the difference is invisible in the score table and decisive in what may be concluded.

Fig 12 :124 adds the weaker reading:

> **Fig 12 :124 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

All three explanations belong to the functional group "accounts of a widespread pattern". The qualifier (which bond actually holds) decides which member we have. Reporting only the prevalence reports the group and not the member.

**Important limit:** the bond is not recoverable from the operands. Fig 12 :46: "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands." So a bigger cross-cultural table is "a bigger space, not a bigger vocabulary" (Fig 12 :27). This is the structural reason survey size cannot substitute for provenance.

### Q3 — Which bond types are actually in play?

Using the Operators table and Fig 12 :55-89:

- **⇒ production:** the environmental condition produces the pattern. This is E. Test: vary the condition, see the pattern move.
- **+ conjunction:** both bearers hold at once. This is the correct form for corroboration only when the bearers are independent (Fig 2 :31-32).
- **≡ identity:** the same score means the same construct in two groups. This is the claim measurement invariance tests. If it fails, the identity is false and the comparison collapses.
- **→ sequence:** the cause precedes the pattern. Needed for any causal reading; a cross-sectional correlation does not supply it.
- **− subtraction:** the pattern with the environmental contribution removed. This is what decorrelation estimates.
- **⋛ comparison:** a threshold relation between two quantities. Fig 12 :52 "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one." Here it appears if we set a criterion for "widespread enough to call universal". That criterion is a **goal**, not a discovery, and must be declared before the run.
- **¬ negation:** a refutation. Fig 5 :53 "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so". This is what fires when a refuter from `01` §4 succeeds. It is not a bond (Fig 12 amendment); it refuses the universal term's standing to be composed over.

Strain note: Fig 12 :46-47 says a term naming only its ingredients has recorded half of itself. The cross-cultural literature usually records ingredients (societies, scores) and not the bond (connection status, construct identity). That is exactly the flat-formula collapse Fig 12 names, not an added metaphor.

### Q4 — Dependency order

Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces." For this question:

1. **Perception:** can the task be perceived and scored in each culture? (translation, stimulus familiarity)
2. **Vocabulary:** are there terms for what the task affords in each culture? (emic concepts)
3. **Objective:** can we say what counts as P in each culture's own terms? (configural and metric invariance)
4. **Planning:** choose the decorrelated sample and the habitat substitution.
5. **Learn and carry:** name what survived the ground check and carry it to the next pattern.

Fig 3 :25-27 explains why this is where work goes wrong: the middle link, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither obvious failures nor obvious tests, so it attracts the least attention. Measurement invariance is precisely that middle link. "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at" (Fig 3 :27).

### Q5 — The analogue set

Three lower-overlap analogues corroborate the shape without deciding it:

| analogue | field | what it contributes | overlap with the figure derivation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Galton's problem and phylogenetic comparative methods | cultural evolution / statistics | independent events of change, not number of cultures; autocorrelation corrections | low: different instrument, same independence law |
| Measurement invariance and differential item functioning | psychometrics | per-item residual; scalar invariance as precondition for comparing means | low: different instrument, same per-slot law |
| Metamerism and the spectrometer | perception / instrument theory | same reading, different source; better instrument resolves it | moderate: same author as the figures, so weak corroboration |

The first two are independent evidence pools. The third is by the framework author and is used to explain the reading, not to witness it.

## FALSIFIER (written before the conclusion)

The isomer claim fails if any of the following holds:

1. Two explanations with the same observation and the same ingredients but a **different bond** can be distinguished without a provenance record, from the contents alone.
2. Measurement invariance can be established by a closed statistical test with no emic or ground input, so the M bond is readable from the data.
3. A design with documented common cause and no independent replication is nonetheless accepted as corroboration by the relevant field, and a later ground reading confirms the universal. Then the independence law is not the discriminator.

If any fires, the isomer table above is a taxonomy of cases, not a method, and `07_the_tether_answer.md` must be narrowed.
