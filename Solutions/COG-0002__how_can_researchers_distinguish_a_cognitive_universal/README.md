# Solution COG-0002

**Question:** How can researchers distinguish a cognitive universal from a widespread pattern caused by similar environments or institutions?

**Source:** cognitive science.txt line 5

**Section:** 1. Universals and cultural variation

**Mode:** `general` on the structural shape of the discriminator, `specified` on the prediction that decorrelating environments preserves a true universal and dissolves an environmentally caused pattern. Nothing here is `grounded`: this derivation performs no measurement and cannot settle which real-world patterns are universals.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

---

## What this solution is

It is a **method shape**, not a list of universals. The figures give the discriminator: a universal is supported only to the degree that the frames whose agreement is cited are independent of the common cause that could also produce the pattern. The answer is a set of tests, their order, their refuters, and an honest statement of what they cannot do. The framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself").

**This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.** *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (Isaiah N, 2026-11-19): "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." This package cannot settle the question it reports on. It can only specify what a settling test would have to look like.

## Restating the question as an effect

- **Option U (universal):** the pattern is produced by a cognitive architecture shared by all humans, invariant to the local environment and institutions.
- **Option E (environmental common cause):** the pattern is produced by similar environments or institutions, so the societies that show it are "duplicate copies of the same original" and their agreement is the pool talking (Fig 2).
- **What each leads to:** if we mistake E for U we name a local regularity as a law of mind and export it (the WEIRD problem). If we refuse U because E is possible we lose the ability to say anything is shared. The residual is that **the observation is identical under both options**: several causes create the same reading. What is owed is an instrument that discriminates sources, not a further argument about the reading.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the method.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that any particular pattern is or is not a cognitive universal.

Three reasons in the figures' own words:

1. **Agreement is not the verdict.** Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures exactly the correlation that disqualifies their agreement (Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool").
2. **Agreement under a shared cause is the failure being diagnosed.** Fig 2 :19-21 "they never talk, and still agree" and "so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other." A mailshot to people already persuaded of a universal is collapse 2 performed deliberately.
3. **A verifier must not hold the interiors.** Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, never the framework.

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in channels avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
