# Expert verification packet: distinguishing a cognitive universal from an environmental common cause

**What this is:** a claim in a form a cross-cultural psychologist or comparative-methods researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the prediction, not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed. This is a method shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the verbatim figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A pattern observed in many societies is not evidence for a cognitive universal until the common cause is decorrelated. The observation looks the same under a shared architecture, a shared environment, and an instrument artifact; only provenance and design discriminate among them. The proposed test is: compile the connections among the societies before interpreting their agreement; establish configural, metric and scalar invariance so the construct is the same; model dependence and count independent events of change rather than societies; report residuals per task and subgroup; and vary the habitat. Agreement that survives these steps is `candidate universal`. Agreement that tracks the environmental variable is `environmental`. Failure of invariance is `instrument artifact`. Failure to find a discriminating instrument is `unreached at this budget`, not a universal.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — comparative method; checkable by reading Figure 2 and the Galton's problem literature

- **Statement:** agreement among societies that share an environmental or institutional cause is one witness, not several, and cannot support a universality claim.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :19 "they never talk, and still agree"; Fig 2 :20-21 "so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other"; Fig 2 :31 "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence"; Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."
- **What to check:** is the independence requirement satisfied in the study being assessed? Are related societies, shared institutions, shared instruments and convenience sampling documented? Is the unit of evidence an independent event of change or a count of societies carrying the trait?
- **Refuter:** demonstrate a case in which two frames with a fully documented common cause show a pattern that survives decorrelation **and** the same pattern converges independently in a second sample with no contact, with both provenance records shown. That would establish that independence is not the discriminator.

### C2 — psychometrics; checkable by anyone who has run multi-group CFA

- **Statement:** measurement invariance is a precondition for the comparison, and a failure of scalar invariance ends the universality claim rather than qualifying it. The construct must be the same before the scores can be compared.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Also Fig 9 :30-31 "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause." And Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
- **What to check:** were configural, metric and scalar invariance tested across all groups; was differential item functioning examined at the item level; were emic concepts consulted; is the result reported per item and subgroup rather than only as a scale mean?
- **Refuter:** a study that demonstrates full scalar invariance and emic equivalence across all groups and still shows the apparent pattern to be a translation or response-style artifact. Then invariance is not the discriminator this claim says it is.

### C3 — habitat and instrument; checkable against the design of the study

- **Statement:** capability and behavior are properties of agent and habitat, so habitat variation is required; but a laboratory test substitutes a habitat rather than removing it, and therefore proves wiring, not capability.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39 "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"; Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another"; Fig 11 :70 "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."
- **What to check:** did the design vary the habitat (migrants, natural experiments, ecological or institutional contrasts)? Does it state which conditions were failed to reproduce and which actors were unintentionally introduced? Is a lab result being treated as a pure measure of the person?
- **Refuter (the figure's own):** an agent-side improvement that moves the ground and reproduces across substituted habitats, showing capability is agent-alone. Fig 11 :72 names this as the refutation of the capability claim.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that any particular capacity is a cognitive universal. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that cross-cultural prevalence is evidence. Fig 2 :19-21.
- Not that a bigger or more diverse sample settles the case. Fig 9 :60 makes sample size a filter for the budget.
- Not that a null result proves innateness. Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
- Not that a lab result isolates the agent. Fig 11 :66.
- Not that the framework can validate itself. *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A mechanism-discriminating instrument that reads the cognitive source rather than the behavioral projection, with invariance across habitats. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists." This would fire refuter 1 in `01` §4.
2. A natural experiment in which the common cause is absent and the pattern persists, with a provenance record showing independence.
3. A decorrelated sample in which the pattern covaries with an environmental variable and disappears when the variable is removed.
4. A failure of scalar invariance, which settles the artifact case and ends the comparison.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :19-21 and :31, Fig 1 :19, and Fig 11 :39 and :66. Six short passages.
2. Take one recent cross-cultural universality claim you know. Was a provenance or dependence analysis done at all? If not, C1 is not met.
3. Was scalar invariance tested and reported per item? If not, C2 is not met.
4. Did the design vary the habitat, and does it say what a lab substitution failed to reproduce? If not, C3 is not met.
5. Name one instrument that would discriminate the mechanism from the behavioral reading, or say why none is available. If none is available, the honest verdict is `unreached at this budget`.
6. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
