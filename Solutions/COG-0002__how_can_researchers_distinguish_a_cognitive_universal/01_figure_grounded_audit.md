## 0. The residual in effect terms

A pattern P is observed in many societies. Two hypotheses explain it:

- **U:** a cognitive architecture shared by all humans produces P regardless of local conditions.
- **E:** similar environments or institutions produce P independently in each place, or transmit it by contact.

The residual R is not "how many societies show P". It is that **the reading is the same under U and E**. Several paths create one behavior. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (Isaiah N, Substack, 2026-08-28): "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information". The observation is a projection; it cannot comment on the source. What is owed is a discriminator, not another count of societies.

Consequences for what an answer must look like:

1. It must be a **test that varies the common cause while holding the candidate mechanism**, not a statistic over more societies.
2. It must state **which links must be instrumented** before the comparison means anything (translation, construct equivalence, sampling).
3. It must say **what would make us call P environmental**, written before the test is run.
4. It must return a **shape** (independence plus provenance plus ground), not a name of any particular universal.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** two competing causes (U, E) plus a third hidden term, the measuring instrument M. The observation is a function of all three. This is a **union compound**, not one hard question. Fig 9 :54-57: "when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again" and "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either".
- **Symmetry:** the observation is symmetric under swapping U for E. If the two causes are swapped, the data are unchanged. Only the **provenance record** breaks the symmetry. Fig 8 :50-52: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them".
- **Scale:** individual cognition (slot) to society (frame) to cross-cultural sample (chain). Crossing these scales is lossy; the round trip needs its gap measured (Fig 4 :30).

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

No line below is admitted as a domain unless it has a nameable anchor.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | partition or union | dependency |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1, the comparison frame** | societies, samples, lineages, instruments | agreement and residual | the ground: behavior under varied conditions | union of U, E and M until unbundled | first: a frame must range against the anchor before its agreement means anything |
| **D2, the measurement chain** | items, tasks, translations, raters, test administrators | per-slot residual `R_s` | the ground: whether the instrument measures the same construct | partition by link (perception, vocabulary, objective, planning) | second: no verdict before the instrument link works (Fig 3 :27) |
| **D3, the habitat** | environmental and institutional conditions, migrants, natural experiments, test harnesses | contact | the ground: which actor changes the residual when introduced | union of agent-side and habitat-side contributions | third: habitat substitution only after D1 and D2 hold |

**No anchor means no domain.** D1 has the ground (behavior), D2 has the ground (construct identity checked against the world), D3 has the ground (which actor moves the residual). There is no fourth domain for "the argument about which label to use": an argument has no anchor and Fig 2 :35 forbids treating agreement as one.

### 1.3 Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. Can we perceive and measure P in each culture at all? (Fig 3 :4-8)
2. Do we have terms for what the task affords in each culture? (Fig 3 :6-8)
3. Can we say what would count as P, in each culture's own terms? (Fig 3 :9-11)
4. Only then: design the decorrelated sample and the habitat substitution (Fig 3 :12-15).

Optimising step 4 cannot rescue a broken step 3 (Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not").

## 3. Findings

### F1 — The two hypotheses are indistinguishable in content (decisive for the method)

Fig 2 :18-21, verbatim:

> **Fig 2 :19 "they never talk, and still agree"**
> **Fig 2 :20-21 "so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other"**

Fig 2 :7 names the failure directly: "collapse 2: common cause (hidden shared source)". Fig 2 :13 names its twin: "no anchor; each updates on the others".

Fig 8 :50-52, verbatim:

> **Fig 8 :50-52 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"**

So the same observed pattern P is compatible with maximal independence (a true universal, converged on separately) and with a shared source (E). The contents do not decide. The **record of provenance** does: which societies are related, which borrowed from which, which share an institution, which were sampled because they were convenient.

**Refuter (written before the conclusion):** an instrument that reads the **mechanism** directly rather than the behavioral projection, and shows invariance across every varied habitat, with a provenance that does not rely on independence of societies. The source essay allows this: *The Hard Problem is About Source* says the spectrometer, not an argument, resolved metamerism, and "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists." If such an instrument exists for a cognitive mechanism, independence of societies is no longer necessary. Until it does, F1 stands.

**Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 2 alignment collapse and Fig 8 closure surplus are different figures with low overlap). World corroboration: phylogenetic autocorrelation / Galton's problem and Currie, Greenhill and Mace 2010 (see `09_watson_answer.md`).

### F2 — Independence is the discriminator, and it must be bought

> **Fig 2 :31 "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."**
> **Fig 2 :31-32 "Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."**
> **Fig 2 :32 "Independence is what makes agreement mean something."**
> **Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."**

Two societies with a shared language, religion, media system, colonial history or school curriculum are two people reading the same report. They are one witness, not two. To test U you must find cases where the environmental and institutional conditions are decorrelated: independent origins, controlled phylogenetic contrasts, migrants into different habitats, natural experiments, and samples assembled before the hypothesis was formed. The unit of evidence is the **independent event of change**, not the number of societies that currently carry the trait.

Fig 13 :38-39 states the same law at a different scope: "independence is how little two frames draw on the same evidence, and it is what makes their agreement mean anything". Note that Fig 13 says it is "Figure 2's condition applied to the set itself", so this is **not** a second independent witness. It is the one law at two scopes. See the retraction log.

**Refuter:** a controlled study in which two frames with fully documented common cause show a pattern that survives decorrelation **and** an instrument independently reads the mechanism as invariant. That would demonstrate that independence was not doing the work. Short of that, a study with no decorrelation has no evidential weight for U.

**Status:** `triangulated` in the sense that the law appears in two figures at two scopes; honestly downgraded to `single-source` as independent corroboration because Fig 13 explicitly derives from Fig 2. World corroboration is strong and independent (Galton's problem; phylogenetic comparative methods).

### F3 — The measurement link has no instrument in most cross-cultural work

> **Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**
> **Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**
> **Fig 9 :30-31 "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"**

Translation, test administration, response styles and the construct itself are links below the break. A pattern measured with an instrument validated in one population and exported to another is a pseudoetic reading. *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* (Isaiah N, 2026-11-10): "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." So define P as an effect ("under task T, group G orders stimuli in way W") before choosing societies. If you name the cause first, any society will seem to fit.

**Refuter:** a study that demonstrates configural, metric and scalar invariance of the instrument across all sampled groups, plus documented emic equivalence, and still finds the "pattern" attributable to translation or response style after correction. If invariance fails, the pattern is an artifact of M and the U versus E question has not been asked yet.

**Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 3 chain order and Fig 9 effect-description rule, low overlap). World corroboration: Fischer and Karl 2019; Cross-cultural psychology reference article.

### F4 — Do not aggregate the signal away

> **Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
> **Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

Here "slot" means the task, the domain, the subgroup and the individual. A cross-cultural average can look universal while hiding slot-level residuals that track the environment. The correct report is per slot: where is P present, where absent, and does its magnitude covary with the measured environmental variable after decorrelation?

The world literature supplies the concrete version: differential item functioning is exactly a per-item residual. Fischer and Karl 2019 write that "one item shows some translation problems in one sample" and that misfit may be "an individual item, scale or sample". An aggregate score hides it.

**Refuter:** a demonstration that all slot-level residuals for P are zero (or unstructured with respect to the environment) after decorrelation, while the aggregate is preserved. Then the aggregate is not hiding anything and the slot rule is idle for this case.

**Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 1 plus the item-level DIF literature, low overlap).

### F5 — A universal is a property of agent and habitat, so the test must vary the habitat

> **Fig 11 :39 "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"**
> **Fig 11 :40 "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**
> **Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."**
> **Fig 11 :70 "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."**

This is the direct figure-grounded reason why "found everywhere" cannot establish U. The pattern is a joint product of the agent and the habitat in which it was measured. To isolate the agent-side contribution you must change the habitat: compare migrants and stayers, compare ecologies, compare institutions, and use controlled substitution. But a lab test substitutes one habitat for another, and "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails" (Fig 11 :68). So neither a natural cross-cultural correlation nor a synthetic lab task settles it alone.

**Refuter (the figure's own):** an agent-side improvement that moves the ground and reproduces across substituted habitats, showing capability is agent-alone. Fig 11 :71-72 names this: "an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim".

**Status:** `single-source` for the strict law (Fig 11 only); the general point is corroborated by the cultural-universal literature, which is a different evidence pool but does not establish the figure's exact capability claim.

### F6 — The anchor is the ground, not the number of agreeing societies

> **Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**
> **Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**
> **Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**
> **Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

Sample size, number of cultures and number of publications are **filters for the budget**. They decide how much can be tried, not whether the pattern is a universal. The anchor is the behavior under varied conditions. Fig 2 :37-38 adds the instrument caveat: "instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."

**Refuter:** a case where a verdict about universality was settled by agreement alone, with no ground contact, and was later confirmed by a ground reading. That would show agreement can substitute for an anchor. None of the sources read supplies one.

**Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 2 anchor rule and Fig 9 filter/witness rule, low overlap).

### F7 — A failure to find an environmental cause is not proof of a universal

> **Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**
> **Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**
> **Fig 6 :34 "Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."**

The method can return "no environmental correlate detected at this budget and with these instruments". It cannot return "therefore universal". Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts (Fig 8 :40-43). This is not a weakness to hide; it is the honest stop.

**Refuter:** a complete enumeration of all possible common causes with a closed instrument that returns zero. Fig 9 :44 says "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room", so this refuter is not available in practice. The rule stands.

**Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 8, Fig 9, Fig 6).

## 3b. Strain test and re-census

Three readings initially strained and were re-censused rather than forced:

1. **"Society is a frame."** The figures define a frame as "any bounded system with a library" (Symbols table :111). A society qualifies only under that broad definition, which is a modeling choice. Declared `specified`, not `grounded`. No strain remains after the declaration.
2. **"Fig 13 gives a second witness for independence."** It does not. Fig 13 :38 says it is "Figure 2's condition applied to the set itself". Downgraded from triangulated to single-source as corroboration. The *application* to the six substrate members is a genuine second scope, but the principle is one.
3. **"Fig 11's capability claim is about cross-cultural psychology."** It is not; it is about capability generally. The application to cognition is low-strain because *The Hard Problem is About Source* and *How Intelligence Actually Works* treat intelligence as mapping against an environment, but the figure line itself is not a result in this field. Marked `single-source`, `specified`.

After re-census: **no hidden strains; three declared modeling choices.**

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per Seat Map :287: "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Mechanism instrument fires:** an instrument reads the cognitive mechanism directly, shows invariance across all varied habitats, and does so without requiring independence of societies. Then independence is not the discriminator (F1).
2. **Shared cause survives decorrelation:** a pattern replicates with the same magnitude in a sample whose common causes are documented and removed or modelled, *and* converges independently in a second sample with no contact, with the provenance record showing both. Then agreement under a shared cause can corroborate (F2).
3. **Invariance fails but the pattern is claimed universal:** scalar invariance is rejected and the claim is maintained anyway. Then the "pattern" is an instrument artifact (F3).
4. **Slot residuals are structured:** after decorrelation, slot-level residuals still covary with the environmental variable. Then the pattern was environmental (F4).
5. **Habitat substitution reverses it:** the pattern disappears when the habitat changes and reappears only in the original habitat. Then it is habitat-bound, not a universal (F5).

If any fires, the method as stated is withdrawn or narrowed and the retraction log below is updated. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the residual, not with a theory of mind: the observation cannot distinguish a shared architecture from a shared environment (F1).
2. Make the first ask a **provenance audit**: list the connections among the societies whose agreement is cited (contact, descent, shared institutions, shared instruments). Without that record there is no case (F1, F2).
3. Require a **decorrelation design** before accepting a universality claim: independent contrasts or phylogenetic comparative methods for historical data; migrants, natural experiments and independently assembled samples for contemporary data (F2).
4. Require **measurement invariance and emic equivalence** as a precondition, reported per slot, not as an afterthought (F3, F4).
5. Treat a lab result as a **substituted habitat**, and say which conditions were failed to reproduce and which actors were unintentionally introduced (F5).
6. Report the outcome as `candidate`, `refuted` or `unreached at this budget`, never as `grounded` on the strength of agreement (F6, F7).
7. Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." Send the discriminator and the refuter, not a list of supposedly universal traits.

## 6. Canonical usage check and retraction log

Two sources govern how the figures may be used and were read after the first five sections: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`, which says "they outrank everything else in the repo: the code, the rulings and the docs answer to them... the quotes are the authority, not this summary" and that the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"; and `THE_FORMULA.md`, whose mode rule and step 7 (SEARCHED, INWARD, OUTWARD) govern the three-question route below.

### Step 7 route, kept distinct

- **SEARCHED (asked first, cheapest):** has the provenance record been compiled and have existing datasets been re-analysed with phylogenetic or autocorrelation controls? Fig 6 :49: "more search at the same reach: is it in here and unfound?" This is where most cross-cultural " universals" fail: the data exist but the dependence structure was never modelled.
- **INWARD:** refine the instrument. Translate, establish scalar invariance, add emic interviews. From an instrument already returning something (Fig 6 :32).
- **OUTWARD:** import a frame whose closure differs. Bring in phylogenetic comparative methods from biology, item response theory from psychometrics, or habitat-substitution designs from ecology. Cast R in effect terms first (Fig 6 :30), so the borrowed method is matched to the residual rather than to a label.

These three are not interchangeable. INWARD cannot substitute for OUTWARD: a better questionnaire still cannot tell you whether two societies share a source.

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is the residual one question?** No. It is a union of mechanism, common cause and instrument (Fig 9 :54-57). Split before searching.
2. **What composes the observation?** Agent and habitat compose it (Fig 11 :39). Neither alone produces it.
3. **What is the anchor?** Behavior under varied conditions, not agreement (Fig 2 :29, :35).
4. **What breaks the U/E symmetry?** The provenance record and decorrelation (Fig 8 :50-52; Fig 2 :31-32).
5. **Is the instrument in place?** Only if invariance and emic equivalence are shown per slot (Fig 3 :27).
6. **Is absence of E proof of U?** No. Fig 8 :39; Fig 9 :44.
7. **Can this run settle the question?** No. No measurement was taken. Mode stays `general`/`specified`, status `candidate`, and the refuter above is the instrument by which an outside frame could move it.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives: a cognitive universal is supported only by agreement that survives decorrelation of the common cause, with a provenance record that distinguishes convergence from copying, with per-slot residuals reported rather than averaged, and with an instrument shown to mean the same thing across the cultures compared. What does not survive is the idea that cross-cultural prevalence itself is evidence, or that a failure to find an environmental explanation proves innateness. Strains after re-census: none hidden, three declared modeling choices recorded in §3b. This is a simulated derivation, internally consistent with the verbatim figure text, and it cannot settle any real case.
