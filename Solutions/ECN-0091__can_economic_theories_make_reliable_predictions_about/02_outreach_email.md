# Outreach draft: check one output before deciding whether the framework matters

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and the six refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 are written down in the packet you carry. The output has to survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** one checkable claim about prediction under adaptation, and its refuter

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a method question and have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** "reliable prediction" in a system where people adapt to a policy cannot be a single percentage. It is a conditional over four overlapping conditions, and the verdicts stay separate:
>
> 1. The residual has to be read per slot, not pooled. An aggregate regularity such as a stable inflation-unemployment trade-off can look reliable while each firm's forecast error is live and moving.
> 2. The thing that settles the prediction must not update on the prediction. Where agents read the policy or the published theory and change their behavior, the outcome is produced by a frame that already saw the forecast, so predictor and object drift together coherently.
> 3. The composition must use terms invariant to the intervention. Reduced-form coefficients are records of one regime. Preferences, technology and constraints are the candidates that can survive a policy change, but whether any of them actually composes into an aggregate prediction is an open measurement question, not a result.
> 4. A metric that becomes a policy target stops being a good measure of the underlying outcome, and a consensus of forecasters sharing one evidence pool is not corroboration.
>
> The framework does not return a yes or no over all economic theories. It returns the **shape** a reliable prediction must have: per-slot residual, a non-updating anchor, an invariant term, independent checking frames, and a seat able to refuse. Whether a given theory meets that shape is a ground question.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that this refutes economics, that reflexivity makes prediction impossible, or that any particular "deep parameter" has been shown to be invariant. Those would be verdicts stated without a measurement, which the framework forbids. The question as posed is a union of conditions, not one question with two opposite answers.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written before this message):**
> 1. an intervention where the aggregate relationship survives unchanged and every slot-level residual stays flat, with agents demonstrably aware of the policy;
> 2. a "deep" parameter that moves with the policy regime as much as the reduced-form coefficient, so no invariant is available;
> 3. a target metric that remains an unbiased sufficient statistic for the underlying outcome across the regime;
> 4. two genuinely low-overlap forecasting frames, ranging against the same non-updating anchor, proving no more reliable than one shared pool.
>
> If any one holds, the corresponding claim is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The loop and mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, state the mode of what you get (`general` / `specified` / `grounded`), and keep the residual indexed to the slot you actually measured. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so a flattering write-up is the expected failure. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks for your time.
