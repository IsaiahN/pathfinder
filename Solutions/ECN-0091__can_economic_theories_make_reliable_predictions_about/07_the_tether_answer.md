# The Tether's answer: prediction under adaptation

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the universal yes/no. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks whether economic theories can make reliable predictions about systems where people adapt to policies and expectations. In effect: a theory issues Γ(b,a), agents read the policy and the published theory and change their decision rules, and the outcome o' is measured. The residual is `R = |Γ(b,a) − o'|` per slot (Symbols table). The question is whether R can be small and stay small when the object of prediction is itself a frame that updates on the prediction.

The question is a **union of four overlapping conditions**, not one question with opposite answers (Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"). Verdicts are kept separate and never averaged into a reliability percentage.

## 2. The shape

1. **Read the residual per slot.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." An aggregate regularity can look reliable while each slot's forecast error is live and trending. Reliability is a per-slot and per-intervention property.
2. **The anchor must not update.** Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update." Where agents adapt to the forecast, predictor and object mutually update and converge coherently. The outcome is then a mirror, not a ground (Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."). Where no non-updating anchor exists, that is a `gap`, not a contradiction.
3. **Invariance is the load-bearing condition.** A prediction is reliable to the degree that it composes from terms that do not move with the policy. Preferences, technology and constraints are the candidate invariants as a binding from the economics frame; reduced-form coefficients are recordings of one regime (Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."). Whether any invariant actually composes across regimes is an open measurement question, a live strain in `06` Q3.
4. **The feedback is a cycle.** Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Reflexivity and positive feedback are cycles. A comparative-statics chain cannot represent a closing loop at all.
5. **A target metric stops being a measure.** Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." This is Goodhart and Campbell in figure terms.
6. **Agreement is not corroboration.** Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Two low-overlap frames ranging against a non-updating anchor are required.
7. **The global yes/no is refused as a gap.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". The figures return the shape; they do not return a reliability coefficient.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that economics cannot predict anything. The claim is conditional and per slot, not a global negation.
- Not that reflexivity makes prediction impossible. It makes a *chain* model of a *cycle* impossible, and it demands a different anchor.
- Not that any particular "deep parameter" has been shown invariant. That is a binding from the economics frame and a candidate, `specified` and unmeasured.
- Not that a consensus of expert forecasters is evidence. It is efficient for a convention and worthless as a verdict (Fig 2 :41).
- Not that a universal reliability percentage is a rough approximation of the answer. It is the specific error the method forbids.
- Not `grounded`. No intervention or natural experiment was run in this derivation.

## 5. What would settle it: the ground

A discrimination instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer, not an argument, resolved it. Here:

- **Slot test:** does the aggregate relationship survive an announced policy while every slot-level residual stays flat? A per-slot measurement settles it.
- **Invariance test:** under a changed regime, does the proposed deep parameter move with the policy as much as the reduced-form coefficient? If yes, the invariant is not there.
- **Target test:** does the metric remain an unbiased sufficient statistic for the outcome after it becomes a target, with no gaming?
- **Independence test:** do two low-overlap frames (for example a structural model and a natural experiment) ranging against the same non-updating anchor agree more reliably than one shared pool?
- **Standing refuters from `01`:** all six in section 4. If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The four conditions are enumerable and the figures constrain their shape, but the numerical reliability of any given theory is a ground question requiring an instrument that this derivation does not have. The canonical rule is that the framework returns "a shape, not a name"; `01` F9 records the global yes/no as a declared `gap`. "Indiscernibility is not identity." The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer "yes" or "no" to whether economics can predict adaptive systems, because that question bundles four different conditions with different anchors. It says that a reliable prediction must keep its error indexed to the slot rather than pooling it; that the thing settling the prediction must not update on the forecast, so an economy where agents read the policy and change their behaviour breaks the naive anchor; that the prediction must be built from terms invariant to the intervention, with reduced-form coefficients treated as records of one regime; that the expectation feedback is a cycle which a chain model cannot represent; that a metric turned into a target stops being a measure; and that agreement among forecasters sharing one evidence pool is never corroboration. It refuses to hand back a universal reliability percentage, because the framework returns a shape, not a name or a number. What it offers is the shape any reliable prediction must have, six refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close each domain. The ground question stays open until an intervention settles it.

## 8. Cost, undoability, reversal

- **Cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, shape and refuters before the conclusion, so an error is findable. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a verdict instead of a generator (Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong.").
- **Undoable:** yes. The six refuters are removable or firable without touching the shape; each domain verdict can be revised on its own evidence; no primitive has to be withdrawn.
- **Reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A per-slot invariance test, a demonstrable non-updating anchor, a target-regime measurement, or a low-overlap independence test would move a domain to `grounded`; a figure re-census returning a universal coefficient would remove the `gap`. Prestige and consensus are not such instruments.
