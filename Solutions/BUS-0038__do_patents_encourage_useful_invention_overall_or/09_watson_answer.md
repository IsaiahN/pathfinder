# WATSON answer, BUS-0038

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural rules, `gap` on the numeric net effect. Not `grounded`.** Figures are primary and decide. World sources are secondary, corroborate but never overrule, and a conflict returns the derivation to the domain census. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. No measurement of patent effects was taken.

## QUESTION

Do patents encourage useful invention overall, or impede follow-on research and market entry?

Restated as an effect: a patent changes the payoff to the first mint and the contact between a teaching and the frames that would compose over it. The question asks for one number across both. R is indexed per slot, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19), so the "overall" scalar is the thing to be examined, not assumed. Anchors: the counter and the market (Fig 2 :29). What an answer would look like, bound before the run: two domain verdicts with separate anchors plus an instrument that discriminates them.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale. Arity: at least four-place (granting institution, first inventor, follow-on frame, paying market), indexed per patent-slot and per follow-on slot. Symmetry: asymmetric, disclosure acts on all frames at once and exclusion acts directionally on the follower. Scale: single inventor loop, population of inventors, legal institution above the frames, market habitat; the transform between scales is lossy (Fig 4).

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 The first mint | inventor, research team, examiner | expected return against the description-length bargain; disclosure | an invention that pays and is disclosed | admitted |
| D2 The follow-on reach | subsequent inventors, entrants, consortia | reach, closure, independence | a term composed and settled downstream | admitted; internally a union of two opposite effects |
| D3 The institution | patent office, courts, legislature | rights, conventions, exclusion, fees | the market and the counter, which the convention must not become | admitted; stake declared |
| D4 The verification frame | economists, economic historians, datasets | residuals and evidence | measurement, never agreement | admitted |
| X1 A single welfare scalar | none nameable | none | none | not a domain yet; absorbed as the aggregation error |

Union, not partition. D1 and D2 overlap at the patent document: the disclosure is output of one loop and input of the other (Fig 9 :54-57). Inside D2 there is a further union of an import that adds an atom and an exclusion that restricts contact. Dependency order: describe the residual in effect terms first; the first teaching must settle before it can be composed over (Fig 12 :104); the institution sets conditions for both; D4 checks each domain separately. Strain test: two over-extensions were retracted in `01` section 7, and no unmanaged strain remains.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The question as posed is a bundling error. The figure-derived answer is a shape kept per domain: a patent can supply support for a costly first mint and can buy disclosure, but a granted right is not a settled invention; for followers the same right is an import that adds an atom and an exclusion that restricts contact, two effects in different currencies; valence is relative to the residual, so the right can help one slot and hinder another; and the enforcing institution has a declared stake because a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one. The numeric net effect is a `gap`, owed to a source-discriminating instrument, not to more agreement. Mode `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural rules, `gap` on the number. Verdicts are kept separate and never averaged. World sources corroborate the shape; they do not settle the sign.

- **cost if wrong:** a single lever applied to a union. Fig 9 :56 "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds." A policy that maximizes first-loop invention would be sold to a case that fails in the follow-on loop, and the reverse error is equally available. The cost is real, bounded and largely reversible; the avoidable part is spending an outside reader's independence by sending them a welfare number instead of a shape and a refuter (Fig 2 :42, "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong.").
- **undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails. Fig 6 :42 "What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall. A refutation adds a term that refuses; it removes nothing, and the reach falls because" something was added rather than because anything was deleted. The shape and its six falsifiers are recorded with provenance so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62).
- **reversed by:** any of the six pre-registered falsifiers in `01` section 4, in particular a per-slot instrument that closes both loops at once, a regime that changes follow-on reach while changing neither the teaching's import nor the exclusion's contact, or a measurement that gives the net sign with an independent anchor. A source with a genuinely different evidence pool returning one stable sign for both domains across industries and time would also reverse the union reading. Prestige or agreement is not such an instrument.
