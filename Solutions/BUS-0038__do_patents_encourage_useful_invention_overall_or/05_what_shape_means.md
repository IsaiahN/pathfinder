## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

From *The Library of Babel*: *"You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."* And: *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."*

A **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question the shape is: a union of two loops, each with its own actor, currency and anchor, an import on one side and a contact restriction on the other, a relative valence, and an institution whose interest must be declared. It is not a number, and it is not a policy verdict.

`SPACE(???)TIME` was the exemplar's shape. The analogous form here is `INCENTIVE(???)FOLLOW-ON`: the two halves are held apart, and the connective is the thing that needs an instrument to name.

## 2. The most important essay-line for this question: the bundle

*The Library of Babel* names the exact failure in the question as asked: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."*

That is the question "do patents encourage useful invention overall, or impede follow-on research and market entry?" in one sentence. "Overall" is the conflation. The two halves have to be searched individually, and the combining has to wait for each answer. Fig 9 :54-57 governs the same move. This is not a rhetorical preference: the essay and the figure agree because both are instances of the same rule, and their overlap is low enough (a search essay and a union figure) that the agreement is worth recording.

## 3. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is *closest* to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. The proxy vocabulary here was "mint", "closure", "contact", "valence", "convention".
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."*
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* Hence the outreach email asks what the recipient would expect to see if the shape were true, before showing the theory.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: the finish line is also the starting place; the searcher leaves as the author. In this simulation the stop is earlier: the shape is stated and the refuter is written, then the loop stops because the numeric residual cannot be closed without an instrument.

## 4. What would license a name (including a number)

Only two things, both outside the shape:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays."* For patents, the ground is the counter, the market, the follow-on product, the measured entry rate, not the consensus of economists.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test. Moser's world-fair data and the Wright brothers episode are candidates; a second paper quoting the same Boldrin and Levine abstract is not.

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the discriminator. An instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source"*; *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* Patent counts read "more invention" without saying which source produced it: a real increase in minting, a change in filing behaviour, a subsidy, or a strategic thicket. *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* That is precisely why the numeric part of BUS-0038 is `gap`, and why the essay's own caution applies: *"you can only ever say nothing has been found yet."*

## 5. Why no single "overall" policy verdict is a shape

*They're Scanning Books When They Should Be Studying Nature* distinguishes outputs from generators: *"You cannot accumulate your way from the category of outputs to the category of generators; they are different kinds of thing, and no quantity of the first sums to the second."* Patent counts are outputs. A welfare verdict about the system that produced them is a claim about a generator. Adding up more counts does not cross that boundary.

The same essay makes the choice of primitives visible: *"A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six."* And: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible, which is worth a great deal, because a visible choice can be revised and an emergent one cannot."* Applying that here: the choice to measure "invention" by patents is a visible choice, and it must be labelled as such, not smuggled in as a neutral metric.

*How a Society of Agents Would Work* gives the practical instrument rule: *"Use the success rate per try, not the total."* and *"Votes come from outcomes, not popularity."* Translated: measure per patent-slot and per follow-on slot, and let the market and the downstream product vote, not the citation count.

## 6. Prestige is not action

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly."* and *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."*

A literature in which a claim is repeated by well-cited authors gains a budget to be tried, not a verdict. The outreach material therefore asks for a refutation from an outside frame and records the verdict before showing the source. It does not ask anyone to trust the framework, and it does not rank recipients by likely agreement.

## 7. My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post."* and *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* The architecture post is blunter: *"Because it is a metatheory, it cannot be validated, nor can it validate itself"*, and LLM simulations *"could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula."*

So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation, checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of patent policy and must be labelled as such. *How Intelligence Actually Works* adds the limit worth carrying into the honest stop: *"The recursion converges toward indiscernibility, and indiscernibility is not identity."* A shape that fits every fact we happen to have is still a shape.

## 8. Correction carried forward from the exemplar

The exemplar's `05` §5b corrected an overstatement of "shape, not a name": that phrase governs a *missing* primitive, not the selection of atoms already held. The same correction applies here. Saying "the framework returns a shape, not a number" is correct about the *net welfare scalar* (no instrument holds it). It would be wrong to say the framework cannot name the two loops: mint, import, contact and convention are all already in the vocabulary. What is missing is the discriminator that would give the net sign, and that is what the refuters in `08` ask an expert to supply.
