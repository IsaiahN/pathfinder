# The two operators inside a patent: structure, order and analogues

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

"Do patents encourage useful invention overall, or impede follow-on research and market entry?" treats a patent as one object with one effect. The figure census in `01` shows it is at least two operators aimed at two slots. Two questions are owed: (1) are the disclosure and the exclusion two arrangements over the same atoms, that is, isomers, or are they different atoms? (2) in what dependency order must the two loops be measured?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms already held in the legal/innovation vocabulary: a teaching, a right to exclude, a term of years, a disclosure document, a market entrant, a follow-on inventor. Bonds available: `→` sequence, `⇒` production, `−` subtraction, `⋛` comparison, `¬` negation. No new primitive is available without an import. Currency: description length and actions (Fig 12 :125-126). Anchor: a measurement in the market or the counter, never the agreement of the frames being graded.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Is a patent one operator or two?

- Fig 6 :22: **"NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world. IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure."**
- Fig 11 :61: **"Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."**
- The published specification is a production bond: `inventor ⇒ disclosure`, and for a follower the disclosure is an import, `disclosure ⇒ new atom in closure(follower)`.
- The exclusion right is not the same bond. It is a negation applied to other frames' contact: `¬(follower may compose over claim)` for a term of years. Negation is an operator, not a bond (Operators table :70-78), so it does not even enter the same price.
- **Result:** two operators of different arity and different direction. They are not one object with a net effect. Mode `specified`. Triangulated (Fig 6 plus Fig 11 plus the Operators table).
- **Falsifier:** show a regime in which the same act both adds the atom to follower closure and removes the contact, with no separable instrument for each. If they cannot be separated even in principle, the two-operator claim fails.

### Q2 - Are they isomers, or a functional group?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Disclosure and exclusion share the same core atoms (a teaching and a right over it) but use different bonds. A regime can weight them differently: a defensive publication has the disclosure without the exclusion; a trade secret has the exclusion-like secrecy without the disclosure; a full patent has both.
- On the strong reading, these are **isomers over the shared atom set**: same atoms, different arrangement, different behaviour. The behaviour difference is decisive and invisible in a headline "patent count", which is exactly the metamerism point from `05`.
- On the weak reading, they are members of a **functional group** (an IP right over a teaching) with different qualifiers. The weak reading is safer and is the one used in the outreach packet.
- **Falsifier:** if a regime can be shown in which changing the bond from disclosure to secrecy leaves the downstream behaviour unchanged, the isomer claim fails and the two operators collapse into one functional group with no behavioural difference. Mode `specified`; the figure definition is `general`, the legal binding is `specified`.

### Q3 - What is the dependency order?

- Fig 3 :20-21: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**
- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."**
- Ordered chain for this question:
  1. describe R in effect terms (two loops, two anchors);
  2. the first mint must produce and disclose a settled teaching before anything can compose over it;
  3. the institution's convention sets the conditions under which both loops run (Fig 10 :47: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**);
  4. only then can the follow-on loop be measured: does a new term get composed and settled downstream?
- **Falsifier:** a case where follow-on composition is measured before the first teaching has settled, and the measurement still predicts downstream outcomes. That would mean the chain is not ordered by dependency here.

### Q4 - The two currencies, held apart

- Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."** Fig 12 :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**
- Loop 1 is priced in expected return against the cost of the search. Loop 2 is priced in reach and entry. A patent touches both prices. The "overall" question silently adds them, which Fig 12 forbids.
- The opened economics sources make the same point from the other side: Boldrin and Levine warn that a partial-equilibrium gain in incentives can be a general-equilibrium loss; the thicket literature reports countervailing effects on licensing. This is corroboration, not the verdict; the verdict comes from Fig 12 plus Fig 9 :60.
- **Falsifier:** a common unit in which both effects are already measured without a new convention being introduced to create it.

### Q5 - A catalyst analogue, recorded but not relied on

- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**
- A patent *right* survives repeated licensing; its *term* is spent. The figure's point is that nothing in the artifact records which kind of part it is. So a ledger that records only "patent granted" cannot tell a regenerating right from a spent term.
- This is an **analogue**, not a figure verdict on patents. Mode `specified`, single-source for the mapping. **Falsifier:** a registry that already records, per patent, whether the right is being renewed by licensing or running down toward expiry, with a measure that predicts downstream behaviour. If such a register exists and behaves as a single kind, the analogue adds nothing.

## CONCLUSION

The two effects of a patent are two operators of different arity and direction, best read as isomers over a shared atom set, with a weaker functional-group reading as the safe form. They must be measured in dependency order, first mint and disclosure, then institution, then follow-on composition, and in two currencies that do not add. The figure-grounded shape is therefore a union, and the "overall" question is a bundling error. The isomer and catalyst readings are supporting structure; they are not the verdict, and neither is used to claim a net sign.
