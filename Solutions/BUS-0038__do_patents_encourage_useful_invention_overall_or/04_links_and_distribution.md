## What the wider literature bears on the question

Four findings matter, and none of them settles the question:

1. **The pro-invention half is a partial-equilibrium result.** Henderson and Boldrin/Levine agree on this narrow point even though they disagree on policy. Boldrin and Levine explicitly warn that a partial-equilibrium improvement in incentives can have a negative general-equilibrium effect. That is the same warning the figures make in the register of slots versus aggregates (F1, F7).
2. **The anti-follow-on half is real but also contested.** Thickets, the anticommons, litigation costs and the Wright brothers history support it. The thicket article itself notes countervailing licensing effects, the anticommons article notes survey work that disputes the effect, and Wright is disputed by Katznelson and Howells. This is a union with live conflicts, not a settled sign (F8).
3. **The metric is part of the dispute.** Boldrin and Levine's central complaint is that patent counts are mistaken for innovation. Fig 1 :27 (*"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces"*) is the figure-grounded version of the same objection.
4. **History supplies independent-ish anchors.** World-fair data, patent-filing trends, litigation costs and the aviation episode draw on different evidence from modern patent-count correlations. They are the best candidates for a second witness, but because Moser's survey cites the same underlying studies as much of the rest of the pool, overlap must be recorded rather than assumed away.

## Practical constraints on submission
