## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not whether a patent is good or bad in the abstract. A patent changes two different things at once, and they are measured at different slots against different anchors.

In figure terms: an invention is a candidate term φ that must pay the description-length bargain `|φ| + left(R,φ) < |R|` before it is admitted (Fig 5 :42-43). "Useful invention" means a term that settles against the ground, not merely a term that gets granted (Formula step 5, *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays"*). A patent does two things to that process:

- **The first loop (the inventor).** The prospect of a temporary exclusion changes the payoff to funding a costly search. In effect terms it can supply SUPPORT, `|R⁺_s| > 0 for some s` (Fig 5 :29), where without it no one bets.
- **The follow-on loop (everyone else).** The published application adds something to other frames' closure, while the exclusion right restricts the contact those frames have with the term. Fig 6 :22 and Fig 11 :61 govern these two halves respectively.

The word **"overall"** in the question asks for one number across these two loops. That is the aggregation the figures forbid: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated"* (Fig 1 :19) and *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average"* (Fig 1 :22). Fig 9 :54 calls this a union rather than a partition and says to unbundle it before searching.

- Option A, "patents encourage useful invention overall": leads to the prediction that one welfare scalar is recoverable and that first-invention and follow-on effects can be added.
- Option B, "patents impede follow-on research and market entry": leads to the prediction that the exclusion channel dominates and that entry falls.
- What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a **shape** consisting of the two conditions kept separate, each with its own actor, currency and anchor, plus an instrument that would discriminate a change in the first loop from a change in the second. Until that shape is written down it is a preference, not a term, and no check in the loop can see it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is that the question is a union compound: the figure-derived shape is Option B's concern kept as one domain and Option A's concern kept as another, never averaged. The numeric net sign is a declared `gap`, settled only by measurement.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the business question, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of intellectual property.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
