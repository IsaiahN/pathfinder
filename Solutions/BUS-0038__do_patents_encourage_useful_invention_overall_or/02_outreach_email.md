# Outreach draft - validate the shape first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until F1, F2, F6 and F9 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are checked against the verbatim figure text. The stage-1 output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One shape claim about patents and follow-on research, with its refuter, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** A patent changes two loops at once. For the first inventor it can change whether a costly search gets funded at all. For everyone downstream it does two opposite things: the published teaching puts the invention into their reach, and the exclusion right changes who may compose over it. The question "do patents encourage useful invention overall, or impede follow-on research and market entry?" asks for one number across both loops.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the shape, `gap` on the number).** The two loops cannot be averaged. The figures index residuals per slot and warn that aggregation is how a live signal disappears; they also say a bundled problem in two conditions is categorically different from either condition alone, so it must be unbundled before it is searched. So the answer has the form of two separate verdicts, each with its own actor, currency and anchor:
>
> 1. **First loop:** a right can supply the support that makes a costly search worth betting on, and it can buy disclosure. Whether it does so net, across industries, is a measurement question.
> 2. **Follow-on loop:** the teaching is an import that expands what other frames can reach; the exclusion restricts contact. These are opposite directions in different units and must not be netted before each is measured.
>
> The framework returns a shape, not a welfare number. The numeric net sign is a declared gap. "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" is how the figures put it. An anchor does exist: a counter, a market, a measured outcome. What is missing is the instrument that separates the two loops per slot.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that patents are good or bad by kind; that the answer is "abolish" or "strengthen"; that patent counts measure invention (a frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces); that the legal institution is neutral, since a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one. An institution that benefits from its rules being used cannot be the verifier of those rules, so that interest is declared here rather than hidden.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim.** I wrote these down before I looked at the economics literature:
> 1. one instrument returns a per-slot residual that closes both loops with nothing left over when they are aggregated;
> 2. a regime changes follow-on reach while changing neither the disclosure that adds the teaching nor the exclusion that changes contact;
> 3. a figure line, not a measurement, returns the numeric net effect;
> 4. the same right has invariant valence across a change of residual in the same habitat;
> 5. the enforcing institution can decide no market outcome and still function as a patent right.
>
> If any holds, I retract or narrow the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Patents and Innovation: Evidence from Economic History, Petra Moser, Journal of Economic Perspectives 27(1), 2013: https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/jep.27.1.23
> - The Case against Patents, Michele Boldrin and David K. Levine, Journal of Economic Perspectives 27(1), 2013: https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/jep.27.1.3
> - Patent thicket, including the Hargreaves and Shapiro material: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patent_thicket
> - Tragedy of the anticommons, including the Heller and Eisenberg 1998 Science result: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tragedy_of_the_anticommons
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is public. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

| Institution's interest declared, not asserted as neutral | Seat Map :171 - *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* |
| Links only after a verdict is recorded | Fig 10 :42 - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Seat 4 must not hold the interiors. |
| No request for trust or prestige | *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* |
| Offer the formula as a method to reuse | Fig 4 :22 - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* |
| Ask users to state their own ground and refuter | `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": a frame *"cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it holds."* |

**Stage discipline.** Stage 1 asks for a verdict on the shape and records it before any theory is shown. Stage 2, opt in only, offers the four links. Stage 3 offers the formula as a method. Later agreement from a recipient who has read the theory is never pooled as corroboration, because after stage 2 they share the evidence pool (Fig 2 :40). A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
