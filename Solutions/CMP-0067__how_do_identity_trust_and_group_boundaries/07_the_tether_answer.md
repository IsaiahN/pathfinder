# The Tether's answer on identity, trust and group boundaries

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the universal coefficient. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A norm or political behavior spreads across a network. The observed adoption pattern departs from uniform-contagion prediction, and the departure is indexed per person, tie and group, not as one network-wide rate (Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."). The question asks how identity, trust and group boundaries change that transmission. The Tether's answer is that they are not one coefficient with three inputs; they are a union of overlapping conditions with different anchors, bonds and valences, so verdicts must be kept per domain and never averaged.

## 2. The shape (one loop per domain, verdicts separate)

**Domain 1, contact structure.** Transmission is per slot. A network-wide rate is a filter for the budget, not a witness (Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."). *Verdict:* `general` on indexing, `specified` on any dataset reading. Triangulated across Fig 1 and Fig 9.

**Domain 2, identity.** Identity is a symmetric `+` bond that lowers independence between frames, because shared-category members draw on the same evidence (social identity and homophily sources). Correlation has two opposite valences (Fig 2 :39 "Correlation has valence."): "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool" (Fig 2 :40) and "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it" (Fig 2 :41). So identity strengthens a norm and corrupts a verdict, and those are not the same mechanism. *Verdict:* `specified` per subdomain, `general` on the two-valence rule. Triangulated across Fig 2 and low-overlap social-psychology sources.

**Domain 3, trust.** Trust is a channel weight, not a property of the ground (Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does."). High trust without an external anchor produces mutual update and coherent drift: "they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence" (Fig 2 :14-15). Manipulation of trust, by methodically discrediting outside sources, is what distinguishes an echo chamber from a poppable epistemic bubble (Nguyen 2020). *Verdict:* `specified`. Triangulated across Fig 10, Fig 2 and social epistemology.

**Domain 4, boundaries.** A boundary is a closure wall. Composition inside a closure cannot add an atom (Fig 8 :32 "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."). Only contact with a differing closure moves the wall (Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."). Repetition inside a wall raises confidence without adding evidence: "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not" (Fig 7 :12-13). *Verdict:* `specified`. Triangulated across Fig 6, Fig 7 and Fig 8.

**Domain 5, the compound.** The three conditions overlap; this is a union, not a partition (Fig 9 :55). A bridge between groups that already share an evidence pool changes topology without changing contact (Fig 8 :48-50), and isolation substitutes a habitat rather than removing one (Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."). *Verdict:* `specified` on the interactions, `general` on the union rule. Triangulated across Fig 8, Fig 9, Fig 11 and the 2026 experiment.

**Domain 6, the number.** No figure line returns a universal coefficient, a global ranking of the three factors, or a threshold for when a bridge helps. Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." *Verdict:* `gap`, of the shape-not-a-name kind, with a per-slot discriminator owed. Unreachable is a verdict, not silence (Fig 13 :134).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a single transmission rate, and not a percentage attributable to each factor.
- Not "trust is good for transmission" or "boundaries are bad for transmission." Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49), and correlation's valence flips between convention and verdict.
- Not that adding more ties always helps. A bridge over a shared evidence pool has no surplus, and a wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives (Fig 6 :24).
- Not that removing a platform or a channel removes a habitat. It substitutes one, with two silent failures (Fig 11 :68-69).
- Not that a widely repeated norm is settled. Settling is necessary and not sufficient (Fig 5 amendment).
- Not grounded. No measurement was taken and no simulation was run.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground, not agreement

A per-slot discriminator, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. The corresponding questions here:

1. **Channel-weight test:** for the same adoption event, what weight did the receiver give the source (the `w` weighting), measured independently of whether the claim was true?
2. **Independence test:** did the two frames draw on different evidence, measured independently of their agreement? If not, their agreement is one pool talking (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Settlement test:** does the transmitted term compose over anything downstream once the source is removed? If not, it was repeated, not settled (Fig 5 amendment, Fig 12 :104).
4. **Substitution test:** after removing a channel, what habitat replaced it, what unchosen actors came with it, and was the original goal reproduced? (Fig 11 :66-69).
5. **Standing refuters:** the six in `01` section 5, including the aggregation refuter, the valence refuter and the closure refuter.

## 5. The honest stop

**Reached the shape; not reached the number.** The figures fix the separator and the per-slot indexing; they do not fix the coefficient. The six-term set is "a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes"; declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity." One live strain is named: boundary as a threshold parameter of the held network atom, not a seventh primitive. Nothing here settles the question it reports on.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not return a single number for how identity, trust and boundaries change diffusion. It says the residual must be indexed per person, tie and group, then separates three overlapping conditions. Identity lowers independence between people, so its effect flips by content: it builds a shared meaning efficiently, but it disqualifies agreement about the world, because the agreeing people drew on the same pool. Trust is a property of the channel, not of the ground; high trust with no external anchor makes a group converge on whoever had the most influence, which is why correcting an echo chamber with more information can backfire. A boundary is a closure wall: repetition inside it raises confidence without adding evidence, and a genuinely new term crosses only from a frame whose closure differs and with provenance that it explains the gap. Adding a bridge between groups that already share evidence changes topology without changing contact, and removing a channel substitutes a habitat you did not choose. What the framework offers is that separator, six failure conditions written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The universal coefficient stays a declared gap until a per-slot measurement settles it.
