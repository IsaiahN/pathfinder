## 0. The claim under audit

> How do identity, trust, and group boundaries change the transmission of norms or political behavior across networks?

The ordinary reading smuggles in three commitments that must be made ordinary terms before the loop can see them (THE_FORMULA, "Direction"):

1. **"Transmission"** is assumed to be one quantity with one coefficient, as in a disease model where each edge carries the same probability per unit time. Bind the alternative as a term: transmission may be several quantities with different anchors, indexed per slot.
2. **"Change"** is assumed to be a single directional effect (more identity, more trust, or a sharper boundary means more or less spread). Bind the alternative: each factor may have residual-relative valence, so the same factor helps one residual and hurts another.
3. **"Identity, trust, and group boundaries"** are assumed to be three independent dials. Bind the alternative: they may be a union of overlapping conditions whose intersections are categorically different from the single conditions.

An answer would look like a **separator**: for each slot, which condition binds, what would count as transmission under it, which bond carries the effect, and what observation would fire a refuter. That shape is bound now, before conclusion, so it can accrue a residual like any other term.

## 1. The residual in effect terms

Let the prediction be a uniform-contagion model: adoption probability on an edge is a constant times exposure. Let the observation be the actual pattern of who adopts what and when. R is the per-slot gap, not a network average:

> R_s = observed adoption state at slot s minus predicted adoption state at slot s

where a slot is a person, a tie, or a group. The live mass is R⁺_s at the slots where the uniform model fails hardest: the clusters, the bridges, the echo chambers, the polarized pockets.

- **Arity.** At least three conditions are in contact with R: who is connected (boundary), whether the tie carries the signal (trust), and whether the receiver is disposed to accept it (identity). This is a union, not a single condition with three levels.
- **Symmetry.** Transmission is ordered: A influences B is not B influences A. So the governing bond is ordered (→ sequence or ⇒ production), not the symmetric +. Identity similarity is symmetric (+, shared attribute). Boundary is a threshold (⋛ comparison, in or out). Trust is directional and weighted (the `w` weighting in the Symbols table). Several bond types govern one residual, which is a reason to expect a union rather than one dial.
- **Scale.** The effect is indexed at the person, the group, and the population. Fig 4 :20 "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." A method that works at one scale is a recording if replayed at another (Fig 4 :25).

Per-slot indexing is not a stylistic preference. Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." And Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." The standard network-level statistic (one diffusion rate, one assortativity coefficient, one global echo-chamber score) is exactly the aggregation the figures forbid as a verdict. It is usable as a filter for the budget, never as a witness.

## 2. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

R described above, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor. A domain without a nameable anchor is not a domain yet.

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.** Arity three or more overlapping conditions, so a union compound (Fig 9 :54). Symmetry mixed: ordered transmission, symmetric identity, threshold boundary, directional trust weight. Scale three levels: person, group, population, with a membrane between each (Fig 4).

### Domain 1, the contact structure (habitat)

- Actors: persons as nodes, ties as edges, communities as closures.
- Currency: exposure events, reachability of a tie. A tie exists or it does not; what crosses it depends on other domains.
- Anchor: measured adoption state over time (an observation that does not move because a researcher admires the theory). This is a legitimate anchor because the question is constitutively about who adopted what.
- Question type: this is the habitat. Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 13 :24 "network" is the held structure that answers "that entities relate, and influence travels." Group boundaries are not a new atom; they are a parameter of the held network structure plus a closure wall (Domain 2).

### Domain 2, the closure and the channel (identity and trust)

- Actors: a frame's library Γ, the source frame, the receiver frame, the bonds between them.
- Currency: independence between frames and the weight a receiver gives a source (the Symbols table `w` weighting: "How far the belief leans on private history versus inherited prior"). The currency is not agreement, it is independence.
- Anchor: the ground each frame ranges against separately. Fig 2 :33 "The anchor must not update." If the reference moves when the group's opinion moves, it is not an anchor and the arrangement is the mutual-update collapse (Fig 2 :13 and :34).
- Question type: identity is a symmetric bond that lowers independence (two frames draw on the same evidence); trust is a channel property that decides whether an edge carries a signal at all; a boundary is a closure wall. These are three different objects, not three levels of one thing.

### Domain 3, the term being transmitted (the norm or behavior)

- Actors: the norm as a molecule of atoms and typed bonds.
- Currency: description cost and action cost (Fig 12 :125 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add.").
- Anchor: whether adoption persists when the source is removed, i.e. whether the term settled against the ground.
- Question type: some content is a convention (a shared meaning) and some is a verdict (a claim about the world). Fig 2 :39 "Correlation has valence." The two get opposite valence, which is a decisive part of the answer.

### Domain 4, the research and intervention frame

- Actors: researchers, platforms, would-be interveners.
- Currency: agreement and residuals, plus the budget for search.
- Anchor: the same measured adoption state as Domain 1. Agreement among researchers is not an anchor (Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you.").
- Question type: in this simulated run all four domains draw on one evidence pool, so this domain's independence is zero. Its agreement with the other seats is internal consistency, not corroboration.

### Partition or union

Union, not partition. Identity, trust and boundary overlap: a tie can be high-trust and within-boundary and identity-matched at once. Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." And Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." A single lever (add cross-group ties, say) clears the region where only boundary binds and leaves the intersections standing.

### Dependency order (Fig 3)

Perception first: can we observe who adopted what and the tie structure at all? Vocabulary second: do we have terms for boundary, channel weight and identity as distinct objects? Objective third: what counts as transmission (a verbatim copy of a phrase, or a persisted behavior)? Planning fourth: which lever, in which slot? Learn and carry fifth: which intervention survived a ground reading? "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance" (Fig 3 :20). The middle link is vocabulary to objective, which is where this field is weakest: researchers can measure exposure well but have no settled term for a channel that has become a mirror of its source. A link with no instrument is not a link that works; it is a link nobody has looked at (Fig 3 :27).

## 4. Findings

### F1 — A single transmission coefficient is the aggregation error (decisive)

Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

The question as posed invites one coefficient: how much does identity, trust or boundary change the rate? But Fig 1 disqualifies the averaged quantity as a verdict. The live signal is which slots are still failing after the average looks fine. Empirically this is exactly the pattern in the echo-chamber literature: the 2026 pre-registered experiment found that removing Twitter access and encouraging cross-ideological interaction raised polarization among politically segregated users while non-segregated users showed little or no response (Bail-style result recorded on the Echo chamber page, `10_source_ledger.csv`). One population-level coefficient would have averaged that interaction away.

Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." A global assortativity coefficient or a network-wide adoption rate is a filter that decides where to spend measurement effort. It is not a witness for the causal claim.

**Mode:** `general` on the indexing rule, `specified` on this dataset reading. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 1 and Fig 9 (low overlap: per-slot indexing versus budget/witness separation). **Refuter:** a ground measurement in which a single network-wide coefficient predicts every slot's adoption state to within measurement error, with no surviving per-slot residual. Until such a measurement exists, the per-slot reading stands and the single coefficient is demoted to a filter.

### F2 — Identity is a correlation producer, and correlation has two opposite valences (decisive)

Fig 2 :31 "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." Fig 2 :39 "Correlation has valence." Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Fig 2 :41 "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."

Identity, in the social-identity and homophily literature, is a shared category that makes people associate and trust one another (Tajfel and Turner 1979; Platow et al. 2012, both opened and quoted in `10_source_ledger.csv`). In figure terms, a shared identity means two frames draw on the same evidence and the same weighting. Their independence is low. The consequence is not uniformly bad; it splits by the kind of thing being transmitted.

- If the content is a **convention** (a norm, a style, a shared meaning), low independence is the product. Agreement is what makes the convention work. Fig 2 :41 is the governing line.
- If the content is a **verdict** (a claim about how the world is), low independence is disqualifying. Two identically situated people repeating the same claim is one evidence pool talking, not two confirmations. Fig 2 :40 is the governing line.

The question conflates these. "Norms or political behavior" spans both: a norm is mostly a convention, a partisan belief about the world is often presented as a verdict. So identity does not have one effect on transmission; it has opposite valences in the two subdomains. This is why "identity strengthens transmission" and "identity creates echo chambers" can both be true without contradiction.

**Mode:** `specified` per subdomain, `general` on the two-valence rule. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 2 and the social-identity / homophily sources (low overlap: figure law versus empirical social psychology). **Refuter:** a measurement in which identity-matched agreement carries the same evidentiary weight as agreement between frames with genuinely different evidence pools, for a claim about the world. That would collapse Fig 2's valence distinction and the finding would be withdrawn.

### F3 — Trust is a channel property, not a property of the ground (decisive)

Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Fig 10 :26 "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings." Symbols table `w`: "How far the belief leans on private history versus inherited prior."

Trust in the social-science literature is a willingness to be vulnerable on the basis of expected honesty and benevolence, and it acts as a decisional heuristic under bounded rationality (Schilke et al. 2021, via the Trust page). The social identity approach finds that trust is granted more readily to in-group members than out-group members even with minimal group cues (Platow et al. 2012; Foddy et al. 2008). Both facts are channel facts: they change whether and how much a tie carries a signal. Neither changes whether the signal is true.

Fig 2 :13 "no anchor; each updates on the others" and Fig 2 :14-15 "they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence" describe the failure mode of high trust without an external anchor: mutual update and coherent drift. A high-trust group can be perfectly self-consistent and completely wrong. Fig 2 :34 "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."

The echo-chamber literature makes the same distinction from the other side. An epistemic bubble excludes sources by omission (low connectivity, poppable by adding the missing source); an echo chamber discredits outside sources and "depends on a manipulation of trust by methodically discrediting all outside sources" (Nguyen 2020, Episteme). Manipulation of trust is an attack on the channel, not on the ground. That is why adding more information to an echo chamber can increase polarization: the channel has been calibrated to reject it (Echo chamber page, 2026 experiment).

**Mode:** `specified` on the channel reading, `general` on the ground/channel distinction. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 10, Fig 2 and the trust/echo-chamber sources (low overlap: framework versus social epistemology). **Refuter:** a measurement in which raising trust in a source changes the truth value of what the source reports, rather than only the receiver's weighting of it. Absent that, trust is a channel variable and must be scored separately from ground contact.

### F4 — A group boundary is a closure wall, and only import moves it (decisive)

Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 8 :32 "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom." Fig 8 :45 "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." Fig 8 :46 "Composition crosses the boundary between them, and that surplus is where imports live."

A group is a closure: a bounded set of terms, a shared evidence pool, and a wall. Transmission inside a closure is composition; it can rearrange what the group already holds and cannot add a genuinely new term. So a boundary changes transmission in a precise way:

- Inside a strong boundary, repetition increases confidence without increasing evidence. Fig 7 :12-13 "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not." Fig 7 :35 "A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
- Across a boundary, the surplus exists only if the two closures differ at the evidence level. Fig 8 :48-49 "How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus." Fig 8 :50 "completely are one frame wearing two names." This is why merely adding a bridge tie often does nothing: if the bridged groups already share the same evidence, the union has no surplus. The undirected union is mute (Fig 8 :19-21).
- An import needs a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :12-13, Fig 8 :52), and is debited against the importing frame's independence. "A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives" (Fig 6 :24).

**Mode:** `specified` on the boundary mechanism, `general` on the closure law. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 6, Fig 7 and Fig 8 (three figures with partly different objects: wall, handoff, surplus). **Refuter:** a measurement in which a group composed a genuinely new term with no contact with a differing closure. That would break closure idempotence and the whole boundary reading would be withdrawn.

### F5 — Trust and identity can defeat the benefit of a boundary bridge (union interaction, not a separate effect)

This is the intersection condition that a partition-style analysis misses. Fig 2 :40 makes agreement worthless when frames share an evidence pool. Fig 8 :48-49 makes the union surplus vanish under the same condition. Fig 8 :47 "Independence is one quantity read twice." So the same independence parameter that decides whether agreement means anything also decides whether a bridge buys new reach.

Put a bridge between two high-trust, identity-matched groups that already draw on the same evidence pool. The bridge exists in Domain 1 (the tie is there). But the channel is saturated with mutual trust (Domain 2) and the content is a convention (Domain 3), so no new atom crosses. The intervention has changed contact in the topological sense while failing to change contact in the figure's sense. Fig 11 :40 "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

This is a union finding, not a fourth factor. A single lever (add bridge ties) clears the region where only boundary binds and leaves the trust-plus-identity intersection standing (Fig 9 :55, :57). **Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 2, Fig 8 and Fig 11. **Refuter:** an experiment where a bridge tie between two overlapping high-trust groups reliably imports a new settled term, with provenance and a measured round-trip gap. Then the interaction claim is wrong.

### F6 — Isolation substitutes a habitat; it does not remove one (intervention side effects)

Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Fig 11 :67-69: move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose; what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails, and what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts.

Interventions that try to break an echo chamber by removing access are habitat substitutions. The 2026 pre-registered experiment found that temporarily removing Twitter access increased polarization among politically segregated users, not just cross-ideological interaction (Echo chamber page). That is the substitution law in data: the old habitat was removed, a new one silently took its place, and the goal (less polarization) was not reproduced.

Seat Map :225 "The seat's power over the habitat is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." You can add a trustworthy external anchor; you cannot subtract the group's habitat by decree. **Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** single-source on the figure side for the substitution law (Fig 11 plus seat map), corroborated by one low-overlap empirical result. **Refuter:** an intervention that removes a communication channel and shows no substituted habitat and no inherited unchosen actor, with the original goal reproduced. Then the substitution claim is narrowed.

### F7 — "Shape, not a name" governs missing primitives, not held bonds (correction kept visible)

The figures README returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)". Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."

My first pass applied this as a ban on naming identity, trust or boundary. That was an overstatement, and it is preserved here rather than erased. The six-term set already holds network (Fig 13 :24) and the Operators table already holds the bond types. Identity is a symmetric `+` bond, boundary is a `⋛` threshold parameter of the network, and trust is a `w`-style weight on a tie. All three are bindings onto held atoms and held operators. Naming them is provenance, not the invention of a seventh primitive. What the framework refuses is a **new kind** or a universal numerical coefficient that no figure line governs. See `05_what_shape_means.md` section 5b and `06_isomers_and_order.md` Q1 for the same correction in the other files.

**Mode:** `general` on the scope of the rule. **Status:** canonically confirmed by the README wording. **Refuter:** a derivation in which a seventh substrate member is licensed from the six, which would break Fig 13 indivisibility and reopen naming.

### F8 — No figure line returns the universal coefficient (declared gap)

After the genuine re-census in section 2, no figure line selects a number for how much identity, trust or boundary changes transmission, no line ranks the three factors globally, and no line returns a universal threshold for when a bridge helps. Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence," and Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

This is a `gap` of the **shape-not-a-name** kind, not an underivable-in-principle claim: the figures fix the shape (union, per-slot, different bonds, different anchors, two valences of correlation) and leave the numerical fill to a measurement. It is also partly unreachable-at-this-budget, because the required instrument is a per-slot discriminator of ground contact versus channel weight that does not yet exist in this package's sources. Underivable and unreachable-at-budget issue different instructions (Fig 9 :40-43), and I have not blurred them.

**Mode:** `gap`. **Status:** gap. **Refuter:** a figure re-census that derives a coefficient, or a ground measurement that returns one with the per-slot residual closed. Until then, unreached is the honest report.

### F9 — Settled is necessary and not sufficient for a term to be composed over

A norm that has spread widely has been repeated, not necessarily settled. Fig 5 amendment: a term is settled when the ground pays for it and usable when something can compose over it, and these are two conditions. A library that grows is not a reach that grows. Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."

So high transmission is not evidence of a settled norm. A slogan can saturate a network and still be unusable as a premise for anything else. The check is whether something can compose over it, which needs an instrument (a downstream behavior that depends on the term), not a count of repetitions. **Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** single-source for the figure rule (Fig 5 and Fig 12 agree, but share the framework pool). **Refuter:** a case where a widely repeated term composes over everything downstream with no settlement against a ground; that would break the necessity claim.

### F10 — The chain breaks between vocabulary and objective in this field

Fig 3 :25-27: perception and planning have obvious tests; the link between having terms for what a situation affords and saying what would count as progress has neither, so it attracts the least attention. Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

In identity and trust research, exposure is measurable (contact data, network ties) and outcomes are measurable (adoption, voting, sharing). What is not well settled is the vocabulary: is "trust" a channel weight, a ground claim, or a shared meaning? Is "identity" a symmetric bond or a closure boundary? Until those are named as distinct objects, any "objective" for transmission is measuring a blend. The echo-chamber literature's own fragmentation (unclear definitions, inconsistent measurement, unrepresentative data, noted on the Echo chamber page) is a direct empirical signature of a broken middle link. Effort aimed at better prediction before the vocabulary is fixed lands above the break and cannot rescue it (Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not.").

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 3 and the empirical measurement-fragmentation source. **Refuter:** a study that fixes the vocabulary and shows prediction was the binding link all along; then the break is relocated, not denied.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach: Seat Map :287 "And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Aggregation refuter.** A single network-wide transmission coefficient predicts every slot's adoption state to within measurement error, with no surviving per-slot residual (breaks Fig 1 :19, :22).
2. **Valence refuter.** Identity-matched agreement carries the same evidentiary weight as agreement between genuinely independent frames for a claim about the world (breaks Fig 2 :39-41).
3. **Channel refuter.** Raising trust in a source changes the truth value of what it reports, not only the receiver's weighting (breaks Fig 10 :24).
4. **Closure refuter.** A group composes a genuinely new term with no contact with a differing closure (breaks Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :32).
5. **Union refuter.** One lever closes all conditions including the trust-plus-identity intersection, so the residual is a partition after all (breaks Fig 9 :54-57).
6. **Substitution refuter.** Removing a channel produces no substituted habitat and reproduces the original goal (breaks Fig 11 :66-69).
7. **Instrument refuter.** A per-slot instrument is produced that returns a stable numerical coefficient and closes R, converting the `gap` to `grounded`.

If any fires, the affected finding is withdrawn and the retraction is recorded in section 7. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Lead with F1 and F2. The aggregation error and the two-valence rule are the strongest figure-grounded points and they are checkable by reading four short passages.
2. Keep the three factor verdicts separate. Do not average identity, trust and boundary into one effect size (Fig 1 :19, Fig 9 :60).
3. Re-ground the intervention question as habitat contact, not channel removal (F6). Add a trustworthy external anchor rather than trying to subtract a habitat.
4. Report the universal coefficient as a `gap` with F8's instrument owed. Unreachable is a verdict, not silence.
5. Send the generator (the refuter and the shape), not a recording of the conclusion (Fig 4 :25 "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion.").

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is there a fact here or several?** Fig 9 :4 "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones." Identity, trust and boundary are three conditions with different anchors, so split them (F1, F2, F3, F4).
2. **What is the residual?** Per-slot departure from uniform contagion (Fig 1 :19).
3. **Which frame predicts its shape?** Closure theory for boundaries (Fig 6), independence theory for identity (Fig 2), channel theory for trust (Fig 10). Describe the effect first, then pick the frame (Fig 9 :27-28).
4. **Is the union a partition?** No. Overlapping conditions, intersections categorical (Fig 9 :54-57).
5. **Is anything owed as an atom?** No. All three bind to held atoms and bonds. A new atom would be a wrong import (Fig 6 :24).
