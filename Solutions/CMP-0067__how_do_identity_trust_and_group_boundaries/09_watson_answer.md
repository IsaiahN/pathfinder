## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the claim that identity, trust and group boundaries change the transmission of norms or political behavior across networks. Option A: treat the three as dials on one transmission coefficient and report a numerical effect for each. Option B: treat them as a union of overlapping conditions with different anchors and bonds, index the residual per slot, keep verdicts per domain, and declare the coefficient a gap. What each leads to: A averages away the live signal, mistakes a shared evidence pool for corroboration, and presents a name where the framework returns a shape; B returns a checkable separator, six failure conditions written in advance, and an honest stop where the instrument runs out.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Per-slot departure of observed adoption from uniform-contagion prediction. Arity: at least three overlapping conditions (who is connected, whether the tie carries the signal, whether the receiver is disposed). Symmetry: mixed, ordered influence, symmetric identity, threshold boundary, weighted trust. Scale: person, group, population, with a membrane between scales (Fig 4 :20 "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change.").
- **Domain 1, contact structure.** Actors: persons and ties, communities as closures. Currency: exposure and reachability. Anchor: measured adoption state over time. Question type: the habitat, enumerated not composed (Fig 11 :43).
- **Domain 2, closure and channel.** Actors: source frame, receiver frame, each library, the bond. Currency: independence between frames and the weighting a receiver gives a source (Symbols table `w`). Anchor: a ground that does not update (Fig 2 :33 "The anchor must not update."). Question type: identity is correlation, trust is channel weight, boundary is a closure wall, three distinct objects.
- **Domain 3, the transmitted term.** Actors: the norm as a molecule. Currency: description cost and action cost (Fig 12 :125 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."). Anchor: whether adoption persists and composes downstream. Question type: convention versus verdict, which get opposite correlation valences.
- **Domain 4, research and intervention.** Actors: researchers, platforms, interveners. Currency: agreement and residuals, plus search budget. Anchor: the same measured adoption state, never agreement. In this simulated run all domains share one evidence pool, so independence here is zero.
- **Partition or union.** Union, not partition (Fig 9 :55, :57). A tie can be within-boundary, high-trust and identity-matched at once; one lever leaves the intersections standing.
- **Order by dependency.** Perception (observe per slot), vocabulary (name the three conditions), objective (define transmission), planning (choose lever and slot), learn and carry (what survived the ground). The middle link, vocabulary to objective, has no instrument (Fig 3 :27).

Strain test: Fig 2 :40 and Fig 2 :41 appear to contradict (correlation disqualifying versus efficient) but govern verdicts and conventions respectively, so re-census dissolves the strain. Fig 8's union surplus versus backfire from bridges is dissolved by Fig 8 :48-50 (surplus requires differing closures). One live strain remains: boundary as a threshold parameter of the held network atom rather than a seventh primitive.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

Transmission is a union of three overlapping conditions with different anchors, indexed per slot. Identity is a symmetric correlation producer with content-dependent valence: efficient for a convention, disqualifying for a verdict about the world. Trust is a channel weight, so high trust without a non-updating anchor gives coherent drift, not accuracy. A boundary is a closure wall: repetition inside it adds confidence without adding evidence, and a new term crosses only from a differing closure with provenance and a shadow test. The conditions interact, so a bridge over a shared evidence pool changes topology without changing contact, and isolation substitutes a habitat rather than removing it. No universal coefficient, factor ranking or bridge threshold is returned; that is a declared `gap`. This is `specified` per domain, `general` on the shape, ungrounded, candidate. The strongest single point is the two-valence rule, because it explains why the same factor can both strengthen a norm and corrupt a verdict without contradiction.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode, six failure conditions and per-slot indexing before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a number or a recording instead of a separator and its refuter. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and the refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The averaged coefficient and the universal ranking are removable without touching the derivation; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove them. No primitive has to be withdrawn, and a sent claim can be superseded by the corrected packet.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A per-slot discriminator returning channel weight, independence and settlement state for the same event would close the gap; a single coefficient that predicts every slot with no residual, identity-matched agreement weighted equally for world claims, a truth-value change from raising trust, a group composing a new term with no differing closure, a single lever closing all intersections, or a channel removal with no substitution would each fire one of the six refuters and break the affected finding. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
