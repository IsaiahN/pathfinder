# Expert verification packet: the Tether on identity, trust and group boundaries

**What this is:** a claim in a form a social-network, social-psychology or computational-social-science researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the universal coefficient. Not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Transmission of a norm or political behavior is not one coefficient with three inputs. It is a union of three overlapping conditions with different anchors, indexed per person, tie and group. Identity is a symmetric correlation producer, so its effect flips by the kind of content: efficient for a convention, disqualifying for a verdict about the world. Trust is a channel weight, not a property of the ground, so high trust with no external anchor gives coherent drift rather than accuracy. A group boundary is a closure wall, so repetition inside it raises confidence without adding evidence and a new term crosses only from a differing closure. The three overlap, so a bridge over a shared evidence pool changes topology without changing contact, and isolation substitutes a habitat rather than removing one. No universal numerical coefficient is returned; that is a declared gap.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1 and 2

- **Statement:** the residual must be indexed per slot, and correlation has two opposite valences depending on whether the transmitted content is a convention or a verdict.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 2 :39 "Correlation has valence." Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool" and Fig 2 :41 "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."
- **What to check:** are the two valences really different objects, or one effect stated twice? Look at Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." and the convention example in :41.
- **Refuter:** a measurement in which identity-matched agreement carries the same evidentiary weight as agreement between genuinely independent frames for a claim about the world, or a single coefficient predicts every slot with no surviving residual. If either fires, this sub-claim is withdrawn.

### C2 — world-facing; checkable by a researcher against their own literature

- **Statement:** trust changes whether a tie carries a signal, and discrediting outside sources is what makes a closed group resistant to correction; removing a channel substitutes a habitat rather than deleting it.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Fig 2 :14 "they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence." Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Seat Map :225 "The seat's power over the habitat is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."
- **What to check:** in the echo-chamber and trust literature, is the manipulation-of-trust distinction real (epistemic bubble versus echo chamber), and did the 2026 pre-registered experiment find that removing access raised polarization among politically segregated users? Is a bridge between already-overlapping groups known to have no surplus?
- **Refuter:** raising trust in a source changes the truth value of what it reports, not only the receiver's weighting; or an intervention that removes a channel shows no substituted habitat and reproduces its original goal. Either would break this sub-claim.

### C3 — framework-internal derivation; checkable by reading Figures 8, 9 and 11

- **Statement:** the three conditions form a union, not a partition, and two networks with the same nodes but different boundary or trust arrangements are isomers over a fixed atom set.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Fig 8 :32 "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom." Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- **What to check:** does one lever close all three conditions and their intersections? If so, this is a partition and the union claim fails. Does the isomer claim require a fixed atom set (it does; adding an external trusted source is an import, not a rearrangement)?
- **Refuter:** an intervention with a single lever that leaves no per-slot residual across all intersections, or a demonstrated relaxation of the fixed-atom condition with no wall movement.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a universal transmission coefficient, a percentage attribution, or a global ranking of the three factors. That is a `gap` (Fig 9 :32).
- Not that trust or boundaries are uniformly good or bad. Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49) and correlation's valence is content-dependent.
- Not that adding more ties always helps. A bridge over a shared evidence pool has no surplus (Fig 8 :48-50), and a wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives (Fig 6 :24).
- Not that removing a platform or channel removes a habitat. It substitutes one (Fig 11 :66-69).
- Not that a widely repeated norm is settled (Fig 5 amendment).
- Not a new substrate primitive. Boundary is a threshold parameter of the held network atom; identity and trust are bindings onto held bonds and the `w` weighting.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment, and this packet is not a test of the claim.

## 5. What would settle it

A per-slot discriminator, not agreement. Three readings for the same event: (1) the receiver's channel weight for the source; (2) the independence between the two frames' evidence pools; (3) whether the transmitted term composes over anything downstream once the source is removed. Plus a substitution test after any channel removal. The six standing refuters in `01` section 5 are the failure conditions. Agreement with this packet, especially after a reader has seen the figures, is not evidence (Fig 2 :40).
