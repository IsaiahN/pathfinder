## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Does "shape, not a name" forbid naming identity, trust and boundary? (correction carried from `05` section 5b)

- The figures README: "what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." The subject is a primitive not held in Γ.
- Network is one of the six held structures (Fig 13 :24). The symmetric, ordered and threshold bonds and the weighting are all held. None of the three factors is a missing primitive.
- Therefore naming them is a **binding** from the empirical frame onto held terms. What is forbidden is a seventh member, a universal coefficient, or a name derived from a reading without a discriminator.
- **I overstated this earlier** and the overstatement is preserved in `01` F7 and `05` section 5b. Retracted as applied to these three bindings; triangulated (README + Fig 13).

### Q2 — Is the residual a partition or a union?

- Fig 9 :4 "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones."
- Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- Identity, trust and boundary overlap. A tie can be within-boundary, high-trust and identity-matched at once. So the residual is a union.
- Consequence: one lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. Adding bridge ties fixes the pure-boundary region but not the bridge-plus-high-trust-plus-shared-evidence intersection (01 F5). This is corroborated by the 2026 experiment in which encouraging cross-ideological interaction raised polarization specifically among already-segregated users.
- **Refuter, written before the run:** an intervention that closes all three conditions and their intersections with a single lever, with no surviving per-slot residual. Then the partition reading wins and this Q is withdrawn. Status `specified`, triangulated (Fig 9 + the low-overlap experiment).

### Q3 — Are two networks with the same nodes but different arrangements isomers?

- Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :5 "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
- Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance."
- Take two networks with the identical node set (same people, same norms available) but different arrangements: in one, high-trust ties run within identity-matched clusters and a single weak bridge crosses; in the other, high-trust ties cross clusters and within-cluster ties are weak. The atom lists are identical. The bonds differ. By Fig 12 they are isomers: different substances with different transmission behavior, and the difference is invisible in a node-and-edge count.
- The strong form requires a **fixed atom set**. If the second network also introduces a new kind of node, an external trusted source for instance, that is an import (Fig 6 :23), not an isomer. The fixed-atom condition is a live constraint, named here rather than hidden. Status `specified`, single-source on the figure definition with the network-science sources as corroboration of arrangement sensitivity.

### Q4 — What is the order of the union?

- Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
- Dependency order for a measurement:
  1. **Perception:** can we observe adoption state and tie structure per slot? This link works in the literature (contact data, surveys, platform logs).
  2. **Vocabulary:** do we have distinct terms for boundary (closure), trust (channel weight) and identity (correlation)? This is the broken middle link (01 F10; Fig 3 :27).
  3. **Objective:** what counts as transmission? A verbatim repetition is not a persisted behavior. Fig 5 amendment: settling is necessary and not sufficient; a repeated term may not compose over anything.
  4. **Planning:** which lever in which slot? Import a trusted external anchor rather than subtracting a habitat (Fig 11 :66; Seat Map :225).
  5. **Learn and carry:** which intervention survived a ground reading, and what would reverse it?
- Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." The residual must be described before the field is chosen.
- Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." What transfers from a person-level finding to a group-level intervention is the method, not the recording of one success.
- **Falsifier:** if a study shows that prediction quality, not vocabulary, is the binding link in this field, the break relocates below step 2 and the order claim is narrowed (not denied). Written before the run.

### Q5 — Why the two currencies matter here

- Fig 12 :125 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 12 :126 "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."
- A short slogan over a costly operand is cheap to state and may take a whole budget to settle (Fig 12 :128-129). A long chain of cheap repetitions is expensive to describe and may cost one action to refute.
- Therefore "how far it spread" (a description-cost proxy) and "how much it cost to check whether the claim holds" (an action-cost proxy) are two readings that do not add. Summing them into one transmission score is the error Fig 12 :130 names: "Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it." This is a structural reason, independent of F1, why a single coefficient is disqualified.

## CONCLUSION

The residual is a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition. Two networks with the same nodes and different boundary or trust arrangements are isomers under Fig 12, provided the atom set is held fixed; introducing a new external source is an import instead. The dependency order puts vocabulary before objective, and the middle link is the one with no instrument. Two currencies (description cost and action cost) must be read separately, which is a second structural reason to refuse a single coefficient. Every claim here is `specified` except the dependency order, which is `general`.
