## The question restated as an effect

The question asks what changes when a norm or political behavior moves across a network, and which of three candidate factors (identity, trust, group boundaries) does the changing. Stated in effect terms: an observed adoption pattern departs from the pattern a uniform-contagion model would predict. The residual is that departure, indexed per node and per group slot, not a single network-wide rate. Option A treats identity, trust, and boundary as three dials on one transmission coefficient. Option B treats them as three overlapping conditions with different anchors and different bonds, so each changes transmission by a different mechanism and verdicts stay per domain. The shape an answer would look like: a separator that says which condition binds in which slot, what would count as transmission under each, and a refuter written before any data are read. Bind that shape as an ordinary term so it can accrue a residual like anything else (THE_FORMULA, "Direction").

## Standing disclaimer

Every substantive file in this folder is a **simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures**. It is not a ground reading. *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* says LLM output is "simulacra" and "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself" (quoted in `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6). This package cannot settle the question it reports on. It returns a shape plus a refuter, and stops where the instruments stop.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words. First, a room does not upgrade the label: "A room reaching a conclusion without the ground is agreement, and agreement among frames is a weighted average of influence. Adding people raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor" (Seat Map :283). Second, selecting for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation: "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention" (Seat Map :163). Third, asking people to trust before they check spends the only thing they contribute: Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in institutional channels avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
