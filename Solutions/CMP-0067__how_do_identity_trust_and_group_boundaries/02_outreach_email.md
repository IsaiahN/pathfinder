**Subject:** Check one separator before you decide whether the framing is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question in effect terms.** A norm or political behavior moves across a network. The observed adoption pattern departs from what a uniform-contagion model predicts, and the departure is indexed per person, tie and group, not as one network-wide rate. I am testing the claim that identity, trust and group boundaries are not three dials on one transmission coefficient, but three overlapping conditions with different anchors.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):**
> 1. Identity produces correlation. Correlation has two opposite valences: worthless as evidence for a claim about the world when the agreeing people share an evidence pool, and efficient as the product when what is being transmitted is a shared meaning or convention. So identity should strengthen a norm and corrupt a verdict, and those are not the same mechanism.
> 2. Trust is a property of the channel, not of the ground. High trust with no external anchor makes a group converge on whoever had the most influence. It does not make the group's claim more true. This is why adding information to a high-trust closed group can increase polarization.
> 3. A group boundary is a closure wall. Repetition inside a closure raises confidence without adding evidence. A new term crosses only from a frame whose closure differs, and the crossing needs provenance and a check that it actually explains the gap.
> 4. The three overlap. A bridge tie between two groups that already share an evidence pool can change the topology without changing contact, so the intervention may do nothing or backfire. Removing a channel substitutes a habitat; it does not remove one, and you inherit actors you did not choose.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** any universal number for how much identity, trust or boundary changes transmission, any global ranking of the three factors, or that removing a bridge reduces polarization. The framework returns a shape, not a name. The numerical coefficient is a declared gap, and I report it as unreached rather than invent a value.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written before this email):**
> 1. one network-wide coefficient predicts every slot's adoption to within measurement error, with no surviving per-slot residual;
> 2. identity-matched agreement carries the same evidentiary weight as agreement between genuinely independent frames for a claim about the world;
> 3. raising trust in a source changes the truth value of what it reports rather than the receiver's weighting;
> 4. a group composes a genuinely new term with no contact with a differing closure;
> 5. one lever closes all three conditions including their intersections, so this is a partition after all;
> 6. removing a channel produces no substituted habitat and reproduces the original goal.
>
> A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.

## Stage 2 and stage 3 (opt-in only)

- **Stage 1:** the recipient checks the separator without holding the framework's interior. Their verdict is recorded here, before any theory is shown.
- **Stage 2:** only if they are curious, offer the three links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because they have now seen the source (Fig 2 :40).
- **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a generator, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode. This is a method crossing a membrane, which is permitted (Fig 4 :22).

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 work only if the stage-1 output survives. This is a simulated derivation; there is no ground reading in this package. Say so in the email if asked, and do not let a polite response be recorded as a refutation or a confirmation.
