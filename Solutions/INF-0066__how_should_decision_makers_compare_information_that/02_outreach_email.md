**Subject:** Would these two kinds of information fit on one scale? A five-minute check

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific classification claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual.** In value-of-information work we price information in one number, usually money or expected utility. But two items behave differently. One narrows belief about a decision whose action set is fixed. The other changes which actions are available at all: a new instrument, a new counterparty, a new option, or information that invalidates an option we thought we had. My claim is that the first kind can never become the second by gathering more of it, and that the two cannot be added without losing something the decision needs.
>
> **The shape claim (`general` on the structure, `specified` on the application).** The first kind is within-closure search: it lowers prediction error per case, but keeps the same atoms and the same wall. The second kind moves the wall, adds a term, changes contact, or removes the standing of a term we were building on. Those two are priced in different units. Bits price the description; actions price finding out whether it holds. A system holding only one accounting will not notice the shortfall. So the claim is: keep two ledgers, report per case rather than as one aggregate, and do not assert an exchange rate.
>
> This is a **shape**, not a decision rule with weights. I am not claiming to have the right number for anything, and I am not claiming that your field's EVPI or EVSI calculations are wrong. Those are budget tools that rank which research to buy. The claim is about what a budget tool cannot settle.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a common unit that ranks both kinds for the same decision, with a measured round trip rather than an asserted equivalence;
> 2. a case where within-closure search adds a genuinely new atom or moves the wall under a fixed library;
> 3. a case where an item creates a genuinely new possible action with no change of reach, contact or standing;
> 4. an aggregate value that provably preserves the per-case "where to look" signal and the overlaps;
> 5. an import that changes the ground itself rather than the decision-maker's access to it.
>
> I wrote these down before writing the conclusion. If any one holds, I will withdraw the two-ledger claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you'd rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **The verdict I am asking for, before any theory:** do you know a decision setting where these two kinds of information really can be ranked on one scale without losing a constraint? If yes, tell me the setting and I will record it as a refutation. If no, tell me what you would count as a failure and I will use that instead of my own list.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures and formula as SVGs, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - The field's own statement of the problem, Glynn et al. 2022: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/environmental-science/articles/10.3389/fenvs.2022.805245/full
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state whether your output is `general`, `specified` or `grounded`. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [name]

## Two-stage offer, stated plainly

1. **Stage 1:** the residual, the two-ledger shape claim and the five refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict **before** showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the three links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because once they hold the framework's interior their independence is spent.
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter, supplies their own ground and states the mode of what they get.

Stage 1 before stage 2 is Fig 10 :52 and Fig 2 :45. Stage 2 is opt-in by construction. Stage 3 sends a generator, which Fig 4 :22 permits. Every stage still needs a ground: `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5 says *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."*
