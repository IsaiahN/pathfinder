## Three things in the wider literature that bear directly on this question

1. **Howard 1966 already knew the scale problem.** His abstract says Shannon's measure depends only on probabilities, so *"no theory that involves just the probabilities of outcomes without considering their consequences could possibly be adequate in describing the importance of uncertainty to a decision maker."* He also found that *"the joint elimination of the uncertainty about a number of even independent factors in a problem can have a value that differs from the sum of the values of eliminating the uncertainty in each factor separately."* This is a low-overlap witness for Fig 12 :125-130 and Fig 1 :22. It is not a figure line and cannot decide the verdict.
2. **Glynn et al. 2022 name the missing branch.** VE3 is information *"that ends up invalidating a model or set of beliefs, or disproving a hypothesis"*, and the paper says it is valuable even though VoI studies rarely consider it. This is the fifth kind of Fig 5 :51-55, from an independent field. It supports the inclusion of refusal as an action-changing item.
3. **Chemero 2024 makes "possible actions" relational.** Affordances are features of the environment relative to an actor's action capabilities, not properties of the information and not mental entities. That is the world-side counterpart of Fig 11 :39, *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*
