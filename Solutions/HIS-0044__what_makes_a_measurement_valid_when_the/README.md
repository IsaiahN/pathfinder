## Question

What makes a measurement valid when the thing being measured, such as intelligence, health, or poverty, is contested?

**Source:** `history of science and knowledge.txt` line 55. **Section:** 5. Categories, facts, and measurement.

**Field for public contact points:** measurement validity research.

## What is being decided, in effect terms

Two options lead to different places. Option A: a measurement of a contested construct is valid when qualified people agree on it, so consensus and a published coefficient settle the matter. Option B: agreement is only a convention until the reading ranges against an anchor that does not move when frames push on it, the construct is described in effect terms before a frame is chosen, and a validation argument survives independent refutation. A buys a convention and can drift invisibly. B buys a checkable shape and leaves the specific operationalisation open until a ground settles it.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.

Reason, in the figures' own words: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." (Fig 2 :35) and "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool" (Fig 2 :40). Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures exactly the correlation that makes their agreement worthless, and asking them to accept the framework before they check the output spends their independence.
