# The Tether's answer: what makes a measurement valid when the construct is contested

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading. The specific operationalisation of intelligence, health, or poverty is a reported `gap`, not a silent omission.

## 1. The residual it answers

A contested construct produces readings that do not settle the dispute about what the construct is. The dispute is a **union compound**, not one hard question: ground contact and construct meaning are overlapping conditions, each with its own loop and its own ground (Fig 9 :54, :57). Reliability, larger samples and expert agreement act mostly on the first and leave the second standing.

## 2. The shape

1. **A reading is valid only if it does not move when frames push on it.** Fig 2 :29-30: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." Fig 1 :27: "A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." Consensus and reliability are frame-internal, so they cannot be the verdict.
2. **The anchor must be legitimated constitutively, not by majority or prestige.** Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Fig 2 :37-38: human judgment is the right anchor for a question about people, but "instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure." For each construct, ask what the question is about before deciding what may settle it. The figures give the test; they do not pre-classify intelligence, health, or poverty.
3. **A declared meaning is not a grounded meaning.** Fig 10 :49: "Declaring a meaning does not ground it." Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." Defining a construct as "what this test measures" and then using the score to allocate an outcome is the named failure. Cronbach and Meehl reach the same point from psychometrics: "Rationalization is not construct validation", and the construct is "at best adopted, never demonstrated to be 'correct.'"
4. **Describe the effect before choosing the frame.** Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." State what you would expect to see if the construct were present or absent, before selecting indicators. This is the "Direction" discipline made public: bind what an answer would look like and let it accrue a residual.
5. **Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong.** Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 2 :40: agreement is "disqualifying for a verdict" when frames share an evidence pool. Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." A benefit-allocating poverty index needs more independence than an academic description.
6. **Validity is a property of the arrangement.** Fig 11 :39-40: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone" and "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Adding items or precision without changing contact with the ground and the consequences of use does not change validity. Messick's unified validity, which includes consequences, converges on the same point from a different field.
7. **The specific operationalisation stays open.** Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." The framework returns a shape. Naming a particular test or index is a binding from a world frame, recorded with provenance and a shadow test, not a derivation.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any existing test, index, or poverty line is invalid. The derivation has no ground reading and takes no position on a named measure.
- Not that consensus is useless. It is "efficient for a convention" (Fig 2 :41); it is "disqualifying for a verdict" (Fig 2 :40). Conventions are needed to coordinate meaning. They cannot settle truth.
- Not that reliability is irrelevant. It is necessary for a usable channel (Fig 10 :24-28), but it is not sufficient for validity.
- Not that the figures name the correct construct. They give a constitutive test and a shape, not a classification.
- Not that a gap is a failure. "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" (Fig 13 :134).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here has been settled by an instrument, dataset, experiment or expert verdict.

## 4. What would settle it : the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it"; the color case was resolved "not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." Here the corresponding questions are:

- **Anchor test:** for construct C, is there a reading that does not move when the measuring frames push on it, and can it be shown to be constitutionally legitimate for the question being asked?
- **Source-discrimination test:** can an instrument distinguish rival constructs that currently produce the same score (Fig 12 :5, the bond the formula did not record)?
- **Independence test:** were the corroborating frames drawing on different evidence, or one pool under two names (Fig 2 :40, Fig 8 :48)?
- **Consequence test:** does the score remain valid when its use changes, or does the channel saturate and the reading decay (Fig 10 :24-28)?
- **Standing falsifiers:** any one of the six in `01` §4 or `08` §3 fires.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The figure set contains the anchor rule and the constitutive-legitimacy rule; it does not contain a unique operationalisation of a contested construct. That is reported as `gap`. The answer stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. "The recursion converges toward indiscernibility, and indiscernibility is not identity" (*How Intelligence Actually Works*). A second seat re-censused the residual from the line alone in `11_audit.md`; it shares this derivation's evidence pool, so its agreement is internal consistency, not corroboration.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not pick a test of intelligence, a health index, or a poverty line, and it does not claim that agreement among experts makes any of them valid. It says a measurement of a contested construct has to range against an anchor that does not change when the measuring frames push on it; that the anchor has to be legitimate because the question is constitutively about it, not because most people prefer it; that a declared definition is a convention until it contacts observations through explicit public inference, and a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention; that the construct must be described in effect terms before an indicator set is chosen; that corroboration must be bought with independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong; and that validity belongs to the whole arrangement of instrument, construct, habitat and consequences, not to the instrument alone. It refuses to name the right operationalisation, because the framework returns a shape rather than a name and reports that specific answer as a gap. What it offers is the shape any valid measurement must have, the falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The center stays open until the ground settles it.
