## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

R is the standing disagreement about what the construct is, which shows up as readings that do not settle it. Different frames (research programmes, institutions, affected communities) use one term, "intelligence", "health", "poverty", for different things. A score produced inside one frame does not resolve the dispute; it usually re-encodes that frame's own convention and presents it as a reading.

- **Arity:** more than one contesting frame, at least two. The disagreement is not a single hard question (compare Fig 9 :57, "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either").
- **Symmetry:** the contesting frames are symmetric claimants. Swapping frame A for frame B leaves the structure of the dispute unchanged. The anchor is the asymmetric term: it is "pinned: it does not update on A or B" (Fig 2 :25).
- **Scale:** the reading is first order (an instrument applied to a case). Validity is a property of the whole arrangement, instrument plus construct plus habitat plus ground, not of the instrument alone: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone" (Fig 11 :39).

What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (formula step "Direction"): a *valid measurement* is a reading that ranges against an anchor which does not move when frames push on it, whose construct is described in effect terms before a frame is chosen, whose independence was bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong, and whose validation argument states in advance what would refute it. The slot "which anchor, which operationalisation" stays marked `???`. That is a shape. Naming a specific IQ test, a specific health index, or a specific poverty line is a binding from a world frame and needs provenance, not a derivation.

## 1. Domain census, before choosing any figure line

One line per domain with actors, currency, anchor. A domain with no nameable anchor is not a domain yet.

- **Domain 1, the reading and instrument.** Actors: instrument, operation, reading `o'`, the case (person, household, patient). Currency: observations. Anchor: the ground, "whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks" (Fig 2 :29). Nameable, so this is a domain.
- **Domain 2, the construct definition and its conventions.** Actors: researchers, standards bodies, communities, operational definitions, nomological nets. Currency: meanings and conventions. Anchor: initially **none**. That is exactly why the construct is contested. It becomes a domain only when an anchor is supplied, either because the question is constitutively about the judgment (Fig 2 :36) or because a definition contacts observations through "explicit, public steps of inference" (Cronbach and Meehl 1955). Until then, naming it a domain would be premature. Recorded as a **provisional domain with an absent anchor**.
- **Domain 3, verification and use.** Actors: reviewers, users of the measure, affected persons, institutions that act on the score. Currency: agreement, residuals, consequences. Anchor: the ground, never agreement (Fig 2 :45). Nameable, so this is a domain.

**Partition or union.** R is a **union compound**, not one hard question and not a clean partition. At least two conditions overlap: (a) does the reading track an anchor (ground contact), and (b) is the construct definition the right one (meaning and consequence). Fig 9 :54: "when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." A single lever, more reliability, more expert agreement, a bigger sample, clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. Each condition needs its own loop and its own ground.

**Dependency order (Fig 3).** Perception (can a reading be obtained at all?) → vocabulary (are there terms for what the construct affords, and what would count as progress?) → objective → planning → learn and carry. For contested constructs the chain usually breaks at the second link: "having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments" (Fig 3 :25-27). "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not" (Fig 3 :19). So finer items and larger samples (link 1) cannot repair a broken vocabulary (link 2).

## 3. Findings

### F1 : A reading is an anchor only if it does not move when frames push on it (decisive)

Fig 2 :29-30:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."**

Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."* Fig 2 :34: if it does, *"everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."*

A score that changes when the scoring frame's preferences change is not a measurement of the contested construct. It is an instrument for the frame. A poverty line that is redrawn whenever the measured headcount becomes inconvenient is not an anchor. Fig 1 :23: *"The ground enters as the only metric."* Fig 1 :27: *"A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Reliability, item count, inter-rater agreement and coverage are frame-internal: they are quantities the measuring frame produces, so they cannot score the measuring frame.

Triangulated: Fig 2 (anchor) and Fig 1 (only metric, frame-internal quantities disqualified), low overlap between the triangulation figure and the agent figure. World corroboration: Cronbach and Meehl 1955 require that the construct's network "make contact with observations" through "explicit, public steps of inference", and warn that "Rationalization is not construct validation"; SEP *Measurement in Science* notes realists take quantities to be "independent of the beliefs and conventions of the humans performing the measurement and of the methods used for measuring."

**Refuter:** show a case where a reading that moves when frames push on it still settles the contested construct without any independent anchor. If that exists, F1 is false.

### F2 : Anchor legitimacy is constitutive, not a matter of majority or prestige

Fig 2 :36:

> **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."**

Fig 2 :37-38:

> **"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."**

This is the pivot for contested constructs. Before asking which measure is valid, ask what the question is constitutively about. For "how well is this person living?", the person's own judgment may be the ground, not a fallible proxy. For "is this person's oxygenation adequate?", a clinical reading is the ground and self-report is only an instrument, wrong on axes it does not measure. The figures do not tell you which case intelligence, health, or poverty falls into. They give the test. Fig 13 :16 names what is being measured and Fig 13 :26 decides what the measurement would mean; the two terms are distinct and neither does the job alone (see the "Decoding Tacit Knowledge" essay, verified 2026-11-23).

Triangulated: Fig 2 :36-38 and Fig 13 :16/:26, different figures, low overlap. Single-source on the specific classification of any named construct, and stated as such.

**Refuter:** produce a case where the entity the question is constitutively about is not a legitimate anchor, or where an instrument on an axis it does not measure is nevertheless the ground for a question about that very axis.

### F3 : A declared meaning is not a grounded meaning, and a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one

Fig 10 :18 and :49:

> **"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"**
> **"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."**

Fig 10 :47:

> **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**

This is the operationalist trap in the contested-construct case. Define intelligence as "what this test measures", publish the test, and then use the score to decide school placement: the convention has quietly become a verdict. It now decides an outcome, so by Fig 10 :47 it is no longer a convention, and by Fig 10 :49 the declaration never grounded it. Fig 5 :43: *"A term that explains everything by saying nothing fails this"*; a construct defined solely by its indicator is exactly such a term.

Fig 10 :24-28 adds the failure mode of the channel: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*, through *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*. A well validated scale administered under a changed institution is a changed channel, and the output is *"you have lost touch"* (Fig 10 :28), not a verdict.

World corroboration is strong and independent of the framework. SEP *Operationalism* (Gillies 1972, as reported there): "if the measurement method defines the concept and there is nothing more to the meaning of the concept, the measurement method is automatically valid, as a matter of convention or even tautology. Metrological validity becomes an interesting question only if the concept possesses a broader meaning than the specification of the method of its measurement." The same entry records Bridgman: "If we have more than one set of operations, we have more than one concept, and strictly there should be a separate name to correspond to each different set of operations." Cronbach and Meehl: "construct validation cannot be claimed" unless the network contacts observations with explicit public inference; "Rationalization is not construct validation"; and "The construct is at best adopted, never demonstrated to be 'correct.'"

Triangulated: Fig 10 (:18, :47, :49, :24-28) and Fig 5 :43, plus two independent world sources.

**Refuter:** show a contested construct whose operational definition genuinely grounds itself, with no anchor outside the definition, and a case where a convention that decides an outcome remains a legitimate convention.

### F4 : Describe the effect before choosing the frame

Fig 9 :27-28:

> **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**

Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."* Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."*

In a contested construct the temptation is to pick a favoured theory (a specific model of intelligence, a specific definition of health, a specific poverty line) and then find data that fit. Fig 9 :53: *"Nobody chooses. The gap does, and the gap is only as good as its description."* Residual first, frame second. The practical form: write down, in effect terms, what you would expect to observe if the construct were present and if it were absent, before selecting the indicator set. This is also the formula's "Direction" discipline made public: write down what an answer would look like, bind it, let it accrue a residual.

World corroboration: the "Decoding Tacit Knowledge" essay states the same operational rule, "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." Cronbach and Meehl make the same demand on construct theory: it "must be reasonably explicit so that validating evidence may be properly interpreted", and "Unless substantially the same nomological net is accepted by the several users of the construct, public validation is impossible."

**Refuter:** demonstrate that frame-first selection reaches a construct definition that independent users with different priors accept as valid, with no prior effect description and no shared evidence pool.

### F5 : Agreement is not corroboration; buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong

Fig 2 :35:

> **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 8 :48: *"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."*

Two teams using the same dataset, the same scale manual and the same review literature are one frame wearing two names, even if they never speak. Agreement among them is the pool talking. This is why a large expert consensus cannot make a contested construct valid, and why a reliability coefficient cannot either. Independence is bought, and its price is set by consequences: a poverty measure that allocates benefits needs more independence than one used for academic description.

Note the distinction in Fig 8 :50: "Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence." Shared content is not the signature of a shared pool; the record of where each came from is. In this simulated run all seats share one evidence pool, so their agreement is one witness, not two (see `11_audit.md`).

**Refuter:** show two frames with fully overlapping evidence producing independent corroboration of a contested construct, or show that consensus alone settles a contested measurement with zero ground contact.

### F6 : The break is at the vocabulary link, not at the instrument

Fig 3 :19:

> **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**

Fig 3 :25-27:

> **"having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."**
> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

Contested constructs are hard because the argument is about link 2 (what the construct affords and what counts as progress), while the measurement community spends its effort on link 1 (instruments, items, sample sizes) and link 4 (planning, modelling). A reading taken below the break is *"a reading of nothing"* (Fig 3 :23). The honest response is to instrument the vocabulary link: specify, in effect terms, the rival construct definitions and what each predicts, before refinement begins. Fig 9 :54-58 requires unbundling the union compound into its separate conditions first.

The "Library of Babel" essay gives the bootstrapping method for a missing vocabulary: "If you don't know the right terms to search for, you will have to use the existing vocabulary you do know that is closest to the words that you wish to describe and grasp the shape of" and "With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."

**Refuter:** show that the validity failure in a contested construct is removed by instrument refinement alone, with no change to what the construct is taken to afford or to what counts as progress.

### F7 : Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134-135:

> **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"**
> **"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."**

Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

After the census, the figures return a shape: an anchor that does not move, an effect description before a frame, bought independence, a validation argument with a stated refuter, and a lineage. They do not return a specific operationalisation of intelligence, health, or poverty. That specific answer is a `gap` in this derivation, reported plainly. It is not a failure of the method and not an invitation to smuggle in a favourite indicator. The canonical figures README states the limit as "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)". Correction carried from the exemplar: that phrase governs a missing primitive. Selecting an indicator already held in the world frame is a binding, not an invented primitive, but it still needs provenance and a shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52). Any condensation of these figures into a formula carries a round-trip gap `R_T` that must be measured, not assumed zero (`11_audit.md` §Round trip).

**Refuter:** derive a unique correct operationalisation of a contested construct from the figure set alone, with no world import and no ground reading. If that is possible, the gap verdict is wrong.

### F8 : Validity is a property of the arrangement, not of the instrument

Fig 11 :39-40:

> **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**
> **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

Adding items, improving formatting, or increasing precision does not change validity if it does not change contact with the ground and with the consequences of use. Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* The world literature converges here from a different direction. Messick's unified validity "integrates considerations of content, criteria, and consequences into a construct framework", and the "Decoding Tacit Knowledge" essay quotes the framework's own "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves" while discussing the same limit. Cronbach and Meehl treat validation as an ongoing network of inferences, not a one-off coefficient; Strauss and Smith 2009 state plainly that "validation is a process not an outcome."

Triangulated: Fig 11 and Fig 6, plus two independent world sources.

**Refuter:** exhibit an instrument improvement that changes the validity of a contested-construct measurement while leaving contact with the ground and habitat unchanged.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A measurement settles a contested construct with no anchor independent of the frames doing the measuring, that is, pure consensus suffices.
2. A declared operational definition grounds itself, so a convention that decides an outcome remains a legitimate convention.
3. Two frames with a fully shared evidence pool supply independent corroboration.
4. An instrument improvement that does not change contact with ground or habitat changes validity.
5. A single coefficient, with no nomological network, no rivals, and no account of consequences, settles construct validity.
6. A unique correct operationalisation of a contested construct is derived from the figure set alone.

If any fires, withdraw the corresponding finding. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with F1 and F2: an anchor that does not move, and a constitutive test for which anchor is legitimate.
2. Treat the question as a union compound (F6) and unbundle it before searching. Instrument link 2, not link 1.
3. Never present agreement or a coefficient as the verdict (F5). Buy independence in proportion to consequences.
4. Describe the construct in effect terms before choosing an indicator set (F4). Record the record of where each binding came from (Fig 8 :50-52).
5. Report the specific operationalisation as `gap` (F7). A shape is the honest stop, not a placeholder for a favourite measure.
6. Offer the two-stage packet in `02`: residual and refuter first, theory only if asked, links last.

### Strains after re-census

- **Constitutive legitimacy versus independence (Fig 2 :36 against Fig 2 :40).** If the question is constitutively about human judgment, that judgment is the anchor (domain 1). But a panel of judges drawn from one shared culture is a shared evidence pool (domain 3). Re-census resolves the strain: the *kind* of anchor is settled by the constitutive rule; the *independence* of the particular judging frames is settled by the evidence-pool rule. Different quantities, no contradiction.
- **Capability (Fig 11 :39) versus reliability.** A scale can be reliable as a channel property (Fig 10 :24) while contributing nothing to validity if contact is unchanged. Both readings are kept; they are not summed.
- **After re-census: two strains, both named, none hidden.**

## 7. Updated verdict

The shape survives. A valid measurement of a contested construct requires (i) an anchor that does not move when frames push on it, (ii) a constitutive determination of what may serve as that anchor, (iii) an effect description before a frame is chosen, (iv) independence bought in proportion to consequences, (v) a validation argument with a pre-registered refuter, and (vi) a lineage that records imported bindings as bindings. The world literature in psychometrics and philosophy of measurement corroborates each requirement from outside the framework, but shares no author with it, so the corroboration is real though partial. What does not survive is any claim that the figures name a correct operationalisation of intelligence, health, or poverty. That remains `gap`. Mode: `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.
