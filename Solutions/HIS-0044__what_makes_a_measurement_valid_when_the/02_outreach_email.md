**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Standing:** the derivation behind this offer is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. Say so in the first paragraph. Do not let a recipient think a measurement was taken.

---

**Subject:** Check one claim about contested measurement before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at. This is a simulated derivation checked against a set of figures, not an empirical result, and I am labelling it that way.
>
> **The question:** what makes a measurement valid when the construct is contested, for example intelligence, health, or poverty?
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question is a bundle of at least two conditions that have to be settled separately. One is ground contact: a reading is an anchor only if it does not change its answer because of what any measuring frame thinks. The other is meaning: the construct definition is a convention until it contacts observations through explicit, public inference, and a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention. From this it follows that reliability, inter-rater agreement, and expert consensus are frame-internal quantities. They cannot by themselves settle validity, because a frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces.
>
> I am **not** claiming that the figures name a correct operationalisation of intelligence, health, or poverty. They return a shape, not a name. The specific indicator set stays open until a ground settles it. I am also **not** claiming that the construct-definition domain is fully mapped; without an anchor it is a provisional domain, and I record that as a gap rather than filling it.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a measurement that settles a contested construct with no anchor independent of the frames doing the measuring, with pure consensus sufficient;
> 2. an operational definition that genuinely grounds itself, with no anchor outside the definition;
> 3. two frames sharing an evidence pool that nevertheless supply independent corroboration;
> 4. an instrument improvement that changes validity while leaving contact with ground and habitat unchanged;
> 5. a single coefficient, with no nomological network, no rivals, and no account of consequences, that settles construct validity.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Canonical figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures README and formula, branch `general-agent`: see `04_links_and_distribution.md`
> - Two outside sources that do not share an author with the framework: Cronbach and Meehl 1955, *Construct validity in psychological tests*; and the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry *Measurement in Science*
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
