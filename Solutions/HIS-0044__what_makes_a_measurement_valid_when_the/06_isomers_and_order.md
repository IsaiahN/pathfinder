# Union, isomers and order in contested measurement

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The measurement-validity question bundles at least two conditions, ground contact and construct meaning. Two supporting questions are owed: (1) is this a partition or a union, and what follows from the answer; (2) can two contested constructs share an indicator set without being the same construct, and what does that imply for a measure that records only the indicator list?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 : Partition or union?

- Fig 9 :54: **"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
- Fig 9 :55-56: "a bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."
- Fig 9 :57: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**
- R has at least two overlapping conditions: (a) does the reading track an anchor, and (b) is the construct definition the right one. Reliability, agreement and sample size act on (a) partly and on (b) not at all. They clear the region where only (a) holds and leave the compound standing.
- ⇒ The conditions must be looped **separately**, with separate grounds. Domain 1 (reading) has the ground of outcomes and counters. Domain 2 (meaning) becomes groundable only via constitutive legitimacy (Fig 2 :36) or a nomological net contacting observations (Cronbach and Meehl 1955). Domain 3 (verification) has the ground, never agreement.
- Real-world check, not a figure line: Sen's two-step poverty measurement, "determining who is poor (identification) and building an index to reflect the extent of poverty (aggregation)", both of which "have been sources of debate over time among academics and practitioners" (UN DESA 2015). That is a union compound in an actual contested construct.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if one lever is shown to settle both ground contact and construct meaning in a contested case, the union reading fails and the problem is a single hard question after all.

### Q2 : Are two constructs with the same indicators isomers?

- Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**
- Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Two contested constructs can be built from the same indicator set, for example reaction time, memory span and self-report wellbeing, arranged under different theories. As *sets of indicators* they are indistinguishable, which is exactly the flat-formula collapse Fig 12 names. Under a different bond, that is a different arrangement and a different substance.
- ⇒ A measure that reports only its item list and a single score has recorded the ingredients, not the construct. It cannot tell an isomer of a rival construct from the construct itself. The arrangement, the nomological net and the consequences of use carry the information the formula dropped.
- **Stronger form worth testing:** if both constructs are written over a full shared indicator set and differ only in which indicators mediate which criterion relations, they are genuine isomers. If they share only one or two indicators, the weaker reading is that they belong to the same **functional group** (Fig 12 :124): same kind of measurement arrangement, different members.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two contested constructs can be fully specified by their indicator sets alone, with no theory of arrangement and no consequence profile, the isomer reading fails and the flat form is sufficient. Conversely, if a single score settles which of two rival constructs is present, the functional-group reading also fails.

### Q3 : What order do the loops run in?

Dependency order from Fig 3, not importance order:

1. **Perception.** Can a reading be obtained at all? This link usually works and has obvious tests.
2. **Vocabulary.** Are there terms for what the construct affords, and what would count as progress? Fig 3 :25-26: "having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments." Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**
3. **Objective.** State what would count as a valid result, in effect terms, before choosing an indicator.
4. **Planning.** Choose the anchor, the independence budget and the refuter.
5. **Learn and carry.** Validate, record the lineage, and carry the method forward, not the recording.

Supporting rule: Fig 3 :19 **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** Most validity effort lands on link 1 because that is where the measurements already exist (Fig 3 :29-30). For contested constructs the break is at link 2.
- **Falsifier for the order:** if a validity failure is removed by instrument refinement alone, with no change to what the construct affords or what counts as progress, the dependency order claimed here is wrong.

### Q4 : What is the round trip cost of compressing this derivation?

- Fig 4 :30: **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."**
- Fig 4 :31-32: "The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold, and it is the only honest report of how much was given up."
- Sending up: Fig 2 anchor rule, Fig 2 constitutive rule, Fig 9 union rule, Fig 12 bond rule. Coming back down: a concrete verdict about a specific test, index or poverty line. The gap `R_T` is the set of constraints the shorthand drops: which anchor is legitimate for this construct, how independence was bought, what the rival constructs predict, and what would refute the choice. `11_audit.md` enumerates it qualitatively; it is not measured numerically because no ground reading was taken.

## CONCLUSION

The residual is a **union compound** and must be unbundled before it is searched. The two conditions have separate loops and separate grounds. Two constructs can share an indicator set and still be different substances, because the bond is the half the score did not record; the isomer reading is real but has a stated falsifier. The loops must run in dependency order with the vocabulary link instrumented first, because optimising a working link cannot rescue a broken one. The honest stop is a shape with a recorded round-trip gap, not a named measure.
