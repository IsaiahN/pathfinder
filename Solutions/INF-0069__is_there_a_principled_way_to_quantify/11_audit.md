## 4. Round trip gap `R_T`

The Symbols table defines `R_T` as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, the difference between what was sent up
and what came back down. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round
trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :19/:22 (per-slot residual), Fig 5 :18/:21/:43-44 (target and bargain), Fig 6 :27 and Fig 8 :40-43 (two kinds of missingness), Fig 9 :60 (filter versus verdict), Fig 12 :125-130 (two currencies), Fig 13 :134-135 (shape not a name).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand used in `07` and `09`: "there is a principled ex-ante rule; it is a shape and a budget filter; two currencies; do not sum; the number is a gap".
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a field phrasing such as "EVPI is the principled ex-ante value of information".

**`R_T` is nonzero.** Five things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- **per-slot indexing**, lost when a single population or organizational value is reported;
- **the two-currency separation**, lost when bits and expected utility are summed into one number;
- **SEARCHED-first ordering**, lost when an import is proposed before the in-closure search is run;
- **provenance and shadow test**, lost when an external valuation is adopted without a source gate;
- **ground settlement**, lost when the ex-ante number is used as a verdict before anything pays.

Conversely, two things survive cleanly: a slot with live residual mass and a compressible gap can be
ranked before content, and bits and actions are different units. That is the residue worth sending.
`R_T` was not measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as
the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the
figures" as identity would set `R_T` to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
