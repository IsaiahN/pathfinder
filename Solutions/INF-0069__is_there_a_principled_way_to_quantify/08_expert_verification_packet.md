# Expert verification packet - the Tether on the value of information before content

**What this is:** a claim in a form a decision theorist, statistician, information theorist or health
economist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader
knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the two checkable sub-claims, `general` on the structural separation, `gap` on
the concrete number. Not `grounded`.** This packet is a simulated derivation checked for internal
consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Before a message arrives, there is a principled ex-ante rule, but its output is a shape and a filter
for the budget, not a settled scalar. The rule ranks object slots by positive residual mass and by
whether the gap is large and compressible, and it applies a minimum-description-length bargain.
The quantity is priced in two currencies that do not add: bits price the description and actions
price finding out whether it holds. Because the content has not arrived, depth (in the closure,
unfound) and genuine absence (needs an import) are indiscernible, so SEARCHED is run first and the
missing thing is described in effect terms rather than named. The concrete value stays open until a
ground that does not update on the frame's opinion pays for it. Six refuters are written in advance.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 1, 5 and the formula

- **Statement:** an ex-ante rule exists and is per slot: support, large-and-compressible gap, MDL
  bargain. It is not a settled value.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 5
  :18 - *"seeks the gap that is large and compressible"*. Fig 5 :21 - *"the gap has real mass
  somewhere"*. Fig 5 :42 - *"The acceptance test is a bargain, not a threshold."* Fig 5 :43-44 -
  *"A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less
  than what was unexplained before."* Formula step 3 gate: `SUPPORT |R⁺_s| > 0 for some s` before the
  search.
- **What to check:** is `R` really per slot in the source text, and is the bargain a comparison of
  costs rather than a threshold? Does the formula really gate minting on support before search?
- **Refuter:** an ex-ante ranking independent of the per-slot residual distribution; or a slot with
  zero live mass selected that the ground still pays for; or a scalar that sums bits and actions and
  is invariant under re-description (this last also fires C2).

### C2 - the decisive cross-field claim, checkable against Shannon 1948 and Howard 1966

- **Statement:** bits and actions are two currencies and must not be summed. The field's two founding
  measures price different things, so a single "value of information" number is a type error unless
  the units are declared.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :125 - *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*
  Fig 12 :126 - *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."* Fig 12
  :127 - *"They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them and
  will not notice the shortfall."* Fig 12 :130 - *"Summing them into one figure is the error this
  names, not the fix for it. A price in bits and a price in actions are two readings, held apart."*
- **What to check:** Shannon 1948 (full text) says *"These semantic aspects of communication are
  irrelevant to the engineering problem"* and measures information logarithmically over a set of
  possible messages. Howard 1966 says *"no theory that involves just the probabilities of outcomes
  without considering their consequences could possibly be adequate in describing the importance of
  uncertainty to a decision maker."* Are these two prices, not two estimates of one price? Note the
  distinct second reason for non-additivity: Howard and the Wikipedia characteristics list report
  submodularity within one currency, which is a different claim from incommensurable units. Both are
  kept; neither is used to flip the other.
- **Refuter:** produce a single scalar that sums bits and actions and show it is invariant under
  re-description of the coding and of the experiment. Either the units are not incommensurable, in
  which case Fig 12 :127 is wrong, or the invariance fails.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation, checkable against Figures 6, 8, 9 and 13

- **Statement:** before content, the missing quantity cannot be classified as in-closure depth or
  genuine absence; SEARCHED is first; the deliverable is a kind of thing and the concrete number is a
  `gap`.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :27 - *"Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine
  absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."* Fig 8 :40-43 - *"Underivable-in-principle
  and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions."* Fig 9
  :32 - *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 13 :134 - *"Unreachable is a verdict
  here, not a silence"*. Formula step 7 asks SEARCHED first because it is cheapest and the other two
  assume its answer.
- **What to check:** does the field's expected information gain (Lindley 1956; Ivanova 2021) actually
  require averaging over outcomes that have not occurred? Ivanova 2021 says *"we can't use IG to find
  the optimal design as it depends on the outcome y and hence can't calculate it until after the
  experiment has been performed."* If that is right, the ex-ante object is an expectation (a shape),
  not the realized value.
- **Refuter:** demonstrate a classification of the missing quantity as in-closure versus needs-import
  before any content arrives, with no instrument and no ground; or a demonstration that the ex-ante
  number settles a decision without a ground reading (Formula step 5: *"A gate is not the ground"*,
  and Fig 1 :23: *"The ground enters as the only metric."*).

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that Shannon entropy is the value. It is one currency.
- Not that EVPI, EVSI or ENBS is a settled verdict. They are expectations and net-benefit filters for
  choosing a study (Fig 9 :60 - *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict."*).
- Not that the content of the message, or which message will arrive, is known. Fig 13 :135 forbids
  naming the missing primitive.
- Not that any source has a standing high value. Fig 11 :51 - *"No standing list of good actors
  survives a change of residual."*
- Not that expert agreement settles the number. Fig 2 :40 - *"agreement about the world is worthless
  when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; Fig 7 :35 - *"A room's agreement is variation,
  not a verdict."*
- Not that the field is wrong. The claim is about which object the question asks for and which
  currency it is stated in, not about the correctness of expected utility theory.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken. The whole packet is a simulated derivation.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an
instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; *"the spectrometer, not an argument"*,
resolved it. Here:

1. **Currency test:** measure description cost and action cost separately on one slot and show
   whether or not they add. A principled rule for combining them, if one exists, is the settlement.
2. **Discriminator:** an instrument that tells in-closure depth from genuine absence before the
   message arrives. This would dissolve the Fig 6 :27 indiscernibility and upgrade the `gap`.
3. **Anchor test:** a ground reading showing that the slot ranked highest by the ex-ante rule is the
   slot the ground actually pays for, and that the ranking fails where it does not.
4. **The six refuters** in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §5. Any one firing withdraws the shape claim.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19/:22, Fig 5 :18/:21/:42-44, Fig 12 :125-130, Fig 6 :27, Fig 9 :32/:60 and Fig 13
   :134-135. Nine short passages, all in the local figure extract.
2. Check C2 against Shannon 1948 and Howard 1966: are bits and expected utility two prices? This is
   the substantive five-minute check.
3. Check C3 against Lindley 1956 and Ivanova 2021: is the ex-ante quantity an expectation over
   unobserved outcomes rather than the realized value?
4. Name one instrument that would discriminate in-closure depth from genuine absence, or say why none
   is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
