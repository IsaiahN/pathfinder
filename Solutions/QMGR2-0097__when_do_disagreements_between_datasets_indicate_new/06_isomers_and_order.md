### Q1 - Does the order of the sort rule matter?

- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces."**
- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**

The order is not stylistic. A significance cut applied before the independence check decides on the wrong quantity, because a p-value computed on one evidence pool twice has no ground meaning regardless of its size. A new-physics mint attempted before the split check mints a term for a bundle that should have been separated. ### Q2 - Are the five bins exhaustive?

- Fig 5 :11-14: *"held"*, *"new"*, *"mis-attached"*, *"mechanism broken"*, and :53 *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"*.
- Fig 5 :33: **"The three guards are a product, not a checklist."** Fig 5 :41: **"Any one of them at zero stops the mint entirely."**
- Fig 9 :5: **"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."**

For a dataset disagreement, each bin has a concrete reading:

| bin | dataset-disagreement reading | kind |
|---|---|---|
| held | the quoted uncertainties were right and the gap is a fluctuation | nothing owed |
| new | the gap is real but unlocalized; aim a probe | perception to extend, no new atom |
| mis-attached | a foreground, calibration or subset model is attached to the wrong term | refit, do not invent |
| mechanism broken | no held composition predicts the shape | a term is owed |
| refuted | a held claim (an uncertainty model, a "same quantity" assumption) is false | a refusal is owed |

The split rule is not a sixth bin. It operates *before* bin assignment and changes what the slots are (Fig 9 :54-58: unbundle before you search again). So the bins are exhaustive over a *single well-formed residual*, which is the domain they were derived for.

- **Falsifier, written before the run:** exhibit a disagreement outcome that is neither a fluctuation, an unlocalized real gap, a mis-attached term, a broken mechanism, a false held term, nor a bundle that should be split. If one exists, the sort rule is incomplete and the completeness claim is retracted.

### Q3 - Same reading, different source; same source, different reading

Fig 8 :50: **"Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."** Fig 13 :121: **"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."**

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Two datasets can agree in value while differing in evidence source (muonic versus electronic hydrogen, different theory chains). Under Fig 13 :121 that is convergence at maximum independence if the routes genuinely differ, and it is the strongest corroboration the figures allow. Under Fig 12 :117 it is **not** an isomer, because the atoms and the arrangement agree; the difference is in the lineage, which is provenance rather than structure.

Two datasets can disagree in value while sharing a source (two analyses of the same beam, one clock reconstruction). That is Fig 2 :18-21's common cause: *"so the agreement is the pool talking,"* *"not two frames confirming each other"*. The difference is not a new substance; it is one frame wearing two names.

The genuinely structural case, the one that would be an isomer in Fig 12's sense, is two analyses over the same atom set where the bond differs (a joint fit versus a split-by-subset fit). Same ingredients, different arrangement, different substance. That is checkable in the record: the bond is stated, and the two analyses are not interchangeable.

- The essay *The Hard Problem is About Source*, fetched 2026-11-23, gives the discriminator: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* A reading never determines its source. Convergence is established by the lineage, not by the number.

### Q4 - SEARCHED, INWARD, OUTWARD as the error/new-physics order

Formula step 7 names three questions and says SEARCHED is asked first because it is cheapest, and that INWARD and OUTWARD are opposed and never read against each other. Fig 6 :49-50: **"more search at the same reach: is it in here and unfound? The closure does not change."** *"Distribute the search, and keep the searchers independent."*

Mapped to this question:

- **SEARCHED:** is the explanation already in the closure and merely unfound? More data, more bins, more independent searchers on the same reach. This is the first and cheapest reading of an "underestimated errors" claim.
- **INWARD:** extend the instrument from one already returning something (Fig 6 :32). A better clock, a dust map, a source-discriminating spectrometer.
- **OUTWARD:** import a frame whose closure predicts the shape (Fig 6 :23). This is the new-physics branch, gated by provenance and a shadow test.

Fig 8 :40-42 is the price rule: an import against a budget limit adds an atom that was not needed, and Fig 6 :24 says **"A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."** Hence the order is a cost order, not a preference for the error hypothesis.

- **Falsifier:** exhibit a real disagreement where OUTWARD succeeded while SEARCHED and INWARD were both still untried and cheap, and show that the figures' cost rule does not apply. Then the order claim is wrong.

## CONCLUSION

The order is a dependency chain anchored at the independence check; the five bins are exhaustive over a single well-formed residual, with the split rule operating before them; and the structural analogue separates convergence (same value, different source, maximum independence) from a common-cause duplicate (different value, shared source) from a true isomer (same atoms, different bond, different substance). All three claims have written falsifiers. None is grounded.
