# The Tether's answer on dataset disagreement

**Mode: `general` on the decision-procedure shape, `specified` on the mapping to physics-statistics practice. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Two datasets disagree by more than their quoted uncertainties. The question asks which outcome is owed: a new term, or a correction to what is held. Fig 9 :4-5 names the first move: *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*. The either/or is a bundle. It must be separated before it is searched.

## 2. The shape

1. **Anchor first.** There must be a quantity that settles the question and does not move when either dataset is pushed. Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."** Without one, there is no domain yet.
2. **Independence before significance.** Fig 2 :31: **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."** Two analyses sharing calibration, theory inputs, code or a parent sample are one frame reporting twice. Fig 2 :40: **"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**. The same rule governs disagreement.
3. **Is there a fact here?** Fig 9 :5: **"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."** If the gap will not shrink and each analysis works on a different subset, the discrepancy is a bundle, and the owed correction is a re-description of the quantity, not a new particle.
4. **Keep the residual per slot.** Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** A pooled mean can hide a live discrepancy or manufacture one.
5. **Sort into five bins, not two.** Fig 5 :11-14 gives held, new, mis-attached and mechanism broken; Fig 5 :53 adds **"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"**. Underestimated errors live in mis-attached and refuted. New physics lives in mechanism broken. "New" only extends perception and adds no atom (Fig 6 :22: **"NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world."**).
6. **Separate underivable from unreachable-at-budget.** Fig 8 :40-42: **"Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions. Underivable: go outside. Unreachable-at-budget: the term is already in the closure and was not found, so the remedy is more search, not another atom."** This is the precise figure statement of the question. Errors are the second case; new physics is the first.
7. **Make the new term pay.** Fig 5 :42: **"The acceptance test is a bargain, not a threshold."** Fig 5 :43: **"A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before."** The error hypothesis is the competitor. A wrong import costs more than more search (Fig 6 :24: **"A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."**).
8. **Settle with the ground, through an independent frame.** Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**. Fig 10 :28: **"you have lost touch"**. A drifted dataset is a lost channel, not changed physics. Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**
9. **A significance value is a filter, never a witness.** Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."** The 5σ convention decides which claims get a reanalysis; it does not decide what they mean. A convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one (Fig 10 :47).

Under this rule, the two answers are not opposite ends of a significance scale. "New physics" means a term is owed from a frame whose closure differs. "Underestimated errors" means the fix is already in the closure and needs search, refit, a split, a refusal, or a better instrument, but no new atom. Both verdicts stay separate per domain and are never averaged.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not which real anomaly is new physics. That needs a ground reading, and this derivation takes none.
- Not a replacement threshold. No number from the figures settles a verdict.
- Not that agreement between two groups is corroboration when they share an evidence pool.
- Not that the metric tensor is a structure, or that any specific boson, foreground or cable is the cause. Those are names; the framework returns a shape.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by measurement.
- Not that a high-significance claim from a prestigious collaboration outranks a low-significance claim from an independent instrument. Prestige buys a claim a budget to be tried; it cannot settle it.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A discriminator, not a further argument. The essay *The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the form: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*, and it was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved the cases. Here:

- **Independence test:** enumerate the evidence shared by the two datasets. If the overlap is large, their disagreement is the pool talking, regardless of σ.
- **Split test:** rerun each analysis on the subsets where it works. If each works on a different subset, split the question and stop treating it as one measurement.
- **Instrument test:** find an instrument already returning something that fails to resolve the quantity (Fig 6 :34: **"It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve."**). A new independent frame or a sharper instrument changes contact.
- **Bargain test:** state the new-physics term and the error hypothesis, and compare cost plus leftover residual. Refuse the new term if it does not pay.
- **Standing refuters from `01`:** a sixth kind of gap; independence proven irrelevant; a figure-derived numeric cut; a moved ground with an intact channel; a new-physics term paying the bargain without a differing closure.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 8 :39: **"a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"** Fig 9 :42: **"narrowing gets you close, the ground decides"**. The figures can say what a decision procedure must look like and what would reverse its assignments. They cannot say which cause produced a particular disagreement. That stop is recorded, with lineage, so a reader can locate the error rather than only feel it.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a new significance threshold for deciding whether a dataset disagreement is new physics or an error. It gives a sort rule. First name an anchor that neither dataset can move. Then check whether the two datasets are independent frames or one evidence pool sharing calibration, theory and code; a p-value cannot fix a shared pool. Then ask whether there is a fact here: if the gap will not shrink and each analysis works on a different subset, split the question instead of searching it. Keep the residual per channel, never in a pooled mean. Sort the gap into five bins: nothing owed, a probe to aim, a mis-attached term to refit, a broken mechanism that owes a new term, or a held term that is false and owes a refusal. Separate the underivable case, which needs an import from a different frame, from the unreachable-at-budget case, which needs more search or a better instrument and no new atom. Make any new-physics term pay the bargain against the error hypothesis. Then let an independent frame or a sharper instrument settle it, because the ground does not decay, the channel does. What the rule returns is a shape and a refuter; it does not name the cause of any particular anomaly.
