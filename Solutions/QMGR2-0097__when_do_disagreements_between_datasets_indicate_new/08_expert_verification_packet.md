# Expert verification packet - the Tether on dataset disagreement

**What this is:** a claim in a form a statistician, a metrologist or an experimental physicist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` shape, `specified` mapping, not `grounded`.** No measurement at any real anomaly is claimed. This is a decision procedure and a route, not a result.

**Status of this packet:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A disagreement between two datasets is not one number with two opposite meanings. It is a sort. First name an anchor neither dataset can move, then check whether the two datasets are independent frames or one evidence pool; if they share calibration, theory inputs, code or a parent sample, their disagreement is the pool talking. Then ask whether there is a fact here: a gap that will not shrink and works only on different subsets is a bundle, and the remedy is to split it. Keep the residual per slot. Sort it into five bins, distinguish the underivable case (a term from a differing closure is owed) from the unreachable-at-budget case (more search or a better instrument, no new atom), and make any new-physics term pay the description-length bargain against the error hypothesis. Settlement requires an independent frame or a better instrument, because the ground does not decay, the channel does. A significance value allocates the budget; it never delivers the verdict.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - independence decides whether a disagreement bears on the ground; σ does not

- **Statement:** two datasets that share an evidence pool are one frame wearing two names. Their agreement or disagreement is not a witness about the ground, whatever the p-value. Two instruments pointed at the same thing are two frames.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :31 *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."* Also Fig 2 :40 *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* and Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
- **What to check:** take a real disagreement (the proton radius puzzle, BICEP2, OPERA). List the evidence shared by the two reports. Did the resolution come from an independent frame, or from more of the same analysis?
- **Refuter:** exhibit two frames with genuinely disjoint evidence and instrumentation whose disagreement carries zero ground information, and show that the figures give no way to distinguish that from a real disagreement. Then C1 is false.

### C2 - the either/or is a bundle; it must be split and then sorted into five bins

- **Statement:** "new physics or underestimated errors" is not a partition. Fig 9 :4-5 says a stubborn gap that works on different subsets is several well-formed questions. Once split, the residual sorts into five bins: held, new, mis-attached, mechanism broken, and refuted. Underestimated errors live in mis-attached and refuted; new physics lives in mechanism broken; "new" only extends perception and adds no atom.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :5 *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* Fig 5 :13 *"mis-attached: refit what you have, do not invent"*; Fig 5 :14 *"mechanism broken: a new term is owed"*; Fig 5 :53 *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"*; Fig 6 :22 *"NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world."*
- **What to check:** is the five-bin list exhaustive for a single well-formed residual? Does the split rule genuinely operate before bin assignment rather than being a sixth bin?
- **Refuter:** exhibit a disagreement outcome that is none of the five and not a bundle. Or show that the split rule and the bins overlap in a way that makes the assignment ambiguous with no figure resource to resolve it. Then C2 is false.

### C3 - underivable versus unreachable-at-budget is the precise form of "new physics versus error"

- **Statement:** if the explanation is already in the closure and merely unfound, the remedy is more search or a better instrument and no new atom. If no arrangement of the held atoms predicts the observed shape, a term from a differing closure is owed. The two look the same from inside the frame, which is why a p-value cannot tell them apart.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :40-42 *"Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions. Underivable: go outside. Unreachable-at-budget: the term is already in the closure and was not found, so the remedy is more search, not another atom."* Also Fig 6 :24 *"A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."*
- **What to check:** does this map cleanly onto the practice of reanalysis versus new-physics claims? Lyons 2013 reaches the same operational conclusion in domain language: *"a 5σ claim should be reduced to merely 2.5σ if the systematic uncertainties had been underestimated by a factor of 2"*.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a case where importing a new atom paid the Fig 5 :43 bargain while the explanation was already in the closure and search was cheap. Or show a figure-derived numeric cut that settles the distinction without a ground reading. Then C3 is false.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a new threshold. No number from the figures decides a verdict.
- Not that any real anomaly has been assigned to a bin. No ground reading was taken.
- Not that agreement between two groups is corroboration when they share an evidence pool.
- Not that a high-significance, prestigious result outranks an independent one. Prestige can fund a reanalysis; it cannot settle it.
- Not that the framework names a specific physical cause. It returns a shape.
- Not `grounded`. The mode is `general` on the shape and `specified` on the mapping.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. Concretely: an evidence-overlap audit of the two datasets; a subset split with each analysis rerun; an independent instrument already returning something that fails to resolve the quantity; a comparison of cost plus leftover residual between a new-physics term and the error hypothesis. Without one of these, the sort rule stays `candidate`.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :31, :40 and Fig 9 :60. Decide whether evidence independence, not σ, is the right gate for a two-dataset disagreement.
2. Read Fig 9 :4-5 and Fig 5 :11-14, :53. Decide whether the bundle-and-five-bins reading is exhaustive for a single well-formed residual.
3. Read Fig 8 :40-42 and Fig 6 :24. Decide whether the underivable/unreachable distinction is the right formal counterpart of new physics versus error, and name one instrument that would operate it.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
