## Two things in the literature that bear directly on the question

1. **Correlation does not decide the source.** The g factor article reports that factor analysis can produce "an infinite number of different factor solutions that are mathematically equivalent" and that solutions exist which do not contain g, so "factor analysis alone cannot establish what the underlying structure of intelligence is." This is the world side of Fig 2 :40: agreement within a shared evidence pool is not two witnesses. Cite it when explaining why a single scale from correlated tests is not automatically a verdict.
2. **Ordinal is not interval.** The Level of measurement article says IQ scores are ordinal and that a ten point difference may carry different meanings at different points of the scale. This is the world side of F2 and F8: the arithmetic a single scale invites may not be licensed by the measurement. Cite it when explaining why R_T must be reported.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual, the shape claim, and the refuter. Ask what they would count as a failure. Record their verdict before showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt in only.** If they are curious, offer the figures and formula. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse. Each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode. The Medium article's own warning applies: outputs "could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula."

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. The stage-1 output is the verdict split, not a claim that a scale is impossible. State that split on every line.
