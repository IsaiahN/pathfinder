## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical README states the limit: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 says the system "cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

Applied here: the shape of an answer to "can intelligence be ranked on a single scale" is not a number and not a named factor. It is a constrained form:

- an ordering is licensed (Fig 13 :20, "ordering, so more and less can be said");
- the per slot residuals must remain visible, because aggregating them throws away the only signal that says where to look (Fig 1 :22);
- the ranked object is agent-and-habitat, not the agent alone (Fig 11 :39);
- the scale's verdict status must be labelled: convention or ground verdict, never silently one for the other (Fig 2 :41).

A shape constrains. It does not fill the slot with g, with a particular test battery, or with a number.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- Describe the residual in effect terms before choosing a frame. Fig 9 :27 to :28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Fig 9 :30 to :31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."
- The sought for shape is made an ordinary term by writing down what an answer would look like and letting it accrue a residual (`THE_FORMULA.md`, "Direction"). This folder does that in `01` section 0 by binding the candidate function f.
- From *How Intelligence Actually Works*: "The definition of what we call General Intelligence is that a system can answer questions to problems it's never seen before and in environments it has no training data for." This is the effect to describe, not the name to fill. Note what it implies: the property is stated relative to unseen problems and untrained environments, which are habitat conditions. That is Fig 11 :39 in a different vocabulary.
- Stop at the limit of the instruments and record the lineage. The canonical README says the framework stops where instruments can no longer distinguish and "Indiscernibility is not identity."

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." For intelligence ranking, the ground is a held out task, a predictive criterion, an instrument. The g factor article records exactly this kind of ground work and its limits: "the measured value of this construct depends on the cognitive tasks that are used."
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs**, with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50 to :52). Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents; only the record separates them.

So a particular factor may be named only by the ground or by a recorded import, never by the shape alone.

## 4. Why "one scale" is the metamerism error

*How Intelligence Actually Works* gives the mechanism with the green light example: a projection reads green and cannot say whether it was blue plus yellow, RGB, CMYK, or "even more arrangements that we are unaware of." The reading does not determine the source. In that essay's terms, "indiscernibility is not identity" and the lineage must be kept.

Applied here: a correlation matrix is a projection. It reads as one common factor and cannot comment on the source. Spearman's g and Thomson's sampling model are compiled readings of the same projection, and the g factor article states that it is "not possible to distinguish statistically between Spearman's model of g and the sampling model; both are equally able to account for inter correlations among tests." Naming the source from the reading is the error the essay forbids. What is owed is a discriminating instrument, plus the recorded lineage of how the score was built.

Two world sources sharpen this. The Level of measurement article says IQ scores are ordinal and that "a 10-point difference may carry different meanings at different points of the scale." The g factor article records "factor indeterminacy": different sets of factor scores reproduce the same correlation matrix. A single scalar over such a reading carries information loss that must be reported as R_T, not assumed away.

## 5. Correction to my own section 3 and 4

I initially overstated "shape, not a name." The canonical phrase is "what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." It governs a primitive not held in the library. Ordering (spectrum) is already one of the six structures; comparison is an operator already defined. So using the word "rank" or "ordering" is not the forbidden act. What is forbidden is naming an unheld latent quantity as the cause of the ordering and treating the name as a ground verdict.

My section 4 metamerism point is not retracted. The reason is still a missing discriminator, not a ban on naming held atoms. Both forms are recorded here so neither is silently lost.

## 6. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** *Arrival of the Fittest* carries the proxy failure sources (Goodhart, Campbell, Strathern) under "proxies, and what happens when a measure becomes a target." A measure that becomes a target stops being a measure of the thing (Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"). So a campaign that gathers prestigious names to endorse a ranking is the named collapse, not corroboration.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** *Arrival of the Fittest*: "Because it is a metatheory, it cannot be validated, nor can it validate itself"; an LLM given the figures produces "simulacra" and "the outputs generated must be seen as simulacra."
