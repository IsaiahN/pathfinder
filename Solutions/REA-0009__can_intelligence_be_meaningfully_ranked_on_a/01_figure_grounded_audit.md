## 0. The residual, in effect terms

**Question as effect.** We are asked whether a single number can *meaningly* order systems by intelligence across tasks and contexts. Three properties are wanted of that number: (a) it orders systems, (b) it transfers to a task or context not used to build it, and (c) it tracks something the ground recognises rather than only coordinating a shared meaning. What each option leads to:

- **Scalar as verdict.** Performance is read on N task slots and compressed to one number, which is then used to settle claims about capability in unseen contexts. This is the option the question most naturally invites.
- **Scalar as convention.** The number coordinates expectations, hiring, admissions, benchmarking. Ordering is real as a shared meaning, but it settles nothing about the world on its own.
- **No scalar, capability reframed.** The system is characterised by its contact with a habitat, and the ranked object is agent-and-habitat, not agent alone.

**Binding the sought shape as an ordinary term (formula step "Direction").** A frame cannot check the direction of its own question, so write down what an answer would look like and let it accrue a residual. Define the candidate term f: a function from a vector of per slot observations `(o'_s1 ... o'_sN)` to one real number r, with the promise that (i) the ordering it induces is invariant to which slots are sampled and (ii) r correlates with a ground measure of capability on a held out task. That is the ordinary term under audit. It is refusable: if the slot sampling changes the ordering, (i) fails; if r does not predict held out performance, (ii) fails. Without this binding the word "meaningfully" would be a preference invisible to every check below.

**R by arity, symmetry, and scale.**

- **Arity:** N slots, one per task or context. The residual is indexed per slot, exactly as Fig 1 :11 and :19 index R by object slot.
- **Symmetry:** a single scale imposes a total order, the comparison relation, and it wants that order invariant to permutation of slots. Observed performance is not invariant to slot permutation. There is a strain between the symmetry the ranking wants and the asymmetry the data has. The strain is named, not smoothed.
- **Scale:** first order is performance inside one task; second order is the aggregate across tasks. Fig 4 :27 names the lossy transform between scales.

D2 and D3 in particular must not be merged, because the same scalar can be useful as a convention while being silent as a verdict about the world.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - Aggregating across tasks discards the signal that says where to look (decisive against scalar as verdict)

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
Fig 1 :28: "The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."

A task or context is a slot in the sense the figure uses. A single scalar across tasks is exactly the aggregation the figure forbids as a measure: if one slot carries live residual, a global near zero average is a legal state but hides where the failure is (Fig 1 :13). This does not forbid computing an average for a purpose. It forbids treating the average as a ground verdict about capability, because the average has thrown away the per slot information that would let the ground settle anything.

**Refuter:** show a scalar that preserves all per slot residuals without loss (a sufficient statistic for the full vector under every ground question asked of it), or show that tasks are not slots in the figure's sense. Either fires F1.

**Status: triangulated.** Fig 1 and Fig 12 (F2) are independent routes with low overlap: one is about where the residual lives, the other about units.

### F2 - A single figure cannot add incommensurable currencies

Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."
Fig 12 :130: "Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."

A ranking of intelligence across contexts typically mixes the cost of describing a behaviour (bits: how much structure must be stated to predict it) with the cost of finding out whether it holds (actions: experiments, samples, exposures). The figure says these are different units and that summing them is the named error. A single scale is not automatically this error, but the burden is on the scale to say which currency it prices and to keep the other one visible.

**Refuter:** exhibit a single unit in which description cost and test cost are commensurable, with the conversion derived rather than stipulated. Fires F2.

**Status: triangulated with F1** (Fig 12 route independent of Fig 1 route).

### F3 - Ordering is legitimate; it is a structure, not a verdict

Fig 13 :20: "ordering, so more and less can be said." (spectrum, a structure)
Operators table: comparison is "a threshold relation between two quantities."
Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."

The figures do not forbid ordering. A spectrum is one of the six structures and is precisely what lets more and less be said. So a rank is a lawful structure. What is forbidden is promoting the ordered kind into a verdict about the world. The framework returns the shape of a thing: here, an ordering over a narrowed habitat, with the slot left described in effect terms. It does not return a name for the latent quantity that causes the ordering.

**Refuter:** show the figures forbid all ordering, or show a figure line that licenses a scalar as a ground verdict. Neither was found after a genuine census. If found, F3 falls.

**Status: single-source for the structure definition (Fig 13 / Operators table), triangulated with Fig 9 for the shape limit.**

### F4 - Verdict and convention are different products

Fig 2 :39: "Correlation has valence."
Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"
Fig 2 :41: "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"
Seat Map :163: "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."

This split is the sharpest thing the figures say about ranking. A single scale can be meaningful in the second sense: people coordinate on a shared meaning, and producing that meaning is the product. It cannot be meaningful in the first sense merely because many tests correlate, since correlation from a shared evidence pool is exactly what disqualifies a verdict. Psychometrics itself supplies the corroboration: the g factor Wikipedia article states that "the measured value of this construct depends on the cognitive tasks that are used" and that "the correlations do not provide any more or less support for the existence of g than for the existence of multiple factors of intelligence."

**Refuter:** show that the scale's correlation is convergent derivation from low overlap frames rather than a shared pool, with the record that separates the two (Fig 8). If that record exists, the convention reading is too narrow and the scale may carry verdict weight.

**Status: triangulated** (Fig 2 and Seat Map are the same figure family but distinct statements; the psychometric source is world corroboration, not a figure).

### F5 - The framework refuses the question "what is intelligence" and replaces it

Fig 11 :35: "The wrong question, and this figure is the replacement for it."
Fig 11 :36: "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."
Fig 11 :39: "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."
Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

If the question is read literally as a request to rank intelligence, the figures say the object of the ranking is misdescribed. What can be ranked, in principle, is capability in a specified habitat: a relation between an agent and the conditions, actors, and relations in contact with its residual. A scale that claims to rank the agent alone across all habitats is ranking something the figures say does not exist as a separable quantity.

**Refuter:** the figure itself names the one that would fire this: "an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim" (Fig 11 reading note). A controlled intervention that changes only the agent and measurably moves the ground across habitats fires F5.

**Status: single-source (Fig 11) for the refusal, triangulated with Fig 1 (per slot residual) at the level of "the score is not the system."**

### F6 - Different rules on different subsets means split, not aggregate

Fig 9 :4 to :5: "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."
Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."

The demand for one scale across tasks and contexts is a demand that one rule work everywhere. Where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, Fig 9 says that is not one hard question but several. The multiple intelligences literature is world corroboration, with the caveat that it is contested: Wikipedia states that "human intelligence is not a single general ability but comprises various distinct modalities," and also that "empirical research often supports a general intelligence factor." Both can be true if the domains are a union of overlapping conditions rather than a single scale, which is exactly why the split must be made before ranking.

**Refuter:** demonstrate one rule that works across all sampled contexts with no subset on which a different rule wins, and predict a held out subset correctly. Fires F6.

**Status: triangulated** (Fig 9 split rule plus the union reading of the census).

### F7 - Ranking is a filter for the budget, not a witness for the verdict

Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."
Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."
Fig 7 :13: "agreement compounds, evidence does not."

A ranking answers "which gets tried first." It does not answer "which is true." Tier scores in this project are explicitly filters. The same discipline applies here: a rank orders attention, and the ground settles capability. If a committee ranks candidates and then treats the rank as the measurement, Fig 10 :47 applies: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."

**Refuter:** produce a case where the rank itself is the ground reading (an instrument whose output is both the ordering and the physical quantity it claims to measure, with no separate anchor). Fires F7 only if such an output is shown to be immune to Goodhart style drift, which the framework's proxy sources (Goodhart, Campbell, Strathern in *Arrival of the Fittest* citations) deny.

**Status: triangulated** (Fig 9, Fig 2, Fig 7, low overlap routes).

### F8 - The round trip from per task performance to one scale loses information

Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."

Compress N slots to a scalar, then try to reconstruct expectations for a specific task. The difference is the round trip gap R_T. It is not noise to be eliminated; it is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold. A single scale report that does not state R_T is dead reckoning.

**Refuter:** show a compression whose round trip gap is zero for every ground question of interest. Fires F8.

**Status: single-source (Fig 4) but structurally decisive for how any scale must be reported.**

### F9 - Tasks and contexts are not three readings of one quantity (analogue, declared low overlap)

Fig 13 :35: "Space, time and energy are not three readings of one quantity. Neither are spectrum, gradient and network."
Fig 13 :36 to :37: "adjacency is not duration, an ordering is not a pathway, a pathway is not a direction."

This is an analogue, not a direct ruling on psychometrics. It corroborates the shape: distinct members of a set answer questions the others cannot, and if they drew on the same underlying fact their agreement would carry no information (Fig 13 :38 to :39). Applied here with the analyst's own judgment (mode `specified`): a verbal task and a spatial task need not be readings of one quantity just because their scores correlate. World corroboration: the g factor article records factor indeterminacy, that "it is not possible to distinguish statistically between Spearman's model of g and the sampling model; both are equally able to account for inter correlations among tests."

**Refuter:** derive the task scores from one quantity with no remainder, showing the correlation is identity rather than projection. Fires F9. Overlap declaration: this is an analogy from the six term substrate, so it is a weak witness, not an independent one.

**Status: single-source analogue.** Not counted with F1 in the triangulation.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

"A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument." The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A scalar is exhibited that preserves all per slot residuals for every ground question asked of it (fires F1).
2. Description cost and test cost are shown commensurable in one derived unit (fires F2).
3. A figure line is found that licenses one scalar as a ground verdict across habitats (fires F3).
4. A controlled agent only intervention moves the ground across habitats (fires F5).
5. One rule is demonstrated across all sampled contexts and correctly predicts a held out subset, with no winning different rule (fires F6).
6. A compression is shown with zero round trip gap for every ground question of interest (fires F8).
7. A ground instrument yields a single number that orders capability across all tasks and contexts and predicts novel task performance. This fires the "convention only" half of the verdict and moves that domain toward `grounded`.

None of these was run. All remain open. Until one fires, nothing in this folder is labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Answer the question with the verdict split intact: yes as a convention, no as a ground verdict from aggregated scores, and the question reframed as capability in a habitat (F1, F4, F5).
2. Never report a single scale without its per slot residual profile and its round trip gap R_T (F1, F8).
3. State which currency the scale prices and keep the other visible (F2).
4. Use the rank to decide what gets tried, not to settle what is true (F7).
5. If the empirical g question is the real target, send it to instruments, not to the figures (F10). Fig 6 :34 to :35 says a search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there. Here something is already there: psychometrics is an instrument returning readings.

## 7. Final verdict of the audit

The shape survives, the naive answer does not. A single scale can be a meaningful ordering and a meaningful convention, and Fig 11 redirects the question to capability in a habitat. It is not a meaningful ground verdict about an agent's intelligence across tasks and contexts when it is formed by aggregating away the per slot residual, mixing incommensurable currencies, or treating correlation from a shared pool as a witness. The empirical existence of a general factor is left to the ground. Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the domain verdicts, ungrounded, candidate.
