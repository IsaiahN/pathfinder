# Solution REA-0009

**Question:** Can intelligence be meaningfully ranked on a single scale across tasks and contexts?

**Source:** reasoning.txt line 12

**Section:** 1. Defining intelligence

**Mode:** `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the per-domain verdicts. Nothing here is `grounded`. The question of whether a general factor exists empirically is a ground question for psychometrics and is left open.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

**Field for public contact points:** intelligence ranking research

## What this solution decides

Restated as an effect: given observed performance across many task and context slots, we want a single scalar that orders systems, transfers to unobserved tasks, and tracks something real rather than only coordinating a shared meaning. Three options lead to different places.

1. A scalar as a **verdict about capability across contexts**. This treats each task as a reading of one latent quantity and aggregates the slots. Fig 1 :19 and :22 disallow the aggregation, and Fig 12 :130 disallows summing incommensurable currencies into one figure.
2. A scalar as a **convention for coordination**. This is legitimate. Fig 2 :41 says agreement about a shared meaning is the product, and Seat Map :163 says high correlation is efficient for a convention. It must not be promoted into a verdict.
3. No scalar at all because capability is **agent-and-habitat**. Fig 11 :39 refuses "what intelligence is" and replaces it with "what makes a system capable," a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone.

The verdicts are kept separate per domain and are never averaged. See `01` and `09`.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk send plan.
- No claim that psychometric g does or does not exist. That is a ground question for instruments, and the figures do not hand down an empirical verdict.

Three figure grounded reasons, stated in `02` and `04`:

1. Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." A list selected for agreement cannot refute the claim.
2. Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." This simulation shares one evidence pool across seats.
3. Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, never the framework interior.

## Honest standing

This folder is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on. No measurement of intelligence, no psychometric dataset, and no experiment was run. World sources were read as secondary corroboration only.
