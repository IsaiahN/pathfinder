# Is a single scale an isomer of a per slot profile? Structure, order, and analogues

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

A single scale and a per task score profile can be built from the same observations. Are they variants of one term, or different substances? And what order does the derivation impose: within task first, then across task, or the reverse? Two questions are owed: (1) what structure is a rank under the figures, and (2) is the aggregate an isomer of the profile under Fig 12's definition?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - What structure is a rank?

- Fig 13 :20: "ordering, so more and less can be said." This is the spectrum structure, one of the six.
- Operators table: comparison is "a threshold relation between two quantities"; the test is whether it is about which is larger rather than both being present.
- A rank over per slot scores is a comparison bond ⋛ applied to quantities at the second order. It is a lawful structure. The figure licenses ordering; it does not license a claim that the ordering is the ground verdict about the thing ordered (Fig 9 :32, "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself").
- **I considered and declined** mapping rank to a spectrum-of-one-quantity in the physical sense. Spectrum here is a structure among six, not a latent variable. Overlap with the psychometric "g" is a similarity of shape, not identity of referent. Mark `specified`, not `grounded`.
- **Falsifier:** show that ordering cannot be composed from held atoms without importing a new primitive. Fires Q1.

### Q2 - First order versus second order

- Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."
- A per task score settled against the ground (a held out item, a criterion) is a first order molecule: observation, instrument, comparison. It can serve as an atom at the next level.
- The cross task aggregate is second order: it composes over first order scores. That is legal only because the first order scores settled. If a per task score is not settled, the aggregate composes over something unusable (Fig 12 :106: nothing composes over what was never admitted as a part).
- Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." Carrying the first order score up and bringing the aggregate back down loses information; R_T is the measurement of that loss.
- **Triangulated** for legality (Fig 12 and Fig 4, low overlap). Single source for the psychometric claim that an unsettled score blocks the aggregate.
- **Falsifier:** show a correct cross task aggregate built over first order scores that were never settled against any ground. Fires Q2.

### Q3 - Are the aggregate and the profile isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Take the same observation vector `(o'_s1 ... o'_sN)`. Composed as a list with per slot residuals preserved, it is a profile. Composed by summation into one scalar, it is a rank. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. Under Fig 12 they are **isomers**, not spellings of one thing, and the difference is invisible in a formula that records only the ingredients.
- The weaker reading: both share the functional group `observation -> comparison -> ordered output`. Under Fig 12 :124 that makes them members of the same kind (a scored representation), with the aggregate as one qualifier and the profile as another. This is the honest claim when the ground has not settled which composition pays.
- The stronger claim worth testing: for every ground question asked of the system, the profile and the scalar give different answers. Then the isomer reading is not a notation point but a behavioural fact.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if the scalar and the profile give the same answers to every ground question of interest (zero round trip gap for all of them), the isomer claim fails and the two are one term under two notations. This is the same test as F8 in `01`, stated in the composition vocabulary.
- **Status: single-source for the figure definition (Fig 12), triangulated with Fig 4 for the behavioural difference.**

### Q4 - What order is the union?

- Fig 9 :4 to :5: "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."
- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."
- The dependency order is: (1) describe the residual in effect terms, (2) refuse any term held that is false before minting (formula step 2), (3) settle first order per slot scores, (4) compose the second order aggregate, (5) range both against the ground and record R_T. This is a dependency order, not a ranking of tasks by importance.
- Tasks themselves are not linearly ordered by the figures. Where different rules work on different subsets, the correct move is a split, not a super order over tasks (Fig 9 :54 to :58).
- **Falsifier:** demonstrate a dependency inversion (a correct aggregate composed before its first order scores settled, with the ground paying for it). Fires Q4.

## CONCLUSION

A rank is the spectrum structure applied through the comparison bond, lawful as an ordering and silent as a verdict. A cross task score is a second order composition over settled first order scores. The aggregate and the per slot profile are isomers under Fig 12 when they give different answers to the same ground questions, and members of one functional group when they do not. The order of the union is a dependency order, not an order over tasks. The isomer claim is checkable and has a falsifier written in advance. Nothing here is settled by an instrument.
