# Outreach draft: check one output before deciding about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the verdict split in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is stated on every line of the output. The output must survive validation or the two stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one claim about single scale ranking before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual:** can intelligence be meaningfully ranked on a single scale across tasks and contexts? Write down what such an answer would look like before judging it: a function from per task performance scores to one number, where the ordering is unchanged when you change which tasks you sample, and the number predicts performance on a task that was not used to build it. That is the claim under test.
>
> **What I recorded before running anything:** the figures I am using distinguish a verdict about the world from a convention for coordination. A single scale can be a real ordering and a real coordination device. The claim that fails is the stronger one: that one aggregated number is a ground verdict about an agent's capability across habitats. Three things refute that stronger claim. (1) Show a scalar that preserves every per task residual without loss. (2) Show that description cost and test cost are commensurable in one derived unit. (3) Run a controlled intervention that changes only the agent and moves the ground across habitats. I wrote these before sending this. A one line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement.
>
> **The verdict I get, stated before any theory:** yes, a single scale is meaningful as a convention. No, an aggregate scalar is not a ground verdict about capability across tasks and contexts. And the framework refuses the framing: it asks what makes a system capable, and treats capability as a property of agent and habitat, never of the agent alone. Mode on those claims is `general` for the shape and `specified` for the per domain verdicts. Nothing here is `grounded`. Whether a general factor exists empirically is a question for psychometric instruments, not for this derivation.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that a general intelligence factor does not exist. That ordering is forbidden. That rankings are useless. That this derivation settles the empirical question. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading, and it cannot settle the question it reports on.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the figures and formula are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Full architecture: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
> - Worked narrative on intelligence and capability: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-intelligence-actually-works
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> [your name]
