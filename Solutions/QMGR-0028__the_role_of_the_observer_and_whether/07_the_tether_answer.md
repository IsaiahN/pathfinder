# The Tether's answer on the observer and consciousness in measurement

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape: not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Quantum theory gives linear evolution of system plus apparatus and a definite-outcome rule at measurement. Applying only the first to a measurement yields an entangled superposition of apparatus-record states; observation yields one definite record. The gap is R. The question bundles two conditions: (A) what role an observer plays in the formalism, and (B) whether consciousness has a causal role in producing the definite outcome. Fig 9 :4 calls a disagreement that does not shrink because each rule works on a different subset *"several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* These two conditions are a union, and must be answered separately (Fig 9 :54).

## 2. The shape, one loop per domain

**Loop 1, D1 (physics of measurement).** Actors: system, apparatus, environment, record, multiple observers. Currency: unitary evolution, decoherence, Born probabilities. Anchor: the experimental record. Verdict: the live residual is per measurement context, not global (Fig 1 :19), and the record is the ground for any claim about definiteness (Fig 2 :29). The observer in this domain is an information-storing physical system; the formalism's cut is movable, and von Neumann did not require a conscious observer at it (SEP, Myrvold, §4.3). The extended Wigner's friend experiments show that the conflict is between recorded facts, not in anyone's consciousness (Proietti et al. 2019). **Per-domain verdict: `specified`, triangulated.**

**Loop 2, D2 (consciousness / mind).** Actors: experiences, reports, neural correlates. Currency: first- versus third-person data. Anchor: none nameable for the causal-collapse claim. Verdict: the strong claim "a definite record requires a conscious observer" is refused by automated detectors and by the Bell-Wigner results (Fig 5 :53). The positive claim "consciousness plays no part" is **not** established: *"a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence"* (Fig 8 :39). Wigner's own later repudiation is provenance, not a measurement. As usually stated, the consciousness claim is a sought shape held as a preference rather than a bound, predicting term (Fig 5 :62), so no check inside the frame can see it. **Per-domain verdict: strong refusal `specified`, triangulated; positive absence `gap`.**

**Loop 3, D3 (verification and outreach).** Actors: expert readers, survey respondents. Currency: agreement and residuals. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Verdict: Fig 7 :13 *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"* and Fig 7 :35 *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict."* The 6 percent and 1 percent poll figures are filters for where to look, not witnesses (Fig 9 :60). In this simulated run all seats share one evidence pool, so their agreement is internal consistency, not corroboration. **Per-domain verdict: `specified`, triangulated on the method, single-source on the numbers.**

The three verdicts are kept separate and are never averaged. There is no single "average answer" to the question.

## 3. What the shape commits to

1. **Split the question.** The structural observer question and the consciousness causal question are a union, not one hard problem (Fig 9 :54). "Necessary", "sufficient" and "privileged perspective" are further distinct claims.
2. **Bind the candidate term before the run.** A consciousness-collapse term must be absent from unitary-only evolution, name a physical-specifiable trigger, change an observable prediction against a baseline, and be settleable by an experiment (Fig 5 :65: *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM"*).
3. **Name the ground correctly.** The ground is the record (interference visibility, detector counts, Bell-Wigner S). Consciousness is an instrument for this question, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure (Fig 2 :37-38, Seat Map :18). It would become an anchor only under refuter 1 below.
4. **Refuse asymmetrically.** Strong necessity is refused; universal absence is `gap`. A refusal is a negation, which is not a physical bond and adds no atom (Operators table :70-78).
5. **Keep the two senses of observer apart.** The physical record-holder (D1) and the checking seat (D3) are a homonym across two closures, not the same object (`06_isomers_and_order.md` Q1). Arguments that move between them commit a closure error.
6. **No new primitive, no interpretation selected.** The six-term set is closed (Fig 13); the figures decide the shape and the ground, not which interpretation of QM is right.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that consciousness is proved irrelevant. Absence of a found role is not proof of no role (Fig 8 :39).
- Not that a poll or a majority of physicists settles the question. Agreement is a room, not an anchor (Fig 7 :35).
- Not that Wigner's later repudiation is a measurement. It is provenance.
- Not that decoherence solves the measurement problem on its own. The literature read this session says it does not settle the definite-outcome question; this solution takes no side on that debate.
- Not `grounded`. No experiment varying a mental trigger at fixed physical conditions was run or cited.
- Not a physical model of collapse, and no seventh primitive.

## 5. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (essay 7, read this session): a reading cannot comment on its source; the spectrometer, not an argument, resolved the metamerism. Here:

1. **Trigger-variation experiment:** vary conscious awareness with apparatus, environment and detector fixed; measure whether the definiteness or statistics of the record change. A positive, registered result moves the strong claim toward `grounded`.
2. **Distinct-prediction model:** a quantitative consciousness-collapse model with a non-mental trigger and a different number would make the claim a term instead of a preference.
3. **Source discrimination:** an instrument that tells collapse, branching, hidden variables and epistemic update apart at the level of the record would name the source rather than read it off the projection.
4. **Standing structural refuters:** a term that closes both union conditions at once; a derivation that the two senses of observer are one closure; a figure re-census that predicts a consciousness signature.

Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 6 :34: *"The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve."* Where nothing is already registering, there is no edge to extend from. The search here found a shape, an anchor and a refuter, and stopped at the limit of what the available instruments distinguish. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."* The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. Nothing in this folder maintains the ground; only a real experiment does.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not decide whether consciousness is involved in measurement, because it is a metatheory about the shape of answers and the ground that settles them, not a physical theory of collapse. It says the measurement residual is per measurement context, not a global property called "the observer"; that the question bundles a structural question about the observer with a separate causal question about consciousness, which must be unbundled; that the ground is the public record, so consciousness is at most an instrument here and cannot be the anchor; that the strong claim "a definite record requires a conscious observer" is refused by automated detectors and by Bell-Wigner experiments, while the claim "consciousness plays no part" is not proved, because failing to find a role is not proof of no role; and that the popular "observer" names two different objects, a physical record-holder and a checking seat, which must not be moved between. What it offers is the shape any settled answer must have, four refuters written in advance, and the kind of source-discriminating instrument that would close it. The center stays open until the ground settles it.
