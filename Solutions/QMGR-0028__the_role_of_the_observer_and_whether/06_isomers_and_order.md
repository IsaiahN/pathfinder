# Isomers and order: two senses of "observer", and the dependency order of the union

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the ordering.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question uses one word, "observer", for two different objects: a physical system that stores a record (D1) and a checking position that grades a claim (D3). Treating them as one object is the most common silent error in this literature. Two questions are owed: (1) are these two senses isomers under Fig 12's definition, or merely homonyms; (2) what is the dependency order of the bundled question, so that effort is not spent above a break?

## DOMAIN CENSUS (re-used from `01`, not re-derived)

Atoms held in D1: system, apparatus, environment, record, unitary evolution, decoherence, Born probabilities. Atoms held in D3: maker, interior auditor, ground maintainer, outside frame, anchor, residual, provenance. Bonds held: the seven plus negation. The word "observer" is a shared **label**, not a shared atom across the two sets. No new primitive is available without import.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Are the two "observers" isomers? CORRECTION TO THE FLAT READING

- Fig 12 :5 **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**
- Fig 12 :117 **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124 **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

The two senses do not share the same atoms. D1's observer is a physical information-storing system (Proietti et al. 2019: *"we define an observer as any physical system that can extract information from another system by means of some interaction and store that information in a physical memory."*). D3's observer is a seat defined by what it may not see: Seat Map Seat 4 *"Holds a different vocabulary. Nothing else"* and *"Must not hold the interiors, the records, the anchor, any stake, or any authority."*

Therefore they are **not isomers** in the strict Fig 12 sense, because isomerism requires the same atoms under a different bond. They are a **homonym across two closures**: the same label applied to two different substances. The weaker, correct statement is that both occupy a functional group *"that stores or grades a record"*, with the qualifier selecting which member: physical record-holder (D1) versus checking seat (D3). Fig 12 :124: *"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

**Correction recorded:** an earlier flat reading of the question treated "observer" as one ingredient appearing in two places, which is the collapse Fig 12 :5 names. It is withdrawn. The two senses are not interchangeable, and an argument that moves from "the observer establishes a fact" (D1) to "the observer must be outside the slice it grades" (D3) commits a closure error. Triangulated (Fig 12 plus Seat Map plus Proietti). Mode `specified`.

### Q2: What is the dependency order of the union?

Fig 3 :20 **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**

The bundled question has this order:

| step | question | figure | what it consumes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1, perception | what counts as a definite record, and how is it told apart from the pre-measurement state? | Fig 3 :5 | the observation o′ |
| 2, vocabulary | what does a measurement afford, i.e. what would count as progress toward a definite outcome? | Fig 3 :7 | step 1 |
| 3, objective | what observable would change if a candidate mechanism were real? | Fig 3 :10 | step 2 |
| 4, planning | which instrument could vary that observable? | Fig 3 :13 | step 3 |
| 5, settle and carry | does the ground pay, and can the result be composed over? | Fig 3 :15 | step 4 |

The question as popularly posed starts at step 4 with "consciousness" as the instrument, skipping steps 2 and 3. Fig 3 :27 **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** Fig 3 :22 **"A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can."** So the first corrective move is not "refute consciousness" but "state the observable that would change". That is the bound term in `01` §0.

A second ordering rule is prior to the chain: refuse before mint. Formula step 2 routes a held term that is false to REFUTED, and the term owed is a refusal, not an explanation. "Collapse" currently names both a knowledge update and a physical process; that ambiguity must be refused/re-fitted before a new "consciousness collapse" term is minted. Fig 12 :112: **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."** Negation is unary and is not one of the seven bonds (Operators table :70-78).

### Q3: Partition or union, after re-census?

Fig 9 :54 **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** Fig 9 :57 **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

Condition A: the structural role of the observer (where the cut goes, what counts as a record). Condition B: the causal role of consciousness (whether a mental trigger produces the definite outcome). They overlap because A's answer is often taken to settle B. It does not: von Neumann's movable cut shows that the formalism does not require a conscious observer at the cut (Myrvold 2016/2022, SEP 4.3), while the question of a mental causal trigger remains a separate empirical claim. The intersection, B together with A, is a specific compound: "a physical process whose trigger is mental". It has its own ground and its own refuter (refuter 1 in `01` §3). A single lever that resolves A does not clear the intersection. Union confirmed.

### Q4: What would settle the compound?

The instrument/source distinction from *The Hard Problem is About Source* (essay 7, read this session): a reading cannot comment on its source. The definite record is a reading; collapse, branching, hidden variables and a mental trigger are candidate sources. The essay's remedy was not a better argument but a better instrument (the spectrometer). Here the corresponding instrument is an experiment that varies the candidate trigger while holding apparatus, environment and detector fixed. Until such an instrument exists, the compound is unreached rather than answered. Fig 6 :32 **"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."** So the question for a proposer is not "could such a sensor exist" but "what already returns something that fails to resolve?" (Fig 6 :34).

## CONCLUSION

The two "observers" are a homonym across two closures, not isomers: same label, different atoms, different anchors, different substance. The bundled question is a union whose dependency order starts at the observable, not at the proposed mechanism. The first obligation is to bind the sought term and state the refuter; the second is to find an instrument that discriminates sources; the third is to let the ground settle. No new physical primitive is licensed, and no interpretation is selected.

## FALSIFIER, written before the run

If a single object is derived that is both the physical record-holder and the checking seat at one level, the homonym finding fails. If the two conditions close against one ground with one term, the union finding fails. If a quantitatively distinct prediction is registered for a mental trigger, the "instrument first" order is satisfied and the conclusion moves toward a ground reading, which is the desired failure. Any of these is recorded in the retraction log of `01` rather than silently absorbed.
