## What the wider literature actually says about this question

The world sources are secondary corroboration. They do not decide the verdict; the figures do.

1. **The law/practice distinction is a named field, not our invention.** Legal realism's core claim, "the law cannot be separated from its application", and its focus on "the law as it actually exists in practice, rather than how it exists in books", is the same distinction as Fig 5's two conditions. Holmes's "prophecies of what the courts will do in fact" is an early ground reading of exactly this residual.
2. **The gap has been measured, and it is a reach gap.** The World Justice Project attributes exclusion to people lacking *legal tools*, not to a lack of rights: identity documents, land tenure, formal work arrangements. That is the figure language of missing atoms in a closure. LSC's 92% figure is a per-slot reach failure, not a count of absent rights.
3. **The capability literature triangulates Fig 11 from a low-overlap field.** Sen and Nussbaum distinguish formal freedom from substantive capability, and Nussbaum's "combined capabilities" are internal capability plus the social, political and economic conditions for choosing. This is development economics, not law, so its agreement is a genuinely different witness.
4. **Legal consciousness locates the break at the middle link.** People interpret law through "context, subtext and pretext" shaped by class position. Sandefur's finding that people do not recognise their problem as legal is a direct reading of Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
5. **Practice names bindings, not the shape.** Namati's phrase "taking law out of books and courtrooms" is a practice binding onto the shape. Its own outcome claim (concrete remedies for over 5 million people) is a ground reading from a world frame, not a derivation from the figures.

Overlap check: the Access to justice, Legal consciousness, UN and WJP sources share a law-and-society pool; their agreement is one witness, not four. The capability approach (development economics) and legal realism (legal philosophy, early twentieth century) are the two low-overlap witnesses. Namati and LSC are practice sources, ground-visible but institutionally interested, so their stake is declared rather than hidden.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 10 :52**, "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Sending the full framework before anyone has described a reach failure sends an interior, not a generator. Lead with the residual and its five refuters; offer links after.
- **Fig 2 :35**, "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Do not ask whether the recipient likes the framework.
- **Fig 9 :30 to :31**, "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause." Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the article.
- **Fig 2 :40**, "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." A mass send selected for people likely to agree produces a convention, not corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the residual, shape claim and refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) record that answer; (4) only then the three links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## Two-stage offer (and stage 3)

1. **Stage 1:** validate the output against the ground. The recipient checks the shape claim without holding the framework's interior.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in:** only if curious, read the theory that produced it. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse. Each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode before running it.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 work only if the stage 1 claim survives. The five refuters are written into the message itself, so a recipient can fire one immediately. That is the design instrument, not a courtesy.
