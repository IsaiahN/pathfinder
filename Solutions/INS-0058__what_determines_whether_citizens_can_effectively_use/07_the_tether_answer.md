# The Tether's answer: what determines whether citizens can effectively use formal rights

**Mode: `specified` on the mapping and verdict, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a measured result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A right is formally admitted, sometimes after litigation, and a citizen still cannot turn it into a remedy. The gap is not disagreement between legal theories. It is between a term in the legal library and a person's ability to compose over it. Fig 5 :70 to :72:

> **"A term is settled when the ground pays for it. A term is usable when something can compose over it."**
> **"a library that grows is not a reach that grows."**

This is a union compound (Fig 9 :55: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"), and it must be measured per citizen. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."

## 2. The answer

1. **Formal existence is necessary and not sufficient.** A direct admission is required before anything can compose over the term: Fig 12 :106, "Nothing composes over what was never admitted as a part." But settling and usability are two conditions, and the law names one. A growing catalogue of rights is a growing library, not a growing reach (Fig 5 :69 to :72).

2. **The determinant is composability, in a specific place.** What decides effective use is whether the right is reachable inside the citizen's own closure: the information, documents, representation, time, means and safety needed to run a route to a remedy. Fig 6 :14 names import as "the only operator that moves the wall", and Fig 6 :23 says "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." If the needed atom is absent and cannot be imported, the right is out of reach while remaining on the books.

3. **The determinant belongs to citizen-and-habitat, not to either alone.** Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." So it is not the citizen's knowledge or virtue, and not the right's wording or scope. Reform that changes only the statute is an improvement that does not change contact.

4. **The break is at the unmeasured middle link.** Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." The missing instrument reads what the right affords this person and what would count as progress for them. Most reform effort lands above the break, where measurements already exist.

5. **The verdict is per citizen at the ground, and counts are disqualified.** Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." Rights-on-books, statute counts and offices opened are quantities the legal system produces. Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." The witness is the outcome for a person.

6. **A paper right is a channel failure, not a false right.** Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Fig 10 :27 to :28: "the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch." The honest report is that contact has been lost, and what is owed is an import with a source gate and a shadow test, not another proclamation.

7. **The answer is a shape, not a list of institutions.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Legal aid, awareness, anti-retaliation rules, identity documents and court access are bindings from the world frame onto the shape, recorded with provenance and tested against the gap they claim to explain. They are not figures-derived names.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that a formal right is worthless, illusory or illegitimate. It is a necessary term and the precondition of any use.
- Not that the citizen is to blame. Capability is agent-and-habitat, and the habitat is not authored by the citizen.
- Not that any single institution is the answer. The framework returns a shape.
- Not that more rights are bad. It says that adding rights without changing contact changes nothing, which is a claim about the determinant, not a ban on legislation.
- Not a derivation of legal doctrine from the six-term substrate. The substrate is not used here as a naming device.
- Not grounded. No controlled ground reading was run in this derivation.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

An instrument at the middle of the chain, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. The corresponding tests here are:

- **Reach test, per slot:** for a named right and a named citizen, does the citizen's closure contain the atoms required to run the route to a remedy? A source-discriminating instrument would distinguish a right that is unusable because of missing information from one unusable because of retaliation risk from one unusable because of distance. The statute cannot discriminate these, which is the metamerism error.
- **Threshold test:** at what point does an imported atom change the ground reading? Fig 6 :14 prices a wrong atom above starting from primitives, so the import needs a source gate and a shadow test.
- **Five standing falsifiers** from `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4: reliable effectiveness with no habitat change; citizen-only improvement that moves the outcome; use arising by search inside an unchanged closure; an aggregate count preserving the per-slot signal; usability settled by agreement alone.
- **Isomer falsifier** from `06_isomers_and_order.md`: no systematic difference in effective use between systems with identical formal rights and different arrangements.

## 5. The honest stop

**Reached in shape, unreached in name.** Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." The figures narrow the determinant to composability within citizen-and-habitat, measured per slot at the ground. They do not select among the institutions that the assigned field studies. The world sources corroborate at different levels of overlap: the capability approach from development economics is the low-overlap witness; the law-and-society sources are one pool, not four. The answer stops at the limit of what the available instruments distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity" (canonical figures README).

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that paper rights are false rights, and it does not name a single institution as the cure. It says that formal existence and effective use are two different conditions: a right on the books is a term admitted to the legal library, and using it is a separate matter of whether a person can compose over that term. Settling is necessary and not sufficient, so a growing catalogue of rights is a growing library, not a growing reach. What decides use is composability, measured per citizen at the ground: whether the right is reachable inside that person's closure, with a habitat supplying the missing atoms of information, access, representation and safety. Capability belongs to the citizen-and-habitat, not to either alone, so reform that changes only the wording changes nothing. The break is at the unmeasured middle link, where a person has to be able to say what the right affords them and what would count as progress. What is offered here is that shape, five refuters written in advance, and the kind of instrument that would close it. The name stays open until the ground settles it.
