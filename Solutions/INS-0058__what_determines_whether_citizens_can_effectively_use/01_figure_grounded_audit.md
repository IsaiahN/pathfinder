## 0. The residual stated in effect terms

> What determines whether citizens can effectively use legal rights that formally exist?

Restated as an effect: a right is on the books (a term admitted to the legal library), and in some cases a citizen can turn that term into a remedy while in others the same term yields nothing. The question asks which quantity makes the difference.

- **Option A:** formal existence is the determinant. Effort goes into enumerating and proclaiming more rights; success is measured by rights on the statute book.
- **Option B:** formal existence is necessary but not sufficient. Effort goes into what lets a citizen compose over the term; success is measured at the ground, per citizen.

**What an answer would look like (the sought-for shape bound as an ordinary term).** An answer names a *determinant of composability*: the conditions under which an admitted right becomes reachable and usable by a particular citizen. Binding this as a term lets it accrue a residual like anything else, which is what the formula's "Direction" discipline requires. The figures return that shape, not the name of any particular institution.

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.**

- **Arity:** two conditions (a right formally exists; a citizen effectively uses it). This is a **union compound**, not one hard question. Fig 9 :55: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound." Adding a right where none existed clears the first condition and leaves the intersection standing.
- **Symmetry:** asymmetric. The map from a right on the books to effective use is lossy and one-way: a generator crosses, an instance does not (Fig 10 :68). A written right is a record, not a capability.
- **Scale:** the individual case (one citizen, one slot) and the institutional system. R is indexed per slot. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

## 3. Findings F1 to F7

### F1 — Settling is necessary, but it is not sufficient (decisive)

Fig 5, amendment at :69 to :72:

> **"settling is necessary for a term to be composed over, and it is not sufficient."**
> **"A term is settled when the ground pays for it. A term is usable when something can compose over it. Those are two conditions and this figure names one."**
> **"a library that grows is not a reach that grows."**

Fig 12 :106 gives the necessity half: "Nothing composes over what was never admitted as a part."

**Mapping.** A right that formally exists is a term admitted into the legal library, and in the strongest case a term the ground has paid for (a right upheld in court). Effective use is the separate question of whether a citizen can compose over it. The figures state that these are two conditions and name one. Therefore formal existence is a precondition of effective use, never its determinant. A growing catalogue of rights is a growing library, and a library is not a reach.

**Mode:** `specified` for the mapping, `general` for the two-condition structure. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 5 amendment and Fig 12 :106 (two figures with low overlap: one governs the mint's sufficiency, the other governs admission necessity).

**Refuter:** show a case where a right that was never formally admitted is nonetheless reliably used as a right by citizens and honoured at the ground without enactment (a purely customary claim recognised and enforced as a right). That would break necessity. Or show that formal admission alone reliably produces effective use across citizens and conditions with no habitat or closure change. That would collapse the two conditions.

### F2 — Capability is a property of citizen-and-habitat, not of either alone (decisive)

Fig 11 :39 to :40:

> **"the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**
> **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

Fig 11 :61: "Introduction is available; exclusion is not."

**Mapping.** "What determines whether citizens can effectively use legal rights" is the institutional form of "what makes a system capable". The answer is not a property of the citizen (their knowledge, diligence or motivation), nor of the right (its wording or scope), but of the citizen-in-habitat: the contact they have with legal aid, information, an impartial forum, protection from retaliation, and the material means to pursue a remedy. Reforming the right's wording while leaving contact untouched is an improvement that changes nothing. Because introduction is available and exclusion is not, adding the needed atoms is possible and removing the habitat is not, which is why this is permanent work rather than a solved state.

**Mode:** `general` for the capability law, `specified` for the mapping to legal rights. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 11 :39-40 (capability) and Fig 11 :61 (asymmetry of introduction), two distinct claims in one figure; corroborated by the capability approach and legal consciousness world sources (§4 of `09_watson_answer.md`).

**Refuter:** a controlled case in which improving the citizen in isolation (legal literacy or motivation only), with no change in habitat or contact, reliably moves the ground reading. Fig 11 :40 says this cannot happen; a real case would refute it.

### F3 — Reachability: what the citizen's closure must contain

Fig 6 :14: "the only operator that moves the wall". Fig 6 :23:

> **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**

Fig 8 :53: "Residual first, frame second."

**Mapping.** A citizen's effective closure is the set of terms they can actually compose with: information, documents, representation, time, money, physical access, and safety from retaliation. If an atom required to use the right (a lawyer, an identity document, a forum, protection) is absent from that closure and cannot be imported from a frame whose closure differs, the right is out of reach even though it is in the library. Import has a source gate (provenance) and a shadow test (it must explain the gap). An institution that is present but explains no gap is a wrong atom, priced above starting from primitives (Fig 6 :24).

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** single-source for the import mapping (Fig 6), triangulated with Fig 8 :53 for the order of operations and with Fig 9 :30 to :31 ("describe the effect rather than the cause").

**Refuter:** effective use arising by search alone inside an unchanged closure, with no new atom entering it. That would break Fig 6 :14 and :23.

### F4 — Measure per slot; frame-internal counts are disqualified

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 1 :27:

> **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."**

Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

**Mapping.** The determinant cannot be read off an aggregate. The right may work for the powerful and fail for the marginalised; an average hides exactly the live slot. Counting rights, statutes, legal-aid offices or training sessions is counting predicates minted, a quantity the legal system produces and cannot use to score itself. Justice-gap statistics are filters for where to spend the budget. The verdict is per citizen at the ground.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 1 and Fig 9 :60.

**Refuter:** demonstrate that an aggregate rights count preserves the per-slot signal at the ground (that it predicts who gets a remedy as well as a per-slot reading does). That would break Fig 1 :19 and :22.

### F5 — The ground decides; a formal right that does not register is a lost channel

Fig 2 :29 to :30: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does". Fig 10 :27 to :28:

> **"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"**

**Mapping.** A formal right that citizens cannot use is not a false right and not a reason to declare the law illegitimate. It is a declaration whose channel to the ground has decayed: stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings (Fig 10 :25 to :26). What settles usability is an outcome for a person, not agreement among lawyers that the right exists. Agreement is efficient for a convention and disqualifying for a verdict.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 2 and Fig 10.

**Refuter:** show usability being settled by professional consensus with no ground reading, and that the consensus predicts outcomes at least as well as the outcome itself. That would make agreement an anchor, which Fig 2 forbids.

### F6 — The break is at the middle link: affordances have no instrument

Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Fig 3 :25 to :27:

> **"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."**
> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

**Mapping.** The determinant sits at the middle of the chain: whether a citizen has terms for what the right affords and can say what would count as progress (a remedy). This link has no instrument, so reform effort lands above the break where measurements already exist, in more rights and more statutes. World corroboration is direct. The access-to-justice article records Rebecca Sandefur's finding: "people do not consider law as a solution for their justice problems; they do not think of their problems as being 'legal,' even when the legal system could help solve them." Namati says "Yet for most people, the law is out of reach." Legal consciousness studies report that "Law may mean different things depending on an individual's location in the various hierarchies of status, prestige, and knowledge associated with membership in a social class."

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** single-source for the figure mapping (Fig 3), with three low-overlap world fields corroborating (law and policy, development practice, sociology of law).

**Refuter:** a case where adding a right at the top of the chain, with no instrument at the affordance link, reliably restores effective use. That would show the break is not where Fig 3 places it.

### F7 — The determinant is a shape, not a name

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 8 :53: "Residual first, frame second." Fig 9 :30 to :31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause".

**Mapping.** The figures narrow the answer to: composability of the admitted right, within a habitat, measured per slot at the ground. They do not name which institution fills the slot. Legal aid, legal awareness, anti-retaliation protection, court access, paralegals and identity documents are bindings from the world frame onto this shape. They must be provenanced as such, with a source gate and a shadow test, and never presented as figure-derived names.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 9 :32 and the canonical figures README. The README's phrase governs a *missing* thing: "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)". Here the missing item is composability in the citizen's closure, which is genuinely not held, so the limit applies.

**Refuter:** a figure recensus that names a specific institution as required by the six-term substrate or the operators. No such line exists; if one were found, this finding would narrow.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

"A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument." The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A right that is formally admitted is shown to be always and reliably effective across citizens and conditions with no habitat or closure change (breaks F1, Fig 5 :69-72).
2. A controlled improvement of the citizen in isolation, with no change in contact, reliably moves the ground reading (breaks F2, Fig 11 :39-40).
3. Effective use is shown to arise by search alone inside an unchanged closure, with no atom imported (breaks F3, Fig 6 :14, :23).
4. Aggregate rights counts are shown to preserve the per-slot signal at the ground (breaks F4, Fig 1 :19, :22).
5. Usability is shown to be settled by agreement alone, with no ground reading, and that agreement predicts outcomes (breaks F5, Fig 2 :35).

If any fires, the shape is wrong and the answer is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`. Note that items 1 to 5 are *tests*, not assertions that the tests have failed: the world sources in §4 of `09_watson_answer.md` suggest they will not fire, but none was run as a controlled ground reading here.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Treat formal existence as necessary and not sufficient (F1). Stop reporting rights-on-books as a success measure.
2. Locate the determinant at citizen-and-habitat (F2). Ask which atoms are missing from the citizen's closure, not which right is missing from the statute.
3. Import the missing atoms with a source gate and a shadow test (F3). An institution earns its place by explaining the observed reach failure.
4. Measure per citizen at the ground (F4). Use aggregates only as budget filters.
5. Instrument the middle link (F6). The unmeasured question is "what would count as progress for this person with this right?", not "how many rights exist?".
6. Send the generator, not the count. Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." The generator here is the shape claim plus its five refuters.

## 7. Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is formal existence sufficient?** No. Fig 5 :69-72 names settling and composability as two conditions.
2. **Which quantity is the determinant?** Reachability of the right in the citizen's closure, within a habitat (Fig 6 :14, :23; Fig 11 :39-40).
3. **Is the citizen alone the determinant?** No. Capability is agent-and-habitat (Fig 11 :39-40); improvement without contact changes nothing.
4. **How is it measured?** Per slot at the ground (Fig 1 :19, :22). Aggregates are filters, not witnesses (Fig 9 :60).
5. **Where is the break?** The middle link, affordance and progress, which has no instrument (Fig 3 :25-27).
6. **Can a specific institution be named from the figures?** No. Fig 9 :32 gives a kind of thing, never the answer itself. Institutions are imported bindings with a source gate.
7. **What is owed?** An instrument at the middle link and an import with a shadow test. Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget differ (formula step 7); here the shape is reachable and the specific institution is unreached at this budget.
