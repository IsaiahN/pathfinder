## 0. The residual in effect terms

**Question as an effect.** Modern life builds from four molecular families: amino acids, sugars (ribose above all), nucleobases, and membrane-forming amphiphiles. The observation `o′` is that these four families exist in biology and that some members are found in meteorites. The prediction `Γ(b,a)` is whatever a chosen prebiotic frame says should form from a given feedstock under a given energy source and setting. The residual `R` is the gap between the four-family observation and any one demonstrated geochemically plausible route that produces them in the same place at the same time.

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term (formula step "Direction").** An answer is a *source-and-route pair*: for each family, a named precursor set, an ordered production path, a habitat that supplies and concentrates it, and a ground reading that discriminates that path from a rival. Writing this down makes the sought-for shape refusable. Without it, any one-pot product list looks like an answer.

**Slots, never an average.** `R` is indexed per molecular family and per sub-question (supply, route, concentration, co-location, chirality). Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A single global verdict such as "prebiotic chemistry works" throws away the slot that is actually failing, which is usually ribose stability, selectivity, or co-location.

**The direction is the unchosen part.** Demonstrating that a reaction *can* produce glycine is a ground reading about possibility. It is not a ground reading about what *did* happen on the Hadean Earth. The bond `⇒` is ordered: Fig 12 :80, *"A produces B; B did not exist before"*. A correlation between feedstock abundance and product presence is symmetric and cannot select the arrow; Fig 2 :39, *"Correlation has valence."* The loop can check the mechanism and cannot check the direction of its own question (formula step 2 and "Direction"), so the direction is handled here as a discipline: the shape is bound, the refuter is written before the conclusion, and the verdict stays `candidate` until a source-discriminating ground reading arrives.

## 1. Domain census, before any figure line was chosen

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity.** The residual is a many-place relation: four product families crossed with at least two supply modes (local synthesis, exogenous delivery) and at least three further conditions (concentration, co-location in space and time, chirality). It is not a two-place "precursor gives product" question.
- **Symmetry.** Production is ordered (`⇒`), not a symmetric join. The product families are related to one another by shared atoms under different arrangements, which is an isomer or functional-group relation, not identity. Fig 13 :121: *"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."*
- **Scale.** Four scales are in play: the single reaction (molecular), the reaction network (pathway), the local setting (stream, pool, ice, vent, parent asteroid), and geological time (accumulation and survival). Each crossing is lossy. Fig 4 :27, *"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."*

### 1.3 Partition or union

**Union, not partition.** Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* The four product families have different chemistries and different failure modes. Supply (local versus imported) is a second axis crossing all four. Concentration, co-location and chirality are further conditions crossing both. A single lever (for example, "add more lightning") clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. Verdicts are therefore recorded per domain and never averaged.

### 1.4 Dependency order (Fig 3)

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* The order for this question is:

1. **Perception.** Can the instrument detect and distinguish the products at all? The history of sugar detection in meteorites is a history of this link: early aldose reports were suspect because the analytic method could not separate isomers.
2. **Vocabulary.** Does the frame have terms for what the feedstocks afford (which bonds can form, which intermediates are stable)?
3. **Objective.** Can it say what would count as progress: yield, selectivity, survival, or a source-discriminating isotope reading? This is the middle link with no instrument in much of the literature. Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
4. **Planning.** A route through the network under incomplete geochemical information.
5. **Learn and carry.** Verify the route, stamp origin and date, and carry a method rather than a recording. Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Effort spent on yield after the selectivity link is broken arrives at a step that cannot use it.

## 3. Findings, with verbatim quotes and a refuter for each

### F1 The question is a union; verdicts stay per family (decisive for the shape)

Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*

The familiar one-pot framing (one atmosphere, one soup, one answer) assumes a partition. It is a union: amino acids, sugars, nucleobases and amphiphiles have different feedstocks, different stability problems, and different supply modes. Published work already splits along those lines: Strecker chemistry for amino acids, formose and photoredox homologation for sugars, HCN oligomerisation and formamide chemistry for nucleobases, and amphiphile self-assembly for membranes. One lever does not clear their intersections.

**Triangulation:** triangulated across Fig 9 (union rule) and Fig 1 (per-slot residual), low overlap between the partition rule and the indexing rule.

**Refuter:** a single route producing all four families in one habitat with measured selectivity, measured concentration, and a ground reading confirming that the route operated there would collapse the union into one answer and would retire this finding.

### F2 Production is ordered; a demonstration of "can" does not settle "did"

Fig 12 :80: *"A produces B; B did not exist before"*
Fig 2 :39: *"Correlation has valence."*
Operators table, on production: the second operand is absent until the first fires.

A Miller-Urey type discharge shows that glycine can form from a reduced gas mixture. It does not show that the Hadean atmosphere was reduced enough, that the site was the discharge, or that glycine accumulated there. The correlation between a laboratory product and a biologically used molecule is symmetric evidence and cannot choose the arrow. The same applies to a meteorite finding: delivery is a production claim about a parent body and a transport claim to Earth, not a claim about synthesis at the site.

**Triangulation:** triangulated across Fig 12 (ordered bond) and Fig 2 (correlation valence).

**Refuter:** a time-resolved measurement at a candidate Hadean site showing the product absent before the precursor fired, and present after, under conditions independently measured at that site.

### F3 Ingredients are not a pathway

Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
Fig 12 :46: *"A recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself."*

Lists such as CH4, NH3, H2 and H2O, or HCN, H2S, phosphate and Cu(I), are ingredient sets. What decides the product is the typed arrangement and the conditions under which the steps run. The same feedstock under different pH, mineral catalysis or delivery order gives different product distributions. A recipe that records only ingredients has recorded half of itself, and nothing downstream can tell the recipes apart.

**Triangulation:** single-source for the figure definition (Fig 12), corroborated in the world literature by the formose selectivity results, which come from a different evidence pool but are not the figure's authority.

**Refuter:** show two different recipes with identical ingredient sets giving identical product distributions and selectivity across all tested conditions. That would make the bond free, which the pricing rule in Fig 12 forbids.

### F4 Local synthesis and delivery are different operators; provenance decides

Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*
Fig 6 :22: *"NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world. IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure."*
Fig 6 :25: *"An import adds no atom to the world."*
Fig 8 :51: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*

Finding uracil in Murchison and synthesising uracil from HCN in a laboratory are identical in the contents: same molecule, same mass, same spectrum. The provenance is carried by the record, in practice by carbon isotope ratios, by racemicity, by the distribution of structural isomers, and by comparison with soil at the fall site. The figures require exactly this: a source gate and a shadow test before an import is admitted. An import adds no atom to the world, so it does not become a new primitive of chemistry; it gives this frame access to atoms another frame already holds.

**Triangulation:** triangulated across Fig 6 (import operator) and Fig 8 (contents versus record), low overlap between the wall-moving rule and the provenance rule.

**Refuter:** a source-discriminating measurement that fails to separate local from exogenous contribution across all four families, so that provenance cannot be recorded even in principle with the available instruments.

### F5 The instrument settles "can" and "did arrive", not "did happen here"

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."*
Fig 2 :30: *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."*
Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."*
Fig 6 :35: *"the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."*

The laboratory experiment and the meteorite assay are real anchors. They do not change because a chemist prefers a theory. But they settle different questions from the one asked. They settle whether a route is chemically possible and whether a molecule arrived from space. They do not settle which route ran at which Hadean site, because no instrument now returns a reading of that site at molecular resolution. This is not a failure of effort. Fig 6 :35 says that where nothing is registering there is no edge to extend from. The historical route is a `gap` of the unreachable-at-this-budget kind, not an underivable-in-principle kind; the two have different instructions and must not be confused.

**Triangulation:** triangulated across Fig 2 (anchor definition) and Fig 6 (instrument rule).

**Refuter:** a ground reading of a candidate Hadean setting that resolves precursor, product, isotope signature and local time order at the micrometre scale.

### F6 The habitat decides the valence of a pathway

Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*
Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*
Fig 11 :46: *"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"*
Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*

A pathway is a capability of pathway-and-habitat, never of pathway alone. The same cyanide chemistry behaves differently in a concentrated evaporite stream under UV, in an ice eutectic, in an alkaline vent pore, and in a dilute ocean. The habitat is read off the world, item by item; it is not assembled from a list of desirable conditions. Meteorite parent bodies are habitats too, with their own aqueous alteration history, and the nucleobase and sugar distributions differ between Murchison, Murray and Tagish Lake in line with that history.

**Triangulation:** triangulated across Fig 11 (capability and habitat) and Fig 2 (anchor is constitutive), low overlap between the habitat enumeration rule and the anchor rule.

**Refuter:** a single pathway with measured equal yield and equal selectivity across all enumerated habitats, so that its valence is invariant to the residual.

### F7 Same atoms, different arrangement: isomer and functional group decide identity

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

The four product families share a small atom set (chiefly C, H, O, N, plus P and S in the activated forms). They are not variants of one substance. Under Fig 12's definition, two molecules with the same formula and different bond are different substances, and a formula that records only ingredients cannot tell them apart. This is not an analogy: in the meteorite data, imidazole carboxylic acids are structural isomers of uracil and are one to two orders of magnitude more abundant, so a method that fails to separate them overestimates uracil. The four families are better read as members of different functional groups produced by a shared atom economy than as one large isomer set, because their formulas differ; the isomer claim holds inside a family and under the shared-atom reading at a coarser level.

**Triangulation:** single-source for the figure definition (Fig 12), with the world isomer data as corroboration from a different pool.

**Refuter:** show that product identity and behaviour are recovered from the molecular formula alone, so that the bond carries no information. The flat-formula collapse in Fig 12 forbids this.

### F8 One pot versus flow: the network needs ordered delivery

Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input."*
Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*
Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."*

The three classic one-pot syntheses (spark discharge, formose, Oró adenine) are each multistep and low yielding in biologically relevant products, and they use mutually incompatible conditions. A network that makes ribonucleotide, amino acid and lipid precursors from HCN and H2S needs periodic delivery of reagents and several ordered additions, which is why the published geochemical scenario uses flowing streams that diverge and merge rather than one pot. Composition inside a fixed closure can rearrange what is held; it cannot add an atom, and repeating the search does not help. What has to be added, if anything, comes in as an import with provenance.

**Triangulation:** triangulated across Fig 8 (idempotence), Fig 12 (cycle) and Fig 6 (mint limit).

**Refuter:** a one-pot experiment producing all four families at biological relevance with measured selectivity and no sequential reagent delivery.

### F9 Provenance is carried by the record, and the record is the term

Fig 13 :121: *"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."*
Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*
Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*

Two routes can produce the same glycine. From the glycine alone the route is not recoverable; it is recoverable from the lineage, which in chemistry means the conditions, the date, the isotope signature and the intermediate measurements. Sending a product list without its lineage sends a recording of one occasion, not a generator. What can cross upward is the method: how the selectivity was measured, how the source was discriminated, and what would have falsified the assignment.

**Triangulation:** triangulated across Fig 13 (lineage) and Fig 4 (membrane rule).

**Refuter:** show that a finished molecule carries, in itself, a readable record of the route that produced it. Chemistry says it does not.

### F10 The literal historical route is a `gap`, and unreachable is a verdict

Fig 9 :44: *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."*
Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*
Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*
Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

After the census, no figure line governs a choice among the candidate historical routes. The figures constrain the shape any admissible route must have and the instruments that would discriminate among candidates. They do not name the route. To claim otherwise would be to certify a frame's own completeness, which Fig 8 forbids. This is recorded as `gap`, not as a hidden answer and not as a silence. The missing thing is a source-discriminating instrument at the site, described in effect terms but not held.

**Triangulation:** triangulated across Fig 9 (shape not answer), Fig 13 (unreachable verdict) and Fig 8 (search is not proof of absence).

**Refuter:** the ground reading named in F5. Until one exists, this finding stands.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the standing instruction that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refused if any of the following holds:

1. One route produces all four families in one habitat with measured selectivity, measured concentration and measured co-location, and a ground reading confirms it operated there. This collapses the union into one answer.
2. A source-discriminating instrument shows local versus exogenous contribution for all four families with isotope and isomer readings that separate the sources. This settles what F4 leaves open.
3. A pathway's yield and selectivity are invariant across the enumerated habitats, so valence no longer depends on the residual. This breaks Fig 11's claim.
4. Product identity and behaviour are recovered from the formula alone, so the bond carries no information. This breaks Fig 12's central claim.
5. The field accepts a demonstration of chemical possibility as a verdict about the historical event, with no source discrimination. This is a mode violation: `specified` presented as `grounded`.

If any fires, the affected finding is withdrawn and the audit returns to the census. No term is deleted; the refusal is added and the reach falls, as Fig 6 describes.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the union and the per-family slots, never with a single recipe (F1).
2. State the direction explicitly: "can form" is not "did form here" (F2, F5).
3. Show the bond and the conditions, not just the feedstock list (F3, F8).
4. Require provenance for every imported molecule: isotope ratio, isomer separation, fall-site soil comparison (F4, F9).
5. Enumerate the habitat before ranking pathways (F6).
6. Record the literal historical route as `gap`, with the discriminator named in effect terms (F10).
7. Figure 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Send the method and the refuter, not a product list.

That is exactly the status of F10.

### Strains and re-census

- **S1, "Miller-Urey settles amino acid supply" versus the habitat claim.** First-pass reading treated the 1953 experiment as a ground verdict for early Earth. Re-census: the experiment is a ground reading about chemical possibility under a chosen atmosphere. Current geochemical models favour a weakly reducing atmosphere, and modified experiments show that buffers and iron change the yield. Different quantities, no contradiction. The first reading is retracted below.
- **S2, "shape, not a name" applied too broadly.** The phrase governs a missing primitive, not an already-held atom or a provenanced binding from another frame. Naming Strecker chemistry or formose chemistry is a binding onto held atoms, not the invention of a seventh primitive. The overstatement is corrected in `05_what_shape_means.md` section 5 and preserved there.
- **S3, isomer versus functional group among the four families.** They do not share one molecular formula, so calling all four isomers is a strain. Re-census assigns Fig 12 :117 to comparisons inside a family and under the coarser shared-atom reading, and Fig 12 :123 to the four-family relation. Named, not hidden.
- **S4, lab and meteorite as rival frames.** They are not rivals; they answer different questions. Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in contents, so no conflict is triggered. The record decides.
- **S5, import moves the wall versus import adds no atom to the world.** Re-census: the wall is a frame's closure; the world gains nothing. Both statements are kept, at different objects.
