# Supporting derivation: order, isomer and functional group in prebiotic production

**Mode: `specified` for the chemistry-facing claims, `general` for the bond rules, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## RESIDUAL

The main audit leaves three questions open that need a second derivation: (1) is "production" an ordered or a symmetric relation here, (2) are the four molecular families isomers, functional groups, or neither, and (3) does a one-pot network or a flow network fit the bond rules better?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held by the chemistry frame: HCN and its derivatives, formaldehyde and glycolaldehyde, ammonia, acetylene, phosphate, hydrogen sulfide, metal ions, energy sources. Bonds held: the seven typed relations plus negation, including `⇒` production and `→` sequence. No new primitive is available without an import carrying provenance.

## DERIVATION

### Q1. Is production ordered?

- Fig 12 :80: **"A produces B; B did not exist before"**. The Operators table adds the identifying test: is there a B before A fires? No.
- The symmetric bonds are `+`, `∥` and `≡`. A correlation between precursor and product is a `+` style co-presence claim: both hold at once. It does not carry the direction.
- Fig 2 :39: **"Correlation has valence."** A symmetric reading of a product list cannot choose the arrow.
- ⇒ A laboratory demonstration that glycine forms from a feedstock is a `specified` claim of the form `feedstock ⇒ glycine` under stated conditions. It is not a claim that the feedstock fired at the Hadean site. The direction of the historical question sits outside what a product measurement can check.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** a time-resolved field measurement showing the product absent before the precursor fired and present after, under independently measured site conditions. If that is returned, the direction claim is settled and this derivation ends.

### Q2. Are the four families isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- As usually written, the four families have different molecular formulas (glycine, ribose, adenine, a fatty acid). Strictly, they are **not** one isomer set: isomers require the same formula. Calling all four isomers is a strain.
- Re-census assigns the Fig 12 rule to two places: (a) **inside a family**, where genuine structural isomers occur, for example uracil and its imidazole carboxylic acid isomers in meteorites, or the pentose aldoses; (b) **under a coarser shared-atom reading**, where HCN and its derivatives supply the C and N of nucleotides, amino acids and lipids, and different arrangements over that shared atom economy give different product classes.
- The four-family relation is therefore primarily a **functional-group** relation: `state → structure → state` style organisation over a shared atom economy, with the group fixing the kind and the qualifier picking the member (amino acid versus sugar versus nucleobase versus amphiphile).
- **Falsifier:** if two families can be derived without any atom the other requires, the shared-atom reading fails and the families are simply separate chemistries with no structural relation worth naming.

### Q3. What order is the network?

- Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input."** The formose reaction is the clearest example: glycolaldehyde catalyses the production of more glycolaldehyde from formaldehyde, so a trace seed starts an autocatalytic cycle. The cycle closes only because the output re-enters.
- Fig 8 :32: **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."** Rearranging HCN derivatives in a closed mixture cannot generate an atom that the mixture lacks; phosphorus and sulfur have to be supplied from minerals, and the geochemical scenario does exactly that through schreibersite corrosion and metal sulfides.
- Fig 6 :21: **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."** The Sutherland network's need for periodic delivery of HCN and H2S, plus ordered introduction of phosphate, cyanamide and cyanoacetylene, is a sequence of compositions with imports at defined points, not a single spontaneous rearrangement.
- Therefore a **flow network with ordered delivery** fits the bond rules better than a single one-pot mixture. This is a `specified` structural conclusion from the figures, corroborated by the published chemistry but not ground-settled for any particular Hadean site.
- **Falsifier:** a one-pot experiment producing all four families at biological relevance, with measured selectivity and no sequential delivery, would retire this conclusion.

### Q4. What does the bond cost, and what does that buy?

- Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**
- Fig 12 :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**
- A proposed pathway is cheap to state (a short reaction scheme) and may take a whole analytical campaign to settle. Conversely, a long pathway over a well characterised precursor may be expensive to describe and cheap to refute with one isotope measurement. The two readings come apart in both directions, so neither can stand in for the other. A source ledger that records only the number of papers, or only the number of reactions proposed, is counting the wrong denominator.
- **Falsifier:** show that a single scalar can rank pathway candidates on both description cost and settlement cost without loss. The figures forbid the sum; a demonstration would break this derivation.

### Q5. Catalyst and spending

- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**
- Mineral surfaces (silicate, metal sulfide, borate) are the catalysts in the published scenarios. Whether a surface survives its own use or is spent by it changes the pathway's behaviour across cycles. A one-pot recipe that records "add mineral" without recording regeneration has recorded half of itself.
- **Falsifier:** show that a catalytic surface and a consumed reagent give indistinguishable time series across repeated cycles at the site. Then the distinction carries no information here.

## CONCLUSION

Production is ordered, so "can form" is not "did form". The four families are not a literal single isomer set; strictly they stand as functional groups over a shared atom economy, with genuine isomer claims inside each family. The bond rules favour a flow network with ordered delivery and imported atoms over a one-pot mixture, and the cost of a pathway has two non-additive currencies. Every conclusion is `specified` or `general`, none is `grounded`, and each carries its falsifier above.
