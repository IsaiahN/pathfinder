## VERDICT (from the figures)

The verdict is the union kept separate per domain, never averaged. D1: ingredients are not routes and production is ordered, so a possible synthesis is a `specified` candidate, not a history. D2: local synthesis and delivery are different operators separated by provenance, and pathway valence depends on the habitat. D3: isomer and functional-group tests, not formulas, decide molecular identity across a shared atom economy. D4: the anchor is a measurement; agreement from one evidence pool is one witness. The literal historical route is a `gap`, unreachable at this budget, with the discriminator named. Mode `specified` on the separators, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`. The strongest figure-grounded point is the union with per-slot indexing, because it explains why one-pot answers keep failing.

- **Cost if wrong:** a wrong route narrative is taught as settled history and effort is aimed at the wrong lever. The cost is bounded and locatable because every sub-claim has a refuter, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The avoidable extra cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending a product list before a discriminator. Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."
- **Undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if a refuter fires; the refusal is added and the reach falls because something was added rather than struck out. The lineage and the date are recorded so a later reader can locate the error.
- **Reversed by:** a source-discriminating isotope or isomer instrument for all four families; a time-ordered field reading at a candidate site; a habitat-invariance result for a pathway; a demonstration that product identity is readable from formula alone; a figure re-census that names the route; or a source with a genuinely different evidence pool showing that one route produces all four at a confirmed site. Agreement and prestige are not such instruments.
