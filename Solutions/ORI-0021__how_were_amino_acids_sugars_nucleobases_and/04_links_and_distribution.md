## Two things in the literature that bear directly on this question

1. **The four families are not one problem.** The Cleaves review and the Sutherland network paper arrive from different evidence pools at the same structural point: separate one-pot chemistries for amino acids, sugars and bases are individually productive but mutually awkward, and the interesting claim is a shared atom economy with ordered delivery. That is the union verdict in `01` F1 and F8, reached by the figures independently of the chemistry.
2. **Provenance is a measurement, not an argument.** Oba et al. separate uracil from its imidazole carboxylic acid isomers chromatographically and compare meteorite with fall-site soil; Furukawa et al. use carbon isotope ratios to establish an extraterrestrial origin for ribose. This is Fig 8 :51 in practice: convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents, and only the record separates them.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 10 :52**, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Lead with the three separators and the refuter. A recipient who has absorbed a whole framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27**, *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"* (the figure line continues onto the next SVG line). Ask what the recipient would expect to see if a separator were true before giving them the links.
- **Fig 2 :40**, *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Most of the chemistry literature reuses the same meteorite specimens and the same analytical methods. Pooled agreement is one witness.
- **Fig 4 :22**, *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* What is offered in stage 3 is a method, not a conclusion.
- **Fig 2 :42**, *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Here the cost is a wrong historical narrative about the origin of life, which is high, so independent readers are worth the trouble of finding through public venues.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the residual and the three separators, (2) the four refuters and the request "what would you count as a failure", (3) record the verdict, (4) only then the four source links, with the note that the figures, where cited, are the authority and not the summary.
