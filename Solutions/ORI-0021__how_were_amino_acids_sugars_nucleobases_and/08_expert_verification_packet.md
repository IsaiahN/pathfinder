# Expert verification packet: the Tether on ORI-0021

**What this is:** a claim in a form a prebiotic chemist or origin-of-life researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain separators, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the literal historical route. Not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question of how amino acids, sugars, nucleobases and membrane-forming amphiphiles were produced is a union, not one recipe. Four separators follow from the figure set: ingredients are not a route, because the bond carries information the ingredient list omits; production is ordered, so a demonstration of possibility is not a demonstration of a historical event; local synthesis and meteorite delivery are different operators whose products are identical in contents and are separated only by provenance; and a pathway's value depends on the habitat it runs in. The literal historical route is a `gap`, unreachable at this budget, with the discriminating instruments named in effect terms. No route is named by the framework and no measurement at a Hadean site is claimed.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 Chemistry-facing, checkable against a proposals table

- **Statement:** ingredient identity does not determine product distribution. Two recipes with the same feedstock but different order, pH, mineral surface or delivery history give different products.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :5, *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Also Fig 12 :46, *"A recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself."*
- **What to check:** take any two published one-pot schemes with similar feedstock and compare their product distributions. Is the difference explained by conditions and order, or only by the ingredient list?
- **Refuter:** show two recipes with identical ingredient sets giving identical product distributions and selectivity under all tested conditions. That would make the bond free.

### C2 Provenance-facing, checkable against meteorite methods

- **Statement:** local synthesis and exogenous delivery are separated by the record, not by the product. Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :51, *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*. Also Fig 6 :23, *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*
- **What to check:** in a meteorite nucleobase or sugar paper, are carbon isotope ratios measured per compound, are structural isomers chromatographically separated before quantification, and is fall-site soil compared? Oba et al. and Furukawa et al. do these things; weaker reports do not. Does a claimed base disappear when its isomers are resolved?
- **Refuter:** a source-discriminating measurement that fails to separate local from exogenous for all four families, so that provenance is unrecoverable in principle.

### C3 Habitat-facing, checkable against a pathway comparison

- **Statement:** a pathway's yield and selectivity are functions of the habitat, so there is no habitat-invariant ranking.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :40, *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Also Fig 11 :43, *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*
- **What to check:** compare the same cyanide or formose chemistry in concentrated evaporite brine, ice eutectic, alkaline vent pore and dilute ocean. Does the product ranking hold? Published flow-chemistry scenarios depend on concentration and sequential delivery, which is evidence against invariance.
- **Refuter:** measured equality of yield and selectivity across all enumerated habitats.

## 4. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that any named route is the one early Earth used. The framework returns a shape and refuters, not a historical name.
- Not that Miller-Urey, formose, Oró chemistry, the cyanosulfidic network or meteorite delivery settles the whole question. Each settles a slot.
- Not that laboratory possibility is historical actuality. Production is an ordered bond: Fig 12 :80, *"A produces B; B did not exist before"*.
- Not that the four families are a literal single isomer set. They are functionally grouped over a shared atom economy; strict isomer claims live inside each family.
- Not that all prebiotic papers share one evidence pool. The claim is narrower: where they reuse the same specimens and instruments, their agreement is one witness, not several.
- Not `grounded`. No Hadean measurement is asserted.

## 6. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it.

1. **Isotope discriminator.** Per-compound carbon and nitrogen isotope ratios compared with biogenic ranges and with the candidate local pathway's fractionation.
2. **Isomer discriminator.** Chromatographic separation of a base from structural isomers before quantification.
3. **Time-order discriminator.** A dated field reading with precursor absent before and product present after, under independently measured site conditions.
4. **Standing falsifiers:** one route producing all four with measured selectivity, concentration and co-location at a confirmed site; a source discriminator for all four; habitat-invariant pathway valence; product identity readable from the formula alone; possibility accepted as actuality without discrimination.

## 7. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 12 :5, Fig 12 :46, Fig 8 :51 and Fig 11 :40. Four short passages.
2. Check C1 against one proposals table you know. Does ingredient identity alone predict the products?
3. Check C2 against one meteorite paper you know. Were isotopes, isomers and soil controls all measured?
4. Name one instrument that would distinguish local synthesis from delivery at a site, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
