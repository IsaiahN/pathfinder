**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the four refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 are written into whatever artifact accompanies this. The offer collapses on contact if the stage-1 claim cannot survive checking.

**Subject:** One claim about machine justification that you can refute in five minutes

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the claim fails, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`general` on the structure, `specified` on the machine binding, not `grounded`):** a high confidence score is not what makes an output a justified belief. Confidence is a number the model produces about its own output, and a frame cannot use a quantity it also produces as its verdict. What changes the output's standing is a relation to something outside the frame: an anchor that does not move when the model changes its mind; settlement against that anchor; a vocabulary that can refuse the claim; and a lineage that travels with the claim as part of it, not as an appendix. Confidence stays useful as a budget for how much checking to do. It is not a witness for the verdict.
>
> **What would refute it (written before this message):**
> 1. An output counts as justified while its confidence comes only from a function that also emits the output, with no anchor that can return "false" against it.
> 2. An output counts as justified while the only evidence is the provenance of its own maker, with no contact with the ground.
> 3. An output counts as justified while the system has no way to negate or withdraw any held term.
> 4. Calibration alone, matching confidence to observed accuracy, is enough across a distribution shift with no new ground contact.
>
> If any holds, I retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I have an algorithm, that this is settled by experiment, that a machine has beliefs in the human sense, or that any particular uncertainty method (calibration, conformal prediction, Bayesian posteriors) is the answer. Those are bindings from a particular field onto the shape, and they are offered with provenance, not as derivations. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency, not a ground reading.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
>
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Canonical article, Arrival of the Fittest: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
