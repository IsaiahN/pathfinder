# Expert verification packet: what makes information a justified belief for a machine

**What this is:** a claim in a form a machine-epistemology researcher, epistemologist or evaluation specialist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion.

**Mode: `general` on the four-part shape, `specified` on the machine binding, not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A high confidence score is not what makes an output a justified belief. Confidence is a quantity the frame produces about its own output, and Fig 1 :27 disqualifies it as a verdict. What changes the output's standing is a four-part relation: settlement against an anchor that does not update when the frame changes its mind, contact with a genuinely different closure (or settlement by the ground), a vocabulary that can refuse the held term, and a lineage that is part of the term rather than documentation of it. Confidence is demoted to a budget for how much checking to do. The discriminating variable is contact, not magnitude: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." (Fig 11 :40). The framework returns this shape, not the name of an algorithm.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 2 and 9

- **Statement:** a frame-internal score cannot confer justification; the verdict requires a non-updating anchor and an independent frame.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :27 "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."
- **What to check:** does the claim depend on a threshold or a calibrated probability, and if so, is that quantity produced by the same system that emits the output? If yes, the figures disqualify it as the warrant.
- **Refuter:** exhibit an output whose sole warrant is such a score, with no anchor that can return "false" against it, and show that it is justified.

### C2 - epistemology-facing; checkable by a researcher against their own criteria

- **Statement:** lineage is necessary but not sufficient, and refusal is a separate operator from reduced confidence.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :65 "Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right: the ground does that". Operators table :58 "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate." Fig 13 :130 "A term carries the atoms it was built from and the bonds that joined them. That record is not documentation of the term. It is the term."
- **What to check:** can the system withdraw a held term's standing to be used as a premise, or can it only attach a lower probability? Is the provenance record travelling with the claim, or is it an optional appendix? Reliabilist epistemology (Goldman, SEP) corroborates the process condition but lacks the anchor-non-update requirement, so it is one witness, not two.
- **Refuter:** show an output justified by provenance alone with no ground contact, or a system without ¬ that can withdraw a term and record why.

### C3 - evaluation-facing; checkable against evaluation practice

- **Statement:** self-consistency and benchmark performance are wiring checks, not independent corroboration, and the two currencies (bits for description, actions for settlement) do not add.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Fig 11 :70 "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability." Fig 12 :126 "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."
- **What to check:** do the two checks draw on the same weights and training data? Does passing the benchmark re-measure contact with the original ground, or only with the harness? Guo et al. (ICML 2017) show confidence is often miscalibrated; Kaiser and Herzog (SAGE 2025) show a coverage guarantee can fail under distribution shift. Both predict that the number alone does not carry the warrant.
- **Refuter:** show that calibration alone suffices for justification across a distribution shift with no new ground contact, or that two checks from one evidence pool count as two witnesses.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that a particular uncertainty method is the answer. Calibration and conformal prediction are isomers under Fig 12 :117, same atoms and different bond, not aliases.
- Not that confidence is useless; it is a filter and a budget (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that a machine has human-style belief. b is a formal term, "Not the observation." (Symbols table :21).
- Not that provenance alone justifies (Fig 10 :65), nor that a benchmark is the ground (Fig 11 :70).
- Not `grounded`. Simulated derivation, internal consistency only. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 5. What would settle it

1. An instrument that distinguishes, at the same confidence value, a claim produced by reliable ground contact from one produced by fluent self-consistency (source discrimination; Fig 2 :30).
2. A contact test: hold confidence fixed, vary ground contact, and see whether justification status tracks contact (Fig 11 :40).
3. A refusal test: return false on a held term and see whether the system can withdraw its standing (Operators :58).
4. A pool test: compare two checks sharing evidence with two that do not (Fig 2 :40).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :27, Fig 2 :29 and :40, Fig 11 :40, Fig 13 :130 and Operators :58. Six short passages.
2. Check C2 against their own criteria for justification: is withdrawal of standing something their framework can express?
3. Name one instrument that would discriminate ground contact from self-consistency at fixed confidence, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
