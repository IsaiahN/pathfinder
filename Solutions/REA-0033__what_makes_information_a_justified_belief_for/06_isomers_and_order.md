## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are calibration and conformal prediction isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Both methods use a score and a threshold. That is the same ingredient set at a coarse reading.
- **Calibration** (Guo et al., 2017) attaches a transform to the model's own confidence so that reported probability tracks observed accuracy on a held-out set. The bond is to the model's internal score, adjusted by past observations. It prices the description. Under Fig 1 :27 it remains frame-internal: the quantity being corrected is one the frame produces.
- **Conformal prediction** (Kaiser and Herzog, 2025) attaches a nonconformity score to a measured coverage guarantee over held-out data, with an explicit finite-sample claim. The bond is to a distribution of observed outcomes, i.e. an anchor-like quantity outside the single prediction. It prices the action of checking, and the SAGE tutorial records that the guarantee fails under distribution shift.
- Same atoms (a score, a threshold, held-out data), **different bond**, and the behaviour differs decisively: a calibrated model can still make an unwarranted claim with a well-formed number; a conformal procedure makes a coverage claim that can be falsified by a shift. They are therefore **isomers** under Fig 12 :117, not variants of one method.
- This is a `specified` observation about the two fields, triangulated for the structural reading (Fig 12 :117 plus Fig 1 :27) and single-source for the specific field claims (one paper per method).
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if calibration and conformal prediction are shown to rest on the same bond to the same anchor, so that the difference is only notation, the isomer claim fails and they are two spellings of one term under a symmetric bond (Fig 12 :92-93).

### Q2 - The two currencies do not add

- Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."
- Fig 12 :127: "They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them" and "will not notice the shortfall." Fig 12 :130: "Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."
- Confidence lives entirely in the bits accounting. A justification claim that cites only confidence is counting the denominator, which the formula's step 8 warns about: "a system measuring progress by terms minted is counting the denominator."
- Settlement lives in the actions accounting. A system can have a very cheap description and an unaffordable test. Fig 12 :128-129: "A long chain of cheap parts is expensive to describe and may cost one action to refute. A short arrangement over a costly operand is cheap to state and may take a whole budget to settle."
- **Consequence:** "high confidence" is not a weaker form of "justified". It is a different accounting. No amount of the one yields the other. This is the structural reason F1 and F10 in `01` are decisive rather than rhetorical.

### Q3 - Refusal is a different operator from absence

- Operators table :73: "Subtraction is the nearest bond and is not it." Subtraction is "A with B removed or absent"; negation "is the refusal of a claim."
- Operators table :58: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."
- Fig 5 :54-55: "What it removes is not a term but a term's standing to be composed over. Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach."
- A machine can lose confidence in an output (a magnitude change, akin to subtraction of mass) without being able to refuse it (a change of standing). Only the second carries a correction. A vocabulary with ¬ can write "this is false"; a vocabulary without it can only write "less likely", so a wrong term stays composable and the reach does not fall.
- **Falsifier:** if a system with no negation operator can withdraw a held term, record why, and prevent further composition over it, then ¬ is not required for the four-part shape and F7 falls.

### Q4 - What order is the shape?

- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces".
- The order here:
  1. **Perception**: read R per slot (Fig 1 :19). A global average is not a reading.
  2. **Vocabulary and objective**: have terms that afford refusal, and a comparison that says what progress would be (Fig 3 :25-26; Fig 12 :87). This is the middle link with no instrument (Fig 3 :27).
  3. **Route**: reach the anchor, or find the frame whose closure differs (Fig 6 :30).
  4. **Settle, then compose**: Fig 12 :104, "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it." Fig 5 :70, settlement is necessary and not sufficient.
  5. **Carry the lineage**: Fig 13 :130, "That record is not documentation of the term. It is the term."
- **Refuse before mint** (formula step 2, Fig 5 :50-56): if the residual's content is that a held term is false, a refusal is owed first, not an explanation. A machine that responds to a failed ground check by manufacturing a more confident explanation has reversed the order.
- **Describe the residual before choosing the frame** (Fig 9 :27): otherwise any uncertainty method picked will seem to fit.
- **Narrowing gets you close, the ground decides** (Fig 9 :42). A reviewer, a benchmark, or an essay does not settle the claim; it only narrows what could settle it.

### Q5 - What is the union here?

- Fig 9 :54-57: a bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions. A single lever clears the region where one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.
- Here the conditions are: C1, the output is held with a tracked per-slot residual (D1); C2, it has been settled by an anchor that does not update (D2); C3, it can be refused (vocabulary); C4, it carries its lineage (record). High confidence clears only a magnitude within C1. It leaves C2, C3 and C4 standing, and the intersection is where justification lives.
- A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either (Fig 9 :57-58). So this is a union with its own loop, not one hard machine-epistemology question to be answered by a better score.

## CONCLUSION

The supporting derivation adds three checkable things. Calibration and conformal prediction are **isomers**, same atoms and different bond, which is why one cannot be passed off as the other (falsifier recorded). Bits and actions are **two incommensurable currencies**, which is why confidence cannot be promoted to a verdict by accumulation. And refusal is a **unary operator**, not a smaller confidence, which is why a system must be able to negate to carry a correction. The order is perception, vocabulary and objective, route, settlement, lineage, with refusal before mint and residual description before frame selection. No new primitive is required; no import that moves the wall is claimed.
