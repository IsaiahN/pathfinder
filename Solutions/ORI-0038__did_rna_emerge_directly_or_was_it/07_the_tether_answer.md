# The Tether's answer: did RNA emerge directly, or was it preceded by a simpler genetic polymer?

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain shape and boundary claims, `general` on the structural split, `gap` on the identity of the first polymer. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## 1. The residual it answers

The question offers two options, but both require the same four conditions: information storage, templated copying above an error threshold, prebiotic monomer availability, and an eventual RNA world. They differ in the bond between regimes. Fig 9 :4-5: *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* The residual is a **union compound**, not one question with opposite answers, and R is indexed per condition: Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*

## 2. The shape, per domain

**D1, framework-internal composition (`specified`, triangulated).** Any first genetic polymer must be a cross-kind composition settled against the prebiotic ground, and once settled it becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. Fig 13 :74: *"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."* Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"* This is a legality claim, not an existence claim. Verdict for this domain: the shape is forced; the molecule is not.

**D2, prebiotic chemistry (`specified` on the boundary, `gap` on the order, single-source for the chemistry facts).** Candidate backbones (PNA, TNA, GNA, HNA) share base recognition and differ in backbone, so over the shared ingredients they are isomers: Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* They are members of one functional group (Fig 12 :123), with the backbone as qualifier. Robertson and Joyce 2012 report that a PNA to RNA transition *"would preserve information"* and that it is fruitful to consider an RNA-later alternative; the ASU News piece reports TNA cross-pairing with RNA as a model for information transfer. These are secondary corroborations of the **shape of the transfer**, not a measurement of early-Earth order. Verdict for this domain: the transfer bond is well-formed; which bond actually fired is unreached.

**D3, information and evolution theory (`general` structural, `specified` on the threshold).** "Precedes" is an ordered relation (→), and "simpler" is a threshold comparison (⋛), not a symmetric join. Fig 12 :87: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* The error-threshold literature supplies the quantity (fidelity q against genome length n and superiority s), and any first polymer, direct RNA or precursor, must satisfy it. Verdict for this domain: the comparison must be cashed out as a measurement; no figure line supplies a number.

**D4, verification (`general`).** Agreement among reviews is one evidence pool, not a second witness. Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* Fig 7 :12-13: *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"* The anchor is a measurement. Verdict for this domain: the shape claim can be checked from outside only if the checker sees the residual and refuter first (Fig 10 :42).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that a pre-RNA polymer existed. The figures do not decide the historical order.
- Not that RNA emerged directly. For the same reason.
- Not that failure to synthesize RNA in the lab proves a precursor. Fig 8 :39 *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
- Not that the framework names TNA, PNA, GNA or HNA. Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- Not that a laboratory XNA or an evolved polymerase is a Precambrian fact. It is an import with a source gate and a shadow test still owed (Fig 8 :50-52).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement of monomer yield, copying fidelity or cross-pairing under a stated habitat was taken in this solution.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not more argument. From the inherited *Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

- **Synthesis test:** does a candidate monomer form under a stated prebiotic habitat at a yield adequate for polymerization?
- **Fidelity test:** does templated copying of that polymer exceed the error threshold at the required length?
- **Transfer test:** does the candidate cross-pair with RNA in a sequence-preserving way, and is the transfer licensed by a provenance record and a shadow test?
- **Exclusion test:** can a direct RNA route be shown to satisfy all four conditions in the same habitat, with no prior polymer and no transfer bond?
- **Standing falsifiers from `01` section 5:** clean partition of the four conditions; "simpler" as a symmetric property; base-set change rather than backbone change; habitat-independent sufficiency.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"* and Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."* The shape is delivered; the name is not. The framework stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Indiscernibility is not identity. A ground measurement could close the gap, and agreement cannot.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not choose between RNA-first and a pre-RNA world, because no figure line names a molecule. It says the two options are a union of overlapping conditions, not clean opposites, and that any first genetic polymer must have a specific shape: an information-bearing, cross-kind composition settled against the prebiotic ground, which can then become an atom for whatever composes over it. Same bases with a different backbone makes an isomer, so a precursor and RNA are different substances in one functional group, and the transfer between them is an ordered, sequence-preserving bond that must be provenanced. "Precedes" is ordered and "simpler" is a comparison of quantities. A failed laboratory route to RNA is a residual, not proof of a precursor. What is offered is the shape any candidate must fit, six failure conditions written in advance, and the measurements that would close the question. The name stays open until the ground settles it.
