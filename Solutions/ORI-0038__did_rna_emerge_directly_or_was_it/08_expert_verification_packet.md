# Expert verification packet: the Tether on a first genetic polymer

**What this is:** a claim in a form a prebiotic chemist or origins-of-life researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural split, `gap` on the identity of the first polymer. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "RNA directly or a simpler precursor first" is a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition: both options require RNA, an information-bearing polymer above an error threshold, and a prebiotic supply of monomers. They differ in the bond between regimes. The shape any first polymer must fit is a cross-kind composition settled against the prebiotic ground, which becomes an atom for whatever composes over it, with an ordered, sequence-preserving transfer to RNA. Same bases with a different backbone is isomerism, so a precursor and RNA are different members of one functional group. "Precedes" is ordered and "simpler" is a threshold comparison on a quantity. The framework does not name the molecule, and a failed synthesis is not proof of a precursor. What is offered is the shape, six failure conditions written before the run, and the measurements that would close the question.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 9, 12 and 13

- **Statement:** the two options are a union, not a partition, and no figure line names a first polymer. The shape is cross-kind, settled, composable over, with an ordered transfer bond.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55-57 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."* Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* Fig 13 :74 *"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."* Fig 12 :104 *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"*
- **What to check:** do the two options actually share the four conditions, and does no figure line select a molecule?
- **Refuter:** show a clean partition of the conditions into two disjoint sets matching the options, or produce a figure line naming a polymer.

### C2 - chemistry-facing; checkable against the candidate polymers

- **Statement:** a backbone change with base recognition retained is isomerism, and the candidates are members of one functional group, not stages of one substance. The transfer to RNA is a sequence-preserving bond that needs provenance and a shadow test.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :123 *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Fig 12 :5 *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Fig 4 :22 *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Fig 8 :50-52 *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."*
- **What to check:** do TNA, PNA, GNA and HNA retain base recognition while changing only the backbone? Is the observed cross-pairing with RNA a sequence-preserving method, or merely a structural similarity? Does the lab-evolved polymerase result carry a provenance record distinguishing an in vitro import from a plausible early-Earth route?
- **Refuter:** a candidate that changes its base set rather than only its backbone kills the isomer claim (the functional-group claim may stand); a demonstration that RNA can arise after precursor decay with no sequence transfer kills the bond claim.

### C3 - theory-facing; checkable against the threshold literature

- **Statement:** "precedes" is an ordered relation and "simpler" is a threshold comparison on a quantity. Any first polymer, direct or precursor, must satisfy the error threshold, which is a property of the polymer plus habitat, not of the polymer alone.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :87 *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* Fig 11 :36 *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."* Fig 11 :39 *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*
- **What to check:** does the error-threshold literature actually state the comparison as fidelity against genome length and superiority (Robertson and Joyce 2012 gives q to the power of n against s)? Is "simpler" being used as a comparison on measured quantities, not as a substance name?
- **Refuter:** "simpler" as a symmetric intrinsic property, or an experiment showing one polymer necessary and sufficient independent of habitat.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that a pre-RNA polymer existed, and not that RNA emerged directly. The figures do not decide history.
- Not that laboratory XNAs are Precambrian facts. They are imports needing a source gate and shadow test.
- Not that a failed laboratory RNA synthesis proves a precursor (Fig 8 :39).
- Not that the framework names the first polymer (Fig 9 :32).
- Not `grounded`. No prebiotic synthesis, fidelity or cross-pairing measurement was taken in this solution.
- Not that prestige, citation count or review consensus settles anything (Fig 2 :35, Fig 7 :12-13).

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Synthesis test:** a measured prebiotic route to a candidate monomer under a stated habitat.
2. **Fidelity test:** a measured copying fidelity above the error threshold at the required length.
3. **Transfer test:** demonstrated sequence-preserving cross-pairing with RNA, with a provenance record and a shadow test.
4. **Exclusion test:** a direct RNA route satisfying all four conditions in the same habitat with no transfer bond.
5. **Partition test:** evidence that the four conditions split into two disjoint sets matching the options.
