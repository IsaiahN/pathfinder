## 0. The residual, in effect terms, bound as an ordinary term

The question as written offers two options: RNA emerged directly, or a simpler genetic polymer came first. Stated as an effect: some polymer first crossed the threshold at which heritable information was stored, copied with sufficient fidelity to avoid error catastrophe, and supplied from a plausible prebiotic pool. What is being decided is the **order of crossing** and the **bond between the crossing and RNA**.

Option A (direct RNA) requires one polymer to satisfy every condition at once. Option B (precursor first) requires two settled molecules and an ordered, sequence-preserving transfer from the earlier one to RNA. These options are not contraries in the ordinary sense: both require RNA to appear, both require an information-bearing polymer above the error threshold, and both require a prebiotic supply of monomers. They differ in the bond between regimes, not in the set of conditions. That is the first thing the census has to test.

**The sought-for shape, written down and bound before the run (Formula "Direction"):** a first genetic polymer is a cross-kind composition, settled against the prebiotic ground, that can become an atom for whatever composes over it, with an ordered information-transfer bond to RNA. This is an ordinary term that can accrue a residual. It is not a name: no figure line names PNA, TNA, GNA, HNA, or RNA as the first polymer. The framework's own limit applies: Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* and Fig 13 :135 *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

**What would settle it:** a ground that does not update on what any frame thinks. Fig 2 :29-30: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* Here the anchor would be a measurement of prebiotic monomer synthesis, templated copying fidelity, or cross-pairing between candidate backbones and RNA. No such measurement was taken for this solution.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The either/or is a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition

Fig 9 :4-5: *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*
Fig 9 :55-57: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*

Both options require RNA to appear and an information-bearing polymer to cross the threshold. They differ only in whether an earlier polymer held the information first. So the question is a compound with its own loop, and the answer is per condition, not a single name.

**Refuter:** show that the four conditions partition cleanly into two disjoint sets, one satisfied only by direct RNA and one only by a precursor, so that one lever selects the option. Until that is measured, the union reading stands. Mode `general` (structural), low strain.

### F2 - The figures return a shape, not a name

No figure line mentions nucleotides, backbones, PNA, TNA, GNA, HNA or RNA. The framework cannot select a molecule. ### F3 - "Simpler" is a comparison bond, not a substance

Fig 12 :87: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*
Operators table :51: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"* (operators table, not a Fig N line).

The word "simpler" in the question does not name a molecule. It compares quantities: number of backbone atoms per repeat, number of monomer synthesis steps, synthesis yield, or required catalytic machinery. Robertson and Joyce report that TNA has a five-atom repeat against RNA's ribose and that Eschenmoser's group replaced threose by glycol to make GNA (world source, secondary). Those are comparisons on quantities, exactly ⋛. Treating "simpler" as a kind of substance is a flat-formula error: the bond is not recoverable from the operands.

**Refuter:** show that simplicity is a symmetric, intrinsic property of the candidate set (so `+` or `≡` would carry it) rather than a threshold on a measured quantity. Mode `specified`, single-source on the figure definition of ⋛, with the chemical quantities supplied by world sources only as corroboration.

### F4 - Same bases, different backbone is isomerism, so precursor and RNA are different substances

Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
Fig 12 :97: *"silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different properties, which is what forced structure into the notation."*
Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

TNA, GNA, PNA and HNA keep a base-recognition element while changing the backbone. Under Fig 12 that is different bond, different substance, not an earlier stage of the same substance. Over the shared atomic ingredients they are isomers. All information-bearing genetic polymers form a functional group; the backbone is the qualifier that says which member. So "pre-RNA polymer" is a well-formed kind, not a contradiction, and the transfer to RNA is a crossing between two substances rather than a continuous growth of one.

**Refuter:** show that a candidate polymer changes the base set, not only the backbone (breaking the shared-atom condition for isomerism), or that PNA/TNA/GNA fail Fig 12 :117's definition because the difference is not carried by a typed bond. Mode `specified`, triangulated across Fig 12 :5, :97 and :117 (low overlap: notation, chemical precedent, definition).

### F5 - The transfer to RNA must be a method, not a recording of one occasion

Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*
Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*
Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*
Fig 8 :50-52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."*

If a precursor came first, RNA did not appear from nothing. The sequence information had to cross. The world source from ASU News reports that TNA can bind RNA by antiparallel Watson-Crick pairing and calls this *"a model for how XNAs could have transferred information from the pre-RNA world to the RNA world"* (Chaput, secondary). That is a candidate for the transfer bond, not a proof of it. The lab-evolved polymerases that copy DNA into XNA and back are imports from a different closure: they need a source gate (provenance) and a shadow test (which gap they explain), and they are not evidence that the same crossing happened on early Earth.

**Refuter:** show that RNA can arise after the precursor decays with no sequence-preserving transfer at all, so the bond term is not owed. Mode `specified`, triangulated across Fig 4 and Fig 6, with the chemistry single-source and secondary.

### F6 - Failure to make RNA directly is not proof that a precursor existed

Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
Fig 8 :32: *"no search, however long, adds an atom"*
Fig 6 :27: *"Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."*
Fig 6 :34-35: *"Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."*

Robertson and Joyce call direct assembly *"The Molecular Biologist's Dream"* and the intractable laboratory mixtures *"The Prebiotic Chemist's Nightmare"*, while explicitly saying the problem is far from solved and that it is fruitful to consider the alternative. A difficulty in the lab is a residual, not a verdict for the other option. Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts with different instructions (Formula step 7), and no frame can certify its own limit.

**Refuter:** a measurement that actually closes the space of direct routes, that is, a sealed room, which the figures forbid as a claim from inside a frame (Fig 9 :44). Mode `specified`, triangulated across Fig 8 and Fig 6, low overlap.

### F7 - "First" is a label applied after a lineage met its ground

Fig 11 :36: *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."*
Fig 11 :39: *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*
Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*
Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*

The question's word "emerged" invites a single designated molecule. Re-grounded, it asks which polymer first satisfied contact in a habitat: monomers, mineral surfaces, wet-dry cycles, temperature, and the relations among them. Which polymer wins is residual-relative; the same backbone can be a benefactor in one habitat and inert in another. The habitat is read off the world, not composed from a preferred polymer list.

**Refuter:** an experiment showing one designated polymer was necessary and sufficient independent of habitat changes, which would refute the agent-and-habitat claim. Mode `specified` on the re-grounding, `general` on the structural move. Triangulated across Fig 11 :36 and :39 (different quantities: post hoc labelling versus contact property).

## 4. Formula step 7: three questions, not one

No `φ` in closure(Γ) closes R here. The three questions are kept distinct.

- **SEARCHED (asked first, cheapest).** Is the answer in the chemistry closure and merely unfound? Yes in part: PNA, TNA, GNA, ANA, HNA and their cross-pairing are already in the published chemistry frame, so more search there returns more candidates but no ordering, because the closure does not change (Fig 8 :32). This is a budget question, not a verdict.
- **INWARD (extend the instrument from one already returning something).** The instrument already returning something is the measured error threshold and the observed cross-pairing of TNA with RNA. Sharpening it means measuring monomer yields and copying fidelities under a stated habitat, not refining the word "simple" forever (Fig 6 :29).
- **OUTWARD (import).** Characterise R in effect terms, then find the frame whose closure predicts its shape. The candidate frames are prebiotic chemistry, XNA synthetic biology, and population-genetics threshold theory. Each import is debited against independence and needs a source gate and shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52).

INWARD and OUTWARD are opposed and are never read against each other. Underivable-in-principle (no instrument could register a historical polymer identity) and unreachable-at-this-budget (we did not run the synthesis measurements) are different facts with different instructions.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required by seat reasoning: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device. The shape claim in section 0 is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A figure line names a specific first genetic polymer, breaking the shape-not-a-name limit (F2).
2. A ground measurement shows a direct RNA route satisfying all four conditions in one step under the agreed prebiotic habitat, with no prior polymer and no transfer bond (breaks the union ordering, F1 and F5).
3. A ground measurement identifies a precursor polymer that satisfies all four conditions and transferred sequence information to RNA, with provenance and a shadow test (closes the F2 gap in favour of option B).
4. The four conditions partition cleanly into two disjoint sets matching the two options, so one lever selects the answer (breaks the union, F1).
5. "Simpler" reduces to a symmetric intrinsic property rather than a threshold on a quantity (breaks the ⋛ reading, F3).
6. A candidate polymer is shown to differ from RNA in base set rather than only in backbone, so the isomer claim fails and only the broader functional-group claim survives (narrows F4).
7. An experiment shows one polymer was necessary and sufficient independent of habitat, breaking the agent-and-habitat claim (F7).

If any fires, the claim is withdrawn or narrowed in this file and in `07`, `08` and `09`, with the earlier reading preserved. Until one is run, nothing here is labelled `grounded`.
