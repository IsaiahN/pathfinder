# Solution ORI-0038

**Question:** Did RNA emerge directly, or was it preceded by a simpler genetic polymer?

**Source:** origins of life.txt line 51

**Section:** 4. RNA-first and genetic information

**Mode:** `specified` on the per-domain shape and boundary claims, `general` on the structural split, `gap` on naming the first polymer. Nothing here is `grounded`.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

**Standing of this derivation:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. The figures cannot settle a historical molecular question. They return the shape a first genetic polymer must have and the refuter that would break that shape, then stop at the limit of the instruments. See `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6 and `09_watson_answer.md`.

**Residual in effect terms:** what is being decided is the order in which an information-bearing polymer first met a ground that pays (heritable storage, templated copying above an error threshold, and prebiotic availability), and what bond carried information from that polymer to RNA. Option A (RNA directly) leads to a single polymer that must satisfy all conditions at once. Option B (a simpler precursor) leads to two settled molecules and an ordered information-transfer bond between them. The two options share their conditions, so the question is a union with an ordering, not a clean partition.

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term:** a first genetic polymer is a cross-kind composition that has been settled against the prebiotic ground and can serve as an atom for whatever composes over it, with an ordered, sequence-preserving transfer to RNA as the bond between the two regimes. This shape is bound in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 0 and is allowed to accrue a residual like any other term (Formula "Direction").
