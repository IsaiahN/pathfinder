# Outreach draft: check the shape before you decide about the polymer

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 5 has been read and the shape claim has survived it. The offer collapses on contact if the stage-1 output does not hold.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about a first genetic polymer, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question, in effect terms:** did RNA emerge directly, or was it preceded by a simpler genetic polymer? The effect being decided is which polymer first crossed the threshold at which heritable information was stored, copied accurately enough to avoid error catastrophe, and supplied from a plausible prebiotic pool.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the shape, `general` on the split, `gap` on the name):** the two options are not clean opposites. Both need RNA to appear, an information-bearing polymer above an error threshold, and a supply of monomers. They differ in the bond between regimes. The shape any first polymer must have is: a cross-kind composition, settled against the prebiotic ground, that can become an atom for whatever composes over it, with an ordered, sequence-preserving transfer to RNA. "Precedes" is an ordered relation and "simpler" is a comparison of quantities, not a kind of molecule. Same bases with a different backbone makes a different substance, so a precursor and RNA are isomers over shared ingredients, not one substance at two stages.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the framework names the first polymer, that failure to make RNA directly proves a precursor, or that a laboratory XNA is evidence of an early-Earth XNA. The framework returns a kind of thing, not a name. A difficulty in the lab is a residual, not a verdict.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim.** I wrote these down before writing this note:
> 1. the four conditions partition cleanly into two disjoint sets, so one lever actually separates direct RNA from a precursor;
> 2. a measurement shows a direct RNA route satisfying all four conditions at once, with no prior polymer and no transfer bond;
> 3. a measurement identifies a precursor that satisfies all four conditions and transferred sequence information to RNA, with provenance;
> 4. "simpler" turns out to be a symmetric intrinsic property rather than a threshold on a measured quantity;
> 5. a candidate polymer differs from RNA in base set rather than only in backbone, so the isomer claim fails;
> 6. one polymer is shown necessary and sufficient independent of habitat.
>
> If any holds, I will narrow or withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would count as a failure in your answer:** an argument that a review consensus settles the order of events, or a plausibility ranking treated as evidence. The ground here is a measurement, and agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Local figure text with line numbers: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Eight-step loop and mode rule: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures path above. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
