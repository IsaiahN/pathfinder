# Isomers and order: same bases, different backbone, and what "first" can mean

**Mode: `specified` on the supporting claims, `general` on the ordering move, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The question says "a simpler genetic polymer." Two things are owed: (1) what relation holds between a candidate precursor and RNA if the base-recognition chemistry is shared but the backbone differs, and (2) what the word "first" is a claim about, given that the two options share their conditions. A third, smaller question is what dependency order the evidence must satisfy.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are a pre-RNA polymer and RNA isomers?

- Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
- Fig 12 :97: *"silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different properties, which is what forced structure into the notation."*
- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

The world sources report that TNA replaces the five-carbon ribose of RNA with a four-carbon threose and links the 2' and 3' positions instead of 5' and 3', while still base pairing with RNA (Wikipedia TNA; Robertson and Joyce 2012). PNA replaces the sugar-phosphate backbone with amide bonds (Robertson and Joyce 2012). In each case the base-recognition element is retained and the backbone bond changes.

Under Fig 12 that is exactly an isomer claim: same ingredients at the level of bases and backbone chemistry, different typed bond, different substance. The difference is decisive in behaviour (duplex stability, nuclease resistance, copying chemistry) and invisible in a flat ingredient list.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a candidate polymer changes its base set rather than only its backbone, the shared-atom condition fails and it is not Fig 12 :117's isomer of RNA. Or if the backbone difference does not change behaviour, the bond is free and carries nothing, so the claim is a flat-formula artefact.

**Verdict:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 12 :5, :97 and :117 (three different moves: notation, chemical precedent, definition). The chemistry itself is single-source and secondary.

### Q2 - What kind of thing is "a genetic polymer"?

- Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."*

All information-bearing genetic polymers share a functional group: base recognition plus a templated backbone that permits copying. The group says "genetic polymer"; the qualifier (ribose, threose, glycol, peptide, anhydrohexitol) says which member. This is why the question is well-formed rather than a category error: there is a real kind here, and RNA is one member of it. But a vocabulary holding only the group cannot name the route from one member to another. The route is the transfer bond of F5 in `01`, and it must be written explicitly.

**Falsifier:** if no shared substructure fixes the copying outcome across the candidates, the functional-group claim fails and each polymer is its own kind with no common name.

### Q3 - "Simpler": comparison, not substance

- Operators table :36-40 defines ⇒ production and ⋛ comparison: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"* (Operators table :51).
- Fig 12 :87: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*

"Did a simpler polymer come first" asks for a threshold on a quantity, not a new substance. Candidate quantities are already measurable: repeat length of the backbone (TNA's five-atom repeat versus ribose), number of monomer synthesis steps, synthesis yield, and copying fidelity. The fact that TNA is described as *"simpler than RNA and can be synthesized from a single starting material"* (Wikipedia TNA) is a comparison on quantities. It is not a claim that TNA is nearer to a framework primitive.

**Falsifier:** if simplicity turns out to be a symmetric intrinsic property (so `+` or `≡` carries it) rather than a threshold on a measured quantity, the ordered reading of the question is wrong.

### Q4 - What is ordered by "first"?

- Fig 12 :103: *"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."*
- Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"*
- Fig 11 :36: *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."*
- Fig 11 :39: *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*

The ordered bond is →: the earlier polymer must be settled before RNA can compose over it, because an unsettled term is not an atom for the next level (Fig 12 :104). But "first" is a label applied after a lineage met its ground (Fig 11 :36), and the capability belongs to polymer plus habitat, not to the polymer alone (Fig 11 :39). So the order is not a property of a molecule in isolation. It is a property of a settling event in an enumerated habitat (Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*).

**Falsifier:** an experiment showing one polymer necessary and sufficient across habitat changes would refute the agent-and-habitat claim and restore a molecule-alone reading of "first."

**Verdict:** `specified` on the recursion, `general` on the re-grounding. Triangulated across Fig 12 and Fig 11 (different quantities: level-relativity versus post hoc labelling).

### Q5 - What is the dependency order of the evidence?

- Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*
- Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*
- Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

Order: describe the four-condition residual (per slot, Fig 1 :19) → state the objective that would count as crossing the threshold (the error threshold, which Robertson and Joyce give explicitly) → measure monomer availability under the habitat → order the polymers → test the transfer bond → only then ask for a name. A claim about the transfer bond cannot be diagnosed until the monomer link has an instrument. Effort spent on more XNA analogues cannot rescue a missing prebiotic synthesis measurement.

**Falsifier:** a measured synthesis route and fidelity value for every candidate would move the monomer link from "no instrument" to "instrument present" and reopen the ordering question at a higher resolution.

## CONCLUSION

A precursor polymer and RNA are **isomers** over a shared ingredient set when only the backbone bond changes, and both are members of one **functional group** of genetic polymers whose qualifier is the backbone (Q1, Q2). "Simpler" is a **⋛ comparison** on quantities such as repeat length, synthesis steps and fidelity, not a kind of substance (Q3). "First" is an **ordered settling event in a habitat**, licensed by Fig 12 :104's settled-molecule rule and re-grounded by Fig 11 :36 and :39, not an intrinsic rank of a molecule (Q4). The evidence must be read in dependency order, with the monomer link's instrument before any ordering claim (Q5).

None of this names a molecule. The isomer claim, the functional group and the ordering are shapes a candidate must fit. The name is still a `gap`, reported as unreached rather than silence (Fig 13 :134-135).
