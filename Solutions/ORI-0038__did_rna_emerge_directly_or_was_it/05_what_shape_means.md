## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical rule: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 13 :134-135: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"* and *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From the inherited *Library of Babel* mapping: *"You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

For this question, a shape is the narrowed form of a first genetic polymer that can be stated before anyone can name which molecule it was. It constrains:

- it bears information (a state that can be ordered);
- it is a cross-kind composition, not a bare list of monomers;
- it is settled against the prebiotic ground, so it pays for its place;
- once settled, it becomes an atom for whatever composes over it, so RNA can build on it;
- the transfer to RNA is ordered and sequence-preserving;
- "simpler" is a comparison on a quantity, not a name.

A named candidate (TNA, PNA, GNA, HNA) is a binding from the chemistry frame, not the shape itself.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* A measurement of prebiotic availability and fidelity would name the first polymer. None was taken here.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs**, with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52). A candidate polymer imported from the XNA literature must say where it came from and which gap it explains.

Inherited *They're Scanning Books*: *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space."* And: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."* The six framework terms are a visible guess at compression axes, not a discovery of nature's own list. Turning one chemical backbone into "the" answer would hide a choice.

## 4. Why the source question matters here

Inherited *The Hard Problem is About Source*: metamerism. An instrument reads green but *"cannot comment on the source"*; one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK can give the same reading. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* The spectrometer, not an argument, resolved it.

Applied to this question: observing that a modern RNA world exists does not identify its source. Direct emergence and a transfer from a precursor could yield the same endpoint, so the endpoint is a lossy projection. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument: a measurement of monomer synthesis, copying fidelity, or cross-pairing under a stated habitat. This is why F2 and F6 in `01` refuse to name a first polymer from the fact that RNA is central today.

Inherited *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."* This file cannot validate its own shape claim.

## 5. Correction to my own reading of sections 1 and 3 (kept visible)

I initially wrote the limit as a flat ban: "the framework returns a shape, not a name, so naming a polymer is forbidden." That overstates it. The canonical phrase is *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* The subject is a missing primitive, one not held in Γ. PNA, TNA, GNA and the rest are not missing framework primitives. They are bindings from the chemistry frame onto arrangements of atoms that already exist. Naming them as candidates is therefore allowed as provenance, provided the binding is recorded as a binding and not presented as a framework derivation or as an import that adds a primitive.

What remains wrong in a naive reading is the metamerism point of section 4: picking a backbone from the endpoint is a source named from a projection, and no instrument here discriminates among sources. The reason is the missing discriminator, not a ban on naming held arrangements. Both claims are kept so neither is quietly erased.

## 7. One-paragraph reading

For this question, "shape" means the form a first genetic polymer must have before we can name it: a settled, information-bearing, cross-kind composition that can become an atom for RNA, with an ordered and sequence-preserving transfer between them, and a comparison on a quantity where the question says "simpler". The framework does not return the molecule, and unreachable is a verdict rather than silence. The name is licensed only by a ground measurement or a provenanced import with a shadow test, and neither has been made in this solution.
