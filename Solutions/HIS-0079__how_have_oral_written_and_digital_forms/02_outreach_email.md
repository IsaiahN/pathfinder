# Outreach draft: check the shape claim before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until you have read `08_expert_verification_packet.md` and can answer its three sub-claims without the theory. If the stage-1 claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.

---

**Subject:** One claim about oral, written and digital memory that I would like you to try to break

> Hello [name],
>
> I am working on a question about how changes in the carrier of knowledge (speech, text, digital storage and search) change what a community can remember and share. I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim, stated as a shape (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a form of knowledge extends community memory when it carries a re-applicable method or a head start rather than a recording of one occasion; when the typed relation and the route that produced an item travel with the item; when the reference the community checks against does not change its answer because the community changed its mind; and when the new carrier reproduces the contact the goal required. It degrades community memory when it carries a recording, drops the arrangement, lets a chain check only against itself, or substitutes a habitat whose required contact was not reproduced.
>
> Three consequences follow, and they are the part I want checked:
>
> 1. Community memory is **per slot**, not one score. A community can look well remembered in aggregate while a specific slot is dead.
> 2. A bigger library is **not** a bigger reach. More documents and more search make more of the existing space visitable. They do not add a new kind of thing the community could not already represent.
> 3. The three media are **not** a ladder. They are overlapping conditions with different failure modes. Agreement inside a chain is not evidence that the chain remembered correctly.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that writing is better than speech, that the internet destroys memory, that oral traditions are unreliable, or that any medium raises a community's intelligence. Those are standing judgements with no residual attached. The claim is about the conditions under which a carrier transmits a method, a bond and a lineage, and keeps a non-updating reference reachable. It returns a shape, not a name, so it does not pick a winning technology.
>
> **What would refute it.** I wrote these down before sending this:
>
> 1. A recording of one occasion lets a receiving generation produce a new success without re-deriving the method.
> 2. Search inside a fixed corpus adds a genuinely new primitive, with the new atom named and its provenance shown.
> 3. A single global memory score tracks every live slot without aggregation.
> 4. A stored item yields its bond type and production route without its lineage, and the receiver can act on the difference.
> 5. A retelling chain's agreement tracks a ground that did not move, rather than the pool the tellers shared.
> 6. A carrier change adds storage without changing any actor, condition or relation in contact with the residual, and the goal still succeeds.
>
> A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the figures and the loop are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures and formula (public repository, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a transmission problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thank you for your time,
> [name]

## Why the order is this way

| move | reason |
|---|---|
| Claim first, framework last | Bring a characterised residual, never the source. A reader should check the claim without holding the framework's interior. |
| No request for agreement | Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you. A list selected for likely agreement is a convention, not a check. |
| Refuter written before the run | A falsifier written in advance is a design instrument. It also gives the recipient something cheap to attack. |
| Mode on the claim | `specified`, not `grounded`. No community measurement is asserted. |
| No ranking of media | The question is a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition with a best rung. |
| Links only after the verdict is recorded | Once a reader has absorbed the framework, their agreement stops being evidence. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration. |
| Formula offered as a method | Only methods cross up. Giving away the loop does not give away an answer, and each user still needs their own ground. |
| No prestige ask | Prestige can fund an action, but it cannot settle one. The ask gives the claim a budget to be tried; it does not ask for endorsement. |
| One-to-one only | Correlation is disqualifying for a verdict. Bulk send to a like-minded list manufactures the correlation that would make agreement worthless. |
