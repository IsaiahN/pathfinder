## 0. The residual in effect terms

The question asks how three forms of knowledge (oral, written, digital) have altered what a community can **remember** (retain and re-find across time and generations) and **share** (transmit to someone who was not present).

Stated as an effect, not as a list of media:

> A community holds a set of slots (things it tracks and can re-find). A change of carrier alters (i) what crosses from one holder or generation to the next, (ii) whether the arrangement and the route that produced an item travel with it, (iii) whether the receiving frame can range the carried item against a reference that does not move when the frame changes its mind, and (iv) which actors and conditions are in contact with the residual. The residual is the per-slot gap between what the community can re-find and what its current carrier lets it re-find.

This is a **union compound**, not one question with three answers. Each medium changes a different condition of the same loop, and the conditions overlap. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :55-56: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*

**Direction bound as an ordinary term (Formula, "Direction").** The thing being sought is not "the best medium." It is a **shape**: a set of conditions any adequate account of remembering-and-sharing must satisfy. Writing that down binds the preference as a term so it can accrue a residual and be refused by the same test as everything else. The shape claim is:

> A form of knowledge extends community memory when it carries a re-applicable method or head start, keeps the bond and the lineage with the atoms, keeps a non-updating reference reachable, and reproduces the contact the goal required. It degrades community memory when it carries a recording of one occasion, drops the arrangement, lets a chain range only against itself, or substitutes a habitat whose required contact was not reproduced.

This is a `specified` claim. It is not a ranking of speech, text and search engines.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** at least four terms in contact: (a) a knowing community, a frame with a library; (b) a carrier or channel, the medium; (c) the content, atoms plus bonds plus lineage; (d) a ground, whatever settles whether what was remembered is true. A fifth term appears in every real case: the habitat of transmission (teachers, schools, archives, platforms), which is where the actors come from.
- **Symmetry:** the three media are **not** interchangeable and are **not** a rank. Writing follows speech and digital follows print in time (an ordered sequence), but the framework's rule is that a crossing is a change of role, not a step upward. Fig 13 :4-5: *"the two kinds are in lockstep, a crossing is a change of role rather than a step upward, and no member of the pair is senior to the other."* The difference between the media is actors, currency and anchor, not amount of knowledge. Fig 4 :19-20: *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."*
- **Scale:** individual recall, community memory, intergenerational transmission. Same loop at each scale, with a membrane between them (Fig 4). The residual must stay indexed per slot. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A claim such as "the internet has weakened memory" aggregates away the only signal that says where to look.

### One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 The medium as membrane and channel** | speaker, scribe, printer, indexer, platform, archive, search engine | what crosses the boundary: method, head start, recording, atom, bond; bits priced separately from actions (Fig 12 :125) | the ground does not decay, the channel does (Fig 10 :24) | partition of what may cross |
| **D2 The community as frame with a library and closure** | members, institutions, curricula, canonical lists | record versus reach; compositions inside closure(Γ) | the ground for settlement; the wall moves only on import (Fig 6 :23) | closure / reachability |
| **D3 The handoff chain and per-slot memory** | each reteller, each teacher, each learner | fidelity of the bracket across handoffs; agreement versus evidence | the ground, against which each frame is corrected separately (Fig 2 :45) | chain, with a possible break at the middle link |
| **D4 The bond, the arrangement and the lineage** | the knower who holds the arrangement; the record that carries atoms and bonds | typed bonds and provenance; description cost versus action cost | a discriminating instrument, not an argument (essay *The Hard Problem is About Source*) | structure / isomer / lineage |
| **D5 The habitat of transmission** | teacher, apprentice, guild, school, archive, algorithm, platform | contact; introduction versus subtraction; valence relative to the residual | capability is a property of agent-and-habitat (Fig 11 :39) | union; isolation is substitution |

### Partition or union

Union. The five domains are conditions of one loop, not separate media arranged in a row. D1 and D4 overlap where the carrier records the bond; D2 and D3 overlap where a settled item becomes an atom for the next generation; D5 overlaps all of them because the habitat decides which actors are in contact with the residual. Because it is a union, one lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55-56), so a single verdict such as "writing improves memory" cannot be returned.

### Dependency order (Fig 3)

1. **Perception:** what is in front of the community, and how it differs from what was there before.
2. **Vocabulary:** does the community have terms for what the medium affords, what can be stored, re-found, copied, corrected, refused.
3. **Objective:** can it say what would count as remembering well, in its own terms, against a reference that does not move.
4. **Planning:** a route to that objective under incomplete information.
5. **Learn and carry:** name what worked, verify it, carry it to the next problem.

The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* The likely break is between 2 and 3: a community can name storage capacity and recall techniques but has no instrument for what would count as progress, so effort lands on building bigger libraries. Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

Within the loop: refuse before mint (Formula step 2); describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).

## 3. Findings

### F1: The carrier is a membrane, and what crosses decides what can be shared (decisive for D1)

Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** Fig 4 :23: **"upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."** Fig 4 :25: **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."**

These are different lines and are quoted separately. Together they say the unit of transmission is not "information" but a typed thing: a method, a head start, or a recording. An oral performance tends to carry a re-applicable method (how to do, how to remember) in the performance itself. A text can carry a method in a form that can be re-applied by a reader who was not there. A search result, by itself, is closest to a head start or a recording: it makes the next lookup cheaper but is not the derivation.

Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does."** Fig 10 :25-26: **"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings."** Fig 10 :27-28: **"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch."**

So "a community forgot" is a claim about a channel, not about the ground. The ground did not go anywhere. The medium's failure modes are named: stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings. Digital media are especially exposed to the last two, because a searchable pile of readings can saturate a metric and pool many sources into one apparent answer.

**Refuter.** Show a case where the carrier transmits a recording of one occasion and the receiving generation can still produce a new success from it without re-deriving the method. That breaks the method/recording distinction at the membrane.

**Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 4 and Fig 10 are low overlap: membrane rule versus channel decay).

### F2: A larger library is not a larger reach (D2)

Fig 5 :72: **"a library that grows is not a reach that grows."** Fig 6 :32: **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."** Fig 13 :115: **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."** Fig 13 :117-118: **"So a frame can search without end and still not arrive: infinite effort inside a finite reach."**

This refuses the common claim that digital storage has simply enlarged what communities can know. It enlarges the record. Reach is derived, and is a different quantity. What a search engine does is described exactly by Fig 6 :49: *"more search at the same reach: is it in here and unfound? The closure does not change. Same atoms, same wall, more of the space actually visited."* That is a budget gain, not an atom gain.

Fig 13 :116-118 makes the same point from the arrangement side: *"Six members, and no amount of arranging produces a seventh."* Applied here, more documents, more links and more indices are more arrangements over the kinds already held. They do not add a new carrier kind. A genuinely new affordance would be an import from a frame whose closure differs (Fig 6 :23), with a source gate and a shadow test.

**Refuter.** Demonstrate that searching a corpus added a primitive that the community could not previously represent at all, with the new atom named and its provenance shown. Until that is done, corpus growth is record growth.

**Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 5, Fig 6, Fig 13 agree across distinct arguments: description length, closure idempotence, set/arrangement gap).

### F3: Community memory stays indexed per slot (D3)

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** Fig 1 :13: *"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"* (see Fig 1 :13-14 in the extract).

A community can look well remembered in aggregate (high literacy, large archive, high search coverage) while a specific slot is dead: a craft, a local ecological observation, a genealogy, a warning. The per-slot rule forbids answering the question with an average across media. The honest form of "digital media changed memory" is a vector of slot verdicts with the live slots named.

Symbols table :38-40: *"what cannot be perceived yet is residual and simply cannot be read."* A slot the current medium cannot represent is not zero; it is unreadable, and it is still residual.

**Refuter.** Exhibit a community-wide memory metric that tracks a live slot loss without aggregating it away, or show that the slot structure disappears at community scale so indexing is inapplicable. Neither has been shown here.

**Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 1 and the Symbols table, different levels of the same distinction).

### F4: The bond and the lineage are the part that is usually dropped (D4)

Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."** Fig 12 :47: **"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."** Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**

When a community stores only the atomic claims (the facts, the list of names, the extracted data), it records the ingredients and not the relation. A reader cannot tell whether the original was a sequence, a production, an identity, a subtraction or a comparison. That loss is invisible in the stored item: nothing signals it (Fig 12 :15-16: *"nothing downstream can tell them apart, and nothing signals that anything was lost"*).

Fig 13 :129: **"Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available."** Fig 13 :130: **"A term carries the atoms it was built from and the bonds that joined them. That record is not documentation of the term. It is the term."**

So the lineage is not a footnote attached to knowledge. It is part of what knowledge is. An oral tradition can carry the route in the performance and in the authority of the performer, but not durably. Writing can carry the route durably in the text and its apparatus. Digital storage can carry it extremely well when the record is preserved, and can destroy it when a database keeps the fields and drops the schema, the derivation or the citation trail.

Fig 12 :107: **"Settling compresses the description and not what it cost to establish."** Fig 12 :108-109: **"the result carries forward and the deriving does not, so every lineage pays again."** Where the arrangement is recorded, re-deriving is a lookup and the actions were spent once. Where it is not, every generation pays again. This is the concrete cost of dropping the bond.

Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**

**Refuter.** Produce a stored item from which the bond type and the production route can be recovered without the lineage, and show that the receiver can act on the difference. If that succeeds, the bond is not the missing half here.

**Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 12 and Fig 13, structure rule versus lineage rule).

### F5: A chain that ranges only against itself drifts, and its agreement is not evidence (D3)

Fig 7 :13: **"agreement compounds, evidence does not."** Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."** Fig 7 :36: **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."**

Fig 2 :34: **"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."** Fig 2 :45: **"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."**

This is the mechanism behind the classic observation that a story retold person to person loses what does not fit and becomes more familiar (Bartlett, as quoted in the essay *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*). It is not that anyone lies. It is that each retelling is a reconstruction against the listener's own history, and the errors compound in one direction when there is no external reference. Fig 2 :33: **"The anchor must not update."** If a community's check on its own memory is another member of the same community, the anchor has been listed as one of the parties, which Fig 2 :47 names as panel 1 arrived at by a different route.

Writing changes this when the text functions as a reference that does not update on what the reader thinks. Digital media change it again, and in both directions: a versioned, citable record is a better anchor than memory; a feed whose content is adjusted to what the reader already agrees with is a mirror.

Fig 2 :40: **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."**

**Refuter.** Show a retelling chain whose agreement tracked the ground rather than the pool, with the ground named and shown not to have moved when the chain changed its mind. That would break the drift claim for that case.

**Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 7 and Fig 2, room chain versus ground panels).

### F6: The medium substitutes a habitat; it does not just add a store (D5)

Fig 11 :39: **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."** Fig 11 :40: **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."** Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**

A new carrier does not simply give the same community more memory. It changes which actors are in contact with the residual (teachers, librarians, algorithms, platforms), what conditions hold, and what relations carry influence. That habitat must be enumerated, not composed from a list of media properties.

Fig 11 :49-51: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* and *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* So "writing is good for memory" and "search engines are bad for memory" are both standing lists with no residual. Given a residual of "the community must re-find a warning three generations later," a griot and an archive can both be benefactors; given a residual of "the community must notice that an inherited rule has stopped working," the same archive can be a malfactor because it preserves the rule and not the correction.

Fig 11 :61: **"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."** A new medium introduces actors and cannot subtract the habitat. Fig 11 :66-67: **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."** Fig 11 :68: **"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails."** Fig 11 :69: **"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts."**

A school, an archive and a platform each reproduce some contact and fail to reproduce others. The two silent failures are the reason a medium can look like pure gain in the short run.

**Refuter.** Exhibit a carrier change that added storage without changing any actor, condition or relation in contact with the residual, and show the goal still succeeded. That would falsify the habitat claim for that case.

**Status:** `single-source` on the figure side (Fig 11 governs capability and habitat; the world sources on transcription and external memory corroborate the mechanism shape but share no figure author's evidence pool beyond the essays).

### F7: Storage and retrieval are priced in different currencies (D1, D4)

Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."** Fig 12 :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."** Fig 12 :129: **"Neither figure predicts the other, so both are read or the choice is made blind."**

A medium can be cheap in bits and expensive in actions, or the reverse. An oral formula is highly compressed and costs the action of a performance each time it is used. A written archive is expensive to produce and cheap to consult. A search index is cheap to state and can take a whole budget to verify whether the retrieved item is true. Conflating "we can store more" with "we remember more" is summing two figures the framework says do not add.

Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."** A mnemonic device, a canonical text, a citation, an index: each survives its own use and changes the cost of the next use. Whether a part is a catalyst or a spent part is not recorded by the atom list, so it must be checked in use.

**Refuter.** Give a single unit in which description cost and action cost add, and show a medium ranking that the two-reading account gets wrong. No such unit is supplied by the figures.

**Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 12 pricing rule plus the catalyst distinction).

### F8: Overlap decides whether more witnesses help (D2, D5)

Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Fig 8 :48: *"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."* Fig 8 :50: **"But shared atoms are not the signature. Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."**

A library, an oral tradition and a digital corpus are three carriers, not automatically three witnesses. If the digital corpus is a digitisation of the same books the schools already used, the union adds nothing at the evidence level (Fig 8 :14: *"their union adds nothing"*). Surplus exists only where closures differ and a residual has been described (Fig 8 :19-21: *"closures differ, so a surplus exists / but no residual is described / nothing was aimed; the union is mute"*).

Fig 2 :42: **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."** Since the cost of a wrong account of collective memory is high and diffuse, independence is worth buying here, which is why the world sources below are drawn from cognitive science, oral tradition studies and the psychology of memory rather than from one commentary stream.

**Refuter.** Show two carriers with demonstrably different evidence pools whose union still produces no surplus on a described residual. That would contradict the surplus claim for this case.

**Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 2 and Fig 8, same independence quantity read twice).

### F9: The question is a union, so no single medium wins (all domains)

Fig 9 :54 (above) and :56: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

Measures such as storage capacity, reach, speed of retrieval and number of readers are **filters for the budget**. They say what gets tried first and what it costs. They are not witnesses for a verdict about whether a community remembers well. A community with a vast searchable corpus can still be unable to re-find the one slot that matters, and a community with no writing at all can retain a warning for generations through a performer who carries the bond and the route.

**Refuter.** Exhibit a single scalar over which oral, written and digital forms rank communities by remembering-and-sharing, with the slot structure preserved and the anchor named. No figure line returns such a scalar.

**Status:** `general` structural, triangulated across Fig 9 and Fig 1.

### F10: The specific name is a gap, reported as unreachable, not as silence (D4, D5)

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."** Fig 9 :27-28: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."** Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"**

No figure line says which technology, which literacy rate, which index design or which platform will occupy a slot in a given community. The figures return the conditions a carrier must satisfy, not the name of the carrier. The wish for a universal historical sequence "oral then written then digital therefore better" is a preference held as a term, which Fig 5 :62-63 says a frame cannot see: it holds the sought shape *"as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term"* and *"nothing here can see it."* The remedy used in section 0 was to bind the shape as an ordinary term.

This gap is of the shape-not-a-name kind, not of the underivable-in-principle kind: an instrument could measure a specific community's slot vector and name what works there. It is unreached at this budget because no such measurement was taken.

**Refuter.** Supply the measurement that fills a named slot with a named carrier and shows the anchor did not move. Until then the slot stays open.

**Status:** `gap`, triangulated (Fig 9 and Fig 13).

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the standing rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument.

The shape claim in section 0 is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A carried recording of one occasion lets a receiving generation produce a new success without re-deriving the method, breaking the method / recording boundary at the membrane.
2. Search inside a fixed corpus adds a genuinely new primitive to the community's closure, with the atom named and its provenance shown.
3. A single global memory score tracks every live slot without aggregation, so the per-slot indexing rule is inapplicable at community scale.
4. A stored item yields its bond type and production route without its lineage, and the receiver can act on the difference.
5. A retelling chain's agreement tracks a non-updating ground rather than the shared pool, with the ground shown not to move when the chain changes its mind.
6. A carrier change adds storage without changing any actor, condition or relation in contact with the residual, and the goal still succeeds.
7. Description cost and action cost are shown to add in one unit, producing a medium ranking the two-currency account gets wrong.

If any fires, the corresponding domain finding is withdrawn. None was run in this simulated derivation, so no part of this folder is labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the union finding and the per-slot rule (F3, F9). Do not answer with a ranking of media.
2. State what crosses the membrane in each case: method, head start or recording (F1). That is the unit of "share."
3. Ask separately whether the bond and lineage travelled (F4). A fact without its route is a recorded ingredient, not remembered knowledge.
4. Name the anchor in every case (F5). If the community's only check is itself, drift is expected and invisible.
5. Enumerate the habitat before judging the medium (F6). Ask which actors and conditions the new carrier introduced and which required contact it failed to reproduce.
6. Keep bits and actions in separate columns (F7). Storage capacity is a budget filter, not a verdict.
7. Report the gap honestly (F10). The framework gives the shape of a good transmission arrangement, not the name of a technology.

Fig 4 :30: **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."** The round trip here is: characterise the residual in effect terms, derive the carrier conditions, then check them against a real community with a non-updating reference. That check has not been done.

## 7. Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **What is the residual?** A per-slot gap between what the community can re-find and what the carrier lets it re-find. Fig 1 :19, Symbols :38-40.
2. **Is this one question?** No, a union of five conditions. Fig 9 :54-56.
3. **What crosses a medium change?** A method, a head start or a recording. Fig 4 :22, :25.
4. **Does the record grow the reach?** No. Fig 5 :72, Fig 6 :32, Fig 13 :115.
5. **What travels with a fact?** The bond and the lineage, or nothing. Fig 12 :5, Fig 13 :130.
6. **What stops the retelling chain from drifting?** A ground that does not update, ranged separately by each frame. Fig 2 :34, :45, Fig 7 :35.
7. **What is still owed?** A measurement of a named community's slot vector with an anchor that does not move. Fig 9 :32; reported as `gap`, unreached at this budget.

**Strains after re-census: none hidden.** Two are recorded and dissolved by assigning each figure to its proper object: (i) Fig 3's dependency order versus the apparent simultaneity of the media (the chain orders the loop's links, not the historical sequence of carriers); (ii) Fig 4's membrane versus Fig 6's closure wall (what crosses between scales versus what a closure can reach are different quantities). One live strain remains: Fig 11's residual-relative valence means a medium cannot be called good or bad in general, so every verdict must carry the slot it is about. That is a design constraint, not a defect.
