## 4. Three findings in the wider literature that bear directly

1. **Externalisation is a change of anchor, not an addition of memory.** The external-memory source reports the ancient complaint that writing is a recipe for reminding rather than memory, and the modern version in which erudition shifts from possessing information to knowing where to find it. This matches Fig 4 :25 (a recording carried upward is a description of one occasion) and Fig 10 :24 (the channel decays, not the ground). It does **not** show that internal memory degraded; that claim is explicitly unsubstantiated in the same source and is refused here.
2. **A chain reconstructs toward its listeners.** The tacit-knowledge essay and the oral-tradition source both report that retellings lose what does not fit and become more familiar. This matches Fig 7 :13 (*"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*) and Fig 2 :34 (coherent drift). Nobody has to lie for the drift to happen.
3. **The digital case is disputed, not settled.** The Google-effect page records a large replication effort that failed to reproduce the original finding, followed by a meta-analysis reporting an association. That is a reason to mark the digital-domain verdict `specified` and not `grounded`. A conflict in world sources sends the derivation back to the census; it does not flip a figure verdict. Here it reinforces F2: the effect is on access and storage strategy, with no new primitive demonstrated.

## 5. Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded rules:

- **Fig 4 :22 and :25.** Only methods cross up; a recording looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion. Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording. Lead with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **Fig 10 :42.** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked. Record the stage-1 verdict first.
- **Fig 9 :27-28.** *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the claim were true before giving them the sources.
- **Fig 2 :40.** Agreement from a list selected for likely agreement is a convention, not corroboration. Use the public, opt-in venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`.
- **Fig 2 :42.** *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* The cost of a wrong account of collective memory is high and diffuse, so low-overlap witnesses are worth the extra effort.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim; (2) the pre-registered refuter and the question "what would you count as a failure?"; (3) record the verdict; (4) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority; (5) if asked, the formula as a method with the instruction to write a fresh refuter per problem.

## 7. Legal and practical constraint

Public opt-in or institutional submission routes avoid both the structural failure (a selected pool of believers) and the practical one (unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted). Nothing in this folder sends anything. Hosting and sending are yours to do; prefer a page with a decline option over an attachment pushed to a list.
