# Structure, order and analogues for the three forms of knowledge

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the bond taxonomy. Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).**

## RESIDUAL

The main audit establishes that the carrier is a membrane and that the bond and lineage are what usually fail to travel. Two supporting questions remain: (1) if we hold the atom set fixed, are oral, written and digital forms **isomers** under Fig 12's definition, and (2) what *order* relates them, given that a historical sequence is not automatically a rank.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms available for this exercise: the per-slot content a community tracks (the "what"), the carrier act (speech, inscription, storage-and-retrieval), the typed relation between content and carrier, and the reference used to check the reading. Bonds available: the seven (`+ ∥ ≡ → ⇒ − ⋛`) plus negation `¬`, which is unary and not a bond (Operators table :70-78).

Scale: one transmission act, repeated across a chain. Symmetry: media succession is ordered in time but not ranked in capability. Anchor: the ground, which does not update.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Are the three forms isomers?

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fix the atom set as `{content slot, carrier act, receiver, reference}`. Under a fixed set, the three forms can be written with different bonds:

- **Oral:** `carrier(speech) → receiver` with the relation carried in the performance. The reference is often another performer, so the loop can close without a ground.
- **Written:** `carrier(inscription) → receiver` with the relation recorded in the text and its apparatus. The reference can be a text that does not update on the reader's view.
- **Digital:** `carrier(storage-and-search) → receiver` with the relation either preserved in the record or flattened into fields. The reference can be a versioned archive, or a feed that adapts to the reader.

Same atoms, different bond and different arrangement, different behaviour. Under Fig 12's definition they are **isomers**, provided the atom set is genuinely fixed.

But the condition matters. A platform introduces a new actor (the index or ranking function) and a new condition (latent retrieval), so the atom set is not fixed for the digital case. Fig 13 :115: **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."** Where the digital carrier adds an affordance the others lacked, the relation is composition plus an **import** from a frame whose closure differs, with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :23). It is not an isomer of the text; it is a different molecule built on more atoms.

- **Falsifier for Q1:** show that a written or digital transmission requires an atom that the oral case does not, and that the two still differ only by bond. Then the isomer claim fails for that pair and the import reading stands.

**Status:** `specified` with a live fixed-atom condition. This is the same condition the exemplar records for its own isomer claim, and it is named rather than hidden.

### Q2: What order relates them?

Fig 12 :30: `→` means **sequence**: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*. The historical fact that inscription follows speech and networked storage follows print is a sequence in this sense. It is not a rank.

Fig 13 :4-5: **"the two kinds are in lockstep, a crossing is a change of role rather than a step upward, and no member of the pair is senior to the other."** Fig 4 :19-20: *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* So moving from one carrier to the next changes the cast, not the amount of knowledge in the world.

Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** The arrow here has a direction, but it is about what kind of thing crosses a scale boundary, not about which medium is higher. A method travels upward from many agents to a community; a head start travels downward to the next agent. Both are permitted; a recording of one occasion is not.

Fig 12 :34-35 defines sequence by the swap test: *"A then B"* and *"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."* Applied to carriers, swapping the order in which a community acquires writing and oral performance does make a different history, but it does not make a more capable community. The capability claim belongs to Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*

- **Falsifier for Q2:** exhibit a carrier transition that increases community capability while leaving actors, currency, anchor and contact unchanged. That would make the transition a step upward rather than a change of role.

### Q3: The remembering cycle

Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**

A remembering community is a cycle: what was remembered becomes an input to the next act of remembering. The sign decides the outcome.

- **Negative feedback with a ground:** the community ranges its recollection against a reference that does not update (Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*). Corrections shrink the residual. This is calibration.
- **Positive feedback without a ground:** the output re-enters as input and each pass confirms the last. Fig 2 :34: **"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."** This is drift, not remembering.

Fig 12 :121 also says a chain cannot close by construction. A purely linear retelling chain has no way to range against anything but itself; the cycle has to be built by adding the reference, not by extending the chain. Fig 7 :36: **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."**

This is why "more transmission" and "better memory" are different quantities. Adding links to a mirror chain adds motion (Fig 7 :32: *"one long room: motion, no progress"*).

- **Falsifier for Q3:** show a linear retelling chain that converges on the ground without an external reference being introduced. That would close a chain by construction and refute the distinction.

### Q4: Catalysts and functional groups in transmission

Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."** In community memory, the mnemonic formula, the canonical text, the citation and the index all survive their own use and change the cost of the next use. Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** The functional group here is the cross-kind arrangement: a content slot joined by a typed relation to a receiver and a reference. The qualifier (speech, inscription, search) says which member.

Fig 12 :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."** A catalyst changes the action cost without necessarily changing the bit cost. A canonical text lowers the cost of re-finding a reading; it does not lower the cost of checking whether the reading is true. Conflating the two is the error Fig 12 :129 names: *"Neither figure predicts the other, so both are read or the choice is made blind."*

Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."** A community that stores only the content slots can say what it knows and cannot say how it got there. That is exactly the failure mode described in the world literature as the loss of context and pretext in written transmission.

- **Falsifier for Q4:** show a stored content slot from which the receiver can recover both the route and the check against a non-updating reference, with no catalyst and no bond recorded. That would collapse the functional-group distinction for this case.

## CONCLUSION

Under a fixed atom set the three forms are Fig 12 isomers: same content and receiver atoms, different typed bonds, decisively different behaviour. The digital case usually breaks the fixed-set condition because the index or ranking function is a new atom, so that case is composition plus an import, not an isomer. Their historical order is a sequence (`→`), not a rank: a crossing changes role rather than moving up (Fig 13 :4-5). What a community remembers is a **cycle**, and its sign is decided by whether a non-updating reference is in the loop (Fig 12 :121, Fig 2 :34). Mnemonics, canonical texts and indices are catalysts that lower action cost; the cross-kind arrangement is the functional group that fixes what kind of thing is being transmitted (Fig 12 :119, :123).

The analogies are structural, not historical claims. No figure line says that any particular society passed through all three forms, or that one replaced the others.
