# Expert verification packet - the Tether on venture funding and startup innovation

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher of entrepreneurship, venture capital or innovation can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No claim is made that any measurement has paid for this shape. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Funding helps a startup innovate only in a qualified form: when the fund imports a frame whose closure differs and a new composition is settled at the customer ground. Money alone is a currency inside an existing closure and cannot, by itself, move what the company can compose. Funding pushes toward narrow growth targets and premature scaling when the fund's growth comparison replaces the startup's own objective and the behavioural stage of team, spend and acquisition outruns the actual stage measured at customer response. These are two conditions with different instruments, not two degrees of one effect, and their verdicts are never averaged. The framework does not name the term sheet or the milestone that would fill either slot; it returns a shape, written falsifiers and the instruments that would close them.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 6 and 8
- **Statement:** capital is a currency, not an atom. Only contact with a differing closure moves reach; money alone does not add a new composable primitive.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :21 "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Fig 6 :25 "An import adds no atom to the world." Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 8 :45 "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."
- **Refuter:** a controlled set in which capital alone, with no new atom entering from a differing closure, reliably raises ground-settled novelty per startup against a matched unfunded set. If that holds, C1 is false.

### C2 - framework-internal and practice-facing; checkable by a researcher against their own data
- **Statement:** a growth target is a comparison bond. When the metric the loop optimises becomes internal to the fund's return model, the frame scores itself with a quantity it produces. Premature scaling is separately a channel failure: bracket fidelity falls while the ground may be healthy.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :112 "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond." Fig 1 :27 "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Fig 7 :37 "Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:" with Fig 7 :38 "the bracket's fidelity across the handoff" and Fig 7 :39 "the ground's pressure on the room".
- **What to check:** in your data, is the founder's objective after financing the same signal the customer ground pays, or a proxy internal to the fund? Is the behavioural stage (hiring, spend, acquisition) running ahead of the actual stage (customer response)? A/B experimentation is the operational bracket; check whether it is present.
- **Refuter:** (a) the fund's growth metric is demonstrably identical, per startup slot, to the customer retention and payment signal over the same window; or (b) startups whose behavioural stage outruns their actual stage still show high, non-declining bracket fidelity and ground pressure. Either breaks C2.

### C3 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 11, 12 and 7
- **Statement:** the valence of capital is relative to the residual, not a property of capital. The same money is a benefactor when a resource gap blocks a faithful test and a malfactor when it removes the constraint keeping an unearned scale honest. A syndicate's agreement is not a witness.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." Fig 12 :119 "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is." Fig 7 :13 "agreement compounds, evidence does not".
- **Refuter:** capital whose measured valence is invariant across a documented change of the startup's residual; or investor agreement, with the shared evidence pool declared, predicting per-startup ground-settled novelty out of sample. Either breaks C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that venture capital is good or bad in general. The two conditions are separate verdicts, never averaged.
- Not that growth targets are always wrong; a comparison bond is legitimate when its ground is the signal being settled.
- Not that "product market fit", "blitzscaling" or "unit economics" are figure-derived primitives. They are bindings from the practice frame, recorded as such.
- Not that Fig 13's six physical terms apply to startup resources. That reading was a strain and was retracted in `01` section 6, S1.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not that a prestigious fund's backing is evidence. Prestige can fund an action; it cannot settle one (the RSI essay).

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminator, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. **Reach test:** a pre-registered, per-startup record of new compositions and the differing closure that entered with the money, verified against customer payment. Not a portfolio-level patent count.
2. **Objective test:** per-slot comparison of the fund's growth metric with the customer retention and payment signal over the same window.
3. **Bracket test:** behavioural stage versus actual stage, with bracket fidelity and ground pressure measured independently.
4. **Standing falsifiers:** the seven in `01` section 4, written before this run.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 6 :21, :23, :25 and Fig 7 :37-39 and Fig 12 :112. Five short passages.
2. Say which of the two conditions your own data can discriminate, and which it currently pools.
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish a new settled composition from a larger number with the same reading, or say why none is possible.
4. Name the strongest refuter from section 2 and say what your data would show if it fired.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
