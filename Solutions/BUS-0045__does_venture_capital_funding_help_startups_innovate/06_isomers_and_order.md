### Q2 - Which bond does capital form?

- Fig 12 :77 "A produces B; B did not exist before". That is production (`⇒`), the bond where the second operand is absent until the first fires.
- Fig 12 :72 "A then B; B needs A to have happened". That is sequence (`->`), where the second operand is already waiting.
- Operators table :16 "the only bond in a flat formula, which is why a formula records ingredients and not arrangement" (conjunction, `+`).

If capital merely coexists with the startup's existing library, the bond is conjunction: both hold, nothing new exists. That is the case where "we raised a round" and "the company can now compose something it could not before" come apart. If the arrival of capital produces a new, settled term that was absent before, the bond is production and F1's qualified claim has fired. **The difference is carried entirely by the bond, not by the amount.** Fig 12 :5 "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if every funded startup that grows can be written with a `+` bond and no new settled term, the production reading fails and capital is only fuel.

### Q3 - The growth target is a comparison installed at the wrong link

- Fig 12 :112 "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."
- Operators table :51 "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"
- Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."
- Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

The dependency chain is perception (1) -> vocabulary (2) -> objective (3) -> planning (4) -> learn and carry (5). The funding frame arrives with an objective already formed, at link 3, before the startup has built the vocabulary of link 2 for what its changed situation affords. That is an out-of-order arrival: the objective is imported from another closure while the middle link still has no instrument (Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."). This is the structural reason "narrow growth targets" is not a moral failing by the fund; it is a dependency-order violation. The fund supplies a comparison before the startup can say what progress would mean in its own terms.

### Q4 - Catalyst or spent part?

- Fig 12 :119 "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."
- Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."

Capital is a catalyst when follow-on funding is contingent on new ground-verified evidence, so the part regenerates across uses. It is a spent part when a round is consumed without producing the evidence that would regenerate the next one. Nothing in the term sheet's description records which it is; that is Fig 12 :119 exactly. The sign of the loop matters: a milestone cycle in which evidence re-enters as input closes a learning loop, while a milestone cycle in which spending re-enters as spending closes a commitment loop. Both are cycles; only one is a catalyst.

**Falsifier:** if a term sheet can be written so that its catalyst or spent-part behaviour is recoverable from the term sheet alone, rather than from what the ground pays across uses, Q4 is false.

### Q5 - Are funding-driven innovation and premature scaling isomers?

- Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Write both conditions over the same atom set {capital, expertise, network, product, team, data, customer access, milestones}:

- C1, qualified help: `capital ⇒ (new settled term)` under import from a differing closure. Production bond, ground settled.
- C2, narrow growth and premature scaling: `capital + (fund's comparison)` with the behavioural stage outrunning the actual stage. Conjunction plus an installed comparison at link 3.

Same atoms, different bond, different substance. That is a real isomer claim under Fig 12 :117. As usually reported, however, the two are described by different atom sets (patents versus headcount), so the weaker reading is that they are members of the same **functional group** (Fig 12 :124): both are funding-in-a-startup compositions, with the qualifier deciding which member. The isomer claim is the stronger, checkable version.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if either condition can be written without an atom the other requires, for example if premature scaling never involves customer access or a milestone, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands instead.

### Q6 - The growth-versus-feedback analogue (low overlap)

The University of Minnesota open biology chapter states: *"if the growth rate is too fast compared with that feedback, the population can overshoot its carrying capacity, which can lead to highly complex outcomes."* It gives the progression from smooth approach at small intrinsic growth rate, through overshoot and damped oscillation, to cycles and chaos at large rates. The cause named is a delay in the feedback between stimulus and response.

Map the atoms, not the words (Fig 4 :20 "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."): the growth rate is the scale commitment; the carrying-capacity sensing is the customer-response feedback; the overshoot is the behavioural stage exceeding the actual stage. This corroborates the shape of F3 in `01` from a field with no shared evidence pool with entrepreneurship research. It does **not** license a business conclusion. An agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim (Fig 11 :40 "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves.").

## CONCLUSION

Capital forms a production bond only when a new, ground-settled term appears that was absent before; otherwise it forms a conjunction and merely coexists with the existing library. The funding frame arrives at link 3 (objective) before link 2 (vocabulary) has an instrument, which is a dependency-order violation and the structural source of "narrow growth targets". Capital is a catalyst or a spent part depending on what the ground pays across uses, and nothing in the term sheet records which. Funding-driven innovation and premature scaling are isomers over the same atom set under the stronger reading, functional-group members under the weaker one. The ecology analogue corroborates the overshoot shape from a different field.
