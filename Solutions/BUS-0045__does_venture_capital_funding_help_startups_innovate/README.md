## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not whether funding is good or bad in the abstract, but what changes in a startup's loop when capital enters it. In effect terms: a startup's library Gamma predicts a market response Gamma(b,a); the market returns o-prime; R = |Gamma(b,a) - o'| is measured per startup slot, never averaged across a portfolio (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22). Innovation is the admittance of new terms that lower R against a ground that still pays. Premature scaling is a mismatch between a startup's behavioural stage and its actual stage, detected at the customer-response ground, not at the cap table.

- Option A, "funding adds the capacity to innovate": predicts that novelty is a function of resources, so more capital should produce more ground-settled new compositions per slot.
- Option B, "funding imposes narrow growth targets and premature scaling": predicts that the investor's objective replaces the startup's objective, so the metric the loop optimises becomes frame-internal to the fund (Fig 1 :27) and the behavioural stage outruns the actual stage.
- What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a shape naming the two conditions that must be separately checked per startup, (C1) whether funding changed contact and reach so that new terms were admitted and settled, and (C2) whether funding set the objective and scale so that the bracket stayed faithful to the customer ground. Plus an instrument that discriminates a failure of C1 from a failure of C2. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no loop check can see it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` keeps both conditions separate and never averages them. It is Option B in `specified` mode for the growth-target and scaling channel, and a qualified Option A for the reach-import channel: funding helps innovation only when it imports a differing closure and the new composition is settled at the ground, not because money is money.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the business question, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of startup innovation.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable investors or founders manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
