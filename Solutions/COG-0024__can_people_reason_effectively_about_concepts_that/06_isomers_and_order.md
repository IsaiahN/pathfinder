# Isomers and order: name versus term, union versus partition

**Mode: `specified` on the application, `general` on the bond taxonomy.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The main audit closes on a shape: a name is neither necessary nor sufficient; contact and closure reach do the work; two opposed tracks; a union of compounds. Three supporting questions remain:

1. What kind of bond relates a public name to a held concept-term?
2. Are the named and unnamed modes isomers, different substances, or different closures?
3. In what order must refusal, search and import be run?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — A name is not an identity bond with a term

Fig 12 :65-69 gives identity: "≡ identity / two names, one referent / yes / substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No."

Take a term t held nonlinguistically for slot s, say the sensorimotor discrimination a practitioner makes before they can say what they are doing. Substitute the public word w for t in the practitioner's reasoning. Something is lost: the correction history, the perceptual resolution, the ability to be surprised at the fine grain. Fig 12 :107: "Settling compresses the description and not what it cost to establish." The word keeps the compressed description; the cost of establishing it stays with the practitioner. So ≡ fails its own test.

Candidate replacement: production ⇒, "A produces B; B did not exist before" (Fig 12 :77). When t is already held, w produces a public reading of t that did not previously exist in L. That fits. When t is **not** held, w produces nothing that pays: it is a bond to a missing operand. Fig 12 :101: "A bond that costs nothing is a bond that carries nothing, which is the flat case." A word without a term is a free bond, and the mint fails reach (Formula step 3: REACHABILITY φ ∈ closure(Γ)).

Conclusion: the name-to-term relation is production, not identity, and is only licensed after the term is settled. This is the structural reason the outreach refuses to treat "no word" as "no concept".

**Falsifier:** show a case where substituting w for t loses nothing measurable on the slot, including correction history and fine-grained surprise. Then ≡ holds and this finding is withdrawn.

### Q2 — The named and unnamed modes are isomers over a shared atom set

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Consider two modes of reasoning about the same slot s in the same person:

- Mode U (unnamed): perceptual and motor terms, contact, no public label.
- Mode N (named): the same perceptual and motor terms, contact, plus the production bond ⇒ to a public word.

They share the atom set. They differ by one bond. Under Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance. The behaviour differs decisively in public reportability and in how cheaply the concept can be handed to another person, while the underlying reasoning may be identical.

As written by the literature, the two modes often do **not** share the full atom set: a verbal task and a practical task measure different things. In that case they are not isomers; they are two members of a functional group (Fig 12 :124) at best, or two different closures at worst. The isomer claim is the stronger, testable form and is the one worth sending to an expert.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if adding only the public word changes the reachable atoms (a new capability appears that is not derivable from the pre-word atoms), the isomer claim fails and the two modes are different closures. If they share atoms but the bond cannot be identified, the weaker functional-group claim stands instead.

### Q3 — Order: refuse, search, then import; never import against a budget limit

Formula step 2 sorts R into five bins, of which the fifth is REFUTED: "a term held is false; what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation". Formula step 3 note: "NOTHING IS EVER DELETED, and the reach falls because something was ADDED." Fig 12 :101 (Operators :57-58): "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."

The determinist claim ("no word, no reasoning") is a held term that is false under this census. It must be refused before any new term is minted. Minting a term for "how people reason without words" first and refusing later would build the machinery to pass its own refuter: the forbidden inversion Fig 5 :66 (and Formula step "Direction") names.

Formula step 7 then gives the search order: SEARCHED first, "Asked FIRST — it is cheapest and the other two assume its answer"; INWARD and OUTWARD are opposed and "never read one against the other". Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts. Importing a new language or instrument against a mere budget limit adds an atom that was not needed, and Fig 6 :24: "A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."

So the order is:

1. Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term (`01` §0).
2. Refuse determinism (Formula step 2, REFUTED).
3. SEARCHED inside the person's current reach (Formula step 7).
4. If R_s still does not close, INWARD: sharpen the instrument from one already returning something (Fig 6 :32 "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something.").
5. If nothing registers at any resolution, OUTWARD: characterise R_s in effect terms and import from a differing closure, with source gate and shadow test.
6. Settle against the ground before the term can be composed over (Formula step 5), and remember that settling is necessary but not sufficient.

**Falsifier:** a case where OUTWARD closes R_s while SEARCHED was never run, yet the imported atom was already in closure(Γ). That is an import against a budget limit and breaks the order.

### Q4 — Partition, union, and why order matters for the dependency chain

Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

The two conditions are: (C1) the slot is reachable in the person's closure; (C2) contact supplies the differential that makes the slot register. Reasoning effectively about an unnamed concept can fail because C1 fails, because C2 fails, or because both fail. These are not one hard question. A single lever (for example, teaching the word) clears the region where only C1 fails and leaves the intersection standing.

Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." The relevant order is perception → vocabulary of affordances → objective → planning → learn and carry. A word enters at link 2 as one possible term. If the break is actually at link 1 (nothing registers on the slot), effort spent teaching a word lands above the break and cannot rescue it. Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

**Falsifier:** a single intervention that restores reasoning in both conditions with no categorically different residue. That would make this a partition with one common cause and dissolve the union claim.

### Q5 — Analogues are corroboration at best, never the derivation

Fig 9 :30-31 "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause". Fig 8 :50 "Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."

The low-overlap analogues read in this run are animal concept learning without language, quantity judgement in non-human animals, pre-verbal infants, historical mathematicians using infinitesimals before formalisation, and Polanyi's tacit knowledge. Each is a separate frame that reaches a similar shape: reasoning can run without a public name. But they share the broad literature of cognitive science (Wikipedia syntheses plus one primary paper), so under Fig 2 :40 they are one weak witness, not five. They corroborate the mechanism shape; they do not decide the human-language question. The figures decide that.

**Falsifier:** a genuinely low-overlap frame that reaches the opposite shape from the residual described in effect terms. It would return us to the census, not flip the verdict.

## CONCLUSION

A public name is a production bond onto a held term, not an identity with it. The named and unnamed modes can be isomers over a shared atom set, with the stronger version still to be tested. The union of reach and contact must be unbundled before search. The operational order is bind the shape, refuse determinism, SEARCHED, INWARD, OUTWARD, settle, then compose. Four falsifiers are written above before any contact with an expert.
