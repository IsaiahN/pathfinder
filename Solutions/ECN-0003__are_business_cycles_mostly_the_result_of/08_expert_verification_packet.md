# Expert verification packet: the Tether on shocks versus feedback in business cycles

**What this is:** a claim in a form a macroeconomist or business-cycle empirical researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion.

**Mode: `specified` on the domain applications, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** No measurement of per-slot attribution is claimed.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "are business cycles mostly unexpected shocks or mostly ordinary decisions and feedback loops" is a bundled union, not one binary. The prediction residual is indexed by sector slot and episode, and a national average hides the live signal. A shock is an import into a forecaster's closure and only an import moves the wall of what that closure can reach; ordinary decisions are compositions inside a closed atom set whose arrangements never run out. Both mechanisms are legal, so the figures decide neither a winner nor a percentage. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument, a measured loop sign, and a source gate and shadow test for each named shock.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1. Framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 1, 9 and 12

- **Statement:** the question is a union compound, the residual is per slot, and feedback is a typed cycle whose sign decides closure.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
  - Fig 9 :57 *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
  - Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
  - Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
  - Fig 12 :121 *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*
- **Refuter:** a documented case where every slot's residual is proportional to the aggregate, or an economy with no re-entrant output whose cycles match the per-slot record.

### C2. Framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 6, 8 and 13

- **Statement:** import and composition are different structures with different effects on the reach, both legal; the figures therefore do not choose between them and do not license a "mostly" verdict.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 6 :14 *"the only operator that moves the wall"*
  - Fig 6 :21 *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*
  - Fig 8 :32 *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."*
  - Fig 13 :115 *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."*
- **Refuter:** show that an import is expressible as a rearrangement of held atoms with no change to the wall, or that composition within closure cannot generate an observed cycle.

### C3. World-facing, checkable against the published literature and the dating record

- **Statement:** both mechanisms have existence proofs, no world source supplies the per-slot attribution, and the measurement record is retrospective and pooled.
- **Sources opened 2026-11-22:** Kydland and Prescott, *Time to Build and Aggregate Fluctuations*, Econometrica 50(6), 1982, abstract at https://www.econometricsociety.org/publications/econometrica/1982/11/01/time-build-and-aggregate-fluctuations; Grandmont, *On Endogenous Competitive Business Cycles*, Econometrica 53(5), 1985, abstract at https://www.econometricsociety.org/publications/econometrica/1985/09/01/endogenous-competitive-business-cycles; NBER Business Cycle Dating at https://www.nber.org/research/business-cycle-dating.
- **What to check:** the Kydland-Prescott abstract reports a model fitted to postwar quarterly data with multi-period construction; the Grandmont abstract reports cycles "not attributable to exogenous 'shocks' nor to any variation of policy since there are none in the model"; the NBER page says "There is no fixed rule about what measures contribute information to the process or how they are weighted in our decisions" and that the approach is retrospective.
- **Refuter:** a single study that, on per-sector data with vintage-invariant measurement and a stated source gate, assigns a stable majority to one mechanism across episodes.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a theory of business cycles and not a replacement for DSGE, RBC, or endogenous-cycle models.
- Not a number, a percentage, or a ranking of schools.
- Not that any named recession was caused by one mechanism.
- Not that the figures decide which mechanism dominates. That is declared `gap`.
- Not `grounded`. This packet is a simulated derivation and cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 5. What would settle it

1. An instrument that separates, per slot and episode, an observation the closure could not have produced from one it could have produced by rearrangement.
2. A measured sign for the re-entrant loop in that slot.
3. A source gate and shadow test for each named shock: where the atom came from and which gap it explains that composition does not.
4. A vintage-invariant recording, so the attribution does not move with revisions or pooling.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19-22, Fig 6 :14, Fig 8 :32, Fig 9 :55-57 and Fig 12 :121. Six short passages.
2. Check C3 against your own knowledge: do the two Econometrica abstracts really establish existence proofs for opposite mechanisms, and does the NBER page really decline to fix weights?
3. Name one per-sector instrument that would discriminate an import from a rearrangement, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. Record the verdict before reading any framework material.
