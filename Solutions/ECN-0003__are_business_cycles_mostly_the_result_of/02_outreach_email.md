**Subject:** One checkable claim about shocks versus feedback in business cycles

> Hello [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. A failure is more useful to me than an endorsement.
>
> **The output (mode `specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "are business cycles mostly shocks or mostly ordinary decisions and feedback loops" is a bundled union, not one binary. A shock is an import into a forecaster's closure, and only an import moves the wall of what that closure can reach. Ordinary decisions are compositions inside a closed set of atoms, and the arrangements over a closed set never run out. Both are legal mechanisms. No figure line in the framework I am using decides which one is "mostly" responsible, and an answer estimated on an aggregate series hides the per-sector signal the question needs.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that shocks are unimportant, that feedback loops alone explain cycles, that a particular variance share is the answer, or that a named recession was caused by one thing. The framework returns a shape, not a name. The shape here is: per-slot residual, two mechanisms, a source gate for any claimed shock, and a signed cycle for any claimed feedback.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written before this message was drafted):**
> 1. One ground test separates import-driven from composition-driven residuals in every sector and episode, and one side holds a stable majority under the same threshold.
> 2. Per-sector residuals are proportional to the aggregate residual, so averaging conceals nothing.
> 3. A closed system with no imports reproduces the whole per-sector record with no re-entrant residual, or a single sourced import explains it all with a passing shadow test.
> 4. Two research groups with genuinely disjoint data, instruments and closures converge on the same per-sector attribution.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is enough. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
