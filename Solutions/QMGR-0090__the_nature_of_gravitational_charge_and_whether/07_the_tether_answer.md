## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that gravitational charge is a signed scalar like electric charge. It is a positive tensor source.
- Not that the Dirac monopole explanation transfers to gravity.
- Not that the graviton exists, not that a specific quantum gravity program is correct, and not that source mass is operator-valued.
- Not that single graviton detection, if achieved, proves quantization. The authors of that proposal say explicitly that it does not.
- Not that a specific quantization unit or Planck-scale value has been derived. The framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself").
- Not a theory of everything. There is no last room (Fig 7 :6), and the instrumentation limit is a place to stop, not a wall to deny.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a measurement.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (read 2026-11-23): an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it, "not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." Here the corresponding tests are:

- Does the gravitational source spectrum resolve into discrete levels where the imported quantum frame predicts them, or stay continuous at every resolution? The single-graviton absorption proposal (Tobar et al., Nature Communications 2024) and the active/passive mass self-acceleration proposal (Fragkos and Pikovski, 2025/2026) are two different candidate instruments.
- Does a quantum source mass produce a gravitational field at all, or only a semiclassical expectation value? This is the operator-valued charge question.
- Standing falsifiers: derive discreteness or the continuum as a seventh primitive; establish a signed scalar gravitational charge at the ground; derive a specific discrete source unit from the six alone.
