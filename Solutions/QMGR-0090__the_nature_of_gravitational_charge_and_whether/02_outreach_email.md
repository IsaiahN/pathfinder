**Subject:** Check one output about gravitational charge before you decide whether the claim is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The word "charge" in this question is borrowed from electromagnetism, where it is a signed, conserved scalar that comes in discrete units. Gravity's source is not that object. It is positive energy-momentum, a symmetric rank-two tensor that couples to a spin-2 field, and no negative gravitational charge is observed. So the first useful move is to split the question: the nature half is largely settled, the quantization half is open.
>
> On the second half, the framework I am using holds neither the continuum nor discreteness as a primitive. It holds six terms in two kinds and says that discreteness, like smoothness, is what a particular composition leaves behind. So "can gravitational charge be quantized" becomes: does a composition of the substrate, once settled against the ground, leave a discrete residue in the source spectrum? The framework returns that shape and the route to it, not a value and not a name. It does not say the graviton exists, and it does not say a source mass is or is not operator-valued. An instrument has to return that.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** Not that gravitational charge is a scalar like electric charge. Not that the Dirac monopole mechanism transfers to gravity. Not that a specific quantum gravity program is correct. Not that single graviton detection, if achieved, proves the quantization of gravity; the authors of that proposal say explicitly that it does not. Agreement from people who share my evidence pool is one witness, not corroboration.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before this run:**
> 1. deriving discreteness or the continuum as a seventh primitive from the other six;
> 2. establishing a signed scalar gravitational charge with negative values at the ground;
> 3. an instrument that resolves the source spectrum and finds continuity at every resolution where the quantum frame predicts a discrete residue;
> 4. deriving a specific discrete source unit or mass scale from the six-term set alone, with no import.
>
> If any one holds, I retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text and citations (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Quantum Gravity": https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/quantum-gravity
> - Tobar et al., "Detecting single gravitons with quantum sensing", Nature Communications (2024): https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-51420-8
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures material. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
