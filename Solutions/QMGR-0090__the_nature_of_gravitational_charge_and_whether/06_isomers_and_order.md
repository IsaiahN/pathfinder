# Supporting derivation: structure, order and analogues

**Mode: `specified`, with `general` on the structural statements.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

Two questions are owed by the audit. (1) If "charge" is a coupling role rather than a substance, which bond structure does it name in each theory? (2) Are electric charge quantization and gravitational source discreteness isomers over the same substrate, or members of a functional group, or unrelated compositions?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: Space, Time, Energy (states) and Gradient, Network, Spectrum (structures). Bonds held: the seven (three symmetric, four ordered) plus negation. No new primitive is available without import (Fig 13 :115 "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."). Electric charge and magnetic monopole are atoms from the physics frame, not from the six; they enter as bindings with provenance.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - What does the word "charge" name in bond terms?

- Fig 12 :46 "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."
- In electromagnetism, charge is a signed scalar label attached to matter, the conserved quantity of a U(1) gauge symmetry. Structurally it is closest to Spectrum (ordering, more and less, signed) bonded to matter: a structure over a state.
- In gravity, the source is Energy (state) arranged by a Gradient over Space or Time: Fig 13 :75 "Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state." So a structure must sit between the states.
- A "charge" is therefore a **role** in a composition: the operand that a field equation reads as its source. It is not an eighth atom and not a seventh primitive. Fig 13 :41 "Each is indivisible: no arrangement of the other five yields it." applies to the six, and charge is not among them.
- **Result:** the role is the same (source operand), the bond structures differ. This is a role isomorphism, not a substance identity.

### Q2 - Are the two charges isomers?

- Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Over the six-atom substrate, electric charge's nearest binding is Spectrum over matter; the gravitational source's binding is Energy with Gradient over Space or Time. They do not share all six atoms under different bonds, so by Fig 12's own definition they are **not isomers** as currently written. They are two members of a functional group: both are `state, structure` source compositions with different atoms in the slots. The group says what kind (a source term); the qualifier says which member (signed scalar versus positive tensor).
- The stronger form: if both are rewritten over the full shared atom set, differing only in which structure mediates which state pair, then they become a real isomer claim. That is the version worth testing, because it is checkable rather than terminological.
- **Falsifier, written before this run:** if the gravitational source can be written with the same U(1) scalar, signed bond structure as electric charge with no loss of predictive content, the functional-group reading fails and the naive charge analogy stands. If a seventh primitive is derived from the six, the whole substrate reading fails.

### Q3 - What is the order of the two questions?

- Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Fig 3 :20-21 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
- The chain is: perceive the charge-role mismatch (link 1), acquire terms for source and discreteness (link 2), state what would count as progress (link 3), plan the ground test (link 4), carry the result (link 5).
- Link 2 is suspect. Fig 3 :25-27 "The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments. A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." The relevant instrument here is a source-spectrum discriminator, and the single-graviton proposal is a candidate for it.
- **Result:** answer the nature half first, but do not assume the vocabulary for the quantization half is adequate. The order is dependency, not importance.

### Q4 - Continuum versus discreteness is a leftover pair, not a pair of substances

- Fig 13 :43 "A continuum was the seventh and was removed for failing exactly this" and Fig 13 :44 "A generating set must not contain what its own members produce."
- Fig 13 :124-125 "Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are each what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member."
- So "continuous source" and "discrete source" are not two candidate atoms to choose between. They are two possible leftovers of different compositions. Asking which one "is real" before specifying the composition is a category error of the same kind as asking whether smoothness is an element of the six.
- This dissolves the apparent conflict between the classical metric picture and the discrete-spectrum programs in the world literature: they are different compositions leaving different residues, and the ground decides which residue this world returns.
- **Strain, named:** Fig 13 :44 removes the continuum as a generating member, but it does not say the continuum is unreal; it says it is produced. Similarly Fig 13 :124-125 does not say discreteness is guaranteed. The verdict is about membership in the generating set, not about ontology. Held as a strain, not smoothed.

## CONCLUSION

"Charge" names a coupling role, not a substance. Under the six-term substrate the electric and gravitational sources are members of a functional group (state with a structure) rather than isomers as the question currently phrases them; a real isomer claim is available if both are written over the full shared atom set. The order of the two halves is dependency order, with the vocabulary link the likely break. Continuum and discreteness are composition leftovers, so the quantization question is a composition question settled by an instrument, not a choice between primitives.
