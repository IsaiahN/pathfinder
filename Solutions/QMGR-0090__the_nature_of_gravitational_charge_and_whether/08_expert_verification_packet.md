## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The word "charge" in this question is borrowed from electromagnetism, where it is a signed conserved scalar with a known quantization mechanism. Gravity's source is positive energy-momentum, a rank-two tensor coupling to a spin-2 field, so "charge" names a coupling role rather than a substance. Conserved energy is already an atom of the six-term substrate, so the nature half needs no new primitive. On the quantization half, the substrate holds neither the continuum nor discreteness as a primitive: both are what a composition leaves behind. The well-posed question is therefore whether a settled composition of the substrate leaves a discrete residue in the gravitational source spectrum. The classical closure cannot derive that answer; it requires an import from the quantum frame, and the residue, if any, is fixed by an instrument. The framework returns that shape and the falsifiers, not a graviton mass or a quantization unit.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that gravitational charge is a scalar like electric charge.
- Not that the Dirac monopole mechanism transfers to gravity.
- Not that the graviton exists, that a specific quantum gravity program is correct, or that an active source mass is operator-valued.
- Not that single graviton detection would prove quantization. The Nature source says in its own text that the experiment "cannot be used as proof of the quantization of gravity."
- Not a specific quantization unit, mass scale or Planck-scale value. The framework returns "a kind of thing, never the answer itself" (Fig 9 :32).
- Not a theory of everything. No last room.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

An instrument, not an argument.

1. A source-spectrum discriminator: does the gravitational source resolve into discrete levels where the quantum frame predicts them, or remain continuous at every resolution? Candidate proposals: single-graviton absorption in a ground-state resonator (Tobar et al., 2024) and active/passive mass self-acceleration with energy superpositions (Fragkos and Pikovski, 2025/2026).
2. A determination of whether a quantum source mass produces a gravitational field, or whether only a semiclassical expectation value sources it.
3. A derivation of a seventh primitive, or of a signed scalar gravitational charge, would refute the shape from the other side.
