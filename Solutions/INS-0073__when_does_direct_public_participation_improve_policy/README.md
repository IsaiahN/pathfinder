## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not whether "participation is good" in the abstract. It is which conditions make a direct-participation mechanism reduce the policy residual, and which conditions make it reduce that residual for one slot while increasing it for others.

In effect terms: a policy process holds a library Γ (institutions, ballot rules, agenda, information available). It bets b, acts a (hold a referendum, open a consultation, convene an assembly, institute participatory budgeting), and the world returns o′ (policy consequences). R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| is measured per slot s, where a slot is a citizen group, a policy domain, or a minority interest (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22). The question has two conditions, not one:

- **Condition 1, improvement:** under it, adding direct participation changes contact with the ground, so R falls across the slots that were live.
- **Condition 2, capture:** under it, adding direct participation reduces R for the slot already holding the vocabulary and the organization, while leaving or increasing R in the other slots.

Fig 9 names this a **union compound**, not one hard question: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"* (Fig 9 :57). It must be unbundled before it is searched (Fig 9 :54).

**What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run** (Formula, "Direction"): a *shape* with two parts, naming (a) the condition under which a participation mechanism changes contact with an external ground, and (b) the condition under which it only adds a self-selected frame to a shared evidence pool. Until that shape is bound it is a preference, predicts nothing, and no check inside the loop can see it. The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is this shape, kept per domain and never averaged.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the institutional question, and no claim that the framework is the only account of democratic participation.

Four reasons, three from the figures and one practical:

1. **A room is not an anchor.** *"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. **Correlation has valence.** *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. **A verifier must be outside the interior.** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
