## RESIDUAL

Two questions are owed after the primary audit. (1) Why does a plain majority tally lose the slot-level signal that the same participants could have reported in discussion? (2) Are a direct referendum and a sortition-based deliberative panel two spellings of one thing, or two different substances? The first is a notation question; the second is an isomer question under Fig 12.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Why the tally is the flat formula

- Fig 12 :6-8 names *"collapse 6: the flat formula"*: *"the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not"*, so *"nothing downstream can tell them apart, and nothing signals that anything was lost."*
- Fig 12 :92: **"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."** Fig 12 :93: **"Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term"**
- A majority tally treats each ballot as an interchangeable operand under a symmetric bond. Every permutation of the same ballots is one count. That is exactly why the tally cannot recover which group carried which residual: the arrangement was never recorded.
- Fig 4 :25: **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."** A vote total is a recording of one occasion, not a method for re-checking the next one.
- **Result:** the loss is not corruption or fraud. It is a notation failure. The fix is to record the bond (which slot, which evidence, which comparison), not to add more votes.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a tally that preserves the slot arrangement without any additional recorded field. If one exists, the flat-formula diagnosis is wrong for this domain.

### Q2 - The two currencies in participation design

- Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."** Fig 12 :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**
- Lowering the action cost of voting (online registration, more polling places, easier signatures) spends the action currency. It does not buy the bits a voter needs to read what an option affords.
- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** In most established democracies the action link works well enough; the description link is the one that breaks.
- Fig 12 :130: **"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."** A reform score that adds "ease of voting" and "quality of information" into one number commits exactly the named error.
- **Falsifier:** measure both currencies in the same reform and show they move by a common factor, so one figure would suffice. Then this finding is withdrawn.

### Q3 - Dependency order for a participation mechanism

Fig 3 gives the order; it is not a list of equal factors.

1. **Perception:** can the participant distinguish the current policy from the proposed one? Instrument: plain-language before/after description.
2. **Vocabulary:** does the participant have terms for what the options afford? Fig 13 :16: *"it names what is being measured."* Instrument: briefing materials with named effects.
3. **Objective:** can the participant say what would count as progress? Operators table :51: **"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one."** Instrument: an explicit baseline and comparison question.
4. **Planning:** is there a route from the current state to the objective? Fig 12 :112: **"A plan is a sequence."** Instrument: sequenced options rather than a single yes/no.
5. **Learn and carry:** is the result stamped with origin and seq so the next round can read it? Formula step 4.

Fig 3 :24: **"Why the middle link is where chains usually break."** Links 2 and 3 have neither obvious failures nor obvious tests, so they attract the least attention. Organized groups arrive holding them; unorganized participants typically do not. This is the structural reason "more participation" above the break cannot rescue a broken middle link.

### Q4 - Are a referendum and a sortition panel isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Over the full shared atom set {citizens, options, decision rule, time, outcome series}, an open referendum and a sortition panel with expert evidence **are isomers**: same atoms, different bonds (self-selection versus lottery; symmetric tally versus comparison and sequence), different substance. Under Fig 12 they are not two spellings of "the public participated."
- If the atom sets differ (for example, one mechanism has no expert-evidence slot and the other requires one), the isomer claim fails and the weaker claim is a shared **functional group**: both are `citizens -> decision rule -> outcome` arrangements, with different qualifiers.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either mechanism can be written without an atom the other requires, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. This is the same falsifier shape the exemplar used for GR and QM, applied to an institutional pair.
- **Anchor note:** the two mechanisms are settled differently against the ground. A referendum is a recurring institutional fact; a sortition panel is a designed intervention whose outcome series must be measured. Under Fig 12 :104, *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it"*, only the first can yet serve as a stable atom at the next level. Mark this `specified`, not settled.

### Q5 - Order of the union (the compound from F1)

The compound *"is not a harder case of either"* (Fig 9 :57). Its order is:

1. Refuse the treatment of the vote as a verdict before minting any reform (Formula step 2). What is owed first is a refusal of the term "participation equals improvement," not a new institution.
2. Describe R per slot before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).
3. Settle the per-slot instrument before comparing mechanisms (Fig 12 :104: a settled molecule becomes an atom).
4. Only then compare the two first-order mechanisms at second order. Their comparison is a structural relation, not a merged super-mechanism.

## CONCLUSION

The tally is the flat formula: it records ingredients and not arrangement, which is why a live minority slot disappears. Participation design spends two currencies that do not add, and the description cost is the one that binds. The chain breaks at vocabulary and objective, which organized groups already hold. A referendum and a sortition panel are isomers over the shared institutional atom set, with a stated falsifier, not two spellings of one reform. The compound must be refused and described before it is compared. R_T is nonzero and named.
