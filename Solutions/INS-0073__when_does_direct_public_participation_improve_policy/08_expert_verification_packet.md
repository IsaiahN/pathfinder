# Expert verification packet - the Tether on direct public participation

**What this is:** a claim in a form a political scientist, institutional designer or public-policy researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No policy outcome is claimed as measured. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Direct public participation improves policy and privileges the best-organized groups under different conditions, and the two must be checked separately because a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. The shape has four constraints and one refusal. (1) The vote must remain a convention for coordinating shared meaning and answer to an external ground, because a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one. (2) The participant pool must be unauthored (lottery, everyone who qualifies, or a pre-existing set) and the frames must draw on different evidence. (3) The outcome residual must be read per citizen group and policy domain, not averaged into one tally. (4) The mechanism must supply sequence, comparison and progress, because a list of options can name a state and cannot name a route. The refusal: the framework does not name which concrete reform wins. An organized group is not a kind of actor but a relation to a residual, so the same group can help one policy and capture another.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 2 and 10

- **Statement:** a vote is a legitimate coordination convention but not a legitimate anchor for policy quality; the aggregate tally cannot show which slot is live.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :47 - **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**; Fig 2 :30 - **"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor"**; Fig 1 :19 - **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated"**; Fig 1 :22 - **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**
- **What to check:** is the distinction between a coordination convention and an outcome-deciding verdict coherent as stated? Does the per-slot reading actually change which mechanism you would choose?
- **Refuter:** exhibit a vote total that is invariant under agenda control, framing, spending and turnout changes, and a case in which all per-slot residuals fall while the aggregate is unchanged with no live slot. Either fires C1.

### C2 - institutional-facing; checkable against a mechanism the respondent knows

- **Statement:** a self-selected participation pool is an authored room; a lottery or an "everyone who qualifies" rule is the named mechanism for an unauthored one; the organized advantage is concentrated description cost, not a standing property of a group.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :73-74 - **"A candidate set chosen by a participant is an authored room however well the selection is reasoned"**; Fig 10 :76-78 - **"a lottery everyone who qualifies a set assembled before the question existed"**; Fig 11 :49 - **"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
- **What to check:** in a consultation you know, were the participants self-selected, and did the outcome-relevant vocabulary sit with a narrow set? Did the same organization help in one domain and capture in another?
- **Refuter:** a self-selected pool statistically indistinguishable from the affected population on outcome-relevant variables with per-group outcomes improving afterward; or an actor whose valence is invariant across two different residuals in the same habitat. Either fires C2.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation with an empirical edge

- **Statement:** participation design spends two currencies that do not add; lowering the action cost of voting cannot pay the description cost, so a reform that optimizes only accessibility leaves the middle dependency link broken.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :125 - **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**; Fig 12 :126 - **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**; Fig 12 :130 - **"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."**; Fig 3 :24 - **"Why the middle link is where chains usually break."**
- **What to check:** can a reform be found that lowers action cost and also lowers description cost by a common measurable factor? If so, the two-currency claim is wrong here.
- **Refuter:** a single measured factor that accounts for both costs, or an atom-only ballot that produces stable per-slot outcome improvement across several domains with no sequence, comparison or progress supplied. Either fires C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a theory of everything and not a final verdict on democratic theory. The question is a union compound with its own loop.
- Not that participation always privileges the organized. Local rule-making can work, and the anchor can legitimately be human outcomes (Fig 2 :36-38).
- Not that organized groups are bad by kind (Fig 11 :49-51).
- Not that sortition, deliberative polling or participatory budgeting is *the* answer. Those are provenanced bindings from an outside frame, not derivations.
- Not that a vote is useless. As a convention for shared meaning it is efficient (Fig 2 :41).
- Not `grounded`. No policy outcome was measured in this derivation.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. The instruments already exist:

1. **Push test:** does the vote move under framing, agenda control, campaign spending and turnout changes?
2. **Per-slot outcome series:** which citizen group and policy domain carries the live residual before and after the intervention?
3. **Evidence-overlap measure:** how much do participants draw on the same sources?
4. **Contact audit:** which habitat actors, conditions and relations changed, and which effects appeared three steps out?
5. **The six standing refuters** in `01` section 6, any one of which withdraws the corresponding finding.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 10 :47, Fig 2 :30 and Fig 1 :19-22 (four short passages).
2. Check C2 against one participation process you know: was the pool self-selected, and did the relevant vocabulary sit with a few actors?
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish a convention from a verdict in that process, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
