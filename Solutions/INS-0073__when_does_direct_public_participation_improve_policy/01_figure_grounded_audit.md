## 1. The residual, stated in effect terms

A policy process holds Γ: institutions, ballot rules, agenda rules, information. It acts a (call a referendum, open a consultation, convene a citizens' assembly, institute participatory budgeting) and observes o′ (what the policy actually did). R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| is indexed per slot s: a citizen group, a policy domain, a minority interest. The sought-for shape is a two-part condition: when the mechanism changes contact with the ground, and when it only re-weights the frames already in the room.

Three things must be kept distinct when routing R (Formula step 2, which asks SEARCHED first, then INWARD, then OUTWARD):

- **SEARCHED:** is the answer already in the frames held by direct democracy research? Yes, in part. Sortition, deliberative polling and per-slot measurement are all already in the literature. The framework does not need a new institution; it needs the conditions under which the existing ones work.
- **INWARD:** sharpen the instruments already returning something (Fig 6 :32-35): per-group outcome measurement, turnout and participation breakdowns, deliberation pre/post opinion change. All exist; none is a sensor built from a description.
- **OUTWARD:** import only where a frame with a differing closure holds a term this one lacks (Fig 6 :23). The social-choice results (independence, aggregation reversal) and the collective-action result (concentrated interests organize) come from outside the framework. They are corroboration, not the verdict, and they are debited against independence.

**No figure line governs after re-census?** No. Every domain below closes with a verbatim figure line. There is no `gap` in this solution. That is a finding, not an achievement claim: a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence (Fig 8 :39).

## 2. Domain census

### 2.1 R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** two conditions (improvement, capture), so a **union compound** (Fig 9 :57). The improvement half and the capture half are not one question with two answers. They are two overlapping conditions, and a single lever (more participation) clears the region where only one holds and leaves the intersection standing (Fig 9 :55).
- **Symmetry:** participation is not a symmetric re-spelling of the same frame. Adding participants changes the closure in play, so order and membership matter. A plain majority tally, by contrast, is the flat case: under a symmetric bond *"k operands have k! spellings and one term"* (Fig 12 :93), so all the spellings collapse into one count and the arrangement is lost.
- **Scale:** first order at the individual vote; second order at the mechanism design; third order at the habitat (campaigns, money, media, cascades). Fig 4 supplies the membrane rule: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* (Fig 4 :22).

### 2.2 One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type | verdict status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Decision mechanism, convention vs verdict | voters, ballots, agenda setters, quorum rules | preference aggregation and coordination | outcomes external to the vote; the vote itself moves when pushed, so it is an instrument, not an anchor | partition (which designs cross from convention to decision) | triangulated, low overlap |
| D2 Independence and the participant pool | participants, campaigns, media, expert frames, affected groups | evidence overlap | policy outcomes; frames range against it separately | union (surplus only where closures differ) | triangulated, low overlap |
| D3 Residual measurement, per slot | citizen groups, policy domains, minority slots | R⁺_s, the live mass in a slot | measured outcomes per slot | partition (per-slot, never aggregated) | triangulated, low overlap |
| D4 Habitat and contact | interest groups, institutions, rules, money, the reformer who introduces the mechanism | contact with the ground; introduction versus exclusion | outcomes; the ground does not decay, the channel does | union compound with cascades | triangulated, low overlap |
| D5 Vocabulary and dependency order | voters' affordance terms and objective terms | bonds versus bare atoms | the middle link of the chain (Fig 3 :24-27) | dependency order, not importance | triangulated, low overlap |

**No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.** Each row has one. D1's anchor is not the vote: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks"* (Fig 2 :29) and *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor"* (Fig 2 :30). A campaign can push a vote; it cannot push a measured policy outcome. D2 to D5 use that same external ground, always read separately by each frame.

### 2.3 Partition or union

D1 is a partition: a mechanism either stays a convention or decides an outcome. D3 is a partition: R is per slot, and averaging across slots is a different operation. D2, D4 and the question as a whole are unions: two frames with differing closures, or two conditions that overlap. Unbundle before searching (Fig 9 :54).

### 2.4 Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. **Perception:** can participants tell what is in front of them and distinguish it from what was there before?
2. **Vocabulary:** do they have terms for what the options afford?
3. **Objective:** can they say what would count as progress, in their own terms?
4. **Planning:** is there a route to that objective?
5. **Learn and carry:** name what worked, verify it, carry it.

The chain is *"ordered by dependency, not by importance"* (Fig 3 :20). *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not"* (Fig 3 :19). The middle link is where chains usually break (Fig 3 :24); organized groups tend to hold it, unorganized participants often do not. Refusal comes before mint (Formula step 2), and the residual is described before the frame is chosen (Fig 9 :27-28).

## 4. Findings

### F1  -  The question is a union compound and must be unbundled (structural, decisive for method)

Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

The two halves of the question overlap. A mechanism can improve aggregate welfare (condition 1) and still privilege an organized slot (condition 2), because the aggregate and the slot are different readings. A single lever, "add more participation," clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing. Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."** Turnout, number of votes cast, and number of participants are budget filters. They are not witnesses for policy quality.

**Refuter:** show that the two conditions are the same quantity under a single measurement, such that every rise in participation quality necessarily and measurably lowers capture in every slot. Then it is one condition, not a union, and this finding is withdrawn.

### F2  -  Participation is a convention; it stops being one when it decides an outcome (D1, decisive)

Fig 2 :41: **"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"**
Fig 2 :43: **"Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."**
Fig 10 :47: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**
Fig 10 :49: **"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."**
Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**

A vote is a convention: a shared rule for coordinating what "the public wants" means. As a convention it is efficient, and agreement about that shared meaning is the product. It becomes a verdict the moment it is asked to decide the policy outcome. At that point it has stopped being a convention, and it needs a ground beyond itself. If the electorate is treated as the ground while also being a party to the process, then *"A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame"* (Fig 2 :47).

This is the answer to the improvement half in one sentence: **direct participation improves policy when it coordinates preference and remains answerable to an external ground; it fails when its own output is taken as the settlement.**

**Status: triangulated.** The convention/outcome rule (Fig 10 :47) and the anchor/mutable-instrument rule (Fig 2 :29-30) are different figures with low overlap. A third, lower-overlap witness is the seat map's *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"* (Seat Map, "Correlation has valence"), which is the same law at the checking layer.

**Refuter:** exhibit a participation mechanism whose vote total does not move under framing, agenda control, campaign spending, or turnout changes, and that is nonetheless used only to coordinate meaning. If a vote cannot be pushed, it is a legitimate anchor for "what the public says" and the Fig 2 :30 bar does not bite. Our claim would then be narrowed, not erased: it would still need the ground for policy quality.

### F3  -  Self-selection authors the room; an unauthored pool is the condition for not privileging the organized (D2, decisive for the capture half)

Fig 10 :73: **"The residual selects the members, but only from what is available."**
Fig 10 :73-74: **"A candidate set chosen by a participant is an authored room however well the selection is reasoned, so the pool must be unauthored first and examined second."**
Fig 10 :74: **"Reverse the order and the stacking is the selection."**
Fig 10 :75: **"A conscientious selection is still a selection, so unauthored needs a mechanism:"**
Fig 10 :76-78: **"a lottery everyone who qualifies a set assembled before the question existed"** (the extract prints the three mechanisms on separate lines; the quotation joins them with the extract's whitespace)
Fig 7 :36: **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."**

An open participation channel is a self-selected pool. The people who walk in are, by construction, the ones with the time, vocabulary, and stake to do so. Fig 10 :74 calls that an authored room, and the authorship is done by the participant, not by the organizer, so good intentions do not fix it. The figure names the mechanisms that make a pool unauthored: lottery, everyone who qualifies, a set assembled before the question existed, or the habitat enumerated (Fig 10 :77-79). Sortition and random representative sampling are the public-field instances of this rule, and they are the reason deliberative polling is structurally different from an open meeting.

**Status: triangulated.** Fig 10 :73-79 governs *how the room is filled*; Fig 7 :35-36 governs *what a room of similar frames produces*. Low overlap, different objects. Fig 8 :48 is a corroborating witness, but it is the same quantity read again and is not counted twice (see F4).

**Refuter:** show a self-selected participation event whose participants are statistically indistinguishable, across the outcome-relevant variables, from the full population the policy affects, and whose residual stays small per slot after the event. Then self-selection did not author a captured room in that case, and the condition is narrowed to domains where the valence of participation is uneven.

### F4  -  Shared evidence disqualifies agreement; independence is bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong (D2, decisive)

Fig 2 :31: **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."**
Fig 2 :40: **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**
Fig 2 :42: **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."**
Fig 8 :47: **"Independence is one quantity read twice."**
Fig 8 :48: **"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."**
Fig 13 :38-39: **"If two members drew on the same underlying fact, their agreement would carry no information, and one of them would be redundant rather than minimal."**

A mass consultation where everyone read the same campaign material is one evidence pool speaking with many mouths. Adding more such participants buys more spelling, not more search. The world source on groupshift (social psychology) predicts that discussion among already-leaning members pushes them further toward the same extreme, which is the same collapse from a different field. The world source on shared information bias predicts that groups spend more time on what everyone already knows and neglect the unique information a minority holds, which is the per-slot loss of D3 from a different field.

**Counting rule, stated honestly:** Fig 2 :40 and Fig 8 :48 are explicitly *"one quantity read twice"* (Fig 8 :47), so they are **one witness**, not two. The second independent witness for D2 is Fig 10 :73-79 (selection mechanism). Agreement between Fig 2 and Fig 8 is internal consistency inside one framework, not corroboration.

**Refuter:** measure the evidence overlap between participants and show it is near zero while their agreement remains stable and their per-slot residual stays small. Under low overlap the agreement means something; our claim that overlap disqualifies it would not apply, and the verdict would fall to D4 and D5.

### F5  -  R is per slot; an aggregate hides the live minority slot (D3, decisive)

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**
Fig 1 :13: **"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"**
Fig 1 :28: **"The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."**
Fig 7 :14: **"blending: novelty swamped to the mean"**

This is the cleanest figure-grounded statement of the capture half. A direct vote produces a single aggregate. If 60 percent prefer option A and 40 percent carry a large live residual under A, the aggregate reads as a healthy decision while the 40-percent slot is exactly where the damage lands. Organized groups are favored not mainly by fraud but by the arithmetic of the tally: the symmetric count reports the mean and discards the slot. *"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing"* (Fig 12 :92); under the symmetric tally, k voter spellings are one term, so the arrangement that carries the harm is unrecorded.

The required instrument is not a better argument but a different reading: R reported per citizen group and per policy domain before and after the mechanism (INWARD, Fig 6 :29). That instrument already exists in survey and evaluation practice.

**Status: triangulated.** Fig 1 :19-28 governs measurement; Fig 7 :14 governs what blending does to a chain; Fig 12 :92-93 governs why the tally cannot carry the arrangement. Three figures, low overlap. The statistics source on Simpson's reversal is a world corroboration of the same arithmetic, not a fourth witness for the verdict.

**Refuter:** provide a case where the per-slot residuals all fall after the vote but the aggregate is unchanged, with no slot carrying positive mass. Then the aggregate was not hiding anything in that case, and the per-slot condition is not the operative one there.

### F6  -  Capability lives in the agent and habitat; introduction is available, exclusion is not (D4, decisive)

Fig 11 :40: **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**
Fig 11 :61: **"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."**
Fig 11 :63: **"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."**
Fig 11 :52-53: **"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**
Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**

Giving everyone a ballot improves the agent in isolation if it does not change what the decision process is in contact with. The habitat (organized interests, media, money, agenda control, the rules that define what counts as an option) is not authored by the reformer. Introduction is available: a reformer can add a sortition panel, an expert evidence session, an affected-group testimony slot. Exclusion is not: the reformer cannot subtract the existing habitat by decree. So a participation reform is permanent maintenance, not a solved state, and its second-order and third-order effects are exactly where the capture residual appears. The framework's own example is biomagnification: the residual that mattered was three steps out.

A recording moved across the boundary fails here too. *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion"* (Fig 4 :25). Publishing a vote tally is a recording; carrying up the method by which the tally was checked against outcomes is a generator.

**Status: triangulated.** Fig 11 :40-63 governs contact and asymmetry; Fig 4 :22-25 governs what crosses between scales. Low overlap.

**Refuter:** demonstrate a participation reform that improved outcomes while leaving the habitat's actors, conditions and relations measurably unchanged, with a maintained effect that did not require further work. That would refute the contact claim and would be a genuine return to census, not a softening.

### F7  -  The organized group is a relation, not a kind (D4, valence rule)

Fig 11 :49: **"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
Fig 11 :50: **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**
Fig 11 :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

A labor union, an industry association, or a neighborhood group is not "the best-organized group" by kind. It is a benefactor to a residual it has the vocabulary to register, and a malfactor to a residual it can absorb into its own frame. Change the residual and its valence changes. This is why the framework refuses a standing blacklist of organized interests, and why the verdict has to be per domain. This finding is also the reason F6 cannot be stated as "unorganized good, organized bad."

**Refuter:** show an actor whose valence is invariant across at least two different residuals in the same habitat, measured by its effect on R. That would break the relation claim and force a re-census of D4.

### F8  -  A ballot of atoms cannot name a route; organized groups bring the grammar (D5, decisive)

Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**
Fig 12 :112: **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."**
Fig 12 :115: **"having terms for what a situation affords was taken for a dictionary and it is a grammar."**
Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**
Operators table :51: **"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"**

A yes/no ballot is a list of ingredients. It names states (approve, reject) and cannot name a route, a goal, or progress. Organized groups arrive with the bonds already in hand: the sequence (what happens first), the comparison (better than what baseline), and the subtraction (how far from the goal). Unorganized participants who hold only the dictionary are structurally unable to supply those bonds in the time a consultation allows. So "more participation" at the level of atom provision cannot rescue a broken middle link (Fig 3 :19). The remedy at the middle link is a mechanism that elicits bonds: briefing materials that state the baseline and the alternatives, moderated small-group discussion, and a question that asks what would count as progress. Those are not new atoms; they are the bonds the vocabulary was missing.

**Status: triangulated.** Fig 12 :111-115 governs the grammar; Fig 3 :19-27 governs the dependency order and the middle link. Low overlap. The Operators table supplies the token reading of a goal as a comparison.

**Refuter:** show a bare atom-only ballot that nevertheless produces stable, per-slot reduction in R across several policy domains, with no separate mechanism supplying sequence, comparison, or progress. Then the grammar condition is not necessary in those domains.

### F9  -  Two currencies: lowering the action cost does not pay the description cost (D5, supporting)

Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**
Fig 12 :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**
Fig 12 :130: **"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."**

Making it easier to vote lowers the action cost of participation. It does nothing to the description cost: the bits the voter must already hold to read the option. Organized groups have paid those bits over years; unorganized participants have not. A reform that optimizes only the action axis (online voting, same-day registration, more polling places) improves the part that already works and leaves the middle link broken (Fig 3 :19). Both readings must be taken or the choice is made blind.

**Refuter:** measure description cost and action cost in the same reform and show they move together by a common factor, so one figure would in fact suffice. That would refute the two-currency claim in this domain.

### F10  -  Participation volume is not a measure of success (method and outreach)

Fig 5 :48: **"Its own output is never a measure of success. A system can be enthusiastically curious and produce nothing."**
Fig 5 :49: **"Machinery firing is not the milestone. A running system can mint and still return nothing new, which is exactly what the third guard exists to record."**
Fig 1 :27: **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."**

A consultation with 50,000 submissions has produced a lot of activity and may have produced no change in contact. The framework explicitly disqualifies frame-internal quantities: coverage and compression achieved, predicates minted and credibility accrued. High turnout is in the same family. It is a filter for the budget, not a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).

**Refuter:** provide a case where the volume of participation is itself the mechanism that changes contact with the ground (for example, where reaching a signature threshold legally forces a source-discriminating inquiry), with a measured drop in R attributable to the volume. Then volume is a witness in that case.

### F11  -  What survives: per-domain verdicts, never averaged

| domain | condition for improvement | condition for capture | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 mechanism | vote stays a convention and is checked against an external ground | vote output is taken as the settlement | triangulated |
| D2 pool and frames | unauthored pool, low-overlap evidence, ground read separately | self-selected room, shared evidence pool, mirror chain | triangulated |
| D3 measurement | R read per slot, live slot addressed | aggregate reported as if it were the whole signal | triangulated |
| D4 habitat | reform changes contact and is maintained as permanent work | "improve the agent in isolation"; habitat persists; cascades three steps out | triangulated |
| D5 grammar | mechanism supplies sequence, comparison, progress | atom-only ballot; organized group supplies the bonds | triangulated |

These verdicts are not combined into a single score for "participation." A mechanism can pass D1 and fail D5, which is the compound the question asks about.

## 5. Strain test and re-census

**Strain A, resolved.** Fig 2 :41 appears to endorse agreement (*"efficient for a convention"*) while Fig 2 :40 disqualifies it (*"disqualifying for a verdict"*). Re-census: they govern different objects. Agreement about shared meaning is the product of a convention; agreement about the world is worthless when the frames share evidence. The figures are consistent; the apparent strain came from reading "agreement" as one quantity. No verdict changed.

**Strain B, resolved.** Fig 2 :36-37 says human judgment is the right anchor when the question is constitutively about people, which a policy question is. Fig 2 :29-30 says an anchor must not move when pushed, and a vote does move under framing. Re-census: the legitimate anchor is human *outcomes* (what happened to people), and the ballot is an *instrument* for estimating preference, which *"can be wrong on axes they do not measure"* (Fig 2 :37-38). Both lines govern; no strain after re-census.

**Strain C, live and named.** Fig 11 :49-51 says valence is relative to the residual, yet the question asks whether participation systematically privileges "the best-organized groups," which sounds like a standing kind. Re-census resolves the kind claim (F7), but the empirical question of whether the same organized actors recur across residuals in a given polity needs a measurement, not an argument. Recorded as a live empirical limit, not a figure conflict.

After re-census: two strains dissolved, one empirical limit named, none hidden.

## 6. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Seat Map, failure genera: **"And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."** The shape claim is refuted if any of the following is measured:

1. A participation mechanism whose vote total is invariant under agenda control, framing, campaign spending and turnout changes **and** whose output is used only to coordinate meaning, with no external ground consulted. This breaks F2 as stated for that mechanism.
2. A self-selected participant pool that is statistically indistinguishable from the affected population on outcome-relevant variables, with per-slot R falling afterward. This breaks F3.
3. A finding that participants' evidence overlays almost completely and their agreement still tracks measured outcomes per slot across repeated cases. This breaks F4.
4. A case where all per-slot residuals fall but the aggregate is unchanged, with no live slot. This breaks F5.
5. A participation reform that improves outcomes while habitat actors, conditions and relations are measurably unchanged and no maintenance is required afterward. This breaks F6.
6. An atom-only ballot that produces stable per-slot R reduction across several domains with no mechanism supplying sequence, comparison or progress. This breaks F8.

If any fires, the relevant domain finding is withdrawn, not softened, and the round trip R_T is recomputed. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 7. Recommendation

1. Report R per slot before and after any participation intervention; never lead with the aggregate (F5).
2. Treat the vote as a convention and name the external ground that settles policy quality (F2).
3. Fill the room by lottery, by everyone who qualifies, or by a set assembled before the question existed; never by self-selection alone (F3).
4. Keep participant frames low-overlap and source-discriminating; buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong (F4).
5. Supply the grammar in the mechanism itself: baseline, comparison, route, progress (F8, F9).
6. Budget for permanent habitat maintenance and enumerate second- and third-order effects before introducing an actor (F6, F7).
7. Lead outreach with the two conditions and the refuter, not with the framework (F10; Fig 10 :52 **"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."**).

## 10. Summary of the derivation

The two halves of the question are a union compound. Direct participation improves policy when it remains a coordination convention answerable to an external ground, when the room is unauthored, when frames are independent and read the ground separately, when R is reported per slot, when the reform changes contact with the habitat and is maintained, and when the mechanism supplies the grammar a bare ballot lacks. It privileges the best-organized groups under the complementary conditions: self-selected pool, shared evidence, aggregate reporting, agent-only improvement, and atom-only ballots. The organized group is a relation to a residual, not a kind. Every verdict is `specified`, triangulated within the figures, and awaiting a ground reading.
