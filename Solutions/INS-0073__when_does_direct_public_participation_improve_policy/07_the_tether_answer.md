# The Tether's answer: when direct participation helps and when it privileges the organized

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 8), not a ground reading. No policy outcome was measured in this run.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question bundles two overlapping conditions. *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"* (Fig 9 :57). So there is no single verdict on "participation." There are two sets of conditions, read per domain, never averaged. The sought-for shape was bound before the run: a contact condition and a pool condition, with a per-slot instrument and a grammar condition.

## 2. The shape, per domain

1. **The mechanism must remain a convention and answer to an external ground.** A vote coordinates what "the public wants" mean, and agreement about that meaning is the product (Fig 2 :41). But *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"* (Fig 10 :47), and *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor"* (Fig 2 :30). Framing, agenda control and turnout push a vote; they cannot push a measured outcome. Improvement happens when the vote is a coordination device under a ground nobody in the process can move. Capture happens when the vote's own output is taken as the settlement.

2. **The pool must be unauthored and the frames low-overlap.** *"A candidate set chosen by a participant is an authored room however well the selection is reasoned"* (Fig 10 :73-74). An open participation channel is self-selected, so the people who arrive are those with the time and vocabulary to arrive. The figure names the honest mechanisms: *"a lottery everyone who qualifies a set assembled before the question existed"* (Fig 10 :76-78). Capture also follows when participants share an evidence pool: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40).

The essay *How a Society of Agents Would Work* adds: *the essay claim that a group is only worth having while its members stay different*.

3. **R must be read per slot.** *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated"* (Fig 1 :19), and *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average"* (Fig 1 :22). A single tally can be healthy while a minority slot is live: *"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"* (Fig 1 :13). The instrument that closes this is per-group outcome measurement before and after the intervention, not a larger consultation.

4. **The reform must change contact with the habitat and be maintained.** *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves"* (Fig 11 :40). *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not"* (Fig 11 :61), so the reformer can add a sortition panel or an expert evidence slot but cannot subtract the existing interests. *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state"* (Fig 11 :63). Effects cascade: *"the residual that mattered was three steps out"* (Fig 11 :52-53).

5. **The mechanism must supply the grammar a ballot lacks.** *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one"* (Fig 12 :111), and *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction"* (Fig 12 :112). A yes/no ballot offers atoms. Organized groups arrive with sequence, baseline and progress already in hand. Hence the middle link of the dependency chain, *"Why the middle link is where chains usually break"* (Fig 3 :24), is the place where capture is manufactured. Fig 12 :130 - *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it"* - rules out a single reform score.

6. **The organized group is a relation, not a kind.** *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries"* (Fig 11 :49), and *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual"* (Fig 11 :51). The same association can supply the vocabulary that corrects an error in one domain and capture the agenda in another. This is why no domain verdict is averaged with another.

## 3. Verdicts kept separate

| condition | improvement when | capture when | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| mechanism | vote is a convention under an external ground | vote output is the settlement | triangulated |
| pool and frames | unauthored pool, low-overlap evidence, ground read separately | self-selection, one evidence pool, mirror chain | triangulated |
| measurement | R per slot, live slot addressed | aggregate treated as the whole signal | triangulated |
| habitat | contact changed and work maintained | agent-only improvement, habitat persists, cascade unenumerated | triangulated |
| grammar | sequence, comparison and progress supplied by the mechanism | atom-only ballot, bonds supplied by the organized | triangulated |

No average is computed. A mechanism can pass the first row and fail the fifth, which is the compound the question asks about.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that participation is always capture. Local rule-making can work: the Ostrom source in `04` shows communities establishing sustainable rules, and Fig 2 :36 says human judgment is the right anchor when the question is constitutively about people.
- Not that organized groups are bad by kind (Fig 11 :49-51).
- Not that any named reform, sortition, deliberative polling or participatory budgeting, is *the* answer. Naming a held term is a provenanced binding, not a derivation (`05` section 7).
- Not that the vote is useless. As a convention for shared meaning it is efficient (Fig 2 :41). The prohibition is on using it as a verdict about policy quality.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement at the ground was taken.

## 5. What would settle it - the ground

An instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* Here the corresponding readings are:

- **Push test:** does the vote total move under framing, agenda control, campaign spending and turnout changes? If it does not move, it may be a legitimate anchor for "what the public says." If it moves, it is an instrument (Fig 2 :30).
- **Per-slot outcome series:** for each citizen group and policy domain, does R fall after the intervention, and which slot carries the live mass? This is the INWARD instrument, already available (Fig 6 :29, :32-34).
- **Evidence-overlap measurement:** how much do participants draw on the same sources? Low overlap makes agreement informative; high overlap makes it one witness wearing many names (Fig 8 :47-48).
- **Contact audit:** which actors, conditions and relations changed, and which third-order effects appeared? *"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles"* (Fig 11 :52-53).
- **Standing refuters:** the six written down in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 6, registered before the conclusion.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The institutional vocabulary is a visible guess, like the six-term substrate in *They're Scanning Books*: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."* Other polities and other sensoria will fill the slots differently. The answer stops where the available instruments stop discriminating, and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity"* (canonical README). All claims stay `candidate` until a ground reading pays for them.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that direct participation is good or bad. It says the question is two conditions that must be read separately. Participation improves policy when the vote remains a convention for coordinating shared meaning and stays answerable to an outcome nobody in the process can move, when the participant pool is unauthored rather than self-selected, when frames draw on different evidence, when the outcome residual is reported per citizen group instead of as one aggregate, and when the mechanism supplies a baseline, a comparison and a route rather than a bare list of options. It privileges the best-organized groups under the complementary conditions: the vote is treated as the settlement, the room selects itself, everyone read the same campaign, the average hides the live minority slot, and the ballot offers atoms while the organized supply the grammar. The organized group is not a kind of actor but a relation to a residual, so the same group can help one policy and capture another. What is offered is the shape any candidate answer must have, the refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The center stays open until the ground settles it.
