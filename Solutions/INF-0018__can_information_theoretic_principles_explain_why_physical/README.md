## What this package decides, in effect terms

The question asks whether a principle borrowed from information theory (entropy, description length, coding, bits) can be the *source* of the form of physical law. Two options: (A) yes, information is the ground from which law-form derives; (B) no, information-theoretic quantities are a frame-internal price and a derivative of the physical substrate, so they can constrain the shape of law but cannot settle why law has that form. Each leads somewhere different. A promotes a frame-internal metric to a verdict and spends the independence of the people asked to check it. B sends a checkable shape, written refuters, and an open slot until a ground settles it.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run: a statement of which domain the information principle occupies (price, import, or ground), a verbatim figure line governing that placement, and a falsifier that would move it. The shape claim is written down and made refusable in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 before the conclusion in §7.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

The figures' reasons: Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 2 :40 *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; Fig 7 :13 *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*. Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the very correlation that makes their agreement worthless.

## Standing limitation

This is a **simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.** It cannot settle the question it reports on. The essays state the limit directly: *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself"* (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, 2026). Every substantive claim below carries its mode.
