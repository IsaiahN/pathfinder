# Expert verification packet - the Tether on defining intelligence

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher on intelligence can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The choice between defining intelligence with reference to human performance and defining it without is a union masquerading as a partition. Figure 11 names "What is intelligence?" the wrong question and substitutes the question of what makes a system capable, a property of agent-and-habitat rather than of the agent alone. A definition is a convention, not a verdict, so it may be issued but not settled by agreement and must not decide an outcome. Human performance is a legitimate anchor only where the question is constitutively about people; elsewhere it is an instrument with blind axes. Removing the reference relocates the anchor, it does not remove anchoring. What survives is a shape: contact rather than substrate, per-slot rather than averaged, a declared non-updating anchor, recorded provenance for every imported atom, and a refuter written in advance. The stop is `unreached`, not silence.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 10 and 2

- **Statement:** a definition is a convention, so agreement among definers cannot settle it, and a human-performance reference is legitimate only where the question is constitutively about people.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :46 - "A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do. The seat may author what has no truth value and nothing that does." Fig 10 :49 - "Declaring a meaning does not ground it." Fig 2 :36 - "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Fig 2 :37 - "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."
- **What to check:** does the claim over-read Fig 2 :37 into a ban on human references? It should not. The figure makes the reference right for questions constitutively about people, and instrumental elsewhere. Is the convention/verdict reading consistent with how the field actually uses working definitions (Wang 2019, read)?
- **Refuter:** a definition settled by a non-updating ground that is also used as a convention without deciding an outcome; or a demonstration that the field's human reference is constitutive for all its questions, not only the person-about-ones.

### C2 - framework-internal and historical; checkable against the figure text and the definition record

- **Statement:** removing the human-performance reference relocates the anchor rather than removing anchoring, and the "human-free" definitions are isomers over a shared atom set of agent, environment and goal.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 - "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." Fig 8 :51 - "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them." Fig 12 :117 - "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- **What to check:** Legg and Hutter's definition, "Intelligence measures an agent's ability to achieve goals in a wide range of environments", substitutes an environment ensemble for the human reference. Their own provenance (read in full at ar5iv cs/0605024) says: "We take a number of well known informal definitions of human intelligence that have been given by experts, and extract their essential features." Is that provenance enough to treat the human-free definition as an adopted import rather than an independent arrival? And do the three human-free definitions actually differ in what they exclude (see `06_isomers_and_order.md` Q1)?
- **Refuter:** a mapping of the human reference class onto an environment-ensemble term that preserves every prediction; or a re-wording derivation showing two of the three definitions are one substance, which would collapse the isomer claim.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation; checkable by reading Figures 11 and 1

- **Statement:** capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, so a definition of an agent alone is the wrong object, and the residual must stay indexed per slot.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39 - "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40 - "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 1 :19 - "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
- **What to check:** does the capability claim govern artifacts and machines, or only biological evolution? The metalaws note read this session ("the habitat, not the agent, is where capability lives") treats it as general, but an expert in machine intelligence may find the scope narrower. Does the universal-intelligence scalar Υ(π) preserve per-slot residuals? This is the folder's live strain (S3), not a settled point.
- **Refuter:** an agent-only improvement that moves the ground; or a decomposition of a general intelligence scalar that retains every live per-slot residual and cannot be saturated by a culturally valued outcome.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that intelligence is undefinable in ordinary language. Dictionaries and working definitions are legitimate conventions.
- Not that the field has failed, or that psychometrics, comparative cognition or AGI research are wrong.
- Not that human performance is always the wrong reference (C1).
- Not that a general intelligence scalar is impossible. S3 is open.
- Not that this derivation settles anything. It is simulated, `general`/`specified`, ungrounded.
- Not that the figures are a theory of everything. The canonical README states the framework is not one and "Indiscernibility is not identity" applies to any definition that merely reads correctly.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. The question to put to it: for a given agent in a given habitat, does the proposed definition leave a live per-slot residual that does not trend down, and does its anchor move when a frame pushes on it? If the anchor does not move and the residual trends down at every slot, the definition pays (Fig 5 :43, read in the audit). If the anchor moves, it was a frame, not a ground (Fig 2 :29). *The Hard Problem is About Source* supplies the precedent: an instrument reading green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :34-35, :39-40, Fig 10 :46/:49, Fig 2 :36-37, Fig 1 :19. Six short passages.
2. Check C1 against your own field: is the human reference constitutive for all its questions, or only for those about people?
3. Check C2 against the source record: did the human-free definitions extract their features from human definitions, and do they exclude different things?
4. Check C3: does the capability claim extend to artifacts, and does a general score preserve per-slot residuals? Say which of these you think is false and why.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
