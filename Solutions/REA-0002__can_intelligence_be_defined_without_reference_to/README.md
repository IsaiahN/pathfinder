# Solution REA-0002 - figure-grounded package

**Question:** Can intelligence be defined without reference to human performance?

**Source:** reasoning.txt line 5

**Section:** 1. Defining intelligence

**Mode:** `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, not `grounded`. Every substantive file states that this is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

**Question restated as an effect.** The word "intelligence" is being used as if two options exist: define it by reference to human performance, or define it without that reference. Option A makes human performance the anchor and therefore makes the term answerable to human judgment. Option B removes that anchor and must either find another one or show that no anchor is owed. What is being decided is not the wording of a definition but which ground, if any, settles the term, and whether a definition is a verdict at all. An answer would look like: a shape any definition must have, the anchor condition it must satisfy, a per-domain verdict on whether the reference to human performance is constitutive or merely instrumental, and a written failure condition. That shape is bound here as an ordinary term so it can accrue a residual like anything else (THE_FORMULA, "Direction").

**Headline verdict.** Fig 11 :34-35 calls "What is intelligence?" the wrong question and offers itself as the replacement. The answerable question is what makes a system capable, and capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone (Fig 11 :39-40). A definition of intelligence is a convention, not a verdict (Fig 10 :46, :49), and a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention (Fig 10 :47). So the yes/no choice is refused as a union of two different questions: whether a term may be defined without a human anchor, and whether the thing named by the term is agent-intrinsic. A human-performance reference is legitimate only where the question is constitutively about people (Fig 2 :36-38); otherwise it is an instrument, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure. No figure line returns a definition. The honest stop is `unreached`, not silence.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with this reading.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **A room does not upgrade the label.** The Seat Map's failure genera: "A room reaching a conclusion without the ground is agreement, and agreement among frames is a weighted average of influence. Adding people raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor." Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
2. **Selecting for likely agreement manufactures the disqualifying correlation.** Seat Map :163: "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention." A contact list ranked by receptiveness is a list selected to agree.
3. **Showing the interior before asking for a verdict spends independence.** Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source."

## Legal and venue constraint

Two of the verified venues publish explicit AI-use rules. Behavioral and Brain Sciences requires any AI use that generated text or analysed material to be declared and described at submission, and does not permit AI tools to generate the arguments or substantial portions of the text. The Journal of Artificial General Intelligence requires submissions to be based on current research rather than personal opinion or speculation. This folder is a simulated derivation, so neither packet is represented as a ground result. The two-stage email in `02` offers a check, never a submission of this text as findings.
