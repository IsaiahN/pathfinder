# The Tether's answer: can intelligence be defined without reference to human performance?

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §8), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

"Intelligence" is being used for three objects with three different anchors: a property of an agent, a performance score against a reference class, and a practice maintained by a seat in a habitat. The question as asked offers two options, with human performance or without it. That pair is a union masquerading as a partition (Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."). Fig 9 :4-5: "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." The 70-odd definitions collected by Legg and Hutter and the mutually contradictory definitions reported by Sternberg are the evidence that the disagreement does not shrink.

## 2. The verdict, in one sentence

**No, not in the sense the question intends, because the question is the wrong one; the human-performance reference is conditionally legitimate, removing it only relocates the anchor, and the answerable question is what makes a system capable, which is a property of agent-and-habitat.**

## 3. The shape, stated as constraints

1. **The definition question is refused, and the contact question substituted.** Fig 11 :34 "What is intelligence?"; Fig 11 :35 "The wrong question, and this figure is the replacement for it."; Fig 11 :39 "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :41: "Which is the same move as asking what generates lift rather than whether a thing is a bird. The substrate question dissolves; the contact question does not."
2. **A definition is a convention, not a verdict.** Fig 10 :46: "A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do. The seat may author what has no truth value and nothing that does." Fig 10 :49: "Declaring a meaning does not ground it." Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." So a definition may be *issued*, but it cannot be settled by agreement, and it must not decide who counts as intelligent.
3. **Human performance is a conditionally legitimate anchor.** Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Fig 2 :37: "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure." Where the question is constitutively about people, the reference is right. For arbitrary systems it is an instrument with blind axes, not a banned term and not a ground.
4. **Removing the reference relocates the anchor.** Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." The human-free definitions substitute environments, goals, reward or insufficient resources. Fig 8 :51: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them." Legg and Hutter's own provenance says they extracted the essential features from definitions of human intelligence, so the "human-free" definition is not independent of the human reference merely because the word is absent.
5. **The residual must stay indexed per slot.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A general score is where the live signal goes.
6. **Capability is agent-and-habitat.** Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :48: "You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."
7. **Agreement among definers is not evidence.** Fig 7 :13: "agreement compounds, evidence does not." Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."
8. **No definition is returned.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence," Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

Verdicts per domain are kept separate and never averaged: D1 (definitional) `general`, a convention question; D2 (unmaintained selection) `specified`, the word does no work there; D3 (maintained capability) `specified`, agent-and-habitat; D4 (verification) `general`, agreement cannot settle it.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that intelligence is undefinable in ordinary language. Dictionaries and working definitions are legitimate conventions.
- Not that definitions of intelligence are worthless. Wang's working definition guides research; Fig 10 :46 gives conventions a real office.
- Not that human performance is always the wrong reference. Fig 2 :37 makes it right for questions constitutively about people.
- Not that psychometrics, comparative cognition or AGI research have failed. Their disagreement is the union that F4 names.
- Not that a general intelligence scalar is impossible. Whether it preserves per-slot residuals is an open instrument question (S3).
- Not a ground reading. No measurement settles any of this.

## 5. What would settle it, and the pre-registered falsifiers

Written before the conclusion (Seat Map :287). The shape claim is refuted if:

1. a definition is produced that is settled by a non-updating ground for arbitrary agents and habitats and returns per-slot residuals rather than an aggregate;
2. Fig 11 :39-40 is shown to govern only biological evolution and not artifacts or machines, so capability can be agent-alone;
3. a source-discriminating instrument shows that human performance is not the operative anchor in the field's actual questions;
4. the field reaches one settled definition with a non-updating anchor, collapsing the union reading;
5. a demonstrably agent-only improvement moves the ground (the standing falsifier recorded in the metalaws note).

**The ground instrument owed:** something that reads, for a given agent in a given habitat, whether a definition's terms leave a live per-slot residual that does not trend down, and whether the proposed anchor moves when a frame pushes on it. That is a discriminator, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (carried from the exemplar's canonical mapping) gives the metamerism precedent: an instrument reading green "cannot comment on the source", and the spectrometer, not an argument, resolved it.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** SEARCHED: the definitional literature has been searched for a century and returned dozens of incompatible conventions; more of the same search adds no atom (Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."). INWARD: the per-slot residual is an instrument already returning readings, so it can be sharpened. OUTWARD: animal cognition, psychometrics and AI definition theory are three differing closures already imported, each with a provenance that must be recorded. INWARD and OUTWARD are opposed and were not read against each other. One live strain (the scalar versus per-slot question) and one gap (no figure line returns a definition) are recorded. "Indiscernibility is not identity" (canonical README), so a definition that reads correctly is not thereby settled.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The short answer is that the choice is malformed. Figure 11 calls "What is intelligence?" the wrong question and replaces it with what makes a system capable, which is a property of agent and habitat rather than of the agent alone. A definition is a convention, not a verdict, so it can be issued but not settled by agreement, and it must not be used to decide who counts as intelligent. Human performance is a legitimate anchor only where the question is constitutively about people; for arbitrary systems it is an instrument that can be wrong on axes it does not measure. Dropping the reference does not drop anchoring: the human-free definitions substitute environments, goals, reward or limited resources, and their provenance determines whether they are independent arrivals or imported human definitions in new notation. What this offers is not a definition but the shape any answer must have: contact rather than substrate, per-slot rather than averaged, a declared non-updating anchor, a recorded provenance, and a falsifier written before the run. The stop is unreached, not silence.
