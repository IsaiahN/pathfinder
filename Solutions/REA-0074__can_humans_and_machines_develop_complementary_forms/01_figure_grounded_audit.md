## 0. The residual in effect terms

R is the gap between what a reasoning frame predicts and what the task returns, per slot: `R_s = |Γ(b,a) - o'|`, indexed by task slot and never aggregated. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*

The question asks whether two frames, H (human) and M (machine), can stand in a relation such that their union reaches what neither reaches alone, instead of M being a copy of H. Stated in effect terms:

- **Imitation regime:** `Γ_M` is built to return H's outputs on the same tasks from the same evidence pool. Overlap is high, the union adds nothing, and agreement certifies the training, not a new reach.
- **Complementarity regime:** `closure(Γ_M)` and `closure(Γ_H)` differ, overlap is low, and there is a task ground that both range against separately. The union has a surplus.

**Anchor.** The ground is whatever settles whether a reasoning result is right on a task. Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Agent-only reports, satisfaction, and mutual agreement are not that anchor. Fig 2 :36: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."*

**Direction bound as an ordinary term.** Fig 5 :65-66 and `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction" require that the sought-for shape be written down and allowed to accrue a residual. Here the bound term is: *a union of two reasoning frames reaches a term neither frame reaches alone, on a ground both range against.* That prediction is what the rest of this audit tests.

## 2. Domain census before choosing any figure line

**Arity.** The relation is three-place (frame H, frame M, ground), indexed per task slot and ordered in time as the loop repeats. It carries a comparison bond (which frame pays on this slot), a sequence bond (delegation), and an import relation (atoms crossing between closures). These are not symmetric: swapping H and M changes the delegation, and Fig 12 :46 says which bond holds is not recoverable from the operands.

**Symmetry.** "Complementary" is not carried by either frame. It is a threshold relation between two frames relative to a residual. Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* The machine is a benefactor or a malfactor relative to the task, not by kind.

**Scale.** Three scales: one frame's loop; the union of two frames; the habitat of tasks, instruments, benchmarks and deployment conditions. The membrane between them is lossy. Fig 4 :20: *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."*

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Human reasoning loop | person, Γ_H, tacit habit | residual reduction on tasks | task ground that pays | admitted |
| D2 Machine reasoning loop | model, Γ_M, training corpus | residual reduction on tasks | the same ground, ranged separately | admitted |
| D3 The union / triangulation | H and M with differing closures | union surplus, imported atoms | the ground; overlap measured as independence | admitted |
| D4 Task habitat and channel | tasks, instruments, benchmarks, deployment | contact, bracket fidelity | the ground, which does not decay | admitted |
| X1 "thinks like a human" as a free-floating property | none nameable | imitation score | already inside D2 as a same-closure claim | not a domain yet |
| X2 "the machine is the intelligent one" | one agent | talent | absorbed by Fig 11 :39 into agent-and-habitat | absorbed |

**Union, not partition.** D1 and D3 form a compound: a frame in contact with a differing frame is a different object from the same frame alone. D2 and D4 form a second compound: a model's reach is a property of model-and-habitat. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*

**Dependency order.** Perception of the task, then vocabulary for what the task affords, then an objective stated in each frame's terms, then planning or delegation, then learning and carrying. Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* A link with no instrument is not a working link (Fig 3 :27).

## 3. Findings

### F1 - Complementarity is the figure-defined union surplus (decisive)

Fig 8 :45: **"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."**

Fig 8 :29: **"low overlap: B does not draw on A's evidence"**

Fig 8 :47-48: **"Independence is one quantity read twice."** **"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."**

Fig 8 :49: **"Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."**

Reading. The figure does not ask whether the machine is "creative" or "conscious." It asks a measurable quantity: how much do H and M draw on the same evidence? If M was trained to reproduce H's outputs on H's tasks, overlap is high and the pair is one frame wearing two names; no surplus is available. If M's closure differs (different training distribution, different representational primitives, a different instrument), a surplus can exist. Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."*

So the answer to the question is conditional and checkable. Yes, if low overlap and a shared unmoved ground are present. No, in the imitation regime. This is `specified`, not `grounded`: the figure fixes the shape, not any real pair's overlap.

**Refuter:** show a union with measurably high evidence overlap that still reaches a term neither frame reaches alone, on the same ground. That breaks the independence claim at Fig 8 :47-48.

### F2 - The mirror is the named failure, but only for a verdict question (strain test)

Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

Fig 7 :12-13: **"same closure, handed along;"** **"agreement compounds, evidence does not"**

Fig 7 :36: **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."**

Fig 2 :39-41: **"Correlation has valence."** **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"** and **"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"**

Here the strain test earns its keep. Read alone, "imitation is a mirror" would condemn every human-like interface. Fig 2 :41 says that is wrong when the question is a convention about shared meaning. Re-census: split by the question asked. "Write in a human style" is a convention, and imitation is the product. "Reach a result the ground settles" is a verdict, and a shared evidence pool disqualifies agreement. The question in reasoning.txt line 91 asks about *forms of reasoning*, which are judged by what they reach on tasks, so the verdict reading governs. But the convention reading is recorded, not erased, because it is the legitimate case the mirror rule does not touch.

**Refuter:** a case where agreement between H and M is the legitimate ground for a claim about the world because the question is constitutively about their agreement. Fig 2 :37: *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."* That would move the case into the convention/people domain and out of this one.

### F3 - The wall moves only by import from a differing closure

Fig 6 :14: **"the only operator that moves the wall"**

Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**

Fig 6 :21: **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."**

Fig 6 :22: **"NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world."**

Fig 6 :25: **"An import adds no atom to the world."**

Reading. Two consequences follow and they point in opposite directions, so both are kept.

1. A machine whose entire contact is human output has a closure contained in the human's. No differing closure is present, so no new reasoning primitive can enter. Training more on the same output is depth, not a new atom.
2. The human can also be the importer. If the machine returns a representation the human did not hold, and it passes a source gate and a shadow test, the human's closure can expand. Complementarity is not one-way. Fig 8 :51-52: **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."**

Mode `specified` for the H/M application, `general` for the wall rule.

**Refuter:** document a reasoning primitive entering `Γ_M` with no source frame whose closure differed, and no prior atom in `Γ_M`. That breaks Fig 6 :14 and :21.

### F4 - The anchor is a task ground, not the pair's agreement

Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."**

Fig 2 :45: **"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."**

Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**

Reading. Whether a combination is complementary cannot be certified by how good the conversation felt, by user satisfaction, or by H and M agreeing. It is settled on the task: a correct diagnosis, a checked proof, a paid price, a correct answer. The measuring channel can decay (stale benchmarks, pooled metrics, an instrument that stopped discriminating), so the channel, not the ground, is what a checker must keep reachable. Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

**Refuter:** show the task ground moving when H or M changes its opinion of it, or show agreement between H and M serving as the anchor for a world claim. Both break Fig 2 :29 and :45.

### F5 - The locus is the pair plus its habitat, not either engine

Fig 11 :39: **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**

Fig 11 :40: **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**

Reading. "Can humans and machines develop complementary forms" is not a question about upgrading the machine alone or the human alone. It is about a coupled system: who does which part, what instruments each can read, and what the task habitat pays. A bigger model that does not change contact with the task ground changes nothing by this rule. The same holds for a better-trained person working with an unchanged tool.

**Refuter:** an agent-only improvement that moves the task ground while contact is held fixed. Fig 11 :72 names exactly this as the result that would refute the capability claim.

### F6 - A "form of reasoning" is a molecule, not a list of habits

Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

Reading. If a reasoning form is only a list of capabilities ("recalls facts, computes, summarises"), it cannot express a route: what follows what, what produces what, what is subtracted, what is refused. With bonds, two forms can be isomers (same primitives, different arrangement, different behaviour) or members of one functional group (same `state to structure to state` skeleton, different atoms in the slots). That is a structural answer to "complementary forms." It does not name which forms, because Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."**

**Refuter:** a reasoning form written with ingredients only (no bonds) that still supports a route to a goal on a task ground, or two forms with identical atoms and identical bonds that behave differently on the same ground.

### F7 - Which particular forms complement is a declared gap

Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"**

Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

Fig 13 :115: **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."**

Reading. No figure line says which human reasoning primitive and which machine reasoning primitive form a working pair, or how much overlap is low enough. That is not a failure of the derivation; it is what a barrier theorem is for. The missing instrument is characterized in effect terms: a measure of whether H and M draw on the same evidence, plus a task ground that both fail alone and the combination passes. Naming it would mean already holding it. `gap`, not a silent answer.

**Refuter:** any figure line that directly assigns a particular reasoning primitive to H or M, or that fixes a numerical overlap threshold. None exists in `03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the seat map field guide :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A union of H and M reaches a term neither reaches alone while their evidence pools measure as the same pool (breaks F1, Fig 8 :47-48).
2. A reasoning primitive enters `Γ_M` with no differing-closure source and no prior atom (breaks F3, Fig 6 :14 and :21).
3. A reported complementary advantage survives replacing the task ground with an agreement-only metric (breaks F4, Fig 2 :29).
4. An agent-only improvement moves the task ground while contact is held fixed (breaks F5, Fig 11 :40).
5. Two reasoning forms with identical atoms and identical bonds behave differently on the same ground, or an ingredient-only vocabulary supports a route (breaks F6, Fig 12 :111 and :117).

If any fires, the claim is withdrawn or re-censused. Until one is run, no part of this is `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Ask the overlap question first, not the label question. "Which reasoning is more human-like" is a convention question; "what does the pair reach that neither reaches alone" is the verdict question.
2. Keep D1 to D4 separate and never average them. A model that helps on creation tasks and hurts on decision tasks is two findings, not one score.
3. Transfer methods, not recordings. Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** Fig 4 :25: **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."**
4. Publish the lineage, since provenance is what separates convergent derivation from adopted import (Fig 8 :51-52).
5. Use the public submission routes in `03_public_contact_points.csv` and ask for a refutation, not belief. Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**
