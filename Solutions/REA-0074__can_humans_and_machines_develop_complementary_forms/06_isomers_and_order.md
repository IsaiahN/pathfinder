# Supporting derivation: structure, order and analogues

**Mode: `general` on the bond structure, `specified` on the human-machine application.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The shape claim says complementarity depends on overlap and closure, but it does not say what a "form of reasoning" is or in what order two forms combine. Two questions are owed: (1) are human and machine reasoning **isomers** under Fig 12, and (2) is their combination a **sequence**, a **comparison**, or an **import**? A third question follows: which analogues are licensed, and which are echo without shadow?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held for this derivation are the six of Fig 13: space, time, energy, spectrum, gradient, network. Bonds held are the seven of Fig 12 plus negation. For the reasoning application, the atoms are whatever primitives each frame actually holds; none are named by the figures. No new primitive is available without import (Fig 6 :21). The relation between H and M is ordered, not symmetric: swapping who does which part changes the molecule.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the two forms isomers?

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

Fig 13 :121-124: *"Different routes can arrive at the same substance... Two chains built from different links may compose functionally identical terms, and the converse holds too: the same ingredients under a different bond are a different substance."*

Three readings, kept separate:

1. **Weak reading (fails the figure's test).** If H and M are described only by capability lists, they share no specified atom set and no bond, so the isomer question cannot be asked. An ingredient list is not a molecule (Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information"*).
2. **Functional-group reading (licensed, ungrounded).** If each form is written `state to structure to state` (perceive, represent, act on a task), they share the skeleton but differ in which atoms fill the slots. Fig 12 :124: *"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* This is the same structure the exemplar used for GR and QM, applied here to reasoning forms.
3. **Isomer reading (the checkable claim).** If both forms are written over the full shared atom set, differing only in which structure mediates which state pair, they are isomers: same atoms, different bond, different behaviour on the same ground. That is a real structural claim, but it is `specified` and no measurement here settles it.

**Falsifier, written before this run:** if either form can be written without an atom the other requires, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands instead. If both forms are written with the same atoms and the same bonds yet behave differently on the same ground, the bond claim itself fails.

### Q2 - What is the order of the union?

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces."* The links are perception of the task, vocabulary for what it affords, an objective in each frame's own terms, planning or delegation, then learning and carrying.

Operators table :30: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"* defines sequence. Operators table :48: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* defines comparison. Operators table :52: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one."*

So a "centaur" division of labour is a **sequence** (H does what H affords, then M does what M affords, with a bracket between), while a comparison of which frame pays on a slot is a **comparison** bond, and a new primitive crossing from one frame to the other is an **import**. These are three different things and one lever cannot clear all three. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*

Fig 12 :106: *"Nothing composes over what was never admitted as a part."* So a hybrid only becomes an operand at the next level if both halves settled against the ground first. The order is licensed by settlement, not by a manager's preference.

**Falsifier:** a union that works while one half never settled against the ground (it would break Fig 12 :106), or a single lever that clears all three conditions with no categorically different residue (it would break Fig 9 :57).

### Q3 - Which analogues are licensed?

Fig 8 :53: **"Residual first, frame second."** Fig 9 :27-28: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."**

- **Licensed as low-overlap witness:** the mathematics case in Shulman 2024, where a proof assistant verifies steps and may train intuition while the human retains ideas and understanding. The residual (what each frame contributes against a proof-checking ground) is described before the frame is chosen, and the field has almost no evidence overlap with management studies.
- **Licensed as task-partition witness:** the Dell'Acqua field experiment, where within-frontier and outside-frontier tasks separate the contribution of AI from the contribution of the consultant. It corroborates the sequence reading, not a claim about "intelligence."
- **Not licensed as corroboration:** the MIT Sloan press release about the Vaccaro meta-analysis, because it draws on the same study. It is one witness wearing two names (Fig 8 :49).
- **Returned to census, not flipped:** Drayson 2025 argues that a complementarity claim can be deflated by an internal surrogate. That conflict sent the derivation back to F3, where the claim was narrowed to differing closures. The verdict did not flip.

### Q4 - What about negation?

Operators table :57: *"is the point that it is false, rather than absent? Yes."* And :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."* A pair that can only add capabilities can record that a combination failed but cannot record that a held claim about the pair is wrong. The pre-registered refuter in `01` supplies that operator by hand, which is a discipline, not something the loop can do for itself.

## CONCLUSION

The two forms are not shown to be isomers. The defensible form is the **functional-group** reading: same `state to structure to state` skeleton, different atoms in the slots. The stronger isomer claim requires the full shared atom set and has a falsifier. Their union is a **sequence** for delegation, a **comparison** for which frame pays, and an **import** when a primitive crosses; these must be unbundled before search. The order is fixed by which half settled against the ground, not by preference. All of this is `general` on the structure and `specified` on the application, with no ground measurement.
