# The Tether's answer: complementary reasoning vs imitation

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claim, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

For a task slot s, a reasoning frame's residual is `R_s = |Γ(b,a) - o'|`. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* The question asks whether two frames can stand in a relation where the pair reaches what neither reaches alone, rather than the machine returning a copy of the human's output. That is a union question with a measurable parameter (evidence overlap) and an anchor (the task ground). It is a **union compound**, not one hard question: the overlap condition, the ground, and the transfer of primitives are three conditions that a single lever cannot clear (Fig 9 :55).

## 2. The shape

1. **Complementarity is a low-overlap union surplus.** Fig 8 :45: *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."* Fig 8 :29: *"low overlap: B does not draw on A's evidence."* Fig 8 :47-48: *"Independence is one quantity read twice."* *"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."* The answer is conditional: yes if the closures differ and both range against a task ground; no in the imitation regime. This is the verdict.
2. **Imitation is a mirror, but only for a verdict question.** Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 7 :13: *"agreement compounds, evidence does not."* But Fig 2 :41 says agreement is *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."* "Write like a person" is a convention; "reach a result the ground settles" is a verdict. The question in reasoning.txt line 91 is about reasoning forms judged by what they reach, so the verdict reading governs. The convention reading is kept, not averaged in.
3. **New reasoning primitives enter only by import.** Fig 6 :14 names *"the only operator that moves the wall."* Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* A model trained only on human output has no differing closure to import from. The human can import from the machine too, if a source gate and a shadow test are supplied.
4. **The anchor is the task ground, not agreement.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* Satisfaction, fluency and mutual agreement are channels, not the ground.
5. **Capability lives in the pair plus its habitat.** Fig 11 :39: *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40: *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*
6. **A reasoning form is a molecule, not a list.** Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* The two forms stand at most as members of one functional group (Fig 12 :124): *"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* The stronger isomer claim needs the full shared atom set and has a stated falsifier.
7. **Which forms actually complement is a `gap`.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."* Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* No figure line names a human primitive or a machine primitive, and none fixes an overlap threshold. What is owed is an instrument that measures whether the two frames draw on the same evidence, plus a task ground both fail alone.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that machines are "the intelligent ones," nor that humans are. That is the agent-only question Fig 11 :39 dissolves.
- Not that imitation is always a failure. It is the product for conventions (Fig 2 :41).
- Not that a larger model alone helps. Without a change of contact, nothing changes (Fig 11 :40).
- Not that any particular reasoning primitive belongs to H or M. That is a binding, not a derivation, and Fig 9 :32 returns a kind of thing.
- Not that the two forms are isomers. Only the functional-group reading survives without extra premises (Fig 12 :117, :124).
- Not `grounded`. No task measurement was made in this run.
- Not that the meta-analysis result of Vaccaro and colleagues settles the question. It is a low-overlap witness for the shape and a task-ground reading, but it uses one evidence pool with the MIT press release.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. The questions to put to a ground:

1. **Overlap test:** do H and M draw on the same evidence for the task slot? An instrument that measures shared evidence distinguishes the mirror regime from the union regime.
2. **Union test:** on a slot where both fail alone, does the combination reach a term neither reaches, with the lineage recorded so convergent derivation can be told from adopted import (Fig 8 :51-52)?
3. **Transfer test:** did any new reasoning primitive enter either frame, and from which differing closure? (Fig 6 :23.)
4. **Channel test:** is the ground still reachable, or have the benchmarks saturated and the readings been pooled (Fig 10 :24)?
5. **Standing refuters:** the five in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4, written before this answer.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Overlap is a quantity the figures say decides the case, but no figure supplies a device to measure it in a real pair. Fig 13 :134-135: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"* and *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."* The figures README adds *"Indiscernibility is not identity."* The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage. The particular human-machine forms remain `gap`, and that is a verdict rather than a silence.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that machines imitate humans and cannot do more, and it does not pick a winning species. It says that complementarity is a two-frame relation decided by one quantity: how much the human and the machine draw on the same evidence. A machine copied from human output is a mirror, which is exactly right for a convention such as writing in a human style and exactly wrong for a question a ground settles. If the two closures differ and both range against a task ground, the union can reach what neither reaches alone. New reasoning primitives enter only by import from a differing closure, so more training on the same output is depth, not novelty. Capability lives in the pair and its habitat, not in either engine. The framework refuses to name which forms complement, because it returns a shape, not a name. What it offers is the shape any candidate answer must have, five falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The particular forms stay open until the ground settles them.
