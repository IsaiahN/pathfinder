# Expert verification packet: complementary reasoning vs imitation

**What this is:** a claim in a form a specialist in human-AI interaction, cognitive science or collective intelligence can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the application, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** No task-ground measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result, and it is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Complementary reasoning is not a property a machine either has or lacks. It is a two-frame relation decided by one quantity: how much the human and the machine draw on the same evidence. If the machine is built to reproduce human output on human tasks, the pair is one frame wearing two names and the union adds no reach; that is correct for a convention such as human-style writing and disqualifying for a question a ground settles. If the two closures differ and both range against a task ground, the union can reach a term neither reaches alone. New reasoning primitives enter only by import from a differing closure, so more training on the same output is depth, not novelty. Capability lives in the pair plus its habitat, not in either engine. The framework does not name which forms complement; it returns a shape, not a name, and leaves the particular forms as a declared gap.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - Framework-internal: the union surplus requires low overlap

- **Statement:** two frames reach what neither reaches alone only where they do not draw on the same evidence. High overlap is one frame wearing two names.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :45 - *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."* Fig 8 :29 - *"low overlap: B does not draw on A's evidence"* Fig 8 :47 - *"Independence is one quantity read twice."* Fig 8 :48 - *"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."* Fig 8 :49 - *"Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."*
- **Refuter:** show a union with measurably shared evidence that still reaches a term neither frame reaches alone on the same task ground; or show that the figure assigns a numerical overlap threshold. It does not.

### C2 - Field-facing: imitation is a mirror for verdicts and a product for conventions

- **Statement:** "machines imitate humans" is one overlap regime, not a universal defect. For a convention about shared meaning, agreement is the product; for a claim about the world, shared-pool agreement is worthless. This is checkable against the human-AI literature: the 2024 meta-analysis finds augmentation on average but no synergy on average, with task type and relative baseline as the significant moderators; the 2023 field experiment finds gains inside a task frontier and losses outside it.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :40 - *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* Fig 2 :41 - *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"* Fig 2 :35 - *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 2 :29 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
- **What to check:** is the task in question a convention or a ground-settled verdict? Does the study report human alone, AI alone and the combination; and does it report an anchor the pair cannot move? If it reports only human versus human-AI, it measures augmentation, not complementarity.
- **Refuter:** agreement between the frames serving as the legitimate ground for a world claim because the question is constitutively about their agreement (Fig 2 :37 makes that the right anchor when the question is about people), or a study whose reported synergy survives replacing the task ground with an agreement-only metric.

### C3 - Field-facing: new primitives need a differing closure, and capability is not in one agent

- **Statement:** a model whose only contact is human output cannot acquire a new reasoning primitive by internal composition. A primitive crosses only on contact with a frame whose closure differs, with provenance and a shadow test. The combined capability is a property of agent and habitat; an agent-only improvement that leaves contact unchanged changes nothing.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :14 - *"the only operator that moves the wall"* Fig 6 :23 - *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 6 :21 - *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* Fig 11 :39 - *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40 - *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*
- **What to check:** in a claimed human-AI gain, can a new primitive be traced to a source outside both frames, or was it already in the model's library? Did the intervention change the task habitat (instruments, division of labour, benchmark) rather than only model scale?
- **Refuter:** a documented reasoning primitive entering the model's library with no differing-closure source, or an agent-only improvement that moves the task ground with contact held fixed (Fig 11 :72 names this as the refutation of the capability claim).

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that machines are inherently complementary or inherently imitative. Both are overlap regimes.
- Not that imitation is a failure for conventions. Fig 2 :41 makes it the product there.
- Not that any particular reasoning primitive belongs to humans or machines. The framework returns a kind of thing, not a name (Fig 9 :32).
- Not that the two forms are isomers. The supported reading is the weaker functional group (Fig 12 :124); the isomer claim needs the full shared atom set and has a falsifier.
- Not that a larger model alone helps (Fig 11 :40), and not `grounded`. No task measurement was made in this run.
- Not that the meta-analysis settles the question. It corroborates the shape and is one evidence pool with the MIT press release.

## 5. What would settle it

An instrument that measures whether the two frames draw on the same evidence, plus a task ground both fail alone. Four operational questions:

1. **Overlap:** do H and M draw on the same evidence on the slot?
2. **Union:** does the combination reach a term neither reaches, with a lineage that separates convergent derivation from adopted import (Fig 8 :51-52)?
3. **Transfer:** did a new primitive enter either frame, and from which differing closure?
4. **Channel:** is the ground still reachable, or have the benchmarks saturated and the readings been pooled (Fig 10 :24)?

A full replication of the Vaccaro design with an added overlap measure and an outside-frontier condition would close C1 and C2 together.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 8 :29, :45, :47-49 and Fig 2 :29, :35, :40-41. Six short passages.
2. Say whether the phenomenon you study is a convention or a ground-settled verdict. That decides whether imitation is the product or a mirror.
3. Name one instrument that could measure whether a human and a model draw on the same evidence for a task, or say why none is possible.
4. Look at one claimed human-AI gain and ask whether human alone, AI alone and the combination were all reported against the same ground.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
