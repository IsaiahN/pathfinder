# Outreach draft: check the output first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the retracted readings in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 6 are reflected in any text you circulate. Sending "imitation is always a mirror failure" invites an immediate correct refutation.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about human-machine reasoning, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to
> read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. The output's mode is
> `specified`, not `grounded`. If it does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output.** Complementary reasoning is not a property a machine either has or lacks.
> It is a two-frame relation, and it is decided by one measurable quantity: how much the
> human and the machine draw on the same evidence. If the machine was trained to reproduce
> human output on human tasks, the pair is *"one frame wearing two names"* (Fig 8 :49) and
> the union adds no reach. If their closures differ and both range against a task ground,
> the union can reach something neither reaches alone. That is the whole claim. It does not
> say which machine or which person qualifies, and it does not claim any current system is
> complementary.
>
> **What is explicitly not claimed.** I am not claiming that imitation is bad in general.
> When the question is about a shared convention, agreement is the product, not a corruption
> of it. The claim is only about questions a ground settles: a correct diagnosis, a checked
> proof, a paid price. For those, *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing
> frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). I am also not claiming that the machine is
> "the intelligent one," that a bigger model alone helps, or that any particular reasoning
> primitive belongs to humans or to machines. Those are bindings, not derivations.
>
> **What would refute it:**
> 1. a union with measurably shared evidence that still reaches a term neither frame reaches
>    alone, on the same task ground;
> 2. a reasoning primitive entering the machine's library with no source frame whose closure
>    differed;
> 3. a reported gain that survives replacing the task ground with an agreement-only metric;
> 4. an agent-only improvement that moves the task ground while contact is held fixed.
>
> I wrote these down before writing this note. If one holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line
> "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not
> engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the public sources. The figures are the
> authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figures and formula (canonical download): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - The systematic review this question most closely touches (Vaccaro, Almaatouq and Malone,
>   2024): https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-02024-1
> - The field experiment on task partitions (Dell'Acqua and colleagues, 2023, 2026):
>   https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=64700
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a
> problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your
> result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The essays warn that an
> output can be simulated and that *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from
> the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* A reported failure is more useful
> to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
