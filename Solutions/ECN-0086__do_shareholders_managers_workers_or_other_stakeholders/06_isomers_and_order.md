# Union, comparison and order: why the four claims cannot be ranked as one list

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground
reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks for the "strongest" of four claims over two objects. Two derivations are owed:
(1) is this a partition or a union, and what is the order of the parts; (2) is the comparison bond
`⋛` well typed across the four claimants, and if not, what would type it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: the four claimant frames (shareholders, managers, workers, other stakeholders), the two
objects (purpose as objective, profit as metric), and the two anchors (human judgment for purpose,
the market for profit). Bonds held: the seven of Fig 12 plus negation. No new currency is available
without a convention that decides the outcome (Fig 10 :47).

## DERIVATION

### Q1  -  Partition or union?

- Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
- Purpose is the objective link, Fig 3 :9-11: *"it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms"*. Profit is the ground metric, Fig 1 :23: *"The ground enters as the only metric."*
- ⇒ The two objects have different anchors (Fig 2 :29 versus Fig 2 :36-37). A firm that maximizes profit while failing purpose, and a firm that serves purpose while failing profit, are not different degrees of one failure. They are different compounds. **Verdict: union.**
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** a single instrument that sets purpose and splits profit in one currency and leaves no categorically different residue. If found, the union becomes a partition and the unbundling is wrong.

### Q2  -  What is the dependency order?

- Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*
- Fig 3 :24: *"Why the middle link is where chains usually break."*
- Order: perception of what the market and claimants signal → vocabulary for what each claimant affords and for purpose as distinct from profit → objective (whose purpose) → planning → learn and carry.
- The objective link is link 3. Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* A governance vocabulary that lists claimants (ingredients) cannot say what would count as settling between them (a route). Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."*
- ⇒ Most governance reform lands above the break, on charters and incentive contracts, because those measurements already exist (Fig 3 :29-30). The unmeasured link is the objective. **Verdict: diagnose at link 3 first.**
- **Falsifier:** a documented firm whose perception, vocabulary and objective links all measure as working, and whose failure is located at planning. That would falsify the "usually at the middle" claim without breaking the dependency ordering.

### Q3  -  Is the comparison bond well typed?

- The Operators table defines ⋛ as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* (line 48) and adds, at line 51, *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*.
- Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 12 :126: *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them"*.
- The four claimants trade in capital return, control, wages and affectedness. These are not the same quantity, so ⋛ lacks commensurable operands.
- Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* Stipulating a single unit would decide the winner by definition. It would be a convention doing the work of a verdict.
- The low-overlap witness from mathematics agrees. The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry on Arrow's theorem quotes Arrow: *"it seems to make no sense to add the utility of one individual, a psychic magnitude in his mind, with the utility of another individual."* The entry reports that no aggregation procedure satisfies the minimal conditions once there are more than two alternatives. **Verdict: type error, not a hard ranking problem.**
- **Falsifier:** an invariant currency that ranks all four claims and is not a convention that decides the outcome. Arrow's theorem does not forbid this for every restricted domain, so this is a real test, not a rhetorical one.

### Q4  -  Are the claimants isomers, a functional group, or neither?

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- The four claimants do not share an atom set in the way two molecules do. They are different actors in the habitat, not rearrangements of one set. Under Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**.
- What they share is a role: each is a frame that can be a benefactor or malfactor relative to a residual (Fig 11 :49-51). That is closer to a **functional group**: the group (interested frame) says what kind; the qualifier (which residual) says which member.
- **Falsifier:** a demonstration that two claimants are the same atoms under a different bond (for example, that a worker's wage claim and a shareholder's residual claim are the same quantity differently arranged). If shown, the isomer reading would apply and the functional-group reading would be too weak.

### Q5  -  What order is the union?

- Fig 9 :43: *"The verdict tells you to leave. The lookup tells you where to go. Elimination is what you do after you arrive."*
- Order of operations for ECN-0086: (1) verdict that the question is a union (Q1); (2) diagnose which link the firm's chain stops at (Q2); (3) check the type of the comparison and refuse it if it is a cross-currency claim (Q3); (4) only then look for a frame that holds the missing discriminator, characterized in effect terms (Fig 9 :27-28, *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*).
- Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* The governance literature is a filter for where to look and one witness; it is not the verdict.

## CONCLUSION

The four claims form a **union**, not a partition, and the union must be unbundled into an objective
question (anchor: human judgment) and a metric question (anchor: the market). The dependency break is
usually at the objective link, which has no instrument. The comparison `⋛` is **mistyped** across
incommensurable currencies; making it well typed requires a convention that would itself decide the
outcome, which the figures forbid. The claimants are not isomers but approximate a **functional
group** of interested frames whose valence is relative to the residual. The order of operations is
verdict, diagnosis, refusal, then outward search for a discriminator.

This framing preserves what is defensible without naming a winner: it says what any settlement must
look like and what would have to be true for a ranking to be legitimate. Which claimant actually wins
in a given firm remains a `gap`.
