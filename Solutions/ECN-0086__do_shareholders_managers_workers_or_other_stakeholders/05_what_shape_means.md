## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look
like (a shape, not a name)"*. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13
governs the honest stop: a frame can report what it lacks and *"cannot say which primitive is
missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*, opened this session: *"You can cut your search short if the set of books
returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that
satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains;
it does not fill. For ECN-0086 the shape is: two objects (purpose, profit), two anchors, no claimant
in the anchor seat, incommensurable currencies, and valences relative to the residual. The name
("shareholders win" or "stakeholders win") is withheld.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *The Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, look for
  recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. *"With each new term gained, you
  narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it
  in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than
  the cause."*
- The same essay on bundles: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems
  masquerading as one?"* This is the direct source for F1's unbundling in `01`.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: *"you enter as a researcher looking for a book, and if successful,
  you will leave as the author."* The answer is composed through the process of locating it.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays."*
   The README: the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish,
   and records the lineage so the stop is honest."*
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-51), with a
   source gate and a shadow test.

The *Shareholder primacy* and *Stakeholder theory* reference pages opened for this solution give
names ("shareholder primacy", "stakeholder theory", "enlightened value maximization"). Those are
bindings from the governance frame onto terms the question already holds. They carry no evidentiary
weight until a source gate and shadow test are supplied, and even then they name positions, not the
anchor. Under Fig 10 :18, *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one."*

## 4. Why naming a winner from a declaration is the forbidden error

The mechanism, carried from the exemplar's essay map (not re-read this session), is metamerism from
*The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument reads a result and *"cannot comment on the source"*;
several paths can produce the same reading, so behaviour is a projection with lossy information.
Applied here: a board declaration of stakeholder commitment, a share-price rise, and a high employee
satisfaction score can all be consistent with several different underlying settlements. Reading the
winner off any one of them is the metamerism error. What is owed is a discriminator, not a name.

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, opened this session, gives the standing warning:
*"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory,
cannot validate itself."* A governance doctrine that certifies its own objective is doing exactly
what this forbids.

## 5. Q: what licenses a particular claimant in the objective seat?

**ANSWERED, no further question needed.** Nothing in the framework names a claimant. The closest it
gets is the anchor rule: an anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it (Fig 2
:36). Corporate purpose is constitutively about people, so human judgment is the right anchor for
purpose; the market is the anchor for the metric. Neither is a claimant. Putting a claimant in that
seat is Fig 2 :47's panel-1 error: *"A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a
frame."* Until a ground settles a particular firm's purpose, the seat stays open.

## 6. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, opened this session:
*"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Prestige can fund an action. It
can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle
it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."* A list of famous governance scholars assembled
to agree with the shape is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask is the opposite:
give the claim a budget to be tried, and let the ground, a measurement or a real firm outcome, settle
it.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an
approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post... However, the truly
testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula
yourself."* So `01` is a simulated derivation, checked for internal consistency against the figures.
It is **not** a test of the claim and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports
on.
