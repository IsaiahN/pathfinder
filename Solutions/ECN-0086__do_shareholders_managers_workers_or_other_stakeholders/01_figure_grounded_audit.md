## 0. The residual in effect terms

The loop defines `R = |Γ(b,a) − o′|` per slot `s` (Formula step 1; Symbols table, `R`). Here:

- **b** is the firm's belief about what the market and the wider habitat will reward, formed from its own history and inherited priors.
- **a** is what the firm does: set a purpose, allocate the surplus, price, hire, invest, govern.
- **Γ(b,a)** is what the firm's library of routines, objectives and governance arrangements predicts will happen.
- **o′** is what the ground actually returns: survival, market share, profit, legal standing, legitimacy.
- **s** is the firm. R is indexed by firm and by time, never aggregated into an industry average.

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** (Fig 1 :19)
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** (Fig 1 :22)

The question asks which claimant's "claim" should (a) set the objective link, that is purpose, and (b) own the residual, that is profit. In effect terms a **claim** is a candidate anchor for the firm's objective and a candidate owner of the metric. The question is therefore an aggregation and ranking problem over four claimants whose currencies differ: capital return, control, wages, and affectedness.

State the residual as: *when a firm decides what it is for and who receives the surplus, what settles the objective and the split, and why does appointing one claimant as the settler fail to settle it?* This is an effect description. It names no winner. "Shareholder," "manager," "worker," "stakeholder" are candidate names and are withheld until the census.

**Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term** (Formula, "Direction"), before the run: the answer would be (1) a rule for which seat can legitimately set the objective, (2) a separation of the objective link from the metric, and (3) an account of why no claimant can occupy the anchor seat. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no loop check can see it. It is now bound, so the refuter in section 3 can fire on it.

**Anchor test.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* The market is named as an anchor. Fig 2 :30: *"you push on it, it is not an anchor."* A share price moves when pushed by sentiment, so it is a gauge, not the ground. What pays the firm (survival and realized return) is the anchor for the metric. For purpose the question is constitutively about people, and Fig 2 :36-37 says human judgment is the right anchor when the question is about people: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* Two anchors, two objects, one union.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### 1.1 Arity, symmetry, scale of R

- **Arity.** R is a three-place relation: firm Γ, action a, ground return o′, indexed by slot s and ordered by time. "Strongest claim" is a further four-place comparison across claimants, so it carries a **comparison** bond (`⋛`, *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, Fig 12 :87; Operators table :48). It is not a symmetric relation: claimants and the firm are not interchangeable.
- **Symmetry.** "Strongest" is a threshold relative to the other claimants, not an absolute quantity (Fig 12 :85-89; Operators table :46-51). The ranking is asymmetric: a claim that wins under one residual need not win under another.
- **Scale.** Three scales are in view: the single firm's step-by-step loop (Fig 4, one-agent panel), the population of firms with *"something that ranks what they produce"* (Fig 4 :5), and the institutional and legal order that constitutes the habitat. The membrane between scales is *"Not a wall: a filter with a direction"* (Fig 4 :21), and *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* (Fig 4 :22). The transform is lossy, so `R_T` must be measured (Fig 4 :30).

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

A domain is admitted only if an anchor can be named. Where none can be named, it is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor, nameable? | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 The firm loop** (one agent) | managers, routines, the firm's Γ | actions and residual reduction | yes: realized market return and survival on the action | admitted |
| **D2 The market habitat** (population) | firms, customers, entrants, suppliers, investors | survival, share, profit | yes: the market as what pays, named in Fig 2 :29 | admitted |
| **D3 The claimant frames** (four interested frames) | shareholders, managers, workers, other stakeholders | capital return, control, wages, affectedness | each has its own instrument, but all move when pushed, so each is a frame, not a ground | admitted as frames; the "winner" slot has no anchor |
| **D4 The institutional habitat** (law, charters, regulators) | courts, regulators, charters, fiduciary duties | legal standing, enforceability | yes: legal outcomes that do not change because a frame thinks; e.g. Dodge v. Ford | admitted |
| **X1 A single strongest claim over purpose AND profits** | none coherent | none common | **no** nameable anchor: purpose and profit have different anchors | not a domain yet, a union masquerading as one quantity |
| **X2 "The market" as a claimant** | the market | share price | share price is a gauge that updates; it is the anchor for the metric, not a party | re-census: folded into D2, refused as a claimant |

X1 fails the anchor test and is excluded before any figure line is applied to it. It is a structural exclusion, not a claim that the debate is meaningless. X2 is the tempting error of turning the anchor into a party, which section 2, F2 resolves.

### 1.3 Partition or union

D1 and D3 overlap: managers are both the loop's operators and one of the claimants. D2 and D4 overlap: the institutional habitat conditions the market. D1/D3 and D2/D4 form compounds with their own loops. The whole question is a **union**, not a partition.

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** (Fig 9 :54)
> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** (Fig 9 :55)
> **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."** (Fig 9 :57)

The unbundled conditions are: (P) what sets the objective, that is purpose, with human judgment and the ground as anchor (Fig 2 :36-37); and (M) what the metric is and who receives the residual, that is profit, with the market as anchor (Fig 2 :29). They have different anchors and different instruments. The Library of Babel essay, opened 2026-11-23, names the same stop: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"*

### 1.4 Dependency order (Fig 3)

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."** (Fig 3 :20)
> **"Why the middle link is where chains usually break."** (Fig 3 :24)
> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** (Fig 3 :27)

Order for this question: 1 perception (can the firm read what the market and each claimant are signalling?) → 2 vocabulary (does Γ have terms for what each claimant affords, and for purpose as distinct from profit?) → 3 objective (can it say what would count as progress, that is whose purpose?) → 4 planning → 5 learn and carry. The middle link, having terms for what a situation affords and saying what would count as progress, is the one with no instrument. Fig 12 :111-115 says a vocabulary holding only ingredients can say what a situation contains and cannot say what would count as getting somewhere from it: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison."* Most governance effort lands above the break, on charters and incentive plans, because that is where the measurements already exist (Fig 3 :29-30).

## 3. Findings

Mode tag on each: `general` if structural, `specified` if a falsifiable prediction about firms, `grounded` only if settled by a measurement. None is `grounded`.

### F1: The question is a union and must be unbundled before a lever is chosen

**Mode: `general`. Domains all. Status: triangulated (Fig 9 and the Library of Babel essay, low overlap between the figure and the search essay).**

Fig 9 :54, :55 and :57 are quoted in section 1.3. Purpose is the objective link (Fig 3 :9-11: *"it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms"*); profit is the ground metric (Fig 1 :23: *"The ground enters as the only metric."*). They are different quantities with different anchors. A single answer such as "shareholders" applies one lever to both and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :56). The specified consequence: any report that blends purpose and profit into one "stakeholder score" has produced a pooled reading and destroyed the diagnostic.

**Refuter:** a single instrument that sets purpose and splits profits in one currency with no categorically different residue left over. If one exists, the union collapses into a partition and F1 is false.

### F2: No claimant may be appointed the anchor (decisive)

**Mode: `specified`. Domains D1, D2, D3. Status: triangulated (Fig 2 and Fig 11, low overlap: anchor rule vs habitat valence).**

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."** (Fig 2 :29)
> **"you push on it, it is not an anchor."** (Fig 2 :30)
> **"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."** (Fig 2 :34)
> **"A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame. Which is panel 1, arrived at by a different route."** (Fig 2 :47)

Shareholders, managers, workers and other stakeholders are all frames that update on one another and on the market. Their preferred outcomes move when pushed. Put one of them in the anchor seat and the anchor becomes a party to the disagreement, which is Fig 2's panel 1: coherent drift that nobody notices because it looks like agreement. This is not a preference for stakeholder theory; it is the anchor rule applied to each claimant in turn. Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* The claimants are the habitat, not the ground.

**Refuter:** a claimant whose preferred allocation is invariant under pressure from the other three frames and from a change in what the market pays. Such a claimant would be a real anchor. None is identified here; if one is measured, F2 is false.

### F3: Managers cannot be the verifier of the firm's purpose

**Mode: `specified`. Domains D1, D3. Status: triangulated (Fig 10 and Fig 11, verifier non-access vs habitat actors).**

> **"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."** (Fig 10 :42)
> **"Soundness requires the checker to be unable to produce what it is checking."** (Fig 10 :43)

Managers produce the firm's decisions and its reported purpose. They are players inside the frame being graded, so they cannot be the seat that checks the grade. The world makes the same point in the opposite direction: Friedman's doctrine, quoted in the *Shareholder primacy* reference page, makes the manager *"the agent of the individuals who own the corporation ... and his primary responsibility is to them"*. That is exactly a player, hence disqualified as the verifier. Goodpaster's "stakeholder paradox" (recorded in the *Stakeholder theory* reference page) is the same structural trap stated in business language: strategic use gives *"business without ethics"*, while making managers fiduciaries for all comers gives *"ethics without business"*.

**Refuter:** an arrangement in which the party that sets the objective demonstrably cannot produce the output being graded. If such a separation is achieved and the ground still pays, F3's disqualification is beaten.

### F4: "Strongest claim" is a cross-currency type error

**Mode: `general` for the structure, `specified` for the firm. Domains D3, D4. Status: triangulated (Fig 12 and Arrow's theorem from social choice, low overlap: composition pricing vs mathematical impossibility).**

> **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."** (Fig 12 :125)
> **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them"** (Fig 12 :126)
> **"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"** (Fig 12 :52)

Capital return, control, wages and affectedness are different currencies. To rank them as "strongest" one must stipulate a common unit. Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* A convention that fixes the ranking would be deciding the outcome, not measuring it. The Operators table defines ⋛ as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, so the comparison presupposes commensurable operands. The world supplies an independent witness from mathematics: the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry on Arrow's theorem quotes Arrow that *"it seems to make no sense to add the utility of one individual, a psychic magnitude in his mind, with the utility of another individual."* The entry reports that no social welfare function satisfies the minimal conditions once there are more than two alternatives. That is the same barrier stated in a field with no stake in corporate governance.

**Refuter:** a demonstrated invariant currency that ranks all four claims and is not a convention that decides the outcome. If such a measure exists and is grounded, F4 is false.

### F5: The claimants are the habitat, enumerated, and their valence is relative to the residual

**Mode: `specified`. Domains D2, D4. Status: triangulated (Fig 11 and biological mutualism, low overlap: habitat enumeration vs ecology).**

> **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."** (Fig 11 :43)
> **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."** (Fig 11 :44)
> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** (Fig 11 :49)
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."** (Fig 11 :50)
> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."** (Fig 11 :51)

The stakeholder question "who counts" is literally Fig 11 :43-44: enumerate everything in contact with the residual, including second-order contacts. The *Stakeholder theory* reference page frames the field as addressing *"the principle of who or what really counts"*, which is this enumeration in business vocabulary. Mitchell, Agle and Wood's power, legitimacy and urgency attributes are an attempt to rank the enumerated set; by Fig 11 :49 those attributes are valences relative to a residual, not permanent kinds. The low-overlap witness is biological: the *Mutualism (biology)* reference page says *"Mutualism describes the ecological interaction between two or more species where each species has a net benefit"* and that *"mutualism may evolve from interactions that began with imbalanced benefits, such as parasitism"*. Mutualism and parasitism are relations that change with conditions, matching Fig 11 :50 with no shared evidence pool.

**Refuter:** a claimant whose valence (benefactor or malfactor) is invariant across a documented change of the firm's residual. F5 predicts none exists.

### F6: A strength turns into a rigidity when the residual changes

**Mode: `specified`. Domains D2, D3. Status: triangulated (Fig 11 and Fig 12, habitat relations vs composition structures).**

> **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."** (Fig 12 :119)
> **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."** (Fig 12 :121)

A governance arrangement that delivered the old advantage (say, a founder's control or a narrow shareholder mandate) is a catalyst if it regenerates across uses and a spent part if it does not. Nothing in the arrangement's description records which. When the residual changes, a former benefactor becomes a malfactor without anything about the arrangement changing (Fig 11 :49-51). The biological source corroborates at a different stratum: *"Under certain conditions species of fungi previously in a state of mutualism can turn parasitic on weak or dying plants."*

**Refuter:** a governance arrangement whose measured contribution is invariant across a documented change of the firm's residual. F6 weakens to `general` if none can be found to change.

### F7: Internal agreement among claimants is not evidence

**Mode: `general` with a `specified` firm consequence. Domains D1, D4. Status: triangulated (Fig 7 and Fig 2, selection between rooms vs anchor triangulation).**

> **"same closure, handed along;"** (Fig 7 :12)
> **"agreement compounds, evidence does not"** (Fig 7 :13)
> **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."** (Fig 7 :35)
> **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."** (Fig 2 :35)

A board, a shareholder coalition or a stakeholder panel that agrees with itself is handing itself the same room. The Business Roundtable's 2019 statement, opened this session, says *"we share a fundamental commitment to all of our stakeholders"* and *"Each of our stakeholders is essential."* That is a convention, a declaration of meaning. By Fig 10 :18, *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one."* The declaration is useful as a budget for trying a policy; it is not evidence that the policy settled anything. The ultimatum-game literature (human cognition, low overlap) corroborates the mechanism that people enforce fairness norms even against their narrow interest: the reference page reports that *"people offer 'fair' (i.e., 50:50) splits, and offers of less than 30% are often rejected"*. Fairness norms are real, but they are a frame's preference, not an anchor.

**Refuter:** an agreement among claimants that demonstrably moves the ground, that is, a change in realized return and welfare that would not have occurred without the agreement. If one is measured, F7's methodological consequence is false.

### F8: The market is the anchor for the metric, human judgment for the purpose

**Mode: `specified`. Domains D1, D2, D4. Status: triangulated (Fig 2 :29, :36-37, :45).**

> **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."** (Fig 2 :36)
> **"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."** (Fig 2 :45)

Profit's ground is what pays: realized market return and survival, the market named in Fig 2 :29. Purpose's ground is human judgment, because a firm's purpose is constitutively about people, customers, workers and communities. Fig 2 :37-38 warns that an instrument can be wrong on axes it does not measure, so a market return does not settle purpose. Conversely a welfare declaration does not settle the metric. Keeping the two anchors separate is the figure-grounded content of the unbundling in F1.

**Refuter:** a firm whose purpose is constitutively not about people, in which case human judgment is not the anchor for its purpose. F8 is narrow and would need re-census for such a case.

### F9: Which claimant wins is a `gap`, not a hidden answer

**Mode: `general`. Status: figure-governed.**

After a genuine re-census no figure line names a winner among the four claimants. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The canonical figures README (opened this session) states the limit as *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*. What is missing is a source-discriminating instrument that would name the anchor for a particular firm's purpose, not a further argument. This is a `gap` and it is recorded as such in `09_watson_answer.md`.

**Refuter:** a figure line that names a specific claimant as the anchor, or a ground measurement that settles a real firm's purpose and surplus split. Until one fires, the stop stands.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the design-instrument rule: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* The shape claim is refuted if any of the following is observed:

1. A single instrument sets purpose and splits profits in one currency with no categorically different residue (breaks F1, Fig 9 :54-57).
2. A claimant's preferred allocation is invariant under pressure from the other three frames and from a change in what the market pays (breaks F2, Fig 2 :29-30, :47).
3. The party that sets the objective also produces the output being graded, and the ground still pays (breaks F3, Fig 10 :42-43).
4. An invariant, non-conventional currency ranks all four claims (breaks F4, Fig 12 :125-126, Fig 10 :47).
5. A claimant's valence is invariant across a documented change of the firm's residual (breaks F5 and F6, Fig 11 :49-51).
6. An agreement among claimants demonstrably moves the ground (breaks F7, Fig 7 :35).
7. Fig 13's six terms are derived from five, or Fig 6's idempotence fails inside a closed set (breaks the figures themselves, not just this reading).

No part of this solution may be upgraded to `grounded` until at least one of these has been run and has failed to fire, and until a ground measurement has paid for the shape.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the residual and the refuter, not with a theory of the firm (Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"*).
2. Keep purpose and profit separate in every report. A blended "stakeholder score" is a pooled reading (Fig 10 :26) and destroys the diagnostic.
3. At each handoff, ask which link the chain stops at and whether that was measured (Fig 3 :29). Do not fund above the break.
4. Treat any list of claimants as Γ, that is, as a candidate habitat whose valence must be tested against the ground, never as the answer (Fig 11 :49-51).
5. Do not let a convention decide the outcome. A common currency that fixes the ranking has stopped being a convention (Fig 10 :47).
6. Use the governance literature as a budget filter and as one corroborating witness, not as the verdict. Friedman, the Business Roundtable and the stakeholder-theory pool overlap (Fig 2 :31, :40).
7. Offer the formula as a method after a verdict is recorded (Fig 4 :22). Each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.
8. Stop at the shape. Do not name which claimant should win; the framework returns *"a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (Fig 9 :32).

## 7. Recursive derivation summary

1. **Is this one question?** No. Fig 9 :54, :55, :57 plus the Library of Babel bundle passage: it is a union of an objective question and a metric question, each with its own anchor.
2. **What is the anchor?** For the metric, the market (Fig 2 :29). For the purpose, human judgment because the question is about people (Fig 2 :36-37). Not consensus, not internal metrics.
3. **Can a claimant be the anchor?** No. Appointing one is Fig 2's panel 1: the ground listed as a party (Fig 2 :47).
4. **Can managers verify purpose?** No. They produce what is being graded (Fig 10 :42-43).
5. **Can the four claims be ranked?** Not without a convention that decides the outcome (Fig 12 :125-126, Fig 10 :47; Arrow's theorem).
6. **Why does the good arrangement turn bad?** Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49-51); a catalyst may become spent (Fig 12 :119).
7. **What can be said at the stop?** A shape and seven falsifiers. Not which claimant wins, not a `grounded` answer. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
