**Subject:** One checkable output on corporate purpose, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about corporate
> governance. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it.
> If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** "who has the strongest claim on a firm's purpose and
> profits?" is not one question. Purpose sets the objective; profit is the metric the market pays.
> They have different anchors. From the figure set I work with, two things follow. First, an anchor
> is whatever settles a question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks,
> which is why a market return can anchor the metric. Second, if you put any one claimant,
> shareholders, managers, workers or other stakeholders, into the anchor seat, that claimant becomes
> a party to the disagreement. The claimed settlement then becomes coherent drift that nobody
> notices because it looks like agreement. So the framework refuses to name a winner. It returns the
> shape any legitimate answer must have: two anchors kept separate, the objective link distinguished
> from the metric, and the claimants treated as the habitat to be enumerated rather than as the
> ground.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that shareholders do not matter, that stakeholder theory is false, or
> that a particular board arrangement is right. The four claims use different currencies, capital,
> control, labour and affectedness, and a single ranking needs a common unit. A common unit that
> fixes the winner has stopped being a neutral convention and has become the outcome. I am not
> asserting such a unit. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
>
> **What would refute the shape:**
> 1. one instrument that sets purpose and splits profits in a single currency with no categorically
>    different residue left over;
> 2. a claimant whose preferred allocation stays fixed when the other three frames push on it and
>    when what the market pays changes;
> 3. an invariant, non-conventional currency that ranks all four claims;
> 4. an agreement among claimants that demonstrably moves the ground.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, I will retract the shape. A one-line "this
> fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is
> needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary, are the
> authority.
> - Verbatim figures with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** run the formula on a governance problem you know well and can
> check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the
> mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
