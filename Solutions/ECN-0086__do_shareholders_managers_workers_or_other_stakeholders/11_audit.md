## Independent domain census

I redid the census before relying on the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the gap in a firm's governance loop plus the unmeasured question of whose objective it runs. Arity: three-place (firm, action, ground return) with a four-place claimant comparison, so a **comparison** bond is present (Fig 12 :87), not a symmetric join. Symmetry: asymmetric, since a winner under one residual need not win under another. Scale: firm loop, population of firms, institutional habitat; the membrane is lossy (Fig 4 :21-30).
- **Domain 1, firm loop.** Actors: managers, routines, Γ. Currency: actions and residual reduction. Anchor: realized market return and survival. Question type: one-agent loop.
- **Domain 2, market habitat.** Actors: firms, customers, entrants, suppliers, investors. Currency: survival, share, profit. Anchor: the market as what pays (Fig 2 :29). Question type: population ranking.
- **Domain 3, claimant frames.** Actors: shareholders, managers, workers, other stakeholders. Currency: capital, control, labour, affectedness. Anchor: none nameable that does not move when pushed, so frames, not ground. The winner slot is not a domain.
- **Domain 4, institutional habitat.** Actors: courts, regulators, charters, fiduciary duties. Currency: legal standing and enforceability. Anchor: legal outcomes that do not change because a frame thinks. Question type: conditions and relations enumerated from the world (Fig 11 :44).

Order by dependency: perception → vocabulary → objective → planning → learn and carry (Fig 3 :20); the objective link is the unmeasured middle link (Fig 3 :24, :27). Union, not partition: D1/D3 and D2/D4 form two compounds, and the whole is a union of an objective question and a metric question with different anchors (Fig 2 :29 versus :36-37).

My census matches the primary's four domains and its union reading. Where the primary derived the union from Fig 9's bundle rule, I derived it from Fig 2's two different anchors. Different starting figures, same domain boundary; low overlap between routes within the framework, but still one evidence pool.

## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `01` F10 and `07`: two objects, two anchors, no claimant in the anchor seat, incommensurable currencies, conditional valence, winner is a gap.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete governance answer, for example "shareholders own the firm so they should set purpose and take the profit", or "all stakeholders count so the board should balance them".

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **two-anchor separation**, which the concrete answers collapse into one objective;
- the **refusal to name a winner**, which the concrete answers replace with a named party;
- the **convention-versus-ground distinction**, which the concrete answers treat as a measurement.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a claimant that settles the question has become a party to it. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a recorded gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` states.
