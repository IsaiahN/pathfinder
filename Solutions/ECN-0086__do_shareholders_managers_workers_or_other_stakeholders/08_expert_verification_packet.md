# Expert verification packet  -  the Tether on corporate purpose and profit claims

**What this is:** a claim in a form a corporate-governance researcher can check. Mode, derivation and
refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle
it.

**Mode: `specified`  -  not `grounded`.** No firm-level measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a
route, not a verdict about who should win.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Who has the strongest claim on a firm's purpose and profits?" is a union of two questions with two
anchors, not one ranking problem. Purpose sets the objective; profit is the market-paid metric. No
claimant (shareholder, manager, worker or other stakeholder) may occupy the anchor seat, because a
claimant that settles the question is a party to it and the result is coherent drift that looks like
agreement. Managers produce what is being graded and cannot verify it. The four claims use
incommensurable currencies, so a single ranking needs a convention that would decide the outcome by
definition, which is forbidden. The claimants are the habitat, enumerated, with valences relative to
the residual. The framework therefore returns a shape, not a name, and which claimant wins is a
declared `gap` until a ground settles it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1  -  framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 2 and 11

- **Statement:** no claimant may be the anchor. Putting one in the anchor seat produces Fig 2's panel 1 (the ground listed as a party), not a settlement.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29  -  *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Fig 2 :47  -  *"A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame. Which is panel 1, arrived at by a different route."* Fig 11 :43  -  *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*
- **Refuter:** a claimant whose preferred allocation is invariant under pressure from the other three frames and from a change in what the market pays. If one is measured, C1 is false.

### C2  -  governance-facing; checkable against the shareholder and stakeholder literatures

- **Statement:** the two canonical positions assert different anchors, neither of which is a ground in this derivation. Friedman's manager-as-agent doctrine puts the owner's interest in the objective seat; the stakeholder field asks *"who or what really counts"* and the 2019 Business Roundtable statement declares a commitment to all stakeholders. All are frames or conventions, not anchors.
- **What to check:** is Friedman's *"the manager is the agent of the individuals who own the corporation ... and his primary responsibility is to them"* an accurate statement of the position; is the Business Roundtable language *"we share a fundamental commitment to all of our stakeholders"* a declaration rather than a measurement; does Goodpaster's "business without ethics / ethics without business" paradox match the figure's verifier rule (Fig 10 :42)?
- **Refuter:** an agreement among claimants that demonstrably moves the ground, that is, a change in realized return and welfare that would not have occurred without the agreement. If one is measured, C2's convention-versus-ground distinction fails.

### C3  -  cross-disciplinary; checkable against the composition rule and Arrow's theorem

- **Statement:** ranking the four claims as "strongest" is a cross-currency type error, not a hard ranking problem. A common unit that fixes the winner is a convention that decides the outcome.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :125  -  *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 12 :126  -  *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them"*. Operators table :48  -  ⋛ is *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*. Fig 10 :47  -  *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."*
- **Refuter:** an invariant, non-conventional currency that ranks capital return, control, labour and affectedness. Arrow's theorem (SEP entry, opened 2026-11-23) reports that no aggregation procedure satisfies the minimal conditions once there are more than two alternatives, and quotes Arrow: *"it seems to make no sense to add the utility of one individual, a psychic magnitude in his mind, with the utility of another individual."* This is corroboration, not the verdict; a restricted-domain measure would still refute C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a winner. No claimant is named as having the strongest claim.
- Not that shareholder primacy is false or that stakeholder theory is true. Both are frames in this derivation.
- Not a governance prescription: no board structure, charter clause, pay rule or legal reform is derived.
- Not a claim that Arrow's theorem settles corporate governance. It corroborates the type error only.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by a firm-level measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminator, not a further argument. Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*

1. For a named firm, identify what actually settles its purpose and test whether that thing changes its answer when the four claimants push on it.
2. Run the union test: one instrument that sets purpose and splits profit with no categorically different residue.
3. Run the type test: an invariant currency that ranks the four claims without being a convention that decides the outcome.
4. Run the valence test: a claimant whose contribution stays invariant across a change of residual.
5. Apply the seven pre-registered falsifiers in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29, :47 and Fig 11 :43 (three short passages). Decide whether the anchor rule, applied to each claimant, yields C1.
2. Check C2 against the governance literature you know: are Friedman, the Business Roundtable and Goodpaster represented accurately, and are they frames or measurements?
3. Name one instrument that would discriminate a genuine settlement from a declared convention for a real firm, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
