## What bears directly on this question

1. **The governance literature is a convention, not an anchor.** The Business Roundtable statement
   *"we share a fundamental commitment to all of our stakeholders"* is a declared meaning. Fig 10 :18
   says *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one."* Use it as evidence that a commitment was made
   and as a budget for trying a policy, never as evidence that the policy settled purpose.
2. **The two canonical positions assert different anchors.** Friedman's manager-as-agent line puts
   the owner's interest in the objective seat; the stakeholder field asks *"who or what really
   counts"*. Fig 2 :47: *"A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame. Which is
   panel 1, arrived at by a different route."* Both positions are frames; neither is the anchor.
3. **The ranking problem is independently known to be hard.** Arrow's theorem and Fig 12 :125
   (*"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*) give the same warning from
   mathematics and composition theory. This is corroboration of the type error, not a verdict on
   governance.
4. **Valence is conditional, not permanent.** Biological mutualism shows relations that change with
   conditions, matching Fig 11 :50: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to
   another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."*

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 10 :52**  -  *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Sending the full framework
  before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape,
  the union split and the refuter.
- **Fig 2 :35**  -  *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* A
  list selected for people likely to agree produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 9 :27-28**  -  *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."*
  Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the framework.
- **Fig 2 :40**  -  *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the
  agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. The governance sources above overlap heavily; count them
  as one witness.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim, the union split and the refuter; (2) ask what
they would count as a failure; (3) only then the source links, with the note that the figures, not
the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer (with the optional third stage)

1. **Stage 1:** send the residual in effect terms (purpose versus profit, two anchors), the shape
   claim, and the four pre-registered refuters. Ask for their verdict before showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the source links. Their agreement with the
   framework after reading it is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse. Fig 4 :22  -  *"Only methods cross up. Only head
   starts come down."* Each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode, because
   `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction" says a frame cannot check the direction of its own question.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 work only if the stage-1 output survives. The honest stage-1
result here is a refusal to name a winner. If that refusal is stripped out to make the pitch sound
more decisive, the output stops surviving and the offer collapses on contact.
