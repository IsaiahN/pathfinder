# The Tether's answer: who has the strongest claim on a firm's purpose and profits?

**Mode: `specified`  -  not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated
derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

"Strongest claim on purpose and profits" bundles two questions with two anchors. Purpose sets the
objective; profit is the metric. A firm lives in a habitat of claimants whose currencies differ:
capital return, control, labour, affectedness. The residual is the gap between what the firm's
governance predicts about what pays and what the ground actually returns, indexed per firm (Fig 1
:19), plus the unmeasured question of whose objective the firm is running. This is a **union
compound**, not one hard problem (Fig 9 :55): a firm that fails purpose and a firm that fails profit
are different compounds needing different instruments.

## 2. The shape

1. **Purpose and profit are two objects, not one.** Fig 1 :23: *"The ground enters as the only metric."* Fig 3 :9-11 makes purpose the objective link. The market anchors the metric (Fig 2 :29); human judgment anchors purpose because the question is constitutively about people (Fig 2 :36-37). The two must not be blended into one score.
2. **No claimant may occupy the anchor seat.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Shareholders, managers, workers and other stakeholders all update on one another and on the market. Put one in the anchor seat and the ground becomes a party: Fig 2 :47, *"A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame. Which is panel 1, arrived at by a different route."* The result is coherent drift, not a settlement.
3. **Managers cannot verify the objective.** They produce what is being graded. Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* The agent is a player, and players are disqualified from the checking seat.
4. **The four claims cannot be ranked without deciding the outcome.** Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* The ⋛ comparison (Operators table: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*) needs commensurable operands. A single unit that supplies them is a convention that decides the outcome, forbidden by Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* Arrow's theorem, from social choice, independently shows the aggregation barrier.
5. **The claimants are the habitat, enumerated, with conditional valence.** Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 11 :49-51: valence is relative to the residual, mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds, and no standing list of good actors survives a change of residual. A shareholder mandate or a stakeholder commitment that delivered the old advantage may become a rigidity when the residual changes (Fig 12 :119).
6. **Internal agreement is not evidence.** Fig 7 :13: *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*; Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* A board consensus or a broad coalition is a room, not an anchor.
7. **The framework refuses to name a winner.** This is a `gap`, recorded as such. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* What is missing is a discriminator, characterized in effect terms but not held here.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that shareholders have no claim, nor that stakeholder theory is false. The derivation does not rank the claimants; it refuses to put any of them in the anchor seat.
- Not that profit is the only metric, nor that purpose is a free declaration. Each has its own anchor; neither colonises the other.
- Not a governance recommendation (no board structure, pay rule or charter clause is derived).
- Not `grounded`. No firm-level measurement settles the surplus split here.

## 4. What would settle it  -  the ground

A discriminator, not a further argument. Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* The relevant questions:

- **Anchor discrimination:** for a named firm, what actually settles its purpose, and does that thing change its answer when shareholders, managers, workers and communities push on it? If it does not move, it is a candidate anchor (Fig 2 :29-30).
- **Union test:** can one instrument set purpose and split profit without leaving a categorically different residue? If yes, F1 falls.
- **Type test:** is there an invariant, non-conventional currency that ranks the four claims? If yes, F4 falls (though Arrow's theorem makes this a demanding ask).
- **Valence test:** does any claimant's contribution stay invariant across a documented change of residual? F5 and F6 predict no.
- **Standing falsifiers:** the seven listed in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4, in particular a claimant whose preference is invariant under pressure and market change, and an agreement that moves the ground.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The claimant list is the habitat, enumerated from what is in contact with the residual, and the enumeration is always incomplete at the second and third order (Fig 11 :44). The anchor for purpose is human judgment, which is a moving target for "good answer" questions, but Fig 2 :36-37 says it is the right anchor precisely because the question is about people. The answer stops where the instruments can no longer distinguish a genuine settlement from a declared convention, and the lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not pick shareholders, managers, workers or stakeholders as the winner, and it does
> not claim to. It says that "purpose" and "profits" are two questions with two different anchors:
> the market pays the metric, and human judgment settles purpose because the question is about
> people. No claimant can occupy either anchor seat, because a claimant that settles the question is
> a party to it, and the result is drift that looks like agreement. Managers cannot verify the
> purpose they produce, and the four claims use different currencies, so ranking them would require a
> rule that decides the winner by definition. What the framework offers is the shape any legitimate
> answer must have: two anchors kept apart, the objective link distinguished from the metric, the
> claimants enumerated as a habitat with valences that change when the residual changes, and
> falsifiers written in advance. Which claimant actually wins stays open until a ground, not a
> declaration, settles it.
