# WATSON answer: who has the strongest claim on a firm's purpose and profits?

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** Figures are primary and decide. World sources are secondary,
corroborate but never overrule, and a conflict returns the derivation to the domain census. This is a
simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. By
its own rule (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: LLM output is a simulation and "the truly
testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself")
it cannot settle the claim it reports on. No firm-level measurement was taken.

## QUESTION

Restated as an effect: when a firm sets its purpose and allocates its surplus, what settles the
objective and the metric, and why does appointing one claimant as the settler fail to settle it?
Option A: name one winner (shareholders, managers, workers or other stakeholders) for both purpose
and profits. Option B: unbundle the question, refuse the anchor seat to every claimant, and return the
shape any legitimate answer must have. A delivers a `general`-mode verdict over incommensurable
currencies and turns the ground into a party. B delivers a checkable shape, written refuters and an
open seat until a ground settles it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Arity: R is a three-place relation (firm Γ, action, ground return) indexed by slot and ordered by
time, with a further four-place comparison over claimants. Symmetry: "strongest" is a threshold
relative to the other claimants (Fig 12 :87), not a symmetric join; a winner under one residual need
not win under another. Scale: the single firm loop, the population of firms, and the institutional
and legal habitat; the membrane between scales is lossy (Fig 4).

Union, not partition: D1/D3 and D2/D4 are two compounds; the whole is a union of an objective
question (anchor: human judgment, Fig 2 :36-37) and a metric question (anchor: the market, Fig 2 :29).
Dependency order: perception → vocabulary → objective → planning → learn and carry (Fig 3 :20); the
break is usually at the objective link, which has no instrument (Fig 3 :24, :27). Strain test: Fig
13's physical six-term set was re-censed to the honest-stop role only after it only fit firm
stakeholders by renaming them as kinds of energy.

Refuter: one instrument sets purpose and splits profit with no different residue.

**D2 Market habitat, status triangulated (Fig 2 and Fig 11, anchor rule vs habitat valence).**

> "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." (Fig 2 :29)
> "A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame. Which is panel 1, arrived at by a different route." (Fig 2 :47)

Reading: the market anchors the metric; no claimant (including the market as a party) anchors the
objective. Mode `specified` for the firm application. Refuter: a claimant whose preference is
invariant under pressure from the other frames and from a change in what the market pays.

**D3 Claimant frames, status triangulated (Fig 12 and Fig 10, cross-currency pricing vs convention rule).**

> "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." (Fig 12 :125)
> "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them" (Fig 12 :126)
> "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one" (Fig 10 :47)

Reading: the four claims are incommensurable; ranking them needs a convention that decides the
outcome. Mode `general` for the type error, `specified` for firms. Refuter: an invariant,
non-conventional currency that ranks all four claims. The ⋛ type question is named, not hidden.

**D4 Institutional habitat, status triangulated (Fig 10 and Fig 11, verifier non-access vs habitat valence).**

> "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." (Fig 10 :42)
> "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." (Fig 11 :49)
> "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." (Fig 11 :51)

Reading: managers cannot verify the purpose they produce; claimant valence changes with the
residual. Mode `specified`. Refuter: a governance arrangement whose contribution is invariant
across a change of residual, or a setter that cannot produce the graded output and the ground still
pays.

**Union and method, status triangulated (Fig 9 and Fig 7, union split vs room chain).**

> "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." (Fig 9 :55)
> "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." (Fig 9 :57)
> "agreement compounds, evidence does not" (Fig 7 :13)
> "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." (Fig 9 :60)

Reading: unbundle before searching; internal agreement is not evidence; the governance literature

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The figure-derived verdict: this is a union of two questions with two anchors, not a ranking of four
claimants. Purpose is the objective link, anchored by human judgment because the question is about
people; profit is the metric, anchored by the market. No claimant may occupy either anchor seat
without becoming a party and producing coherent drift; managers cannot verify what they produce; the
four claims are incommensurable, so ranking them requires a convention that decides the outcome. The
claimants are the habitat, enumerated, with valences relative to the residual. The framework returns
a shape, not a name, so which claimant wins is a declared `gap`, not a hidden answer. Mode
`specified`. The governance evidence pool corroborates only after re-mapping Friedman and the
Business Roundtable to frames and conventions; the low-overlap fields corroborate the mechanism, not
the firm-level fact.

- **cost if wrong:** misdirected diagnosis. A firm failing the objective link would be sold a metric instrument, or a metric failure would be treated as a purpose crisis, and a single lever would be applied to a union that needs two. In outreach terms, an expert's twenty minutes would be spent refuting a shape that was never the right shape. The cost is real but bounded and largely reversible, because the shape and its seven falsifiers are recorded in a provenance trail.
- **undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails. A refutation is added and the reach falls because "something was added rather than because anything was deleted" (Fig 6 :42-43). The shape and its falsifiers are stamped with origin and date so a later reader can locate the error rather than only feel it (Fig 10 :62-64).
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. Specifically: one instrument that sets purpose and splits profit in a single currency with no categorically different residue (Fig 9 :54-57); a claimant whose preference is invariant under pressure from the other frames and a change in what the market pays (Fig 2 :29-30, :47); an invariant non-conventional currency that ranks all four claims (Fig 12 :125-126, Fig 10 :47); a claimant whose contribution is invariant across a change of residual (Fig 11 :49-51); an agreement that demonstrably moves the ground (Fig 7 :35); or a figure re-census that admits the market as a party without collapsing into panel 1. Prestige or agreement is not such an instrument.
