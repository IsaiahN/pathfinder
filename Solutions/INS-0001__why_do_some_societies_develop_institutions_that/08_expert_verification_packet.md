# Expert verification packet - the Tether on institutional constraint

**What this is:** a claim in a form an institutional scholar can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No institutional dataset or historical case was measured. This is a shape and a route, not a causal result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

An institution that constrains powerful actors has a checkable shape: a checking arrangement whose keeper is outside the slice it grades and gains nothing from a particular reading, sustained by a habitat, with a feedback loop whose sign decides whether the check survives its own use, and an anchor that does not move when the powerful push on it. Consolidation of power is the collapse of that arrangement: the keeper benefits from the value the record returns, and a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention. The question as asked bundles two conditions, getting a check established and keeping it from being captured, so a single lever often clears only one. The framework does not name the historical cause of any society's outcome; it returns a shape, not a name. It offers three refuters written in advance and the instruments that would close the open slot.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 10 and 2

- **Statement:** a checker must sit outside the slice it grades and gain nothing from a particular reading. If the keeper benefits from the value the ground returns, the constraint has collapsed.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :86: "Outside the slice being graded. Whoever is measured by a reading cannot own it." Fig 10 :59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying." Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." Fig 10 :93: "A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."
- **What to check:** is the seat condition stated as a structural requirement rather than a description of a particular office? Is the binding from "seat" to "checking body" declared as a cross-domain binding at `specified` mode?
- **Refuter:** a durable constraint record through crisis in a society whose grading body is drawn from, appointed by and materially dependent on the powerful; or a case where constraint survives with no keeper outside the slice.

### C2 - world-facing; checkable by an institutional scholar against known cases

- **Statement:** capability to constrain is a property of the arrangement and its habitat, not the design alone; and two societies with the same atoms but a different independence bond are isomers in Fig 12's sense.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- **What to check:** do the world cases support the claim that a written charter without a change in monitoring and sanctioning contact fails to move the constraint record? Ostrom's commons findings and the Federalist 51 argument are the two outside witnesses; are they being used as corroboration rather than as the source of the verdict?
- **Refuter:** an agent-only institutional change that moves the constraint record with no change in habitat contact; or identical institutions and identical independence bonds producing opposite outcomes with the difference outside the arrangement.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation

- **Statement:** the question is a union compound (establishment and capture resistance), so a lever clearing one condition leaves the intersection standing; and the loop sign decides closure.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :4: "First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." Fig 12 :121: "The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Fig 12 :119: "A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."
- **Refuter:** one variable driving both conditions with no interaction term; or a self-reinforcing concentration failing to close without an opposing check; or a checker consumed by its first successful check with constraint nevertheless persisting.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a causal finding about any named country, and not a selection among geography, disease, endowment, class balance or contingency.
- Not a claim that any particular institution is necessary or sufficient.
- Not a derivation of political history from the Tether; the seat is a cross-domain binding declared at `specified` mode.
- Not a theory of everything, and not the only framework that could produce this shape.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by an institutional dataset or a crisis record.
- Not a claim that the eight essays or the world sources are independent witnesses. They are declared as one evidence pool where they share an author or a source.

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminator, not an argument.

1. **Anchor test:** does the constraint record hold in a crisis where the powerful have an interest in breaking it and the keeper's reputation is on the line?
2. **Catalyst test:** does the same checking body survive a second use, or is it spent by the first?
3. **Isomer test:** two societies with identical formal institutions and identical independence bonds but opposite outcomes. Written as a check; finding it fails the isomer claim.
4. **Import gate:** for a named case, a causal claim with provenance and a shadow test that explains the residual. That moves D4 from unreached to grounded for that case.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 10 :47, :59, :86, :93 and Fig 12 :117, :119, :121, seven short passages.
2. Check C2 against two cases you know well: did the written change in institutions move the constraint record, and what in the habitat changed?
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish a real anchor from a rating the powerful can move, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
