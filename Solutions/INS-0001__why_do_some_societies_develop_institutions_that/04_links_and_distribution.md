## Wider literature that bears on this question

1. **Acemoglu and Robinson (2012).** The inclusive/extractive distinction is a low-overlap framing of the same contrast. Their de jure versus de facto distinction is the nearest outside statement of the agent/habitat split: a written constitution and the actual distribution of power are different objects. Their feedback claim, that institutions today shape institutions tomorrow, is the world's version of Fig 12's cycle. Their geography critique matters here: the framework cannot decide between geography and political choice, which is exactly the held D4 slot.
2. **Ostrom (1990; Nobel 2009).** Rules made by users, monitored by insiders, beat rules imposed from outside in the studied commons. That is a world-source statement of F1 and F2: the keeper must be outside the slice it grades, and capability sits in the arrangement and habitat.
3. **Federalist 51 (1788).** "Ambition must be made to counteract ambition" is an early statement that a check needs its own motive and independence, not merely a written role. It corroborates Fig 2 :42 (buy independence) and Fig 10 :59 (the keeper gains nothing from a particular reading).
4. **De Waal (1982) and ethology.** Coalitions of subordinate males against higher-ranking males are a non-human instance of the same shape: constraint of a dominant actor by a checking relation, arrived at independently in a different field. This is corroboration at low overlap, not a derivation of human institutions from animal behaviour.
5. **Reverse causality critique (Subramanian, via the Why Nations Fail overview).** Institutions may be a consequence of prosperity rather than its cause. In this derivation that is a warning against importing a cause without a source gate (Fig 6 :23). It does not flip the shape verdict; conflict sends the census back to work.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 4 :22 to :25:** "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator.
- **Fig 10 :42:** "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who has absorbed the whole framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28:** "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the article.
- **Seat Map :163:** "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention." A mass send selected for people who already agree produces a convention, not corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its three refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) record their verdict; (4) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

- **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52, "Bring a characterised residual, never the source," and Fig 2 :45, "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." The recipient checks the shape without holding the framework's interior.
- **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** A recipient's agreement after seeing the theory is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool").
- **Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted** (Fig 4 :22), and each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode (Formula, "Direction").

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 shape survives. The three refuters in `02_outreach_email.md` are the test; do not soften them to get agreement.
