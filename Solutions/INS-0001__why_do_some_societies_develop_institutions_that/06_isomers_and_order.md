## RESIDUAL

The record asks why one society constrains power and another consolidates it. Two questions are owed: (1) can two societies with the same formal institutions differ in outcome because of the arrangement between them, and (2) what is the dependency order of the check, the habitat and the outcome?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the two outcomes isomers?

Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance."

A society that constrains power and one that consolidates it can hold the same list of institutions. What differs is the bond: in one, the checker is independent of the checked; in the other, the checker is appointed by and dependent on the checked. The ingredient list is identical; the substance is not.

- **Strong claim (isomer):** over a fixed atom set including an independent checker, the two outcomes are Fig 12's isomers. Same atoms, different bond, different substance.
- **Weak claim (functional group):** as usually described the atom sets differ, so they share only a functional group, Fig 12 :123: "A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." The group is "a checking arrangement"; the qualifier says whether it is captured.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two societies with identical formal institutions and identical independence relations produce opposite constraint outcomes, with the difference attributable to a factor outside the arrangement, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands alone.

### Q2 - Which part is a catalyst?

Fig 12 :119: "A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."

A checking body that checks the powerful and remains able to check again is a catalyst. One whose first successful check exhausts its independence is spent. Nothing in the institution's name records which it is. This is why the distinction must be measured across at least two uses, with the second use as the discriminator. Fig 12 :119 is echoed by the world source on Ostrom: monitoring and enforcement work better when conducted by insiders, and rules imposed from outside are more likely to be violated.

**Falsifier:** a body that is spent by its first check yet constraint persists through later crises without a replacement check.

### Q3 - What is the loop sign?

Fig 12 :121: "The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."

Consolidation: capture the keeper, then use the keeper to capture more. Output re-enters as input with a positive sign; the loop closes. Constraint: a check whose successful use renews the checker's standing. Output re-enters with a sign that preserves the checker. A chain cannot close by construction, so a one-off constitutional convention is not a constraint; only a loop can be.

**Falsifier:** a self-reinforcing concentration of power that fails to close without any opposing check, or a constraint that persists with no feedback from use to standing.

### Q4 - What is the dependency order?

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."

Read the chain for an institutional question:

1. **Perception:** can the frame tell which societies carry the live residual, rather than averaging them? Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
2. **Vocabulary:** are there terms for what a situation affords, what can be checked and by whom? This is the link with no instrument.
3. **Objective:** can the frame say what would count as progress, that is, what a held constraint would look like in a crisis?
4. **Planning:** is there a route from the current arrangement to that objective?
5. **Learn and carry:** is the method that worked carried up as a method, not as a recording of one occasion (Fig 4 :22)?

Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." The break in institutional explanations is usually at link 2, not at the data.

**Falsifier:** a case where effort spent at links 4 and 5 (reform design and best practice transfer) reliably rescues a society whose links 1 and 2 are missing.

### Q5 - Where does import enter?

Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."

Fig 8 :50-52: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."

The names "inclusive" and "extractive," and the causal claims about disease or endowments, are imports from the political-economy and history frames. They are welcome as imports with a source gate and a shadow test. Without the record they are indistinguishable from a term the framework derived, and the distinction is the whole issue. ## CONCLUSION

The two outcomes read as isomers over a fixed atom set, with the weaker functional-group claim standing if the atom sets differ. The checking part is a catalyst only if it survives its own use. Consolidation is the positive-sign cycle and constraint the preserving-sign cycle, and neither is a chain; only a loop closes. The dependency chain breaks most often at vocabulary and objective, where there is no instrument. All of this is `specified` and unmeasured. It rescues the structural content of the question without naming a cause.
