# Outreach draft - check the shape first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the re-census in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §6 is reflected in the text. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one shape claim before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a derivation about why some societies build institutions that constrain powerful actors while others concentrate power. I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with me. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the framework treats an institution that constrains power as a *checking arrangement*. Its rule is that a checker must sit outside the slice it grades and gain nothing from a particular reading; if the keeper of the record benefits from the value the record returns, the constraint has already collapsed, and a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention. A second rule says that the capacity to constrain is a property of the arrangement and its habitat, not of the design alone, so a charter that does not change contact with the ground changes nothing. A third says the sign of the feedback loop decides whether a check survives being used or is spent by it.
>
> This is a **shape**, not a causal finding. The framework cannot tell you which historical factor, geography, disease, endowment, class balance or contingency, produced a given society's outcome. It says what any such factor would have to do: change contact in the habitat and alter the independence of the keeper. The question as phrased also bundles two conditions, getting a check established and keeping it from being captured, which is why a single lever often clears only one of them.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that this derivation explains any named country; that a particular institution is necessary or sufficient; that a constitutional design guarantees constraint; that the framework settles the empirical debate; or that it is the only framework that could produce this shape. The figures return a kind of thing, never the answer itself. Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents; only the record separates them.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a society with a durable constraint record through crisis whose grading body is drawn from, appointed by and materially dependent on the powerful;
> 2. an agent-only institutional change that moves the constraint record without changing any contact in the habitat;
> 3. two societies with identical institutions and identical independence bonds but opposite constraint outcomes, with the difference attributable to something outside the arrangement.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will withdraw the shape claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I will send the three public sources below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Figures and formula for direct download (branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Why Nations Fail (overview of the inclusive/extractive distinction): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Why_Nations_Fail
> - Economic governance: the organization of cooperation (Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences, 2009): https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/economic-sciences/2009/popular-information/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and its author warns that outputs "could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula." A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - [your name]
