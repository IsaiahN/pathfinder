## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A discriminator, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

- **Anchor test:** does the constraint record hold in a crisis in which the powerful have an interest in breaking it, and does it hold when the keeper's reputation is on the line? Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."
- **Catalyst test:** does the same checking body survive a second use, or is it spent by the first?
- **Isomer test:** two societies with identical formal institutions and identical independence bonds, one constrained and one consolidated. If found, the isomer claim fails (it was written as a check, not a prediction of easy confirmation).
- **Establishment/capture split:** does a lever that establishes a check fail in the intersection where capture also binds? If not, the union reading is wrong.
- **Import gate:** for a named case, a causal claim with provenance and a shadow test that explains the residual. That would move D4 from unreached to grounded for that case.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not explain any named country and does not claim to settle the geography-versus-choice debate. It says that an institution constraining power has a checkable shape: a checker that sits outside what it grades and gains nothing from a particular reading, a habitat that sustains it, a loop whose sign decides whether the check survives being used, and an anchor that does not move when the powerful push on it. A society that consolidates power is what that arrangement looks like when the keeper benefits from the record and a convention deciding outcomes stops being a convention. The question also bundles two conditions, establishment and capture resistance, which is why a single reform often clears only one. The framework cannot name which historical factor produced a given outcome; it returns a shape, not a name. What it offers is the shape any answer must have, three refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close the open slot. The slot stays open until the ground settles it.
