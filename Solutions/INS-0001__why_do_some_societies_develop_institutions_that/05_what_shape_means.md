## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."

From *The Library of Babel*: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question: "a checking arrangement whose keeper is outside what it grades, sustained by a habitat, with a loop sign that decides closure" is a shape. "Disease environment in the colonies caused inclusive institutions in the settler states" is a name.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. "With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."
- The same essay names the question as the anchor: "you need something to tether and anchor you to reality... This anchor is provided by your own question."
- Fig 6 :30: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: "the finish line will also be the starting place" and the paradox that by then you have "composed the answer through the process of locating it."

Applied here: the residual was described first (a per-society gap between predicted and observed constraint), then the frame was chosen. Not the reverse. If we had picked Acemoglu and Robinson first and then found the question, every field would have seemed to fit (Fig 9 :27-28).

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." The README: the framework "stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*They're Scanning Books When They Should Be Studying Nature* sharpens this: "The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space." Also: "A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six." And: "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."

For institutions this means: naming a historical cause is an import from the history or political-economy frame. It is legitimate only with provenance and a shadow test (does it explain the residual?). This derivation ran neither, so the cause stays unreached.

## 4. Why reading the artifact for the source is the forbidden error

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. Metamerism: an instrument reads green and "cannot comment on the source." One green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy." It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved it.

The institutional analogue: a country's written constitution is the artifact. Reading "a constitution that limits the executive" as proof that power is constrained is the metamerism error. Several arrangements (an independent court, a fragmented elite, a reputation cost, a foreign audience) can produce the same reading. What is owed is a discriminator, a crisis in which the constraint either holds or does not, not a name.

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."

## 5. The question as a union, and the shape of its parts

*How a Society of Agents Would Work* states the core: "a group is only worth having while its members stay different." It also quotes Fig 11 that capability is agent-and-habitat, and notes that votes should come from outcomes rather than popularity. Fig 9 :4 supplies the split: the question bundles establishment and capture, and those are two conditions, not one.

So the shape is not a single lever. It has two parts, each with its own ground:

- **Establishment:** a checker is introduced into the habitat and survives its own use (Fig 12 :119, catalyst).
- **Capture resistance:** the checker stays outside the slice it grades and keeps contact with the record (Fig 10 :86; Fig 2 :29).

A lever that clears only establishment leaves the intersection standing. That is why institutional reforms that look correct on paper can fail without the habitat, and why the author's own caution about an unauthored pool matters (Seat Map :213).

## 6. Prestige is not action, and agreement is not corroboration

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Someone let prestige stand in for proof... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."

A roll call of famous institutional economists agreeing with the shape is the named collapse, not evidence. The legitimate ask is the opposite: give the claim a budget to be tried, let the ground settle it, and record refuters in advance.

## 7. My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

Same essay: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." And: "the outputs generated must be seen as simulacra." *Arrival of the Fittest* adds that outputs "could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula."

So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation, checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of any institutional theory and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on.

## 8. Correction to my own §3

I initially treated "shape, not a name" as a blanket ban on naming anything in an answer. That overstates the rule. The README's phrase is about "a **missing** thing," a primitive not held in the library. "Checking arrangement," "habitat" and "anchor" are not missing primitives being smuggled in; they are the effect-terms the derivation used to describe the residual. What remains forbidden is naming the specific historical cause, because that cause is not held here and no import gate was run.

Both readings are recorded: the narrow one (correct) and the overreach (retracted). Nothing is silently erased.
