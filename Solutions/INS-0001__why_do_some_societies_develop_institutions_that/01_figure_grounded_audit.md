## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

Let `s` be a society, one per object slot. `R_s = |Gamma(b, a) - o'|`: the gap between a frame's predicted institutional trajectory and the observed one.

- **Arity.** At least two conditions are bundled in "some societies constrain, others consolidate": (i) a checking arrangement gets established and maintained, (ii) it resists capture. These are not one lever. Fig 9 :4: "First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."
- **Symmetry.** The two outcomes are not mirror images. Introducing a check is available; subtracting the habitat that produces capture is not (Fig 11 :61: "Introduction is available; exclusion is not."). The move is asymmetric, so no symmetric bond will do.
- **Scale.** Individual actors at one scale (elites, office holders, citizens); the checking arrangement at the next; the society-level outcome at the next. What crosses between scales is a method, never a recording (Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down.").
- **Slot indexing.** Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." "Some societies" is exactly such an average. The first act is to re-census which slots carry live mass, not to average them.

**Bound sought-for shape (Formula, "Direction").** An answer must name (i) a checking arrangement whose keeper is outside the slice it grades, (ii) a habitat that sustains it, (iii) the sign of its feedback loop, and (iv) an anchor that does not move when the powerful push on it. Written down here before the census so it is refusable.

## 3. Findings, with verbatim quotes and refuters

### F1 - Consolidation of power is seat capture; constraint requires a keeper outside the slice (decisive, `specified`)

Fig 10 :86: "Outside the slice being graded. Whoever is measured by a reading cannot own it."

Fig 10 :59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"

Fig 10 :93: "A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."

Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"

Seat Map :200-205 states the position rule: the seat is not chosen; it is outside the slice graded, gains nothing from a particular reading, and sees what agreement hides. Institutional translation, declared as a binding: an institution that constrains power is a checking arrangement whose keeper cannot be captured by the party it grades. Consolidation is the collapse in which the keeper benefits from the value the ground returns, so the convention deciding outcomes stops being a convention and becomes a verdict for the powerful.

This is a cross-domain binding, not a derivation of political history. Mode `specified`. Triangulated across Fig 10 and Fig 2 / Seat Map (low overlap: Fig 10 governs the maintainer seat; Fig 2 governs anchor legitimacy).

**Refuter:** show a society in which the body grading the powerful is itself drawn from, appointed by, and materially dependent on the powerful, and the constraint record still holds through a crisis; or show that the seat condition is not necessary, by a case where constraint survives with no keeper outside the slice.

### F2 - Capability is agent-and-habitat, so design alone does not constrain (single-source figure, world corroborated)

Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"

Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

Fig 11 :61: "Introduction is available; exclusion is not."

Writing a constitution that limits power changes the agent's description, not necessarily its contact with the ground. Whether it changes contact depends on the habitat: who can monitor, who can sanction, what cascades follow an attempt at capture. Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." The list is read off the world, not invented from the theory.

**Refuter:** an agent-only institutional improvement (a new charter, a new office) that demonstrably moves the constraint record without changing any contact in the habitat. Fig 11 itself names this as the refutation of the capability claim.

### F3 - The question is a union compound and must be unbundled (`specified`)

Fig 9 :4 (quoted in §0) and Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"

"Establishment of a check" and "resistance to capture" are two conditions. A single lever (hold elections, write a charter, decentralise) clears the region where only one binds and leaves the intersection standing, because the second condition still binds (Seat Map :211). The residue after unbundling is categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either.

**Refuter:** show that one variable drives both conditions with no interaction term, so the two-outcome picture collapses to one scale.

### F4 - The question as phrased averages away the live slot (`general`)

Fig 1 :22 (quoted in §0). Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."

"Some societies versus others" is a cross-section average. The figures require the per-slot residual. The operational form is: which society has a live positive residual on the constraint slot, and what is in contact with it? The anchor for that question is the constraint record itself, not an expert's classification of a regime type.

**Refuter:** a demonstration that the per-slot residual is zero everywhere and the observed variance is entirely measurement noise in the classifier.

### F5 - The figures return a shape, not the name of the historical cause (`general`, canonical limit)

Fig 9 :32 (quoted above). Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"

The canonical figures README states the limit as "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." So the derivation may say what a cause must be like: it must change contact in the habitat and alter the independence of the keeper. It may not say which cause did it in a given history. That is an import needing a source gate and shadow test (Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."). No such gate was run here, so the specific cause is held as unreached.

**Refuter:** a ground measurement that discriminates among candidate causes for a named case, with provenance, which would move one cause from `candidate` to `grounded` for that case.

### F6 - Same institutions, different bonds, different outcomes (`specified`)

Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

Fig 12 :123: "A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Two societies can hold the same atoms (a constitution, a court, an election commission, a legislature) and differ in the bond: whether the checker is independent of the checked. A list of institutions cannot tell them apart; the arrangement can. Over an identical atom set they are **isomers**; as usually described, with different atoms in the slots, they are members of the same **functional group** ("a checking arrangement"). This is a checkable structural claim, not a metaphor (see `06_isomers_and_order.md`).

**Refuter:** a pair of societies with identical formal institutions and identical independence relations but opposite constraint outcomes, with the difference attributable to a factor outside the arrangement.

### F7 - The sign of the loop decides whether power is checked or consolidated (`specified`)

Fig 12 :121: "The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."

Fig 12 :119: "A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."

Consolidation is a positive feedback cycle: capture the keeper, then use the keeper to capture more. Constraint is a negative feedback cycle: a check that is preserved by being used. A checking body that survives its own use is Fig 12's catalyst; one that is spent by its first use is not. "Nothing records which a term is," so the distinction must be measured, not assumed from the institution's name.

**Refuter:** a case where a checking body is consumed by its first successful check and constraint nevertheless persists; or a case where a self-reinforcing concentration of power fails to close despite no opposing check.

### F8 - Independence is bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong (`specified`)

Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence"

Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."

An elite consensus that the regime is constrained is variation inside a room, not a verdict. The cost of being wrong about power capture is high, so the figure says to buy independence deliberately: frames whose evidence does not come from the same pool. Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."

**Refuter:** a case where agreement among frames sharing one evidence pool correctly predicted a constraint outcome and no independent frame existed. That would be a lucky pool, not corroboration; it would not refute the valence rule, but it would bound its predictive use. Recorded as a boundary, not a hidden exception.

### F9 - A ground that updates on the powerful is not a ground (`specified`)

Fig 2 :29 (quoted above). Fig 2 :35 (quoted above). Seat Map :18-22: a legitimate anchor is not negotiable and the question is constitutively about it; an anchor listed as one of the parties has become a frame.

If the measure of "constraint" is whichever classification the powerful accept, the anchor moves when pushed and the arrangement is the mutual-update collapse. The question is constitutively about whether power is actually constrained, so the legitimate anchor is the constraint record that survives crisis, not a reputation or a rating produced by the actors being graded.

**Refuter:** a measure of constraint that the powerful can move by persuasion or by funding, and that nevertheless tracks the ground in a held-out crisis.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Required before any outreach, per Seat Map :287: "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A society shows a durable constraint record through crisis while the body grading the powerful is drawn from, appointed by, and materially dependent on the powerful (breaks F1).
2. An agent-only institutional change moves the constraint record with no change in any habitat contact (breaks F2).
3. Two societies with identical institutions and identical independence bonds produce opposite constraint outcomes, with the difference attributable to a factor outside the arrangement (breaks F6).
4. A ground measurement discriminates among historical causes for a named case with provenance (does not break the shape; moves the held slot from unreached to grounded).

Until one of 1 to 3 is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.
