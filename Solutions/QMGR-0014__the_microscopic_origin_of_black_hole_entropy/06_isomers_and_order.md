## DERIVATION

### Q1 : Is the counting shape a legal composition?

- Fig 13 :74: **"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."**
- A first-order molecule here is `state → structure → state`: a bounded region (Space), a set of related and ordered configurations (Network, Spectrum), and a finite energy bound (Energy is the state that makes the count terminate).
- Reading: `Space → (Network, Spectrum) → Energy`. The horizon supplies the bounded region; the microstates supply the configurations; the energy bound makes the count finite. Entropy is the logarithm of the count, a derived quantity at the next level.
- Fig 13 :75: **"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."** The composition above crosses kinds at every step, so it is legal. A proposal that sets `Space → Energy` directly, with no structure between, would not be.
- Verdict: the shape is legal under Rule 1. `general`. Triangulated across Fig 13 :74 and :75 plus the minimality rows (low overlap: the composition rule and the thermodynamic test rows are different arguments inside the same figure).

**Falsifier, written before the run:** show a derivation of one of the six from the other five, or admit a state-state or structure-structure composition the framework cannot refuse. That breaks the grammar the shape is written in.

### Q2 : Where each term is load-bearing for the second law

Fig 13's minimality table can be read as a dependency order, not merely a list:

| removed term | figure line (verbatim) | consequence for entropy counting |
|---|---|---|
| spectrum | Fig 13 :56 *"no ordering, so no equivalence over thermal state, so no transitivity"* | no thermal state to count over |
| network | Fig 13 :58 *"no relation between systems, and no configurations to count"* | no configurations |
| space | Fig 13 :62 *"no region to make isolated mean anything"* | no bounded system |
| energy | Fig 13 :68 *"finiteness in a finite region is what makes the counting terminate"* | the count does not terminate |
| gradient | Fig 13 :70 *"no direction to drive the flow, and no remainder left after each step"* | no flow to the horizon, no residual per step |

Dependency: the region comes first, then the finiteness bound, then configurations, then ordering, then the flow whose remainder is what the count leaves. Fig 3 :20 orders the chain *"ordered by dependency, not by importance."*

**Falsifier:** derive one established thermodynamic law without the term the table says it needs. Fig 13 :72 says the derivations *"were run before anyone asked for a minimality argument, which is why this is evidence rather than a case built to fit."* A successful counterexample would be evidence against the table.

### Q3 : Are the microstate programs isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- At the macro level the string count, the loop count, the lattice count and the Planck-bit count all write `S ∝ A`. In the formula they are indistinguishable. They share a **functional group**: a finite discrete cross-kind count. The qualifier (which structure sits in the slot, which bond joins it to the region) is what differs.
- Over the shared atom set, differing in arrangement, they are **isomers**: same atoms under the figure's test, different substance off extremality and in the correction terms. Carlip's *"problem of universality"* is the name physics gives to the fact that the macro formula does not discriminate them.
- Fig 13 :121: **"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."** That is the first half. Fig 12 :95–96: **"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart"** is the second half. Both are needed: the programs converge on a substance and differ as things.

Verdict: `specified`, single-source for the figure definition (Fig 12); the physics-facing half is corroborated by the four papers in `10_source_ledger.csv`, which are low-overlap closures.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two programs are related by a definitional identity (Fig 12 :65, *"two names, one referent"*), the isomer claim between them fails. If all four reduce to one, both the isomer claim and the underdetermination verdict in `01` F2 fail.

### Q4 : What is the order of the union?

- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"**.
- The area law is settled at the thermodynamic level (first law, area theorem, gravitational-wave area increase). It can serve as an atom at the next level.
- The first-order molecule is the count. The second-order composition is `(settled area law) ⋛ (candidate count)`, a threshold comparison between a macro reading and a micro degeneracy. It is legal because the first-order term settled. It needs no new primitive.
- The comparison bond ⋛ is defined by the Operators table as *"a threshold relation between two quantities."* At second order the operands are molecules now serving as atoms. Whether they count as *quantities* at that level is a type question that needs an instrument, not an argument. This is a live strain, named rather than hidden. It does not affect the shape, which is stated in effect terms.

### Q5 : The inverse-problem analogue

The independent frame: *"An inverse problem in science is the process of calculating from a set of observations the causal factors that produced them"*, and when the forward map is rank deficient, *"the solution of the inverse problem will be undetermined. This is a first difficulty."* The Wikipedia article connects Weyl's law to the question of whether it is possible to *"hear the shape of a drum"*: a spectral/geometrical summary is asked to determine the configuration that produced it.

Analogy, stated as an analogy and not as evidence: the area is the drum's summary and the microstates are the configuration. The map from configuration to area is many to one. The inverse problem is therefore underdetermined until a second observable is added. This corroborates Fig 9 :32, *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*, from a field that does not draw on the framework.

**Falsifier for the analogue:** exhibit a theorem that the area reading is injective over the relevant microstate space. That would name the source from the reading and break the analogy.

## CONCLUSION

The counting shape is a legal first-order composition across the two kinds. Each of the six terms is load-bearing for the second law in the order the minimality table gives. The several microstate programs share a functional group and, over the full atom set, stand as isomers: same atoms under a different arrangement, indistinguishable in `S = A/4` and different in behaviour. Their union with the settled area law is second-order and licensed by Fig 12's settled-molecule rule. The inverse-problem analogue corroborates the underdetermination from outside physics.

What this rescues: the structural content of the question, without picking a program or minting a seventh primitive. What it does not rescue: the name of the source. `metric tensor = Gradient`-style bindings and `D-branes are the origin` claims stay unproven. The one open type question is ⋛'s operand class at second order.
