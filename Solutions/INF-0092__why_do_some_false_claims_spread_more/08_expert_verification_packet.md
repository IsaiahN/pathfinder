# Expert verification packet - the Tether on false-claim spread

**What this is:** a claim in a form a misinformation researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No measurement in this packet settles the question. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "why do some false claims spread more effectively than accurate information, even when corrections are available?" is a union of two conditions with different instruments. **C1, transmission:** a claim travels far when the chain of tellers keeps the ground's pressure near zero and keeps high fidelity to what earns social reward; agreement compounds while evidence does not, so a same-feed crowd is one long room rather than many witnesses. **C2, correction:** a correction is a refusal that removes the claim's standing to be composed over, not another claim added beside it; it fails when the receiving frame lacks a term for falsity or the verifiable record is no longer reachable. Over the same atoms the two are isomers, with different bonds and different behaviour, and their dependency order is: describe the residual, install the refusal, refuse before the claim accrues standing, then restore ground contact in the chain. The framework names no single mechanism as the cause and returns only a shape with falsifiers written in advance.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal plus habitat-facing; checkable by reading Figures 7 and 11 and by reviewing your own spread data

- **Statement:** an agreeing chain is one long room; spread has two factors, fidelity and ground pressure, and zero either stops progress even while motion continues.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 7 :13 - "agreement compounds, evidence does not". Fig 7 :36 - "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced." Fig 7 :37-39 - "Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place: the bracket's fidelity across the handoff, the ground's pressure on the room." Fig 11 :39 - "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."
- **What to check:** in a real cascade, are repeat exposures from overlapping sources producing confirmation without new ground contact? Can fidelity to social reward and ground pressure be separated empirically, or are they always confounded in your data?
- **Refuter:** a cascade in which bracket fidelity and ground pressure both measure high and non-declining, yet the false claim still out-travels the accurate one. Or a chain of measured low-overlap tellers, each selected against the residual surfaced by the last, that still shows spread tracking agreement rather than ground contact. If either holds, this sub-claim is false.

### C2 - framework-internal plus cognition-facing; checkable by reading the Operators table and Figure 5

- **Statement:** correction is a negation, a refusal that removes standing to compose over; a vocabulary without it can only accumulate. A warning label is a cue inside the frame, not a refusal.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :53 - "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so". Fig 5 :55 - "What it removes is not a term but a term's standing to be composed over. Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach." Operators table :58 - "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate." Operators table :73 - "Negation is the refusal of a claim. A vocabulary with − and without ¬ can say what is missing from an arrangement and cannot say that an arrangement is wrong, so it can only ever accumulate."
- **What to check:** in your correction studies, does the intervention remove the claim's standing to be reused in later inferences, or does it merely add a competing item? Does a "disputed" flag change the latter without changing the former? Pennycook, Cannon and Rand (2018) report exactly that dissociation: "even a single exposure increases subsequent perceptions of accuracy" and "tagging such stories as disputed is not an effective solution to this problem."
- **Refuter:** a frame demonstrably lacking a term for refusal that still drops a false claim's standing to be composed over, with no new atom added and no ground measurement. That breaks this sub-claim and the operator rule it rests on.

### C3 - the union and the stop; checkable by reading Figure 9 and Figure 2

- **Statement:** C1 and C2 form a union, not a partition, and cannot be blended into one score or cleared by one lever. Agreement is not evidence when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 - "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57 - "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Fig 2 :40 - "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Fig 2 :35 - "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
- **What to check:** do your outcome measures conflate exposure, agreement and belief into a single "misinformation index"? If so, can they be re-separated per claim and per source? Are your "independent" raters drawing on one feed?
- **Refuter:** one intervention that restores both conditions in all failure types with no categorically different residue left over. Or two frames with measured low overlap whose agreement still carries zero information about the world. Either would collapse this sub-claim.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that all false claims spread more than all true ones, or that any named mechanism is the cause.
- Not that corrections always backfire. This packet records a genuine conflict: Nyhan and Reifler (2010) report backfire effects; Wood and Porter (2019), with 10,100+ subjects across 52 issues, report "no corrections capable of triggering backfire". The figures were not changed to fit either result; the correction condition was split into subtypes.
- Not that a better warning label solves C2. A label is a frame-internal cue.
- Not that adding more fact-check articles increases reach. "A library that grows is not a reach that grows" (Formula step 5).
- Not a theory of everything. There is no last room (Fig 7 :6).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the case was settled "not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." Concretely:

1. A habitat-substitution experiment holding claim text fixed (tests C1).
2. A two-factor measurement of bracket fidelity and ground pressure in a live cascade (tests C1).
3. A standing-to-recompose test after a refusal versus after a warning (tests C2).
4. A source-discriminator that names which mechanism, if any, is necessary across habitats (tests the honest stop).

2. Read the Operators table :58 and :73 and Fig 5 :53-55. Decide whether your correction intervention is a refusal or an added item.

4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
