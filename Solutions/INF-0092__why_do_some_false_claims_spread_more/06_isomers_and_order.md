## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are C1 and C2 isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Written out, C1 is a **sequence plus a comparison**: `frame → (reward ⋛ ground) → frame`, over many handoffs. C2 is a **refusal**: `¬(claim)`, which removes standing to compose over rather than adding a term (Fig 5 :55).
- Same atoms (ground, frame, R), different bonds (`→` and `⋛` versus `¬`), decisive difference in behaviour (one compounds agreement, the other removes standing). By Fig 12's definition they are **isomers**, not variants of one condition.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if C2 can be written as just another `→` step in the same chain, with no ¬ and no change in standing to compose over, the isomer claim fails and the two conditions collapse into one chain (a functional group at best).

### Q2 - What order do they run in?

- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."
- Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
- Dependency: a refusal cannot reach a frame that never received the claim, so C1's transmission is upstream of C2's delivery. But the refusal must fire **before** the claim is composed over: Formula step 2 sorts REFUTED before MINT, and Fig 5 :53 says "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so". So the operative order is:
  1. describe the residual in effect terms (Fig 9 :27-28);
  2. install the vocabulary for "this is false" (Fig 3 middle link; Operators table :58, "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate");
  3. refuse before the claim accrues standing (Formula step 2; Fig 5 :55);
  4. only then run the transmission chain with ground pressure restored (Fig 7 :37-39).
- Most intervention effort lands at step 4 or later, where the measurements already exist (more fact checks, more warnings), which is Fig 3 :29-30's warning.
- **Falsifier:** a population in which corrections reliably restore ground contact while no shared term for falsity exists in the receiving frames. That would invert steps 2 and 3 and break the dependency order, though not the union reading.

### Q3 - Does the animal analogue survive this order?

- van de Waal, Borgeaud and Whiten (Science 2013) report that "wild vervet monkeys will abandon personal foraging preferences in favor of group norms new to them." No language is required for the C1 chain: conformity runs on social contact alone.
- This is a **structural analogue**, not a human mechanism. Fig 4 :20: "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." The kernel (same closure handed along) crosses the scale; the anchor is re-named at the new scale (food preference and group norm, not truth).
- It corroborates the shape of C1 from a field with no shared evidence pool with political communication. It says nothing about C2: the vervet study has no ¬, and that is exactly why the refusal step is a separate condition.
- **Falsifier:** evidence that the vervet conformity required a ¬-like representation of the alternative as false. That would collapse the analogue into the human correction case and remove the low-overlap witness.

### Q4 - Why a repeated claim can become self-maintaining

- Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
- Fig 7 :14: "blending: novelty swamped to the mean." Each retelling is both output of the last handoff and input to the next. When the sign of that loop is positive (each share rewards the next share) and ground pressure is zero, the claim closes a cycle that a plain chain cannot close.
- Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is." A claim that regenerates attention across retellings behaves as a catalyst; a claim whose novelty is spent behaves as a spent part. Nothing in the claim's text records which.
- **Falsifier:** a cascade that decays to zero with no external ground contact and no change in habitat. Under a closing cycle it should not; under a spent part it should. The two must be distinguishable by an instrument, not by hindsight.

## CONCLUSION

The two conditions are **isomers**: same atoms, different bonds, different behaviour. Their dependency order is describe → install ¬ → refuse before mint → restore ground pressure in the chain. The animal-behaviour analogue supports C1 only and leaves C2 intact as a separate condition. A repeated claim becomes self-maintaining by a Fig 12 cycle, not by the sheer length of a chain. All four claims are `specified`, ungrounded, and each carries a falsifier.
