## Wider literature that bears on the residual, and how it was used

1. **Vosoughi et al. and Berger and Milkman** corroborate the habitat finding (F1): what travels depends on what the audience already holds and on arousal, not on the text alone. They are from network science and marketing respectively, so their agreement is low overlap on this point.
2. **Pennycook et al.** corroborates the room-chain finding (F2): repetition raises perceived accuracy even when the story is flagged as disputed. This is the clearest world-side demonstration that a warning is not a refusal.
3. **Guess et al.** corroborates the bracket-fidelity factor (F3), but its authors name several candidate mechanisms and the data are observational. It is a budget filter, not a verdict.
4. **Nyhan and Reifler vs Wood and Porter** is a real conflict about whether corrections backfire. It was not averaged away. It was returned to the census and resolved by splitting C2 into an ordinary channel and a mutual-update channel (Fig 2 :13). No world source flipped a figure verdict.
5. **van de Waal et al.** corroborates the shape from animal behaviour with no shared evidence pool: conformity can override a private reading without language.
6. **Centola and colleagues** supply the network-science vocabulary of thresholds and reinforcement, which is the room chain's ρ ≈ 1 condition in another field.

Overlap declaration: Vosoughi, Pennycook, Guess, Nyhan and Wood all draw on Western political-communication and social-media pools in places. They count as one pool on shared claims, not as five independent witnesses. The animal and marketing sources are genuinely low overlap.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22** - "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Sending the full architecture before anyone has described the residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape and the refuter; offer the links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** - "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** - "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the article.
- **Seat Map :163** - "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention." A mass send selected for people who already agree produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 2 :40** - "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Later agreement is never pooled as a second witness.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the two conditions and their refuter; (2) ask what the recipient would count as a failure and record it; (3) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## What to do with a refutation

If a recipient returns a refutation, copy it into `11_audit.md` with provenance and have the auditor seat re-evaluate it as if it came from any other source. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. Do not argue from prestige, and do not treat a polite agreement as evidence.
