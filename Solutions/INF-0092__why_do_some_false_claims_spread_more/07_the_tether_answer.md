# The Tether's answer on false-claim spread

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 8), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question "why do some false claims spread more effectively than accurate information, even when corrective evidence is available?" unpacks into two conditions with different instruments (Fig 9 :54-57): why a low-residual claim stays low across many tellers, and why supplying the ground's reading fails to close the gap. "Spread" and "correction failure" are not one problem at two strengths. They are a union of overlapping conditions, and a single lever clears one region and leaves the intersection standing (Fig 9 :55).

## 2. The shape

1. **Spread is claim-and-habitat, not claim alone.** Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." A claim's text does not determine its reach; the audience's history and the conditions around it do.
2. **An agreeing chain is one long room.** Fig 7 :13: "agreement compounds, evidence does not." Fig 7 :36: "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced." Ten thousand people who heard the same feed are one frame wearing many names (Fig 8 :49). Repetition raises familiarity, not evidence: Fig 7 :14, "blending: novelty swamped to the mean."
3. **Spread has two factors.** Fig 7 :37: "Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:" Fig 7 :38: "the bracket's fidelity across the handoff" Fig 7 :39: "the ground's pressure on the room." A claim travels far when fidelity to social reward is high and ground pressure is near zero. Either factor at zero stops genuine progress.
4. **Correction is a refusal, not another claim.** Fig 5 :53: "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so." Fig 5 :55: "What it removes is not a term but a term's standing to be composed over. Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach." Operators table :58: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate." A "Disputed" warning is a frame-internal cue, not a refusal.
5. **The ground does not decay; the channel does.** Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Fig 10 :26 names the failure modes: "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings." Fig 10 :28: the output is "you have lost touch", not a verdict about the claim.
6. **The frame cannot certify its own completeness.** Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." What settles a claim is a verifiable record that no frame can move (Fig 2 :29-30), not the number of people who repeat it. Fig 1 :26 calls "credibility accrued" frame-internal.
7. **The two conditions are isomers.** Over the same atoms (ground, frame, R), C1 uses a sequence and a comparison, C2 uses a refusal. Same atoms, different bonds, different behaviour (Fig 12 :117). Their order is describe → install ¬ → refuse before mint → restore ground pressure in the chain (Formula step 2; Fig 3 :20).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that all false claims spread more than all true claims. The world result is a tendency with categories and conditions, and the framework does not turn it into a law (Fig 1 :19, per-slot residual).
- Not that any named mechanism (novelty, arousal, partisan identity, age, digital literacy) is *the* cause. The framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32). Those names are bindings from the world frame, provenanced as such.
- Not that corrections always backfire. That claim conflicts with Wood and Porter 2019 and was returned to the census and split into subtypes (`01` section 6, re-census R2).
- Not that a better warning tag solves C2. A tag is a cue inside the frame; a refusal requires ¬ and two-way contact (Fig 5 :55; Operators table :58).
- Not that a fact-check campaign adding more items increases reach. A library that grows is not a reach that grows (Formula step 5).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement in this folder pays for the shape.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the case was settled "not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." Here the corresponding tests are:

- **Habitat substitution:** does a claim's propagation distance change when the ground's payment for it is removed, with the text held fixed? (F1)
- **Low-overlap chain:** do tellers selected against the residual surfaced by the previous teller produce less agreement-driven spread than a same-feed chain? (F2)
- **Two-factor measurement:** with bracket fidelity and ground pressure independently measured high, does the false claim still out-travel the accurate one? (F3)
- **Negation test:** can a frame lacking a term for refusal drop a claim's standing to be composed over, with no new atom? (F4)
- **Source discriminator:** which mechanism, if any, is necessary across habitats? Until one is found, the slot stays open. (F9)
- **Standing falsifiers:** a ground that moves because a community changed its mind; two frames with measured low overlap whose agreement still carries zero information; one lever restoring both conditions in all failure types.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** *The Hard Problem is About Source* warns that several paths can create the same reading, and that an instrument which cannot distinguish them "demonstrates its own inadequacy." Retweet counts, engagement metrics and belief self-reports all read a behavior. None comments on the source. The answer stops where the instruments can no longer distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name a single cause of misinformation spread, and it does not claim that every correction fails. It says the question is two questions. First, a false claim travels far when the chain of tellers keeps the ground's pressure near zero and keeps high fidelity to what earns social reward; each retelling adds agreement, not evidence, so an agreeing crowd is one long room, not many witnesses. Second, a correction is a refusal, not another claim: it removes the claim's standing to be built on, and it fails when the receiving frame has no term for "this is false" or the verifiable record is no longer reachable. The two conditions are isomers over the same atoms and run in a dependency order: describe the residual, install the refusal, refuse before the claim accrues standing, then restore ground contact in the chain. What the framework offers is that shape, the falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. Which particular mechanism fills the slot stays open until a source-discriminating instrument settles it.
