## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the two-part phenomenon: some false claims travel farther and persist longer than accurate information, and providing corrective evidence often fails to close the gap. Option A: treat this as one hard problem and name a single mechanism such as novelty, identity or inattention. Option B: treat it as a union of two conditions with different instruments, keep their verdicts separate, and stop at a shape until a source-discriminating instrument is held. What each leads to: A names a source from a reading that cannot discriminate among sources, spends the independence of the people checking it, and offers a lever that clears one region and leaves the intersection standing. B offers two checkable conditions, written refuters, and an open slot until the ground settles it. The residual in effect terms is R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| per claim and per person, where Γ predicts the social return of sharing or correcting and o′ is what the verifiable record returns.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Gap between what a person's library predicts about a claim and what the ground returns, both during spread and after a correction. Arity: three-place (frame, action, ground return), indexed per claim. Symmetry: ordered in time, with a threshold comparison for "more effectively"; not symmetric. Scale: single person, passing chain, attention habitat across handoffs. Fig 1 :19 indexes R per slot, and Fig 9 :54-57 names the whole a union compound.
- **Domain 1, individual belief-and-sharing loop.** Actors: person, Γ, belief b, weighting w. Currency: residual reduction, fluency and novelty reward. Anchor: verifiable truth of the claim and consequences of acting on it, named as a candidate in Fig 2 :29. This is a partition boundary question about which compositions a frame can carry.
- **Domain 2, transmission chain.** Actors: tellers, audiences, claim as content. Currency: exposure, repetition, agreement. Anchor: per-handoff ground contact, the two factors of Fig 7 :37-39. Where "spread" happens.
- **Domain 3, correction and refusal frame.** Actors: corrections, the ¬ operator, fact-checkers. Currency: reach, refusal, standing to be composed over. Anchor: the ground, what settles the claim (Fig 2 :45).
- **Domain 4, attention habitat.** Actors: ranking systems, incentives, novelty, emotion, age cohort, digital literacy. Currency: contact, cascade, supply of claims. Anchor: the ground (what actually helps or harms); the habitat updates, the ground does not (Fig 11 :73).
- **Excluded candidates X1 and X2.** "The claim is intrinsically stickier" has no anchor independent of the receiving habitat. "People are gullible" has no instrument independent of the claims they meet. Both are absorbed into D4 and D1 respectively (Fig 11 :49, :40).
- **Order by dependency.** Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); install the term for falsity at the middle link (Fig 3 :24-27); refuse before mint (Formula step 2); then restore ground pressure in the chain (Fig 7 :37-39).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; no single mechanism is named. Spread is a property of claim-and-habitat, an agreeing chain is one long room with two factors (fidelity and ground pressure), and correction is a refusal that fails when the frame lacks ¬ or the anchor is unreachable. The two conditions are isomers over the same atoms, with a dependency order: describe, install the refusal, refuse before mint, restore ground contact. A repeated claim can close a self-maintaining cycle (Fig 12 :121), and a claim that regenerates attention behaves as a catalyst (Fig 12 :119). Which particular mechanism fills the slots is `gap`, not a silent failure: Fig 9 :32 returns a kind of thing, never the answer, and the source-discriminating instrument is not held here. This is a `specified`, ungrounded result from a simulated derivation.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet is mode-labelled, states refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a recording instead of a generator, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and its refuter. A second cost is naming a mechanism prematurely, which is the metamerism error: an instrument that reads a behavior cannot comment on its source.
- **undoable:** yes. No mechanism name is shipped, so none has to be withdrawn. The two conditions can be re-described separately if a refuter fires, and `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already keep them separate. A sent packet can be superseded by a corrected one without deleting any record (Fig 5 :55: nothing leaves the library).
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A habitat-substitution experiment holding claim text fixed; a cascade in which fidelity and ground pressure both measure high yet the false claim still out-travels the truth; a frame with no ¬ that still drops a claim's standing; a ground that moves because a community changed its mind; or a source-discriminating instrument that names one necessary mechanism across habitats. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
