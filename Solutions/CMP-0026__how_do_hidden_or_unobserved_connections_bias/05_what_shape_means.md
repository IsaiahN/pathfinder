## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical figures README states the limit: the framework reports *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

For CMP-0026 the shape is therefore **not** an edge list. It is a constrained description of what a hidden connection would have to be: a bond between named operands whose absence is invisible in an ingredient-only record, whose behavioral effect may sit two or three steps out in the contact cascade, and whose status (depth versus genuine absence) cannot be settled from inside the same closure. That constrains an answer. It does not supply one.

`SPACE(???)TIME` is the exemplar's shape. The equivalent here is `observed nodes + (???) bonds -> per-slot conclusions`, with the `???` deliberately open.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary already held that is closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. *"With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."*
- *Library of Babel* on bundles: if the final search fails, *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually."* This is Fig 9 :54-58 in prose and is why the four error conditions are unbundled in `01` section 1.3.
- Fig 6 :30: "outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."*
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."*
- The stopping rule in *Library of Babel*: *"you will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place."* Here that means the answer stops where the instrument stops returning a discriminating residual, and the stop is recorded.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* A specific hidden edge is named when an instrument pays for it: a perturbation, a measurement, a reconstruction validated against a held-out ground truth.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23; Fig 8 :51-52), with a source gate and a shadow test, debited against independence.

Without either, naming a particular edge is the searcher's own contribution and must be recorded as such. Fig 8 :51: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them," (continued :52). Two people who infer the same edge, one from dynamics and one from a prior, have produced identical content with different standing.

## 4. Why naming an edge from a projection is the specific forbidden error

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. An instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source"*; the source might be one green light, yellow plus blue, RGB, CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* The spectrometer, not an argument, resolved it: *"It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."*

That maps directly onto network inference under hidden connections. Guimerà and Sales-Pardo's reconstruction study is the empirical version: many adjacency matrices are consistent with the same noisy observation, so the observation does not determine the source graph. Picking one and calling it the hidden truth is the metamerism error. Fig 8 :54: *"underivable is not unknowable"*, so the answer is a better instrument or a provenanced import, never a name read off the projection.

The same essay's warning about compression applies to "coverage" and "reconstruction accuracy" as single numbers: *"Settling compresses the description and not what it cost to establish"* (Fig 12 :107). A coverage percentage keeps the number and loses the lineage that produced it.

## 5. Q1 - ANSWERED: what shape does this question return?

**The framework returns the following, and nothing more.**

1. A hidden connection is a missing or wrong **bond**, so the omission is invisible in the ingredient-only record (Fig 12 :5, :14-16).
2. The residual is **per conclusion slot**, never one global bias number (Fig 1 :19, :22, :28).
3. The bias mechanism has **four overlapping conditions**, a union not a partition (Fig 9 :54-58): absent edge, hidden node, spurious edge, truncated boundary.
4. Hidden common sources corrupt both **correlation** and **corroboration** (Fig 2 :6-8, :18-21, :39-40).
5. Behavior errors are often **second or third order** in the contact cascade (Fig 11 :52-53).
6. **Depth and genuine absence are indiscernible from inside** the same closure (Fig 6 :27, Fig 8 :39).
7. The observed to inferred round trip has a **gap that must be measured** (Fig 4 :30), priced in bits and actions as two currencies (Fig 12 :125-127).
8. Which particular edge is hidden in a given system is a declared **`gap`**, an unreachable verdict, not silence (Fig 13 :134, Fig 8 :39).

## 6. Correction to my own sections 3 and 4

I initially wrote the shape-not-a-name rule as if it forbade naming any particular edge at all. That overstates it. The README's phrase is *"what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* The subject is a primitive not held in the library. A bond is already in the vocabulary (Fig 12 defines seven), and a candidate edge is a binding of held atoms, not an invented eighth primitive.

So the narrowed rule is: naming a candidate edge is **not forbidden**; it is a binding that requires provenance or an instrument. What remains forbidden is (a) inventing a new primitive to explain the edge, (b) claiming the binding moves the wall when it adds no atom (Fig 6 :22: *"IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure"*), and (c) reading the source of a graph off a projection without an instrument that discriminates among sources. My section 4 metamerism point is **not retracted**. The reason it stands is the missing discriminator, not a blanket ban on naming held things. Both readings are recorded so neither is quietly lost.

This matches the correction carried in the exemplar's `05` section 5b and `06` Q1, reached here independently from the README wording.

## 7. Two consequences for outreach, and the status of this document

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."* A campaign that gathers well-known people to agree that hidden links matter is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask gives the claim a budget to be tried and lets a ground settle it.

**This audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* So `01` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of the claim and must be labelled as such. Nothing in this folder is `grounded` on the strength of the world sources either: they corroborate shape claims but do not settle the framework's derivation.
