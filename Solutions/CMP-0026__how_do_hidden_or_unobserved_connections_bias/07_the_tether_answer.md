# The Tether's answer: how hidden connections bias conclusions

**Mode: `specified` for the shape and all per-domain verdicts, `general` for the substrate rule, `gap` for which particular edge is hidden. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a measurement. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

An observed graph is a set of entities plus recorded relations. Some real relations were not recorded, some recorded relations are spurious, some apparent relations run through unmeasured entities, and the system boundary may be truncated. From that record people conclude structure (centrality, clustering, communities, paths, assortativity) and behavior (spread, synchronization, robustness, cascade reach). The residual is the gap, per conclusion slot, between what the incomplete record supports and what the full system would settle. It is a union of four overlapping conditions, not one hard question (Fig 9 :55-57).

## 2. The shape, per domain, kept separate

**D1, the bond grammar of the record.** The hidden connection is a missing bond. Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Fig 12 :16: "and nothing signals that anything was lost". A node list with omitted edges is notationally indistinguishable from a correct, sparser graph, so the bias is invisible from inside. Two graphs over the same nodes with different edges are isomers (Fig 12 :117): *"different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* The framework does not say which edge, only what a missing edge would have to be.

**D2, the inference instrument.** There is no global bias direction. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Some measures are sensitive (betweenness, distance), others robust (in-degree, transitivity), and the direction can reverse with the missing-data mechanism (world: Kossinets 2006; Smith and Moody 2013). Depth and genuine absence are indiscernible from inside one closure (Fig 6 :27, Fig 8 :39), so the honest answer to "which edge is missing" is a declared `gap`, not a fabricated name. SEARCHED, INWARD and OUTWARD are three questions and must be kept distinct (Formula step 7).

**D3, the behavior frame.** Network is a structure (Fig 13 :23-24), and influence travelling over it is a flow composed from a state and a spendable gradient (Fig 13 :76). A hidden edge is therefore a structural change whose behavioral consequence is a **compound** with the process, not a property of the adjacency matrix. The effect is usually not local: Fig 11 :52-53 gives the DDT case, where *"the residual that mattered was three steps out."* So a graph can look locally fine and still produce a wrong cascade, threshold or robustness conclusion.

**D4, the verification frame.** A hidden common cause makes two observed nodes correlate with no observed edge, and makes two analysts agree from one evidence pool (Fig 2 :6-8, :18-21). Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Whether an edge was observed, derived independently or imported is part of the quantity: Fig 8 :51-52, *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."*

The four verdicts are never averaged. They have different actors, currencies and anchors (see `01` section 1.2).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any particular edge is hidden in your system. That is a `gap`, an unreachable verdict, not silence (Fig 13 :134).
- Not one bias number, not "more edges always means more X". The residual is per slot (Fig 1 :19, :22, :28).
- Not that imputation yields truth. The observed to reconstructed to observed round trip has a gap that must be measured (Fig 4 :30). Bits and actions are two currencies that do not add (Fig 12 :125-127).
- Not that coverage or reconstruction accuracy settles anything. Those are filters for the budget, not witnesses for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that negation is priced. The framework requires ¬ to refuse a recorded edge but leaves its cost open (Operators table :77-78). This is a live strain, recorded in `01` F8 and `11`.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken by this agent; world sources corroborate the shape but do not settle the derivation.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer settled it. Here:

1. **Edge-discriminating observation:** a perturbation, time series or intervention that returns whether a candidate bond actually carries influence, at a resolution where the current instrument returns nothing discriminating.
2. **Hidden-node instrument:** an observation that returns the mediating node or its effect, so that a mediated correlation can be separated from a direct edge (Fig 2 :6-8).
3. **Round trip test:** reconstruct, then compare the reconstruction's properties against a held-out ground truth; report `R_T` as a gap, not accuracy (Fig 4 :30).
4. **Per-slot refuters:** apply the six conditions in `01` section 4. Any one that fires withdraws its finding, not softens it.
5. **Ground for the union:** settle each of the four conditions separately before asking about "the incomplete network" as a whole (Fig 9 :54-58).

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable in principle.** Fig 8 :54: *"The atom cannot be derived from inside A. But the union is searchable from contact, so underivable is not unknowable."* Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,". The answer stops where the instruments stop discriminating, and the lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."* A frame cannot certify its own completeness, so the verdict stays `candidate` until a ground pays for the specific bond.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The figures do not name a hidden edge and do not give a single bias number. They say that a connection is a typed bond, and an omitted bond is invisible in a node-and-edge record because the arrangement is the half nothing was recording; two graphs over the same nodes with different edges are isomers, different substances whose behavior can differ decisively while the ingredient formula looks identical. The bias is indexed per conclusion slot, so betweenness can move while in-degree stays put, and a hidden common cause both creates correlation and makes two analysts agree from one evidence pool. Behavioral errors are usually second or third order in the contact cascade, so a graph can look locally correct and still give the wrong spread or robustness answer. A hidden-but-real bond and a genuinely absent one look the same from inside one closure, and a search that finds nothing is not proof of absence. What the framework offers is the shape any answer must have, six refuters written in advance, and the instrument questions that would close it; the particular edge stays an open `gap` until a ground settles it.
