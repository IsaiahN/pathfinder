## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what a hidden or unobserved connection does to conclusions about network structure and system behavior. Option A: report one general bias direction and a single correction recipe. Option B: keep the shape the figures license, separate the four overlapping error conditions, report verdicts per conclusion slot and per process, and declare the particular edge a `gap` until an instrument settles it. ## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The per-slot gap between conclusions drawn from the observed graph and conclusions the full system would settle. Arity: a relation between at least two operands, often with a third unobserved operand mediating them, so a bond question. Symmetry: which bond holds is not recoverable from the operands; absence uses subtraction, refusal needs negation. Scale: first order in the graph, second and third order in the contact cascade, plus a round trip between observed and inferred graphs. Fig 1 :19 governs: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
- **Domain 1, bond grammar of the record.** Actors: entities as atoms, edges as typed bonds, the seven bonds plus negation. Currency: bits to state the arrangement and actions to settle an edge (Fig 12 :125, :126). Anchor: the figure definitions of bond, molecule and isomer, and the ground for any measurement. Question type: partition at first order but a union of four overlapping error conditions at the level of the question.
- **Domain 2, the inference instrument.** Actors: observer, instrument, observation, generative model family, hidden nodes. Currency: residual and reliability. Anchor: the ground, which does not update (Fig 2 :29). Question type: three distinct questions, SEARCHED, INWARD and OUTWARD (Formula step 7).
- **Domain 3, the behavior frame.** Actors: processes, flows, gradients, cascades. Currency: influence travelling over a network structure. Anchor: a measured process outcome. Question type: a compound of structure with state and gradient, not a property of the adjacency matrix (Fig 13 :74, :76).
- **Domain 4, the verification frame.** Actors: outside frames, reviewers and their instruments. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement (Fig 2 :35, :45). In this simulated run all four domains draw on one evidence pool, so their agreement is one pool talking and this domain's independence is zero.
- **Order by dependency.** Enumerate the habitat before modelling edges (Fig 11 :43); unbundle the union before searching (Fig 9 :54); describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); separate SEARCHED, INWARD and OUTWARD; measure the round trip gap (Fig 4 :30).

Strain test applied: the first pass read network as a state. Re-census assigns network to structures (Fig 13 :23-24) and flow to a state-structure-state composition (Fig 13 :74, :76). Different quantities, so no strain remains. One strain is live and named: negation is required to refuse a spurious edge but its price is open in the Operators table.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; a single bias direction and any named hidden edge do not. A missing connection is an unrecorded typed bond, so its omission is invisible in the ingredient-only record; two graphs over the same nodes with different edges are isomers whose behavior can differ decisively. The residual is per conclusion slot and per process, never a global number. The question is a union of four overlapping conditions, so the conditions are unbundled and each keeps its own verdict. Hidden common sources corrupt both correlation and corroboration. Behavioral effects are usually second or third order in the contact cascade. A hidden-but-real bond and a genuinely absent one are indiscernible from inside one closure, so the specific edge is a declared `gap`. The observed to inferred round trip carries a nonzero gap measured in bits and actions, and refusing a spurious edge needs negation, whose price remains an open item. This is `specified` and `candidate`, not `grounded`.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states the shape, six refuters and the provenance of every quote, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending an edge list or a single bias number instead of a generator. Fig 2 :42 says "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and the refuters.
- **undoable:** yes. The per-slot verdicts and the open price of negation are removable or refinable without touching the bond grammar or the substrate. A published claim can be superseded by a corrected packet; no primitive has to be withdrawn. By contrast, a specific fabricated edge would be much harder to undo, which is why it is withheld.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An edge-discriminating observation that returns whether a candidate bond carries influence would name the slot; a hidden-node observation separating mediated correlation from a direct edge would break the hidden-common-source reading; a held-out ground truth with a measured `R_T` would settle the reconstruction claim; a representation carrying arrangement with no bond term, or two graphs with different edges and identical behavior under all processes, would break the isomer finding. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
