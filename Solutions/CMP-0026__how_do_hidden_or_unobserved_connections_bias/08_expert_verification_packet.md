# Expert verification packet - hidden connections and network conclusions

**What this is:** a claim in a form a network-inference researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` for the shape and the per-domain verdicts, `general` for the substrate rule, `gap` for the particular hidden edge. Not `grounded`.** No measurement at a ground is claimed by this agent.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A hidden connection is a missing or wrong typed bond, and an omitted bond is invisible in an ingredient-only record because nothing downstream signals that the arrangement was lost. Two graphs over the same entities with different edge sets are isomers under the figure definition, so conclusions about behavior can differ decisively while the recorded structure looks identical. The bias is indexed per conclusion slot, never as one global number; four overlapping conditions (absent edge, hidden node, spurious edge, truncated boundary) form a union with its own loop; hidden common sources corrupt both correlation and corroboration; and behavioural effects are usually second or third order in the contact cascade. A hidden-but-real bond and a genuinely absent one are indiscernible from inside the same closure, so the specific edge is reported as a `gap`. The observed to inferred round trip has a measurable gap priced in two currencies. The framework offers the shape, the refuters and the instrument questions, not an edge list.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 12

- **Statement:** a hidden connection is a missing bond, and the omission carries no signal in an ingredient-only record. Two graphs over the same nodes with different edge sets are isomers: same atoms, different bond, different substance.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :5 - "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Fig 12 :14-16: "the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not"; "nothing downstream can tell them apart,"; "and nothing signals that anything was lost". Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- **Refuter:** exhibit a representation that carries the arrangement with no bond term, so two different edge sets over the same nodes give the same recorded structure and the same downstream behavior; or exhibit two such graphs that are identical in behavior under every process on the substrate.

### C2 - measurement-facing; checkable against the network-inference literature

- **Statement:** the bias has no single direction or magnitude. It is per measure, per process and per system, and the missing-data mechanism can reverse the sign. Hidden common causes both create correlation without an observed edge and produce false corroboration between analysts drawing on one evidence pool.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 - "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 - "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 2 :7 - "(hidden shared source)"; Fig 2 :19-21: "they never talk, and still agree"; "so the agreement is the pool talking,"; "not two frames confirming each other". Fig 2 :40 - "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool".
- **What to check:** is the per-slot claim consistent with what you know of centrality, clustering and distance under missing data? Smith and Moody (Social Networks 2013) and Kossinets (Social Networks 2006) report exactly this variation, including sign reversal by mechanism. Is the hidden-common-source claim consistent with hidden-node inference results (Huang 2015, J. Phys. A)?
- **Refuter:** show one identical bias function of missingness for all measures, processes and systems; or show that hidden nodes change inferred couplings only by adding variance and never direction.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation with a named live strain

- **Statement:** the four error conditions form a union to be unbundled before search; depth and genuine absence are indiscernible from inside the same closure; the observed to reconstructed to observed round trip has a nonzero gap measured in bits and actions; and refusing a spurious edge requires the unary operator negation, whose price is open.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 - "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57 - "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Fig 6 :27 - "Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same." Fig 8 :39 - "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 4 :30 - "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." Fig 12 :125 - "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Operators table :73-74: subtraction is *"the absence of an operand"* while negation is *"the refusal of a claim"*, and *"What a unary operator costs is open"* (:78).
- **Refuter:** show an instrument at the same closure and resolution that separates a hidden-but-real edge from an absent one without new observation or import; or show a reconstruction with zero round trip gap for every property at every error rate; or refuse a false-positive edge using only the seven binary bonds with the refusal priced inside the bond formula.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that any particular edge is hidden in a named system. That is a `gap`: Fig 9 :32 - "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"; Fig 13 :134 - "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence".
- Not one bias number, not "more edges always means more spread, more robustness or more X". Fig 1 :19, :22, :28 govern per-slot residuals.
- Not that imputation recovers truth. Guimerà and Sales-Pardo (PNAS 2009) themselves find that below about 10 percent observation error the observed network can outperform the reconstruction.
- Not that coverage, reconstruction accuracy or number of searchers is a verdict. Fig 9 :60 - "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."
- Not a derivation of a specific network measure's sign from the figures. The direction claims come from world sources and are secondary.
- Not that negation has a price in the current framework. Its price is an open item in the Operators table and is recorded as a live strain in `01` F8 and `11`.
- Not `grounded`. This packet is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. An edge-discriminating observation that returns whether a candidate bond carries influence at a resolution where the current instrument returns nothing discriminating.
2. A hidden-node observation that separates a mediated correlation from a direct edge.
3. A held-out ground truth for a reconstruction, with `R_T` reported as a gap rather than accuracy.
4. A per-slot refutation using the six conditions in `01` section 4.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 12 :5, :14-16 and :117, Fig 1 :19 and :22, and Fig 2 :39-40. Six short passages.
2. Check C2 against your own field: do betweenness and in-degree really respond differently to missing data, and can the sign reverse with the mechanism?
3. Name one instrument that would separate a hidden-but-real edge from a genuinely absent one, or say why none exists at your current resolution.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
