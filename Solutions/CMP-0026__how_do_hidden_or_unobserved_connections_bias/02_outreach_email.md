**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the six refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 have been checked against the figures in your own copy. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about missing links, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about network inference. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** An observed graph is a set of entities plus a set of recorded relations. Some real relations were not recorded, some recorded relations are spurious, some apparent relations run through unmeasured nodes, and the system boundary may be truncated. From that record people conclude both structure (centrality, clustering, communities, paths) and behavior (spread, synchronization, robustness). These four conditions overlap, so they are a union with its own loop, not one hard problem.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** Three things follow from the figure definitions alone:
>
> 1. A node list with some edges omitted is notationally indistinguishable from a correct, sparser graph. Fig 12 :5 says the bond is *"the half nothing was recording"*, and Fig 12 :16 says *"nothing signals that anything was lost"*. So the bias is invisible from inside the record. Same nodes, different edges, is an isomer claim: *"different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour"* (Fig 12 :117).
> 2. There is no single bias direction. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Betweenness moves under missing data, in-degree and transitivity often do not. Each measure, each process and each system keeps its own verdict; averaging them throws away the signal that says where to look.
> 3. A hidden common cause makes two observed nodes correlate with no observed edge, and makes two analysts agree from one evidence pool. Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* A hidden edge three steps out can change a cascade even when local structure looks fine (Fig 11 :52-53).
>
> **What I am not claiming.** Not that any particular edge is hidden in your system. The specific edge is a declared `gap`: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (Fig 9 :32), and *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"* (Fig 13 :134). Not that imputation gives the truth: the observed to reconstructed to observed round trip has a gap that has to be measured (Fig 4 :30). Not that a bigger network or a higher coverage number settles anything; those are filters for the budget, not witnesses for the verdict (Fig 9 :60). Not `grounded`: this is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a measurement.
>
> **What would refute it, written before this message was sent:**
> 1. A representation that carries the arrangement with no bond term, so two different edge sets over the same nodes give the same recorded structure and the same behavior.
> 2. One identical bias function of missingness for all measures, processes and systems.
> 3. An instrument at the same closure and resolution that distinguishes a hidden-but-real edge from a genuinely absent one without new observation or import.
> 4. Hidden nodes that change only variance and never the direction of an inferred coupling.
> 5. A reconstruction whose round trip gap is zero for every property at every error rate.
> 6. A false-positive edge refusable with only the seven binary bonds, priced inside the bond formula.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers, local to this workspace: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`.
> - The canonical usage rule, local copy: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_READONE_canonical.md` (file name in the workspace is `FIGURES_README_canonical.md`).
> - Network Structure Inference: A Survey, Brugere, Gallagher and Berger-Wolf, ACM Computing Surveys, https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.00782
> - Missing and spurious interactions and the reconstruction of complex networks, Guimera and Sales-Pardo, PNAS 2009, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2799723
>
> **If you want to use the method:** the formula is in the figures. Run it on a network problem you can check against a ground, write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - [your name]
