## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are two graphs over the same nodes, with different edge sets, isomers?

- Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
- Fig 12 :46: *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."*
- Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance."
- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :97-98 (the chemistry case): "This is not an analogy. Chemistry hit it first: silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different properties, which is what forced" structure into the notation.

A graph is an arrangement of entities under a typed relation. Two graphs with the same entity set and a different relation set are therefore **isomers** under the figure definition: same atoms, different bond, different substance. Their difference is invisible in the ingredient-only formula and decisive in the behavior, which is exactly why an observer who records nodes and omits edges can be confidently wrong. The name "isomer" here is not an analogy imported from chemistry; the figure applies it to any composition with the same atoms under different bonds.

**Caveat, kept visible.** Under a symmetric bond (Fig 12 :59 "swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No."), k operands have k! spellings and one term, so an edge list that treats (A,B) and (B,A) as two edges overcharges by log(k!). Under an ordered bond (Fig 12 :72 "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."), different orders are different terms and the charge is correct. Whether a given empirical "connection" is symmetric or ordered is a measurement, not a notation choice, and Fig 13 :36 says *"adjacency is not duration, an ordering is not a pathway, a pathway is not a direction."*

### Q2 - Isomer or functional group?

- Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Two incomplete observations of different systems can share a **functional group**, for example a hub-and-spoke pattern with different hub entities, and still be different substances. If only the motif pattern is shared, the functional-group reading is correct; if the full entity set is shared and only the arrangement differs, the isomer reading is correct. The distinction is a level decision under Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom".

**Falsifier for Q1 and Q2:** exhibit two graphs over the same entity set with different edge sets that have identical behavior under every process on the substrate. That would break the "decisive in the behaviour" clause of Fig 12 :117.

### Q3 - In what order are the four conditions searched?

- Fig 9 :56: "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."
- Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

The dependency order from the primary audit (perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, carry) applies **inside each condition**. Across conditions the rule is unbundle first (Fig 9 :54), then search each with its own instrument. The practical order is:

1. **Boundary truncation** first, because it changes the habitat list and therefore what the other three conditions even mean (Fig 11 :43-44). Enumerate the habitat before modelling edges.
2. **Hidden nodes** second, because a spurious-looking edge between observed nodes may be a real mediated path through an unobserved one (Huang 2015; Fig 2 :6-8).
3. **Missing edges** third, using an instrument already returning a residual (Fig 6 :34).
4. **Spurious edges** fourth, because refusal needs the unary operator ¬ and its price is open (Operators table :73-78).

This order is a dependency order, not a ranking of importance. An error in step 1 makes steps 2 to 4 diagnose a graph that was never the right habitat.

### Q4 - Which analogues are licensed?

The framework permits an analogue only after the residual is described in effect terms (Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking"; :28: "any frame you pick will seem to fit"). The residual is: a typed relation is absent or wrong, and the behavioral consequence may be non-local. Three analogues pass that test:

| analogue | frame | what it supplies | figure status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Link prediction | statistics and network science (Lü and Zhou 2011; Liben-Nowell and Kleinberg 2007) | an inward instrument that ranks candidate edges by a score computed from the observed graph | INWARD; it extends access without adding an atom (Fig 6 :29) |
| Hidden-node and network tomography inference | signal processing and statistical physics (Huang 2015; Ching and Tam 2019; arXiv:2306.17364) | a model of the unobserved node's influence on the observed subgraph | OUTWARD import candidate; needs a source gate and shadow test (Fig 8 :51-52) |
| Measurement-error reclassification | social network measurement (Wang et al. 2012; Smith and Moody 2013) | the vocabulary of false negative, false positive, aggregation and disaggregation | a binding onto held distinctions (absence versus negation), not a new primitive |

**Convergent derivation versus adopted import.** If two of these frames independently arrive at the same candidate edge shape, that is corroboration at maximum independence (Fig 8 :50). If one imports the other's prior without a record, the contents look identical and the surplus is zero (Fig 8 :51-52). The ledger is what separates them.

### Q5 - What does a settled reconstruction become at the next level?

- Fig 12 :104 (quoted above). Once a reconstruction is settled against a ground, it can serve as an atom for a higher composition, for example a comparison of two reconstructed networks.
- Fig 12 :107-110: where the arrangement is not carried across a boundary, *"the result carries forward and the deriving does not, so every lineage pays again"*. A published edge list without its lineage forces the next study to re-earn the inference.
- Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Send the generator, not the list.

## CONCLUSION

Two graphs over the same entities with different edge sets are isomers under Fig 12 :117: same atoms, different bond, different substance, invisible in the ingredient formula and decisive in behavior. If only a motif is shared, the weaker functional-group reading applies (Fig 12 :124). The four error conditions are unbundled before search, then ordered by dependency (boundary, hidden nodes, missing edges, spurious edges), with the boundary first because it determines the habitat. Three analogues are licensed after the residual is described: link prediction as INWARD, hidden-node inference as a gated OUTWARD import, and measurement-error classification as a binding onto held distinctions. The round trip gap is measured; a settled reconstruction becomes an atom at the next level only if its lineage travels with it.
