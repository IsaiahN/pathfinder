## Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual line before relying on the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is what fails to register about welfare when the slot, observation and objective are fixed. Arity: four-place, because the objective is a separate operand, not a property of the other three. A three-place measurement model cannot see the question. Symmetry: aggregation over persons is symmetric, so permuting persons leaves the total unchanged; the objective is an ordered comparison, so swapping operands changes the answer. Scale: slot, aggregate, intergenerational chain; the transform is lossy. This is a **union compound** (Fig 9 :55), not one hard question.
- **D1 Measurement.** Actors: agencies, accounts, instruments. Currency: quantities. Anchor: the thing measured, only if the question is constitutively about it. Admitted.
- **D2 Objective.** Actors: modellers, planners, welfare functions. Currency: residual reduction under a declared objective. Anchor: the ground after the objective is fixed. Admitted. This is the load-bearing domain.
- **D3 Ground selection.** Actors: experiments, audits, affected persons. Currency: what pays. Anchor: a ground that does not update. Admitted.
- **D4 External checking.** Actors: differing closures. Currency: imports with provenance. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Admitted.
- **X1 Value-free economics by definition.** No nameable anchor for a welfare question. Candidate refused, not silent.

Order by dependency: describe the residual, choose a legitimate anchor, bind the objective as an ordinary term, then measure, aggregate, optimize, settle. A value choice cannot be checked after the fact by the frame that made it (Formula, "Direction").

My census matches the primary's four domains and one refused candidate. Where the primary derived from Fig 3 and Fig 12 for D2, I derived from Fig 9's union/partition split and Fig 2's anchor legitimacy. Different starting figures, same domain boundaries: low overlap between routes, so the agreement is weak but not circular within the framework. It is still one evidence pool.

## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, the gap between what was sent up and what came back down. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07` and `08`: "objective selection is non-neutral, execution can be neutral, which values is a gap."
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete policy claim: "this cost-benefit analysis is value-laden because its discount rate is a verdict", or "this GDP target is illegitimate as a welfare anchor".

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **type of the operands of ⋛** at the level of a distribution versus a threshold (the Operators table restricts ⋛ to two quantities; a welfare objective often compares a distribution with a standard);
- the **requirement of a legitimate anchor**, which the shorthand compresses into "non-neutral" and which must be re-stated per question before any concrete claim is made;
- the **gap on which values**, which the shorthand can make sound like a settled position when it is only a declared stop.

One thing survives cleanly: a declared objective can be executed neutrally against a ground, and choosing the objective is a comparison that decides outcomes. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is a condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set R_T to zero, which is the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
