## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the claim that economics can be value-neutral. Option A: the choices of what to measure and optimize are conventions declared before the work, so economic statements describe the world only. Option B: those choices embed ethical and political judgments, because the objective is a comparison that decides outcomes. What each leads to: A preserves the positive/normative distinction but hides a verdict inside a declared convention and gives outside checkers no purchase; B names the exact link at which the value choice enters, keeps neutral execution as a separate surviving claim, and declares the choice of which values as a gap.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** What fails to register about welfare when the slot, the observation and the objective are fixed. Arity: four-place (frame, action, observation, objective). Symmetry: aggregation over persons is a symmetric bond, permuting persons leaves the total unchanged; the objective is an ordered comparison, so swapping operands changes the answer. Scale: single slot, population aggregate, intergenerational chain; the transform is lossy. Fig 9 :54-57 makes this a union compound, not one hard question.
- **D1 Measurement.** Actors: statistical agencies, accounts, instruments. Currency: measured quantities (GDP, CPI, QALY, DALY). Anchor: the thing measured, only if the question is constitutively about it. Admitted.
- **D2 Objective and optimization.** Actors: modellers, planners, welfare functions, discount rates. Currency: reduction of R under a declared objective. Anchor: the ground, reachable only after the objective is fixed. Admitted; decisive link.
- **D3 Ground selection and settlement.** Actors: experiments, audits, affected persons. Currency: what pays, not what is asserted. Anchor: a ground that does not update on any frame's opinion. Admitted.
- **D4 External checking.** Actors: frames with differing closures (philosophy, other disciplines, affected communities). Currency: imports with provenance. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Admitted.
- **X1 "Pure positive economics, value-free by definition".** Actors: the detached observer. Currency: prediction accuracy. Anchor: none nameable for a welfare question. Candidate refused, not silent (Fig 13 :134).
- **Union, not partition.** The four domains are overlapping conditions. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55-57).
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Describe the residual first, choose a legitimate anchor, bind the objective as an ordinary term, measure per slot, aggregate, optimize, settle. Effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it.

Strain test applied: the claim that Friedman 1953 conflicts with Fig 10 :47 was re-censused. Fig 10 governs conventions that decide outcomes; Friedman governs prediction claims before an objective is applied. Different objects, no strain. The claim that Mongin 2005 conflicts with F5 was re-censused: Mongin rebuts *strong* non-neutrality, while F5 claims non-neutrality only at the objective link. The verdict was narrowed, not smoothed.

**D2 Objective, status triangulated (Fig 3, Fig 12 and Operators table, least-instrumented link vs comparison bond).**

- Reading: value embedding at the objective link is structural, not a removable defect. Mode `specified`. Refuter: a policy ranking with no quantity, threshold or operand ordering selected.

**D3 Ground and legitimacy, status triangulated (Fig 2 and Fig 10, anchor test vs declared stakes).**

- Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." -> an anchor must be nameable.
- Fig 2 :36 "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." -> legitimacy is question-relative.
- Fig 2 :37-38 "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure." -> a market is an instrument for welfare questions.
- Fig 10 :59 "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying." -> declare the stake.
- Reading: economics can be settled against a ground, but the ground must fit the question; market output is not the ground for welfare. Mode `specified`. Refuter: a welfare question whose answer is constitutively about predictive accuracy.

**D4 External checking, status triangulated (Fig 6, Fig 7 and Fig 8, import vs room vs union).**

- Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." -> more economic data cannot supply the value criterion.
- Fig 7 :35 "A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." -> outside frames are not a jury.
- Fig 8 :50-52 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ." -> provenance is the whole issue.
- Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool" -> do not pool agreement as corroboration.
- Reading: values enter as imports with a source gate and a shadow test; agreement is not evidence. Mode `specified`. Refuter: a panel selected for agreement that added a new atom to the closure.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

**The figure-derived verdict, kept per domain and never averaged:** economics is not value-neutral at the selection of the objective, because the objective is a comparison requiring a quantity and a threshold and a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention; it can be neutral in the execution of a declared measure; it can be checkable when the objective is written down as an ordinary term and ranged against a legitimate ground; aggregation hides the distributional judgment; which values ought to be adopted is a declared gap. Mode `specified`. The world sources corroborate the narrowed shape from low-overlap fields and supply two conflicts that were settled by re-census, not by overruling the figures.

- **Cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. If the verdict is wrong, the error is in one of four named places (objective as comparison, aggregation symmetry, anchor legitimacy, or external-check import), each with a recorded figure line and a refuter. The larger practical cost is spending an outside checker's independence by sending a finished conclusion instead of a refutable shape, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable.
- **Undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails; a refutation is added and the reach falls because something was added rather than because anything was struck out (Fig 6 :42-43, "What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall."). The shape and its four falsifiers are recorded with provenance so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-64).
- **Reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A measure settling a welfare choice with no outcome-deciding constant; a frame-internal check of the question's direction; a figure line governing a welfare weight; an aggregate preserving every per-slot residual without extra declaration; or a source with a genuinely different evidence pool showing that the objective link carries no comparison. Until one of these fires the verdict stands as `candidate`.
