## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not whether economic models predict well. It is whether the choices that make prediction possible, namely which slot to measure, which quantity counts as the observation o′, and which direction of change counts as progress, can be held outside the claims that use them.

- Option A, **value-neutral**: those choices are conventions declared in advance and then held constant, so the resulting statements are about the world and carry no ethical or political standing. Milton Friedman's canonical form is *"Positive economics is in principle independent of any particular ethical position or normative judgments"* (source opened 2026-11-23).
- Option B, **value-embedding**: the objective is a comparison, and a comparison requires a chosen quantity and a chosen threshold. A convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention, so the judgment is already inside the result. The honest remedy is declaration and external checking, not a pretence of neutrality.

What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a *shape* stating which links in the chain carry a value choice, which links can be neutral once the choice is declared, what instrument would discriminate a convention from a verdict, and what would refute the whole distinction. Until it is bound it predicts nothing and no check in the loop can see it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is a specified form of Option B, kept per domain and never averaged: not value-neutral at the objective link, neutral in execution after declaration, and `gap` on which values ought to be adopted.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the economics question, and no claim that one welfare function is the correct one.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A list selected for agreement is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
