# The Tether's answer on value neutrality in economics

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive verdict, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks whether the choices that make economic measurement possible, namely which slot is tracked, what observation counts, and which direction of change counts as progress, can be held outside the claims that use them. The residual is what those choices hide. It is a **union compound**, not one hard question: D1 (what to measure), D2 (what to optimize), D3 (which ground settles it), and D4 (who may check) are overlapping conditions (Fig 9 :54-57). A single lever such as "add more indicators" clears the region where only D1 holds and leaves the others standing.

## 2. The shape, per domain, never averaged

1. **Selection of the objective is not neutral.** The objective is link 3 of the dependency chain, *"it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms"* (Fig 3 :9-11), and it is the least instrumented link in the chain (Fig 3 :25-27). A goal is a comparison (Operators table :52), a comparison needs a threshold, and a convention that decides an outcome is a verdict: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"* (Fig 10 :47). This is the decisive finding, mode `specified`.
2. **Execution of a declared measure can be neutral.** Once the slot, the observation and the objective are written down, the calculation can range against a ground without further value choices. Fig 1 :23: *"The ground enters as the only metric."* Fig 2 :36: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* This part of Friedman's claim survives, mode `specified`. Treating the whole of economics as non-neutral is an overstatement the figures do not support.
3. **Aggregation hides the distributional judgment.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Under a symmetric bond, permuting persons leaves the total unchanged, so whose loss counts is invisible in the aggregate. Publish the distribution alongside the total.
4. **A market is not the right anchor for a welfare question.** Fig 2 :36-38 gives the legitimacy rule and names human judgment as the right anchor for a question about what is good for a person. A market is *"an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."* Using market output as a welfare score is that error.
5. **Which values ought to be adopted is a declared `gap`.** No figure line selects a welfare function, a discount rate or a distributional weight. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Naming one would be a binding from a human frame, provenance owed. "Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence."
6. **Checking must come from outside the economic frame.** Fig 5 :61-63: *"Every check above runs on the MECHANISM and none runs on the DIRECTION OF THE QUESTION"*. External frames contribute imports with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), never votes. A room selected for agreement is a room, not an anchor (Fig 7 :35).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that all economic statements are value-laden. Prediction claims within a declared frame are testable and can be neutral in execution.
- Not that values are arbitrary or that "anything goes". The ground is not negotiable, and the objective must be stated as an ordinary term so it can be refused (Formula, "Direction").
- Not that one welfare function, one discount rate, or one index is correct. That is the `gap`.
- Not that consensus among economists settles a value question. Agreement is a convention at best, and a disqualifying correlation at worst (Fig 2 :40).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement in this folder distinguishes a convention from a verdict at the ground.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

- **Convention or verdict?** Does a constant that can change a policy outcome remain checkably inert? An instrument here is a sensitivity test that flips the constant and records whether the policy recommendation changes. If it changes, Fig 10 :47 applies.
- **Distribution preserved?** Does the published reporting preserve per-slot residuals, or does it hide them in a total? This is directly observable.
- **Legitimate anchor?** Is the question constitutively about the thing the anchor measures? Test by changing the anchor and asking whether the original question still makes sense.
- **Ground behaviour of measures:** does repeated high-stakes use of the measure as a target produce drift (Goodhart) or corruption (Campbell)? Both are observable in two low-overlap fields.
- **Standing falsifiers from `01`:** a measure settling a welfare choice with no outcome-deciding constant; a frame-internal check of the question's direction; a figure line governing a welfare weight; an aggregate preserving every per-slot residual without extra declaration.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The objective has been narrowed to a shape, the gap on which values is declared, and the instruments that would close it are named in effect terms. The essays add the decisive caveat: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible, which is worth a great deal, because a visible choice can be revised and an emergent one cannot. But visibility is not neutrality."* That is where this answer stops. The record of the route is what keeps it checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that economics is worthless or that every economic statement is political. It says that the two halves of the question must be kept apart. Once a measure, an observation and an objective are declared, the calculation can be neutral in execution and can range against a ground. But choosing the objective is not neutral, because an objective is a comparison that needs a quantity and a threshold, and a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention. Aggregating across people hides whose loss counted, and a market price is an anchor only for questions constitutively about markets, not for questions about what is good for people. Which values ought to be adopted is a declared gap: the framework returns a shape, not a name. What it offers is the shape any answer must have, four falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close the gap. The slot stays open until the ground settles it.
