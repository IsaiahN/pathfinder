## 0. The claim under audit

> Option A: economics can be value-neutral. Choices about what to measure and optimize are conventions declared before the work, and the resulting statements describe the world only.
>
> Option B: those choices inevitably embed ethical and political judgments. A measure that decides an outcome is no longer a convention, and the judgment is in the result whether or not it is declared.

**Sought-for shape, bound before the run** (Formula, "Direction"): a shape that (a) locates the link at which a value choice becomes load-bearing, (b) distinguishes a neutral execution of a declared choice from a neutral choice of the objective, (c) names an instrument that would discriminate a convention from a verdict, and (d) states what would refute the distinction. It is bound here as an ordinary term so it can accrue a residual like anything else.

## 1. The residual, in effect terms

An economic frame Γ predicts Γ(b,a) for an action a under belief b; an observation o′ arrives; R = |Γ(b,a) − o′| is measured per object slot s (Symbols table :38-44). The question is not the size of R. It is the provenance of the three things that must be fixed before R can be computed at all:

1. **Which slot s is tracked.** A national account, a household, a patient, a species, a future person.
2. **What o′ counts as.** Market output, a price index, a health measure, a self-report.
3. **Which direction of change counts as progress.** The objective, which is a comparison, not a quantity (Operators table :52).

These are held in the frame before any measurement. Fig 9 :30-31 warns that even the describing words are borrowed: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."* The residual for this audit is therefore: **what fails to register about welfare when the measurement and the objective are fixed, and whether that failure can be declared without changing the answer of the frame that fixed them.**

## 2. Domain census before choosing any figure line

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.**

- *Arity:* four-place in effect (frame, action, observation, objective). The objective is a separate operand, not a property of the other three. A three-place model of measurement cannot see the question at all.
- *Symmetry:* aggregation over persons is a symmetric bond. Fig 12 :92: *"Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term, so a code that charges for the sequence overcharges by log₂(k!) while the bond term undercharges. Both wrong, in opposite directions, and only one is usually noticed."* A total is invariant to permuting persons, so the political choice hides in what was summed. The objective is an ordered comparison: swapping operands changes the answer.
- *Scale:* single slot, population aggregate, and intergenerational chain. The transform between them is lossy (Fig 4 :30).

**One line per domain. A domain needs actors, a currency, and a nameable anchor.**

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Measurement | statistical agencies, accounts, instruments, datasets | measured quantities: GDP, CPI, QALY, DALY | the thing measured, only if the question is constitutively about it (Fig 2 :36) | admitted |
| D2 Objective and optimization | modellers, planners, welfare functions, discount rates | reduction of R under a declared objective | the ground, reachable only after the objective is fixed | admitted |
| D3 Ground selection and settlement | experiments, audits, affected persons, proof | what pays, not what is asserted | a ground that does not update on any frame's opinion (Fig 2 :29-30) | admitted |
| D4 External checking | frames with differing closures: philosophy, other disciplines, affected communities | imports with provenance | the ground, never agreement | admitted |
| X1 "Pure positive economics, value-free by definition" | the economist as detached observer | prediction accuracy | none nameable once the question is about welfare | candidate refused, see F4 |
| X2 "Values are purely private preferences outside method" | the individual | taste | already inside D2 | absorbed |

**Partition or union.** A union, not a partition. D1 through D4 are overlapping conditions. A single lever, such as adding more indicators, clears the region where only D1 holds and leaves D2, D3 and D4 standing (Fig 9 :54-57). Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

**Dependency order (Fig 3).** Describe the residual first (Fig 9 :27-28), then choose a legitimate anchor (Fig 2 :36), then bind the objective as an ordinary term (Formula "Direction"), then measure, then optimize, then settle. Effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it (Fig 3 :20). The objective is link 3 of the chain, *"it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms"*, and it is exactly the link with no instrument.

**X1 is not a domain yet.** It has no anchor for a welfare question. A ground that moves when a frame pushes on it is not a ground (Fig 2 :30). It is retained in the census as a refused candidate so the refusal stays visible.

## 4. Findings

### F1 - The objective is the least instrumented link in the chain (decisive for D2)

Fig 3 :9-11:

> **"it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms"**

Fig 3 :25-26:

> **"being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."**

Fig 3 :27:

> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

Fig 3 :19:

> **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**

Economics has strong instruments for perception (data collection) and planning (models), and weak ones for saying what counts as progress. That asymmetry is the structural reason value judgments are embedded but invisible. It is not a failure of individual economists; it is a fact about where the instruments are. **Mode `specified` for economics; `general` for the chain claim.** Triangulated across Fig 3 and Fig 12 (the objective is a comparison bond). Refuter: exhibit an instrument that says what would count as progress in the frame's own terms and that is independent of the objective it is supposed to justify. World corroboration: Schroeder 2019 opens with *"Many economic measures are structured to reflect ethical values."*

### F2 - A goal is a comparison, so a threshold is unavoidable

Operators table :52:

> **"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"**

Fig 12 :111-112:

> **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**
> **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."**

Fig 12 :99-101:

> **"A bond costs what it could have been, and nothing more."**

⋛ is *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* (Fig 12 :87; Operators table :49). Choosing the two quantities and the threshold is not optional if the frame is to say that one policy is better than another. The choice records an option not taken, which is what the bond price measures. This is where ethical and political content enters by construction. **Mode `specified` for the welfare application, `general` for the bond claim.** Triangulated across Fig 12 and the Operators table. Refuter: show a policy ranking that can be derived without selecting a quantity, a threshold, or an ordering of operands. World corroboration: the SEP entry on Arrow's Theorem records that *"Arrow himself took them to be 'value judgments'"* (SEP, 2025 revision).

### F3 - Aggregation hides the distributional judgment

Fig 1 :19 and :22:

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

Fig 1 :27:

> **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."**

Fig 12 :92 adds the symmetry mechanism: permuting persons under a symmetric bond leaves the total unchanged. So a single national figure can look value-free while deciding whose loss counts. Fig 1 :23: *"The ground enters as the only metric."* The ground for a question about people is not the produced aggregate. **Mode `specified`.** Triangulated across Fig 1 and Fig 12. Refuter: show an aggregate that preserves every per-slot residual without an additional declaration of whose slots are weighted how. World corroboration: Goodhart 2013 and Campbell 1976 (two different fields, one mechanism) show what happens when an aggregate becomes a target.

### F4 - X1 refused: the value-free claim fails the anchor test

Fig 2 :29-30:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."**
> **"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."**

Fig 2 :36-38:

> **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."**
> **"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."**

Friedman's *"Positive economics is in principle independent of any particular ethical position or normative judgments"* is defensible for questions constitutively about prediction, and it is an instrument, not an anchor, for questions about welfare. Using prediction accuracy to settle a welfare question is using an instrument on an axis it does not measure. **Mode `specified` for the mapping to economics, `general` for the anchor rule.** Triangulated across Fig 2 and Fig 10. Refuter: show a welfare question whose answer is constitutively about predictive accuracy rather than about people. World source Friedman 1953 is recorded as **conflicting as written** with the figure verdict and was returned to the census, not used to flip it. After re-census it survives only as a claim about D1 execution, which is the part the figures permit.

### F5 - A convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one

Fig 10 :47-49:

> **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**
> **"a convention nothing can check is a constant the seat authored, carrying the seat’s authority"**
> **"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."**

Fig 10 :46: *"A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do."*

This is the shortest figure-grounded answer to the question. A discount rate, a welfare weight, or a choice of index is a convention only while it does not decide a policy outcome. The moment it does, it is a verdict in the vocabulary of a convention. Neutrality in the sense of "no value content" is therefore not available at D2, but neutrality in the sense of "declared, checkable, and not hidden" is available. **Mode `specified`.** Single-source on this exact sentence (Fig 10), with world corroboration from two low-overlap fields: Goodhart's *"any observed statistical regularity will tend to collapse once pressure is placed upon it for control purposes"* and Campbell's *"The more any quantitative social indicator is used for social decision-making, the more subject it will be to corruption pressures and the more apt it will be to distort and corrupt the social processes it is intended to monitor."* Refuter: show a convention that decides an outcome and remains checkably inert, that is, a constant whose value no affected party could contest.

### F6 - The direction of the question cannot be checked from inside

Fig 5 :61-63:

> **"A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term. It is not bound, it predicts nothing, and it accrues no residual, so nothing here can see it."**
> **"Every check above runs on the MECHANISM and none runs on the DIRECTION OF THE QUESTION"**

Fig 5 :65:

> **"The remedy is a discipline rather than a branch: make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM."**

An economic frame can test its models, its estimators and its data. It cannot test whether "welfare" was the right thing to aim at, because that aim is a preference held by the frame, not a term it holds. The consequence is not that values are arbitrary. It is that the check must come from outside the frame (Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*). **Mode `general` for the limit, `specified` for the economics case.** Single-source within Fig 5, triangulated with the Formula "Direction" paragraph and Fig 9 :32 (*"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*). Refuter: show a frame-internal procedure that checks the direction of its own question and is not merely checking a term it already bound.

### F7 - Which values are correct is a genuine gap

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*

No figure line selects a welfare function, a discount rate, or a distributional weight. After a genuine re-census of D2 and D4, the gap is not the data and not the model. It is the absence of a legitimate anchor for the question "which values ought to be adopted." **Mode `general`.** Mark `gap` plainly. World corroboration: Mongin 2005 rebuts the strong non-neutrality thesis while defending a weak one; SEP reports Samuelson's line that *"It is a legitimate exercise of economic analysis to examine the consequences of various value judgments, whether or not they are shared by the theorist."* Both support a disciplined transparency, not a canonical value set. Refuter on the gap: produce a figure line that governs a particular welfare weight, or a ground reading that settles one. Until then, naming one would be a binding from a human frame, provenance owed.

### F8 - External frames are imports with a source gate, not votes

Fig 6 :23:

> **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**

Fig 6 :30:

> **"outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."**

Fig 8 :50-52:

> **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."**

Fig 7 :35: *"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*

Philosophers, affected communities and other disciplines are not a jury. They are frames whose closures differ from the economic frame, and what they contribute is an import with provenance and a shadow test. Agreement selected for receptiveness is the collapse at Fig 2 :40. **Mode `specified`.** Triangulated across Fig 6, Fig 7 and Fig 8. Refuter: show that a panel selected for agreement with the economic frame added a new atom to the closure rather than a vote.

### F9 - Declared stakes, not pretended absence of them

Fig 10 :59-60:

> **"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."**
> **"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."**

Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

A metric designer has a stake in the metric. That is unavoidable. What is disqualifying is an interest in which value the ground returns today. The practical rule is declaration: name who chose the measure, who benefits from the threshold, and what would change if the other operands were chosen. **Mode `specified`.** Triangulated across Fig 10 and Fig 2. Refuter: exhibit a measurement practice in which the choice of measure has no beneficiary and no contestable alternative. World corroboration: Friedman and Kahn/Borning/VSD both make explicit that design and method choices have value consequences; VSD's *"Value Sensitive Design is a theoretically grounded approach to the design of technology that accounts for human values in a principled and comprehensive manner throughout the design process"* is an engineering-level parallel for declaration.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the seat map's *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A measure is demonstrated that settles a welfare policy choice while ranging against a legitimate ground and containing no constant that decides an outcome (breaks Fig 10 :47).
2. A frame-internal procedure is shown to check the direction of its own question without first binding that direction as an ordinary term (breaks Fig 5 :61-63).
3. A figure line is found that governs a particular welfare weight, discount rate, or distributional index (closes the F7 gap).
4. An aggregate is exhibited that preserves every per-slot residual without a further declaration of whose slots are weighted how (breaks Fig 1 :19-22 and Fig 12 :92).
5. Repeated high-stakes use of a measure as a target shows no drift, corruption, or proxy substitution in two independent fields (weakens the world corroboration at F5; does not by itself flip the figure verdict, which would return to census).

If any of 1 to 4 fires, the shape is withdrawn or re-censused. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Replace "value-neutral" with two separate claims: neutral execution of a declared measure, and non-neutral selection of the objective. Do not average them (F1, F2, F10).
2. State the objective as an ordinary term before the run, with its operands and threshold named (F6, Formula "Direction").
3. Treat every constant that can decide an outcome as a verdict with provenance, not as a convention (F5).
4. Keep per-slot residuals visible; publish the distribution alongside the aggregate (F3).
5. Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong: two low-overlap frames, not a large agreeable room (F8, Fig 2 :42).
6. Say `gap` on which values ought to be adopted. Do not name a welfare function as if the framework returned a name (F7).
7. Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Send the derivation and the refuter, not a finished conclusion.

### Three questions at the limit, kept distinct

- **SEARCHED.** Is the value choice in here and unfound? Within D1, yes: it is a held atom of measurement theory and more data will not remove it (Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."*). Asked first; cheapest.
- **INWARD.** Extend the instrument. What would sharpen our own terms? Per-slot distribution tables, sensitivity analysis over thresholds, and declared objective functions are instruments already returning something. They refine the reporting of a value choice, they do not license it.
- **OUTWARD.** Import from a frame whose closure differs: moral philosophy, affected communities, other disciplines. Fig 6 :30 requires the description to travel in effect terms. Source gate, shadow test, and a debit against independence. INWARD and OUTWARD are opposed and were never read against one another.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives in a narrowed form: **economics cannot be value-neutral at the selection of the objective, because the objective is a comparison that decides outcomes and a convention that decides outcomes has stopped being one; it can be neutral in the execution of a declared measure, and it can be checkable when the objective is written down as an ordinary term and ranged against a legitimate ground.** Which values ought to be adopted is a declared `gap`, not a hidden answer. Strains after re-census: one, the source conflict with the strong non-neutrality thesis, resolved by narrowing the claim, not by smoothing it.
