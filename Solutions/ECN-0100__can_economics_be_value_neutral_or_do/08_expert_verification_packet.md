# Expert verification packet - the Tether on value neutrality in economics

**What this is:** a claim in a form an economist, economic methodologist or philosopher of economics can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the verdict, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No measurement in this folder claims to have distinguished a convention from a verdict at the ground. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Economics cannot be value-neutral at the selection of the objective, because an objective is a comparison needing a quantity and a threshold, and a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention. It can be neutral in the execution of a declared measure, and it can be checkable when the objective is written down as an ordinary term and ranged against a legitimate ground. Aggregating across people hides whose loss counted, and a market is an anchor only for questions constitutively about markets. Which values ought to be adopted is a declared gap, not a hidden answer. What is offered is the shape any answer must have, four falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close the gap.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 3, 10 and the Operators table

- **Statement:** the objective link is the least instrumented link in the chain, and a comparison that decides an outcome is a verdict, not a convention.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 3 :9 *"it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms"*; Fig 3 :25-26 *"being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."*; Fig 10 :47 *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*; Operators table :52 *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*.
- **Refuter:** exhibit an economic objective that ranks policies with no threshold relation between two quantities, or a convention that changes a policy outcome yet remains checkably inert.

### C2 - economics-facing; checkable by an economist against actual practice

- **Statement:** neutral execution after declaration is a real property of an economic calculation, but the declaration itself is a value choice; per-slot distribution is lost in aggregation; a market price is an instrument, not a legitimate anchor, for a welfare question.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*; Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*; Fig 2 :36 *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."*; Fig 2 :37-38 *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."*
- **What to check:** take one published cost-benefit analysis. Is the objective function and its threshold stated before the results? Are distributional weights explicit? Does the reporting preserve per-slot outcomes? Is the discount rate defended as a convention or as a value? A single counterexample to all four would damage C2.
- **Refuter:** show a welfare policy ranking whose objective, threshold and weights are all declared and whose aggregate preserves every per-slot residual without an additional weighting choice; or show a welfare question whose answer is constitutively about predictive accuracy.

### C3 - framework-internal limit; checkable by reading Figures 5, 8, 9

- **Statement:** the direction of the question cannot be checked from inside the economic frame, so the check must come from a low-overlap frame, and which values ought to be adopted remains a gap.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :61-63 *"A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term. It is not bound, it predicts nothing, and it accrues no residual, so nothing here can see it."* / *"Every check above runs on the MECHANISM and none runs on the DIRECTION OF THE QUESTION"*; Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*; Fig 8 :39 *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
- **Refuter:** show a frame-internal procedure that checks the direction of its own question without first binding it as an ordinary term, or produce a figure line that governs a particular welfare weight, discount rate or distributional index.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that all economic statements are value-laden. Prediction claims inside a declared frame can be neutral in execution.
- Not that values are arbitrary. The objective becomes refusable once it is written down and ranged against a legitimate ground.
- Not that one welfare function, discount rate or index is correct. Which values is a `gap`.
- Not that consensus among economists settles the question. Fig 7 :35: *"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*
- Not `grounded`, and not a claim that this simulated derivation settles the question.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument.

1. **Convention or verdict:** flip the constant and record whether the policy recommendation changes. If it does, the constant was a verdict.
2. **Distribution:** publish per-slot residuals beside the aggregate and see whether the decision changes when they are visible.
3. **Anchor legitimacy:** change the anchor and ask whether the original question still makes sense. If it does not, the first anchor was an instrument, not a ground.
4. **Ground behaviour:** does repeated high-stakes use of the measure as a target produce drift (Goodhart) or corruption (Campbell)? Both are observable; neither was measured here.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 3 :9, :25-27, Fig 10 :47 and Operators table :52. Four short passages.
2. Check C2 against one cost-benefit analysis you know: is the objective and threshold declared up front, and are distributional weights visible?
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish a convention from a verdict in your field, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
