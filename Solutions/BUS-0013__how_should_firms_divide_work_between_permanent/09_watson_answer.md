## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about dividing work among four governance modes. Option A: answer with a fixed mix of permanent employees, contractors, suppliers and automated systems, justified by cost. Option B: answer with a per-slot shape in which each mode is a binding onto a distinct structural condition, each with its own ground, failure mode and refuter. What each leads to: A delivers a frame-internal average that cannot say which named work slot is failing and spends the independence of the people checking it; B delivers a checkable shape, written refuters, and an open naming slot until a ground settles it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The per-slot gap between what a firm's staffing library predicts it can deliver and what the market actually pays for. Arity: four-place (work slot, firm, allocation act, market return), ordered by time, so an ordered bond and a comparison bond. Symmetry: asymmetric in firm versus market and in time, so not a symmetric join. Scale: single worker, firm population with a shared library, inter-firm habitat, and the time channel across contract renewals. Fig 1 :19 indexes R per slot, never as a mix ratio.
- **Domain 1, the internal work loop.** Actors: employees, routines, the firm's Γ. Currency: actions and residual reduction. Anchor: realized market payment for the delivered work, per slot. Question type: composition inside a closure.
- **Domain 2, the external closure.** Actors: contractors, suppliers, their own Γ. Currency: deliverables and imported atoms. Anchor: delivered work with a source gate and shadow test. Question type: import, the only operator that moves the wall.
- **Domain 3, the composition of work.** Actors: tasks as atoms, seven bonds, negation. Currency: reach and |φ| cost, bits versus actions. Anchor: settlement against the ground. Question type: bond typing, isomer versus functional group.
- **Domain 4, the channel and the generator.** Actors: instruments, metrics, percepts, non-person generators. Currency: contact and fidelity. Anchor: the ground, which does not decay. Question type: generator versus recording, and channel maintenance.
- **Partition or union.** Union, not partition. D1/D3 and D2/D4 form two overlapping compounds; the four conditions C1 contact, C2 reach, C3 channel, C4 generator need separate instruments (Fig 9 :54-57).
- **Order by dependency.** Perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry (Fig 3 :4-15). The middle link and the carry link are the usual breaks.
- **Excluded.** A fixed optimal percentage (no nameable ground independent of the slot) and cheapest-source-as-verdict (a frame-internal quantity, Fig 1 :27). Filters, not domains.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the fixed mix does not. Divide work per unit, not by a global ratio. Keep inside the closure the work whose value comes from composing inside the firm's library; import from a differing closure the work whose value is an atom the firm lacks, with source gate and shadow test; treat the contractor's head start and the supplier's settled molecule as different bonds rather than different prices; admit automated work only as a generator that changes contact, never on a harness solve; keep the channel to the ground reachable; and expect valence to flip when the residual changes. The two currencies do not add. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate. The specific vendor, tool or ratio is a `gap`, not a silence: the framework returns a shape and the instrument that would name the source is not held here.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable, but not trivial. The world cost of a wrong division is paid at the per-slot ground: rework, lost capability, supplier dependence and skill decay. The framework's own cost is spending the independence of outside checkers by sending a recording instead of a generator. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes, in part. A governance bond is reversible at the margin, and real-options work (Foote and Folta 2002) prices that reversibility. The irreversible part is contact: an imported atom cannot be un-imported, and a decayed channel has to be rebuilt through use, not by editing a contract (Fig 10 :24). The packet is fully undoable; a committed hiring or outsourcing decision is not.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A source-discriminating instrument that names the atom an external arrangement supplied and shows the market paying for it; an automated system passing a ground test the slot previously failed with the channel independently measured as unchanged; a firm renewing reach with no import across a documented habitat change; a valence-invariant governance mode; or a single lever restoring all four conditions. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
