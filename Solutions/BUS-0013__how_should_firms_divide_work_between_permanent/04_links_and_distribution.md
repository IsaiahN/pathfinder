## Wider literature that bears on the residual

These are corroboration only. The verdict comes from the figures.

1. **Coase 1937**, the firm boundary. It corroborates that there is a real cost at the internal/external seam, and that the boundary is a margin, not a fixed ratio. It does not corroborate any particular mix. The figures add that the seam's cost is a filter for the budget (Fig 9 :60), not a witness for which mode keeps R small.
2. **Foote and Folta 2002**, temporary workers as real options. Corroborates reversibility and the value of deferring commitment under uncertainty. This matches Fig 12 :126: the price of finding out whether the arrangement holds is a different currency from the price of stating it.
3. **Bainbridge 1983**, ironies of automation. Corroborates that automation moves the hard residual into the human takeover task and that skill decays through disuse. This is Fig 10 :26 at a human-factors stratum, and it is why a generator needs a maintained channel, not just a clean harness result.
4. **Dekker and Woods 2002**, the MABA-MABA critique. Its abstract states that quantitative "who does what" allocation does not work because automation transforms practice. That is a direct low-overlap corroboration of the refusal to treat the four modes as fixed slots on an allocation list.
5. **Woods 2002**, the law of stretched systems. Corroborates that efficiency gains are consumed to raise tempo rather than to reduce demand, matching Fig 11 :40: an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing.
6. **Gordon 2015/2016**, social-insect task allocation. Corroborates that robust allocation is a live response to current conditions rather than a standing assignment of types to tasks, matching Fig 11 :49 to :51.
7. **CONFIRM 2009**, train-or-recruit queueing model. Corroborates that the internal/external mix depends on volatility and skill deterioration, not on a universal constant.

Overlap check: items 1, 2 and 7 are in or near the management/OR pool, so their agreement is one broad witness, not three (Fig 2 :31). Items 3, 4, 5 and 6 come from human factors, cognitive systems engineering and animal behaviour, so they are genuinely low-overlap with the framework, but they corroborate mechanism shape, not the firm-level verdict.
