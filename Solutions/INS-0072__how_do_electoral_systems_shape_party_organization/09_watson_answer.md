## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about a single lever (the electoral system, the rule mapping ballots to seats) and four outcomes (party organization, representation, polarization, accountability). Option A: treat the four as four effects of one lever and return a ranking of systems or a universal effect size. Option B: keep the four as a union of overlapping conditions with different anchors, return a shape and a refuter per domain, and record the identity of any best system as a `gap`. What each leads to: A delivers a `general`-mode verdict with no measurement and spends the independence of the people asked to check it; B delivers checkable per-domain shapes, written refuters, and an open slot until a ground settles it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** One rule applied to four outcomes. Arity: four, therefore a **union of overlapping conditions** (Fig 9 :55), not one question with four adjectives. Symmetry: the ballot to seat map is ordered and thresholded, so a symmetric influence reading is a type error; "shape" is one-directional. Scale: the rule is fixed at the room or constitutional scale, effects register at the party scale and the party-system scale, with a lossy membrane between (Fig 4 :20 to 22).
- **D1, the rule.** Actors: voters, candidates, parties, seats. Currency: ballots to seats conversion, district magnitude, thresholds. Anchor: the stated convention, which must not itself decide the outcome. Question type: a bond over held atoms, not an atom.
- **D2, party organization.** Actors: parties, candidates, organizers. Currency: entry cost, coordination, internal settlement. Anchor: a party's reproduced contact with its ground. Question type: method versus recording; overlaps D3.
- **D3, representation.** Actors: voters, parties, seats. Currency: vote share versus seat share. Anchor: the non-updating preference distribution. Question type: per-slot threshold comparison; overlaps D1 and D2.
- **D4, polarization.** Actors: parties, rooms, evidence pools. Currency: distance between rooms, agreement within rooms. Anchor: the ground that selects between rooms. Question type: room divergence; overlaps D3.
- **D5, accountability.** Actors: office holders, voters, a refusal seat. Currency: the capacity to refuse and the reach of the channel. Anchor: a non-updating ground the channel can reach. Question type: negation plus channel; overlaps all four.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry. Settle the convention before attributing effects; describe the residual before frame selection (Fig 9 :27 to 28); refuse before mint (formula step 2). The vocabulary-to-objective link is the likely break, because no instrument discriminates sources of a reading.

Strain test: Fig 8's union surplus and Fig 2's agreement-worthlessness look opposed, but govern different quantities (where a surplus lives when closures differ, versus what agreement means when they do not). Re-census dissolves the strain. The isomer claim is live-conditioned on a fixed atom set and is reported as such.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The union survives; the specific system and the numbers do not. The electoral system is a threshold bond over held atoms, so two systems over the same atoms are isomers under the fixed-atom condition. Party organization turns on method versus recording; representation is a per-slot comparison whose national index is a filter; polarization is room difference selected against a non-updating ground; accountability needs anchor, channel, and a refusal seat, and a rule that decides the outcome has stopped being a convention. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged. The best system and every effect size are a declared `gap` of the unreached kind, not silence and not an invitation to invent a number. Mode `specified` per domain, `general` on the union skeleton, not `grounded`.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. Every claim carries a mode, a verbatim figure line and a refuter, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The larger cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a theory before a shape, which Fig 2 :42 prices: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The `gap` is removable by a ground reading without touching the union skeleton or the four anchors. The specific binding of a system to an outcome is not asserted anywhere in the packet, so no claim has to be withdrawn to add one. A sent claim can be superseded by the corrected packet.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A per-slot instrument that discriminates sources would name the representation slot; a case showing accountability improving while its reference updates on the institution's own reports and no seat can refuse would break D5; two rooms sharing one evidence pool correcting each other independently would break D4; a reform adding a genuinely new primitive would break the isomer claim; a figure line returning a system or a number would break the `gap`. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
