# Expert verification packet: the Tether on electoral systems

**What this is:** a claim in a form a scholar of electoral systems can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so the reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the four per-domain shapes, `general` on the union skeleton, `gap` on any best system or numerical effect size. Not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

An electoral system is a typed threshold bond over held political atoms (voters, candidates, parties, seats), not a new atom; two systems over the same atoms are isomers, same atoms and different bond, hence different substance. The four named outcomes are a union of overlapping conditions with four different anchors, not four effects of one lever, and the verdicts must be kept separate. Party organization turns on method versus recording; representation is a per-slot threshold comparison, not a national score; polarization is a room difference selected against ground, not a mood; accountability requires a non-updating anchor, a reachable channel, and a seat able to refuse. The framework returns these shapes and their refuters. It does not name a best system or a number.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 Framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 9 and 12

- **Statement:** the four outcomes form a union, not a partition or a chain; the rule is a bond over held atoms, and two rules over the same atoms are isomers.
- **Source, verbatim.** Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- **What to check:** is the atom set the same under both systems, with only the rule changed? If a new party or a new office enters, the relation is composition plus import, not isomerism.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a reform that adds a genuinely new primitive, or show that two systems with the same atoms and the same bond are nevertheless different substances.

### C2 Field-facing, checkable by a scholar of electoral systems

- **Statement:** representation must be indexed per party slot and anchored in a distribution that does not update on the institution's own report; a single national score is a filter for the budget, not a witness.
- **Source, verbatim.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Operators :51: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one."* Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
- **What to check:** does a disproportionality index preserve the per-party live signal, or does it discard the very slot that would tell you where to look? The field's own effective-number-of-parties measure says the effective count is smaller than the raw count unless all parties are equally strong.
- **Refuter:** show a single index that retains the per-slot live signal and settles a causal claim without an external anchor.

### C3 Framework-internal plus field-facing, checkable by anyone who has seen an election audited

- **Statement:** accountability requires a non-updating ground, a reachable channel, and a seat able to refuse; a rule that decides the outcome has stopped being a neutral convention.
- **Source, verbatim.** Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*; Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Operators :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."*
- **What to check:** in a case you know, is the reference independent of what the office holder reports about it, and is there a seat that can actually refuse? If the reference updates on the institution's own reports, the channel is broken even when everything looks normal.
- **Refuter:** show accountability improving while the reference updates on the institution's own reports and no seat can refuse.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that first past the post, list PR, mixed member, or any other system is the answer.
- Not an optimal proportionality coefficient, polarization index, accountability score, or universal effect size.
- Not that Duverger's law is an exceptionless law. The field calls it a tendency.
- Not that expert agreement settles any domain. Seat Map :163: *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."*
- Not a derivation of one party system from another, and no new primitive.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by measurement.
- Not that the author's reading is disinterested. The interest in the outcome is declared under Seat Map :171: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."*

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminator and an independent frame, not more argument.

1. **Per-slot instrument:** can the vote-to-seat comparison be measured for each party slot without collapsing into a national index?
2. **Anchor test:** is the accountability reference a distribution that does not move when the institution reports on it?
3. **Room test:** do the competing rooms draw on different evidence, or one pool?
4. **Standing refuters:** a figure line selecting a system or a number; a new primitive; accountability without anchor and refusal; a cross-field measure that changes form; two shared-pool rooms correcting each other.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :55, Fig 12 :5 and :117, Fig 1 :19 and :22, and Fig 10 :47, :24 and :42. Eight short passages.
2. Check C2 against one dataset you know: does the standard disproportionality measure keep the per-party signal, or erase it?
3. Name one case where an accountability reference moved because the institution reported on itself, or say why none exists in your domain.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. Record your stage 1 verdict before reading any framework material.
