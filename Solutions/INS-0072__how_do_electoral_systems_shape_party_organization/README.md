## The answer in one line

The four named outcomes are a **union of overlapping conditions, not four effects of one lever**. The electoral system is a rule that fixes a bond between held atoms (voters, candidates, parties, seats); it re-arranges them rather than adding a new atom, so systems over the same atoms stand to each other as isomers. Per domain the figures return a shape and a separator, never a best system or a number: party organization depends on whether what crosses the boundary is a method or a recording; representation must be indexed per party slot and anchored in a non-updating distribution; polarization is a difference between rooms that matters only when the rooms differ and select against ground; accountability requires a non-updating anchor, a reachable channel, and a seat able to refuse. Where the rule decides the outcome it has stopped being a neutral convention.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of anyone by likelihood of agreement.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that a particular electoral system is the answer.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **A room does not upgrade the label.** Seat Map :283: *"A room does not upgrade the label. A room reaching a conclusion without the ground is agreement, and agreement among frames is a weighted average of influence. Adding people raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor."*
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** Seat Map :163: *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures exactly the correlation that makes their agreement worthless.
3. **Asking for trust before checking spends independence.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* The outside frame must not hold the interiors.

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one.
