## 0. The residual, in effect terms

The question asks how **one lever**, the electoral system, shapes **four named outcomes**: party organization, representation, polarization, accountability. Stated as an effect, the residual is:

> Given a change in the rule that maps ballots to seats, which downstream quantity changes, through what contact, and against what anchor that does not move when the institutions being measured report on themselves?

Three observations set up the census before any figure line is chosen.

1. **The verb "shape" is unbound.** The formula's "Direction" warning applies: *"the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it holds."* The remedy is to make the sought-for shape an ordinary term. Here an answer would look like: **a constraint on the form of each outcome, stated per domain, with its anchor and its refusal condition**, not a best system and not a number. That binding is written down now and made refutable in section 3.
2. **One lever, four outcomes, is a union, not a partition.** The four outcomes have different actors, currencies, and anchors. They overlap. One lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :54 to 57). Verdicts must be kept per domain and never averaged into an "electoral systems matter somewhat" score.
3. **The question hides a type error.** "Electoral system" names a **rule or bond**, not a new actor or atom. The atom set (voters, candidates, parties, seats, preferences) is already held. Changing the system re-arranges those atoms. Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*

An answer would bind each outcome to a figure-governed shape, mark the `gap` where no line governs, and state what would settle it. Anything that returns "FPTP causes X" is a verdict in `general` mode, the output-layer failure genus named at Seat Map :281.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.** Arity: one rule applied to four outcomes, therefore a union of four overlapping conditions (Fig 9 :4 to 5, :54 to 58), not one hard question with four answers. Symmetry: the ballot to seat map is ordered and thresholded (Operators :49 to 51), so a symmetric "influence" reading is a type error; the relation "shape" is one-directional. Scale: the rule is fixed at the room or constitutional level, the effects register at the party (agent) scale and the party-system (population) scale, with a lossy membrane between (Fig 4 :20 to 25).

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Settle the convention first (D1); then per domain, anchor before measurement; describe the residual before choosing any frame (Fig 9 :27 to 28). A reading taken below a broken link is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :20 to 23).

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | partition or union | status before figures |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R, the residual | one rule, four outcomes | change in downstream quantity | four different grounds, to be named | **union** of four overlapping conditions | described in effect terms above |
| D1, the rule itself | voters, candidates, parties, seats | ballots to seats conversion; magnitude; threshold | the stated convention, which must not decide the outcome | boundary question: which compositions the rule admits | a bond, not an atom |
| D2, party organization | parties, candidates, organizers | entry cost, coordination, internal settlement | a party's own ground contact and capacity to settle a program | overlap with D3 (the unit of competition) | method-versus-recording question |
| D3, representation | voters, parties, seats | vote share versus seat share | the non-updating distribution of preferences | overlap with D1 and D2 | per-slot, threshold comparison |
| D4, polarization | parties, rooms, evidence pools | distance between rooms; agreement within rooms | the ground that selects between rooms | overlap with D3 (who counts as a party) | a divergence, not a mood |
| D5, accountability | office holders, voters, refusal seat | ability to refuse; channel reach | a non-updating ground that the channel can reach | overlap with all four | requires negation and a seat |

**Partition or union.** Union. D1 through D5 overlap. A change of district magnitude touches all five. Treating them as four independent effects of one rule is the collapsed reading Fig 9 :55 warns about: *"a bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*

**No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.** D3's anchor is the preference distribution, which does not update because a party says it did. D5's anchor is the same kind of thing at the governance scale. D2's anchor is a party's reproduced contact with its ground. D4's anchor is the ground that votes between rooms. All five have anchors, so all five are domains.

## 3. Findings F1 to F10

### F1 The four outcomes are a union, not four effects of one lever

Fig 9 :54 to 58 (verbatim, joined across wrapped lines):

> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**

and

> **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."**

The lever that fixes party organization (the unit of competition) is not the lever that fixes representation (the vote to seat map), even though district magnitude touches both. A single reform clears the region where only one condition binds and leaves the intersections standing. The second-order value is that the residue becomes describable (Fig 9 :59: *"not that the pile is smaller, but that the residue is finally describable."*).

**Verdict: `specified`, triangulated** across Fig 9 and Fig 8 (different figures, different quantities, low overlap). **Mode: general on the union claim, specified on each domain.**

### F2 The electoral system is a bond over held atoms, not a new atom

Fig 12 :5:

> **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**

Fig 12 :46 (verbatim):

> **"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."**

An electoral system is a set of rules for turning ballots into seats. World source (opened 2026-11-22): *"An electoral system is a set of rules and mechanisms used to determine the results of an election"* (Wikipedia, *Electoral system*, 2026). That is a rule, not an additional actor. The atoms (voters, candidates, parties, seats) are already in Γ.

Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* Fig 13 :115: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."* So changing the rule explores the arrangement space; it does not mint a seventh primitive.

**Verdict: `specified`, triangulated** (Fig 12 plus Fig 6 plus Fig 13). **Refuter:** exhibit a system that introduces a genuinely new primitive into the political atom set, not a re-arrangement of held atoms.

### F3 Two systems over the same atoms are isomers, not variants

Fig 12 :117:

> **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

World corroboration (cross-field, low overlap): the effective number of parties measure is *"equivalent to the Herfindahl–Hirschman index, used in economics; the Simpson diversity index in ecology; the inverse participation ratio (IPR) in physics; and the Rényi entropy of order α=2 in information theory"* (Wikipedia, *Effective number of parties*, 2026, opened 2026-11-22). One atom (a weighted count) carries the same form across four unrelated fields. This is exactly Fig 13 :121: *"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."*

So plurality and list PR over the same voter and party atoms are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance. They are not a more and a less of one thing. Where the atom set genuinely differs (say, a new regional party enters), the relation is composition plus import, and import needs a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 8 :12 to 13).

**Verdict: `specified`, triangulated** (Fig 12 plus Fig 13 plus the cross-field measure). **Refuter:** show the measure changes form when carried into one of those four fields.

### F4 Party organization and the method/recording boundary

Fig 4 :22:

> **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**

Fig 4 :25:

> **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."**

Fig 11 :40:

> **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

World source: Duverger's law is described as *"a tendency rather than an invariant rule; its effects also depend on social and political context, the geographical distribution of party support, and strategic behavior"* (Wikipedia, *Duverger's law*, 2026, opened 2026-11-22). The seat product model adds that *"the most effective electoral reforms to increase or decrease the number of parties... are to change either the assembly size or the average district magnitude"* and treats the electoral formula as secondary (same source).

The figure-governed shape: a rule changes **which unit competes** (candidate or list) and **the cost of coordination**. What survives across a scale boundary is a method (a re-applicable way to coordinate) or a head start, never a recording of one election's success. A party organization that only replays a past win cannot produce a new one. The rule does not name the organization that will form; it constrains its form.

**Verdict: `specified`, triangulated** (Fig 4 plus Fig 11 plus world). **Gap:** no figure line names which organization forms in a given country. That is unreached, not silence (Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*).

### F5 Representation is per-slot and a comparison, not a single score

Fig 1 :19:

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**

Fig 1 :22:

> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

Operators table :51:

> **"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"**

Fig 12 :87: comparison is *"a threshold relation between two quantities."*

Representation is the comparison between vote share and seat share, indexed per party slot. The threshold (a legal quota, a district magnitude) is the comparison operator. A single national "representativeness" index is an aggregation that hides the live slot. World source: *"The number of parties equals the effective number of parties only when all parties have equal strength. In any other case, the effective number of parties is smaller than the number of actual parties"* (Wikipedia, *Effective number of parties*, 2026). That is an aggregation warning in the field's own vocabulary.

Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* A disproportionality index is a filter for where to look, not a witness for a cause.

**Verdict: `specified`, triangulated** (Fig 1 plus Operators plus Fig 12 plus world). **Gap:** no figure returns an optimal proportionality coefficient.

### F6 Polarization is a difference between rooms, selected against ground

Fig 7 :35:

> **"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**

Fig 7 :36:

> **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."**

Fig 2 :40:

> **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**

Polarization has two readings. If rooms (parties, electorates) differ and are selected against a ground that does not update, the difference carries information and is the engine of Fig 7 :33: *"variation inside, selection between."* If they draw on one evidence pool, their apparent divergence is a mirror chain: Fig 7 :12 to 13, *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not."* Duverger's own usage supports the mechanism: he *"called this trend polarization"* when voters coordinate to defeat a disliked outcome (Wikipedia, *Duverger's law*, 2026). Low-overlap corroboration: quorum sensing shows that *"the local concentration of signaling molecules may exceed its threshold level, and trigger changes in gene expression"* (Wikipedia, *Quorum sensing*, 2026, opened 2026-11-22), a threshold rule changing collective state without adding a new kind of cell.

The rule shapes polarization by setting the threshold at which a difference becomes a seat, and by deciding whether rooms must be nationally broad or can be locally concentrated. It does not set the amount of polarization.

**Verdict: `specified`.** The room mechanism is **single-source** on the figure side (Fig 7 with Fig 2, high overlap on independence but distinct objects), with world corroboration from quorum sensing and Duverger. **Gap:** no figure returns a polarization index or an optimum.

### F7 Accountability is a channel and a refusal condition, not a rule count

Fig 10 :47:

> **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**

Fig 10 :24:

> **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**

Fig 10 :42:

> **"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."**

Operators :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."*

Accountability requires three things the figures name separately: a ground that does not move when the office holder reports on it, a channel that stays reachable (stale percepts, saturated metrics, pooled readings are the failure modes at Fig 10 :25 to 28), and a seat able to refuse. Negation is not a bond; a vocabulary with only `+` can accumulate and cannot carry the correction. Where an electoral rule itself fixes the outcome, it has become a verdict and stopped being a neutral convention.

World corroboration (low overlap, mathematics): Arrow's theorem *"shows that no group decision-making procedure based on ordinal utilities... can satisfy the requirements of rational choice"* (Wikipedia, *Arrow's impossibility theorem*, 2026, opened 2026-11-22). And Condorcet's jury theorem is a *"double-edged sword"*: when voters are more likely correct, adding voters improves the verdict; when less likely, *"the optimal jury consists of a single voter"* (Wikipedia, *Condorcet's jury theorem*, 2026, opened 2026-11-22). Both say the same thing: the verdict depends on the anchor and the independence of the readers, not on the rule alone.

**Verdict: `specified`, triangulated** (Fig 10 plus Operators plus two low-overlap mathematical witnesses). **Gap:** no figure returns an accountability score.

### F8 No figure line returns a best system or a number

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 9 :27 to 28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."*

The eight essays' canonical phrase is *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"* (figures README, local copy, opened this session). There is no figure line selecting FPTP, list PR, mixed member, or a numerical effect on any of the four outcomes. The universal "system X causes outcome Y" claim is therefore a declared `gap` of the unreached kind, not a refusal to answer and not an invitation to invent a number. What is owed is a ground reading: a measurement with a non-updating anchor and an independent frame.

**Verdict: `gap`, plainly stated.**

### F9 Description before frame selection; imports need a gate

Fig 9 :27 to 28 (verbatim, joined across wrapped lines): *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Fig 8 :50 to 52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"* (full line continues, *"and the surplus lives only where the closures differ"*). Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*

A study that starts with "plurality versus PR" and then looks for effects has picked the frame before describing the residual. Anything then seems to fit. Cross-national borrowings are imports from live peers: they need provenance (source gate) and must explain a gap the borrower could not close (shadow test). World sources drawn from different pools (mathematics, biology) count as genuinely lower-overlap corroborators; the political-science sources share one evidence pool and are one witness, not four.

**Verdict: `specified`, triangulated** (Fig 9 plus Fig 8 plus Fig 6).

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim is refused if any of the following holds:

1. A figure line is found that selects a specific electoral system or returns a numerical effect size for any of the four outcomes. That breaks the shape-not-a-name finding (F8).
2. A reform is demonstrated to introduce a genuinely new primitive into the political atom set, rather than re-arranging held atoms. That breaks F2 and F3.
3. A measured case shows accountability improving while the reference updates on the institution's own reports and no seat can refuse. That breaks F7.
4. The cross-field measure (inverse Simpson / Laakso-Taagepera) is shown to change form in one of the four fields where it is said to be equivalent. That breaks the isomer corroboration in F3.
5. Two rooms drawing on one evidence pool are shown to correct each other independently. That breaks F6 and the independence rule in Fig 2 :40.

Until one of these is run, no part of the answer may be labelled `grounded`.

4. Treat cross-national comparisons as imports with provenance and a shadow test (F9).

### Verdict of this audit

The shape survives: a union of four conditions, a bond rather than an atom, isomeric systems over a fixed atom set, per-slot representation, room-selected polarization, and anchor-plus-channel-plus-refusal accountability. The specific bindings do not. Mode `specified` per domain, `general` on the union skeleton, `gap` on the best system and the numbers. Nothing here is `grounded`.
