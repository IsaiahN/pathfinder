# Outreach draft: check one shape before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the mode labels and refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are intact. Stage 1 must survive on its own or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Four outcomes, four anchors: can you break this shape claim?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** An electoral system is a rule that maps ballots to seats. The question asks how that one rule shapes four things: party organization, representation, polarization, and accountability. The effect I would like checked is not "how much" but "what form." My claim is that these four are a union of overlapping conditions with different anchors, not four effects of one lever.
>
> **The shape claim (`specified`, not `grounded`; this is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a measurement):**
>
> 1. The rule is a bond over atoms you already hold (voters, candidates, parties, seats). It re-arranges them; it does not add a new kind of thing. Two systems over the same atoms are isomers: same atoms, different bond, different substance.
> 2. Party organization turns on whether what crosses a scale boundary is a re-applicable method or a recording of one past win.
> 3. Representation is a threshold comparison indexed per party slot. A single national score is an aggregation that hides the live slot.
> 4. Polarization is a real difference between rooms only when the rooms differ and are selected against a ground that does not move when they report on themselves. Rooms sharing one evidence pool produce a mirror chain, not corroboration.
> 5. Accountability needs a non-updating ground, a channel that stays reachable, and a seat able to refuse. Where the rule itself decides the outcome, it has stopped being a neutral convention.
>
> **What I am explicitly not claiming:** that any one system (first past the post, list proportional representation, mixed member) is the answer; that there is an optimal proportionality number or polarization index; that Duverger's law is an exceptionless law. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Those specific claims are recorded as a `gap`, not an answer.
>
> **What would refute it.** I wrote these down before sending this:
> 1. A figure line that selects a specific system or returns a numerical effect size for any of the four outcomes.
> 2. A reform shown to introduce a genuinely new primitive rather than re-arrange held atoms.
> 3. A case where accountability improves while the reference updates on the institution's own reports and no seat can refuse.
> 4. A demonstration that two rooms drawing on one evidence pool still correct each other independently.
>
> If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop and mode rule: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** run the loop on a problem you know well and can check against a ground, and write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Two-stage discipline

- **Stage 1:** the recipient checks the shape claim and the refuter without seeing the framework. Record their verdict before showing anything else.
- **Stage 2:** only if curious, offer the figures and the essays. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because by then they hold the interiors and share the evidence pool (Fig 2 :40, *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*).
- **Stage 3:** offer the method, not the answer. Each user must state their own ground and refuter before running it.

No link is placed before the refuter. No sentence asks for belief. No sentence asks for a show of hands.
