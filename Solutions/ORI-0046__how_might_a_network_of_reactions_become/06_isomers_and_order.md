Two supporting questions remain. (1) What is the order of composition: how does a reaction network sit relative to the compartment that carries it? ## DERIVATION

### Q1 — What is the order of the composition?

Fig 13 :74: **"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."**
Fig 13 :75: **"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."**

Read chemically, a reaction network is a structure (Fig 13 :23-24: *"network that entities relate, and influence travels"*). A food or energy source is a state (energy at Fig 13 :13-14: *"conserved, finite, directionless alone"*), and the gradient that can be spent is a structure (Fig 13 :21-22: *"a difference that can be spent"*). A first-order molecule is therefore `state → structure → state`: food/energy, through a spendable gradient and a catalytic network, to products and a maintained compartment state. This is the same cross-kind rule used in the exemplar, applied to chemistry rather than to spacetime. It is a `specified` mapping, declared, not a claim that the six-term set is chemistry's own list.

At second order, the *settled network in the parent* becomes an atom for the daughter compartment: Fig 12 :104 - *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."* The crossing is therefore not a first-order reaction; it is a composition over a settled first-order term. Fig 12 :106: *"Nothing composes over what was never admitted as a part."* This is why a parent whose closure has not settled cannot be a source of inheritance: there is no atom to cross.

**Falsifier for Q1:** exhibit a heritable network in which the catalytic structure bonds directly to another structure with no state between, under the same six-term reading. That breaks Fig 13 :74-75 and forces a re-census.

### Q2 — Are parent and daughter networks isomers?

Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
Fig 12 :123: **"The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

- **Weak reading.** Parent and daughter are the same *kind* of thing: a food-generated catalytic closure inside a boundary. They share the functional group `state → structure → state` and differ in which specific molecules and which sampled concentrations occupy the slots. Under Fig 12 :123 that makes them members of one functional group, not isomers.
- **Strong reading.** Hold the atom set fixed as the parent's full species list plus the food set and boundary, and let the daughter differ only in the *arrangement* of catalytic relations (which core survived the unequal division, which inducer crossed, which side reaction was lost). Then parent and daughter are Fig 12 isomers: same atoms, different bond, different substance. This is the checkable claim.
- **The decisive variable is type, not degree.** A daughter with the same atoms and the same bonds but different concentrations is a different *state* of one term; a daughter with the same atoms and different bonds is a different *term*. Fig 12 :5 - *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*

**Falsifier for Q2, written before the run:** if every parent-daughter difference that persists can be accounted for by concentrations alone with an unchanged catalysis graph, the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim stands.

### Q3 — Which named structures do the work?

| Fig 12 structure | verbatim line | role here |

| catalyst | :119 *"A part that survives its own use."* | the species that persists across division and can catalyse again in the daughter |

A second-order caution from Fig 12 :125-126: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Bits price the description of the inherited arrangement; actions price finding out whether it actually re-runs in the daughter. A model that scores only the bit cost of the species list will propose arrangements it cannot afford to test. Fig 12 :126 - *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*

**Falsifier for Q3:** a heritable network whose persistence requires no part that survives its own use and no loop whose output re-enters as input. That breaks the structural analogue.

### Q4 — What is the relation between persistence and inheritance?

Fig 10 :70: **"If levels were copies there would be one lineage drifting and no branches."**
Fig 7 :36: **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."**

Persistence is not inheritance with variation. A closure that reappears identically in every compartment is a chain of mirrors: one long room, motion without progress (Fig 7 :32). Inheritance with variation requires that the next compartment *differ* from the last and *match the residual the last surfaced* (a lost core, a shifted balance of flux, a new inducer). The two-factor condition of Fig 7 :37-39 (fidelity and ground pressure) is what separates the two. Fig 10 :70 supplies the outside view: a non-invertible transform yields branches, exactly what the compartment literature models when unequal division drops a viable core (Hordijk and Steel 2014) or when a permeable inducer changes a neighbour (Hordijk et al. 2018).

## CONCLUSION

The parent and daughter compartments compose at second order: each is a settled first-order `state → structure → state` closure, and the daughter is built over the parent now serving as an atom. Over a fixed atom set they are a real isomer claim (same species, different catalytic arrangement); over a variable atom set the weaker and safer claim is a shared functional group. The structures doing the work are the ones Fig 12 already names: catalyst, cycle, functional group, isomer. Inheritance with variation, not bare persistence, requires both fidelity and ground pressure. No new primitive, no import that moves the wall, and no numerical fidelity threshold are required for this framing.

The world literature corroborates the shape at low overlap but does not supply the missing identity: Hordijk and Steel 2014 formalize the irreducible RAF as the heritable core; Hordijk et al. 2018 show the loss and inducer mechanisms; Taneja and Higgs 2025 show that division is physical and unequal, not a logical copy. The chemical identity of the carrier remains a `gap`.
