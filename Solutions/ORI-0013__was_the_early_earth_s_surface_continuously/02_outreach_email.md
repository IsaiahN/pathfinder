**Subject:** Check one claim about early Earth habitability before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about the Hadean and early Archean. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural constraints, not `grounded`):** the question "was the early Earth's surface continuously habitable, or did impacts repeatedly disrupt it?" is not one hard question. It is a union of two conditions, a baseline habitable state and a process of impacts, and both can hold at different times and places. A global binary hides the signal. The framework's rule is that residual is indexed per slot, never aggregated.
>
> The word "continuously" assumes a continuum. In the framework's six-term substrate, a continuum is not a primitive. It is a derivative that must be composed and paid for, so the honest question is at what resolution the rock record resolves into slots and what fills the gaps.
>
> Habitability is also not a property of the planet by itself. It is a property of an organism-and-habitat pair in contact. An impact is a malfactor to surface chemistry and can be a benefactor to subsurface thermophiles. The framework's habitat rule says isolation is not removal of a habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another. So "repeatedly disrupted" is true at the surface and not equivalent to "uninhabited".
>
> **What the framework does not claim:** it does not decide whether the surface was in fact continuous or punctuated, and it does not date the bombardment. The figures govern the shape of an acceptable answer, not the geological timeline. The rock record is the anchor and it settles the history. The impact chronology is still contested in the literature, and that contest is reported as a gap rather than smoothed over.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a ground measurement that resolves the whole window as one homogeneous habitable state at the resolution in question;
> 2. a demonstration that impacts never crossed a surface-sterilizing threshold anywhere in that window;
> 3. an anchor that changes its answer depending on which frame pushes on it;
> 4. a case where an impact removed all habitats with no substituted refuge anywhere;
> 5. a derivation of a continuum from the other five substrate members alone.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the source links are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Figures and formula for direct download (SVG, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
