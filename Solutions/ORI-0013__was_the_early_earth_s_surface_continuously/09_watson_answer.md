## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the early Earth surface. Option A: continuously habitable, a gapless window for prebiotic chemistry. Option B: repeatedly disrupted by impacts, with sterilization and recovery. What each leads to: A gives origin-of-life questions ORI-0011 and ORI-0012 an uninterrupted window; B gives them a punctuated window with refugia. The figures decide the shape of any acceptable answer. They do not decide which history the rock record returned.

- **Domain 2, habitat and contact frame.** Actors: conditions, impactors, early organisms. Currency: contact. Anchor: capability is agent-and-habitat, never agent alone (Fig 11 :39). Question type: what "habitable" means; actor valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49).

- **Domain 4, verification frame.** Actors: expert readers. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement (Fig 2 :35, :40). In this simulated run all frames draw on one evidence pool, so independence is low and agreement is not corroboration.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Fix the anchor before adjudicating history; describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); enumerate the habitat before judging continuity (Fig 11 :43); settle per slot before any global claim (Fig 1 :19).

## WORLD (secondary sources read)

World sources are corroboration only and never overrule a figure. Each was opened on 2026-11-23.

- **Earth history synthesis.** Wikipedia contributors, "Hadean" (Wikipedia, accessed 2026-11-23): "Asteroid impacts during the Hadean and into the Archean would have periodically disrupted the ocean" and "the Earth may have gone through long periods when liquid oceans and life were possible." Predicts punctuated surface conditions with habitable intervals; corroborates the shape. Overlap: compiles the primary sources below, so it is not independent.
- **Planetary science / astrobiology.** Nicolle E. B. Zellner, "Cataclysm No More: New Views on the Timing and Delivery of Lunar Impactors" (Origins of Life and Evolution of Biospheres, 2017): "most evidence supports a prolonged lunar (and thus, terrestrial) bombardment from ~4.2 to 3.4 Ga and not a cataclysmic spike at ~3.9 Ga." Predicts long quiescent intervals and no single terminal reset; corroborates the union shape and supports the `gap` on chronology. Overlap: shares Apollo samples with the cataclysm view.
- **Geophysics / early climate.** N. H. Sleep, K. Zahnle and P. S. Neuhoff, "Initiation of clement surface conditions on the earliest Earth" (PNAS, 2001): "subsurface high-temperature organisms are the likely survivors of an ocean boiling asteroid impact that otherwise sterilized the planet." Predicts surface sterilization plus subsurface refugia; corroborates Fig 11 :66. Overlap: distinct from lunar chronology (different archive and instruments).
- **Geochemistry / zircon evidence.** Simon A. Wilde, John W. Valley, William H. Peck and Colin M. Graham, "Evidence from detrital zircons for the existence of continental crust and oceans on the Earth 4.4 Gyr ago" (Nature, 2001): "This zircon thus represents the earliest evidence for continental crust and oceans on the Earth." Predicts early liquid water and crust; corroborates that habitable conditions existed at all. Overlap: distinct archive from the impact record.
- **Planetary science synthesis.** Wikipedia contributors, "Late Heavy Bombardment" (Wikipedia, accessed 2026-11-23): "The existence of the LHB was once widely accepted by the academic community but is now debated; definitive evidence either way remains elusive." Predicts a contested chronology; corroborates the `gap` verdict. Overlap: same underlying sample pool as Zellner.
- **Impact geology (not quoted directly).** D. R. Lowe and G. R. Byerly, "Geologic record of partial ocean evaporation triggered by giant asteroid impacts, 3.29-3.23 billion years ago" (Geology, 2015). Publisher page returned HTTP 403; cited only via the two Wikipedia pages and Zellner. Predicts real surface disruption; corroborates the process, not a date. Overlap: reported through secondary sources, so weak.

No world source overrides a figure verdict. Two sources (Zellner and the LHB page) conflict with the older cataclysm picture; per the standing rule a conflict sends us back to the census, and the re-census kept D3 as a `gap` rather than flipping a figure verdict. ## VERDICT (from the figures)

The binary is refused as posed. What survives is a shape: habitability is a time-indexed contact relation read per slot; "continuously" is a derivative that must be composed, not a primitive; an impact substitutes a habitat rather than removing one, so surface disruption is compatible with refugia and recovery; and the rock record is the anchor that settles the history, not agreement among frames. The figures do not decide whether the surface was in fact continuously habitable or how often impacts struck. The bombardment chronology is a `gap`, reported as a verdict. World sources read here, as secondary evidence only, lean toward punctuated surface conditions with long quiescent intervals and subsurface or hydrothermal refugia. This is a `specified`, ungrounded result.

- **cost if wrong:** a wrong shape sends researchers to a single global proxy instead of per-slot records, and can mistake a sedimentation hiatus for an absence of life. Fig 2 :42 prices this: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and its refuters.
- **undoable:** yes. The shape is a claim about form, not a dated history. It can be amended without withdrawing any primitive; `06` already re-specifies the one isomer claim that failed its strain test.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A per-slot record that stays homogeneous across the whole window; a demonstration that no impact crossed a sterilizing threshold; an anchor that moves when frames push on it; a case with no substituted refuge; or a derivation of a continuum from the other five substrate members. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
