# Structure, order and analogues for the habitability question

**Mode: `specified` on the structure, `general` on the bond typology. Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).**

## RESIDUAL

The question offers two histories but does not say what kind of thing the difference between them is. Two derivations are owed: (1) what is the **order** of the union, and (2) can the two histories be treated as **isomers** under Fig 12's definition, or only as members of a shared functional group?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: conditions (water, temperature, terrain), impactors, early organisms, mineral archives (zircon, spherule beds). Bonds held: the seven plus negation. No new primitive is available without import. Currency: contact and measurement. Anchor: the rock record.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: What is the order of the union?

- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step"** (continuing :21 *"that cannot use it."*).
- The dependency order for this question is: fix the anchor (Fig 2 :29-30) before adjudicating history; describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); enumerate the habitat before judging continuity (Fig 11 :43); settle per slot before any global claim (Fig 1 :19).
- The impact process itself is an ordered bond, `→` sequence: Operators table :30 **"A then B; B needs A to have happened"** Fig 12 :35 **"A then B"** appears as the gloss for `→`.
- The continuous-vs-punctuated distinction is therefore a claim about the **arrangement** of the same atoms over time, not about which atoms exist. Operators table :34: **"the only bond a flat chain can express, so every term today is a sequence whether or not it should be"**. This is a standing warning: a chronology written as a pure sequence may be mistyped, because a habitat state is a conjunction (`+`, both hold at once) while an impact is a sequence.
- Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."** Listing "habitable" and "impacts" names two states. The answer needs the route between them.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a faithful history can be written as a single state with no ordered transitions (no `→` and no threshold), the order claim fails.

### Q2: Are the two histories isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- As the question writes them, "continuously habitable" and "repeatedly disrupted" do **not** share an atom set in any transparent way. The first names a state over time; the second names a process. By Fig 12's definition they are not isomers. This is the strain I retracted in `01` §5; it is recorded here rather than erased.
- The stronger, checkable form: write both histories over the **full shared atom set** (water, temperature, terrain, impactors, mineral archives), differing only in the bond arrangement (a long `+` conjunction versus a `→` sequence with `⋛` thresholds at the impacts). Under that construction they **are** isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. That is the claim worth testing.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either history requires an atom the other does not (for example, subsurface hydrothermal refugia required by one but not the other), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands instead.

### Q3: Which Fig 12 structures actually bear?

- **Catalyst**, Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."** A hydrothermal system that persists through a surface impact and then seeds recovery is the catalyst-like part. World check (secondary): Sleep et al. 2001 names subsurface organisms as the *"likely survivors"* of an ocean-boiling impact; Zellner 2017 reports hydrothermal vents as a *"sanctuary"*. Whether the same system is spent or regenerating across impacts is unmeasured. Mode `specified`, triangulated for plausibility only.
- **Cycle**, Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."** Recovery is a loop (impact, habitat substitution, recolonization, next impact). The sign decides whether the loop closes into a resilient biosphere or opens into extinction. A pure chain cannot represent this. The figures do not give the sign; the ground does.
- **Functional group**, Fig 12 :124 (quoted above): the recurrent motif `conditions → habitat → survivors` is the functional group; "warm surface ocean" and "sub hydrothermal system" are members. This is the weakest but safest claim and it survives regardless of the chronology dispute.
- **Continuum**, Fig 13 :43-44 (quoted in `05`): not a structure in the six. It is what a composition leaves. Treating it as a member of the atom set is the error that makes the original binary look like a choice between primitives.

### Q4: What does the threshold bond say about sterilization?

- Operators table :49: **"a threshold relation between two quantities"** for `⋛`, with the test *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."*
- Sterilization is exactly a threshold claim: delivered energy crosses a limit and the surface term changes state. Fig 12 :89: **"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."**
- The two quantities are impactor size/energy and the tolerance of the surface habitat. World check (secondary): Wikipedia Hadean reports objects up to 100 km boiling off up to 100 m of a global ocean and raising atmospheric temperature to about 500 C (attributed there to Lowe and Byerly 2015, whose publisher page returned 403 on direct fetch and is not quoted directly here). Zellner 2017 cautions that heating *"was perhaps only severe locally"* in some models.
- Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."** A magnitude estimate is a filter for which impact to study; it does not settle whether the threshold was crossed in a given slot.

## CONCLUSION

The union is ordered by dependency, not by importance (Q1). The two headline histories are not isomers as written, but a real isomer claim exists when both are expressed over the full shared atom set (Q2, with a stated falsifier). Fig 12's catalyst, cycle and functional-group structures each name something the flat binary cannot write (Q3). Sterilization is a `⋛` threshold, and the threshold is ground-decided (Q4).

This framing rescues what is defensible in the question without pretending the figures date a crater. No new primitive is introduced, no import moves a wall, and no continuum is smuggled in as a member of the six.
