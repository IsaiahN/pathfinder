**Subject:** One checkable claim about restoration and ecological interactions

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read any theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with me. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A species list and a physical habitat description are an ingredients record. An ecological interaction is a typed relation between two of those ingredients. The two are not interchangeable, and the relation is not recoverable from the list. So the question "can restoration recreate interactions, or does it mainly rebuild habitat and species lists" is not one question with two answers. It has three slots, and a site can score well on the first two while an interaction slot is still dead. I predict that ingredient replacement alone leaves a positive residual on the interaction slot, and that the failures surface two or three steps out rather than at the planting.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not saying restoration cannot recreate interactions. A managed project can introduce actors for their cascades, and that is one way arrival is worked on. I am saying it does not follow from planting a historically correct list, that the relation has to be enumerated and measured on its own terms, and that a managed ecosystem stays at work rather than becoming a solved state. I am not claiming that any particular species, site or threshold follows from the derivation. Those are a declared gap, and I will not fill them with a plausible name.
>
> **What would refute the output.** I wrote these down before sending this.
> 1. A model using only the species list and measured habitat conditions predicts measured interaction rates for several ordered pairs at two sites out of sample, with no residual left on the interaction slot.
> 2. A site's target relations are fully derived from its species list and conditions with no field reading, and every derived rate matches the ground.
> 3. A synthetic plot result transfers to the full field site with no new ground reading and no interaction residual.
> 4. One actor keeps the same beneficial or harmful classification across two materially different residuals.
> 5. A checklist verdict is treated as settling the question while interaction rates remain degraded for several seasons, and is not withdrawn.
>
> If any one holds, the relevant part of the output is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would count as a failure of my own question:** if you tell me the interaction slot was already measured per site and closed years ago, then my residual is a filter mistake, not a finding, and I will say so in the record.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Local verbatim figure text, Figures 1 to 13 plus the Operators and Symbols tables: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in this package.
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`.
> - The eight essays by Isaiah N, including "The Hard Problem is About Source" and "The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap": https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/the-hard-problem-is-about-source
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label your mode. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
