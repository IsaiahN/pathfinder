## 0. The residual under audit

> Question 85: Can restoration recreate ecological interactions, or does it mainly rebuild physical habitat and species lists?

Stated as an effect, not as a topic. A degraded site has three kinds of slot: physical conditions (substrate, light, water, terrain), actors (the species present and returning), and relations (pollination, predation, competition, dependency, and the cascades they carry). An intervention changes some of these. Define R per slot as the gap between what the intervention's own library predicts for that slot and what the field returns: `R = |Γ(b,a) − o′|`, indexed by slot, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19, :22). The residual of this question is therefore:

- A site can have near-zero residual on the habitat slots and the species-presence slots while one live interaction slot still carries positive mass. That is a legal state, not an inert one (Fig 1 :13, :20).
- The two options offered in the question are not exhaustive. "Recreate interactions" and "rebuild habitat and species lists" are different slots, not competing answers to one slot. A project can do the second and leave the first untouched.

**What an answer would look like, written down and bound before the run** (Formula step "Direction"): a shape with (i) a per-slot verdict for each of the three layers, (ii) a named anchor for each, (iii) a stated condition under which an interaction slot can be closed, and (iv) a refuter that fires if ingredient replacement alone closes that slot. Committing this shape before the run makes the sought-for preference refusable like any other term (Fig 5 :65-66).

The anchor is the measured interaction rate in the field, which does not change because a report calls the site restored: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* (Fig 2 :29). Agreement among practitioners is not the anchor.

## 1. Domain census, before any figure line is chosen

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity.** Three-place and more: conditions, actors, relations. Each relation itself joins two actors, and the relation has a direction (who pollinates whom, who eats whom), so the residual is indexed per ordered pair, not per site total.
- **Symmetry.** A species list is a symmetric conjunction: swapping the order of names changes nothing (Fig 12 :59, operators table `+` conjunction). An interaction is an ordered, typed bond: predation and mutualism are different relations over the same two actors, and swapping operands changes the meaning (Fig 12 :71-74, :117). This is the decisive structural asymmetry of the question.
- **Scale.** First order at the site (an actor and a condition in contact), second order at the cascade (the DDT case is three steps out, Fig 11 :52), and across the membrane from a plot or mesocosm to a landscape (Fig 4).

### 1.2 One line per candidate domain: actors, currency, anchor

A nameable anchor is required before a candidate is admitted as a domain.

| candidate | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 Physical conditions** | substrate, light, water, terrain, season | condition values, area, structure | measured microclimate and substrate in the field | admitted |
| **D2 Species list** | introduced, returning and resident species | presence, abundance, cover | field counts and detections | admitted |
| **D3 Relations and cascades** | pollination, predation, competition, dependency, engineering | typed bonds, interaction rates, cascade reach | measured interaction rates per ordered pair, plus second-order effects | admitted |
| **D4 Verification channel** | instruments, monitors, plots, reports, reviewers | contact, bracket fidelity, residual | the ground, which does not decay (Fig 10 :24) | admitted |
| **X1 "The site is restored" as a checklist verdict** | the declaring seat | agreement | none nameable that does not move when the seat pushes on it | not a domain yet (Fig 2 :30) |
| **X2 "The historical baseline" as an anchor** | a reference state | resemblance | moves with climate, land use and the reference chosen | absorbed into D1/D2 as a convention, not an anchor |
| **X3 "Restoration success" as one score** | the scoring framework | aggregate index | none at the resolution of an interaction slot | absorbed into D4 as a filter, not a witness (Fig 9 :60) |

X1, X2 and X3 fail the anchor test. Declaring a meaning does not ground it (Fig 10 :18, :49). X2 is a convention that can be useful for setting a target, but if it decides the outcome it has stopped being a convention (Fig 10 :47). X3 is a filter for the budget, never a witness for the verdict.

### 1.3 Partition or union

The split between D1/D2 (ingredients) and D3 (arrangement) is a **partition in notation only**: a species list can be written without writing the interactions, and Fig 12 says that is exactly the half of the record that goes missing. In effect the question is a **union**: a real pollination relation needs both a pollinator (D2) and a flowering plant in suitable conditions (D1), and it is itself a bond (D3). So unbundle before searching (Fig 9 :54-57):

- A case with habitat rebuilt but actors absent is a single condition.
- A case with actors present but relations absent is a different single condition.
- A case with both ingredients present and the relation dead is a **compound**, categorically different from either, and a lever that clears only one condition leaves the intersection standing (Fig 9 :55-56).

This union reading is why the either/or framing of Question 85 must be refused. It is not a rhetorical refusal. It follows from the dependency of the bond on its operands and from Fig 9's union rule.

### 1.4 Dependency order (Fig 3)

1. **Perception.** Can the site return a reading of which actors and conditions are there, and can it tell the current reading from the previous one (Fig 3 :4-5)?
2. **Vocabulary.** Are there terms for what the actors afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked (Fig 3 :6-7)? A species list supplies names; it does not supply a term for the relation between two names.
3. **Objective.** Can the project say what would count as progress on an interaction slot, in its own terms (Fig 3 :9-10)? This is the link that usually breaks. It has neither the obvious failures of perception nor the obvious tests of planning (Fig 3 :24-27).
4. **Planning.** A route to that objective under incomplete information: which actors to introduce, in what order, with what provenance (Fig 3 :12-13).
5. **Learn and carry.** Name what worked, verify it, carry it to the next site (Fig 3 :14-15). Only methods cross up (Fig 4 :22).

Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not (Fig 3 :19). Planting more species optimises links 2 and 4 when the chain is broken at link 3, which is why an ingredient-only programme can look busy and return nothing on the relation slot. A link with no instrument is not a link that works; it is a link nobody has looked at (Fig 3 :27).

## 3. Findings

### F1  -  A species list is a recipe with the bond left out (decisive for the shape)

Fig 12 :5:

> **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**

Fig 12 :46-47:

> **"A recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself."**

Fig 12 :94:

> **"The arrangement is the substance."**

Fig 12 :117:

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

A species list is written under the symmetric conjunction `+`: swap two names and the list is the same. An interaction is a typed relation between two of those names: pollination is not predation, and reversing who eats whom changes the thing. The bond information is not recoverable from the operands (Fig 12 :46). So a project that restores substrate and plants a historically correct list has recorded the ingredients and not the arrangement. Nothing downstream can tell that apart from the full record, and nothing signals that anything was lost (Fig 12 :16-17). This is a notation finding with an effect: the missing information is precisely the thing the question asks about.

**Mode:** `general` for the notation rule, `specified` when applied to restoration sites. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 12 notation and Fig 11 habitat enumeration, low overlap).

**Refuter:** a predictive model using only the species list and measured habitat conditions reproduces measured interaction rates for several ordered pairs at two sites out of sample, with no residual on any interaction slot. If that holds, the bond carries no additional information here and F1 does not govern this residual.

### F2  -  The habitat is enumerated, and relations are on the list

Fig 11 :43-44:

> **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**
> **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**

Fig 11 :45-48:

> **"actors: what acts on it, and what it acts on"**
> **"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"**
> **"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"**
> **"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."**

The figure's own enumeration contains three categories. "Physical habitat and species lists" covers the first two. The third is the question's missing term. A restoration plan that enumerates actors and conditions but not relations has not enumerated the habitat; it has enumerated a subset and called it the whole (Fig 11 :43). Because the list is read off the world rather than composed, the relations cannot be inferred from a species inventory by reasoning inside the frame. What the instruments cannot yet perceive is the residual, owed, not silently filled.

**Mode:** `general` for the enumeration rule, `specified` for a restoration census. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 and Fig 9 :26-27, residual-first).

**Refuter:** a documented site where the full relation set was derived from the species list and conditions alone, with no additional field reading, and every derived interaction rate matched the ground.

### F3  -  No actor carries a fixed valence; relations are not kinds

Fig 11 :49-51:

> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**
> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

A restoration recipe that says "introduce species X, it is a good mutualist" treats a relation as a property of an actor. The figure refuses that. Whether an introduced actor helps depends on what residual is at stake at that site. The same actor can be a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another. This is why a generic native-species list cannot be a restoration target for interactions: the list is a set of kinds, and interactions are relations.

**Mode:** `general` (structural) and `specified` for any named species. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 valence and Fig 12 bond typing).

**Refuter:** an invariant beneficial or harmful classification of one actor across two documented, materially different residuals, confirmed at the ground. Note the world literature partly supplies this in the other direction: Svenning et al. 2015 call rewilding controversial, which is consistent with context-dependent valence.

### F4  -  Introduction is available, exclusion is not, so this is permanent work

Fig 11 :61-63:

> **"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."**
> **"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state. And in neither mode is the habitat composed: unmaintained nothing composes it, maintained the seat adds to a habitat it still did not author."**

Fig 11 :58:

> **"Maintained. A seat exists and can act: choose the instruments, distil the impactful actors, introduce what was not there. That is the garden, and it is how arrival is worked on rather than waited for."**

This is the part of the question that can answer yes. A seat can introduce an actor for its cascade; that is how arrival is worked on. But it cannot subtract the habitat, and what it adds lands in a habitat it did not author. So the yes is conditional on a maintained seat and on continued contact, not on a one-time planting. A "completed restoration" is a category mistake under this rule: a managed ecosystem is permanent work.

**Mode:** `specified`, with `general` asymmetry rule. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 action rule, Fig 4 head-start rule, low overlap).

**Refuter:** a site where the seat subtracted the habitat itself, not merely one actor, and reached a state that stayed at target with no further contact and no new readings. That would break the asymmetry rule at habitat scale.

### F5  -  Cascades are second-order and their failures are invisible until they act

Fig 11 :52-54:

> **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**

Fig 11 :68-69:

> **"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"**
> **"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"**

A first-order scope (the species planted, the substrate fixed) misses the residual three steps out. The two silent failure modes are named in the figure: something needed was not reproduced and is invisible until the goal fails; something not chosen was introduced and is invisible until it acts. An ingredient-only restoration has exactly these two blind spots. An interaction rate can be dead for years and then surface when a depended-on partner fails to arrive, or an introduced actor can act years later with an unenumerated cascade (Fig 11 :65: *"Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated."*).

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 cascade and Fig 1 per-slot indexing).

**Refuter:** a site where the second-order cascade was fully predicted from first-order presence data with no residual, sustained across seasons and at least one partner failure.

### F6  -  Isolation substitutes a habitat; a plot proves wiring, not capability

Fig 11 :66-70:

> **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."**
> **"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."**
> **"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."**

A mesocosm, a small fenced plot or a synthetic community is a substituted habitat. Success there proves that the wiring works under the substituted conditions. It does not prove that the capability survives in the field, because the field's actors were not chosen and the conditions that matter may not have been reproduced. This is the round-trip problem again: the lossy transform between scales means a result carried up and brought down will not match (Fig 4 :28-30). The abstract reading "interactions restored in the mesocosm" has a nonzero round-trip gap R_T when concretised to the site.

**Mode:** `general` for the substitution rule, `specified` for mesocosm-to-field transfer. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 and Fig 4).

**Refuter:** a synthetic or plot result that transfers to the full field site with no new ground reading and no residual on any interaction slot. That would set R_T to zero for this case and break F6.

### F7  -  The anchor is the measured interaction, not the declaration

Fig 10 :18 and :49:

> **"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"**
> **"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."**

Fig 2 :29 and :35:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**
> **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

Calling a site "restored" because it meets a checklist installs a convention and then lets the convention decide an outcome. Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* The anchor has to be something that does not move when the declaring frame pushes on it: an interaction rate, a recruitment count, a nutrient flux measured in the field. Agreement among restoration practitioners about what "restored" means is efficient for a convention and disqualifying for a verdict (Fig 2 :39-40). This is also why the question must be answered per slot (Fig 1 :19): a site-average score lets a live interaction residual disappear into an average.

**Mode:** `general` for the anchor rule, `specified` for restoration reporting. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 2, Fig 10, Fig 1, low overlap between anchor theory and per-slot measurement).

**Refuter:** a site whose checklist verdict is treated as settling the question while measured interaction slots remain at degraded values over several seasons, and the declaration is still not withdrawn. That would show the anchor moving with the frame.

### F8  -  The either/or question is a bundled union, and filters cannot settle it

Fig 9 :54-57:

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
> **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

Fig 9 :60:

> **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

The phrase "or does it mainly rebuild habitat and species lists" invites a comparison between two single levers. Under Fig 9 the comparison is invalid: the two conditions overlap, and the compound case (ingredients present, relation absent) is categorically different from either. A lever that adds species clears only the region where "actors missing" holds alone and leaves the intersection standing. A cover-percentage index, a species-richness score or a hectare count is a filter for where to spend effort, never a witness for whether interactions returned.

**Mode:** `general` (union rule) and `specified` for this question. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 9 and Fig 12).

**Refuter:** one intervention that closes all three condition classes, including the intersection, with no categorically different residue left and no further maintenance. That would dissolve the union and collapse the question back to one lever.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the recommendation and before any outreach, per the seat map at `THE_SEAT_MAP_Field_Guide_amended.md :287`: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* The shape claim is refuted if any of the following fires:

1. **Ingredient sufficiency.** A model using only species list plus measured habitat conditions predicts measured interaction rates for several ordered pairs at two sites out of sample, with no interaction-slot residual. This kills F1 and the whole bond argument.
2. **Relation-free habitat enumeration.** A site's target relations are fully derived from its species list and conditions with no additional field reading, and every derived rate matches the ground. This kills F2.
3. **Invariant valence.** One actor keeps the same beneficial or harmful classification across two materially different residuals, confirmed at the ground. This kills F3.
4. **Reversible subtraction.** A seat subtracts the habitat itself and reaches a target state that persists with no further contact and no new readings. This kills F4's asymmetry.
5. **Zero-gap transfer.** A synthetic plot result transfers to the full field site with no new ground reading and no residual on any interaction slot. This kills F6 and sets R_T to zero.
6. **Label as anchor.** A checklist verdict is treated as settling the question while interaction slots remain degraded for several seasons and is not withdrawn. This kills F7.
7. **One lever clears the union.** A single intervention closes habitat, species and interaction conditions including their intersection, with no categorically different residue and no maintenance. This kills F8.
8. **A genuinely independent world source**, not sharing the ecology evidence pool, shows that ingredient replacement closes interaction slots across sites with no maintained relation work. That would send the derivation back to the census and could flip the verdict.

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. **Answer per slot, never as one average.** Report habitat, species and interaction slots separately (Fig 1 :19, :22). A global "restored" score is the denominator being counted as progress (Formula step 8).
2. **Enumerate relations before choosing an intervention.** The habitat list must include pollination, predation, competition and dependency (Fig 11 :45-48). Describe the residual before picking a frame (Fig 9 :27-28), or any technique will seem to fit.
3. **Treat interactions as bonds, not as extra species.** Add a relation work package with provenance and a shadow test (Fig 6 :12-13), not another name on the seed mix (Fig 12 :46-47).
4. **Keep the ground reachable.** Install an interaction-rate instrument with a declared anchor, and keep the channel alive after planting ends (Fig 10 :24, :29). A channel that decays produces the answer "you have lost touch," not a verdict.
5. **Price in two currencies.** Description cost in bits and settlement cost in actions do not add (Fig 12 :125-127). A cheap species list can take a whole monitoring budget to settle.
6. **Do not send the theory first.** Lead with the shape and the refuter (Fig 10 :52). Send the generator, not the recording (Fig 4 :22-25).
7. **Keep the gap visible.** Say which relation, species, site and threshold are still unknown; do not fill them with a plausible name (Fig 9 :32).

### 6.3 Strain test and the three questions when no term closes R

- **Strain, resolved:** Fig 12 versus Fig 11 :65 (notation versus action), Fig 11 :43 versus :58 (enumeration versus introduction), world "self-regulating" versus figure "permanent work" (system dynamics versus channel maintenance).
- **Strain, live and named:** the world literature speaks of "restoring interactions" as if the relation were a directly manipulable object. The figures say a bond is a typed relation that is not recoverable from its operands. The phrase is usable only as shorthand for introducing actors and maintaining contact under a declared interaction objective. It is not a figure term.
- **SEARCHED:** is an interaction-slot term already in the restoration library and merely unfound? The field does have interaction metrics; the question is whether they are applied per slot. Cheapest first; if the metric exists and was not used, no import is owed.
- **INWARD:** can the current instrument hold an interaction rate at all? Extend from an instrument already returning something, for example a visit count or a recruitment count (Fig 6 :32-34). A sensor for something that has never registered anywhere is not available by description.
- **OUTWARD:** which frame already predicts the shape of this residual? Network ecology's missing-and-spurious-interactions work is such a frame (Guimerà and Sales-Pardo 2009, opened 2026-11-23), and its atom crosses back only with provenance and a shadow test (Fig 8 :26-28).

### 6.4 Verdict of this audit

The question's either/or framing is refused as a union compound. The shape survives all re-censuses: ingredients and arrangement are different slots, the relation is a bond not a species, a maintained seat can work on arrival but cannot subtract the habitat, the ground is the measured interaction rate, and the particular relation for a particular site is a `gap`. Mode `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural rules, not `grounded`. No strain was smoothed over and no earlier reading was deleted.
