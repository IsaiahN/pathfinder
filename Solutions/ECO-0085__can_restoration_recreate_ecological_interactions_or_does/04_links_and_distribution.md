## 2. Wider literature actually read

Corroboration only. Figures are primary and decide. Overlap is stated, and a shared evidence pool counts as one witness, not two (Fig 2 :40).

- **Restoration ecology.** Truman P. Young, Daniel Petersen and Jeffrey J. Clary, *The ecology of restoration: historical links, emerging issues and unexplored realms*, Ecology Letters 8(6): 662-673, 2005. Opened via Crossref/OpenAlex abstract 2026-11-23. Quote: *"restoration was to be the 'acid test' of our ecological understanding"*; also *"the effects of contingency, such as year effects and priority effects."* Prediction: outcome depends on historical contingency, which a species list cannot hold. **Corroborates** F2 and Fig 3 :24-27. Overlap: restoration ecology pool.
- **Restoration practice and function.** Charles H. Peterson, Romuald N. Lipcius and Sean Paul Powers, *Conceptual progress towards predicting quantitative ecosystem benefits of ecological restorations*, Marine Ecology Progress Series 264: 297-310, 2003. Opened via abstract 2026-11-23. Quote: *"Scaling of restoration projects has historically been done by area of habitat, assuming that function follows."* Prediction: area and structure have been used as proxies for function. **Corroborates** F1 and F7 directly. Overlap: restoration ecology pool, different authors.
- **Biodiversity and interaction loss.** Alfonso Valiente-Banuet et al., *Beyond species loss: the extinction of ecological interactions in a changing world*, Functional Ecology 29(3): 299-307, 2014 (published 2015). Opened via DOI/Crossref record and abstract 2026-11-23. Quote: *"a missed component of biodiversity loss that often accompanies or even precedes species disappearance is the extinction of ecological interactions"*; also *"the loss of species and interactions are decoupled, such that ecological interactions are often lost at a higher rate."* Prediction: species-level recovery can coexist with interaction-level loss. **Corroborates** F1 and F2 at high independence from the framework. Overlap: ecology, but different author pool from the restoration papers.
- **Rewilding and interaction restoration.** Jens-Christian Svenning, Pil Birkefeldt Møller Pedersen, C. Josh Donlan et al., *Science for a wilder Anthropocene: Synthesis and future directions for trophic rewilding research*, PNAS 112(36), 2015. Opened via abstract 2026-11-23. Quote: *"Trophic rewilding is an ecological restoration strategy that uses species introductions to restore top-down trophic interactions and associated trophic cascades to promote self-regulating biodiverse ecosystems."* Prediction: introductions can work on interactions, which is the maintained-seat route in Fig 11 :58, :65. **Corroborates** F4's conditional yes. Overlap: conservation biology, partly shared with the restoration pool; the paper also notes rewilding remains controversial, which fits F3.
- **Ecosystem engineering.** Gianalberto Losapio, Luísa Genes and Christopher J. Knight, *Monitoring and modelling the effects of ecosystem engineers on ecosystem functioning*, Functional Ecology, 2023. Opened via abstract 2026-11-23. Quote: *"Ecosystem engineers are species that directly influence ecosystems by modifying biophysical environments, creating novel habitats, altering biogeochemical cycles, increasing biodiversity and/or modulating ecological processes."* Prediction: an actor can be introduced for its cascade. **Corroborates** Fig 11 :65 at the mechanism level. Overlap: ecology, distinct author group.
- **Network science, low overlap.** Roger Guimerà and Marta Sales-Pardo, *Missing and spurious interactions and the reconstruction of complex networks*, PNAS 106(52), 2009. Opened via abstract 2026-11-23. Quote: *"the reliability of network data is a source of great concern in all fields where complex networks are studied"*; also *"reliably identify both missing and spurious interactions in noisy network observations."* Prediction: presence data does not give the interaction list; both omissions and false links need a separate instrument. **Corroborates** Fig 11 :68-69 and Fig 12 :46-47 from a genuinely different field.
- **Chemistry, low overlap.** Wikipedia, *Isomer*, opened 2026-11-23. Quote: *"isomers are molecules or polyatomic ions with an identical molecular formula ... but distinct arrangements of atoms in space."* Prediction: identical ingredients can be different substances. **Corroborates** Fig 12 :117 at the definitional level. ## 3. Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 4 :22**  -  *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full framework before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator.
- **Fig 10 :42**  -  *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28**  -  *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see on an interaction slot before showing the derivation.
- **Fig 2 :40**  -  *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Do not pool later agreement as corroboration.
- **Fig 2 :42**  -  *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Buy a second, low-overlap frame, not a larger room of agreeing readers.
- **Seat map**  -  receptiveness is a contact estimate, never a verdict weight. No list of likely believers.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the per-slot shape claim and its refuter, (2) the question "what would you count as a failure on the interaction slot?", (3) record the answer, (4) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority, (5) offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each person writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

## 4. The two-stage offer

**Stage 1.** Send the residual and the pre-registered refuter only. The recipient checks the claim against their own ground while not holding the framework's interior. Mode `specified`, not `grounded`. This is Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source,"* and Fig 2 :45, *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious after recording a verdict, offer the three source links above. Their stage-1 verdict is already on record and is never pooled as corroboration.

**Stage 3, method only.** The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer (Fig 4 :22). Each user must name their own ground, write their own refuter before the run and label their mode, because a frame cannot check the direction of its own question (`THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction").

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 claim survives. The refuter in `01` section 4 is live; if one fires, the claim is withdrawn before any outreach.
