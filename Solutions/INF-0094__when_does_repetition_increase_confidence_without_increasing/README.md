## The question restated as an effect

Two quantities are to be kept apart: (1) confidence in a claim, a readout a person can feel and report, and (2) evidence for the claim, whatever would settle whether it is true. The effect to explain is that (1) rises under repetition while (2) stays flat. The answer takes the shape of a *condition*: repetition raises confidence without raising evidence precisely when each repetition is the same claim again (identity) at a later time (sequence), with no new diagnostic contact and no independent frame added. Bind that shape as an ordinary term, as the formula's "Direction" step requires, so it can accrue a residual and be refused.

What an answer would look like before we start: a separator that distinguishes a channel effect from a ground effect, stated so a cognitive scientist could fire a refuter at it. That separator is the sought-for shape, bound in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 2 and tested in `08_expert_verification_packet.md`.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals. Public institutional and opt-in channels only.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree that repetition is a channel effect.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **Agreement is not a verdict.** Fig 7 :13: *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*. A list of people selected for agreeing with the shape claim is a list selected to manufacture the very signal the claim is about.
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. The sources in `10_source_ledger.csv` declare their overlap for the same reason.
3. **A verifier must not hold the interiors.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Stage 1 in `02_outreach_email.md` sends the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
