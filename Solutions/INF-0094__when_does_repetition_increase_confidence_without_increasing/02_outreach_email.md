# Outreach draft: check the separator before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until you can state the residual and its refuter without the framework. The two-stage offer collapses on contact if stage 1 needs the theory to make sense.

---

**Subject:** Would you count this as a failure? Repetition, confidence, evidence

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific shape I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the shape does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms:** a claim is presented again and again. Confidence in it rises. The evidence for whether it is true does not. I want a separator that says exactly when those two come apart, and when they would move together again. My candidate shape (`specified`, not `grounded`) is this: repetition raises confidence when each presentation is the same claim again at a later time, with no new diagnostic information and no independent source added. It stops being true when each repetition is itself an independent measurement of the claim. That is a condition, not a mechanism. I am not claiming to know which brain process implements it.
>
> **The figure-grounded reason, in one line:** the framework disqualifies a quantity a frame produces for itself from being the metric that settles the claim. Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." The anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer when you push on it (Fig 2 :29-30). Confidence moves when you push; a diagnostic measurement does not move merely because you pushed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that repeated false claims are always believed, that knowledge never helps, that the effect is unconditional, or that I have run an experiment. I am also not claiming that confidence is *useless*. It is a readout of contact with a channel; it is not a readout of the ground.
>
> **What would refute the shape:**
> 1. A controlled study in which a claim is repeated, an independent ground reading is taken each time, and confidence and evidence move together with no new diagnostic information and no independent frame introduced.
> 2. A reading of the figures showing that accrued credibility is a ground metric, or that handed-along agreement does add evidence.
> 3. A case where a presentation is both the same claim under identity and a production of new diagnostic content.
>
> I wrote these before sending this. If any one holds, I withdraw the shape. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Newman et al. 2014, "People with Easier to Pronounce Names Promote Truthiness of Claims", PLOS ONE 9(2):e88671: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3935838
> - Hasher, Goldstein and Toppino 1977, "Frequency and the conference of referential validity", Journal of Verbal Learning and Verbal Behavior 16(1):107-112, doi:10.1016/S0022-5371(77)80012-1
> - Processing fluency and the illusion of truth (overview): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Processing_fluency
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so a result contrived to fit the formula is worth nothing. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [your name]

## Deliberately absent
