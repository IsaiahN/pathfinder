# Expert verification packet: repetition, confidence, evidence

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher on mere exposure or truth effects can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** No experiment is claimed. This is a separator and a route, not a measured result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Confidence in a claim (C) is a frame-internal readout and cannot serve as the metric that settles the claim. Evidence (E) is ground-anchored. Repetition raises C without raising E exactly when each presentation is the same claim again (identity) at a later time (sequence), with no new diagnostic contact and no independent frame added. In a group, handed-along agreement compounds while evidence does not. The two quantities can be held apart because they are different substances producing the same outward reading, and a reading cannot comment on its own source. When a repetition is itself an independent measurement, or imports a low-overlap frame with a shadow test, C and E can move together; that is the boundary, not an exception.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1 and 2

- **Statement:** the confidence readout is a frame-internal quantity and fails the anchor test.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :27 - "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." Fig 2 :29 - "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :30 - "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."
- **Refuter:** show that the figures treat accrued credibility as a ground metric; or produce a case where repeating a claim leaves C unchanged while E changes, with no other manipulation.

### C2 - Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 6, 7 and 8

- **Statement:** more presentations at the same reach add no atom, and handed-along agreement compounds while evidence does not.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :49 - "more search at the same reach: is it in here and unfound? The closure does not change. Same atoms, same wall, more of the space actually visited." Fig 8 :32 - "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom." Fig 7 :12-13 - "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not".
- **Refuter:** a controlled study in which repeating a claim changes a diagnostic cue, with a ground reading showing E moved; or a demonstration of two genuinely low-overlap frames whose agreement tracks E rather than merely repeating a shared pool. The second is the legitimate boundary case and would refine, not merely confirm, the claim.

### C3 - World-facing; checkable by a cognitive scientist against the literature

- **Statement:** the empirical effect is a fluency/source-monitoring phenomenon, and the confidence reading cannot discriminate its own source; reliance on it falls when diagnostic information is available.
- **What to check:** Newman et al. 2014, "People with Easier to Pronounce Names Promote Truthiness of Claims", PLOS ONE 9(2):e88671, full text opened: "subjective, tangential properties such as ease of processing can matter when people evaluate information attributed to a source." Also the opened Wikipedia overviews of the illusory truth effect, processing fluency and source-monitoring error, and Zajonc's 1968 mere-exposure result. Does the literature support a *condition* of this shape, or does it show something categorically different?
- **Refuter:** a study in which repetition changes an independently scored diagnostic cue while contact with the ground is held fixed, and confidence tracks the cue. That would make repetition a production of evidence and break the identity/sequence analysis in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that repeated false claims are always believed, or that prior knowledge never protects anyone.
- Not an effect size, not a repetition threshold, not a neural mechanism.
- Not that "processing fluency" is a primitive the framework discovered. It is an import from the cognitive-science frame, provenanced in `10_source_ledger.csv`.
- Not grounded. Nothing here has been settled by an instrument.
- Not a criticism of any named researcher. The packet asks for a failure of the shape, not an endorsement of the framework.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument: a method that tells whether a rise in confidence came from a new diagnostic cue or from repeated exposure to the same content. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Two operational versions:

1. Repeat a claim and, at each repetition, take an independent ground reading of the claim's truth. Does C track E or the count?
2. Manipulate source diagnosticity independently of exposure count. Does reliance on fluency fall when a diagnostic cue is available, as the opened PLOS ONE article's discussion predicts?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :27, Fig 2 :29-30, Fig 6 :49, Fig 7 :12-13 and Fig 8 :32. Five short passages.
2. Check C3 against their own reading of the literature: is the separation of confidence and evidence the right cut, or is there a better one?
3. Name one instrument that would discriminate the source of a confidence rating, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
