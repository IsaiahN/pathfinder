# Structure, order and analogues: what kind of thing is a repetition?

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The primary audit left one structural question open. We established that confidence C is a frame-internal readout and evidence E is ground-anchored, but we did not say what *kind of compound* a repetition is. Without that, "same claim again" is a phrase rather than a derivable condition. Two questions are owed: (1) which bond holds between successive presentations, and (2) is a repetition an isomer of a diagnostic observation, or something else?

Atoms available: the claim, a presentation event, a perceiver, a confidence reading, a diagnostic cue, a ground. Bonds available: the seven plus negation. No new primitive is licensed. Actors and anchors are as in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 1. The order of dependency is bond, then molecule, then loop.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Which bond holds between presentation 1 and presentation 2?

- Fig 12 :67: *"two names, one referent"*. Identity `≡` is symmetric. Presentation 2 has the same referent as presentation 1. Nothing is added to the claim.
- Fig 12 :72: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*. Sequence `→` is ordered. Presentation 2 cannot occur "before" presentation 1; the count is an ordered access, so presentation 3 is not interchangeable with presentation 1.
- Operators table :38: production `⇒` is *"A produces B; B did not exist before"*. This is the bond a genuinely new diagnostic observation would require.

So the repetition compound is **identity over sequence**: `claim ≡ claim → claim ≡ claim ...`. It is not production. The referent is fixed while the number of ordered accesses grows. This is the structural reason C can rise while E stays flat, and it is a derivation rather than a metaphor: a vocabulary with identity and sequence but no production cannot turn the n-th spelling of a claim into a new atom.

**Falsifier Q1:** show a successive presentation that is the same claim under identity *and* introduces a new diagnostic atom under production. The compound would then be admissible as both, the bond analysis fails, and C and E cannot be separated this way.

### Q2 - Are repetition and diagnosis isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

Write the two molecules:

- Repetition: `{claim, presentation, perceiver, confidence reading}` under identity and sequence.
- Diagnosis: `{claim, diagnostic cue, ground, confidence reading}` under production and comparison.

They do **not** share the diagnostic atom or the ground. By Fig 12's own definition they are **not isomers**; they are different substances that can produce the *same outward reading* (a higher confidence number). What they share is a **functional group**: both are "a cue that raises C". The group says what kind (confidence-raising cue); the qualifier says which member (repeated presentation versus diagnostic cue). This is exactly the metamerism described in *The Hard Problem is About Source*: one reading, several sources, and the reading cannot discriminate them.

**Falsifier Q2:** if both molecules can be written over the same atom set with the same ground atom, differing only in bond, the isomer claim is restored, and the right experiment is a within-subject bond manipulation rather than a source-discrimination instrument. I wrote this before running the derivation.

### Q3 - Order, spelling and counting

Fig 12 :92-93: *"Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term"*. Identity is symmetric, so n presentations of the same claim give one term, not n! candidates and not n new terms. Sequence is ordered, so the *count* is carried by the arrangement, not by the referent. This is why a repetition count is a legitimate filter on how much of a space has been visited (Fig 6 :49) and a disqualified witness for truth (Fig 9 :60). The system counting repetitions as if each were a term is counting the denominator; formula step 8 warns that a library that grows is not a reach that grows.

Operators table :59-62: under a symmetric bond the swap test changes nothing; under an ordered bond swapping the operands changes the thing. The swap test separates the two cases in one pass, so this is checkable without an experiment on memory.

### Q4 - Is repetition a catalyst or a cycle?

Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."* Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes."*

Repeated exposure is a cycle: the output C of presentation n becomes part of the input state of presentation n+1 (familiarity is carried forward). The sign decides whether it closes against the ground or swirls inside the frame. Fig 7 :12-13: *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"*. Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* So a repetition cycle with low ground pressure is a mirror chain; the same cycle with high ground pressure is selection. The difference is not repetition count but what the cycle is ranged against.

**Falsifier Q4:** a repetition loop whose output does not re-enter as input (each presentation is independent of the last) would break the cycle reading. In that case repetition is a set, not a chain, and the count has no compounding effect on C.

### Q5 - Analogues from low-overlap fields (world, secondary)

These corroborate the shape but do not decide it. Each was opened; all are in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

- **Animal behaviour.** *Mere-exposure effect* (Wikipedia, 2026): Zajonc's chick experiment, where chicks chose the tone played prenatally. Familiarity changes preference without any new evidence about the tone. Predicts domain-agnosticism of the effect; corroborates.
- **Business.** Same source: Huberman 2001, "Familiarity Breeds Investment", traders favour domestic securities merely because they are familiar. *Processing fluency* (Wikipedia, 2026) adds pronounceable stock names and ticker symbols performing better after IPO. Predicts that exposure alone moves confidence in a domain with real money; corroborates.
- **Information science.** The frequency-validity relationship discussed under Gigerenzer in the *Illusory truth effect* source: in natural environments frequency often *is* correlated with validity, so the fluency heuristic is not a pure error. Predicts the boundary case where repetition and evidence legitimately move together, namely when the environment makes frequency diagnostic. Corroborates and bounds.
- **Human cognition, source discrimination.** *Source-monitoring error* (Wikipedia, 2026): the false-fame effect, where repeated non-famous names are later misidentified as famous. Predicts that repetition changes the source attribution rather than the underlying fact; corroborates F8 of the primary audit.

Overlap warning: the four Wikipedia pages share one encyclopedia pool. They are one witness with several faces, not four (Fig 2 :40).

## CONCLUSION

A repetition is identity over sequence, not production. It is therefore not an isomer of a diagnostic observation; both are members of the same confidence-raising functional group, and their identical outward reading is a projection that cannot identify its source. Repetition is a cycle whose sign depends on what it is ranged against: low ground pressure gives a mirror chain, high ground pressure gives selection. Order and count live in the arrangement, so a count is a filter and never a witness. The falsifiers for Q1, Q2 and Q4 were written before the derivation.
