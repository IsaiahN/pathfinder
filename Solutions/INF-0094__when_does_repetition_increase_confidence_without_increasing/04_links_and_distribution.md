## 2. Relevant wider literature, read second-hand where noted

- **Hasher, Goldstein and Toppino 1977**, "Frequency and the conference of referential validity", Journal of Verbal Learning and Verbal Behavior 16(1):107-112. The original frequency-validity study. Read via the opened Wikipedia overview and the reference list of the opened PLOS ONE article.
- **Zajonc 1968**, "Attitudinal Effects Of Mere Exposure", Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 9(2, Pt.2):1-27. Source of the "sufficient condition" quote. Read via the opened Wikipedia overview. The field name for this solution comes from this tradition.
- **Bornstein 1989**, "Exposure and affect: Overview and meta-analysis of research, 1968-1987", Psychological Bulletin 106(2):265-289. Meta-analysis of 208 experiments, r = 0.26, strongest for brief unfamiliar stimuli; liking can turn down after many repetitions. Read via the opened Wikipedia overview.
- **Newman et al. 2014**, PLOS ONE 9(2):e88671. Opened in full. Key boundary: people rely on fluency most "*when diagnostic information is unavailable*". This is the empirical shadow of the figure claim that repetition adds no atom.
- **Fazio et al. 2015**, "Knowledge does not protect against illusory truth", Journal of Experimental Psychology: General 144(5):993-1002. Second-hand via Wikipedia; use with the caveat, do not quote directly.
- **Dechêne et al. 2010**, "The truth about the truth: A meta-analytic review of the truth effect", Personality and Social Psychology Review 14:238-257. Second-hand via the PLOS ONE reference list; use with the caveat.

Overlap check: the four Wikipedia pages share one encyclopedia pool. Where they agree, that is one witness, not four (Fig 2 :40). Newman et al. 2014 is a distinct primary source. The three essays share an author and an evidence pool with the figures; their agreement with the figure verdict is expected and carries little independent weight.

- **Stage 1, before anything else.** Send the residual in effect terms and the four refuters from `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4. Record their verdict *before* showing any theory.
