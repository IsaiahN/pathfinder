# Public links and distribution

All links below were fetched and read on 2026-11-23. Where a fetch failed, that is recorded rather than hidden, and a different open version of the same work was found or the source was dropped.

## Verified sources I actually opened

| what | URL | verified content |
|---|---|---|
| Reinhart and Rogoff, "Growth in a Time of Debt" | https://www.nber.org/papers/w15639 | NBER WP 15639, January 2010, revised December 2011. 44 countries, about 200 years, over 3,700 annual observations. Reports a debt/GDP threshold near 90 percent, `no apparent contemporaneous link between inflation and public debt levels for the advanced countries as a group`, and that in emerging markets `inflation rises sharply as debt increases`. |
| Bordo and Levy, "Do Enlarged Fiscal Deficits Cause Inflation: The Historical Record" | https://www.nber.org/papers/w28195 | NBER WP 28195, December 2020, published in Economic Affairs 41(1), 2021. Two centuries of history; relation `generally holds in wartime`; the 2007-2008 crisis is a case where expansionary fiscal and monetary policy did not raise inflation. |
| Friedman, "Crowding Out Or Crowding In?" | https://www.nber.org/papers/w0284 | NBER WP 0284, October 1978, Brookings Papers on Economic Activity 3, 1978. `Debt-financed deficits need not "crowd out" any private investment, and may even "crowd in" some.` Direction depends on substitutability among money, bonds and real capital, and on debt management policy. |
| Angeletos, Lian and Wolf, "Deficits and Inflation: HANK meets FTPL" | https://www.nber.org/papers/w33102 | NBER WP 33102, November 2024, revised June 2026. FTPL deficits drive inflation only when the fiscal authority is active or dominant; the HANK result holds `provided that two testable conditions are met`: high marginal propensities to consume and `that deficits are not accompanied by interest rate or tax hikes in the near term`. |
| Plante, Richter and Zubairy, "Revisiting the Relationship Between Interest Rates and Government Borrowing" | https://www.nber.org/digest/202510/revisiting-relationship-between-interest-rates-and-government-borrowing | NBER Digest, 1 October 2025, summary of WP 34018. A 1 point rise in debt/GDP raises the five-year-ahead five-year Treasury rate by about 3 basis points; about three quarters is term premium; the close relation between expected rates and expected inflation broke down in the early 2000s. |
| Catão and Terrones, "Fiscal deficits and inflation" | https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/moneco/v52y2005i3p529-554.html | Journal of Monetary Economics 52(3), 2005, pages 529-554. 107 countries, 1960-2001. `Yet empirical research has had limited success in uncovering this relationship`; strong positive association among high-inflation and developing groups `but not among low-inflation advanced economies`. |
| Kim and Ulate, "Are Inflation Expectations Currently Well Anchored?" | https://www.frbsf.org/research-and-insights/publications/economic-letter/2026/11/are-inflation-expectations-currently-well-anchored/ | FRBSF Economic Letter 2026-32, 9 November 2026. Sensitivity measure of perceived credibility; recent US expectations `likely to be well anchored`; the nominal anchor is treated as a credibility instrument, not as a measurement of fiscal causation. |
| IMF, "Inflation Peaking amid Low Growth" | https://www.imf.org/en/publications/weo/issues/2023/01/31/world-economic-outlook-update-january-2023 | WEO Update, 31 January 2023. Advises `Target fiscal support at those most affected by elevated food and energy prices` and `Withdraw broad-based fiscal relief measures`: a conditional fiscal stance, not a blanket deficit verdict. |

## Wider literature that bears on the question

The eight sources above were chosen from separate instruments, not separate praises of one conclusion:

- **Historical narrative:** Bordo and Levy, with the wartime and post-2008 contrasts.
- **Cross-country panel:** Catão and Terrones, with the nonlinear inflation-tax-base formulation.
- **Long-run cross-country data:** Reinhart and Rogoff, with the threshold and the advanced/emerging split.
- **Portfolio theory:** Friedman, with the crowd-in possibility and debt-management condition.
- **DSGE model comparison:** Angeletos, Lian and Wolf, with two testable conditions.
- **High-frequency asset pricing:** Plante, Richter and Zubairy, with the 3 basis point estimate and term-premium decomposition.
- **High-frequency expectations:** Kim and Ulate, with the credibility sensitivity gauge.
- **Institutional policy:** IMF WEO, with the targeted-versus-broad fiscal stance.

All eight are economics. That overlap is declared in `10_source_ledger.csv`; their agreement is one field with several instruments, not eight independent witnesses. Fig 2 :40: `agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool`.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 4 :22**, `Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down.` Sending the full framework before anyone has described a residual sends a recording rather than a generator. Lead with the split, the per-domain verdicts and the refuter; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42**, `A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it.` A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27**, `describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit.` Ask what they would expect to see if the conditional shape were true before showing them the sources.
- **Fig 2 :42**, `Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong.` Here the cost of being wrong is a wrong policy verdict on deficits, so seek readers whose data and instruments differ from the one pool used here.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the residual and the four-part shape; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) record their verdict; (4) only then the three links in `02_outreach_email.md`, with the note that the papers, not my summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1:** output and refuter first. No theory, no links, no request for agreement.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in:** the sources and the packet in `08_expert_verification_packet.md`. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.

**Hard precondition:** stage 1 does not work if the shape claim is wrong. The refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 are the test. If one fires, withdraw the claim before any outreach.
