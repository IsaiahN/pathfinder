## Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual line alone, before reading the primary's conclusions, in the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the gap between a deficit-only forecast and the observed inflation and yield readings under named conditions. Arity: two effects plus a condition variable, at least three. Symmetry: the effects are not interchangeable; swapping an ordering of prices for a directed price of borrowing changes the claim, so both bonds are ordered. Scale: flow (deficit), stock (debt), rate of change (inflation), price (interest rate). Union, not partition (Fig 9): a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either.
- **Domain 1, fiscal financing.** Actors: treasury, legislature, issuers. Currency: budget balance, debt stock. Anchor: the budget constraint. Financing instruments are an internal partition.
- **Domain 2, monetary and price formation.** Actors: central bank, bond market, households, firms. Currency: money, price level, yields. Anchor: measured inflation and yields. The inflation target revises, so it is a convention; this domain is a union of regime, expectations and dominance conditions.
- **Domain 3, interest-rate formation.** Actors: bond market, issuers. Currency: yields, maturity, premia. Anchor: observed rates. Union of term premium, expected real rates and expected inflation as sources of one reading.
- **Domain 4, measurement and verification.** Actors: datasets, econometricians. Currency: estimates, standard errors. Anchor: the data, with instrument limits. Independence must be bought.
- **Not a domain:** public opinion on deficits. No nameable anchor from the residual line alone, so it stays in the habitat.

Order by dependency: refuse the single-coefficient shape before minting (step 2); perceive both slots before averaging anything (Fig 1); describe the residual before picking a frame (Fig 9); measure `R_T` at the flow-to-stock crossing before comparing magnitudes (Fig 4).

The Symbols table defines `R_T` as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried through the folder: "deficits are conditional; report inflation and rates separately; thresholds are filters; the flow-to-stock crossing loses information."

**`R_T` is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- **Which condition binds.** The shorthand says "conditional" without selecting. The concretisation needs a named regime (wartime, advanced low-inflation, emerging high-inflation, non-accommodating central bank) before any sign is predicted.
- **The unit of the effect.** "Inflation" in the shorthand is one slot; the sources measure it as a price-level growth rate over different horizons and samples, and "rates" is a five-year-ahead forward rate, not a policy rate. The shorthand cannot hold which instrument produced the reading.

- **The threshold's status.** The shorthand says "filter." Re-applying the 90 percent cut as a result rather than a search guide would silently set `R_T` to zero and commit the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a structure must be split into regimes and two effect slots kept separate.
