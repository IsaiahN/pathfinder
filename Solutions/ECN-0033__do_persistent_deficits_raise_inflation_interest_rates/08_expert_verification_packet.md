# Expert verification packet: the Tether on persistent deficits, inflation and interest rates

**What this is:** a claim in a form a fiscal or monetary economist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** No new measurement is claimed.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The four offered answers form a union of overlapping conditions, so "limited in some circumstances" is an instruction to split rather than a fourth coefficient. Inflation and interest rates are different quantities and must be reported in separate rows. The measured data are the anchor; a central bank's inflation target is a convention that revises and so cannot settle the question. A debt/GDP threshold is a filter for search budget, never a witness. "Persistent" names a lossy flow-to-stock crossing. Per regime, the world evidence says the inflation link is strong in high-inflation and emerging-market episodes and weak or absent in low-inflation advanced economies, while the interest-rate link is real but bounded and mostly a term premium. No general coefficient is derivable from the figures; that is recorded as a `gap`.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 : framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 9 and Figure 1
- **Statement:** the question is a union compound; unbundle before searching; keep the two effect slots separate and never average them.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54 `And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again.` Fig 9 :56 `A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones.` Fig 1 :19 `The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated.` Fig 1 :22 `Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average.`
- **What to check:** do the four offered answers actually overlap across regimes, or is one condition sufficient in every case? If one condition settles all four, the union reading is wrong.
- **Refuter:** show a single mechanism mapping the deficit to both effects with the same sign and magnitude in all regimes, no condition doing work. Or show that averaging the two slots preserves a signal that the separate slots lose.

### C2 : monetary and price formation; checkable by an economist against published evidence
- **Statement:** the inflation response is conditional on the monetary and expectations regime, and the inflation target is a convention rather than the anchor.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :33 `The anchor must not update.` Fig 2 :35 `Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you.` The world check is Catão and Terrones (2005), who report that among 107 countries the association is strong in high-inflation and developing groups `but not among low-inflation advanced economies`; Bordo and Levy (2020), who find the relation `generally holds in wartime` and was absent after 2007-2008; and Kim and Ulate (FRBSF, 2026), whose sensitivity gauge measures credibility and treats the nominal target as an instrument.
- **What to check:** is the regime split real out of sample, or is it a post-hoc label fitted to the same episodes? Can a central bank revise its target? If it can, the target is not the anchor by the figure's definition.
- **Refuter:** two genuinely low-overlap datasets converging on one unconditional effect with no regime interaction; or evidence that a published relationship moves systematically when re-analysed by a frame with a declared stake.

### C3 : interest-rate formation and the flow-to-stock crossing
- **Statement:** the rate response is bounded and mostly a term premium; "persistent" is a lossy flow-to-stock crossing whose gap must be recorded.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :30 `So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked.` Fig 1 :19 `The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated.` The world check is Plante, Richter and Zubairy (NBER Digest, 2025): a 1 percentage point rise in debt/GDP raises the five-year-ahead five-year Treasury rate by about 3 basis points, roughly three quarters term premium, with the expected-rate/expected-inflation link breaking down in the early 2000s; and Friedman (1978), who shows debt-financed deficits `need not "crowd out" any private investment, and may even "crowd in" some` depending on asset substitutability.
- **What to check:** does the term-premium decomposition survive a different sample or instrument? Does a deficit with no debt accumulation produce the same rate effect as one with a large stock?
- **Refuter:** a rate response of comparable size in every regime on low-overlap data, or a demonstration that the flow-to-stock crossing is lossless for these quantities (`R_T = 0`).

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a general numerical coefficient. Magnitudes are a `gap` for the ground (Fig 8 :39).
- Not that deficits are harmless; "limited in some circumstances" is conditional.
- Not that 90 percent debt/GDP is a verdict. It is a filter (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that the central bank's target is the ground. It revises (Fig 2 :33).
- Not a causal direction between inflation and rates. They are a functional group; the isomer claim in `06_isomers_and_order.md` is conditional and has its own falsifier.
- Not `grounded`. This packet was not settled by a measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A low-overlap natural experiment isolating a deficit shock under a fixed monetary regime, with inflation and yields measured separately.
2. A decomposition of long-yield moves that can comment on the source: term premium versus expected real rates versus expected inflation.
3. An out-of-sample test of the condition variables (accommodation, denomination, slack, expectations regime).
4. A measurement of `R_T` at the flow-to-stock crossing.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :54-56, Fig 1 :19-22, Fig 2 :33 and Fig 4 :30, four short passages.
2. Check C2 against one dataset you know well: does the regime split hold there, or is it fitted to the episodes cited?
3. Check C3 against your own yield decomposition: is the 3 basis point estimate mostly term premium in your sample?
4. Name one instrument that would discriminate the conditions out of sample, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
