# Outreach draft: check the shape before you decide whether the framing is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` survive your own check. If the stage-1 output fails, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One claim about deficits, inflation and rates, with its refuter written in advance

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question, in effect terms:** what changes in measured inflation and measured interest rates when a government runs a deficit persistently, and under which conditions does that change become large, small or absent? The four offered answers, inflation, rates, both, and limited in some circumstances, are not four competing coefficients. They are a bundle of overlapping conditions. The third and fourth are not extra magnitudes; they are an instruction to split the bundle.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):**
> 1. The two effects are different quantities and must be reported in separate rows. Inflation is an ordering of prices over time; an interest rate is a directed price on intertemporal exchange. A headline that averages them throws away the signal that says which one is live.
> 2. The measured data are the ground. A central bank's inflation target is a convention that the bank itself can revise, so it is efficient for coordinating expectations and disqualified as the thing that settles this question.
> 3. The response is conditional. The inflation link is strong in high-inflation and emerging-market episodes and weak or absent in low-inflation advanced economies; the interest-rate link is real but small and mostly a term premium. A debt/GDP threshold is a filter for where to look next, never a witness for what the deficit did.
> 4. "Persistent" is a flow accumulating into a stock. Compressing it to "more debt" loses denomination, maturity, holder and monetary response; the loss has to be recorded, not assumed away.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** a number for the general case. I could find no figure-grounded way to derive a coefficient, and I am recording that as a gap rather than filling it with an estimate. The numbers in the literature are corroboration from the world, not results the framework produced.
>
> **What would refute the shape:** I wrote these down before running this.
> 1. One mechanism maps the deficit to inflation and to rates with the same sign and comparable magnitude in every regime, with no condition doing work.
> 2. The relationship changes systematically when the same data is re-analysed by a frame with a declared stake in the answer.
> 3. Two genuinely low-overlap datasets converge on one unconditional effect with no regime interaction.
> 4. The condition variables turn out to be post-hoc labels with no out-of-sample predictive power.
>
> If any holds, I retract the shape. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** these are the public sources I actually read. The papers, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Reinhart and Rogoff, "Growth in a Time of Debt" (NBER Working Paper 15639, 2010): https://www.nber.org/papers/w15639
> - Bordo and Levy, "Do Enlarged Fiscal Deficits Cause Inflation: The Historical Record" (NBER Working Paper 28195, 2020): https://www.nber.org/papers/w28195
> - Kim and Ulate, "Are Inflation Expectations Currently Well Anchored?" (FRBSF Economic Letter 2026-32): https://www.frbsf.org/research-and-insights/publications/economic-letter/2026/11/are-inflation-expectations-currently-well-anchored/
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the eight-step loop is public at https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether . Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state whether your mode is general, specified or grounded. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks for your time.
> [name]

| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35 `Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you.` |

| Per-slot rows, no average | Fig 1 :19 `The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated.` |

| Threshold called a filter | Fig 9 :60 `Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict.` |

| No request for trust or prestige | *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: `Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it.` |

## Two-stage sequence

1. **Stage 1:** send the residual, the four-part shape claim and the four refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict before showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, send the three links above and the packet in `08_expert_verification_packet.md`. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40: `agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool`).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the method, with the instruction that each user writes a refuter before running it.
