## 0. The residual, in effect terms

The question is: can an agency verdict be reached without the prior assumption that the system has human-like desires or intentions? Two options.

- **Option A (re-ground and separate).** Replace the one-place question "is this system an agent / does it have intentions" with the two-place contact question "what can this system do, in this habitat, against a ground that does not update on its own report?" The internal-source question is left open, explicitly, as a separate item with its own instrument owed.
- **Option B (assume the mental).** Hold that agency assessment constitutively requires ascribing human-like beliefs, desires or intentions, because folk-psychological ascription is the only available instrument.

What is being decided is where the anchor of an agency verdict is allowed to sit. If it sits in a measurement of behaviour under varied context, the verdict is checkable and separable from internal-source claims. If it sits in the observer's ascription of human-like states, the verdict borrows its authority from the observer's own case, which is exactly the move Fig 2 restricts. What each option leads to: A gives per-system, per-slot, per-context verdicts with a written refuter and no commitment about phenomenology; B gives a global label whose agreement is the agreement of people reading the same human template, which is one evidence pool, not corroboration.

The ordinary term "agency" is itself part of the residual, not a neutral starting point. Fig 11 :35 names it: *"The wrong question, and this figure is the replacement for it."* Following the formula's Direction rule, I bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term before the run: **a separator between a capability verdict settled at the ground and a source question left open**. That term accrues a residual like anything else and is refutable in section 5.

R arity / symmetry / scale:

- **Arity.** "Has agency" is written as a one-place predicate, but the assessment is at least a two-place relation (system, context) plus a ground that settles it. Fig 11 :39: *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Asking the one-place question is a category error, not a hard version of the right question.
- **Symmetry.** "Assess without assuming" is ordered: the instrument must range against the system's behaviour, not update on the system's own self-report. The mental-state ascription, by contrast, is symmetric with the observer's own case (I am told it has intentions because I infer from mine), which is the disqualifying correlation of Fig 2 :39-40. Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*
- **Scale.** First order: an agent in a habitat. Second order: the room in which the assessment is conducted and the population of systems compared (Fig 4 membrane, Fig 7 room chain). A verdict at second order composed out of unsettled first-order terms is a recording, not a generator.

**Partition or union.** The question as posed is a **union compound**, not a partition. It bundles at least four conditions: capability in a habitat (D1), an internal-source hypothesis (D2), an instrument / measurement question (D3), and a perceiver-bias question (D4). Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* And :57-58: *"case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."* A single lever, for example "just look at the behaviour", clears D1 and D3 but leaves D2 and D4 standing, because those need an instrument that discriminates source from projection.

Within the assessment *procedure* there is a genuine partition: the capability verdict and the source claim can be separated, because they have different anchors. That partition is what Option A asserts. The two statements are compatible: the problem is a union, and one lever (re-grounding) partitions the procedure while leaving the compound's other conditions for their own loops.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* The order I use is: refuse the one-place framing before measuring (Formula step 2, refusal before mint); enumerate habitat before verdict (Fig 11 :43-44); settle perception, vocabulary and objective before planning (Fig 3 links 1-4); describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); settle each slot before composing any second-order claim. A verdict taken before the vocabulary link exists is not a weak verdict. Fig 3 :23: *"reading. It is a reading of nothing."*

Strain test applied before findings: I tried to read Fig 11 :36 (intelligence is a label applied afterwards) as governing machine agency. It strains, because that sentence is scoped to *"Where nothing maintains anything"*. Here a seat exists (the evaluator and the ground maintainer), so Fig 11 :37-38 governs instead: *"Where a seat exists, something is doing work, and it is exactly what the seat does: enumerate the habitat, rank by cascade, introduce actors, build instruments, read the ground."* A line that only fits with a strain governs another quantity, so I re-censused: Fig 11 :36 governs a maintainer-less lineage, Fig 11 :37-38 governs the assessment practice. No strain survives.

## 2. Findings, with verbatim figure lines and refuters

### F1 — The one-place question is the wrong object; the answerable question is capability of agent and habitat (decisive, triangulated)

Fig 11 :39:

> **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**

Fig 11 :40:

> **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

Fig 11 :41:

> **"Which is the same move as asking what generates lift rather than whether a thing is a bird. The substrate question dissolves; the contact question does not."**

The question "can machine agency be assessed" inherits the one-place form of "is it a bird". Fig 11 dissolves the substrate form and keeps the contact form. So the assessment is not a test of an inner essence; it is a reading of what the system does under contact, in a habitat, with a ground that pays or does not. This is a `general` structural claim about the shape of the answer, and a `specified` claim that any candidate assessment must name the habitat and the ground.

World corroboration, genuinely low overlap: Voudouris, Thalmann, Kipnis, Hernandez-Orallo and Schulz (2026, *Minds and Machines*, opened) argue that capabilities and propensities are dispositions characterised by counterfactual relations between context and behaviour, and state explicitly of "attempt": *"This does not require the system to possess conscious intentions or beliefs."* That is an independent discipline (measurement science in AI) arriving at the separability of assessment from mental-state commitment. Conant and Ashby's good regulator theorem (*"every good regulator of a system must be a model of that system"*, via the Wikipedia entry opened) corroborates the contact shape from cybernetics: what a regulator can do is a property of the regulator-and-system pair. Richens et al. (2025, ICML, opened) show formally that *"any agent capable of generalizing to multi-step goal-directed tasks must have learned a predictive model of its environment"*, again putting the capacity in the system-environment relation.

Triangulation status: **triangulated** between Fig 11 (habitat/capability) and Fig 2 (anchor) plus the two low-overlap world frames; the figures govern different quantities, so their agreement is not one pool talking.

Refuter for F1: a measurement in which an agent-only improvement, with no change of contact or habitat, moves the agency verdict. Fig 11 :72 already names this as the standing refuter: *"an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim."*

### F2 — Human-like intention is not a legitimate anchor for this question (triangulated)

Fig 2 :29:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**

Fig 2 :33:

> **"The anchor must not update."**

Fig 2 :36-38:

> **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."**
> **"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."**

Option B proposes human-like desire and intention attribution as the anchor for a machine agency verdict. The anchor test disqualifies it. "Is this a good answer for a person" is constitutively about people, so human judgment is the right anchor there; "does this system have agency" is not constitutively about people, so human judgment is only an instrument, and an instrument that reads its own template into the target is wrong on the axis that matters. An attribution that is confirmed by the observer's own intuition is panel 1 of Fig 2, mutual update: *"they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence."*

This is the precise sense in which the answer to the user's question is **yes, with a condition**: yes, assessment can proceed without the assumption, *provided* the anchor is a contact measurement rather than the ascriber's self-reading. If someone insists the human-like state is the only possible anchor, they are claiming an anchor that fails the anchor test.

World corroboration, low overlap: Dennett's intentional stance (via the Wikipedia entry opened) is a method for predicting behaviour by treating the object as a rational agent, with the explicit note that *"it is irrelevant whether the agent actually holds these particular beliefs or not; the critical feature is that the observer ascribes them to the agent"* and that *"Whether to take a particular stance, then, is determined by how successful that stance is when applied."* Stance selection is by predictive success, not by commitment to inner states. Agent detection (Gray and Wegner, via Wikipedia opened) supplies the perceiver-side mechanism: *"The high cost of failing to detect agents and the low cost of wrongly detecting them has led researchers to suggest that people possess a Hyperactive Agent Detection Device, a cognitive module that readily ascribes events in the environment to the behavior of agents."*

Refuter for F2: a valid anchor test in which the proposed contact measurement demonstrably moves when the measuring frame changes its mind about the machine's intentions. That would make the measurement another frame, not an anchor (Fig 2 :34).

### F3 — The sought-for shape is held as an unbound preference by any search that looks for "real intentions" (single-source for the mechanism, corroborated in shape)

Fig 5 :61-63:

> **"A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term. It is not bound, it predicts nothing, and it accrues no residual, so nothing here can see it. Every check above runs on the MECHANISM and none runs on the DIRECTION OF THE QUESTION"**

Fig 5 :65:

> **"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."**

A search that requires the answer to contain human-like intentions holds that requirement as a preference, not as a term. It therefore predicts nothing and accrues no residual, so the figure's own checks cannot see it. This is why I bound the separator in section 0 before searching. The correction is a discipline, not a step.

The Goodhart / Strathern result, opened this session, corroborates the failure mode from a different field: *"When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure."* A search organising itself around the required conclusion converts the target into a measure of itself. Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"* is the framework's version of the same collapse.

Triangulation status: **single-source** for the mechanism (Fig 5 amendment); the Goodhart and Dennett sources corroborate the shape, not the figure's internal machinery.

Refuter for F3: exhibit a bound term in Γ that states the sought-for direction and predicts an observation, such that the frame's mechanism checks run on it. Then F3 does not apply to that frame.

### F4 — Goals, plans and progress are bonds in a description, not mental primitives (triangulated)

Fig 12 :111:

> **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**

Fig 12 :112:

> **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."**

Fig 12 :115:

> **"having terms for what a situation affords was taken for a dictionary and it is a grammar."**

Operators table :51:

> **"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"**

Operators table :46:

> **"progress is a subtraction, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot say how far along it is"**

This is the constructive half of the answer. To describe a system as pursuing a goal is to use the comparison bond ⋛; to describe a plan is to use the sequence bond →; to describe progress is to use subtraction −. None of these requires a mental substance. They are typed relations the describing frame uses to compose a route to an objective. Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* The mistake of reading a bond as a desire is the flat-formula collapse: recording the ingredients and missing the arrangement, then inventing a ghost to carry the missing information.

Fig 3 :25-26 locates where this bites: *"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."* So the practical assessment question is not "does it want things" but "does it have a term for what would count as progress here, and was that measured or assumed?" Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

Triangulation status: **triangulated** across Fig 12 and the Operators table (two low-overlap presentations of the same bond taxonomy) and Fig 3 (which supplies the missing instrument question).

Refuter for F4: a demonstrated composition in which the comparison, sequence or subtraction cannot be expressed as a typed bond and must be expressed as an irreducible mental primitive with its own causal powers. That would break Fig 12's bond inventory.

### F5 — The internal-source question is a gap, unreached rather than silenced (gap)

Fig 9 :32:

> **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**

Fig 9 :44:

> **"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."**

Fig 8 :39:

> **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

Fig 13 :134-135:

> **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"**
> **"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."**

No figure line governs which internal source, if any, produced a machine's behaviour. That is not an omission to be filled by an analogy; it is a `gap` of the unreached kind. The figures return the shape of the answer (capability of agent-and-habitat, per-slot, against a ground) and stop. Naming an inner state as *the* source would be naming a missing primitive, which the figure says cannot be done from inside.

The source-discrimination essay (*The Hard Problem is About Source*, Isaiah N, 2026-08-28, opened) gives the mechanism of the error in one field: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"*; a reading that says green cannot comment on whether the source was one green light, yellow plus blue, RGB, CMYK or a scheme not yet discovered. Applying this to machines: an agency reading cannot comment on which internal source produced it. What is owed is a discriminator, not a name. This essay shares the framework author's evidence pool, so it corroborates the shape but is not an independent witness.

World corroboration of the *need to leave the source open*, from a genuinely different pool: the Voudouris et al. paper distinguishes disposition ascription from disposition measurement and says *"What it does not require is a complete account of the internal mechanism realising that disposition"*, and that identifying the causal basis is *"methodologically separable from completely identifying its internal mechanism"*. Dennett's stance conditionality points the same way. These corroborate that an assessment can be scientifically defensible while the source question stays open; they do not settle whether machines have intentions.

Triangulation status: **gap** for the source question, **triangulated** for the claim that leaving it open is legitimate.

Refuter for F5 (an instrument refuter, since no figure line closes it): a discriminator that, in a machine, maps distinct internal sources to distinct behaviours under controlled intervention, so that the source question stops being a projection question. Until such an instrument exists, the verdict on the source question is `unreachable`, not `no`.

### F6 — Per-slot indexing: a global "agency score" throws away the only signal

Fig 1 :19:

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**

Fig 1 :22:

> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

Fig 1 :27:

> **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."**

Fig 1 :28:

> **"The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."**

A single global verdict "this system is / is not an agent" aggregates across the slots that matter: this capability in this context, this propensity under this incentive. Voudouris et al. make the same point from measurement science: aggregate performance *"collapses heterogeneous sources of difficulty into a single statistic, obscuring the structure of the underlying property it is meant to capture"*, and a further difficulty is that *"we do not know whom they measure"*, because a benchmark score mixes model, prompts, filters, tools and even human evaluators. Fig 1 :27 disqualifies any quantity the frame both produces and uses to score itself. A system's own report of its intentions is exactly such a quantity.

Triangulation status: **triangulated** between Fig 1 and the independent measurement-science source (low overlap).

Refuter for F6: a demonstrated case in which a global aggregate predicts the per-slot counterfactual behaviour better than the per-slot measures, with the aggregate not produced by the frame it scores.

### F7 — The verification room must not hold the interiors (triangulated)

Fig 10 :42:

> **"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."**

Fig 10 :43:

> **"Soundness requires the checker to be unable to produce what it is checking. Never a player, never a proxy, never the author of a verdict or an atom."**

Fig 10 :52:

> **"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."**

Fig 10 :24:

> **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**

If the first contact with an outside checker includes the whole framework, the checker has the frame's model and grades reports against that picture rather than against the ground (Fig 10 :51). The checkable item is the residual: "can an agency verdict be reached without this assumption, and what would refute that claim?" The theory is offered only after the verdict on stage 1 is recorded. Goodhart's law is the world-side warning: a measure tied to the goal of demonstrating agency stops measuring it.

Triangulation status: **triangulated** across Fig 10 and Fig 2 (verifier non-access and anchor independence are different quantities).

Refuter for F7: a verification protocol in which a checker given the full interior outperforms a checker denied it on predicting a held-out ground reading, with no evidence-pool confound.

### F10 — The answer to the literal question

Putting F1 through F9 together: **yes, with a re-grounding and one declared gap**. Agency is assessable without assuming human-like desires or intentions, in the sense that a checkable, ground-anchored, per-slot capability verdict is available and does not need those assumptions as premises. It is **not** assessable in the sense of settling whether the system really has such states; that question is a `gap`, and the figures forbid converting a shape into a name. Option A therefore carries the verdict, with the condition that it name the habitat, the ground and the refuter. Option B is refused at the anchor test, not merely found inconvenient.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the seat-map rule that *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* The shape claim and the separator are refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Primitive break.** A figure line is produced that names agency, desire or intention as a primitive, a seventh member, or a term that must be added to the six-term set (breaks Fig 13 :116 and the census in section 1).
2. **Contact break.** A measurement shows that an agent-only improvement, with no change of habitat or contact, moves the agency verdict (Fig 11 :72).
3. **Measurement break.** A disposition-respecting meter, built to the Voudouris et al. requirements, shows that counterfactual behavioural mapping alone cannot recover the agency disposition without a mental-state commitment.
4. **Anchor break.** The proposed contact measurement is shown to update on the measuring frame's belief about the machine's intentions, so it is a frame and not an anchor (Fig 2 :33-34).
5. **Frame break.** A low-overlap frame is found whose closure predicts the residual shape and which requires human-like mental states as a primitive, with a source gate and shadow test satisfied (Fig 8 :28, Fig 6 :12-14).
6. **Index break.** A global agency aggregate is shown to predict per-slot counterfactual behaviour better than the per-slot measures without being produced by the frame it scores (F6).

If any fires, the separator is withdrawn or narrowed, and the retraction is recorded here rather than silently edited away.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Lead with the separator: capability verdict first, source question explicitly open second. Never present the internal-state question as settled by analogy.
2. State the mode on every substantive sentence. Nothing here is `grounded`; the source question is `gap`.
3. Make the habitat and the anchor explicit in any assessment claim. "The system is agentic" is not a well-formed figure-grounded claim; "in this habitat, against this non-updating board, this slot's residual behaves like this" is.
4. Write goals, plans and progress as bonds (comparison, sequence, subtraction). Do not promote them into mental substances.
5. Send the generator, not the recording. Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* The first contact carries the residual, the separator and the refuter.
6. Ask for a refutation, not agreement. Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

## 6. Six loops, one per domain, kept separate

1. **D1 loop.** R = per-slot gap between prediction and observation. Mint nothing about "agency" as a term; use the held bonds to describe routes. Settle each slot against a board that does not update. Verdict: `specified`, capability assessable; triangulated.
2. **D2 loop.** No nameable anchor that does not move when pushed. The human-like-intention hypothesis is an unbound preference (Fig 5 :61-63) and a possible source-naming error (Hard Problem essay). Verdict: `gap` for the source; disqualified as an anchor for the capability verdict.
3. **D3 loop.** Checker must not hold the interior; bring a characterised residual. Verdict: `specified`, triangulated across Fig 10 and Fig 2.
4. **D4 loop.** Enumerate the perceiver habitat: ambiguous stimuli, asymmetric false-positive cost, neuroimaging double dissociation. Anchor: signal-detection data. Verdict: `specified` world-side, single evidence pool, corroborates the necessity of the "without assuming" move but does not decide the framework verdict.
5. **Second order.** Only after first-order slots settle, a comparison ⋛ across them can be composed (Fig 12 :104). No figure line licenses treating that comparison as a unified "agency" faculty; it is a structural relation, and a `specified` one.
6. **Round trip.** The condensation "agency is contact, not intention" drops the anchor test, the per-slot indexing and the source gap. R_T is nonzero; enumerated in `11_audit.md`.

**End of audit. The verdicts in this file are `specified` / `general` / `gap`, not `grounded`, and the whole file is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency, not a ground reading.**
