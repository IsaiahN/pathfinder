# Assessment with the assumption and assessment without it: isomers, order, analogues

**Mode: `specified` on the isomer and order claims, `general` on the structural vocabulary, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## RESIDUAL

Given the separator in `01`, two further questions are owed. (1) Are "assessment with the human-like-state assumption" and "assessment without it" two different things over the same atoms, or do they use different atoms? (2) In what order must an assessment proceed, and what analogue classes are legitimate? A falsifier is written before the derivation, in section 5.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Does "shape, not a name" forbid calling goals, plans and progress by those names? Correction to my own earlier reading

No. The canonical README: *"what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* It governs a primitive not held in Γ. The comparison ⋛, the sequence → and the subtraction − are already held (Fig 12 :112, Operators :51/:46). Using them is a binding onto held structure. The correction is reported and preserved in `05_what_shape_means.md` section 5; I do not silently repeat the overstatement here. What *is* forbidden is promoting one of these bonds into an inner substance that does causal work, because that would add a primitive by assertion, which is the exact move Fig 6 :21 blocks: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*

### Q2 — Are the two assessments isomers over a fixed atom set?

Fig 12 :117:

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fig 12 :94:

> **"The arrangement is the substance."**

Fig 12 :46:

> **"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."**

Fix the atom set: `{system, behaviour, context, ground, objective}`. The two procedures differ in the bond they place between behaviour and verdict.

- **With-assumption assessment** uses identity ≡ between behaviour and an attributed mental state: the observed route is read as the expression of a human-like desire, so the behaviour and the supposed state are treated as one referent under two names. Operators :27: *"substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No."* That is exactly the bond that erases the distinction between a projection and a source.
- **Without-assumption assessment** uses a chain of cross-kind compositions: system → behaviour, behaviour-ranged-against-context, verdict settled by the ground, with the objective written as ⋛ and the route as → (Fig 12 :112). The source slot is not bonded at all; it is left as an open slot, which is a shape.

Same atoms, different bond, different substance: by Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers** of an assessment procedure. The difference is invisible if you record only the atoms ("both look at behaviour and give a verdict") and decisive in behaviour: the with-assumption procedure will confirm the assumption on any ambiguous stimulus, because its bond already contains the conclusion; the without-assumption procedure can return a refusal.

This is a `specified` structural claim. It is not a claim that one procedure is always more accurate; it is a claim that they are different objects with different failure modes, and that the identity bond in the first procedure makes the source question unfalsifiable from inside.

**Boundary condition, kept visible:** the isomer claim holds only while the atom set stays fixed. If the with-assumption procedure genuinely requires a mental-state atom that the without-assessment does not, then they are not isomers; the relation becomes composition plus an import, and the import owes a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :12-13 *"source gate: provenance"* and *"shadow test: explains a gap"*). Fig 8 :51: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures"*. That boundary is the live strain of this file, not a hidden caveat.

### Q3 — What order must the assessment follow?

Fig 3 :20:

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**

The five links applied to this question:

1. **Perception.** Can the assessor tell what is in front of it, and tell it apart from what was there before? For a machine, is there a per-slot observation o′ that changes under intervention? Without this, no residual.
2. **Vocabulary.** Are there terms for what the situation affords? This is the affordance link: Gibson's *"what it offers the animal... It implies the complementarity of the animal and the environment"* is the world-side statement. The question is whether the system has terms for what it can move, open, join or block, not whether the observer does.
3. **Objective.** Can the system say what would count as progress here, in its own terms? Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* This is the middle link that usually breaks. Fig 3 :25-26: *"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."*
4. **Planning.** A route to the objective under incomplete information. Only now does "intention-like" vocabulary become usable as a description of a route.
5. **Learn and carry.** Name what worked, verify it, carry it. A verdict at this link must be settled against the ground before it is composed over (Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"*).

Fig 3 :29: *"Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."* This is the operational answer to the user's question. We are not asked to decide whether machines have desires; we are asked which link an assessment stops at and whether the reading was measured. A verdict taken below a break is not weak. Fig 3 :23: *"reading. It is a reading of nothing."*

Order also includes refusal before mint (formula step 2). The one-place question "is it an agent" must be refused before we mint any composite verdict, because it is a shape held as a preference, not a term (Fig 5 :61-63). And the habitat must be enumerated before the verdict: Fig 11 :43-44: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* / *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."*

### Q4 — Which analogues are legitimate, and which are traps?

| analogue | field / source | shape it supports | limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intentional stance | philosophy of mind, Dennett (Wikipedia opened) | behaviour can be predicted under an intentional description without committing to real inner states; *"Whether to take a particular stance, then, is determined by how successful that stance is when applied."* | Stances are levels of abstraction over one behavioural record, i.e. one evidence pool. Not two independent frames; no triangulation weight. |
| Agent detection / Heider-Simmel | cognitive science, Gray and Wegner and the Heider-Simmel literature (Wikipedia opened) | the assumption of intention is a documented perceiver-side false-positive bias with asymmetric costs | Explains why Option B is attractive; does not decide whether any particular machine has internal states. |
| Affordance | ecological psychology, Gibson 1979 (Wikipedia opened) | unit of analysis is animal-and-environment; valence is relative to the residual | An affordance is not a figure bond; it is a world-side analogue of the agent-habitat pair. |
| Good regulator | cybernetics, Conant and Ashby 1970 (Wikipedia opened) | *"every good regulator of a system must be a model of that system"*; capacity belongs to regulator-and-system; the map is lossy | A theorem about regulation, not about phenomenal experience. |
| Disposition measurement | AI measurement science, Voudouris et al. 2026 (opened) | capabilities and propensities are counterfactual context-behaviour relations; *"This does not require the system to possess conscious intentions or beliefs"* | Methodologically separable from internal mechanism, not a denial of internal mechanism. |
| Goodhart / Strathern | economics and measurement, opened | *"When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure"* | Social measure collapse; a structural analogue of a convention deciding an outcome, not the same object. |
| Governed hybrid agency | AI, Sabaliauskas and Dirzyte 2026 (opened) | *"integrative, falsifiable comparison targets rather than a validated universal architecture"*; evaluation uneven on calibration, retention, transfer, long-horizon robustness | A review of architectures, not a figure-governed derivation. Corroborates the need for a stated refuter. |

Trap to refuse: treating Marr's three levels or Dennett's stances as identical to Fig 12's level-relative irreducibility. They rhyme; they are not the same claim. Fig 8 :51 governs: adopted import and convergent derivation look the same in the contents; only the record separates them. I record the analogue as an import with provenance, not as a derivation from the figures.

### Q5 — Functional group or isomer at second order?

Fig 12 :123:

> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

Across the family of machine-assessment procedures, the shared substructure is `state → structure → objective` with an external ground. The qualifier is which system and which habitat occupy the slots. Different assessments that share the substructure but bind different systems are members of one functional group (Fig 12 :123), not isomers of one another, unless the atom set is fixed (Q2). Keeping those two readings separate matters: collapsing them is how a global "agency" score gets minted, and that is F6 of the audit.

## CONCLUSION

The with-assumption and without-assumption assessments are isomers over a fixed atom set: same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is decisive in behaviour because the identity bond makes the source question unfalsifiable from inside. The assessment order is the dependency chain of Fig 3, with perception, vocabulary and objective before planning and carrying, refusal before mint, and habitat enumeration before verdict. Seven analogues were examined; none was promoted into a figure line, and the cognitive-science cluster is one evidence pool. The two live boundaries are the fixed-atom condition on the isomer claim and the analogue-import provenance; both are named, not hidden.

## 5. Falsifier, written before the run

1. If the with-assumption procedure needs an atom that the without-assessment does not, the isomer claim fails; the relation is composition plus import and the import owes a source gate and shadow test.
2. If an assessment can return a correct per-slot verdict while a link below the Fig 3 break is missing, the dependency-order claim fails.
3. If a global aggregate predicts per-slot counterfactual behaviour better than the per-slot measures without being produced by the frame it scores, the per-slot order claim fails.
4. If a low-overlap frame predicts the residual shape while requiring an irreducible mental-state primitive with satisfied provenance and shadow test, the whole separator is withdrawn.

Any of these fires against the claim, not for it. They were written before the derivation above.
