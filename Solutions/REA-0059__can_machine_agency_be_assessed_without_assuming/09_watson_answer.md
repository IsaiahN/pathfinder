# WATSON answer: can machine agency be assessed without assuming human-like desires or intentions?

**Mode: `specified` on the separator and per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the literal internal-source question. Not `grounded`.**

**Status: SIMULATED DERIVATION, not a ground reading.** This WATSON run synthesises `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, `05_what_shape_means.md`, `06_isomers_and_order.md`, `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md`. It is checked for internal consistency against the figures. By its own rule (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself"*) it cannot settle the question it reports on. No machine was measured.

## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, whether an agency verdict can be obtained without the premise that the system has human-like desires or intentions. Option A: re-ground the one-place question as a two-place capability claim about an agent-and-habitat, anchor it outside the frame, index it per slot, read goals and plans as bonds, and leave the internal-source question open. Option B: hold that the mental-state ascription is the only legitimate instrument. What each leads to: A yields a checkable, per-system, per-context verdict with a written refuter and no commitment about phenomenology; B yields a global label whose agreement is the observer's own template, which the anchor test disqualifies. The sought-for shape was bound before the run as a separator between a settled capability verdict and an open source question.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and dependency order.

- **R (the residual).** The question as posed, and the felt necessity of assuming human-like states. Arity: the one-place predicate "has agency" conceals a two-place relation (system, habitat) plus a ground. Symmetry: the mental-state ascription is symmetric with the observer's own case and therefore carries the disqualifying correlation; the assessment relation is ordered from ground to frame. Scale: first order inside one agent-habitat pair, second order at the verification room and the population of systems. Fig 9 :55 calls it a union compound: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either.
- **Domain 1, capability of the machine in its habitat.** Actors: system library, action, observation, habitat conditions, actors and relations. Currency: per-slot residual and settlement against what pays. Anchor: a board or measurement that does not update on the system's own report (Fig 2 :29, :33). Status: a domain, with a nameable anchor.
- **Domain 2, the internal-source claim.** Actors: observer and observed. Currency: attributed beliefs and desires by analogy. Anchor: none nameable that does not move when pushed; human judgment is legitimate only when the question is constitutively about people (Fig 2 :36-38). Status: not a domain yet, a proposed source. Verdict on anchor use: disqualified. Verdict on the source itself: `gap`.
- **Domain 3, verification and outreach.** Actors: outside checkers. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement (Fig 2 :35, Fig 10 :24). In this simulated run the second seat shares the primary's evidence pool, so its independence is zero.
- **Domain 4, perceiver-side bias.** Actors: human perceivers and ambiguous stimuli. Currency: asymmetric false-positive cost, reaction time, neural double dissociation. Anchor: signal-detection data and experiments. Status: a domain with its own anchor, low overlap with the framework.
- **Partition or union.** The question is a union of four overlapping conditions; the *procedure* admits a partition between the capability verdict and the source claim, because they have different anchors. One lever clears the union's overlaps only partly. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- **Dependency order.** Refuse the one-place framing before minting (formula step 2); enumerate the habitat before the verdict (Fig 11 :43-44); settle perception, vocabulary and objective before planning and carrying (Fig 3 :20); describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).

Strain test applied: Fig 11 :36 seemed to dissolve agency, but it is scoped to the maintainer-less case. Re-census assigns :37-38 to a seat-bearing assessment practice. No strain survives. Fig 12 level-relativity versus Dennett and Marr's stances was re-censused as different objects, one analogy with provenance, not a contradiction.

## WORLD (secondary, each source read)

Sources are corroboration only. Overlap is declared; the figures decide.

- **AI measurement science.** Voudouris, Thalmann, Kipnis, Hernandez-Orallo and Schulz, "Measuring What AI Systems Might Do: Towards A Measurement Science in AI", *Minds and Machines* 36:50, 2026, opened 2026-11-23: *"This does not require the system to possess conscious intentions or beliefs."* Predicts that a disposition can be measured by counterfactual context-behaviour mapping without a mental-state premise; corroborates D1 and C2. Also *"we do not know whom they measure"*, corroborating per-slot indexing. Low overlap with the framework.
- **Machine learning theory.** Richens, Abel, Bellot and Everitt, "General agents contain world models", ICML 2025 / arXiv:2506.01622, opened 2026-11-23: *"any agent capable of generalizing to multi-step goal-directed tasks must have learned a predictive model of its environment"*. Predicts that capacity belongs to the agent-environment relation; corroborates Fig 11 :39. Low overlap.
- **Philosophy of mind.** Dennett, "Intentional stance", via the opened Wikipedia entry, 2026-11-23: *"it is irrelevant whether the agent actually holds these particular beliefs or not; the critical feature is that the observer ascribes them to the agent"* and *"Whether to take a particular stance, then, is determined by how successful that stance is when applied."* Predicts that stance selection is by predictive success, not inner commitment; corroborates D2's anchor disqualification. Shares a cognitive-science pool with the next source.
- **Cognitive science / evolutionary psychology.** Gray and Wegner, "Agent detection", via the opened Wikipedia entry, 2026-11-23: *"people possess a Hyperactive Agent Detection Device, a cognitive module that readily ascribes events in the environment to the behavior of agents"*. Predicts that the assumption of human-like agency is a cheap perceiver-side default; corroborates D4. Same pool as Dennett and the Heider-Simmel material, so one witness on that axis.
- **Ecological psychology.** Gibson 1979, "Affordance", via the opened Wikipedia entry, 2026-11-23: *"The affordances of the environment are what it offers the animal, what it provides or furnishes, either for good or ill. ... It implies the complementarity of the animal and the environment."* Predicts agent-environment complementarity; corroborates the shape of D1. Low overlap.
- **Cybernetics.** Conant and Ashby 1970, "Good regulator theorem", via the opened Wikipedia entry, 2026-11-23: *"every good regulator of a system must be a model of that system"*; the mapping is only homomorphic. Predicts that the regulator's capacity is relational and its model lossy; corroborates Fig 11 :39 and the round-trip gap. Low overlap.
- **Economics / measurement.** Goodhart 1975 / Strathern 1997, "Goodhart's law", via the opened Wikipedia entry, 2026-11-23: *"When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure."* Predicts that a metric tied to the goal of demonstrating agency stops measuring it; corroborates Fig 10 :47 and F3. Low overlap.
- **AI systems review.** Sabaliauskas and Dirzyte, "Governed Hybrid Agency", *JAIR* 87, 2026, opened 2026-11-23: *"integrative, falsifiable comparison targets rather than a validated universal architecture"*. ## VERDICT (from the figures)

The question is a union compound; verdicts remain per domain. Capability is assessable without the human-like-state premise, as a per-slot claim about an agent-and-habitat anchored outside the frame, with goals, plans and progress written as bonds. Human-like desire or intention is disqualified as an *anchor* for that verdict by the anchor test, because the question is not constitutively about people. The internal-source question itself is a `gap`, unreached at this budget: no figure line governs it and no available instrument discriminates among candidate sources, so the honest report is unreachable rather than denial. With-assumption and without-assumption assessments are isomers over a fixed atom set, differing in the bond between behaviour and verdict; the identity bond makes the first procedure's conclusion unfalsifiable from inside. The answer to the literal question is yes, with a re-grounding and one declared gap. Mode `specified` on these claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the source; not `grounded`.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode, separator and refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable. The real cost is spending outside checkers' independence by sending a recording or a conclusion instead of a residual. Fig 2 :42 prices it: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* That cost is avoidable by leading with the separator and the failure conditions.
- **undoable:** yes. The separator is a method, not a primitive. The source question is left open rather than foreclosed, so a later discriminator can close it without withdrawing the capability verdict. Refusals are recorded rather than deleted, so the fall in reach is computable (Fig 6 :42 *"the closure and the reach are two quantities: one monotone, one not"*).
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A source-discriminating instrument that maps internal states to distinct behaviours would close the gap; an agent-only improvement that moved the verdict would refute the capability claim (Fig 11 :72); an anchor shown to update on the frame's belief would demote the measurement to a frame; a global aggregate outperforming per-slot measures would break the indexing claim; a figure re-census that names agency or intention as a primitive would break the census. Prestige and agreement are not such instruments.
