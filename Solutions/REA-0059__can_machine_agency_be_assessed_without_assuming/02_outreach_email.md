# Outreach draft: check the separator before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Precondition:** send the residual and the refuter first. The framework, if it comes at all, comes only after the recipient has recorded a verdict on stage 1. No request for trust, no request for agreement.

---

**Subject:** Can an agency verdict be reached without a mental-state assumption? One output to check

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one narrow claim in machine agency assessment I would like you to check against work you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "can machine agency be assessed without assuming human-like desires or intentions" bundles four different things. The one-place question "is this thing an agent" is the wrong object. What can be assessed is a two-place relation: what this system can do in this habitat, against a board or measurement that does not change its answer because of what any observer thinks. Goals, plans and progress are then typed relations in the description (a comparison, a sequence, a subtraction), not mental substances. The internal-source question, which state if any produced the behaviour, is left open with an instrument owed. The claim is that the capability verdict can be settled while the source question stays open, and that trying to settle both at once makes the verdict depend on the observer's own case.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that machines have no internal states; that behaviour alone settles everything; that a benchmark score is a measurement of a disposition; or that the framework can name which internal source produced a behaviour. The figures return a shape, not a name. The internal-source question is a declared open item, not a hidden answer.
>
> **What would refute it, written before this message:**
> 1. a measurement in which an agent-only improvement (no habitat or contact change) moves the agency verdict;
> 2. a disposition-respecting meter showing that counterfactual behavioural mapping alone cannot recover the disposition without a mental-state commitment;
> 3. a proposed anchor that demonstrably updates on the measuring frame's belief about the machine's intentions;
> 4. a global agency aggregate that predicts per-slot counterfactual behaviour better than the per-slot measures, without being produced by the frame it scores.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Voudouris, Thalmann, Kipnis, Hernandez-Orallo and Schulz, "Measuring What AI Systems Might Do: Towards A Measurement Science in AI", *Minds and Machines* 36:50 (2026, open access): https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11023-026-09805-9
> - Richens, Abel, Bellot and Everitt, "General agents contain world models", ICML 2025 / arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01622
> - Dennett's intentional stance, summary and primary quotation: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intentional_stance
> - Canonical figure text with line numbers (local copy in the package): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on an assessment problem you already know, write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Stage order

1. **Stage 1:** send the separator, the four refuters, and nothing else. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict before any theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if curious, send the three source links and the local figure extract. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method, with the instruction that each user names a ground and writes a refuter first.

No stage asks anyone to trust the framework. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
