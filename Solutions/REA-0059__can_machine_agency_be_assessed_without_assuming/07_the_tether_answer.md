# The Tether's answer: can machine agency be assessed without assuming human-like desires or intentions?

**Mode: `specified` on the separator and on each per-domain verdict, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the literal internal-source question. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. No machine was measured.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question bundles four conditions, so it is a **union compound**, not one hard question with two opposite answers. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* The four are: (D1) capability of a system in a habitat; (D2) a hypothesis about an internal source, human-like desire or intention; (D3) the instrument by which an outside checker assesses the claim; (D4) the perceiver-side bias that makes the assumption feel compulsory. One lever, "just look at behaviour", clears D1 and D3 and leaves D2 and D4 standing. Verdicts below are kept separate and are never averaged into a single "mostly yes".

## 2. The verdict per domain

**D1, capability (triangulated).** Fig 11 :39: *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* The one-place question "is it an agent" is the wrong object; the two-place contact question is answerable. An agency assessment is a per-slot reading of what the system does under varied context, against a ground that does not change its answer because of what any observer thinks (Fig 2 :29, :33). Goals, plans and progress are bonds in that description, not mental substances: Fig 12 :112 *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."* Verdict: `specified`, capability assessable without the mental-state premise.

**D2, internal source (gap).** No figure line supplies a desire or intention primitive, and no available instrument discriminates among candidate sources. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*; Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* An agency reading cannot comment on its source, any more than a green reading can comment on whether the source was one lamp or an RGB mix. Verdict: `gap`, unreached rather than silenced. Human-like states are disqualified as an *anchor* for D1 (Fig 2 :36-38), but their existence or absence is not decided.

**D3, verification (triangulated).** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* The first contact carries the residual and the failure conditions; the theory comes only after the outside verdict is recorded. Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*. Verdict: `specified`.

**D4, perceiver bias (world-side `specified`, one evidence pool).** Agent detection and the intentional-stance literature show that ascribing intentions to ambiguous or non-human targets is cheap, fast and false-positive prone. This explains why the question is asked; it does not decide the framework verdict. Dennett, agent detection and Heider-Simmel share a pool and count as one witness.

**Second order.** Only after first-order slots settle can a comparison across them be composed (Fig 12 :104). That comparison is a structural relation, not a unified "agency faculty". No figure line licenses the latter.

**Answer to the literal question: yes, with a re-grounding and one declared gap.** Yes, a checkable agency verdict is available without the mental-state assumption, provided it names habitat, ground and slot and keeps goals as bonds. No, that verdict does not settle whether machines really have internal states, and the figures forbid turning the open shape into a name.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that machines lack internal states, consciousness or intentions. That is the `gap`, left open with an instrument owed.
- Not that behaviour alone settles everything. A behaviour reading is a projection with lossy information; the source is not recovered from it.
- Not that a benchmark score is a measurement of a disposition. Voudouris et al. (2026) make the same point from measurement science, and Fig 1 :27 disqualifies a quantity the frame both produces and uses to score itself.
- Not a global "agency score". Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
- Not that the intentional stance, affordance or good regulator results are the same claim as a figure line. They are analogues with provenance, not derivations from the figures.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is settled by a measurement.

## 4. What would settle it, and the standing refuter

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened): *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"*; it was a spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved the colour case. Here the corresponding tests are:

1. Does a disposition-respecting meter, built to the Voudouris et al. requirements, recover the agency disposition from counterfactual context-behaviour mapping alone, or does it need a mental-state commitment?
2. Does an agent-only improvement, with no change of habitat or contact, move the agency verdict? Fig 11 :72 already names this: *"an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim."*
3. Does a proposed anchor update on the measuring frame's belief about the machine's intentions? If so it is a frame, not an anchor (Fig 2 :33-34).
4. Does a low-overlap frame whose closure predicts the residual shape exist, with source gate and shadow test satisfied, that requires a mental-state primitive? Fig 8 :28: *"the atom crosses back"* after *"describe R, then find the frame"*.
5. Does a global aggregate predict per-slot counterfactual behaviour better than the per-slot measures without being produced by the frame it scores?

These five, plus the primitive break (a figure line naming agency or intention as a seventh term), were written before the conclusion in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 3. If any fires, the separator is withdrawn or narrowed and the retraction is recorded there.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable in principle.** Fig 6 :27: *"Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."* The source question may be hard rather than closed; this run cannot tell. Fig 4 :30 requires the round trip to be measured: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* The condensation used here, "agency is contact, not intention", drops the anchor test, per-slot indexing and source gap. That dropped set is R_T, enumerated in `11_audit.md`. The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

Also note the mode of the *answer* itself: `specified`, candidate. Agreement with the figures, including the simulated second seat in `11_audit.md`, is internal consistency, not corroboration (Fig 2 :40).

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not decide whether machines have human-like desires or intentions, and it does not say they do not. It says the question as asked bundles four things, and that the one-place form "is it an agent" is the wrong object. What can be assessed is a per-slot capability of an agent-and-habitat, ranged against a ground that does not change its answer because of what any observer thinks; goals, plans and progress are then typed relations in the description, a comparison, a sequence and a subtraction, not mental substances. That verdict is reachable without the mental-state premise. What is not reachable at this budget is the internal-source question: an agency reading, like a green reading, cannot comment on which source produced it, and naming one would be naming a primitive the framework does not hold. The package offers the separator, the anchor test and five failure conditions written in advance. The source slot stays open until an instrument closes it.
