# Expert verification packet: the Tether on machine agency assessment

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in machine agency assessment can check. Mode, separator and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the separator and per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the literal internal-source question. Not `grounded`.** This packet is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading of any machine.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "can machine agency be assessed without assuming human-like desires or intentions" bundles four conditions: capability of a system in a habitat, an internal-source hypothesis, the instrument of assessment, and the observer's bias toward agency. The one-place question "is it an agent" is the wrong object. What can be assessed is a two-place capability of an agent-and-habitat, indexed per slot, ranged against an anchor that does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. Goals, plans and progress are typed bonds in that description (comparison, sequence, subtraction), not mental substances. The internal-source question stays a declared `gap`, because no figure line governs it and no available instrument discriminates among candidate sources. So the answer is yes on assessability, with the source question left open, and no on any attempt to settle the source question by analogy.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal and measurement-facing; checkable by reading Figure 11 and Figure 2

- **Statement:** the one-place agency question is the wrong object; capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, and the anchor for its assessment must not update on what any frame thinks.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39 *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :36 *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."*
- **What to check:** is the assessment unit in your own work the model alone, or the model-in-environment? If a scored result changes when the evaluator's prior about the system's intentions changes, the anchor has failed.
- **Refuter:** a measurement in which an agent-only improvement, with no habitat or contact change, moves the agency verdict (Fig 11 :72 *"an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim"*), or a proposed anchor shown to update on the frame's belief.

### C2 — language-level; checkable against the bond inventory

- **Statement:** describing a system as pursuing a goal, executing a plan or making progress uses bonds already in the library, not mental primitives.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :112 *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."* Operators :51 *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*. Fig 12 :111 *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."*
- **What to check:** can your assessment language be rewritten as counterfactual relations among context properties, system properties and behavioural outputs, with an independently operationalised task-demand variable and incentive variable? Voudouris et al. (2026) give the test in your own idiom: *"This does not require the system to possess conscious intentions or beliefs."*
- **Refuter:** a demonstrated composition in which comparison, sequence or subtraction cannot be expressed as a typed bond and must be expressed as an irreducible mental primitive with its own causal powers.

### C3 — source-discrimination; checkable by any instrument designer

- **Statement:** the internal-source question is a `gap`, not a settled no and not a settled yes. An agency reading is a projection with lossy information about its source.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*; Fig 13 :134 *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*; Fig 8 :39 *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
- **What to check:** name an instrument that would map distinct internal sources to distinct behaviours under controlled intervention, or say why none is possible. The framework essay *The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the analogue: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"*, resolved in the colour case by the spectrometer, not by argument.
- **Refuter:** such an instrument, demonstrated, closes the gap and forces a rewrite of this sub-claim. Until then the honest report is `unreachable`.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that machines lack internal states, intentions or consciousness. C3 is open.
- Not that behaviour alone settles everything; the source question is not recovered from a reading.
- Not that a benchmark score measures a disposition. Fig 1 :27 *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*
- Not a global agency score. Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
- Not that Dennett's stances, Gibson's affordances or the good regulator theorem are figure lines. They are analogues with provenance (section 4 and `06_isomers_and_order.md`), one evidence pool where they overlap.
- Not `grounded`. No machine was measured in producing this packet.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A disposition-respecting meter showing whether counterfactual context-behaviour mapping alone recovers the disposition without a mental-state commitment.
2. An agent-only improvement that moves the agency verdict, or a demonstration that contact changes are irrelevant to it.
3. A discriminator that maps distinct internal sources to distinct behaviours under intervention.
4. A low-overlap frame with a satisfied source gate and shadow test that requires an irreducible mental-state primitive.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :39 and Fig 2 :29/:36 (three short passages). Decide whether your field's unit of assessment is the model alone or the model-in-environment.
2. Check C2 against your own assessment vocabulary: can goals, plans and progress be written as counterfactual context-behaviour relations without adding a mental primitive?
3. Read the Voudouris et al. abstract and the sentence *"This does not require the system to possess conscious intentions or beliefs."* Decide whether it agrees or conflicts with C1 and C2.
4. Name one instrument that would discriminate internal sources, or say why none is possible. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
