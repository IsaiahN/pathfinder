## 0. The claim under audit, in effect terms

> "How do online platforms change competition when the most valuable resource is control over customer access and data?"

The premise smuggles in a picture: that there is a *resource* called control, held in a *stock*, whose amount decides the competitive outcome. The figures do not license that picture. They ask what the residual is indexed by, what the anchor is, and what composes over what. Restated:

- **R (the residual).** Competition's shape under a shift in what is scarce. Arity: at least three overlapping conditions (access, data, network effects). Symmetry: "control over access" is an ordered, threshold relation (who may reach whom), not a symmetric conjunction. Scale: per market slot (Fig 1 :19), and across a scale membrane from one firm to an ecosystem (Fig 4).
- **What an answer must decide.** Which frames can range against a ground that does not update on their own reports; what a reading (data) can and cannot discriminate; whether the change is a new atom or a new arrangement over held atoms; and per condition, not on average, what changes.
- **Direction bound in advance (formula step "Direction").** An answer must name the channel holder, an independent reading, the live condition per slot, and a reversal. It must not return a universal remedy, a market-share threshold, or a standing list of always-good actors. Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

Two readings are compared throughout. **Reading A**, the one the question's ordinary wording invites: control is a stock, more is better, the winner is the largest holder, and a single lever (portability, interoperability, breakup) fixes it. **Reading B**, the one derived below: the question is a union of conditions with different anchors, control changes who may range against the ground independently, data is a reading with a source problem, and no figure line returns a named remedy. The verdict is B. Reading A is not called false; it is called a flattening that loses the bond information (Fig 12 :5).

## 3. Findings

### F1: The question is a union, not one dial (decisive, triangulated)

Fig 9 :4-5 (quoted above) and Fig 9 :54 (quoted above). Fig 9 :55-57: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds." And: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."

The three conditions in this question have different anchors:

| condition | anchor | what lever clears it | what the lever leaves standing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Access control (who may reach customers) | realized customer contact, a non-updating market ground | multi-homing, access rules, non-discrimination | the data condition, where switching does not give you the reading |
| Data control (who holds the observation) | a source-discriminating instrument, not yet present | portability, sharing, source-gated import | the access condition, where a reading without customers is inert |
| Network effects (who gets more valuable with size) | the sign of the loop at the slot (Fig 12 :121) | changing the loop's sign or opening the closure | the two conditions above, where the loop amplifies both |

One lever does not clear all three. The world sources agree on this shape without deciding it. Rochet and Tirole 2006 (RAND Journal of Economics): "We identify two-sided markets with markets in which the structure, and not only the level of prices charged by platforms, matters." Structure, not level, is the bond word. Simone and Laudando 2025 (Electronic Markets): "the economic power of gatekeepers comes from multiple sources involving different levels of competition (i.e. within and between platforms)." They add that the regulator treats "all rents extracted by gatekeepers as homogeneous," which is exactly the flattening Fig 9 :55 names.

**Refuter:** a re-census showing one anchor for access, data and network effects, so that a single lever clears all three. Until that is run, per-condition verdicts are kept separate and never averaged.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 9 :4-5 and Fig 9 :54 (low overlap between the "several well-formed questions" rule and the union/partition rule), with world corroboration from two independent fields (industrial organization economics and platform regulation).

### F2: The decisive change is who holds the channel to the ground, not who holds more data (triangulated)

Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Fig 2 :29 (above) defines the anchor. Fig 2 :35 (above) forbids treating agreement as correction. Fig 7 :35 (above) names the same failure at the room scale.

When many sellers each range against paying customers independently, competition is triangulation: many frames, one non-updating ground (Fig 2 :31 "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence"). When one actor stands between them and the customer and also supplies the ranking through which customers choose, the other frames range against one frame's reading of the ground. Their apparent agreement is a single pool talking (Fig 2 :40). The structural change is in the channel, not in the amount of data held.

Martens 2021 (JRC, European Commission) supplies the world-side description: "That gives them a privileged market overview that none of the individual users has." And: "The dominant platform becomes a gatekeeper to the market. It sets market conditions." Note what this is: a *reading* advantage that becomes a *channel* advantage. Fig 10 :24 governs the second, which is why it is decisive; the first alone is frame-internal (F3).

Vital and Martins 2009 (Ethology), a genuinely low-overlap animal-behaviour witness, describe the same structure: "Single individuals, termed 'gatekeepers', can have a profound impact on information flow in a group, whether that gatekeeper be a large male despot which strictly controls access to resources and mates, or an infant that is freely passed around among members of a social group." Access control and information-flow control are named as one role, matching the question's own pairing. Lee, Ounsley, Coulson, Rowcliffe and Cowlishaw 2016 (Proceedings of the Royal Society B) add the condition: "resource ecology, defined by scarcity, depletion rate and monopolizability, determines patterns of individual differences in social information use." It is *monopolisability* that decides whether information control changes competition, not the mere existence of an information holder.

**Refuter:** a measurement showing competing frames ranging independently against the same non-updating ground while one actor owns the ranking and the access rule. That would break the channel-capture claim.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 10 :24 and Fig 2 :29/:35/:40 (different quantities: channel decay versus anchor definition), with low-overlap world corroboration from animal behaviour and economics.

### F3: Data volume is a frame-internal quantity; it cannot score the platform (single-source on the figure side, world-confirmed)

Fig 1 :24-27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." Listed there are coverage and compression achieved, predicates minted and credibility accrued. A platform's count of user records, clicks and interactions is the same genus: a quantity the frame produces and then scores itself with. Fig 9 :60 makes the correct use: it is a filter for the budget, not a witness for the verdict.

Fig 5 :70: "A term is settled when the ground pays for it. A term is usable when something can compose over it." Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom." So "more data" is more composition inside a closure. It may improve matching within the closure (which is real, F5), but it does not add the missing atom of independent customer access for an entrant. Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

The world sources sharpen rather than overturn this. Lam and Liu 2020 (International Journal of Industrial Organization): "data portability may hinder switching and entry due to the demand-expansion effect: the prospect of easier switching due to data portability may entice consumers to provide even more data to the incumbent, which strengthens the incumbency advantage." A lever aimed at the stock (make it portable) can strengthen the very stock it targets, because it changes ex ante provision. This is a world/figure *conflict with reading A only*, and the conflict rule sends it back to the census: it corroborates the union verdict (two conditions with opposite signs), it does not flip the figure verdict. Martens 2023 (Bruegel Working Paper 21/2023) records the same two-sign structure: "Regulators should be careful however with sharing of pooled data to avoid weakening network effects, because doing so may be welfare-reducing for users."

**Refuter:** show a figure line that licenses a frame to score itself with a quantity it also produces, or a ground measurement in which data volume alone, with the channel held fixed, independently changes the entry verdict.

**Status:** single-source on the figure side (Fig 1 :27 is the governing line). World sources agree about the two-sign effect but they are one broad economics pool, not independent witnesses. Recorded honestly as such.

### F4: Data is a reading, not a source; the discriminator is missing (gap, not silence)

The instrument reads what was clicked, shown, bought. It cannot say why. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (Isaiah N, opened 2026-11-22): "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information (it cannot define consciousness in full)." The essay's colour case: "the instrument is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source." And the resolution: "It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."

Mapped to this question: a platform's data is a projection of customer behaviour. Several sources (genuine preference, default position, ranking-induced choice, scarcity of alternatives) can produce the same click. Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause." Symbols :38-40 (above): the residual is a slice, and what cannot yet be perceived is simply unread.

So the specific question "which data field discriminates which competitive harm" is a **gap**, not an omission. Fig 13 :134 (above) "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence." Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." The remedy is an instrument, and instruments are improved from worse ones already returning something (Fig 6 :32 "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."). It is unreachable at this budget, not underivable in principle, because source-discriminating instruments already exist in adjacent fields (the spectrometer in the essay; experiments and audit methods in economics). Importing one requires a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 8 :52).

**Refuter:** a source-discriminating instrument applied to platform data that can state which source produced a given reading. That fires the gap and closes it. It has not been run here.

**Status:** `gap`, declared before the conclusion. World corroboration from human cognition / measurement (the essay), which shares the framework author's evidence pool and therefore carries no independent witness weight.

### F5: The efficiency surplus is real, and it is a different verdict from the gatekeeping verdict (triangulated)

Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." Fig 8 :46: "Composition crosses the boundary between them, and that surplus is where imports live." A platform that reduces the information cost of matching creates a genuine surplus. Martens 2021: "Digital data technology has dramatically reduced information cost and thereby expanded user choices." Rochet and Tirole 2003 (Journal of the European Economic Association): platforms "must 'get both sides of the market on board,'" and the model "compares the outcomes with those under an integrated monopolist and a Ramsey planner." Armstrong 2006 (RAND Journal of Economics): "To be able to compete effectively on one side of the market, a platform needs to perform well on the other side (and vice versa). This creates a downward pressure on the prices offered to both sides compared to the case where no externalities exist."

These are not denials of the efficiency case. They are a **separate domain verdict**. Fig 11 :49 (above): an actor's valence is relative to the residual. The same platform is a benefactor to the "unmet matching need" residual and a malfactor to the "entrant cannot reach an independent reading" residual. Fig 11 :50: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." Averaging the two into "platforms are net good/bad for competition" destroys the only signal (Fig 1 :22).

Fig 12 :121 (above) supplies the mechanism for the tipping side: network effects are a cycle, and the sign of the loop decides whether it closes. Martens 2021: "Market tipping occurs because users prefer to congregate on the largest platform. This strengthens the position of the incumbent platform at the expense of potential new entrants." The cycle is the same structure as the efficiency flywheel; which sign dominates is a slot measurement, not a general law.

**Refuter:** a measurement in which the matching surplus at a slot falls to zero while the access advantage stays fixed (would dissolve the two-domain split for that slot), or a figure re-census that makes the efficiency and gatekeeping effects one anchor.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 8 :45 (surplus from differing closures) and Fig 11 :49-50 (valence relative to residual), with world corroboration from industrial organization. The three economics papers are one evidence pool, not three witnesses.

### F6: Data is not one structure: a live type strain, named not hidden (strained)

Fig 13 :20 "ordering, so more and less can be said" (the spectrum entry). Fig 13 :22 "a difference that can be spent" (the gradient entry). A ranked list of customers is ordering-like (spectrum); a directed nudge generated from it is directedness (gradient). Which one "data" is depends on the residual. Fig 11 :49 gives the rule: valence and role are relative to the residual. So I do **not** name data as the spectrum atom or the gradient atom. I record it as an observation o′ (Symbols :31-32 "What actually happened. Read o-prime") that can be bonded either way, and the type question stays open.

This is a genuine strain: forcing either figure line alone would govern the wrong quantity. It is named in `06_isomers_and_order.md`, `09_watson_answer.md` and `11_audit.md`. Fig 13 :143 "Indivisibility and minimality are claims and both are refutable" is the standing permission to keep it open rather than conceal it.

**Refuter:** a measurement that shows a single data artifact behaving as pure ordering (no spendable directedness) or pure directedness (no ordering content) at the same slot. Until then, both readings are candidates.

**Status:** live strain, single-source on each side, not upgraded to triangulated.

### F7: Entry is an import with a source gate, not a property of the entrant (triangulated)

Fig 6 :23 (above): the wall moves only on contact with a differing closure. Fig 6 :24 "Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives." Fig 8 :52 (above): provenance separates convergence from adoption. Fig 11 :40 (above): capability is agent-and-habitat, never agent alone.

An entrant's problem is therefore not only "we have less data." It is: what atom are we missing, from which closure can it be imported, does the shadow test explain a real gap, and what independence is debited by taking it? Multi-homing is a maintained channel, not an intrinsic property. Fig 11 :66 (above): "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Moving a business onto a platform reproduces some required contact and inherits actors it did not choose, with two silent failures: Fig 11 :68 "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails" and Fig 11 :69 "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts."

World side: Armstrong 2006 models "competitive bottlenecks" where one group joins all platforms; the single-homing or multi-homing choice changes the price structure. Lam and Liu 2020 show that the *type* of data that is portable decides whether entry helps. Simone and Laudando 2025 distinguish four sources of gatekeeper power at two levels of competition; a single lever aimed at one source leaves the others. These corroborate the import-and-habitat shape without supplying the missing atom.

**Refuter:** show an entrant whose reach grows with data volume alone while its channel to customers, its imported atom and its habitat all stay fixed. That would break the import claim.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 6 :23 and Fig 11 :40/:66 (closure wall versus habitat substitution, low overlap).

### F8: Business models are isomers over a fixed atom set; the "resource" framing hides the bond (single-source, with a falsifier)

Fig 12 :117 (above): same atoms, different bond, different substance. Fig 12 :5 (above): the ingredients are not the thing. Ad-funded, subscription and transactional platform models can be written over the same atoms (customers, sellers, attention, data, ranking, access rule) with different bonds among them. Calling "control over access and data" the *most valuable resource* converts a bond difference into an ingredient difference and loses the arrangement.

Fig 12 :119 "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is." Data that compounds through use (a catalyst) and access granted for one transaction (a spent part) are different terms even when the ingredient list is identical. Fig 12 :125 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." The description cost of the ranking and the action cost of testing whether a harm occurred are different units; summing them into one "value of data" figure is the named error.

Falsifier, written before the run: if two platform models written over the same atom set can have one moved to the other by a change of quantity only (no bond change), the isomer claim fails. If moving one atom changes the substance without changing any bond, the fixed-atom condition fails. Both are recorded in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

**Status:** single-source (Fig 12 definitions), unmeasured at the ground. The economics sources corroborate that structure and price structure matter, not the isomer claim specifically.

### F9: The premise "most valuable resource" is relative to the residual, not a standing list (correction, kept visible)

Fig 11 :51 (above) refuses a standing list of good actors. The same refusal applies to a standing list of *valuable resources*. The question's premise is accepted as a report of the current residual for a class of firms, not as a law. Fig 10 :72 "When the question changes, the room should change. A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom." If the residual moves from matching efficiency to entrant discovery to consumer autonomy, the identity of the "most valuable resource" moves with it.

This is a correction to my own first pass, which treated "control over customer access and data" as two of the six terms (network and spectrum). That reading was retracted at re-census: they are bindings over held atoms, not primitives. The retraction is preserved in section 6 and in `11_audit.md`; it is not silently erased.

**Refuter:** a derivation of a resource whose value is invariant across a change of residual. None of the six terms has that property (Fig 13 :144 "Indivisibility and minimality are claims and both are refutable"), so such a derivation would also break the substrate claim.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required by the seat map :287: "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A re-census shows one anchor for access, data and network effects, so the union collapses to a partition and one lever clears all three (breaks F1).
2. A measurement shows competing frames ranging independently against the same non-updating market ground while one actor owns the ranking and the access rule (breaks F2).
3. A source-discriminating instrument applied to platform data can state which source produced a given reading (closes the F4 gap against the "missing instrument" claim).
4. A figure line is found that returns a specific remedy, concentration threshold or effect magnitude (closes the D3 gap in the direction reading A wants).
5. A held term such as "network effects" is shown to add an atom rather than recompose a cycle (breaks F5 and F7).
6. Two platform models over the same atom set are shown to be transformable by quantity alone, with no bond change (breaks F8).

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the verdict is re-censused. Until one is run, nothing in this folder may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Stop asking for one answer. Unbundle the three conditions and record a verdict per slot (F1).
2. Move the decisive variable from data volume to channel ownership (F2, F3). A platform's own data count is a frame-internal score, not a market verdict.
3. Treat the missing source-discriminating instrument as the honest stop (F4). Report `unreachable` at this budget, not silence and not an invented dataset.
4. Keep the efficiency surplus and the gatekeeping harm as separate domain verdicts (F5). Do not average them into a net welfare number the figures do not license.
