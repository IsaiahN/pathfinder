## Three things in the wider literature that bear directly on this question

1. **Structure and price structure matter, not just volume.** Rochet and Tirole 2006 (RAND Journal of Economics) define two-sided markets as those in which "the structure, and not only the level of prices charged by platforms, matters." Armstrong 2006 adds the competitive bottleneck and the coupling between sides. Both corroborate the figure claim that the arrangement, not the ingredient list, is the object (Fig 12 :5). They are one economics evidence pool, not two independent witnesses.
2. **A lever can reverse sign.** Lam and Liu 2020 show data portability can raise entry barriers when the demand-expansion effect dominates; Martens 2023 warns that pooled-data sharing can weaken the network effect that created the value. These are world-side corroboration of the union verdict (Fig 9 :54), not a ground verdict about any specific market.
3. **The low-overlap witness is animal behaviour.** Vital and Martins 2009 name "gatekeepers" as controllers of information flow and resource access; Lee et al. 2016 make monopolisability the condition under which information control matters. These are genuinely independent of the platform economics pool and corroborate the mechanism shape (channel plus monopolisability), not the market fact. Fig 8 :50: "But shared atoms are not the signature. Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22** "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the union split and the refuter; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who has absorbed the whole framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what they would expect to see if the channel-capture claim were true before giving them the article.
- **Fig 2 :40** "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." A send selected for people already inclined to agree produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 1 :19** "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Ask about a market they know well, not about "platforms" in general.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its four refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and record it; (3) only then the three links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (4) only if asked, the formula as a method with the instruction to write a fresh refuter per problem.

## Two-stage offer, stated plainly

Stage 1 is the residual and the refuter, with no theory attached. Stage 2 is opt-in reading of the source links. Stage 3 is opt-in reuse of the formula. Stage 1 must be recorded before stage 2 is shown, because once a recipient holds the framework's interior their agreement stops being evidence (Fig 10 :42). Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration. An approval is worth less than a precise failure.

## Submission cautions to carry into `08`

The Electronic Markets guidelines state that large language models do not satisfy authorship criteria and that their use must be documented in Methods. Since this entire folder is a simulated derivation, a submission must either be reframed as a human-authored analysis using the figures as a lens, with the simulation disclosed, or not be submitted as empirical work. Do not present these files as a ground result. The same caution applies to SSRN working papers: opt in, disclose, and ask for refutation rather than endorsement.
