**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` have been checked against the figure text by you. If the shape claim does not survive, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about platform competition, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "how do online platforms change competition when the most valuable resource is control over customer access and data" does not have one answer. It is a union of three overlapping conditions with different anchors: who may reach the customer (access), who holds the observation (data), and the feedback loop by which size becomes more valuable (network effects). One lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. So a single number, a single remedy, or a single "platforms help/hurt competition" verdict is the wrong object.
>
> The structural claim underneath it is this. The ground is realized customer demand, which does not change because a ranking says so. What a platform mainly changes is the **channel** to that ground: when one actor stands between every would-be competitor and the customer and also supplies the ranking through which the customer chooses, the other frames are no longer ranging independently against the market. They are reading one frame's reading. Their agreement is one evidence pool talking, not corroboration. Data matters here because it is the observation that lets a frame range at all, and controlling it controls what can be resolved, not merely how much can be stored.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that a big platform's data stock is by itself a moat; that more data always means more competitive advantage; that portability, interoperability or breakup is the answer; that platforms are net good or net bad for competition. A platform can be a benefactor to the "unmet matching need" residual and a malfactor to the "entrant cannot reach an independent reading" residual at the same time, and those verdicts are kept separate, never averaged. I am also not claiming the framework names the remedy. It returns a shape. The specific instrument that would discriminate among sources of a customer click is owed, and I do not have it.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a re-census showing one anchor for access, data and network effects, so one lever clears all three;
> 2. a measurement showing competing firms ranging independently against the same non-updating market ground while one actor owns the ranking;
> 3. a source-discriminating instrument applied to platform data that can say which cause produced a given reading;
> 4. a figure line that returns a specific remedy, concentration threshold or effect size.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Figures and formula for direct download (SVG, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Full architecture (19 min): https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
> - Worked narrative and the six-term derivation: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-intelligence-actually-works
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a platform market you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## The two-stage (in practice three-stage) offer

1. **Stage 1:** the union split, the channel claim, and the four refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict *before* showing any framework material.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, the three links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3, opt-in only:** the formula as a method, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode. Fig 4 :22 permits a generator to cross; an instance or recording does not.
