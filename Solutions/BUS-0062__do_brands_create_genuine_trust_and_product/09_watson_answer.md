# WATSON answer, BUS-0062

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** Figures are primary and decide. World sources are secondary, corroborate but never overrule, and a conflict returns the derivation to the domain census. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## QUESTION

Do brands create genuine trust and product value, or mainly influence perceptions and willingness to pay? Restated as an effect: does the brand lower R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| by changing contact with the experienced outcome, or only shift the belief b and the premium while R persists? R is indexed per consumer-product slot, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19). Anchor: the market and the experienced outcome (Fig 2 :29), with the person's own judgment as anchor for the person-level trust sub-question (Fig 2 :37).

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Arity: a three-place relation (consumer library, choice, market return), indexed by slot and ordered in time; carries an ordered bond and a comparison bond, not symmetric ones. Symmetry: "genuine" is a threshold against what the ground pays, not a symmetric merge of perceptions (Fig 12 :87). Scale: first order at a purchase, second order at accumulated trust.

| D2 Market ground | firms, products, prices, repeat buyers | payment, share, premium | the market | admitted |

Union, not partition: C1 (contact changed) and C2 (percept shifted) overlap, so a single lever leaves a categorically different residue (Fig 9 :55, :57). Dependency order: describe the residual, discriminate percept from ground, decide contact, read the signal, then update the library (Fig 3, Fig 9 :27-28).

Strain test: Fig 4 :22 governs what crosses between scales, Fig 6 :23 governs what moves a closure wall. Different quantities, so no strain after re-census.

## WORLD (secondary, each source opened; field, author/venue/year, quote, prediction, corroborates|conflicts)

**Pool A, marketing and consumer research (one witness, not four).**

- **Marketing science / consumer information.** Bart Bronnenberg, Jean-Pierre H. Dubé and Robert E. Sanders, *Consumer Misinformation and the Brand Premium: A Private Label Blind Taste Test*, NBER Working Paper 25214, 2018; published Marketing Science 39(2), 2020. Read via NBER page, 2026-11-22. Quote: *"an overwhelming majority systematically chooses the private label in the blinded test"*; the information effect *"decays slowly over time."* Prediction: the brand barrier is information and memory of it fades. **Corroborates** the channel-decay reading (Fig 10 :24) and the need for an instrument (Fig 3 :27); does not settle C1 versus C2.
- **Marketing / brand equity.** Kevin Lane Keller, *Conceptualizing, Measuring, and Managing Customer-Based Brand Equity*, Journal of Marketing 57(1), 1993, and David Aaker's equity model, read via encyclopedia record, 2026-11-22. Quote: *"a strong brand name works as a credible signal of product quality for imperfectly informed buyers and generates price premiums as a form of return to branding investments."* Prediction: a premium can be a return on quality investment. **Corroborates** C1 at the summary level, but shares the pool with Shapiro and is a secondary summary, so one witness.

**Pool B, neuroscience (independent witness for the mechanism shape).**

- **Neuroscience / consumer preference.** Samuel M. McClure, Jian Li, Damon Tomlin, Kim S. Cypert, Latané M. Montague and P. Read Montague, *Neural correlates of behavioral preference for culturally familiar drinks*, Neuron 44(2), 2004. Read via ASU Pure abstract record, 2026-11-22. Quote: *"brand knowledge for one of the drinks had a dramatic influence on expressed behavioral preferences and on the measured brain responses."* Prediction: a brand cue shifts expressed preference and brain response without changing the substance. **Corroborates** C2 at the neural stratum.
- **Neuroscience, same study reported for a general audience.** Kelly Lowenberg, *Neuro-Cola*, Stanford Law Biosciences Blog, 2008. Read 2026-11-22. Quote: *"They preferred the drink with the label 73% of the time, even though the contents of the cups were identical."* Prediction: the labelled cup wins although the cups are identical. **Corroborates** C2, but shares the same experiment as the Neuron record, so it is the same witness, not a second one.

**Pool C, economics (independent witness for C1).**

- **Economics / reputation.** Carl Shapiro, *Premiums for High Quality Products as Returns to Reputations*, Quarterly Journal of Economics 98(4), 1983. Read via EconPapers abstract, 2026-11-22. Quote: *"markets in which buyers cannot observe product quality prior to purchase"* and *"This premium also serves the function of compensating sellers for their investment in reputation."* Prediction: a premium compensates real investment in quality under imperfect information. **Corroborates** C1, low overlap with the neuroscience evidence.

**Pool D, evolutionary biology (low-overlap witness, internally conflicted).**

- **Evolutionary biology / honest signalling.** Dustin J. Penn and Szabolcs Számadó, *The Handicap Principle: how an erroneous hypothesis became a scientific principle*, Biological Reviews 95(1), 2020. Read via PMC, 2026-11-22. Quote: *"The most widely cited explanation for the evolution of reliable signals is Zahavi's so-called Handicap Principle, which proposes that signals are honest because they are costly to produce."* Prediction: honesty has been explained by cost.
- **Evolutionary biology / signalling games.** Szabolcs Számadó, István Zachar, Dániel Czégel and Dustin J. Penn, *Honesty in signalling games is maintained by trade-offs rather than costs*, BMC Biology 21:4, 2023. Read 2026-11-22. Quote: *"Our results refute the claim that signals must be costly at the evolutionary equilibrium to be reliable."* Prediction: honesty does not require cost at equilibrium. **Conflicts** with the costliness claim inside the same field. Returned to the census: the figure verdict depends on contact and settlement, not signal cost, so the conflict carries zero verdict weight and corroborates only the structural point that honesty is a property of the signal-in-habitat.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The figure-derived verdict is the union kept separate and never averaged: experience and the market settle value; a person's own judgment is the anchor for the person-level trust sub-question; a premium is a frame-internal filter, not a witness; a brand is a head start and a decayable channel; genuine trust is a second-order settled term that the brand regenerates only while it survives use; value is brand-and-habitat, not the name alone. Which condition dominates in a named market is a `gap`, not a hidden answer. The neuroscience and blind-test sources corroborate the percept-shift and channel-decay shape; the economics and brand-equity sources corroborate the genuine-value shape; the biology sources corroborate the structure while disagreeing about cost. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** misdirected diagnosis and spent independence. A market failing C1 (contact) would be sold better messaging instruments, or a market failing C2 (percept only) would be told to invest more in product quality when the barrier is information. In outreach, an expert's twenty minutes would be spent refuting a shape that was never the right shape. The cost is real but bounded and reversible, and Fig 2 :42 prices the safeguard: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*
- **undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails; a refutation is added and the reach falls because something was added rather than deleted (Fig 6 :42-43). The shape and its five falsifiers are recorded with provenance so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-64).
- **reversed by:** any of the five pre-registered falsifiers, in particular (1) a brand lowering prediction error with no contact change, (2) a premium persisting with no blind discrimination over a long full-information horizon, (3) human judgment ceasing to be the anchor for a person-level question, (4) C1 and C2 clearing under one lever with no categorically different residue, or (5) trust composable with no settled outcome. A source with a genuinely different evidence pool showing that experienced quality is fully predicted by branded surveys alone would also reverse it. Prestige or agreement is not such a source.
