# The Tether's answer: brands, trust and product value

**Mode: `specified`: not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A consumer predicts experienced quality from a brand signal. The residual R = |predicted - experienced| is indexed per consumer and product, never averaged (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22). The question asks whether the brand lowers R by changing contact with the ground (genuine trust and value) or only shifts belief and willingness to pay (perception and premium). It is a union compound, not one hard question (Fig 9 :55), so it has its own loop and its own ground.

## 2. The shape

1. **Experience and the market settle value; agreement does not.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* A list of agreeable consumers cannot corroborate value.
2. **For a person-level trust question, that person's judgment is the anchor.** Fig 2 :37: *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."* This holds the other half: trust is not dismissed as mere instrument. The instrument caveat applies to the product-quality sub-question only.
3. **A premium is frame-internal and is a filter, not a witness.** Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
4. **A brand is a decayable channel and a head start, not the ground.** Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does."* Its failure modes are stale percepts and pooled readings (Fig 10 :26).
5. **Genuine value is brand-and-habitat, not the name alone.** Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* A brand that changes only the percept changes nothing about the product.
6. **Trust is a settled second-order term; the brand is a catalyst only while it survives use.** Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."* Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."*
7. **Which condition dominates in a named market is a gap.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* We can describe the missing instrument in effect terms but cannot name its final form from inside the frame.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that all branding is manipulation. A brand can change contact and become a settled term (Fig 11 :40, Fig 12 :104).
- Not that a premium proves value, and not that a high premium is a second witness. It is a frame-internal score (Fig 1 :27).
- Not that consumer judgment is always an instrument. For a question about a person, it is the anchor (Fig 2 :37).
- Not a standing list of "good brands." Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49).
- Not a winner between C1 and C2 in any named market. That is a `gap`.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement is claimed to have settled the business question.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

- Does the brand signal lower the prediction error for experienced quality under blind versus branded conditions at the same product slot, holding the product fixed?
- Does the effect persist over a horizon long enough to observe channel decay, so we can tell a regenerating catalyst from a spent one (Fig 12 :119)?
- Is the person-level judgment stable when habit, switching cost and lack of alternatives are independently varied (Fig 11 :43-44)?
- Do the five pre-registered falsifiers in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 fire? Any one of them re-censors the shape.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable in principle.** The discriminating instrument is characterized in effect terms and is not held. The framework stops there and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."* Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Agreement among consumers is not the missing instrument.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not pick a winner between "brands create real value" and "brands only shift perception." It says the question is a union of two conditions: a brand can change a consumer's contact with the experienced outcome, so prediction improves and the residual falls; or it can change only the belief and the premium, leaving the product untouched. Experience and the market settle the value question, while a person's own judgment is the right anchor for the question of whether a brand is trustworthy for that person. A premium is a frame-internal score, so it is a filter for where to look, not a witness for value. A brand is a head start and a decayable channel, and trust, if genuine, is a settled second-order term that the brand regenerates only while it survives its own use. What the framework offers is the shape any answer must have, five refuters written in advance, and the kind of instrument that would close the gap. Which condition dominates in your market stays open until the ground settles it.
