## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, computed as gap(x, (T_E composed with T_A)(x)), measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :19, Fig 2 :29 and :37, Fig 10 :24, Fig 12 :104 and :119, Fig 9 :32.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07`: experience settles value; a person's judgment anchors trust; a premium is a filter; a brand is a channel; trust is settled second order; the winner is a gap.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete market claim, for example "brand X creates genuine value because its premium is high and its customers are loyal."

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **anchor distinction** between person-level trust and product-level quality, which the concrete claim collapses;
- the **filter/witness distinction**, which the concrete claim erases by treating a premium and repeat purchase as proof (Fig 7 :35);
- the **gap**, which the concrete claim fills with a winner the framework does not return.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a percept-only change does not change contact, so it cannot by itself lower R (Fig 11 :40). That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the formula as identical to the figure set would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a measured gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` states.
