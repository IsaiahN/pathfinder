## What the wider literature bears on the question

1. **A brand cue can shift preference without changing the product.** The McClure/Neuron work, read through the Stanford report, found that identical cups were preferred 73% of the time when one was labelled Coke. That is C2 (percept shifted) at the neuroscience stratum, and it is low overlap with the marketing surveys. It does not by itself show that value was not created, because the preference itself is real for the person (Fig 2 :37).
2. **Information can close the gap, and the effect decays.** The NBER blind taste test found that consumers systematically chose the private label in the blinded test even though they predicted they would not, and that the effect of the information decayed slowly. That is a channel result (Fig 10 :24) and it corroborates the need for a discriminating instrument (Fig 3 :27). It supports both C1 and C2: the barrier was information, and the memory of it faded.
3. **A premium can be a return to real quality investment.** Shapiro's reputation model and the brand-equity summary both model a premium as a credible signal under imperfect information and a return on investment in quality. That corroborates C1 (genuine value), but it is one economics/marketing evidence pool, not two witnesses.
4. **A field-internal conflict is recorded, not used to flip the verdict.** Penn and Számadó argue that the costly-signal explanation is erroneous; Számadó et al. show honest signals need not be costly at equilibrium. These two sources disagree about the mechanism of honesty. The figure verdict does not depend on signal cost; it depends on contact and settlement (Fig 11 :40, Fig 12 :104). The conflict is therefore returned to the census and carries no verdict weight, exactly as the rule requires.
5. **No world source settles which condition dominates in a named market.** That remains the declared gap of F7.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22**: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full framework before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape, the split and the refuter; offer sources after.
- **Fig 10 :42**: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the architecture before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28**: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if C1 rather than C2 were true before giving them any source.
- **Fig 2 :40**: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (whole line begins *"disqualifying for a verdict: "*). Ten marketing surveys are one witness.
- **Fig 2 :42**: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Here the cost is bounded, so a small number of low-overlap instruments, blind test, experienced-outcome record, and a person-level trust judgment, is enough.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim, the two conditions and the refuter; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and what instrument would discriminate C1 from C2; (3) only then the local figure text and the links, with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

- **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* The recipient checks the shape without holding the framework interior.
- **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. Their verdict on stage 1 is recorded before any theory is shown, and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
