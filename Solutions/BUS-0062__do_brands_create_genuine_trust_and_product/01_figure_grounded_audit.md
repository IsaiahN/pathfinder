## 0. Residual stated in effect terms

A consumer chooses under incomplete information about product quality. The consumer's library Γ predicts quality from available signals; the action is the choice; the market returns o′, the experienced outcome. R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| is measured per consumer-product slot, never as a single global number.

> "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." (Fig 1 :19)
> "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." (Fig 1 :22)

The question asks where the brand sits in that loop. Two readings are possible:

- C1: the brand changes the consumer's contact with the ground, so the prediction of experienced quality improves and R falls. Trust is a settled term paid for by repeated outcomes.
- C2: the brand only changes the belief b and the willingness to pay, leaving the product and the experience untouched, so R persists and the premium is generated inside the frame.

The sought-for shape, bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a shape that keeps C1 and C2 separate, identifies which is a channel and which is ground, and names the instrument that would discriminate them. Until it is bound, it predicts nothing and no check in the loop can see it.

Symbols table :38-40: *"It is the aim, not only the error signal, and it is always a slice: what cannot be perceived yet is residual and simply cannot be read."*

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.** Arity: a three-place relation (consumer library, choice, market return), indexed by slot and ordered in time. Symmetry: "genuine" is a threshold comparison against what the ground pays, not a symmetric join of two perceptions (Fig 12 :87, comparison ⋛). Scale: first order at the single purchase, second order at the accumulated trust built from repeated purchases.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Consumer prediction loop | consumer, belief b, choice a, observation o′ | residual reduction R | experienced quality | admitted |
| D2 Market ground | firms, products, prices, repeat buyers | payment, share, premium | the market (Fig 2 :29) | admitted |
| D3 Brand signal / channel | name, associations, credibility, memory | information carried, search cost saved | none of its own; it is a channel | admitted as channel, not as ground |
| D4 Verification / measurement | blind tests, surveys, longitudinal records, instruments | discrimination of perceived from experienced | the ground, never agreement | admitted |
| X1 Brand value as a free-floating property of the name | the name alone | prestige | none nameable independent of consumer and market | not a domain yet (Fig 11 :39) |
| X2 Willingness to pay as sole proof of value | the premium | price | already inside D2 | absorbed into D2 |

**Partition or union.** Union, not partition. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* C1 and C2 overlap: a single lever that clears only one leaves the intersection standing, and the residue becomes a specific compound, not a smaller mixture (Fig 9 :57). There is no nameable anchor for X1, so it is not admitted as a domain. X2 is absorbed because a premium is an instrument that can be wrong on axes it does not measure (Fig 2 :38).

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Describe the residual before naming a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); discriminate percept from ground (D4) before deciding contact (D2); read the brand as a channel (D3) before updating the consumer library (D1). This is perception to vocabulary to objective to planning to learn and carry.

## 3. Findings

### F1: The market and experience, not agreement, are the anchor (decisive for the frame)

> "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." (Fig 2 :29)
> "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." (Fig 2 :35)

A willingness-to-pay survey is a frame reading. It does not settle whether value was created. The market and the experienced outcome are the anchor, and a ground that moves when a frame pushes on it is not an anchor (Fig 2 :30). This refuses Option B when Option B is stated as "a premium proves value."

**Refuter:** show a case where the experienced outcome is fully determined by the stated premium with no product change and no information change, such that the anchor moves with the frame.

### F2: But trust is a person-level judgment, so human judgment is the anchor for that sub-question (correction to my first reading)

> "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people." (Fig 2 :37)

My first reading treated every consumer report as an untrusted instrument. That was too broad. For the sub-question "is this brand trustworthy for this person," the question is constitutively about a person, so the person's experienced judgment is the anchor, not merely an instrument (Fig 2 :36-38). The instrument caveat applies to the other sub-question, "is the product objectively better," where human judgment is an instrument that can be wrong on axes it does not measure. Both claims are now held: the trust sub-question has a human anchor, the product-quality sub-question needs an instrument. This is a re-census of domain D4, not a flip of the verdict.

**Refuter:** a figure showing that human judgment is never the right anchor for a question about persons, despite Fig 2 :37.

### F3: A premium is frame-internal and cannot validate the brand

> "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." (Fig 1 :27)

Willingness to pay, credibility accrued, and perception scores are produced by the same frame they would validate. Fig 5 :42: *"The acceptance test is a bargain, not a threshold."* A brand term that "explains" quality only by relabeling the perception it helped create fails that bargain: *"A term that explains everything by saying nothing fails this, and so does a term that explains one occasion perfectly."* (Fig 5 :43-44)

**Refuter:** a measure of value that is not produced by the consumer frame that reports it, for example a blind experienced-outcome record held by a third party.

### F4: The brand is a channel, and channels decay

> "the ground does not decay, the channel does" (Fig 10 :24)
> "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings" (Fig 10 :26)
> "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." (Fig 4 :22)

A brand is a head start that makes the next search cheaper. It carries information; it is not the product. Its failures are channel failures: a famous name with a stale percept, a saturated metric, a pooled reading. Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* A brand built from one success and replayed is a recording, not a generator. This is the precise sense in which Option B can be true without being the whole truth.

**Refuter:** a brand whose information content stays discriminating after the product quality changes without re-learning, which would make the channel a ground.

### F5: Genuine value is agent-and-habitat, not the name alone

> "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone" (Fig 11 :39)
> "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." (Fig 11 :40)
> "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." (Fig 11 :49)

A brand that changes only the percept changes nothing about contact, however much it improves the stated premium. Its valence is relative to the residual: a trusted name is a benefactor when it points to a good product and a malfactor when it masks a bad one (Fig 11 :50). We cannot keep a standing list of "good brands" independent of the product and consumer involved.

**Refuter:** a brand whose valence is invariant across a documented change of product quality and consumer residual.

### F6: Trust is a settled second-order term, and the brand is a catalyst only while it survives use

> "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world." (Fig 12 :104)
> "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is." (Fig 12 :119)

Each satisfactory purchase settles a first-order term. Once settled, the brand becomes an atom that the consumer composes over, and the accumulated trust is second order. A brand is a catalyst only if it survives its own use: repeated experience either regenerates the trust or spends it, and no survey records which (Fig 12 :119). This gives the mechanism for "genuine trust" without naming a mystical quality.

**Refuter:** a trust term that can be composed over by a consumer who has never had an outcome settle a purchase, or a brand that survives repeated use without any regeneration mechanism.

### F7: The decisive instrument is missing, so the empirical winner is a gap

> "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." (Fig 3 :27)
> "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself" (Fig 9 :32)
> "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence," (Fig 13 :134)

No figure line decides whether C1 or C2 dominates in a particular market. That is an empirical question about a source-discriminating instrument. We can characterize the missing instrument in effect terms: it must separate perceived quality from experienced quality at the same product slot, blind and branded, over a horizon long enough to see the brand's channel decay. We cannot name its final form from inside the frame. "A search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence" (Fig 8 :39). This is a `gap`, not a hidden answer and not a silence.

**Refuter:** produce an instrument that discriminates the two conditions at the ground in a named market, and the gap closes for that market.

### F8: Blind and branded readings are low-overlap frames, but a pool is one witness

> "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone" (Fig 8 :45)
> "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool" (Fig 2 :40)

The blind taste test and the branded choice are two readings that disagree. Their disagreement is information, because the frames differ in what they draw on (taste versus cultural memory). But collapsing many branded surveys into "consumer opinion" is one evidence pool, not many witnesses (Fig 2 :40). Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Here the cost is moderate, so two or three genuinely different instruments are enough; a mass survey is not.

**Refuter:** show that blind and branded readings draw on the same evidence, in which case their disagreement is a spelling difference and carries no surplus.

### F9: Use filters for budget, witnesses for verdict

> "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." (Fig 9 :60)

A premium size, a market-share number, or a brand-valuation figure is a filter that decides how much to spend on investigation. It is not a witness for whether value is genuine. This is the same error the strategy pilot found when it used variance decomposition as a verdict (BUS-0001, `09_watson_answer.md`).

**Refuter:** a decision procedure in which a premium size alone flips the verdict about genuine value.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach, per the seat rule: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A brand lowers R with no change to the product, no new information, and no change in contact, so contact is not needed for prediction to improve.
2. A brand's premium persists when blind tests show no discrimination and experienced quality is identical over a long horizon under full information, so the premium is fully decoupled from the ground.
3. Human judgment ceases to be the right anchor for a question that is constitutively about a person, breaking F2.
4. C1 and C2 are not separable: one lever clears both with no categorically different residue, breaking the union reading.
5. A settled trust term can be composed over by a consumer with no settled outcome, breaking Fig 12 :104.

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or re-censused. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the split, not a winner: C1 contact and C2 percept are different conditions, and a single lever will not clear both (F1, F7, Fig 9 :55).
2. Ask what would discriminate the two conditions before showing any theory (Fig 9 :27-28, Fig 10 :52).
3. Treat the premium as a budget filter, never as a witness (Fig 9 :60).
4. In outreach, send the generator, the shape and the refuter, not a recording of a success (Fig 4 :25).
5. Record the absence of the discriminating instrument honestly as a `gap` (Fig 13 :134).

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is experience an anchor?** Yes, Fig 2 :29. The market settles, not agreement.
2. **Is the person's judgment an anchor for trust?** Yes, Fig 2 :37, because the question is about people.
3. **Can a premium validate?** No, Fig 1 :27. It is produced by the frame it would validate.
4. **Does a brand carry information?** Yes, as a channel and head start, Fig 4 :22; it can decay, Fig 10 :24.
5. **Does a brand move the wall?** Only if it imports a new atom (Fig 6 :23); a premium adds no atom.
6. **Is trust a term?** Yes, second-order and settled, Fig 12 :104; the brand is a catalyst only while it survives use, Fig 12 :119.
7. **Which condition dominates?** No figure governs it. `gap`, Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134. SEARCHED first (cheapest), then INWARD toward the discriminating instrument, never OUTWARD import against a budget limit.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives: the question is a union of contact change and percept change; experience and the market are the anchor; a person's judgment is the anchor for the person-level trust sub-question; a premium is a frame-internal filter; a brand is a decayable channel whose trust is a settled second-order term. The empirical dominance claim is unreachable at this budget and is recorded as a `gap`. Strained after re-census: **none** in the figures; one live conflict inside the world evidence pool, named, not hidden.
