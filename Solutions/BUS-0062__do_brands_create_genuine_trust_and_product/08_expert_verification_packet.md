# Expert verification packet: the Tether on brands, trust and product value

**What this is:** a claim in a form a branding or consumer-behavior researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified`: not `grounded`.** No market measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question is a union of two conditions, not one: C1, a brand changes a consumer's contact with the experienced outcome so the prediction error R = |predicted - experienced| falls; C2, a brand changes only the belief and the willingness to pay, leaving product and experience untouched. Experience and the market settle value, while a person's own experienced judgment is the anchor for the person-level trust sub-question. A premium is a frame-internal score and is a filter for where to look, not a witness for value. A brand is a head start and a decayable channel, and genuine trust is a second-order term settled by repeated outcomes, regenerated only while the brand survives its own use. Which condition dominates in a named market is a declared gap until a discriminating instrument closes it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal and methodological; checkable by reading Fig 1, Fig 2 and Fig 9

- **Statement:** the value question is settled by the experienced outcome and the market, not by agreement among consumers; a willingness-to-pay score cannot validate the brand that produced it; the empirical winner is a gap.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- **Refuter:** a brand lowers the prediction error with no product change, no new information and no change in contact; or a premium size alone is shown to settle the value question without any external outcome.

### C2: framework-internal but person-facing; checkable by reading Fig 2 and Fig 10

- **Statement:** for a question about a person, that person's experienced judgment is the anchor, not merely an instrument; the brand is a channel that can decay, not the ground.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :37: *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."* Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does."* Fig 10 :26: *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings."*
- **Refuter:** a figure or case showing human judgment is never the right anchor for a question constitutively about a person; or a brand whose information content stays discriminating after product quality changes without re-learning, so the channel behaves as ground.

### C3: structural; checkable by reading Fig 11 and Fig 12

- **Statement:** value is brand-and-habitat, not the name alone; trust is a settled second-order term; the brand is a catalyst only while the outcome regenerates it.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."* Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."*
- **Refuter:** a trust term composable by a consumer with no settled outcome; or a brand whose valence is invariant across a documented change of product quality and consumer residual.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that all branding is perception manipulation. A brand can change contact and become a settled term.
- Not that a premium proves value, and not that ten surveys are ten witnesses. Shared evidence pools are one witness (Fig 2 :40).
- Not that consumer judgment is always an instrument. For a person-level trust question it is the anchor (Fig 2 :37).
- Not a standing list of good brands. Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49, :51).
- Not a winner between C1 and C2 in any named market. That is a `gap`.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. Does the brand signal lower the prediction error for experienced quality under blind versus branded conditions at the same product slot, holding the product fixed?
2. Does the effect persist over a horizon long enough to observe channel decay, so a regenerating catalyst can be told from a spent one (Fig 12 :119)?
3. Is the person-level judgment stable when habit, switching cost and lack of alternatives are independently varied (Fig 11 :43-44)?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29, :37 and Fig 10 :24, three short passages.
2. Check the C1 and C2 statements against your own knowledge of branding research: is a premium a frame-internal score in the sense Fig 1 :27 names, and is the person's judgment the anchor for a trust question?
3. Name one instrument that would separate perceived from experienced quality at the same product slot and over a decay horizon, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
