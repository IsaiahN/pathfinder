## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about two bundled residuals. **Origin:** is Lorentz invariance a primitive input, a derivative composition leftover, or a contingent selection effect? **Exactness:** does it hold at all energies, break at a finite scale, or is the question beyond the reach of current instruments? What each leads to: a postulate stops at a stipulation; a derivative predicts symmetries are not members; a selection effect makes exactness contingent; a broken-symmetry claim needs a detector; an exactness claim is a convention unless a sealed closure is searched to the end; a budget-limited question needs better instruments, not a new atom.

- **Order by dependency.** Settle D1 (what composes) before D2 (the binding) before D3 (what an instrument returns). Describe R before picking a frame; refuse before mint.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives, the specific claims do not. A space-time symmetry is a composition leftover and cannot be a seventh member; the symmetry relates independent terms and depends on their presence; no direct space-time bond is composable. On exactness the verdict is **unreached, not confirmed and not refuted**: a null bound is not a proof of absence, so no finite search closes a claim about all energies, and the case is budget-limited rather than underivable. Naming a mechanism, a dispersion relation or a numeric LIV scale as *the* origin is a binding from the physics frame and needs provenance and a shadow test. This is `specified`, ungrounded, candidate; `general` on the shape.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet is mode-labelled, states four refuters before the conclusion, and records the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a recording instead of a generator, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The refused items (a mechanism name, an exactness verdict, a chosen phenomenological fit) are removable without touching the derivation. `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove them. A superseded claim costs a correction, not a primitive.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A source-discriminating instrument that separates intrinsic emission lag from propagation lag at the relevant scale would move R2 to a grounded verdict either way; an exhibited sealed closure would make exactness testable in principle; a derivation of a composition leftover as a seventh member, a direct state-state bond that cannot be refused, or a demonstration that origin and exactness form a partition would break the shape claim. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
