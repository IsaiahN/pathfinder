**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` sections 5 to 7 are intact. If the stage-1 output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.

**Subject:** One claim about fair evaluation that you can refute in a few minutes

> Hi [name],
>
> I am writing with one specific output to check against what you already know about performance measurement or organizational justice. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output fails, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` shape, `specified` failure predictions, not `grounded`):** when a firm cannot measure an output cleanly, or when the output is produced by a team, the fair-evaluation problem is not primarily a measurement problem. The question "is this a fair evaluation of a person?" is constitutively about people, so the thing that settles it is a maintained procedure people can push on: voice, consistency, accuracy, absence of bias, and a way to correct errors. The metric is an instrument in that procedure, not the anchor. A second requirement follows: the residual must be indexed per person, because "aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A single pooled team number therefore cannot, on this reading, be a fair evaluation of individuals.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I can name the scoring formula, that any one rating scale (forced ranking, 360 feedback, OKRs) is the answer, or that managers are careless. The framework returns a kind of thing, never the answer itself. It says what form a fair evaluation must have; it does not fill the slot. If a particular instrument is chosen before the residual is described, any instrument will seem to fit.
>
> **What would refute this, written down before I sent the note:**
> 1. A single pooled team metric that preserves a discriminating per-member residual at every slot.
> 2. A metric that settles a fairness dispute without any procedure affected people can push on, and whose answer does not move when they push.
> 3. Two raters who clearly share one evidence pool, yet whose agreement still buys a genuine gain in resolving who contributed what.
> 4. An instrument that returns, at some resolution, a signal that fails to resolve the supposedly unmeasurable contribution.
>
> If any of these holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract in this folder's framework): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures and the formula, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - The architecture essay: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get (`general`, `specified`, or `grounded`). A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - Isaiah
