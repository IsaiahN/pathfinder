# Expert verification packet - the Tether on fair evaluation

**What this is:** a claim in a form a performance-measurement or organizational-justice researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the failure predictions, not `grounded`.** No field measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A question about whether an evaluation of a person is fair is constitutively about people, so the thing that settles it is a maintained procedure with voice, accuracy, absence of bias and a correction path; the metric is an instrument, not the anchor. The contribution residual must be indexed per person, because aggregating across slots makes the live signal disappear into an average. The two conditions in the question (output hard to measure, output pooled across a team) are structurally different isomers over the same atoms, so one lever cannot clear both. A criterion that cannot be violated is a convention, and raters who share one evidence pool are one witness, not two. The team score is a second-order composition over settled person-level terms, with a measurable round trip gap. The framework names no scoring formula; it returns a shape with refuters written in advance.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 2 and 10

- **Statement:** for a person-question, human procedure is the legitimate anchor and the metric is an instrument; a pooled aggregate destroys the per-person signal.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."
  - Fig 2 :37: "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."
  - Fig 2 :38: "It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."
  - Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
- **Refuter:** exhibit a metric that settles a fairness dispute without any procedure affected people can push on, whose answer does not move when they push; or a pooled team metric preserving a discriminating per-member residual at every slot.

### C2 - field-facing; checkable by a researcher against the justice and appraisal literatures

- **Statement:** the shape maps onto procedural justice (voice, consistency, accuracy, lack of bias, error correction), and the pooled-reading failure maps onto the idiosyncratic rater effect and Holmstrom's joint-output free-rider result.
- **What to check:** Tyler 1988 reports that procedural justice "has a major influence on both satisfaction and evaluation" and lists "the opportunities for error correction" among its criteria. The organizational justice literature reports Colquitt's four-factor model (distributive, procedural, interpersonal, informational). The appraisal literature reports that "the rating mainly measured the unique rating tendencies of the rater and thus reveals more about the rater than about the person who is rated." Are those characterizations faithful, and do they actually predict that a single pooled number cannot carry individual fairness?
- **Refuter:** show that procedural justice does not have the predicted effect when outcomes are favorable, or that a single pooled metric does discriminate contributions after rater effects are removed. Either weakens the world corroboration (not the figure verdict).

### C3 - framework-internal derivation about the two conditions and the two scales

- **Statement:** hard-to-measure and pooled-team are isomers (Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."), and the team score is second-order over settled person terms (Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."), with a non-zero round trip gap (Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked.").
- **Refuter:** show one lever that clears both conditions including their intersection (kills the isomer claim, leaves a functional-group claim); or show a team rule that replays the person rule with no loss and no gap (kills the two-scale claim).

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a scoring formula, and not a preference for forced ranking, 360 feedback, OKRs or calibration committees.
- Not that fairness is subjective or that managers are careless. The failure is structural: a missing middle instrument (Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at.").
- Not that a team number is illegitimate as pay design; Fig 1 governs measurement, not pay.
- Not that more raters produce truth. Agreeing frames sharing a pool are one witness (Fig 2 :40).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by an experiment or a field reading.

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminator, not more agreement. From The Hard Problem is About Source: an instrument that reads a source-less value "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved the case. Here:

1. An instrument that separates contribution from rater idiosyncrasy, assignment difficulty and shared narrative at some resolution.
2. A team metric that preserves a discriminating per-member residual at every slot.
3. A fairness judgment that does not move when affected people push on it, with no correction path (which would break C1).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :36-38 and Fig 1 :22 - four short passages.
2. Check C2 against their own knowledge of procedural justice and appraisal ratings. Are the characterizations faithful?
3. Name one instrument that would discriminate contribution from rater effect at the resolution they work with, or say why none is possible.
4. Apply the Fig 9 :55-58 union test: do the two conditions really need different levers, including at their intersection?
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
