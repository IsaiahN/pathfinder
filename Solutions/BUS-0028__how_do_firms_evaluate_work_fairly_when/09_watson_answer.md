## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about fair evaluation when outputs are hard to measure or depend on team contributions. Option A: treat the metric as the anchor and refine it until it looks precise. Option B: treat the question as constitutively about people, so a maintained procedure with voice and error correction is the anchor, with the metric in the instrument role and the residual indexed per person. What each leads to: A sharpens an axis that cannot carry the signal, pools away the per-person residual and lets a convention decide an outcome while calling itself measurement; B sends a checkable shape, written refuters and an honest stop at the limit of the available instruments.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between what a fair allocation would reward and what the readings can resolve. Arity: two conditions (hard-to-measure and pooled team), so a union compound, not one hard question; Fig 9 :55 says a case in two conditions "is not a harder case of either". Symmetry: fairness is a comparison (⋛), not a symmetric join. Scale: first order per person, second order at the team.
- **Domain 1, performance measurement.** Actors: metrics, KPIs, raters, outputs. Currency: measured quantities and scores. Anchor: the actual contribution, which does not move when a frame pushes on it. Question type: an instrument problem, not an anchor problem.
- **Domain 2, organizational justice.** Actors: employees, peers, managers, procedures. Currency: voice, consistency, accuracy, lack of bias, error correction. Anchor: the affected people and the procedure they can push on, legitimate because the question is constitutively about people. Question type: the anchor question.
- **Domain 3, verification / outside frame.** Actors: expert checkers, outside peers. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all seats draw on one evidence pool, so its independence is zero.
- **Domain 4, individual/team scale.** Actors: individual contributors, the team, the crossing method. Currency: head starts and reapplicable methods. Anchor: the measured round trip gap R_T.
- **Order by dependency.** Describe R before choosing an instrument (Fig 9 :27-28); refuse a reading that cannot be violated before installing a criterion (Formula step 2, Fig 10 :19); settle person-level terms before the team comparison (Fig 12 :104); triangulate independent frames after the residual is described (Fig 2 :31-32).

Strain test applied: Fig 2 :37 makes human judgment the right anchor for person-questions, while Fig 2 :29 requires an anchor not to move when frames push on it. Re-census resolves this: the anchor is the maintained procedure with a correction path, not an individual opinion. Different objects, so no strain after re-census (see `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 6).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; a single-metric answer does not. For a question constitutively about people, the anchor is a maintained procedure with voice, accuracy, no bias and error correction, and the metric is only an instrument. The contribution residual must be indexed per person, because aggregation destroys the signal that says who contributed. Hard-to-measure and pooled-team are structurally different isomers over the same atoms, so one lever cannot clear both, including their intersection. A criterion that cannot be violated is a convention; raters sharing one evidence pool are one witness. Person-level terms settle first and the team score is a second-order composition with a measurable round trip gap. The framework names no scoring formula. This is a `general`/`specified`, ungrounded result. Where no reading exists at any resolution, the verdict is `unreachable`, not silence, and not a licence to invent a number.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet is mode-labeled, states refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable. The real cost is a wrong fairness procedure that is hard to undo: pay and reputation decisions compound. Fig 2 :42 says "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is reduced by leading with the refuter and paying for genuinely independent checkers, not a larger agreeing audience.
- **undoable:** partly. The shape claim is adjustable without withdrawing the derivation; a scoring method chosen under it can be replaced. A published rating decision is not undoable in the same way, which is why the correction path (Fig 10 :19) is part of the shape rather than an afterthought.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A discriminator that separates contribution from rater effect at some resolution reopens the inward search; a pooled metric preserving per-slot residuals breaks the aggregation finding; a fairness judgment that does not move when people push on it breaks the anchor-legitimacy claim; one lever clearing both conditions including their intersection breaks the isomer claim. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
