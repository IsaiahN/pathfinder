# Isomers, bonds and order in a fair evaluation

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

A fair evaluation must do four things the flat "one score per person" form cannot: compare contributions, record a route to a result, state how far along a goal sits, and refuse a wrong reading. Which bonds does the question need, and is the hard-to-measure case an isomer of the pooled-team case? Two sub-questions are owed: (1) what is the bond structure of the shape, and (2) what is the order of operations between refusal, measurement and composition across the two scales?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: person slot s, contribution residual R⁺_s, the metric, the procedure, the team output, the ground. Bonds held: the seven (three symmetric `+` conjunction, `∥` disjunction, `≡` identity; four ordered `→` sequence, `⇒` production, `−` subtraction, `⋛` comparison) plus negation `¬` (Operators table, Fig 12). No new primitive is available without import. Fig 13's six substrate terms are assumed throughout; "space" here is the arrangement of person slots, "time" the review interval, "network" the peer relations, "spectrum" the ordering of ratings, "gradient" the difference a reward can spend, "energy" the bounded reward budget. This is a binding from the management frame onto held atoms, not a new member of the six.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Which bonds does the shape need?

- Operators table :51: "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one." Fairness is a comparison: ⋛ between contributions. Fig 12 :86-89 identifies ⋛ as "a threshold relation between two quantities" and says the test is "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present?" So fairness needs ⋛, and a vocabulary holding only `+` (a list of scores) cannot state a goal at all.
- Operators table :46: "progress is a subtraction, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot say how far along it is." A fair evaluation must say how far a person moved from a baseline, so it needs `−`. A single absolute score cannot carry progress.
- Fig 12 :35: "A then B; B needs A to have happened." A procedure is a sequence: describe the residual, install a violable criterion, gather independent readings, declare stakes, decide, correct. So it needs `→`.
- Fig 12 :77: "A produces B; B did not exist before." The reward decision produces an outcome that was not waiting in the scores, so `⇒` is licensed.
- Operators table :58: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate." When a reading is shown to be a rater artifact, the system must refuse it (¬), not merely add another rating. This is the Formula step 2 order: refuse before mint.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a fair evaluation can be stated with `+` only (a flat list of scores, no comparison, no subtraction, no sequence, no refusal), then Q1 fails and the bond analysis is surplus.

### Q2 - Are the two conditions isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Condition A (hard to measure): the metric exists but the reading does not resolve the contribution. Bond failure: the instrument is sharpened on an axis that cannot carry it (Fig 6 :29).
- Condition B (pooled team output): the reading exists but is an aggregate over person slots, so the live signal disappears into an average (Fig 1 :22).
- Both conditions draw on the same atoms (person slot, residual, metric, procedure, ground). They differ in which relation fails: A is an instrument/ground relation; B is an aggregation/slot relation. By Fig 12's definition they are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different behaviour. This is why they must not be averaged into one "measurement problem"; a fix for A (a better instrument) does not fix B (an aggregation), and vice versa.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if one lever clears both conditions, including their intersection, the isomer claim fails and the two are one condition. This is the Fig 9 :55-58 union test: a single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.
- **Weaker reading that survives if the falsifier fires:** they are a **functional group** (Fig 12 :124: "the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member"), both instances of a residual that fails to index to a person slot, with different qualifiers.

### Q3 - Does "shape, not a name" forbid using a metric already held? CORRECTION TO MY OWN `05` SECTION 8

- The canonical README's rule governs "what a **missing** thing would have to look like." The subject is a missing discriminator, not an instrument already in the firm's library.
- A KPI, a peer rating or a customer score is already in the firm's closure. Naming one is a **binding** from the management frame onto a held atom, not the invention of a new primitive.
- What is forbidden is claiming the binding is a derivation, or claiming it moves the wall (Fig 6 :25: "IMPORT adds no atom to the world"). If the firm already holds the signal, import adds nothing; the work is the shadow test, not the atom.

### Q4 - What is the order across the two scales?

- Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."
- A per-person residual, once settled by an independent reading, becomes an atom for the team-level comparison. The team score is therefore **second-order**, composed from settled person-level molecules. It is legal only because those settled.
- Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." What crosses to the team level is the method of indexing residuals and declaring stakes, not the individual's recorded score.
- Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." The team rule carried back down will not match the person rule, and the difference is R_T, to be enumerated rather than assumed zero (see `11_audit.md`).
- **Falsifier:** if a team rule can be replayed at the person level with no loss and no gap, the two-scale reading fails and there is only one scale.

### Q5 - Refusal before measurement

- Formula step 2 routes a residual that owes a refusal before a mint. A rater artifact is a REFUTED term: the reading is wrong, and what is owed is a negation, not a new rating scale.
- Fig 5 amendment :53: "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so." Operators table :58: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."
- So the order is: refuse the artifact (¬), then install the violable criterion (Fig 10 :19), then compose to the team. Reversing this (adding more ratings to a corrupt reading) is the accumulation failure.
- **Falsifier:** show a corrupt reading corrected by accumulation alone, with no term refused.

## CONCLUSION

The shape is not a flat score. It is a bonded arrangement: ⋛ for the fairness comparison, `−` for progress, `→` for the procedure, `⇒` for the decision, and ¬ for refusing a wrong reading. The two conditions (hard to measure, pooled team) are Fig 12 isomers over the same atoms with different failed bonds, which is why one lever cannot clear both. Person-level residuals are first-order; the team score is second-order and legal only after the person-level terms settle. The person-to-team transform is lossy and carries a measurable R_T. A metric already in the library is a binding, not an import that moves the wall. These are `specified` structural claims, ungrounded, each with a written falsifier.
