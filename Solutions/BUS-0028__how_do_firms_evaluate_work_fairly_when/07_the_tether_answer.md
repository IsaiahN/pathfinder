# The Tether's answer on fair evaluation when outputs resist measurement

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the failure predictions, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A firm must reward and give feedback on work whose contribution does not register cleanly. Two conditions overlap: the output is hard to measure, and the output is pooled across a team. Fig 9 :54-58 makes this a **union compound**, not one hard question; a case in two conditions is categorically different, not a harder case of either. It has its own loop and its own ground.

## 2. The shape

1. **The anchor is a procedure, not a metric.** Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Fig 2 :37: "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people." A metric "is only an instrument" and "instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure" (Fig 2 :38). So fairness is settled by a maintained procedure with voice, consistency, accuracy, absence of bias and error correction, with the metric in the instrument role.
2. **Index the residual per person.** Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A single pooled team number destroys the signal that says who contributed. Per-person accounting precedes any team composition.
3. **Diagnose a degraded channel, not an absent ground.** Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does." "saturated metrics, pooled readings" (Fig 10 :26) are a lost channel. The remedy is to keep the anchor reachable (Fig 10 :29), not to refine a metric on an axis that cannot carry it (Fig 6 :29).
4. **Install what can be violated.** Fig 10 :19: "install what can be violated." A criterion with no failing case is "a convention that decides an outcome" (Fig 10 :47), not a measurement. An appeal or correction path is the violation surface.
5. **Triangulate low-overlap frames.** Fig 2 :32: "Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are." Self, peers, customers and objective counts are separate frames only if they draw on different evidence. Their agreement is worthless as corroboration if they share a pool (Fig 2 :40).
6. **Declare stakes and keep interested readers off the reading.** Fig 10 :59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying." Where the stake cannot be removed, declare it (Fig 10 :60). A verifier who can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it (Fig 10 :42).
7. **The two conditions are isomers.** Same atoms, different failed bond (Fig 12 :117). A better instrument fixes the hard-to-measure case and leaves the pooling case standing. They must not be averaged into one "measurement problem."
8. **The team is a second-order composition.** Person-level residuals settle first, then become atoms at the team level (Fig 12 :104). What crosses up is the method, not the recording (Fig 4 :22), and the round trip gap R_T must be measured (Fig 4 :30).
9. **The framework names no scoring formula.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." A specific method is a binding from the management frame, provenanced as such, not a derivation.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that managers are careless or that fairness is purely subjective. The failure is structural: a missing middle instrument (Fig 3 :27).
- Not that any one method (forced ranking, 360 feedback, OKRs, calibration committees) is the answer.
- Not that a pooled team number is illegitimate as pay design. Fig 1 governs measurement, not how a bonus is paid.
- Not that more raters produce truth. Agreeing raters with one evidence pool are one witness (Fig 2 :40).
- Not `grounded`. No instrument, experiment or field reading settled any of this.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. The Hard Problem is About Source: a reading that "cannot comment on the source" needs a spectrometer, not a debate. Here:

- **Discriminator test:** an instrument that separates contribution from rater idiosyncrasy, assignment difficulty and shared narrative at some resolution. If it exists and returns a failing signal, the inward search reopens (F3 refuter).
- **Per-slot test:** a team metric that preserves a discriminating per-member residual at every slot (F1 refuter).
- **Procedure test:** a fairness judgment that does not move when affected people push on it, with no correction path. If such a thing exists, the anchor-legitimacy claim fails (F2 refuter).
- **Standing refuters:** derive a person-level residual that is pure rater artifact and cannot be refused; show two frames sharing a pool buying a surplus; exhibit a criterion with no firing condition that decides outcomes without being a convention.
- **The pre-registered set in `01` section 5 is the authoritative list.** Any one firing retracts the corresponding finding.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The firm's six-atom picture is "a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes"; declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" (Fig 13 :134), but "a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence" (Fig 8 :39). Where the ground is held by another frame (customers, peers, later outcomes), it is imported with a source gate and a shadow test, debited against independence. Nothing here is settled.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name a scoring formula and does not claim firms are bad at measurement. It says that a question about whether an evaluation is fair is constitutively about people, so the anchor is a procedure they can push on, with voice, accuracy, no bias and a way to correct errors; the metric is only an instrument. It says a pooled team number cannot be a fair measure of individuals, because aggregation makes the per-person signal disappear into an average, and that the two failures (hard to measure, pooled) are structurally different isomers that one lever cannot clear. It says a criterion that cannot be violated is a convention, not a measurement; that two raters who share one evidence pool are one witness; and that the team score is a second-order composition whose round-trip gap to the person level must be measured. What it offers is the shape any fair evaluation must have, the refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close the open discriminator. The discriminator stays open until the ground settles it.
