## 0. The question under audit, stated in effect terms

> What is the minimum information-processing capacity needed for a system to adaptively respond to its environment?

**Residual R.** What would we have to observe in order to say a system has enough capacity to respond adaptively, and what would we have to observe to say it has too little? The residual is not "how many bits does it hold." It is "does a loop close against a ground as the environment varies?" Fig 1 :23: "The ground enters as the only metric." Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."

**R by arity, symmetry and scale (Fig 9 :22).**

- **Arity:** two relata. A capacity and an environmental demand. "Minimum" is a relation, not a property of one term. This is the first structural result, and it arrives before any figure line is chosen: a one-term question ("how much capacity") has no answer because the threshold moves with the second term.
- **Symmetry:** asymmetric. The environment supplies disturbances, the system supplies responses, and the direction (disturbance -> response -> outcome) matters. The relation is a threshold comparison, not a symmetric join. `Fig 12` comparison ⋛ is "a threshold relation between two quantities," and applying it here is a type question that needs an instrument, not an argument.
- **Scale:** at least two levels. A loop at one scale (perceive, bet, observe, read residual) and a system in a habitat at the next (Fig 3 chain, Fig 4 membrane, Fig 11 habitat). A minimum stated at one scale does not transfer to the other without a lossy transform and a measured round-trip gap (Fig 4 :30).

**Two readings and what each leads to.**

- **Reading A (numeric):** the answer is N bits, N states, or a physical cost such as kT ln 2 per erased bit. This leads to a quantity that is a *budget filter*. Its value changes with the environment, the observer's powers of discrimination and the code. Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." If offered as a verdict, it is a frame-internal quantity scoring itself.
- **Reading B (structural):** the answer is the shape any adaptive system must have, with the number left open until a ground settles it. This leads to a checkable claim with a pre-registered refuter and an honest stop. Fig 13 :133: "what it returns is a shape rather than a name." Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."

This audit derives Reading B and refuses Reading A as a verdict. It keeps Reading A as a budget instrument.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor. No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet. All four domains below have anchors, so all four are legitimate.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D1: the single-system loop** | library Γ, belief b, action a, observation o', residual R per slot s, ground | residual reduction per slot, settled by the ground | the ground as "the only metric" (Fig 1 :7, :23) |
| **D2: the minimum viable substrate** | space, time, energy (states); spectrum, gradient, network (structures); seven bonds and negation | composition across the two kinds | "remove one and something stops" (Fig 13 :48) |
| **D3: the capacity chain** | perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry | status per link (reached / not reached) | an instrument at each link (Fig 3 :27) |
| **D4: agent and habitat** | habitat actors, conditions, relations; the ground | contact; introduction available, exclusion not | the ground, which does not update (Fig 11) |

**Partition or union.** This is a **union compound**, not a partition. Adaptive response requires the loop to close AND the substrate to compose AND the links to be reached AND contact to be maintained. Fig 9 :54-58: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either," and "a bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions." A single lever (more bits) clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. The question must be unbundled before it is searched, or any capacity number will seem to fit.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Habitat and contact first (D4), then perception (D1 residual registers), then vocabulary, then objective, then planning, then learn and carry (D3). The substrate (D2) runs underneath all links. Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Consequently the minimum is set by the first link that is absent, and capacity added above the break is wasted.

**Strain test applied up front.** A reading that makes Fig 11 (capability is agent-and-habitat) contradict Fig 13 (no system functions without six terms) has misidentified a domain, not found a flaw. Re-census: Fig 13 is a *constitutive* minimum (what a system must contain to compose anything); Fig 11 is a *relational* claim (what makes a system capable in a habitat). Different quantities, no strain. See section 6.

## 3. Findings

### F1: The minimum is relational, not an intrinsic number (decisive)

Fig 11 :39: **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."** Fig 11 :40: **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."** Fig 11 :41: **"The substrate question dissolves; the contact question does not."**

So "how much capacity must the system have" is the substrate question. It dissolves. The answerable question is what makes a system capable, and that is agent-and-habitat. A number attached to the agent alone measures a thing that cannot, by itself, make the system adaptive.

**Refuter:** an agent-only change that does not change contact produces adaptive response where none existed. Fig 11 itself names this as the test that would break the claim (the capability claim "fit cases already known and has not been tested forward"). Until that is shown, the relational reading stands.

### F2: The framework returns a shape, not a number (decisive for the mode)

Fig 13 :133: **"what it returns is a shape rather than a name."** Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."** Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"** (the wording in the extract ends with a comma; the quoted substring stops at "silence").

The figures license a shape: the structured conditions any adaptive system must satisfy. They do not license a numeric threshold. To convert the shape into N bits requires a ground reading that this derivation does not have.

**Refuter:** a context-free number N, justified by the figures, that decides adaptive capacity across all habitats. If such a number is derived, the shape-only verdict fails.

### F3: A minimal loop is per-slot, not aggregated

Fig 1 :10: **"one bet per object slot, every action."** Fig 1 :11: **"residual R indexed by object slot."** Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

A system with a tiny global residual and one live slot is a legal state (Fig 1 :13). The minimum capacity therefore includes the ability to keep slots separate. A global "information throughput" average throws away the only signal that says where to look.

**Refuter:** show that an aggregated global residual directs adaptive action as well as a per-slot residual under the same disturbance. That would collapse the indexing requirement.

### F4: The chain is ordered by dependency, and the first missing link sets the floor

Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."** Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** Fig 3 :29: **"Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."**

The minimum is not the sum of capacities at all five links. It is the first link that is absent: no contact, no residual; no perception, nothing to steer; no vocabulary, no affordances; no objective, no progress measure; no planning, no route; no carry, no accumulation. Fig 3 :21 notes that effort above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it. The middle link (vocabulary and objective) is where chains usually break because it has the fewest instruments (Fig 3 :24-27).

**Refuter:** show a case where adding capacity strictly above a missing link restores adaptive response. That would break the dependency ordering.

### F5: Bonds, not ingredients, are required to state a route

Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."** Fig 12 :112: **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."** Fig 12 :113: **"A system holding only ingredients can say what a situation contains and cannot say what would count as getting somewhere from it."**

"Capacity" measured as a store of items (ingredients, bits, states) misses the bond term. To adaptively respond is to take a route to an objective, and a route needs sequence, comparison and subtraction. Under the Operators table the bond costs log₂(|bonds|), and "a bond that costs nothing is a bond that carries nothing, which is the flat case."

**Refuter:** exhibit a system with only an ingredient vocabulary that takes a route to an objective and acts on it. That would falsify the bond requirement.

### F6: There is a constitutive minimum set, and it is closed

Fig 13 :3: **"No system functions without any of them, none is built from the others, and everything else is a composition across the two kinds."** Fig 13 :41: **"Each is indivisible: no arrangement of the other five yields it."** Fig 13 :48: **"And the set is minimal: remove one and something stops."** Fig 13 :74: **"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."**

This is the closest the figures come to a "minimum." It is a minimum of *kinds*, not of bits. Six terms in two kinds (states: space, time, energy; structures: spectrum, gradient, network), with composition only across kinds. Fig 13 :118: **"infinite effort inside a finite reach."** The set is closed; the arrangements are not, so no amount of internal search yields a seventh.

**Refuter:** derive one of the six from the other five, exhibit a seventh member that is not a composition of the six, or remove one and show an established law still derives. These are exactly the refuters Fig 13 :143 offers: "Indivisibility and minimality are claims and both are refutable."

### F7: "Intelligence" is the wrong quantity; capability is the right one

Fig 11 :36: **"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."** Fig 11 :39 and :41 above complete the replacement. A question phrased as a threshold of "intelligence capacity" inherits the dissolved substrate question. A question phrased as the minimum conditions for adaptive contact does not.

**Refuter:** an instrument that reads intrinsic intelligence independently of contact and predicts adaptive response across habitats. None is supplied by the figures, and Fig 11 declares the label posterior.

### F8: Bits and joules are budget filters, not verdicts

Fig 9 :60 above. Fig 1 :23, :27 above. Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."** Fig 12 :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**

Landauer's bound (kT ln 2 per erased bit) prices one physical operation, not adaptive success. Ashby's requisite variety gives a *necessary* condition on the regulator: "only variety can destroy variety," and "a perfect regulator must have at least as many distinguishable states as the phenomenon it is intended to regulate." Both are budget instruments. They set how much it would cost to try a candidate; they do not say whether the candidate meets its ground. Fig 9 :60 forbids promoting either to a verdict.

**Refuter:** a budget number that, by itself, decides adaptive success across all habitats and instruments. If one is derived and settles the ground, this finding fails.

### F9: The substrate members are independent frames, so the minimum is not reducible to one axis

Fig 13 :38: **"Six members that overlapped would be fewer members wearing six names."** The preceding lines give the reason: adjacency is not duration, an ordering is not a pathway, a pathway is not a direction. Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."

If the six reduced to one scalar ("information capacity"), their agreement would carry no information and one of them would be redundant rather than minimal. So a single-capacity answer is not merely unproven; it would collapse the set the figures say is minimal.

**Refuter:** derive two of the six from one underlying quantity with no loss, and show the derived pair still loads distinct laws.

### F10: The habitat must be enumerated, never composed

Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."** Fig 11 :48: **"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."** Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."

Because the minimum is agent-and-habitat, finding it requires reading the habitat off the world, not deriving it from a list of actor types. Fig 11 :70: **"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."** A synthetic demonstration of "N bits suffice" proves wiring, not capability.

**Refuter:** compose the habitat from a fixed actor taxonomy and show that the resulting capacity minimum predicts adaptive response in an unenumerated habitat.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the seat discipline, a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim (Reading B, F1 to F10) is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. An agent-only change that does not change contact produces adaptive response where none existed (breaks F1, Fig 11 :39-40).
2. One of the six terms is derived from the other five, a seventh member is exhibited, or removing one leaves an established law intact (breaks F6, Fig 13 :41, :48).
3. A global aggregated residual directs adaptive action as well as a per-slot residual under the same disturbance (breaks F3, Fig 1 :19).
4. A context-free number N, justified by the figures, decides adaptive capacity across all habitats (breaks F2 and F8, Fig 13 :133, Fig 9 :60).
5. A vocabulary with only ingredients states a route to an objective and acts on it (breaks F5, Fig 12 :111).
6. Adding capacity strictly above a missing dependency link restores adaptive response (breaks F4, Fig 3 :19-20).

If any fires, the shape claim is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of the answer may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Answer with the shape and refuse the number. State the mode on every claim.
2. Lead with the reframe (Fig 11): capability is agent-and-habitat, so the minimum is a relational threshold, not an intrinsic bit count.
3. Present the minimum as a union of four conditions (loop, substrate, links, contact), unbundled (Fig 9 :54-58). Do not average them.
4. Keep bits and actions as two currencies (Fig 12 :125-126). Use Landauer and requisite variety as budget filters only (Fig 9 :60).
5. Enumerate the habitat before naming any capacity (Fig 11 :43, :48).
6. Send the generator (shape claim and refuter), not a recording of a conclusion (Fig 4 :22-25).
7. Say plainly: "unreachable is a verdict, not a silence" (Fig 13 :134, quoted as substring).

Overall: the question as posed (a numeric minimum) is the dissolved substrate question. The answerable form has a structural shape, and that shape is `specified`, ungrounded, candidate. The number stays open until an instrument and a habitat are named. Fig 13 :118 applies: "infinite effort inside a finite reach." Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence.
