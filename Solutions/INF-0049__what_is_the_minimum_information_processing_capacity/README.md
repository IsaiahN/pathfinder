## The question restated as an effect

The question asks for a minimum. A minimum is a threshold, and a threshold is a relation between two things: what the system can do and what the environment demands. Stated in effect terms, the question is: what must be present for a system to keep a residual registering against a ground, choose an action, and read the outcome back, as the environment varies? Option A answers with a number (bits, states, joules per bit). That yields a budget figure whose value changes with the environment and the observer's powers of discrimination. Option B answers with a shape (the structured minimum any adaptive system must have) and refuses to name a number until a ground settles it. This folder takes Option B and records Option A as a `general`-mode error when it is offered as a verdict (Fig 9 :60).

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with this answer.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **Agreement is not a ground.** Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." A list of people selected for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that would make their agreement worthless.
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." This simulated derivation shares an evidence pool with its own auditor, so their agreement is one pool talking, not corroboration.
3. **Sending the interior spends the reader's independence.** Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, never the framework.

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one.
