# Outreach draft: check the shape before you decide about the number

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` sections 3 and 4 are intact. A recipient must be able to refute stage 1 without holding the framework's interior.

---

**Subject:** What would you count as a failure of this minimum-capacity claim?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one structural claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual.** The question is what minimum information-processing capacity a system needs in order to adaptively respond to its environment. As stated, "minimum" compares a system capacity with an environmental demand, so it is a relation with two terms, not a number stored inside the system. What I would count as adaptive response is a loop that keeps closing against a ground as the environment varies: a residual registers against something that does not move when the system thinks about it, an action is chosen, and the outcome is read back into the next step.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The framework's minimum set has six terms in two kinds: states (space, time, energy) and structures (spectrum, gradient, network), with the rule that composition runs across kinds and never within one. From that it returns a shape, not a number. What any adaptive system must have, in my reading, is four things unbundled rather than averaged: a per-slot residual rather than a global average; a closed loop with a ground that is the only metric; a dependency chain in which the first missing link sets the floor; and contact with an enumerated habitat, since capability is a property of agent-and-habitat and never of the agent alone. Fig 13 :133 says the framework returns "a shape rather than a name." Fig 11 :39 says "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." I am not claiming a bit count.
>
> **What would refute it (written before I ran this):**
> 1. an agent-only change that does not change contact produces adaptive response where none existed;
> 2. one of the six terms is derived from the other five, or a seventh is exhibited;
> 3. a global aggregated residual steers adaptation as well as a per-slot residual;
> 4. a context-free number of bits, justified by the figures, decides adaptive capacity across all habitats;
> 5. an ingredient vocabulary with no bonds states a route to an objective and acts on it;
> 6. adding capacity above a missing dependency link restores adaptive response.
>
> If any holds, I retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am explicitly not claiming:** that the answer is N bits, that Landauer's kT ln 2 per erased bit settles adaptive capacity, that Ashby's requisite variety is sufficient rather than necessary, or that a synthetic simulation with N bits demonstrates capability. Those are budget instruments and wiring demonstrations, not verdicts. "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict" is the framework's own rule.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula, public repository, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical figures README: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Worked architecture narrative: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and the author warns that outputs could be contrived to fit the formula. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
