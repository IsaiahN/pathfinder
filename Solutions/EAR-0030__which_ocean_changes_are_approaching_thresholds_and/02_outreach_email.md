# Outreach draft: check the classification rule before deciding about the framework

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the refuter below is stated in the recipient's own terms. If they cannot say what would fail, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One check on a per-slot test for ocean thresholds versus trends

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question: how to tell, for one ocean change at a time, whether it is approaching a threshold or is better described as a continuing trend. I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read any framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):**
>
> 1. Classify per slot, never by a global ocean average. A global mean is a different slot and hides a live signal in one basin. The rule I am checking is: the threshold question is always a comparison between a current control value and a critical value, plus a feedback whose sign closes the loop so the change becomes self-sustaining past the comparison. A continuing trend is an ordered direction over time, a gradient being spent, which a model tracks with a shrinking prediction error.
> 2. The diagnostic I would use is the behaviour of the residual on that slot. If the prediction error keeps shrinking as more observations arrive, the trend reading holds. If it persists and does not trend down, the model is missing the post-threshold regime, and a new term is owed rather than a better fit.
> 3. The two classes are not mutually exclusive. An element can weaken as a trend while approaching a threshold. The interesting case is the overlap, and it needs its own treatment rather than being split between two lists.
>
> **What I am explicitly not claiming:** that this rule tells you which named ocean change is closest to a threshold. It does not. It gives the shape any answer must have. Assigning AMOC, a grounding line, a reef, or an oxygen minimum to a class is a job for measurements, and I have no ground reading that settles it. I have recorded a provisional map from the published literature, with the disagreements left visible, but I do not offer it as a result.
>
> **What would refute the rule:**
> 1. A case where a global ocean mean predicts a named basin's regime shift better than that basin's own slot residual.
> 2. A case where a slot's prediction error stays flat because the instrument has hit a ceiling, and there is no unmodelled regime behind it.
> 3. A closing positive feedback that produces no qualitative state change after a small additional perturbation, or a qualitative state change with no comparison between two quantities.
> 4. A demonstration that for every ocean element the trend condition and the threshold condition are mutually exclusive.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will withdraw or re-census the rule. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the public sources. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Canonical figure text with line numbers: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in this folder's project
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Two public, independent statements of the same threshold shape: van Westen, Kliphuis and Dijkstra, Science Advances 2024, https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adk1189 ; Borter and Dijkstra, Earth System Dynamics 2026, https://doi.org/10.5194/esd-17-1671-2026
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the shape test is yours to try on a slot you know well. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1.** Send the per-slot rule and its refuter. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure on a slot they know. Record that verdict before any framework material is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt in only.** If they are curious, offer the links above. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because agreeing after seeing the source means sharing the evidence pool (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the shape test as a reusable method, with the instruction that each user writes a fresh refuter and states a mode.

## Hard precondition

Stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage 1 output is stated so it can fail. As drafted, it can fail on four named conditions. If a recipient supplies a fifth, add it visibly rather than quietly absorbing it.
