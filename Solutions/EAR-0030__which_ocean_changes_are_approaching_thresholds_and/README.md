**Question:** Which ocean changes are approaching thresholds, and which are better described as continuing trends?

**Source:** earth systems.txt line 40.

**Section:** 3. Oceans and circulation.

**Status:** done.

## Effect restatement

The question asks for a partition of observed ocean changes into two classes. Class T: a control parameter is approaching a comparison value and, past it, a feedback becomes self-sustaining, so the change is a candidate threshold. Class G: the change is a monotonic direction over time, well described by extrapolation, so it is a continuing trend. The two lead to different actions. T calls for an early-warning instrument and a plan for a qualitative regime shift. G calls for extrapolation and gradual adaptation. What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run: a per-slot rule that assigns each ocean change to T, G, or the overlap of both, plus the instrument that would move a slot from one class to another.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the shape claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons in the figures' own words:

1. A room is not an anchor. Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Adding recipients raises vocabulary, not evidence.
2. Selecting for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation. Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."
3. Asking for trust before a check spends the reader's independence. Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one. The contact points in `03` are institutional and opt-in.
