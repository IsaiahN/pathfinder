# Expert verification packet: the Tether on ocean thresholds versus trends

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ocean or climate scientist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so the reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** No slot is asserted as measured. This is a shape and a route, not a classification of named ocean changes.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Ocean changes must be classified one slot at a time, never by a global ocean average. A threshold is a comparison between a current control value and a critical value plus a feedback whose sign closes past the comparison, so the change becomes self-sustaining. A continuing trend is a gradient spent over time, which a model tracks with a shrinking prediction error. The diagnostic is the behaviour of that error: shrinking means the trend reading holds; persisting and not trending down means a post-threshold term is missing. The two classes overlap, so the case that is both weakening and approaching a threshold is a specific compound with its own loop. The framework returns this shape and stops; it does not name which ocean change is closest.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 9 and 12

- **Statement:** the unit is the slot; a threshold is a comparison bond, a trend is a sequence plus a spent gradient; the two form a union, not a partition.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 12 :87 "a threshold relation between two quantities". Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 12 :112 "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."
- **Refuter:** show that a global ocean mean predicts a named basin's regime shift better than that basin's own slot residual; or that the two conditions are mutually exclusive for every ocean element. Either result withdraws the finding.

### C2: Instrument-facing; checkable by an ocean modeller or observer

- **Statement:** the operational difference is the residual's trend. A shrinking error under a declared model supports the trend reading. An error that persists and does not trend down supports the missing-regime reading. Published early-warning indicators are operational versions of this, but they disagree with each other and share the same record.
- **What to check:** fit a declared model to one slot over a lengthening record. Does the error shrink? Then ask whether the candidate warning signal, rising variance or lag-1 autocorrelation or a physics-based freshwater transport index, survives a change of time window and a change of proxy. Ditlevsen and Ditlevsen 2023 report a mid-century AMOC collapse with a 95 percent interval of 2037 to 2109; van Westen et al. 2024 report the classical statistical indicators are sensitive to the analysed time interval; Wood et al. 2024 report that proxy consistency and relevance "remain the subject of debate"; Armstrong McKay's explainer says the ~15 percent AMOC decline "cannot yet be clearly differentiated from natural variability".
- **Refuter:** exhibit a slot whose error stays flat because of an instrument ceiling, with no unmodelled regime behind it. This is the depth-versus-absence confusion Fig 6 :27 warns about: "some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."

### C3: Framework-internal mechanism; checkable by reading Figure 12

- **Statement:** a threshold is not a longer trend. Past the comparison, the feedback closes. Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
- **What to check:** for any named slot, is there an identified loop with a positive sign that becomes self-sustaining past a critical value? If the answer is that the change is monotonic but no such loop has been identified, the element belongs in the trend class for now, with `candidate` status attached to any threshold claim.
- **Refuter:** show a closing positive feedback with no qualitative state change after a small additional perturbation, or a qualitative state change with no comparison between two quantities. Either breaks the shape.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a ranking of ocean changes by proximity to tipping. The packet contains no such ranking as a verdict; the table in `07` is a recorded, contested reading of sources.
- Not that the global mean is a valid slot for a basin question.
- Not that statistical and physics-based early warning signals are independent witnesses. They share the AMOC record.
- Not that high impact implies proximity. Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A per-slot record long enough to show whether the prediction error shrinks or stays flat under a declared model.
2. A source-discriminating instrument that distinguishes natural variability, gradual forcing and an approaching bifurcation on that slot. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads one value "cannot comment on the source"; the case was closed by a better instrument, not an argument.
3. A physically identified loop with a signed feedback past a critical value, tested on a slot where the threshold claim is made.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, :22; Fig 12 :87, :112, :121; Fig 9 :54. Five short passages. (About three minutes.)
2. Name one ocean slot you know well. State what its prediction error has done over a lengthening record. (Five minutes.)
3. State whether a signed feedback past a critical value has been identified for that slot, or whether the case rests on a proxy trend. (Five minutes.)
4. Name one way the apparent warning signal could be a false alarm. (Three minutes.)
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. (Four minutes.)
