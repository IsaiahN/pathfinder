## 1. The residual in effect terms

The question asks to sort observed ocean changes into two classes. Let each named change be a slot `s`. The residual on `s` is `R_s = |Γ(b,a)_s − o'_s|`: the gap between what the current model predicts for that change and what is observed. Two outcomes are possible under more observation:

1. `R_s` trends down. The model tracks the change; the change is a direction that can be extrapolated. This is the trend class (G).
2. `R_s` persists and does not trend down. `THE_FORMULA.md` step 2 names this exact quantity: *"the residual that PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN"*. Under a threshold reading, the model lacks the term for the regime that lies past a comparison value. This is the threshold class (T).

The decisive structural point comes before either outcome. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." So the question cannot be answered for "the ocean" as one slot. A global mean sea surface temperature or a global ocean heat content is a different slot from AMOC strength or Labrador Sea convection. A live threshold residual in one subsystem vanishes into a global average.

What an answer would look like, written down and bound before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a rule that takes a per-slot time series and returns T, G, or both, plus the name of the instrument that would change the assignment. The rule is the sought-for shape. Binding it makes it refusable.

## 4. Findings

### F1: The question is a bundled union, not a partition (decisive for framing)

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

The phrase "which are approaching thresholds, and which are trends" presupposes a partition. The figures say to unbundle. An ocean change can satisfy both conditions: a monotonic weakening (trend) while a control parameter closes on a critical value (threshold). The world literature confirms this overlap rather than resolving it. Armstrong McKay's own explainer states that "The AMOC has for example declined by ~15% over the last 50 years, but this cannot yet be clearly differentiated from natural variability," while other work reads the same system as "on route to tipping" (van Westen et al. 2024). Both readings can occupy the same slot.

**Refuter:** show that for every named ocean change the two conditions are mutually exclusive, that is, no element can be both monotonically trending and approaching a critical value. If that holds, the partition stands and this finding is withdrawn.

**Verdict:** `triangulated` (Fig 9 union rule plus the world overlap, low overlap between a figure about problem structure and a climate assessment).

### F2: The unit is the slot, never the global mean

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 1 :28: "The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."

World corroboration, independently worded: Wood et al. 2024 write that "Long-term consistency and continuity of existing datasets is particularly critical because the (at least) decadal timescale of AMOC tipping means that early warning indicators could be contaminated by data inhomogeneities." A global ocean average cannot carry a slot-specific salinity transport signal.

**Refuter:** exhibit a case where a global ocean mean predicts a regime shift in a named basin better than that basin's own slot residual, with a stated ground. Until then, the per-slot rule stands.

**Verdict:** `triangulated` (Fig 1 plus an independent Earth-observation source).

### F3: What a threshold is, in the figures' vocabulary

Fig 12 :87: "a threshold relation between two quantities". Fig 12 :89: "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes." Operators table: "a threshold relation between two quantities". The Operators table also identifies the test: "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."

A threshold is not a large trend. It is a relation between two quantities, a current control value and a critical value. Fig 12 :121 adds the mechanism: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." A self-sustaining change past a threshold is a closing loop, not a longer chain. Fig 13 :31: "a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos". The world literature says the same in different terms: Armstrong McKay defines a tipping point as change that becomes "self-perpetuating beyond a forcing threshold"; Borner and Dijkstra 2026 propose defining a tipping event as "a persistent nonlinear transition, shifting focus from abruptness and irreversibility to nonlinear response and self-amplifying feedback."

**Refuter:** show a closing positive feedback that does not produce a qualitative state change after a small additional perturbation, or a qualitative change that occurs without any comparison between two quantities. Either breaks the figure-grounded shape.

**Verdict:** `triangulated` (Operators table plus Fig 12 cycle plus Fig 13 remainder, with two independent definitional sources).

### F4: What a continuing trend is, in the figures' vocabulary

Fig 12 :71: "A then B; B needs A to have happened" (→ sequence). Fig 13 :22: "a difference that can be spent" (gradient). Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."

A trend is an ordered succession of differences with a direction, a gradient being spent over time. It is represented by → and by a gradient over time; Fig 13 :76 confirms these are distinct terms: "A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is." A trend whose residual trends down is Fig 5 :11 "held: the model was right, nothing owed".

**Refuter:** show a change that is well modelled as a gradient over time but whose residual persists and does not trend down under more observation, with no comparison against a critical value. That would break the diagnostic and require a re-census.

**Verdict:** `single-source` for the bond mapping (Fig 12 and Fig 13 share the framework's evidence pool); the world literature corroborates the behavioural distinction without using the figure vocabulary.

### F5: The diagnostic is per-slot residual behaviour, not magnitude of change

`THE_FORMULA.md` step 2 names *"the residual that PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN"* as the quantity that points to a wrong held term. Fig 5 :14: "mechanism broken: a new term is owed". Fig 5 :13: "mis-attached: refit what you have, do not invent". Applied to this question: a trend routes to `held` or `mis-attached`, while a threshold approach routes to `mechanism broken`, because the current model does not contain the post-threshold regime.

This is a `specified` mapping, not a measurement. The figures sort gaps by what is missing; they do not run a time-series test. World sources supply the operational versions: rising variance and lag-1 autocorrelation as *"generic early-warning signals"* (Scheffer et al. 2009), and a physics-based indicator, "the minimum of the AMOC-induced freshwater transport at the southern boundary of the Atlantic" (van Westen et al. 2024).

**Refuter:** demonstrate that a slot with a persistently flat residual has no unmodelled regime, that is, the flatness comes from an instrument ceiling rather than a missing mechanism. Fig 6 :27 warns: "some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."

**Verdict:** `triangulated` for the shape; `single-source` for each operational indicator until an instrument discriminates them.

### F6: The figures cannot name which ocean change is near a threshold

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 8 :30: "underivable, and searchable". Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 9 :44: "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room."

No figure line contains the name AMOC, Greenland, coral, or deoxygenation. The figures return the shape of a threshold test, not a classification of ocean subsystems. The classification is a ground reading, owed to the instrumental and palaeo record. This is a `gap` for a figure-only derivation, not a silence and not a failure. "Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence" (Fig 13 :134, canonical README).

**Refuter:** produce a verbatim figure line that names a specific ocean change and assigns it to T or G. None exists in `03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`; this refuter was checked by reading the whole extract.

**Verdict:** `gap` on naming; `triangulated` on the claim that the shape transfers.

### F7: The world literature is contested, so it cannot be promoted to a verdict

Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Fig 8 :51: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."

The world sources disagree with each other. Ditlevsen and Ditlevsen 2023 report a mid-century AMOC collapse with a 95 percent interval of 2037 to 2109. van Westen et al. 2024 say reanalysis products indicate the AMOC is on route to tipping and that classical statistical indicators are sensitive to the analysed time interval. The Global Tipping Points Report 2023 says considerable uncertainty remains around those timelines. Wood et al. 2024 record that "Both the consistency of the proxies ... and their relevance for AMOC tipping as opposed to recent variability ... remain the subject of debate." Armstrong McKay's explainer says the observed decline "cannot yet be clearly differentiated from natural variability." This is a conflict inside the world pool, not a figure conflict. Per the method, a conflict sends us back to the census; it never flips the verdict. The census already assigns naming to the ground, so the conflict is expected and recorded.

**Refuter:** none needed at the figure level; the world disagreement is data, not a claim being asserted. If any single world source were promoted to a verdict, the exact refuter would be the next source that contradicts it, of which there are several above.

**Verdict:** `single-source` per world claim; no triangulation across the pool because several sources restate the same underlying studies.

### F8: Agreement cannot classify a slot

Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict." Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."

A panel of researchers asked whether AMOC is close to tipping produces a weighted average of influence unless each ranges separately against the ground. In this simulated derivation all seats share one evidence pool, so this seat's agreement with the primary census is internal consistency only.

**Refuter:** exhibit two frames with genuinely different closures and independent instruments that converge on the same per-slot classification. That would be corroboration at maximum independence (Fig 8 :50). Until then, agreement is not a witness.

**Verdict:** `triangulated` for the rule against consensus.

### F9: High impact is not evidence of proximity to a threshold

Wood et al. 2024 classify several ocean tipping points as "high impact, low likelihood" hazards and note that "in many cases uncertainty in current scientific understanding is so deep that it is not possible to provide robust estimates of how likely the events are to occur." Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Impact is a filter for how much attention a slot deserves. It is not a witness for whether the slot is near its threshold.

**Refuter:** show a case where the size of the impact, held fixed against measurement, changes the estimated distance to the threshold. If that can be done, impact has entered the witness channel and this finding is withdrawn.

**Verdict:** `triangulated` (Fig 9 filter rule plus an independent assessment source).

### F10: The stop is honest: unreached, not unreachable

Fig 6 :27: "some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same." Fig 9 :44: "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room." Fig 8 :40: "underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions."

The figures cannot close the classification because they hold no ocean measurements. That is not a defect in the figures and not proof that the question is unanswerable. It is a budget and domain boundary. The instrument is a sustained per-slot observing system, not a further argument.

**Refuter:** an instrument that returns a per-slot distance-to-threshold for a named ocean change would move that slot from `candidate` toward `grounded`. That is the reversal condition, written before the conclusion.

**Verdict:** `triangulated`.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per Fig 10's rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A verbatim figure line names a specific ocean change and assigns it to the threshold class or the trend class. None was found in the full extract.
2. The two conditions are shown to be mutually exclusive for every ocean slot, so Fig 9 :54's union reading is wrong for this domain.
3. A slot with a residual that persists and does not trend down is shown to have no unmodelled regime, that is, the flatness is an instrument ceiling and not a missing mechanism.
4. A closing positive feedback is demonstrated with no qualitative state change after a small additional perturbation, or a qualitative state change is demonstrated with no comparison between two quantities.
5. A global ocean mean is shown to predict a named basin's regime shift better than that basin's own slot residual, with a stated ground.

If any fires, the shape claim is withdrawn or re-censused. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 8. Strains and re-census

- **"Threshold" in two senses.** Fig 5 :42 says "The acceptance test is a bargain, not a threshold." That is a threshold in the minimum-description-length acceptance sense, a cost comparison. Fig 12 :87 defines ⋛ as "a threshold relation between two quantities", a control value versus a critical value. They are different quantities. Re-census: no strain. The word is shared; the object is not.
- **Fig 9 union rule versus the question's partition wording.** The strain is in the question, not in the figures. Re-census puts the two readings in different domains: partition is a question format, union is a property of the world. The union governs.
- **Statistically detected early warning versus a physical indicator.** These are two world frames with low overlap, but they draw on the same AMOC record, so they are not independent witnesses. Recorded as overlap, not as a conflict requiring re-census.
- **After re-census: no figure strain remains.** The one open item is the budget boundary of F6, which is a stop, not a flaw.
