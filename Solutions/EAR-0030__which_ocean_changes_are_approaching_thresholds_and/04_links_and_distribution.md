## Wider literature that bears on the question

- **Definition of the object.** Armstrong McKay et al. 2022 and Borner and Dijkstra 2026 are low overlap in form (a multi-author assessment and a definitional paper) but agree on self-sustaining change beyond a threshold. Their agreement is expected from a shared evidence pool and is recorded as one witness.
- **Operational detection.** Scheffer et al. 2009 supplies generic early warning signals; Ditlevsen and Ditlevsen 2023 supplies a statistical estimator; van Westen et al. 2024 supplies a physics-based indicator and reports that classical statistical indicators are sensitive to the chosen time interval. Three different instruments, one shared AMOC record, so not three independent witnesses.
- **The contrary case.** Wood et al. 2024 and the Global Tipping Points Report 2023 record that proxy relevance and tipping timelines remain debated. This is the conflict that keeps the world map provisional.
- **The aggregation warning.** Earth System Dynamics 2026 records information loss from common aggregation approaches. This corroborates Fig 1 :19 from a low-overlap field.
