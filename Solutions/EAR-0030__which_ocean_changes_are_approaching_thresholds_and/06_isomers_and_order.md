# Bond types, union structure and order: supporting derivation

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks for a two-way classification, but the two classes may be a union rather than a partition. Three sub-questions are owed: (1) which bond expresses each class, (2) whether two ocean changes over the same atoms can be isomers or only members of a functional group, and (3) what order the derivation must follow so that effort lands above the break.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held for each slot: a state (temperature, salinity, density, oxygen, ice extent), a structure (gradient, network, spectrum), a time ordering, and a control parameter with a critical value. Bonds held: the seven, chiefly ⋛ comparison and → sequence, plus the cycle structure and negation. No new primitive is available without import.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Which bond expresses each class?

- Operators table: "a threshold relation between two quantities" and the identifying test "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes." Fig 12 :89 carries the same test.
- Fig 12 :71: "A then B; B needs A to have happened" for → sequence. Fig 13 :22: "a difference that can be spent" for gradient.
- Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction." A trend is a sequence plus a spent difference; a threshold is a comparison.
- ⇒ The two classes are not two magnitudes of one quantity. They are two different bond types. Writing "a big trend" for a threshold is a notation error: it records the operands and loses the bond, which is Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
- **Falsifier:** show a threshold classification that uses no comparison between two quantities, or a trend classification that requires one. If either is demonstrated, the bond mapping fails and the census is redone.

### Q2: Union or partition?

- Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- ⇒ Ocean elements that both weaken monotonically and approach a critical value are not harder examples of a trend. They are a specific compound with its own loop and its own ground. A lever that clears trend-only elements leaves the intersection standing (Fig 9 :56).
- **Falsifier:** a demonstration that no ocean element can satisfy both conditions. Then the union reading is wrong for this domain.

### Q3: Are two ocean changes isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Take AMOC and subpolar gyre convection. They share much of the same atom set (temperature, salinity, density, a control parameter, a feedback) but differ in which structure mediates which state pair and in timescale. If the atom sets are taken as identical and only the arrangement differs, they are **isomers** under Fig 12. If the atom sets differ (as they do when basin geometry and timescale are counted as atoms), they are two members of a **functional group**, a threshold-prone circulation element, with different qualifiers.
- The honest reading: the strong isomer claim is a `specified` hypothesis, not a result. The weak functional-group claim is the one the figure text supports without further measurement.
- **Falsifier:** show that one of the two elements requires an atom the other does not, and cannot be written over the shared set. That kills the isomer claim and leaves the functional-group claim standing.

### Q4: What order does the derivation follow?

- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces."
- Order: (1) perception of each slot, what is measured; (2) vocabulary for what the slot affords, comparison versus sequence; (3) objective, what would count as progress on that slot; (4) a route to the objective, the instrument; (5) carry what worked, with provenance.
- Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Most published effort in this field lands at the instrument and modelling stages. If the vocabulary link (trend versus threshold) is the broken one, more instruments do not fix it. Conversely, if the instrument is the broken link, more debate about definitions does not fix it. Which link is broken is a reading, never a property of the chain (Fig 3 :18).
- Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Residual first, frame second.
- **Falsifier:** show that the classification is settled before the slot is measured, from definition alone. That would reverse the dependency order and break this finding.

### Q5: Analogues, and where they stop

- **Chemistry (isomerism).** Fig 12 :97-98: "silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different properties, which is what forced structure into the notation." The analogue licenses asking whether two ocean elements share atoms but differ in arrangement. It does not license naming which element is which substance.
- **Dynamical systems (bifurcation).** Ditlevsen and Ditlevsen 2023 model the AMOC fingerprint as a stochastic process approaching a saddle-node bifurcation and report a 95 percent interval of 2037 to 2109. That is a world analogue for Fig 12 :121: "The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." It is secondary corroboration, not a figure line.
- **Aggregation in assessment science.** Earth System Dynamics 2026 records "the risk of information loss inherent in commonly used aggregation approaches" (read from the journal homepage 2026-11-22). This corroborates Fig 1 :19 from a low-overlap field.
- **Where analogues stop.** An analogue is a shape transfer, not an import that adds an atom (Fig 6 :25: "An import adds no atom to the world."). Treating the bifurcation model as if the figures had derived it would be the convergent-derivation versus adopted-import confusion of Fig 8 :50-51.

## CONCLUSION

The threshold class is a comparison bond plus a closing cycle; the trend class is a sequence plus a spent gradient. They are different bond types, not different magnitudes. The question's two-way split is a union, so the intersection needs its own loop. The strong isomer reading of two circulation elements is a checkable hypothesis; the functional-group reading is the one the figures support now. The order is perception, vocabulary, objective, instrument, carry. No new primitive is needed for any of this.
