# The Tether's answer: ocean thresholds versus continuing trends

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a classification of named ocean changes. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

For each ocean change `s`, the residual is the gap between prediction and observation on that slot: `R_s = |Γ(b,a)_s − o'_s|`. The question is whether `R_s` shrinks under more observation (a trend the model tracks) or persists and does not trend down (a regime the model cannot represent). The question as asked is a **union compound**, not a partition: an element can be both weakening as a trend and approaching a threshold (Fig 9 :54-57). It has its own loop and its own ground.

## 2. The shape

1. **Classify per slot, never by a global ocean mean.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A global mean is a different slot and hides a live threshold signal in one basin.
2. **A threshold is a comparison, not a large trend.** Fig 12 :87: "a threshold relation between two quantities"; Fig 12 :89: "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes." The two quantities are a current control value and a critical value. Past the comparison, the feedback closes: Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Fig 13 :31: "a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos."
3. **A continuing trend is a gradient spent over time.** Fig 12 :71 "A then B; B needs A to have happened"; Fig 13 :22 "a difference that can be spent"; Fig 13 :76 "A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is." Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."
4. **The diagnostic is residual behaviour, not change magnitude.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 2 names *"the residual that PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN"*. A shrinking residual routes to Fig 5 :11 "held: the model was right, nothing owed". A flat residual routes to Fig 5 :14 "mechanism broken: a new term is owed". This is a `specified` mapping onto a future instrument, not a measurement.
5. **The two classes overlap.** Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." The intersection is categorically different from either class (Fig 9 :57) and needs its own loop.
6. **The figures do not name the slots.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." The classification of AMOC, a grounding line, a reef or an oxygen minimum is a ground reading. This is a `gap` in the figure-only derivation, not a silence.

## 3. Provisional world map, `specified` and contested

Recorded separately from the figure verdict, with status attached.

| slot | present reading | figure status |
|---|---|---|
| AMOC | Multiple EWS studies and a physics-based indicator read it as approaching a tipping point; proxy dependence and false-positive critiques are live | strongest threshold candidate, still `candidate` |
| Subpolar gyre and Labrador and Irminger convection | A central threshold near 1.8 C with a roughly decadal timescale, strongly model dependent | threshold candidate, large uncertainty |
| Greenland and West Antarctic marine grounding lines | Central thresholds near 1.5 C, completion over centuries to millennia; mass loss is also a continuing trend | threshold candidate on long timescales; both classes present |
| Low-latitude coral reefs | Central threshold near 1.5 C, die-off over roughly a decade | threshold candidate with fast response |
| Global ocean warming, heat content, mean sea level | Documented continuing trends with no demonstrated self-sustaining threshold at the global-mean slot | trend class at that slot, with the per-slot caveat |
| Deoxygenation, Beaufort Gyre storage, Arctic halocline, Kuroshio large meander | Candidate ocean tipping elements; early-warning capability at a research stage | undetermined, `gap` at this budget |

The sources disagree with each other, and several draw on the same underlying studies. This is one evidence pool, not three witnesses. Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any named ocean change is definitively near a threshold. No ground reading was taken here.
- Not that the global average is a valid slot for a basin-specific question.
- Not that a large trend is a threshold, and not that impact size measures proximity (Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict.").
- Not that statistical early warning signals and physics-based indicators are independent confirmations. They share the AMOC record.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

A per-slot, source-discriminating instrument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* states that an instrument reading one value "cannot comment on the source"; it was a better instrument, not an argument, that resolved the case. Here the corresponding questions are:

- Does the residual on this slot shrink or stay flat as the observing record lengthens, under a declared model?
- Does the control value close on a critical value, and is there a feedback with a positive sign that will close past it? (Fig 12 :121.)
- Is the apparent warning signal a real approach to a bifurcation or a false alarm? Wood et al. 2024 and Ditlevsen and Ditlevsen 2023 disagree on exactly this, and both can be checked against a longer per-slot record.
- Standing falsifiers: a global mean that predicts a basin's shift better than the basin's own residual; a flat residual caused by an instrument ceiling; a closing feedback with no qualitative state change; mutually exclusive trend and threshold conditions.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 6 :27: "some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same." Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." The figures hold no ocean measurements; the assignment of a slot is owed to the observing system. The answer stops at the limit of what the available instruments distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not rank ocean changes by how close they are to tipping, and it does not claim to settle which one is nearest. It says that the question must be asked per ocean change, never from a global average; that a threshold is a comparison between a control value and a critical value plus a feedback that closes past it, while a continuing trend is a gradient spent over time; and that the diagnostic is the prediction error on that change, which either shrinks as observations accumulate or stays flat because the model lacks the post-threshold regime. The two classes are not mutually exclusive, so the interesting case is the change that is both weakening and approaching a threshold. The framework supplies this shape and a falsifier written in advance, then stops. Which named change sits where is a job for measurements, and the published literature is still contested.
