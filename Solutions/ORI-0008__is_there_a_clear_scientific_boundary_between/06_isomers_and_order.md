### Q1: Which bond does "boundary" name? Three bonds collapsed into one word

- Operators :48 defines `⋛` comparison as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* with the test *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."*
- Fig 12 :111-113: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."** *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."*
- The question bundles at least three bonds:
  1. `⋛` comparison: where is the threshold between chemistry and life?
  2. `→` sequence: what comes first, replication, metabolism, or compartment? (Operators :30: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*)
  3. `−` subtraction: what is absent before life and present after it? (Operators :42: *"A with B removed or absent"*)

Fig 12 :14-16: *"the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not / nothing downstream can tell them apart, / and nothing signals that anything was lost."* A question that says only "boundary" has recorded the ingredients and not the bond.
- **Reading:** one lever cannot close all three. The union rule applies (Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*). Mode `general` for the bond typing, `specified` for the origins binding.

**Refuter, Q1:** show that a single bond, correctly typed, closes the residue for all three readings. Then the union finding is wrong.

### Q2: Are prebiotic chemistry and the first life isomers, or members of a functional group?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- As the question usually writes them, a prebiotic reaction network and a first cell share only a tendency toward self-maintenance. By Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**; they are two members of a **functional group** (self-maintaining chemical systems) with different atoms in the other slots.
- The stronger version: write both over the full shared six-term set `{Space, Time, Energy, Spectrum, Gradient, Network}`, differing only in which structure mediates which state pair and which bond closes the loop. Then they are **isomers**: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. That is the checkable structural claim, and it is the one worth testing, because it does not need a new primitive.
- Fig 12 :95-96: **"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."** This is exactly the risk in saying "life is just chemistry": the phrase names ingredients and loses the bond.

**Refuter, Q2:** show that one of the two requires an atom the other does not. Then the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands.

### Q3: The order is set by settlement, and the "first unit" is a level fact

- Fig 12 :103: **"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."**
- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"**
- Fig 12 :106: **"Nothing composes over what was never admitted as a part."**
- Prebiotic chemistry is settled in the laboratory (amino acids, nucleotides, lipids, autocatalytic networks). It can therefore serve as an atom at the next level. A "first life" that has not settled cannot serve as an atom yet. So the dependency order is: settle the chemical molecules, then compose the cell, then compose the lineage. Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*
- Fig 4 :34-35: **"A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."** The first life, on this reading, is a seed (a generator), not a recording of the first success. That is why a date cannot be read off it.
- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."** Autocatalytic sets are the catalyst case: the part survives its own use. This is a structural reason the transition looks continuous from one level and discontinuous from another.

**Refuter, Q3:** exhibit an absolute bottom unit that cannot be composed at a lower level and that loses nothing across the level transform.

### Q4: Union, partition, and the round trip gap

- Fig 9 :54 (quoted): unbundle before searching. Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

That set is the round trip gap R_T, enumerated in `11_audit.md`. Treating "the framework says no boundary" as identical to the concrete question would set R_T to zero and be the dead-reckoning error.

**Refuter, Q4:** demonstrate a lossless transform from the figure level to the origins-of-life level, so R_T is zero.

## CONCLUSION

"Boundary" bundles three bonds (`⋛`, `→`, `−`) that must be unbundled before search. Prebiotic chemistry and the first life are at best members of a functional group as usually written, and a real isomer claim only over the full shared atom set. The order is dependency order: settled chemistry becomes an atom for the cell, and the cell becomes an atom for the lineage. The "first unit" is therefore level-relative, not a bottom. What survives the round trip is a membrane shape; what does not is any date or single cut. The strongest single supporting claim is Fig 12 :119 (catalyst), because it explains why the transition can look both continuous and discontinuous depending on which level is measured.
