## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the transition from a latent identity category to a politically mobilizing force. Option A: a standing list of properties, with ethnic, regional, religious and class identities ranked by how much of each they possess. Option B: a union of overlapping conditions, mobilized through differing-closure contact in a habitat, bonded into a route, and settled by an outcome anchor, with no figure name for which identity wins. What each leads to: A produces a ranking whose valence flips when the question changes and cannot survive Fig 11 :51. B produces a checkable shape and a stated refuter, at the price of declaring a gap where a local name would go.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** A category that predicts nothing political becomes a force that musters action. Arity: four named axes plus their intersections, so R is multivariate and indexed per slot, per Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Symmetry: the axes are not interchangeable; salience flips with the question, so the relation is ordered in context. Scale: individual, local, national, diaspora; the same loop recurs with different actors. Fig 9 names the object a union compound, not four questions and not one additive score.
- **Domain 1, the identity frame.** Actors: individuals, narratives, categories. Currency: shared meaning and belonging. Anchor: none internal, per Fig 10 :18 "a declared meaning is not a grounded one". No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet; it becomes one only at contact.
- **Domain 2, the habitat and material conditions.** Actors: institutions, markets, demography, territory, elites, shocks. Currency: contact, resources, openings. Anchor: an outcome such as a census, a policy change or a livelihood shift that does not move with the group's story. The habitat is enumerated, never composed.
- **Domain 3, the political and organizational frame.** Actors: movements, parties, brokers, voters. Currency: votes, protests, policy, organization. Anchor: an outcome that settles whether the claim landed. Bonds are required here: a comparison (goal), a sequence (plan), a subtraction (progress), a negation (grievance).
- **Domain 4, the research and verification frame.** Actors: researchers and reviewers. Currency: evidence and agreement. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run every seat draws on one evidence pool, so agreement here is internal consistency, not corroboration.

Partition or union: union. Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." A single lever clears the region where one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.

Order by dependency: describe R before choosing a frame; unbundle the union before searching inside one axis; enumerate the habitat before picking a lever; cross to a differing closure; add bonds; settle at an anchor. A reading below a broken link is a reading of nothing.

Strain test applied: Fig 13's six-term physical substrate was initially mapped onto social identities. Re-census assigns Fig 13 to the physical substrate and leaves the social claim to Fig 9, Fig 11, Fig 6, Fig 12 and Fig 2. The strain is retracted and preserved, not smoothed.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the standing list and the single score do not. The four axes are a union of overlapping conditions. An identity becomes politically mobilized through contact with a differing closure in a habitat with resources and an opening, through bonds that turn a category into a route (comparison, sequence, subtraction, negation), and under an outcome anchor that does not update on the group's own account. Valence is residual-relative, so no axis is inherently mobilizing. Which axis wins in a given place is a declared `gap`, not a silence and not a guess. This is `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The claim states refuters before the conclusion and keeps the gap open, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending a ranking instead of a checkable shape, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the union claim and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The refused parts (ranking, single score, name of the winner) are removable without touching the shape; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove them. A sent claim can be superseded by the corrected packet, and no social primitive has to be withdrawn. The one retracted reading (Fig 13 applied to identities) is preserved, so the cost of undoing it is a paragraph of explanation, not a silence.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A discriminator that predicts the salient axis from the described residual on a held-out case would close the gap; a case of mobilization with no contact change and no opening would break the contact claim; a partition with zero residue after one lever would break the union claim; a working bondless mobilization would break the bond claim; an anchor that moved when a frame pushed on it would convert a verdict into a convention. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
