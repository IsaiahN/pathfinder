# The Tether's answer on identity mobilization

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the shape, `gap` on which identity wins in a given case. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A category such as ethnicity, region, religion or class predicts almost nothing political on its own. Yet the same category sometimes becomes a force that musters votes, protests and policy. The residual is that transition, indexed per identity slot and per intersection (Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*). It is a **union compound**, not four questions and not one question with four additive inputs (Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*).

## 2. The shape

1. **Unbundle before you search.** The four axes overlap. Fig 9 :54 *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* A class-only lever clears the region where class alone binds and leaves the intersections standing, because Fig 9 :57 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
2. **A category cannot leave its own closure.** Fig 6 :23 *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Mobilization begins at contact: an opposing group, an institution, a market shock, a state opening. Fig 11 :40 *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*
3. **The habitat supplies the resources and the opening.** Fig 11 :39 *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"*. Grievance plus resources are still insufficient without an opening. Fig 11 :43 *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*
4. **Bonds turn a category into a route.** Fig 12 :111 *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* Fig 12 :112 *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."* Mobilization needs a goal (comparison), a plan (sequence), a measure of progress (subtraction), and a refusal of the existing account (negation).
5. **Valence is relative to the question.** Fig 11 :49 *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :51 *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* No identity is inherently mobilizing in a fixed direction.
6. **The anchor is an outcome, not the group's account.** Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :41 *"agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"* while Fig 2 :40 says *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Internal consensus is a convention, efficient for coordination and disqualified as a verdict.
7. **Scale recurses.** Fig 4 :20 *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* A settled local cell becomes an atom for a national coalition: Fig 12 :104 *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."* A recording of one successful campaign cannot be replayed as a new success.
8. **The winner is not named.** Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 13 :134 *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*. No figure line selects ethnicity over class in a given election. That is a declared `gap`.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a standing list of mobilizing identities, and not a ranking of ethnic, regional, religious and class identities by inherent power (Fig 11 :51).
- Not a single "identity salience" score. The currencies do not add (Fig 12 :125 *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*).
- Not that a category alone is sufficient. The minimal group studies show that arbitrary categorization produces in-group favouritism; that is bias, not mobilization, and the difference is carried by the bonds in step 4.
- Not that the group's own telling is evidence. It is a convention (Fig 2 :41).
- Not that a particular identity is the hidden real driver behind the others. That is a name where the framework returns a shape.
- Not `grounded`. No survey, experiment or election was analysed here.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not another argument. From "The Hard Problem is About Source": an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

- **Discriminator test:** an instrument that predicts which axis becomes salient from the described residual alone, and does so on a held-out case. That would close the `gap` in step 8.
- **Contact test:** a case where contact with a differing frame changes nothing, or a case that mobilizes with no such contact. Either fires the F3 refuter.
- **Bond test:** a mobilization with no comparison, no sequence, no subtraction and no refusal. Fires F5.
- **Anchor test:** an outcome that changed when a frame pushed on it. Fires F7 and converts a supposed verdict into a convention.
- **Partition test:** one lever that clears every intersection with zero residue. Fires F1 and F9.
- Standing refuter from `01`: a standing list retaining the same valence across a changed residual, or a recording of one occasion crossing a scale membrane and still producing a new success.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The four identity words are the question's own vocabulary, a visible choice rather than nature's list. Fig 13 :134 permits a verdict of unreachability; Fig 8 :39 *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The answer stops at the limit of the available discriminators and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Indiscernibility is not identity.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not rank ethnic, regional, religious and class identities by how naturally they mobilize, and it does not offer a single salience score. It says the four are a union of overlapping conditions, so a lever aimed at one axis leaves the intersections standing. An identity becomes political not because of what the category is but because the category is brought into contact with a frame whose closure differs, inside a habitat that supplies resources and an opening, and because bonds arrive that the bare category lacks: a goal (a comparison), a plan (a sequence), progress (a subtraction) and a refusal of the existing account (a negation). Its valence is relative to the question, so no standing list of mobilizing identities survives a change of question. Success is settled by an outcome that does not move when the group's own account changes; internal agreement is a convention, not a verdict. The framework does not name which identity wins in a given place. It offers the shape any answer must have, refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close the remaining gap.
