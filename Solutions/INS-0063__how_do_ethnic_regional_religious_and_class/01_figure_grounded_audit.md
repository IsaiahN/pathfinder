## 0. The claim under audit, stated as an effect

The question as posed invites a list: name the mechanism by which an identity becomes political, then rank ethnic, regional, religious and class identities by how strongly each mobilizes. Two options:

- **A (list reading):** there is a standing set of mobilizing properties. An identity mobilizes to the degree it possesses them. This predicts that the same identity is mobilizing in the same direction wherever it appears.
- **B (shape reading):** there is no standing list. The four named axes are overlapping conditions in a union compound, and an identity mobilizes when its frame is brought into contact with a differing frame inside a habitat that supplies an opening, and when bonds arrive that the bare category lacks. The anchor is an outcome that does not move when the group's own account changes.

What each leads to: A produces a ranking that cannot survive a change of question, because *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* (Fig 11 :51). B produces a separator that can be checked from outside, at the price of not naming which identity will win in a particular place. The residual that must be explained is the transition itself: **a category that predicts nothing political becomes a force that musters action.** For slot `s` (an identity axis or an intersection of axes), `R_s` is the mobilization that the category alone fails to predict.

I bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term before running anything: *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM."* (Fig 5 :65). The bound term is: **"a route from a category to a political outcome, composed of contact, habitat, bonds and an anchor."** That term is now refusable by the same test as everything else.

## 1. Domain census, before any figure line is chosen

**R by arity, symmetry and scale.** Arity: at least four named axes (ethnic, regional, religious, class), plus their intersections, so R is multivariate and indexed per slot. Fig 1 :19 states *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Symmetry: the axes are not interchangeable. Which one is salient flips with the question, so the relation is ordered in context, not symmetric in a category list. Scale: individual, local association, national institution, diaspora; the same loop recurs with different actors. "The kernel is the part that does not change with size": *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* (Fig 4 :20).

**One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor.**

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | nameable anchor? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1, the identity frame | individuals, narratives, categories, symbols | shared meaning, belonging, agreement | none internal; *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"* (Fig 10 :18) | **No.** Not a domain yet until it meets a ground. |
| D2, the habitat and material conditions | institutions, markets, demography, territory, elites, shocks | contact, resources, openings | census, livelihood, an opening that does not move with the group's story | Yes |
| D3, the political and organizational frame | movements, parties, brokers, leaders, voters | votes, protests, policy, organization | an outcome that settles whether the claim landed (Fig 2 :29) | Yes |
| D4, the research and verification frame | researchers, reviewers, datasets | evidence and agreement | the ground, never agreement | Yes, in principle; in this run it is shared, see below |

D1 being anchorless is a finding, not an omission. A frame with no nameable anchor is not yet a domain that can settle anything. It becomes one only through contact with D2 and D3. Treating internal agreement as if it were an anchor is the named collapse in Fig 2 :13-15: *"no anchor; each updates on the others... they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence."*

**Partition or union?** Union. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Ethnic, regional, religious and class categories overlap in the same people; no axis is exclusive of the others. Fig 9 :57 adds: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* The compound is categorically different from either condition alone. This is why a single summed score is wrong: it adds currencies that do not add. Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* The order is:
1. Describe R in effect terms before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).
2. Unbundle the union before searching inside one axis (Fig 9 :54).
3. Enumerate the habitat before picking a lever (Fig 11 :43).
4. Bring the frame into contact with a differing closure (Fig 6 :23).
5. Add the bonds that make a route stateable (Fig 12 :111).
6. Let the ground settle; only settled terms can be composed over at the next scale (Fig 12 :104).

A reading taken below step 3 is not a weak reading of step 6. It is a reading of nothing: *"A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can."* (Fig 3 :22).

**Strain declared at the census.** There is a standing temptation to call an ethnic or class identity a "state" in the six-term substrate and a party or institution a "structure", then apply Fig 13 :75's ban on direct state-state bonds. I decline that mapping. Fig 13's six terms are the minimum physical substrate, with a specific load-bearing argument in thermodynamics (Fig 13 :49-50). Social identities are not those terms. Forcing them would be a strain, and the rule is to re-census rather than to soften the reading. The social claim is governed by the union, habitat, contact, bond and anchor figures below. The strain is preserved in section 7 and re-checked in `11_audit.md`.

## 3. Findings

### F1: The four axes are a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition and not an additive score (triangulated)

Fig 9 :54 states *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :55 states *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57 states *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

A class-only lever clears people for whom class is the only binding condition. It leaves the ethnic-class and religious-regional intersections standing, because the second condition still binds. A summed "identity salience" variable merges the currencies before asking which condition holds. Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*

**Triangulation:** the figure route (Fig 9 union logic) is low overlap with the legal-studies route (Crenshaw 1989, opened via the Intersectionality page: *"Intersectionality opposes analytical systems that treat each axis of oppression in isolation"*) and with the essay route (Library of Babel: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"*). Three different objects, one shape.

**Refuter:** show that the four axes form a mutually exclusive and exhaustive partition, or exhibit one lever that demonstrably clears every intersection. DeGraffenreid v. General Motors is a case where a single-axis court decision failed at precisely the intersection, so the burden is on the partition claim.

### F2: Identity agreement is a convention, not a verdict (triangulated)

Fig 2 :41 states *"agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"* and Fig 2 :40 states *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Fig 10 :18 states *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"*. Fig 10 :47 states *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*.

An identity group's internal consensus is efficient for coordination. It is exactly the wrong instrument for deciding whether a political claim is true, because the members share the evidence pool that produced the consensus. When the group's own telling is used as the measure of its success, the anchor updates on the frame and the system enters the coherent drift of Fig 2 :13-15. Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*

**Triangulation:** Fig 2 and Fig 10 are two separate figures with different offices (alignment and ground maintenance). Social identity theory adds a third, lower-overlap witness: discrimination and conflict are *"anticipated only in a limited set of circumstances"*, and the effects of identity variables are *"powerfully determined by the previous social, economic, and political processes"*. The group's self-description is not the ground.

**Refuter:** exhibit a group whose internal consensus tracks an external, non-updating measure without importing that measure from outside the group, and show the tracking survives when the group's preferred story changes.

### F3: Mobilization requires contact with a frame whose closure differs (single-source on the figure side, corroborated)

Fig 6 :23 states *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 11 :39 states *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"* and Fig 11 :40 states *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

An identity category held alone is a frame with a fixed closure. It does not generate a political programme from its own vocabulary. The move happens at contact: an opposing group, a state, a market shock, an institution that opens or closes a channel. This is why the same category is quiet in one setting and mobilized in another. Resource mobilization supplies the low-overlap corroboration: *"grievances are not sufficient to explain creation of social movements"*; political opportunity adds the opening condition: *"When there are no political opportunities, simply having grievances and resources is not enough."*

Marked `single-source` on the figure side: only Fig 6/Fig 11 govern the contact claim directly. The world sources corroborate but do not upgrade the figure verdict.

**Refuter:** find an identity that becomes politically mobilized with no change in contact with any differing frame, no resource inflow and no opening. If one exists, the closure claim is false.

### F4: Valence is residual-relative; there is no standing list of mobilizing identities (triangulated)

Fig 11 :49 states *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :50 states *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* Fig 11 :51 states *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*

A religious identity can be a mobilizing resource in one dispute and an inert classification in another. Class can unify in one context and divide in the next. The question, not the identity, selects what counts. This kills option A directly, and it explains why a ranking of identities by inherent mobilizing power cannot be derived.

**Triangulation:** Fig 11's valence law and the political opportunity source are low overlap (habitat enumeration versus political-sociology openings); they agree that context selects the lever. The minimal group result creates a genuine tension: arbitrary categorization alone produces in-group favouritism. That is a result about bias, not about mobilization, and the difference is carried by F5. The tension is recorded in `11_audit.md` rather than smoothed.

**Refuter:** produce a standing list of identities that mobilize in the same direction across every residual, with the same valence in a setting where the question has changed.

### F5: A bare category cannot name a route; mobilization needs bonds (triangulated)

Fig 12 :111 states *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* Fig 12 :112 states *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."* The Operators table :51 states *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"* and :58 states *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*.

To become political, a category must acquire:
- a **comparison** (a goal: what the group wants relative to what it has);
- a **sequence** (a plan: what happens first, what depends on what);
- a **subtraction** (progress: how far along, what remains);
- a **negation** (a refusal of the current account: the grievance). Negation is not one of the seven bonds; it is a unary operator, and that is why a vocabulary lacking it can only accumulate.

Resource mobilization names the same two structures empirically: *"diagnostic"* frames identify sources of blame (negation), *"prognostic"* frames *"lay out a plan of attack"* (sequence). Identity politics supplies a third witness: identity is used *"as a tool to frame political claims"*, which is a bond-bearing use, not a bare label.

**Refuter:** exhibit a political mobilization with no comparison, no sequence, no subtraction and no refusal of the existing account. A spontaneous riot may have a negation without a plan; that is a partial case and the claim must then be narrowed to the kind of mobilization being explained. I state that narrowing now, before the conclusion.

### F6: Scale recursion: local cells settle before national composition (single-source)

Fig 4 :20 states *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* Fig 4 :22 states *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Fig 4 :25 warns that *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Fig 12 :104 states *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."*

A local organization settled against its own ground becomes an atom for a national coalition. The method of organizing crosses up; the record of one successful campaign does not. Fig 12 :106: *"Nothing composes over what was never admitted as a part."*

This is a structural claim about scale, not a claim that every national movement grew from local cells. It is marked `single-source` because only Fig 4 and Fig 12 bear directly and no world source was opened that settles the composition order.

**Refuter:** show a national movement that composes over an unsettled local cell, or show that a recording of one occasion crossed the scale membrane and still produced a new success rather than a replay.

### F7: The ground must be an outcome that does not update on the group's telling (triangulated)

Fig 2 :29 defines the anchor: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :33 states *"The anchor must not update."* Fig 7 :35 states *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 8 :42 states *"narrowing gets you close, the ground decides"*.

For political mobilization the candidate anchors are an election result, a policy change, a census count, a protest turnout, a legal ruling. Each is legitimate only if it is constitutively about the question and does not move when a frame pushes on it. Fig 2 :36: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* A poll of group sentiment is not such an anchor: it is another frame reading the same pool.

**Triangulation:** Fig 2 (alignment), Fig 7 (room chain) and Fig 8 (narrowing) are three figures with different offices, so this is triangulated on the figure side. Horizontal inequality provides lower-overlap corroboration that material group conditions, not self-report, predict conflict mobilization.

**Refuter:** show an anchor that changed its answer when a frame pushed on it, so that what was presented as a verdict was a convention; or show a question for which no non-updating outcome exists, in which case the claim is withdrawn for that domain rather than patched.

### F8: Which identity wins is a `gap`, not silence (single-source, declared)

Fig 9 :32 states *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 13 :134 states *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"* and :135 states *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."* Fig 8 :39 states *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

No figure line selects ethnic over class, or religious over regional, for a given time and place. The framework returns the shape of the compound and the conditions under which a slot becomes live. The selection itself needs an instrument that discriminates among sources, which is exactly what *The Hard Problem is About Source* says a spectrometer supplied in the colour case: *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy"*.

This is a `gap` of the **unreachable-at-this-budget** kind, not a proof of underivability in principle. The world literature contains candidate discriminators (political opportunity measures, horizontal inequality indices), but none was run here. Importing one now would add an atom without a source gate and shadow test (Fig 6 :12-13, Fig 8 :50-51).

**Refuter for the gap:** name an instrument that, from a described residual alone, predicts which axis becomes salient in a new case, and show it on a case held out from the one that motivated it. Until then, `gap` stands.

### F9: The union must be unbundled before a lever is applied (single-source)

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :55: *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing"*. Fig 3 :19 states *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*

Practical consequence: a policy that addresses only class grievance leaves the ethnic-class intersection live; a mobilization that addresses only religious identity leaves the regional-religious intersection live. The residue after a single lever is smaller and categorically different, which is the second-order value Fig 9 :57 names.

**Refuter:** show a case where one lever reliably clears all intersections, with a measured residue, not merely a smaller pile.

### F10: The research frame must be declared as a shared evidence pool (single-source, honesty finding)

Fig 2 :31 states that independence is *"how little they draw on the same evidence"*. Fig 2 :40 states that agreement is worthless *"when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Fig 10 :59 states *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"* and :60 states *"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it."*

This primary derivation and the second seat in `11_audit.md` read the same figures, the same seven world pages, the same essays and the same notes. Their agreement is internal consistency, not corroboration. The political science and sociology sources also share one broad pool. I declare both rather than count either twice.

**Refuter:** provide a genuinely independent frame, that is, one that does not draw on the same evidence and whose verdict could differ, and show that it still returns the same shape. A source that merely quotes the same review is not that frame.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written here before the conclusion, per Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Partition:** the four axes are shown to be mutually exclusive and exhaustive, or one lever clears every intersection with a measured residue of zero.
2. **No contact:** an identity becomes politically mobilized with no change in contact with any differing frame, no resource inflow and no opening.
3. **No bonds:** a mobilization is exhibited with no comparison, no sequence, no subtraction and no refusal of the existing account, after allowing for the narrower riot case stated in F5.
4. **Standing list:** a list of identities is shown to retain the same valence across a genuine change of residual.
5. **Updating anchor:** the anchor used to declare success changes when a frame pushes on it.
6. **Scale replay:** a recording of one occasion crosses a scale membrane and produces a new success, rather than a replay.
7. **Discriminator found:** an instrument predicts which axis becomes salient from the residual alone, on a held-out case, which would close F8 and require the gap to be withdrawn.

If any fires, the affected finding is withdrawn and the census is rerun. Until one is run, no part of this folder may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Send the union framing and the refuter, not a ranking of identities (F1, F4).
2. Ask for a failure condition before showing any theory (Fig 10 :52).
3. Treat group self-report as a convention and insist on an outcome anchor (F2, F7).
4. Keep the shape-not-a-name limit: do not predict which identity wins in a named place (F8).
5. Unbundle before proposing a lever (F9).
6. Declare the shared evidence pool and the author's stake (F10).
7. Carry methods, not recordings, into the next question with a similar residual (Fig 4 :22).

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **One question or four?** Union, by Fig 9 :54-57; unbundle before search.
2. **Can the category do it alone?** No. Fig 6 :23 needs contact with a differing closure; Fig 11 :39 needs habitat.
3. **What converts category to action?** Bonds: comparison, sequence, subtraction, negation (Fig 12 :111-112; Operators :51, :58).
4. **What counts as success?** An outcome anchor that does not update (Fig 2 :29, :33, :36).
5. **Does scale change the loop?** The kernel is fixed; actors, currency and anchor change (Fig 4 :20); settled cells become atoms (Fig 12 :104).
6. **Can the slot be named?** No. Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134-135. A discriminator is owed, not invented.
7. **Route of the run:** describe R (Fig 9 :27-28), bind the shape as an ordinary term (Fig 5 :65), unbundle (Fig 9 :54), enumerate habitat (Fig 11 :43), cross (Fig 6 :23), bond (Fig 12), settle (Fig 2).

### Updated verdict

The shape survives: a union of overlapping identity conditions, mobilized through differing-closure contact in a habitat, bonded into a route, and settled by an outcome anchor that does not move with the group's account. The standing list, the single summed score, and the prediction of which identity wins are refused. After re-census, one strain remains live (minimal group categorization versus mobilization, resolved by scope, not by force) and one strain is retracted and preserved (the six-term physical substrate applied to identities). No figure line returns a country-specific verdict. `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.
