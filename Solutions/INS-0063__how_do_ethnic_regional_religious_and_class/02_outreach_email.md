# Outreach draft: check the output before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** this letter carries a `specified`, ungrounded claim. Do not present it as a finding about any named country or group. If the stage-1 output does not survive the reader's check, there is nothing else to show them.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about how identities become political, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing further to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "how do ethnic, regional, religious and class identities become politically mobilized?" is not four questions and is not one question with four additive inputs. It is a **union of overlapping conditions**. A lever that addresses only one axis clears the region where only that axis is in play and leaves the intersections standing. So a class-only account, an ethnic-only account or a single summed "identity salience" score each describe a real subset and miss the compound.
>
> On this reading, an identity does not mobilize because of what the category is. It mobilizes when four things come together:
> 1. **Contact with a frame whose closure differs.** An identity frame on its own cannot leave its own closure; it changes only on contact with an opposing group, an institution, a market or a state.
> 2. **A habitat that supplies resources and an opening.** Grievance plus resources are still not enough if there is no opening. The same category is quiet in one setting and mobilized in another.
> 3. **Bonds the bare category lacks.** A category can name a state of affairs; it cannot by itself name a route. Mobilization needs a goal (a comparison), a plan (a sequence), and a refusal (a negation) of the current account.
> 4. **An outcome that does not update on the group's own telling.** Group agreement is a convention, efficient for coordination. It is not a verdict about the world.
>
> The valence of any axis is relative to the question being asked: the same identity can be a mobilizing resource in one dispute and inert in another. No figure line names which axis wins in a given case, so I do not claim one. I claim only the shape that any candidate explanation would have to have.
>
> **What would refute this:**
> 1. Show that the four axes form a partition, that is, mutually exclusive and exhaustive categories, or exhibit one lever that clears every intersection.
> 2. Show an identity becoming politically mobilized with no change in contact with any differing frame and no habitat resource or opening.
> 3. Exhibit a mobilization with no comparison, no sequence and no refusal of the existing account.
> 4. Produce a standing list of identities that mobilize in the same direction regardless of the question asked.
> 5. Show an anchor that changed its answer when a frame pushed on it, so that what looked like a verdict was a convention.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical figures README: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Eight essays and the formula, in `05_what_shape_means.md` and `04_links_and_distribution.md`
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** write down what an answer to your own question would look like, bind it as an ordinary term, and state what would refute it before you run anything. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

| Output first, theory second | *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* (Fig 10 :52) and *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"* (Fig 9 :27-28). |

| Pre-registered refuter | *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device"* (Seat Map :287). |

| Union, not partition | *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* (Fig 9 :55). |
| No standing list of identities | *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* (Fig 11 :51). |
| Shape, not a name | *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (Fig 9 :32). The letter never says which axis wins. |
| Links only after the verdict | *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* (Fig 4 :22). Sending the architecture first sends a recording rather than a generator. |
