## DERIVATION

### Q1: Union or partition?

- Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- Fig 9 :57 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
- The four axes overlap in the same people; none is exclusive of the others. Partition is therefore refused. The object is a union compound with its own loop, not four lists to be added.
- **Falsifier:** a partition shown to be mutually exclusive and exhaustive, or a demonstrated zero residue after one lever.

### Q2: Are the axes isomers over a fixed atom set?

- Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :5 *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
- Take a fixed set of atoms, for example `{ethnic, regional, religious, class, institution, territory, resource, outcome}`. Two cases can hold exactly these atoms and differ only in which axis is bonded to the institution and which axis is negated as the grievance. By Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**: same ingredients, different arrangement, different political substance. A demographic table listing the ingredients cannot tell them apart; the bond carries the difference.
- The isomer claim is therefore a claim about **type**, not degree. Two movements may have identical demographic composition and behave inversely, because one bonds the institution to a comparison and the other negates it. Fig 12 :96: *"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart."*
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two cases with the same atom set and the same bond arrangement nevertheless differ in political behaviour, the isomer claim has no purchase and the difference must come from an imported atom (a shock, a leader, an external opening). That is an import and needs provenance; it cannot be smuggled in as an arrangement.

### Q3: Or are they a functional group instead?

- Fig 12 :123 *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- If the claim is weaker, namely that each axis is a member of a common pattern *"category bonded to institution, negated as grievance"*, then the four axes are members of a **functional group**, differing only in the qualifier that says which member. The group fixes the kind (identity-based political claim); the qualifier fixes which identity.
- Which reading applies is not decided by the figure alone. It depends on whether we hold the full atom set fixed (isomer) or hold only the pattern fixed (functional group). Both are legitimate at different levels. Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."*
- This is a level claim, not a factual claim about a country. It is `specified`, unmeasured.

### Q4: What is the order of the union?

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* The correct order is:

1. **Describe the residual** before choosing a frame: Fig 9 :27-28 *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."*
2. **Unbundle the union**: Fig 9 :54 *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :55 explains why: a single lever clears the region where one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.
3. **Enumerate the habitat**: Fig 11 :43 *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 11 :48 *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."*
4. **Cross to a differing closure**: Fig 6 :23 *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*
5. **Add the bonds**: Fig 12 :111 *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."*
6. **Settle at an anchor**: Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*

## CONCLUSION

The four axes are **not a partition**. Over a fixed atom set they are **isomers** under Fig 12 :117, differing in which bond attaches which atom and in what is negated; over a fixed pattern they are a **functional group** under Fig 12 :123. The order of the union is describe, unbundle, enumerate, cross, bond, settle. The physical six-term substrate does not license naming an identity a "state"; that first reading is retracted and preserved. The whole derivation is `specified`, unmeasured, and cannot be promoted to `grounded` without an outcome anchor that does not move with the group's account.
