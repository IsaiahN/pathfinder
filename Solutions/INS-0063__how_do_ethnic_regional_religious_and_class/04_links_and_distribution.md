## Wider literature that bears on the residual

1. **Legal studies and intersectionality.** Crenshaw 1989 is the direct low-overlap witness for the union claim. The DeGraffenreid case is the plainest example: the court weighed race and sex separately and dismissed a claim that only existed at their intersection. That is Fig 9 :55-57 in a courtroom.
2. **Experimental social psychology.** The minimal group studies show that the thinnest possible category produces in-group favouritism. That is a warning against my own claim: categorization alone may be sufficient for bias, so it cannot be sufficient for *mobilization*. The distinction is what the bonds and habitat conditions in `01` supply. This source partly strains the figure verdict and the strain is recorded in `11_audit.md`.
3. **Sociology of social movements.** Resource mobilization and political opportunity both say that grievance and identity are not sufficient. McCarthy and Zald say access to resources is the crucial factor; Eisinger and Tarrow say an opening is required. Both corroborate the habitat and contact conditions.
4. **Political theory.** Butler's "doer behind the deed" line corroborates the claim that the political actor is constituted in the deed rather than being a pre-existing identity that then acts. Crenshaw's intragroup-differences critique corroborates the union reading from a second, opposing direction.
5. **Development economics and conflict.** Horizontal inequality makes the material condition explicit and residual-relative: group-level inequality raises conflict risk, but the same group is not mobilized in every setting.

Overlap: the five political science and sociology Wikipedia pages share a broad social-science evidence pool and are counted as one witness, not five. The experimental psychology and legal studies pages are lower overlap. The three essays share the framework author's pool completely and carry no independent witness weight.
