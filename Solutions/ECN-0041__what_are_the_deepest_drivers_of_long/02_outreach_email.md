# Outreach draft: check the shape before you decide whether the framing is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until you have read `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §5 and §6 and accept that the literal single-driver question is reported as `gap`. The email must not claim to have found the answer.

---

**Subject:** One checkable shape about growth drivers, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output about the long-run growth question that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question as an effect.** Which candidate driver, when pushed, leaves a growth residual that persists and does not trend down, and whose failure cannot be repaired by pushing the others? That makes "deepest" a position in a dependency chain, not an importance rank.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** Three constraints and one honest gap.
>
> 1. The named candidates form a **union** of overlapping conditions, not a partition. Each works well on a different subset. The instruction this gives is "split rather than search", not "pick the winner".
> 2. Accumulation of a reproducible input inside a closed set can change the level and the transition speed. By the closure rule it cannot, on its own, set the long-run rate. That is a structural claim, not a statement that capital or schooling do not matter.
> 3. Anything that could set the long-run rate must have one of two shapes: an import from a frame whose closure differs (the shape that ideas have, because using an idea does not consume it), or a habitat condition that changes which actors are in contact with the ground (the shape that rules and incentives have).
> 4. **Gap:** the framework does not name a single deepest driver. It returns a kind of thing, never the answer itself. Any claim that it has named one is my addition, not a derivation.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that technology is the answer and institutions are not, that institutions are the answer and technology is not, that capital and education are irrelevant, or that any of this is settled by data I have not read. The names "technology" and "institutions" are bindings from your field onto the shapes, recorded as such. Agreement between schools that share an evidence pool is one witness, not several.
>
> **What would refute it, written before I ran this:**
> 1. a sustained rise in per-capita growth generated entirely inside the closure of a reproducible factor, with no import and no change in contact;
> 2. one candidate carrying the persistent residual across independent subsets and closures, so the union collapses to a partition;
> 3. a measurement showing the residual is fully recoverable from a single global aggregate, so per-slot tracking is unnecessary;
> 4. a figure re-census that restores a legitimate single-root derivation.
>
> If any one holds, I retract the shape. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send the sources. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority. Ask and I will send the links.
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the formula is public. Run it on a growth question you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
