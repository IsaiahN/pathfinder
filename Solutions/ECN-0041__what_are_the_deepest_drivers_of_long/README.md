## The question restated as an effect

The question asks which candidate sits at the bottom of the causal chain that produces persistent growth in output per person. In effect terms: push on candidate X (more capital, more schooling, a new technology, a different rule set) and ask which push leaves a growth residual that *persists and does not trend down*. The candidate whose removal or failure cannot be rescued by improving the others is the "deepest" one. This is an ordering claim about a dependency chain, not a ranking by importance (Fig 3).

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run: a shape that every viable candidate driver must have, plus the names of the domains where the shape applies, plus a refuter that would fire if the shape were wrong. The phrase "shape, not a name" is recorded in `05_what_shape_means.md` and `06_isomers_and_order.md`. Binding it as a term is what lets the pre-registered refuter test it rather than merely prefer it.
