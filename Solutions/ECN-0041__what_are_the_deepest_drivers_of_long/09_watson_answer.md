The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."*) it cannot settle the question it reports on. No growth measurement was taken here.

## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the claim that long-run economic growth has a single deepest driver among technology, capital, education and institutions, or something else. Option A: name a single winner and rank the rest below it. Option B: keep the figure-derived shape, unbundle the candidate set, and report the single-driver slot as `gap`. What each leads to: A delivers a `general`-mode verdict where only a `specified` shape is licensed, ignores that each candidate works on a different subset, and selects a favourite driver before describing the residual. B delivers checkable constraints, four refuters written in advance, and an open slot until slot-level ground settles which link binds.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Persistent differences in real output per person across countries and eras, including the escape from Malthusian stagnation, that do not trend down when one reproducible input is increased. Arity: four named candidates plus an open slot. Symmetry: the candidates are not interchangeable (stocks, a flow of nonrival knowledge, a set of rules). Scale: firm, sector, nation, centuries. Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
- **D1, accumulation frame.** Actors: savers, investors, firms, machines, workers with embodied skills. Currency: investment, capital stock, output. Anchor: measured output and market prices. Closed frame: composition inside it changes the level and transition speed, not the long-run rate.
- **D2, ideas / technology frame.** Actors: inventors, researchers, firms, the pool of ideas. Currency: nonrival knowledge, TFP, spillovers. Anchor: TFP and patent-citation measurement. Import frame: an atom from a closure that differs.
- **D3, habitat / institutions frame.** Actors: rules, property rights, the state, markets, networks. Currency: incentives and constraints, contact between agents and the ground. Anchor: market outcomes and natural experiments. Habitat, not atom.
- **D4, verification frame.** Actors: expert readers, data, methods. Currency: residuals and agreement. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all four domains draw on one evidence pool, so D4 independence is zero.
- **Partition or union. Union.** Fig 9 :54 *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- **Order by dependency.** Split the union, check what is inside each closure, check what crosses a closure, check what changes contact, then measure the binding link per slot (Fig 3 :20-21).

Strain test applied: an earlier attempt to map capital, institutions, education and technology onto energy, network, spectrum and gradient in Fig 13 forced within-kind bonds and was retracted after re-census. Fig 13 is used only for its generic closure statement. No live strain remains; the Fig 12 catalyst / cycle / functional group analogues are declared `general` and are not allowed to hand a verdict.

World sources are corroboration only. Overlap is declared rather than hidden. All were opened 2026-11-23.

- **Field: growth theory / macroeconomics.** Robert M. Solow, Prize Lecture, Nobel Foundation, 1987, printed 1992. Quote: *"the permanent rate of growth of output per unit of labor input is independent of the saving (investment) rate and depends entirely on the rate of technological progress in the broadest sense."* Prediction: accumulation alone cannot set the long-run rate; institutions show up as *"important institutional peculiarities"* and a possible *"third factor"*. Corroborates F2, F3, F4 and F1. Overlap: independent of the framework, but shares the growth-accounting pool with the other economics sources.
- **Field: institutional economics.** Daron Acemoglu, Simon Johnson and James Robinson, NBER WP 10481, 2004. Quote: *"differences in economic institutions are the fundamental cause of differences in economic development."* Prediction: rules and incentives are a habitat condition that changes outcomes. Corroborates F4. Overlap: different subfield from Solow, but shares the development-economics evidence pool.
- **Field: economics of ideas.** Charles I. Jones, NBER WP 10767, 2004. Quote: *"Ideas are different from nearly all other economic goods in that they are nonrivalrous."* Prediction: an idea can cross a closure without being consumed, which is the D2 shape. Corroborates F3. Overlap: shares the growth literature with Solow.
- **Field: innovation and network economics.** Esteban Ortiz-Ospina, Our World in Data, 2019. - **Field: economic history.** Max Roser, Our World in Data, 2020. ## VERDICT (from the figures)

The literal question receives `gap`: the figures do not name a single deepest driver and Fig 9 :32 forbids inventing one. What is licensed is a `specified` shape, triangulated across Fig 9, Fig 6, Fig 8 and Fig 11: the candidates are a union of overlapping conditions, so split rather than search; pure accumulation inside a closure cannot set the long-run rate; any driver that could set it must have the shape of an import from a different closure and of a habitat that changes contact; and the residual must be tracked per slot. Technology and institutions are the world's best bindings for those two shapes, not figure-licensed winners. Capital and education set levels and transitions. Nothing is `grounded`.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. If we wrongly treat the set as a partition, budgets land on a factor that cannot rescue a broken upstream link (Fig 3 :20). If we pick a favourite driver and then find evidence for it, we commit the inversion Fig 9 :27-28 forbids. The larger avoidable cost is spending outside readers' independence by sending a conclusion instead of a refuter (Fig 10 :42); Fig 2 :42 prices that, *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*
- **undoable:** yes. The shape is a set of constraints, so narrowing or withdrawing it requires no primitive to be retracted. The bindings of ideas and institutions to their shapes are provenanced and can be replaced without touching the derivation. A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A slot-level measurement showing which domain carries a persistent residual would narrow the `gap`; a sustained growth episode with no import and no change in contact would break the closure verdict; one candidate carrying the residual across independent subsets would collapse the union to a partition; a figure re-census licensing the six-term mapping would reverse the retracted strain. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
