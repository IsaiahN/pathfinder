## RESIDUAL

Three questions are owed before any driver can be called deepest:

1. Is the candidate set a partition (one winner) or a union (overlapping conditions)?
2. In what dependency order must the domains be settled?
3. Do the structural analogues in Fig 12 (catalyst, cycle, functional group, isomer) name anything here, or are they a strained mapping that must be declined?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Union or partition?

- Fig 9 :4-5: *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*
- Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- Fig 9 :56-57: *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*
- The four candidates each work on a different subset: D1 on levels and transitions, D2 on nonrival productivity shifts, D3 on which actors reach the ground, D4 on whether any verdict is admissible. That is the Fig 9 signature.
- **Result: union.** The question "which single driver is deepest" is a bundled question that must be unbundled before it is searched. Triangulated (Fig 9 :4-5 and :54-57, with Solow's "third factor" passage as a world witness).

### Q2 - What order?

- Fig 3 :20-21: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*
- Fig 3 :22-23: *"A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can. Which is why a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing."*
- Dependency order for ECN-0041: (a) split the union (Fig 9 :54); (b) decide what is inside the closure of each domain (Fig 6 :21); (c) decide what crosses a closure (Fig 6 :23); (d) decide what changes contact (Fig 11 :40); (e) only then ask which link is binding in a given slot.
- **Result: order is dependency, not importance.** A claimed driver ranked first by size of coefficient is a rank by importance and can sit above the break. This is `general` structural guidance until a slot-level instrument names the binding link.

### Q3 - Import and habitat: different kinds or one?

- Fig 6 :23 governs the closure: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* The moving thing is an atom.
- Fig 11 :40 governs contact: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* The moving thing is the habitat.
- Fig 10 :68-69: *"Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between. That is why there is no top rung and no regress: a generator crosses, an instance does not, and the level it lands on remakes it."*
- **Result: different kinds.** Technology and institutions are not rival names for one slot. They are an atom-shape and a habitat-shape. This is the reason F1 calls the question a union rather than merely an unresolved ranking.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a rule change can be shown to add an atom to the production closure in the same sense that a new idea does, the two-kind split collapses and import and habitat are one domain.

### Q4 - Do the Fig 12 structures name anything here?

- Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :119: *"A part that survives its own use."* (catalyst)
- Fig 12 :121: *"The output re-enters as input."* (cycle)
- Fig 12 :123: *"A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome."* (functional group)
- Tempting mapping: institutions as a catalyst (rules that persist and change the rate without being consumed by one transaction); capital accumulation as a cycle (output re-enters as investment); a technology bundle as a functional group (the presence of a general-purpose technique fixes what follows).
- **Re-census:** these are analogues from chemistry and composition theory applied to economics. They do not share an anchor with the growth domains, and Fig 12 :114 warns that what was taken for a dictionary is a grammar. The mapping is retained as `general` structure only. It is not counted as a second witness for F3 or F4, and it is not allowed to hand a verdict (Fig 9 :60, *"Never let a filter hand you a verdict"*).
- On isomer specifically: the four domains do not share one atom set, so they are not isomers under Fig 12 :117. They are different substances with different atoms, related by a union. Claiming isomerism would repeat the flat-formula error the figure names.

### Q5 - What about the six-term substrate?

- Fig 13 :115: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."*
- The earlier attempt to map capital to energy, institutions to network, education to spectrum and technology to gradient was retracted in `01` §5. It forced within-kind bonds and imported economic categories into a physical composition rule.
- What survives is the generic point: a closed set plus non-exhausting arrangements is why an import is owed. That is the shape D2 fits. It is not a derivation of "technology" as a name.

## CONCLUSION

The candidate set is a **union** of overlapping conditions, not a partition with one deepest member. The correct order is **dependency**: unbundle, check closure, check crossing, check contact, then measure the binding link per slot. Import and habitat are **different kinds**, so the technology versus institutions debate is a category confusion as much as a disagreement. The Fig 12 structures are **analogues only** and were declined as verdict-bearing. This rescues the checkable part of the question: a shape and a route, with a `gap` where the figures return no name. `06` contributes no new primitive, no import that moves a wall, and no claim that any driver has been named.
