# The Tether's answer on the deepest drivers of long-run growth

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the shape-not-a-name rule, `gap` on the literal single-driver question. Not `grounded`.** A simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §8), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Persistent differences in real output per person across countries and eras, including the escape from Malthusian stagnation, that do not trend down when a single reproducible input is increased. The question asks which candidate sits at the bottom of a dependency chain (Fig 3), not which has the largest coefficient. The residual is per slot: Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*

## 2. The answer, in four parts

1. **The candidates form a union, not a partition.** Fig 9 :4-5 *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* Fig 9 :54 *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Each candidate works on a different subset. The persistent disagreement across schools is the signature of overlapping conditions, not of a single root nobody has found.

2. **Pure accumulation cannot set the long-run rate.** Fig 6 :21 *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* Fig 8 :32 *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."* Capital and education-as-embodied-skill change levels and transition speeds. This is not a claim that they are unimportant; Solow's own lecture says investment affects embodiment and intermediate-run growth, and Denison's decomposition gives education a substantial share (Solow, 1987/1992).

3. **Anything that could set the long-run rate must have one of two shapes.** *An import from a frame whose closure differs*: Fig 6 :23 *"The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Ideas have this shape because using one does not consume it (Jones, 2004). *A habitat condition that changes contact*: Fig 11 :40 *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Rules and incentives have this shape (AJR, 2004). These are two kinds of thing, not rival names for one slot (Fig 10 :68-69).

4. **The single-driver slot stays open.** Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* The framework returns a shape. Calling that shape "technology" or "institutions" is a binding from an economics frame, provenanced as such, not a verdict the figures deliver. The literal question therefore ends in `gap`, reported plainly rather than filled with a favourite name.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "technology is the answer". Solow's and Jones's evidence makes that the strongest world-side candidate, but the figures return a shape.
- Not "institutions are the real answer". AJR's evidence makes that the strongest habitat-side candidate, but F1 shows the two are a union.
- Not "capital and education do not matter". They cannot set the long-run rate inside the closure; they do set levels and transitions.
- Not a single global coefficient that selects a winner. Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
- Not `grounded`. No slot-level measurement was taken in this derivation.
- Not a derivation of one school from another. No new primitive was minted.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A slot-level discriminator, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument reading green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. The corresponding tests here are:

- **Bindings per slot:** in a given country and era, which of the four domains has a residual that persists and does not trend down when the others are pushed? Fig 3 :27 *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
- **Closure test:** does a sustained per-capita growth episode occur with no import and no change in contact? If yes, F2 breaks.
- **Union test:** does one candidate carry the residual across independent subsets and closures? If yes, F1 breaks and the question becomes a partition.
- **Per-slot test:** is the signal recoverable from a single pooled aggregate? If yes, F6 breaks.

Standing falsifiers from §6 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are unchanged and were written before the conclusion.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable in principle.** The limits here are the ones the figures name: a frame cannot return a name where it holds only a shape (Fig 9 :32), and a search that finds nothing is not a proof of absence (Fig 8 :39 *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*). The four domains were searched at a modest budget, and the three failed source fetches in `04` are recorded rather than hidden, so this is `unreachable at this budget` for a name and `specified` for the shape. Agreement among the sources is one evidence pool, not several witnesses (Fig 2 :40). The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not pick a winner among technology, capital, education and institutions. It says the question is a union of overlapping conditions, so it must be split before it is searched: capital and schooling change levels and transitions, ideas are an import from a different closure, and institutions are a habitat that changes who reaches the ground. Pure accumulation inside a closed set cannot set the long-run rate. Anything that could set it must have one of those two shapes, but the framework returns a kind of thing, never the answer itself, so the single-driver slot stays open and the literal question is reported as a gap. What it offers is the shape any driver must have, four refuters written in advance, and the slot-level instruments that would close it. The ground decides which link binds in a given place and era; agreement among schools does not.
