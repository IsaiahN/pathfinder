# Isomers, identity and order in the supply question

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The supply claim uses three words that need separating: "organic compounds", "building blocks" and "delivered". Are delivered molecules the same substances as biological ones, merely similar, or something whose difference is carried by provenance rather than structure? And in what order must the gates be checked?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: the molecular species reported in carbonaceous chondrites (amino acids, nucleobases, sugars, carboxylic acids, HMT, insoluble organic matter). Bonds held: the seven of Fig 12, including ≡ identity, − subtraction and ⋛ comparison, plus ¬ negation. No new primitive is available without import. Two frames are in contact: the cosmochemistry frame (which measures sources) and the prebiotic-chemistry frame (which measures pathways).

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Same molecule, different source: identity or isomer?

- Operators table :24-26: identity ≡ is "two names, one referent", with the test "substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No."
- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- A meteoritic glycine molecule and a terrestrial glycine molecule have the same atoms and the same bonds. Under ≡ they are one referent with two names. They are **not** isomers under Fig 12's definition; isomerism needs a different bond.
- Fig 12 :130: "A term carries the atoms it was built from and the bonds that joined them. That record is not documentation of the term. It is the term."
- Therefore the difference between delivered and endogenous glycine is carried by the **lineage record**, not by the molecule. This is why F4's source gate is load-bearing: if the record is lost, the two become one referent and no measurement of the molecule can recover the difference. Stable isotope anomalies are the trace that keeps them separate.
- **Falsifier:** show that for the supply question the two sources are interchangeable even with a perfect isotope trace, that is, provenance makes no difference to whether the molecule enters the inventory. Then the lineage distinction is decorative and F4 falls.

### Q2 - The delivered set is a mixture, not a molecule

- Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
- Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance."
- Meteorites deliver a distribution of compounds. The ingredient list is recorded (Furukawa 2019 reports 14,000 molecular compounds in Murchison, per the opened Wikipedia map source), but the bonds that would make a pathway are not. The arrangement has to be supplied by local chemistry.
- Collapse 6 of Fig 12 applies: a flat formula records ingredients and not arrangement, "nothing downstream can tell them apart, and nothing signals that anything was lost". Treating "delivered organics" as one substance commits this collapse.
- **Consequence:** the head-start claim (F2) is a claim about lowering search cost, not about delivering a finished route. Fig 4 :34: "A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a delivered mixture in which the bonds are already fixed so that no local composition is required. Then the flat-formula finding is wrong for that case.

### Q3 - Isomerism inside the delivered inventory is real and measured

- Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- The four aldopentoses (ribose, arabinose, xylose, lyxose) are reported together in Murchison and NWA 801 (Furukawa 2019, opened 2026-11-23). They share a functional group and differ in arrangement. Under Fig 12 :117 they are different substances, and under :124 they are members of one functional group with different qualifiers.
- This matters for the supply question because "a sugar was delivered" is not one claim. It is a family of claims, and Fig 9 :4-5 warns that disagreements which do not shrink with effort are several well-formed questions, not one hard question: "Split it rather than search." Ribose matters for RNA; deoxyribose was below detection in the same study. Lumping them is a bundling error (Fig 9 :54-58).
- **Falsifier:** show that the aldopentoses are interchangeable for prebiotic function, so the isomer distinction has no consequence. Then the functional-group split is unnecessary.

### Q4 - Order of the gates

- Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
- Fig 8 :53: "Residual first, frame second."
- The correct order for this question is: (1) describe the supply residual; (2) enumerate the habitat (Fig 11 :43); (3) establish whether internal composition reaches the inventory (Fig 6 :28-31); (4) if not, gate the import with provenance and shadow test (Fig 6 :12-13); (5) check the channel (Fig 10 :24); (6) check incorporation as a head start (Fig 4 :11); (7) only then compare magnitudes with ⋛ and let the ground decide (Fig 9 :42).
- Running step 7 before steps 4-6 is the failure Fig 3 names: a reading taken below a break is a reading of nothing. Asking "what percentage did meteorites supply?" before establishing provenance or survival spends the budget on a step whose inputs have not arrived.
- **Falsifier:** demonstrate that the comparison of magnitudes can be run before provenance and survival are settled, because those conditions cannot change the result. Then the dependency order is wrong and this section is withdrawn.

## CONCLUSION

Two things that look like one are not one. A delivered molecule and an endogenous molecule of the same structure are one referent with two lineages (≡ plus Fig 12 :130), so provenance is the only discriminator and the source gate is load-bearing. A set of delivered molecules is a mixture, not a molecule (Fig 12 :5), so the contributing work is done by local chemistry, not by the delivery. Within the mixture, genuine isomer families exist and must be split rather than averaged (Fig 12 :117 and :124, Fig 9 :4-5). The gates have a dependency order, and magnitude comes last.

This framing rescues what is defensible in the supply claim without asserting that supply happened at a material level. No new primitive is needed, no wall-moving import is claimed for the framework itself, and no percentage is named.
