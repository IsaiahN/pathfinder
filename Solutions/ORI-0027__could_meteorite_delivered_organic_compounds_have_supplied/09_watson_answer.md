## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about exogenous organic delivery as a source of life's building blocks. Option A: assert that meteorites supplied a meaningful share. Option B: assert they did not. ## WORLD (secondary, corroboration only)

Every source below was opened 2026-11-23. Overlap is declared; most science sources share samples, laboratories or authors and are therefore one witness pool.

- **Planetary science / institutional communication.** William Steigerwald, NASA news release RELEASE19-23, 2019: "meteorite bombardment on ancient Earth may have assisted the origin of life with a supply of life's building blocks." Predicts that delivery is a plausible contributor. Corroborates the reachability shape. Overlap: high with the PNAS sources below (same result, same laboratories).
- **Cosmochemistry / prebiotic chemistry.** Yoshihiro Furukawa et al., PNAS 116, 2019: "Meteorites were carriers of prebiotic organic molecules to the early Earth; thus, the detection of extraterrestrial sugars in meteorites establishes the existence of natural geological routes to make and preserve them as well as raising the possibility that extraterrestrial sugars contributed to forming functional biopolymers like RNA on the early Earth or other primitive worlds." Predicts a passed source gate and a real head start; also reports sugars about three orders of magnitude below amino acids, which is a magnitude caveat, not a share. Corroborates with a caveat.
- **Cosmochemistry.** Zita Martins et al., Earth and Planetary Science Letters 270, 2008 (arXiv 0806.2286): "Carbon-rich meteorites, carbonaceous chondrites, contain many biologically relevant organic molecules and delivered prebiotic material to the young Earth." Carbon isotope ratios for uracil and xanthine indicate non-terrestrial origin. Predicts an import with provenance. Corroborates F1 and F4. Overlap: shares authors and samples with the NASA Goddard sources.
- **Cosmochemistry.** Michael P. Callahan et al., PNAS 108, 2011: "meteorites may have served as a molecular kit providing essential ingredients for the origin of life on Earth and possibly elsewhere." Also reports that canonical nucleobases remain contamination-ambiguous while rare analogs support an extraterrestrial origin. Predicts the source gate is live and sometimes unresolved. Corroborates with a stated limit.
- **Review / thermodynamics.** Sandra Pizzarello and Everett Shock, Cold Spring Harbor Perspectives in Biology 2:a002105, 2010: "Whether this evolution resumed on the Earth to foster biogenesis after exogenous delivery of meteoritic and cometary materials is not known." Reports 1 to 3 percent of meteorite weight as carbon during the early impact period. Predicts that delivery happened but that contribution to biogenesis is unresolved. **Corroborates the honest stop**, and conflicts with any claim that the share is already settled.
- **Planetary science / institutional communication.** William Steigerwald, NASA article, 2020: "Finding HMT in meteorites confirms the hypothesis that it is a stable source for ammonia and formaldehyde in asteroids." Predicts a carrier that survives the channel. Corroborates F5.
- **Background map.** Wikipedia, "Murchison meteorite", accessed 2026-11-23: "This specimen demonstrates that many organic compounds could have been delivered by early Solar System bodies and may have played a key role in life's origin." Used as a mapping aid only. Low weight; a tertiary source.
- **Business administration, genuinely low overlap.** Wikipedia, "Absorptive capacity", accessed 2026-11-23, reporting Cohen and Levinthal 1990: absorptive capacity is "a firm's ability to recognize the value of new information, assimilate it, and apply it to commercial ends" and "depends greatly on prior related knowledge". Predicts that an import helps only where the receiver can recognise and use it. **Analogue only**, corroborating the shadow test as a structural idea; it is not evidence about meteorites and carries no verdict weight on the chemistry.

No source conflicts with the figure verdict once the verdict is stated as shape rather than magnitude. ## VERDICT (from the figures)

For the reachability part, **yes at `general` mode**: a closure cannot add an atom to itself, and delivery is an import, so an exogenous source can expand the prebiotic reach. For the material part, **shape only at `specified` mode**: a real contribution must carry a provenance trace, explain a gap the receiving chemistry has, survive the channel, and become a head start that local chemistry composes over; "meaningful share" is a ⋛ comparison, not a name. For the magnitude, **gap**: the figures return a kind of thing, never the share, and the ground has not returned a number in this derivation. The answer is `specified` and ungrounded, not `grounded` and not a refusal.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, gates and refuters before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is asking readers to endorse a magnitude that the figures do not return, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The four conditions are stated separately and can be removed or repaired one at a time; no primitive has to be withdrawn. A superseded packet can replace this one without touching the reachability result.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. Independent isotope and chiral source attribution, measured survival fractions through entry and alteration, a demonstration that the delivered distribution lowers the cost of reaching a functional building block, and an inventory comparison with and without delivery under the same local chemistry would together move the magnitude from gap to settled. A demonstration that a closure added an atom by composition alone would break the reachability claim. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
