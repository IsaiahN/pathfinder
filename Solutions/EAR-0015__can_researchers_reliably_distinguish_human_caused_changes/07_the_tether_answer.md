# The Tether's answer: can researchers reliably distinguish human-caused changes in extreme events from natural variability?

**Mode: `specified` on the split verdict, `general` on the structural shape, `grounded` on the observation-only sub-claim.** This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §5), not a ground reading and not a new climate result.

## 1. The residual it answers

Given an observed extreme event or class of events, the gap between what a natural-only hypothesis predicts and what the instruments return, indexed per slot (event class, region, variable, time window). The word "reliably" is the question's own tether: bound it as "positive per-slot residual against a non-updating anchor, returned by low-overlap frames" and it becomes refusable rather than an invisible preference.

The question arrives as one sentence but is a **union compound**, not one hard question. Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Its three conditions are the observed change, the counterfactual baseline, and the scale of the claim (class versus one event).

## 2. The answer, per domain, never averaged

**D1, the observed record at class scale: yes, reliable in the specific sense available here.** The measurement is a genuine anchor: Fig 2 :29, "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." The residual is per slot: Fig 1 :19, "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Where the world source reports an established change against that anchor (for hot extremes at global and continental scale, the IPCC AR6 WG1 Chapter 11 assessment says *virtually certain*), the observation-only step is `grounded`. The attribution step added on top remains `specified`, because the source of the change is not registered by the measurement alone.

**D2, the counterfactual natural baseline: reliable only conditionally.** There is no observed anchor for a world without anthropogenic forcing. Bellprat et al. (2019), read this session: "no observations are available for a world without climate change." The baseline is imported from a model frame, so its reliability is conditional on independence between the frames used and on a reliability check against observed frequencies. Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." Fig 6 :53: "searchers drawing on the same evidence are one searcher wearing many names." Ten ensembles from one family and several reanalyses of one realisation are one witness. Where different framings converge and the provenance shows convergence rather than adopted import, class-level attribution is as reliable as the evidence permits; it is still a `specified` claim, not a settled one.

**D3, one specific event: the discrimination is `unreached`.** The observed magnitude is one reading with several sources. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (read this session, high overlap, declared): "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." Fig 6 :32: "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something." At this resolution nothing returns the source, so there is no edge to extend (Fig 6 :35). This is not "no" and not "unreachable in principle". Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 8 :40-43 separates underivable-in-principle from unreachable-at-budget; the figures cannot tell us which case this is, so both remain open. The reliable object is the class, not the individual occurrence: Bellprat et al. and Perkins-Kirkpatrick et al. (2022) both make that distinction.

**D4, verification: agreement is not a witness.** Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." The check is sent as a residual and a refuter, one to one, and the verdict is recorded before any framework material is shown.

## 3. The shape that survives

1. **Per slot, not per average.** Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
2. **Anchor where an instrument returns one, frame where it does not.** Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."
3. **Class, not singleton.** The two are isomers under Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
4. **Provenance for every import.** Fig 8 :51-52: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"
5. **A shape, not a name.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Greenhouse gases, aerosols and internal modes are bindings from climate science, not products of this derivation.
6. **Open source slot where no instrument discriminates.** Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence", and Fig 9 :42, "narrowing gets you close, the ground decides".

## 4. What this answer explicitly does not say

- Not that "no, researchers can never tell." That would be a proof of absence the figures forbid (Fig 8 :39).
- Not that "yes, every extreme event can be attributed." That would force an anchor onto a model-generated counterfactual and aggregate away the per-slot residual.
- Not that IPCC confidence categories are the ground. They are filters (Fig 9 :60): "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."
- Not that this derivation improves on any published attribution method, names the forcing in a slot, or overrides the assessment literature.
- Not `grounded` beyond the observation-only sub-claim. No climate model was run and no measurement taken here.
- Not a final answer. Fig 13 :115: "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."

## 5. What would settle it, and what would reverse this answer

The ground, per frame, separately. The concrete instruments are:

1. **Single-event discriminator:** an instrument that returns the same human-versus-natural source for one event under two low-overlap framings, with simulated probabilities matching observed frequencies. This would settle D3 and reverse the `unreached` verdict.
2. **Independence check:** two genuinely different framings (different observational products with non-correlated errors, different model families, different event definitions) returning the same per-slot risk ratio with non-overlapping uncertainty, and provenance showing convergence rather than import. This would move D2 toward `grounded`.
3. **Counterfactual observation:** a natural-only baseline observed directly at the event's resolution, removing the model-dependence of `P_NAT`. This would dissolve the D2/D3 split.
4. **Re-census failure:** the Fig 2 anchor line shown to govern the counterfactual as well, with no strain, or a single lever clearing all three union conditions. Either would withdraw the split verdict.

The four refuters were written in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §3 before the conclusion. Their order is part of the design, not a retrospective addition.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached at the single-event scale, not unreachable.** The two are different facts with different instructions (Fig 8 :40-43). The record stops at the limit of what the available instruments distinguish and keeps the lineage so the stop is checkable. The canonical README's phrase governs: the framework returns "a shape, not a name", and "Indiscernibility is not identity." A verdict of `unreached` is a report, not a silence.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer "yes" or "no" to whether researchers can reliably distinguish human-caused changes in extreme events from natural variability. It says the question is a union of three different questions. Against the observed record, a class of events can be reliably shown to have changed, because the measurement is an anchor; that observation-only step is grounded. The counterfactual natural world, however, has no observed anchor, so the human-versus-natural split is a comparison between frames whose reliability depends on their independence and on a provenance record; that step is specified, not grounded. And for one specific event, the observed magnitude is a single reading with several possible sources, and no instrument at that resolution returns the source, so the honest status is unreached rather than unreachable. What the framework offers is the shape any acceptable answer must have, per slot and per regime, with four refuters written in advance and an open source slot where no discriminator exists. The ground decides; agreement does not.
