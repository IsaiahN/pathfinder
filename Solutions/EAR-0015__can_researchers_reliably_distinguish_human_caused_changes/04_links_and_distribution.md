## Three findings in the wider literature that bear directly on this question

1. **The baseline is not observed.** Bellprat et al. (2019) state that no observations exist for a world without climate change and show that calibrating model probabilities changes the fraction of attributable risk. This is the world-side statement of F3: the counterfactual is a frame, not an anchor.
2. **The reliable object is the class, not the one event.** Bellprat et al. and Perkins-Kirkpatrick et al. both separate a class-level risk ratio from a singular event or singular impact. This is the world-side statement of F5.
3. **Agreement is only as good as independence.** Otto et al. (2024) show that observational products over the same realisation have correlated variability and that related model families share assumptions; Bellprat et al. show that adding models and members does not remove a shared structural deficiency. This is the world-side statement of F4.

These are corroboration, not a second authority. No world source overrules a figure line, and a conflict would send the derivation back to the domain census.
