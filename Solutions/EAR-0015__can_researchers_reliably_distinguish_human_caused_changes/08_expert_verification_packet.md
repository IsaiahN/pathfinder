# Expert verification packet: the Tether on event attribution reliability

**What this is:** a claim in a form an attribution researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so the reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the split verdict, `general` on the structural shape, `grounded` on the observation-only sub-claim.** No new climate measurement is claimed and no climate model was run.

**Status:** simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Can researchers reliably distinguish human-caused changes in extreme events from natural variability?" is not one question. It is a union of three conditions with different answers. Against the observed record, a *class* of events can be reliably shown to have changed, because the measurement is an anchor, and the residual is indexed per slot. The counterfactual natural-only world has no observed anchor, so the human-versus-natural split is a comparison between frames whose reliability depends on independence between framings and on recorded provenance. For one *specific* event, the magnitude is a single reading with several possible sources and no instrument at that resolution returns the source, so the status is `unreached`, not `unreachable`. Reliability is therefore a per-slot, per-regime property. The deliverable is that shape plus four refuters, not a new global verdict.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 2 and 9

- **Statement:** the question is a union compound; reliability is per slot; the anchor governs the observation, not the counterfactual.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."
- **What to check:** does forcing "anchor" onto a model-generated natural-only world strain the reading? If it does, the domain split is correct; if it does not, the split was unnecessary. Check also whether Fig 2 :36, "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it", supports assigning the anchor to the change question and not to the source question.
- **Refuter:** a single lever that clears the observed-change, counterfactual-baseline and scale conditions together, or a re-census in which the anchor line governs the counterfactual without strain. Either collapses the union into a partition.

### C2 - science-facing; checkable by an attribution researcher against the published literature

- **Statement:** the reliable object is a class of events, not the singular occurrence. The class claim and the singular claim are isomers under Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- **What to check:** is this faithful to practice? Bellprat et al. (2019, *Nature Communications*) write that single events "happen only once in the exact same manner and their probability is. therefore. strictly speaking infinitely small, which inhibits any attribution", and the field therefore defines a class. Perkins-Kirkpatrick et al. (2022, *Environmental Research Letters*) write: "FAR measures the change in frequency of a class of events, and an impact is a specific outcome of a singular event." Does that match how the claim was actually framed in the study you know?
- **Refuter:** an instrument that returns the same human-versus-natural attribution for a single event under two genuinely independent framings, with simulated probabilities matching observed frequencies. That would make the singular claim the same kind of term as the class claim, and the isomer reading fails.

### C3 - independence-facing; checkable against the structure of model and data ensembles

- **Statement:** agreement is only as good as independence. Multiple ensembles from one model family, or several reanalyses of the same realisation, are one witness.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." Fig 6 :53: "searchers drawing on the same evidence are one searcher wearing many names". Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool".
- **What to check:** Otto et al. (2024, *ASCMO*) state that models from different framings, for example SST-forced, regional and coupled global models, are expected to reduce biases from shared modelling assumptions, and that observational products over the same realisation have strongly correlated natural variability. Bellprat et al. (2019) show a common structural deficiency survives multi-model and large-ensemble approaches. Do those statements support the figure reading, or does a recent result show independence is not the parameter?
- **Refuter:** two genuinely low-overlap frames converging on the same per-slot risk ratio with non-overlapping uncertainty where the record shows **convergent derivation** rather than adopted import. Fig 8 :51-52: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them". If convergence survives that provenance check, class-level reliability is stronger than this packet allows.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not "no, researchers can never tell." Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
- Not "yes, every event can be attributed." That would force an anchor onto a model-generated counterfactual and aggregate away the per-slot residual (Fig 1 :22).
- Not that IPCC confidence categories are the ground. Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."
- Not that this derivation names greenhouse gases, aerosols or ENSO as the occupant of a slot. Those are bindings from climate science, provenanced as such.
- Not that this improves on any published attribution method, overrides the assessment literature, or is `grounded` beyond the observation-only step.
- Not a final answer. Fig 13 :115: "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument or a genuine independence result, not further agreement.

1. **Single-event discriminator:** same source returned for one event under two low-overlap framings with matched observed frequencies.
2. **Convergence with provenance:** low-overlap frames returning the same per-slot risk ratio with non-overlapping uncertainty, shown to be convergent rather than imported.
3. **Counterfactual observation:** a natural-only baseline observed at the event's resolution.
4. **Dependency check:** event definition shown not to change per-slot attribution across representative framings would reverse the dependency claim in `06_isomers_and_order.md` Q3.

All four were written before the run in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §3 and `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :54, Fig 1 :19, Fig 2 :29 and :31, and Fig 6 :53. Five short passages.
2. Check C2 against the study you know best: was the attribution claim about a class or about a singular event? Was the event definition fixed before the comparison?
3. Name one thing that would make two attribution frames genuinely independent in your field, or say why none is available.
4. Write a one-line failure if you find one. A failure is more useful than an endorsement, and no reply is needed if you would rather not engage.
