# Outreach draft: check one output before you decide whether the framing is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the checker can see the residual and the refuter without the framework's interiors. If the two-stage order is reversed, the checker joins the frame being checked and their agreement stops being evidence (Fig 10 :42).

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about event attribution, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a small derivation about a question in climate attribution and I would like you to check one output against what you already know. I am not asking you to read any framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not a new scientific result).** The question "can researchers reliably distinguish human-caused changes in extreme events from natural variability" is not one question. It bundles three things that have different answers:
>
> 1. Did the class of events change, measured against the observed record? Here there is a real anchor: the measurement. For some classes, especially hot extremes at global and continental scale, the change is settled against that anchor.
> 2. What would have happened in a world without anthropogenic forcing? Here there is no observed anchor. The natural-only baseline is produced by a model, so the discrimination is only as reliable as the independence between the frames used to produce it. Agreement among models from one family, or among reanalyses of the same realisation, is one witness, not several.
> 3. Did *this specific event* have a human cause? Here the observed magnitude is one reading with several possible sources. No instrument at that resolution returns the source. The honest status is "unreached", not "no" and not "yes".
>
> So the answer I am asking you to check is a shape, not a verdict: a per-slot, per-regime statement with an open source slot where no discriminator exists. What I am explicitly **not** claiming: that this derivation settles the IPCC assessment, that it names which forcing or mode occupies a slot, that it improves on any published attribution method, or that it is `grounded` outside the observation-only step.
>
> **What would refute it (written before I ran this):**
> 1. An instrument that returns the same human-versus-natural attribution for a single event under two genuinely independent framings, with simulated probabilities matching observed frequencies. That would make single-event attribution grounded and collapse my third point.
> 2. Two low-overlap frames converging on the same per-slot risk ratio with non-overlapping uncertainty, where the record shows convergence rather than one frame borrowing the other's answer. That would upgrade my second point.
> 3. A natural-only baseline observed directly at the event's resolution, removing the model-dependence of the counterfactual. That would dissolve my second and third points.
> 4. A re-census showing that the measurement anchor governs the counterfactual as well, with no strain, so my domain split was wrong.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will withdraw or narrow the claim. A one-line "this fails because ..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - IPCC AR6 WG1 Chapter 11, Weather and Climate Extreme Events in a Changing Climate: https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/chapter/chapter-11/
> - Naveau, Hannart and Ribes, Statistical Methods for Extreme Event Attribution in Climate Science (2020): https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-statistics-031219-041314
> - Bellprat et al., Towards reliable extreme weather and climate event attribution (2019): https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/s41467-019-09729-2
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the formula is public in the figures repository. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## The two-stage offer, explicit

**Stage 1.** Send the residual in effect terms, the per-domain shape, and the four refuters. Ask: what would you count as a failure here? Record the answer before showing any framework material.

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If the checker is curious, offer the public links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because once they have absorbed the framing they are no longer an outside frame.

**Stage 3, opt-in only.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode. Fig 4 :22 again: a generator can be given away without giving away an answer.

**No stage 4.** There is no ask for endorsement, no request to forward, and no list of people selected for likely agreement.
