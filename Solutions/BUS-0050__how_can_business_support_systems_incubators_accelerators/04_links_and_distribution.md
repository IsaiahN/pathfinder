## 2. What the wider literature bears on the question

**One topical pool, several designs.** Hallen, Cohen and Bingham, Cohen, Bingham and Hallen, Gonzalez-Uribe and Leatherbee, the accelerator meta-analysis, and Lenihan all study enterprise support. They do not share one dataset: the first uses accepted versus almost accepted applicants, the second an eight-case inductive study, the third a regression discontinuity at a capacity cutoff and a pitch-score cutoff, the fourth a meta-analysis of 21 studies, the fifth Irish policy evaluation records. That gives them different within-pool designs, but they are still one topical field drawing on overlapping literature, so their agreement is not independent corroboration (Fig 2 :31). Treat them as one witness with several instruments, and record the design difference rather than inflating the witness count.

- **Selection is the named failure.** Hallen et al. find *"some evidence of sorting dynamics"* and state that *"sorting’s relationship to venture development is not causal and merely correlational"*, finding acceptance more likely for ventures with more web traffic, more full-time founders and prior venture capital. The meta-analysis reports a *"selection bias, as many accelerator programs tend to favor start-ups that have already obtained some form of funding before entering the program."* Lenihan names *"the notion of backing or picking winners"* in agency behaviour. All three corroborate F4 in the same pool.
- **Deadweight is the named outcome of picking winners.** The Irish technical annex: *"Deadweight refers to the policy support outcomes which would have occurred anyway."* Innovate UK: *"Impacts seen in an evaluation should be measured against a baseline of what would have happened had the intervention not occurred."* These corroborate F1 from the policy-evaluation side of the pool.
- **Cash alone is inert in the one clean design that separates it.** Gonzalez-Uribe and Leatherbee: *"we find no evidence that basic accelerator services of cash and coworking space have a treatment effect on fundraising, scale, or survival"*, while entrepreneurship schooling raised financing probability by 21.0 percent. This corroborates F2 and F3. It is local to that subpopulation and those services; do not generalize it into "money never helps".
- **Agreement from inside the room is not a verdict.** The meta-analysis finds *"publication bias, indicating that the existing literature may overrepresent studies with significant positive results while underreporting studies with null or negative findings."* The Innovation Growth Lab trial found participants valued supervision and did more tasks while performance did not improve. These corroborate F8.
- **The mechanism is reach, not resources.** Cohen, Bingham and Hallen report founders *"systematically satisfice prematurely"* and benefit from *"reigniting search for problems that they already view as solved"*. Hallen et al. name *"broad, intensive, and paced consultation"* as the key driver. These are the business-field names for Fig 6 :23 and Fig 3 :27.

**Low-overlap witnesses, each from a different field and each read directly:**

1. **Causal inference and statistics.** Imbens and Rubin: *"at most one of the potential outcomes can be realized and thus observed"*; Holland via the Oxford lecture notes: *"it is impossible to observe the effect of D on Y for unit i."* This corroborates F5's honest stop and refuses the fantasy of a per-startup answer from a single observation. The same field supplies the population-level average as a budget-scale substitute, which Fig 9 :60 classifies as a filter.
2. **Ecology.** Jones, Lawton and Shachak 1994: ecosystem engineers *"modify, maintain and create habitats"*; Figure 11 :65 names this paper for ecosystem engineering. This corroborates the mechanism shape of F3 (introduction changes habitat, exclusion is unavailable) at a different stratum and with no shared evidence pool with accelerator research. It is an analogue, not a startup measurement.
3. **Human judgement and experimental policy design.** The Innovation Growth Lab accountability trial is a randomized design inside the accelerator field but with a different instrument from the observational studies. Its dissociation between participant sentiment and measured performance corroborates F2 and F8 at the mechanism level; the field overlap is recorded, so it is not a third independent witness.

**Framework-internal essays** are used for the canonical reading of "shape" in `05_what_shape_means.md` only. They are not independent world witnesses, because their author wrote the figures. That overlap is recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

## 3. Distribution rules

Figure-grounded reasons for how material is offered:

- **Fig 4 :22:** *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Send the generator, the residual and the refuter, not the conclusion. A recording carried upward *"looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion"* (Fig 4 :25).
- **Fig 10 :42:** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :28:** *"any frame you pick will seem to fit"* if the residual is not described first. Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before showing the source.
- **Fig 2 :40:** agreement from a shared pool is worthless as a verdict. Later agreement about the framework is never pooled as corroboration.
- **Fig 10 :59:** *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* Declare the stake; do not ask for belief.
- **Fig 10 :75:** *"A conscientious selection is still a selection, so unauthored needs a mechanism:"* the checking pool itself must not be hand-picked from people likely to agree.

**A hard practical constraint.** Each venue has its own rule about AI-assisted work. The *Entrepreneurship Theory and Practice* guidelines state: *"AI chatbots, for example ChatGPT, should not be listed as authors."* The AOM policy recorded in the parallel BUS-0001 solution states that submitted work must be created by the authors and not be a product of AI tools unless appropriate to the research question and properly cited. This whole solution is a simulated derivation produced with an LLM and is labelled as such everywhere. It must **not** be submitted as an authored empirical or theoretical article. The legitimate use is as a private checking instrument: post the residual and the refuter, invite a human to run the check, and let that human author whatever they conclude. Any submission must be rewritten and owned by a human author who can defend the derivation, with the AI contribution disclosed under the applicable policy.

**Order in any reply:** (1) the shape claim and the pre-registered refuter; (2) the question "what would you count as a failure here?"; (3) only after a verdict is recorded, the source links with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (4) the formula as a reusable method, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

## 4. The two-stage offer, compressed

**Stage 1.** Send `08_expert_verification_packet.md` sections 1 to 3 only: the one-paragraph claim, the three checkable sub-claims with their verbatim figure lines and refuters, and the non-claims. No provenance bundle, no essay links.

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If the recipient is curious and has already recorded a verdict on stage 1, offer the three public links: the figure text, the formula and usage rule, and the essays. Their later agreement is a conversation, not corroboration (Fig 2 :40).

**Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method, never as an answer (Fig 4 :22). Each user writes a refuter before running it and states the mode of what they get.

**Stop rule.** If stage 1 fails, the theory is not offered. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
