# Outreach draft - validate the output first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. No personal emails are used; the recipient must have an opt-in or public institutional route.

**Hard precondition:** the stage-1 output must survive the recipient's check before any theory is shown. The output here is a `specified` shape with no ground measurement, so the legitimate ask is a refutation of the shape, never endorsement of it. If the recipient cannot be shown a failure condition they can actually test, do not send.

**Subject:** Check one claim about startup support before you decide whether any of this is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one narrow claim about incubators, accelerators and public funding that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. I am asking for a failure. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** support helps a startup only when three conditions hold at once:
>
> 1. it changes the startup's contact with a market that actually pays, rather than only adding money, space or a stipend to the founder;
> 2. it moves the startup's reach outward through contact with people and situations whose ways of working differ from its own, so the startup gains something it could not have composed on its own;
> 3. the cohort and the comparison group are assembled before the outcome is known, by lottery, by "everyone who qualifies", or at a fixed capacity cutoff, and the result is read per startup, never as a programme average.
>
> "Favoring businesses that would succeed anyway" is not a smaller kind of success in this claim. It is the case where the support input has nothing to work on: the startup was already heading toward what customers pay, so the programme's machinery worked and the answer was already known. Selecting on signs of likely success is the failure mode, because the selection manufactures the very correlation the programme then cites as evidence.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I can name which startups will win, that any particular accelerator is a case in point, that this is settled by evidence, or that it is the only account of startup support. I am offering a shape any candidate answer would have to have. A shape is not a name. The average treatment effect is a budget filter, not a verdict about any one startup, because the per-startup counterfactual cannot be observed for the same startup at the same time.
>
> **What would refute it, written down before I sent this:**
> 1. a startup's post-support performance is unchanged while its pre-support reading was already flat and no new contact or capability was recorded;
> 2. a startup whose cash and office resources increased, with no change in contact or reach, still moves the market ground per startup across a change of market;
> 3. a participant-selected cohort keeps its outcome advantage under an unauthored near-cutoff or lottery comparison;
> 4. an instrument returns both the supported and unsupported outcomes for the same startup at the same moment;
> 5. a programme-produced metric (pitch score, demo-day attendance, alumni valuation) predicts per-startup market payment prospectively without being fitted to the same outcomes;
> 6. a startup whose reading of customers, vocabulary for the market and objective all measure as working fails only at planning.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw or narrow the claim. A single line saying which one holds and why is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send the three public sources below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
>
> - Figure text with line numbers: the local canonical figure text in this solution folder.
> - Formula and usage rule: the `figures` folder on the public `general-agent` branch of the `tether` repository.
> - Essays on search shape, source and the prestige/action separation: my Substack archive.
>
> I will send URLs only after you tell me what you would count as a failure.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is public. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a real ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

| Deadweight named as a no-mint bin | *"nothing new here"* and *"the machinery worked; the answer was already known"* (Fig 5 :29-30) |
| Average declared a filter, not a verdict | *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* (Fig 9 :60) |

## Sending rules

- One recipient at a time. No distribution lists, no mail-merge, no forwarding chain.
- Use only a public institutional contact, a call for submissions, or an address the person has published themselves. Record the source and date.
- A Track B style reply is a conversation, never corroboration. If a recipient returns a refutation, copy it into `11_audit.md` with provenance and have the auditor seat re-evaluate it as if it came from any other source.
- Do not follow up with the theory after a "no". An interest in the outcome is unavoidable; an interest in the reading is disqualifying (Fig 10 :59).
- This is LLM-assisted simulated work. Do not submit it as an authored empirical or theoretical article. A human researcher who runs the check owns and writes whatever is published, with disclosure under the venue's policy (see `04_links_and_distribution.md`).
