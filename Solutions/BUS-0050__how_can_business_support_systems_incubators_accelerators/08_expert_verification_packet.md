# Expert verification packet - the Tether on startup support and additionality

**What this is:** a claim in a form an entrepreneurship researcher, programme evaluator or empirically minded policymaker can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No per-startup market measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

**Boundary of the check.** The first three sections are the whole stage-1 ask. Provenance, links and the method offer come later and only if a verdict on stage 1 is recorded first.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Support helps a startup only when three conditions hold at once: the support changes the startup's channel to a market that actually pays, rather than only adding money, space or a stipend; it moves the startup's reach outward through contact with people and situations whose ways of working differ from its own, so the startup gains something it could not have composed alone; and the cohort and comparison group are assembled before the outcome is known, by lottery, by everyone who qualifies, or at a fixed capacity cutoff, and read per startup rather than as a programme average. "Favoring businesses that would succeed anyway" is not a smaller kind of success in this claim. It is the zero-support bin in which the startup was already heading toward what customers pay, so the programme's machinery worked and the answer was already known. Selecting on signs of likely success is the failure mode, because the selection manufactures the correlation the programme then cites as evidence. The same input can change sign when the startup's residual changes, so a mentor can be a catalyst in one phase and a spent part in another. The average treatment effect is a budget filter, not a verdict about any one startup.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 5, 11 and 2

- **Statement:** a startup with no live residual gives the support input nothing to work on, so the "would succeed anyway" case is a no-mint bin; useful support changes contact with the paying ground, and the market, not the programme's own metric, is the anchor.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 5 :21 - *"the gap has real mass somewhere"*; Fig 5 :29 - *"nothing new here"*; Fig 5 :30 - *"the machinery worked; the answer was already known"*
  - Fig 11 :39 - *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*
  - Fig 11 :40 - *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*
  - Fig 2 :29 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* The same line names *"a market"* as an instance.
  - Fig 10 :93 - *"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."*
- **What a researcher can check against their own knowledge:** Gonzalez-Uribe and Leatherbee write *"we find no evidence that basic accelerator services of cash and coworking space have a treatment effect on fundraising, scale, or survival"*, while entrepreneurship schooling raised the probability of additional financing by 21.0 percent for the subpopulation randomized in by the pitch cutoff. Does that not separate an agent-only input from a contact-changing one? A "no" to that reading is a real refutation of the mapping.
- **Refuter:** a startup whose post-support performance is unchanged while its pre-support reading was already flat and no new contact was recorded; or a startup whose cash and office resources increased with no contact change and whose market ground nevertheless moved per startup across a habitat change. Either fires against C1.

### C2 - Framework-internal derivation plus one programme-design check

- **Statement:** reach renewal requires contact with a closure that differs; internal composition cannot add an atom; the usual break is at the vocabulary/objective link, so effort spent above it cannot rescue it.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 6 :14 - *"the only operator that moves the wall"*; Fig 6 :21 - *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*; Fig 6 :23 - *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*
  - Fig 8 :32 - *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."*
  - Fig 3 :19 - *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*; Fig 3 :27 - *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
  - Fig 11 :61 - *"The seat’s power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."*
- **What a researcher can check against their own knowledge:** Cohen, Bingham and Hallen report that founders *"systematically satisfice prematurely across many decisions"* and benefit from *"increasing the amount of external information searched, often by reigniting search for problems that they already view as solved."* Hallen, Cohen and Bingham name *"broad, intensive, and paced consultation"* as the key driver. Are those not descriptions of imports from a differing closure and of the middle link being rebuilt? A documented case of a startup renewing its reach purely by internal composition, with no new atom and a passing ground test across a habitat change, would break C2.
- **Refuter:** a startup that keeps its own closure for two years, imports no new atom, and still produces new market-verified readings across a changed habitat; or a startup whose perception, vocabulary and objective links all measure as working and whose only failure is at planning (which would falsify the "usually middle" reading, though not the ordering).

### C3 - Selection, verification and the honest stop

- **Statement:** an authored cohort manufactures the correlation used as evidence; agreement from one evidence pool is not corroboration; the per-startup counterfactual is unreachable, so the population average is a filter and not a verdict.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 10 :75 - *"A conscientious selection is still a selection, so unauthored needs a mechanism:"*
  - Fig 2 :40 - *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*
  - Fig 7 :13 - *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*; Fig 7 :35 - *"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*
  - Fig 9 :60 - *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
  - Fig 13 :134 - *"So the system can report that it lacks something rather than producing an answer anyway. Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*
- **What a researcher can check against their own knowledge:** the accelerator meta-analysis reports that *"the results reveal the presence of a selection bias, as many accelerator programs tend to favor start-ups that have already obtained some form of funding before entering the program"* and finds *"publication bias"* toward positive results. Hallen et al. report *"some evidence of sorting dynamics"* and say *"sorting’s relationship to venture development is not causal and merely correlational."* Imbens and Rubin state that *"at most one of the potential outcomes can be realized and thus observed."* Does the evaluation design separate these, or does it reuse the selection score as the outcome predictor? A "separates" answer with an unauthored pool is compatible with the claim; a participant-authored pool presented as a causal verdict is the failure C3 names.
- **Refuter:** a participant-authored cohort whose selection score correlates with outcomes but retains that correlation under an unauthored near-cutoff or lottery comparison; or an instrument that returns both potential outcomes for the same startup at the same moment. Either breaks C3.

## 5. What would settle it

A prospective, per-startup study that builds the unauthored pool before the cohort is chosen, records bonds as well as atoms, and reports which condition failed before the decline. Concretely:

1. **Contact test:** does the startup's reading of the market degrade while the market keeps paying someone? Indicators: stale percepts, saturated metrics, pooled readings (Fig 10 :25-26).
2. **Import test:** did a new atom enter the startup's reach from a closure that differs, before the improvement? If not, C2 is refuted.
3. **Unauthored pool test:** was the comparison group assembled by lottery, by everyone who qualifies, or at a fixed cutoff known before outcomes, rather than chosen by a participant (Fig 10 :75)?
4. **Per-slot test:** are outcomes reported per startup, with the cohort average labelled a budget filter (Fig 1 :19; Fig 9 :60)?
5. **Source-discriminating instrument:** can the study distinguish a protected capability from a paying habitat from an accelerator signal from luck when all four produce the same funding reading? The source essay says a reading alone *"cannot comment on the source"*.

## 7. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 5 :21, :29-30, Fig 11 :39-40, Fig 2 :29, Fig 6 :21 and :23, Fig 10 :75, Fig 7 :13, Fig 9 :60 and Fig 13 :134. Ten short passages, about eight minutes.
2. Check C2 against your own knowledge of how startups actually renew: is there a documented case of years of new market-verified readings with no import and no change of closure? About five minutes.
3. Name one instrument that would discriminate a protected capability from a paying habitat from luck using observable startup data, or say why none exists. About five minutes.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
