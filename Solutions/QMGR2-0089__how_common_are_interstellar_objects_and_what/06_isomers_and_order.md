## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are 1I and 2I isomers? CORRECTION TO MY FIRST READING

- Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :124: *"a substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- As observed, the two objects do **not** share an atom inventory: 1I shows no common cometary gases and no dust coma (Jewitt 2024), while 2I is a strong CO and H2O source with CO/H2O near 1, far above the Solar System average. Different inventories, so the strong isomer claim fails as written.
- What they share is a **functional group**: ejected planetesimal, `disk → planetesimal → ejection → interstellar space`. The qualifier (icy versus devolatilized, formation zone, ejection mechanism) decides the member. Jewitt 2024 notes that most ejectable bodies lie beyond the snowline, which is why the population should be comet-like, and that 1I's lack of activity is the anomaly.
- **I overstated this earlier.** My first reading called them isomers because they are both planetesimals from other systems. Fig 12 requires identity of atoms and a difference of bond, not merely membership in a category. **Retracted as applied to 1I versus 2I.** The functional-group reading stands. Triangulated (Fig 12 plus two independent reviews of the observed compositions).

### Q2 - Is there a weaker isomer claim worth keeping?

- Fig 12 :96: *"Two entries can share every ingredient and be inverses of one another."*
- Hopkins et al. 2026 infer that both 2I and 3I contain hypervolatiles such as CO and N2 trapped in less-volatile ices, similar to typical Solar System comets. If two objects share the full volatile inventory and differ only in how the hypervolatiles are bonded or trapped, they would be Fig 12 isomers: same atoms, different bond, different outgassing behaviour.
- That is a real claim about 2I versus 3I, not about 1I versus 2I. It is `specified`, unmeasured at the required fidelity.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if 2I and 3I differ in bulk volatile inventory, not merely in arrangement, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands for all three objects.

### Q3 - What is the dependency order of the ejection mechanisms?

Fig 3 :20-21: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*

Read off the literature (Moro-Martin 2022; Fitzsimmons et al. 2023; Jewitt 2024), the habitat is:

1. Protoplanetary disk forms and planetesimals grow (the meter-size barrier is the open problem; Moro-Martin 2022).
2. Giant planets form and scatter planetesimals; the Safronov number decides whether ejection occurs. Jupiter dominates in the Solar System.
3. Birth-cluster flybys strip outer-disk planetesimals; most efficiently in the first 10 Myr.
4. Post-main-sequence mass loss and exo-Oort release liberate weakly bound bodies later.
5. Tidal shredding multiplies fragments near a star or planet, but most fragments do not escape.

**The chain stops at link 1 in our knowledge:** all mechanisms produce roughly 0.1 to 30 Earth masses per star, so mass alone cannot select a winner (Fitzsimmons et al. 2023). The discriminators are velocity and composition, which are outputs of links 2 to 4, not of link 1. Effort spent refining the ejection velocity of one mechanism before the SFD is constrained lands above the break.

- **Falsifier:** if a single mechanism is shown to account for both the observed velocity distribution and the composition distribution at current precision, the order collapses to a single dominant link and the multi-mechanism habitat is unnecessary.

- Type caveat, still open: ⋛ is defined in the Operators table as *"a threshold relation between two quantities."* At second order the operands are a density and a composition class. Whether they count as quantities at that level needs an instrument, not an argument. Named, not hidden.

## CONCLUSION

1I and 2I are **not isomers** as written; they share a functional group (ejected planetesimals) with different qualifiers. A narrower isomer claim may hold between 2I and 3I if their volatile inventories match and only the bonding differs, with a stated falsifier. The ejection mechanisms form a dependency chain that currently stops at the planetesimal-formation link, so velocity-and-composition work should follow SFD work, not precede it. The union of count and reveal is second order and must wait on both settled first-order results.

This framing rescues what is defensible without forcing a category the observations do not support: no seventh primitive, no import that moves the wall, and no naming of a unique parent system.
