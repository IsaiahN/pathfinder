## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not a single abundance number and not a single origin story. In effect terms: a survey holds a model Γ that predicts a detection count Γ(b,a) from an assumed number density; the sky returns o′; R = |Γ(b,a) − o′| is measured per survey-object slot s, not averaged across surveys (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22). A second residual asks how a composition reading c constrains a parent-system property p, where many p can produce the same c (a lossy projection).

- Option A, "interstellar objects are common and their composition names their parent system": leads to the prediction that number density is a well-measured scalar and that a composition maps one-to-one onto a source star, so no further instruments are owed.
- Option B, "abundance is a bounded per-slot measurement with large Poisson and size-distribution uncertainty, and composition constrains a kind of parent system but does not name one": leads to the prediction that more survey search and source-discriminating instruments are owed before either half closes.

What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a shape stating (i) the per-slot detection residual with its uncertainty, (ii) the two-scale transform from a local count to a galactic population with its measured round-trip gap `R_T`, and (iii) the class of parent systems consistent with the observed composition, plus the instrument that would discriminate among members of that class. Until that shape is bound it predicts nothing and no check in the loop can see it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B in `specified` mode, kept per domain and never averaged: the count question is unreachable-at-budget (more search), and the reveal question returns a shape and a constraint, not a name.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the abundance question, and no claim that the framework is the only account of small-body populations.

Four reasons, three from the figures and one practical:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). Every paper reviewed here draws on the same three detections; their agreement is one pool talking.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
