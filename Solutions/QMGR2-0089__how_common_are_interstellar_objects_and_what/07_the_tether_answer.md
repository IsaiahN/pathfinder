# The Tether's answer on interstellar objects

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on every substantive claim, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question is two overlapping conditions. The count residual: a survey predicts a detection count from an assumed density and selection function, the sky returns an observation, and R = |Γ(b,a) − o′| is indexed by survey slot (Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*). The source residual: a composition reading c constrains a parent property p, but several p give the same c. It is a union compound, not one hard problem (Fig 9 :54).

## 2. The shape

1. **Unbundle count from reveal.** They have different grounds: a characterized survey for count, a discriminating instrument for source. Running one search over the bundle lets whichever half has the better instrument decide the answer. Fig 9 :54: *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again"*
2. **Count is per slot and budget-limited.** The density near 0.1 au^-3 comes from the characterized Pan-STARRS detection; the informal 2I survey cannot tighten it; the Poisson interval still spans orders of magnitude. "Common" is a comparison whose threshold is not grounded while the size-frequency distribution is unconstrained. The remedy is more independent survey search, not a new object class. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
3. **Scale the count with a measured gap.** 0.1 au^-3 implies roughly 10^4 bodies inside Neptune and roughly 10^26 in the Galaxy, but the local-to-galactic transform depends on the assumed size distribution and is not invertible. Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*
4. **Reveal returns a class, not a name.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* Composition ratios and isotope ratios narrow a metallicity range and a formation zone. Fig 13 :121: *"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."* A unique parent star is not returned by the framework; it needs a discriminator.
5. **The ejection habitat is enumerated.** Scattering, cluster flybys, post-main-sequence loss, tidal shredding and exo-Oort release are read off the world (Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*). Mass alone cannot pick a winner; velocity and composition are the discriminators.
6. **1I and 2I are not isomers as written.** Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* They share a functional group, not an atom inventory. A narrower isomer claim between 2I and 3I is left as a falsifiable test.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a settled density. No number here is `grounded` by this derivation; the reported values are world measurements with large uncertainties.
- Not a named parent star, and not a unique ejection mechanism. Those are bindings and enumerations, not derivations from the figures.
- Not an import against a budget limit. A new object class per anomaly adds an atom that was not needed (Fig 8 :40-43).
- Not one question. The two halves stay separate and are never averaged.
- Not a claim that the framework is the only account of small-body populations. Metatheories cannot validate themselves.

## 4. What would settle it, and the ground

A better instrument, not a better argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved that case. Here:

- **Count test:** a survey with published depth, area and cadence that detects enough objects to shrink the Poisson interval and constrain the size distribution. Rubin/LSST and NEO Surveyor are the named candidates (Stahl and Betts 2025; Siraj and Loeb 2022).
- **Scaling test:** an SFD-independent map from number density to ejected mass density. Without it the round trip stays open and the mass-budget claim remains `specified`.
- **Source test:** an isotope or volatile-ratio discriminator that separates candidate parent systems. Hopkins et al. 2026 identify ammonia as a metallicity tracer and note that isotope ratios already separate 3I/ATLAS from 2I/Borisov.
- **Standing falsifiers from `01`:** a negligible-uncertainty density; an invertible count-to-mass map; a composition vector naming one star; a shared anchor for count and source.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"* The count half is unreachable at this budget, which is a different fact from underivable in principle, and it issues a different instruction (more search, not another atom). The reveal half returns a shape at the limit of current discriminators. Indiscernibility is not identity; the lineage of which discriminator settled what is kept so the stop is checkable. From *How Intelligence Actually Works*: *"Being 'below the union horizon' is never a settled verdict, only a report that nobody has reached it yet."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give one number for how common interstellar objects are, and it does not name a home star. It says the question is two overlapping questions: count and reveal, with different grounds. The count residual must be kept per characterized survey, so the usual 0.1 au^-3 is a budget-limited estimate with an interval spanning orders of magnitude, not a settled density; scaling it to a galactic population carries a round-trip gap that has to be measured. "Common" is a comparison whose threshold is ungrounded until the size distribution is constrained, so the right move is more independent survey search, not a new kind of object. The reveal is a class of parent systems plus the instrument that would discriminate among them: composition and isotope ratios constrain metallicity and formation zone, but different routes can arrive at the same substance, so no unique star is returned. The ejection mechanisms are enumerated from the literature, and 1I and 2I share a functional group rather than being chemical isomers. What the framework offers is the shape any answer must have, four falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. Both halves stay open until the ground settles them.
