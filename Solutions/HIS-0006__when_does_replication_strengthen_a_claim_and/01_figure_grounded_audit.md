## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

The residual is a disagreement about what repetition buys.

- **Option A:** repetition itself is evidence. More replications, more repetitions, more agreement, therefore a stronger claim.
- **Option B:** repetition is evidence only under conditions. The repeated attempts must be separate frames ranging against one unmoving ground, their agreement must not come from a shared evidence pool, and the quantity they read must be the same quantity. When methods differ, the direct form of repetition can be unavailable; what remains is indirect triangulation through effect terms.

What each leads to: A makes "how many replications" a verdict proxy and, if the replicators are chosen for likely agreement, manufactures the exact correlation that disqualifies a verdict. B yields a set of necessary conditions, a name for the failure modes, and an honest stop where no common frame exists.

The residual is a **union compound**, not a single hard question. Fig 9 :4-5: *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* The two conditions (strengthening, and impossibility under method change) can co-occur, so each gets its own loop.

## 1. Domain census (before any figure line was chosen)

### R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** two. There is a repetition condition and a method-commensurability condition, and they can both bind at once.
- **Symmetry:** not all repetitions are interchangeable. A second reading from the same pool and a reading from a separate instrument are different objects even when their contents match. Fig 2 :18-21 names this: *"one evidence pool"*, *"they never talk, and still agree"*, *"so the agreement is the pool talking,"*, *"not two frames confirming each other."*
- **Scale:** within a frame (same method, same instrument, same slot) versus across frames (different methods, a lossy transform between). The across-frame case is where the direct form of replication can fail and the indirect form begins.

### One line per domain

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | nameable? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1, measurement and ground | the claim, the instrument, the observation, the thing measured | observations / measurements | the ground, which does not update on what any frame thinks | yes: an experiment, a counter, a patient outcome, a board |
| D2, replication and agreement | the original study, the repeat attempt, the pool they draw on | agreement and residual | the ground, never the agreement | yes: a second instrument pointed at the same thing (Fig 2 :31) |
| D3, method comparison and transfer | the two methods, the frame transform F, the instrument history | readings and the lossy transform between them | the ground, approached through effect terms | yes: a probe that is already returning something that fails to resolve (Fig 6 :34-35) |
| D4, the standards / historical record | historians, methodologists, journals, preregistrations | recorded standards of proof | the ground, never consensus about standards | yes: a public protocol, a registered report, a published correction |

Four domains, not a partition of one problem. D2 is a compound that overlaps D1 and D3 where a repeat uses the same instrument (D1) or a different one (D3). Per Fig 9 :57-58 a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either, so the overlap keeps its own loop.

### Partition or union, and dependency order

Union across D2 and D3; partition inside D1 (does this observation range against an anchor or not?). Dependency order, from Fig 3 :20-21, *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces"*:

1. **anchor fixed** (D1), otherwise there is nothing to range against;
2. **independence established** (D2), otherwise agreement has no meaning;
3. **agreement interpreted** (D2, D4), otherwise the verdict is a count;
4. **method commensurability checked** (D3), otherwise a direct repeat is not the same quantity.

A step below a break is not a weaker reading. Fig 3 :22-23: *"A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can. Which is why a reading taken below the break is not a weak"* ... *"It is a reading of nothing."* Counting replications before fixing the anchor is step 3 before step 1.

## 3. Findings

### F1 : Replication strengthens only as triangulation, never as a mirror chain (decisive)

Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

Fig 7 :12-13: **"same closure, handed along;"** **"agreement compounds, evidence does not"**

Fig 6 :53-54: **"searchers drawing on the same evidence are one searcher wearing many names."** Independence *"makes it the parameter rather than a caveat."*

Fig 2 :31-32: **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."**

A repetition adds evidential weight only when it is a second instrument, not a second reader of the same report. When the repeat shares the original's evidence pool (Fig 2 collapse 2, *"one evidence pool"*, *"the agreement is the pool talking"*), or when the frames update on each other (Fig 2 collapse 1, *"no anchor; each updates on the others"*, they converge on *"whoever had the most influence"*), the added agreement is motion, not progress. Fig 7 :36: *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."*

- **Mode:** `general` (structural). The world literature predicts the same thing (see §8 and `09_watson_answer.md`), but world agreement is secondary.
- **Verdict:** `triangulated`. Three figures with low overlap: the alignment figure (Fig 2, anchor and independence), the room-chain figure (Fig 7, selection between rooms), and the closure figure (Fig 6, searcher independence). They reach the same boundary from different structures.
- **Refuter:** exhibit two frames that draw on the same evidence pool and show that their agreement changes the warrant of the claim by more than the pool already carried; or show a chain of same-closure rooms whose evidence increases with length.

### F2 : The anchor must not update; a repeat that shifts the endpoint is a mirror, not an instrument

Fig 2 :29-30: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."** If it moves when you push on it, *"it is not an anchor."*

Fig 2 :33: **"The anchor must not update."**

Fig 2 :34: **"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."**

Fig 2 :24-27: **"pinned: it does not update on A or B"**; **"both range against it; it is not"**; **"a third party to the disagreement"**.

Fig 10 :24-26: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**; *"stale percepts, dropped transients,"* *"saturated metrics, pooled readings"*. A repeat that changes what counts as the result, or that pools readings until the discriminator is saturated, has broken the channel, not improved the measurement.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Verdict:** `triangulated` (Fig 2 anchor rule plus Fig 10 channel rule; the dependency census in §1 gives a third route, Fig 3).
- **Refuter:** a replication that changes the operational definition after seeing the data and is still counted as a strengthening; or a ground that changes its answer because of what a frame thinks.

### F3 : When methods differ, direct repetition can fail for lack of a shared pose; what survives is indirect triangulation

Symbols table :79-82: the frame transform F is *"the map between allocentric and egocentric at one scale. Distinct from T_A and T_E, which run between scales. Cannot be run without a pose."*

Fig 6 :30-31: **"outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."** **"Neither answer tells you much about the other. Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other."**

Fig 10 :68: **"Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between."**

Fig 10 :80-81: **"Frames whose independence is renewed by separate experience can be maintained; frames whose correlation is fixed at origin can only be rotated,"** *"because no amount of use makes them less alike and replacement is the only refresh."* Two methods whose correlation is fixed at origin cannot be made into a direct repeat by doing them more often; only a different frame refreshes them.

Fig 11 :67-70: isolation is *"substitution of one habitat for another"*, with *"what you failed to reproduce"* and *"what you unintentionally introduced"* invisible until the goal fails or the actor acts. Swapping the method for a "better" one is a substituted habitat, not a cleaner copy.

- **Mode:** `specified` when applied to any pair of actual methods; `general` on the structural claim that a frame transform needs a pose.
- **Verdict:** `triangulated` (Symbols table pose rule, Fig 6 two-track rule, Fig 10 lossy-transform and fixed-correlation rules). Low overlap between a notation file and two different figures.
- **Refuter:** demonstrate a frame transform between two methods with no shared pose, or show a direct replication across genuinely different instruments that preserves the same quantity at the same scale. The Duhem-Quine case noted by the world literature (a null result blamed on the instrument, a positive result blamed on the theory) is an instance of the fixed-correlation failure, not a refutation.

### F4 : Different methods can be the strongest corroboration, not the weakest

Fig 8 :50: **"Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."**

Fig 8 :51-52: **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them,"** and the surplus *"lives only where the closures differ."*

Fig 8 :47-49: **"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus. Two frames that overlap almost"** *"completely are one frame wearing two names."*

So method difference is not itself a defect. It is a defect only when the frames are then compared as if they were one frame, or when the record of which came from where is lost. Convergence from independently chosen methods is corroboration at maximum independence; a borrowed conclusion replayed in two vocabularies is one witness wearing two names.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Verdict:** `triangulated` (Fig 8 convergence/provenance plus Fig 2 independence definition).
- **Refuter:** show that convergence from independently chosen methods adds no warrant over a single frame, or that provenance does not change how a reader should weight the agreement.

### F5 : Correlation has valence; agreement is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention

Fig 2 :39-42: **"Correlation has valence."** **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**; **"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"**; **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."**

Fig 2 :44: **"The effective-sample-size correction for correlated observations is standard in survey statistics and meta-analysis; the collapse is the same arithmetic read as a warning."**

This is the hinge of the historical question. Standards of proof can legitimately treat agreement as the product when the thing being settled is a convention (a shared meaning, a unit, a reporting rule). The same agreement is disqualifying when the thing being settled is a claim about the world. A community that mistakes one for the other produces a convention and calls it a result.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Verdict:** `single-source` (Fig 2 carries the whole claim). The local seat map corroborates with *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention,"* but it is the same framework pool, so it is not a second witness.
- **Refuter:** a case where agreement among frames that demonstrably share one evidence pool settles a question about the world.

### F6 : "Number of replications" is frame-internal and cannot be the ground metric

Fig 1 :23: **"The ground enters as the only metric."**

Fig 1 :24-27: *"coverage, and compression achieved"*, *"predicates minted, and credibility accrued"*, **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."**

Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

A replication count is a budget filter: it tells you how much search was distributed and whether independence was bought. It is not a witness for the verdict. The verdict comes from what the ground returns.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Verdict:** `triangulated` (Fig 1 ground-is-the-only-metric plus Fig 9 filters-versus-witnesses; Fig 2 :42 prices the independence purchase).
- **Refuter:** an instrument whose reading is literally "number of repetitions" and for which the ground pays on that reading.

### F7 : Where direct repetition is unavailable, unreached is a verdict, not a silence

Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

Fig 9 :44: **"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."**

Fig 6 :34-35: *"The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve. Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."*

Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"**.

When two methods share no pose, the honest report is not "the claim is false" and not silence. It is: direct repetition is unreachable with the frames held, here is the effect-term description that could travel to a frame that holds it, and here is what would have to be imported. The historical analogue is an observation that is reliable but hard to reproduce because the material conditions are gone; the shape of the missing condition is reportable even when its name is not.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Verdict:** `triangulated` (Fig 8 metaframe limit, Fig 9 unsealed room, Fig 6 proto-instrument, Fig 13 verdict-not-silence).
- **Refuter:** a genuinely sealed frame in which exhaustive search is possible, so that finding nothing would prove absence.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the local seat map: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim (F1 through F7) is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A repeat that shares the original's evidence pool changes the warrant of the claim by more than the pool already carried (breaks F1; Fig 2 :40, Fig 7 :13).
2. A repeat that changes what counts as the result, or saturates/pools the reading, is still counted as strengthening (breaks F2; Fig 2 :33, Fig 10 :24-26).
3. A frame transform is demonstrated between two methods with no shared pose, or a direct repeat succeeds across different instruments while preserving the same quantity at the same scale (breaks F3; Symbols :82, Fig 10 :68).
4. Convergence from independently chosen methods adds no more warrant than a single frame, or provenance does not change the reading (breaks F4; Fig 8 :50-52).
5. A ground metric is found that is simply the count of repetitions (breaks F6; Fig 1 :23-27).
6. A sealed frame is exhibited in which exhausting search proves absence (breaks F7; Fig 9 :44).

If any fires, the shape claim is withdrawn. Until one is run and fires or fails, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Treat independence, not repetition count, as the quantity to buy. Fig 2 :42: buy it in proportion to the cost of being wrong.
2. Fix the anchor before judging a repeat. If the operational endpoint moves after the data are in, record the repeat as a convention-setting exercise, not a verdict (F2, F5).
3. Distinguish the three repetition types explicitly (direct, systematic, conceptual; see `06_isomers_and_order.md`). A direct repeat needs a shared pose; the other two are indirect routes and should be labelled as such.
4. Keep provenance with every result so convergent derivation is not mistaken for adopted import (F4).
5. Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict (F6). Publish the count as a description of effort, never as the verdict.
6. Where no common frame exists, report the shape of what is missing and stop honestly (F7).
7. State mode on every sentence. Nothing here is a measurement.
