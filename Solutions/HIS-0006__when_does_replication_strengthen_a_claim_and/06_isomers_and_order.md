## RESIDUAL

The word "replication" is being used for at least three different operations. If they are interchangeable for evidential weight, the three-condition shape in `01` is wrong. ## DERIVATION

### Q1 : Union or partition?

- Fig 9 :4-5: *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*
- Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
- Fig 9 :55-57: a single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds; what remains is *"not a harder case of either"* but *"a specific compound."*
- The two conditions bind independently: a repeat can use the same method (so direct repetition is possible) yet fail independence, or be independent yet use a different method (so direct repetition is impossible). Both can bind at once.
- **Result:** a **union**, not a partition. Each condition keeps its own loop. Verdict `general`, triangulated (Fig 9 plus the shape-not-a-name limit in the canonical README).

### Q2 : What is the dependency order?

- Fig 3 :20-21: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**
- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
- Fig 3 :22-23: **"It is a reading of nothing."**
- Order derived: anchor fixed (D1) → independence established (D2) → agreement interpreted (D2, D4) → method commensurability checked (D3). A protocol lodged after the result is a broken link 1, so everything downstream of it is unassessable, not weak.
- **Falsifier for the order:** a case in which agreement about a result legitimately fixes what counts as the anchor, with the ground still unmoved. If that exists, step 1 does not precede step 3.

### Q3 : Are the three repetition types isomers?

- The world source (Wikipedia, fetched 2026-11-23) distinguishes *"direct (repeating procedures as closely as possible)"*, *"systematic (repeating with intentional changes)"*, and *"conceptual (testing hypotheses using different procedures to assess generalizability)"*.
- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :94-96: **"The arrangement is the substance."** Two entries with an identical ingredient set are *"different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."*
- All three repetition types share the goal (test the same hypothesis against the ground) and the atoms (a claim, an instrument, an observation, a ground). They differ in the bond between the repeat and the original:
  - direct: identity-like, *"two names, one referent"* (Fig 12 :66), but only if the pose is shared;
  - systematic: comparison (⋛), *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* (Fig 12 :86), with the change made intentionally;
  - conceptual: production (⇒) or convergence, a different route to the same atom, *"A produces B; B did not exist before"* (Fig 12 :77).
- **Result:** under Fig 12 they are **isomers**, not spelling variants. Same atoms, different bond, different evidential substance. Writing all three as "replication" is the flat-formula error of Fig 12 :5, *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two of the three types can be swapped without changing either the evidential weight or the required conditions, the isomer claim fails and they are one term with three spellings. A survey showing that practitioners use the labels interchangeably would not settle this; what would settle it is a case where the same atoms under two of the bonds receive different warrant from a ground.

### Q4 : What is the order among the three?

- Fig 12 :103-104: **"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."** **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."**
- Direct repetition is first-order: the same method applied again, one crossing.
- Systematic repetition is second-order: the first-order result becomes an operand and is compared against a deliberately changed condition.
- Conceptual repetition is a different route to a first-order result, and under Fig 8 :50 it is *"corroboration at maximum independence"* if the route was chosen independently and the provenance is recorded.
- This is a recursion, not a hierarchy of quality. Fig 13 :4-5: *"The two kinds are in lockstep, a crossing is a change of role rather than a step upward, and no member of the pair is senior to the other."* A conceptual repeat is not "better" than a direct one; it answers a different question.

### Q5 : Where does "different methods make direct replication impossible" sit?

- Fig 6 :29-30: inward and outward are *"two tracks"* and *"Neither answer tells you much about the other."* Sharpening your own instrument (inward) and finding a frame that already holds the atom (outward) are not readings of one another.
- Symbols table :82: the frame transform F *"Cannot be run without a pose."* Without a shared pose there is no map between the two readings, so a direct repeat is not merely hard, it is not the same operation.
- Fig 11 :66-70: isolation *"is substitution of one habitat for another"*, with invisible losses and introductions. Changing the method is a substituted habitat. A synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability; Fig 11 :70: *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."*
- Fig 10 :80-81: frames *"whose correlation is fixed at origin can only be rotated,"* so repeated use does not make them less alike. Repetition cannot manufacture the missing pose.
- **Result:** the impossibility claim belongs to Q3's bond question, not to the count of repeats. A direct repeat needs the identity bond, and the identity bond needs a pose. Where the pose is absent, the available bonds are comparison and production, which are different operations with different warrants.

## CONCLUSION

The strengthening question and the method question form a **union**, settled in a fixed dependency order (anchor, independence, agreement, commensurability). The three repetition types are **isomers** under Fig 12: same atoms, different bond, different substance. Direct repetition is the identity bond and requires a shared pose; where the pose is missing, the direct form is unavailable and the remaining forms are indirect, with provenance carrying the warrant. This rescues the useful distinction without adding a primitive or a new operator. What remains unproven is whether any historical case actually met all three conditions; that is a name, and names need protocols and ground readings.
