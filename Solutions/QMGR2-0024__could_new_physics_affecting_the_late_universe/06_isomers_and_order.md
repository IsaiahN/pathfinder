## RESIDUAL

The audit left four open structures: (1) is late-universe new physics an import or a mint;
(2) is the residual a union or a partition; (3) in what order must the pieces be searched;
(4) are early-time and late-time proposals isomers over a shared shape. Each is derived below with
a falsifier.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Import or mint?

- Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."
- Fig 6 :22: "NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world. IMPORT adds an atom, and the
  added atom is what expands the closure."
- A re-fit of w within a fixed wCDM model uses held atoms: a mint. A new dynamical field or a
  modified-gravity sector adds an atom: an import.
- Fig 6 :14: "the only operator that moves the wall" is import. Import carries a source gate
  (provenance) and a shadow test (it must explain a named gap), Fig 6 :11-13.
- ⇒ Late new physics is an import, cannot be derived from ΛCDM's closure, and is not forbidden.
  Triangulated (Fig 6 and Fig 13 :115).
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a late-time effect that is already expressible in ΛCDM's closure and that
  closes the residual by re-arrangement alone; then it is a mint and no import is owed.

### Q2 - Union or partition?

- Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of
  separate ones."
- Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two
  conditions is not a harder case of either."
- The three slots overlap in data (SNe appear in both ladders; θ* appears in early inference and
  inverse ladder). A lever on one condition leaves the intersections standing.
- Pantos and Perivolaropoulos 2026 (world, secondary) report a 6.5σ ladder-versus-rest tension,
  larger than Planck-SH0ES, supporting the union reading.
- ⇒ Union, not partition. Triangulated for the figure rule; single-source for the physics number.
- **Falsifier:** show the three slots share one dominant systematic so their readings are
  perfectly correlated; the union then collapses to one frame.

### Q3 - What order, and which instrument first?

- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."
- Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked
  at."
- Fig 6 :32: "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument
  already returning something."
- Order: (1) per-slot residual (Fig 1), (2) effect description (Fig 9 :27-28), (3) frame choice,
  (4) import test (Fig 5 guards), (5) ground settlement (Fig 2).
- Fig 8 keeps SEARCHED / INWARD / OUTWARD distinct. SEARCHED: is the import already in the closure?
  No (Fig 8 :32). INWARD: sharpen low-z instruments (inverse ladder, null test). OUTWARD: import a
  frame whose closure predicts the shape. Inward and outward are opposed; never read one against
  the other.
- ⇒ The null test (Kouniatalis and Saridakis 2026) is an INWARD move built on existing lensing and
  siren instruments. A new dark-energy field is an OUTWARD move. Both are legitimate, but they
  answer different sub-questions of the union and cannot be pooled.
- **Falsifier:** if the null test's dimensionless ratio were shown to be already fixed by the
  acoustic scale alone, it would not discriminate early from late and the order above would need
  re-census.

### Q4 - Are early-time and late-time proposals isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is
  invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is
  a fact about a level and never about the world"
- Over the shared atoms {θ*, r_d, H(z), S8}, an early-time proposal (changes r_d) and a late-time
  proposal (changes H(z) at fixed r_d) are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different
  predicted observables. This is why the World Cup comparison (Schöneberg et al. 2026) can rank
  them on one footing: they are alternative arrangements of the same held set.
- The exemplar's QMGR-0037 finding is mirrored: a weaker "functional group" reading applies if
  the two proposals do not share all atoms. Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose
  presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." If one
  proposal requires a new field the other does not, they are functional-group cousins, not isomers.
- World corroboration: Schöneberg et al. find early models best and late models no better than
  ΛCDM; that is a ranking *within* the shared arrangement space, not a derivation of the isomer
  claim.
- **Falsifier:** if a proposed late-time fix required an atom absent from the early-time set
  (for example a new long-range force with no early analogue), the isomer claim fails and the
  functional-group reading stands.

## Q5 - The shape wall as an analogue, not an identity

- Sabogal et al. 2026 report δH0/H0 ≈ δI/I at fixed r_d and θ_s, capping late-time changes at about
  2% against about 8% needed.
- Fig 6 :14 names "the only operator that moves the wall" (import); Fig 8 :32 gives closure
  idempotence. The physical wall is a constraint surface; the figure wall is closure of a library.
- The analogue is structural: both say a closed set cannot generate what it lacks. The identity is
  refused and logged as strain S1 in `01`. A figure cannot derive a numerical 2% bound.

## CONCLUSION

Four structures survive with falsifiers attached. Late new physics is an import, the residual is a
union, the search order is localize before import, and early versus late proposals are isomers over
a shared atom set when they share all four atoms. The shape wall is an analogue, not a figure
result. None of this names the physics. The shared falsifier across Q1 to Q4 is the same one the
audit pre-registered: a late-time-only import that delivers the required H0 shift at fixed
acoustic scale and sound horizon while keeping BAO, SNe and growth fits would refute the framing
and require a new census.
