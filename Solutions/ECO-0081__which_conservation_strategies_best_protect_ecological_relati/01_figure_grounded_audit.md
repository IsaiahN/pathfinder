## 1. The residual, stated in effect terms

A manager chooses a strategy S from a set that includes protected areas, single-species recovery, habitat restoration, ecosystem-based management, invasive-species control, aided migration, and ex-situ breeding with reintroduction. For each S the prediction is a pair:

- species slot: does the target population persist at the ground?
- relationship slots: do the interactions that make the target and its community viable (pollination, predation, competition, dependency) persist at the ground?

R is the gap between those predictions and what the ground returns. It is a vector over slots, not a scalar. The question is which S drives the largest number of slots to small R, including the second- and third-order slots that a first-order species count misses.

Arity: two kinds of slot, atoms (species) and bonds (relationships). Symmetry: bonds are typed and directional in effect; a relationship is a relation, not a kind, and its valence depends on the residual (Fig 11 :49-51). Scale: first order (direct contact) and second or third order (cascades such as DDT to eagles, Fig 11 :52-53); also across scales (site to landscape, Fig 4).

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a rule that ranks strategies by per-slot residual over an enumerated habitat, scored against a measured ground, with cost and feasibility used only as budget filters. That is the sought-for shape. Binding it now makes it refusable by the same test as everything else.

## 2. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Two kinds of slot; typed directional relations; first to third order. It is a **union compound**, not one hard question: species persistence and relationship persistence overlap, and a single lever clears only the region where one condition holds (Fig 9 :54-58).
- **Domain 1, the habitat and field.** Actors: species, abiotic conditions, interactions. Currency: contact and cascade. Anchor: field measurement of persistence. Nameable anchor exists, so this is a domain. Governed by Fig 11.
- **Domain 2, the management seat.** Actors: agency, monitoring instruments, introduced actors. Currency: asymmetric power (introduction available, exclusion not). Anchor: the stated goal, which is persistence of the residual. Governed by Fig 11 :55-70 and Fig 3.
- **Domain 3, the verification and evidence frame.** Actors: researchers, reviewers, monitoring instruments. Currency: agreement versus residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Governed by Fig 2, Fig 4, Fig 10.
- **Partition or union.** The two goals are a union (Fig 9 :54). A species-only strategy and a relationship-only strategy each clear their own region; the intersection is a compound with its own loop.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Perceive the actors and relations, acquire terms for what a situation affords, state the objective, then plan, then learn and carry. Describe the residual before choosing a strategy (Fig 9 :27-28). Refuse before mint (Formula step 2).

Strain test applied during the census: Fig 11 :61 (introduction is available) and Fig 11 :65 (cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated) do not strain. One is a statement of power, the other a statement about its unenumerated use. Re-census not required.

## 4. Findings F1 to F12

Each finding states the verbatim figure line first, then the consequence, then its own refuter, then mode and triangulation status.

### F1 : A species list is half the answer

Fig 12 :5 "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
Fig 12 :46-47 "A recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself."
Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance."
Fig 11 :47 "relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"

A strategy scored only by species richness or population counts records the atoms and drops the bonds. The bond carries information the species do not, so the ranking is structurally blind to the very thing the question asks about. World corroboration is secondary: Simmons et al. 2020 state that "Interactions between species generate the functions on which ecosystems and humans depend."

**Refuter:** show that a species list plus abundances fully predicts interaction persistence, so the bond carries no information beyond the atoms.
**Mode:** `general` (structural). **Triangulated:** Fig 12 plus Fig 11, low overlap between the composition figure and the habitat figure.

### F2 : The residual is per slot, never averaged

Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
Fig 1 :28 "The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."

A single biodiversity index that folds species and interactions into one number hides the live slot that is failing. The question is answered by a vector of slots, and a strategy is ranked by how it moves each component.

**Refuter:** demonstrate a case where an averaged metric preserves a live slot signal that no per-slot reading shows.
**Mode:** `general`. **Triangulated:** Fig 1 plus Fig 9's union rule.

### F3 : Enumerate the habitat, never compose it, and follow the cascade

Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."
Fig 11 :44 "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."
Fig 11 :48 "You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."
Fig 11 :52-53 "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."

A strategy cannot be scored before the habitat is enumerated. Scoping to direct contact is exactly how a distant effect becomes the dominant residual. The EPA CADDIS case record (opened 2026-11-23) independently reports that DDT caused reproductive failure through eggshell thinning and that the ban was followed by population recovery, corroborating the cascade at the ground without being used as the verdict.

**Refuter:** show a strategy whose ranking is invariant to adding second- and third-order contacts to the habitat list.
**Mode:** `general` (rule), `specified` (DDT case). **Triangulated:** Fig 11 plus Fig 8 :39 (a search finding nothing is not proof of absence); world source secondary.

### F4 : Valence is relative to the residual, not a property of a species

Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."
Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

There is no universally good strategy or universally good species. A predator is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another. Any ranking that assumes a standing list of beneficial actors has already changed the residual without saying so.

**Refuter:** exhibit a standing actor list whose valence is invariant across all residuals in the decision.
**Mode:** `general`. **Single-source** for the figure rule (Fig 11), with secondary world corroboration from Runge et al. 2019 that single-species umbrella coverage is not generally adequate.

### F5 : Management is asymmetric and never finished

Fig 11 :61 "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."
Fig 11 :63 "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."
Fig 11 :64 "adds to a habitat it still did not author."

A manager can add an actor and cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world. So "set and forget" protected areas and one-off reintroductions are category errors. A strategy that does not budget for permanent maintenance is not a strategy that protects relationships. Jones, Lawton and Shachak 1994 (opened 2026-11-23) supply the world vocabulary for the same asymmetry: organisms "modify, maintain and create habitats."

**Refuter:** show a strategy whose relationship residual stays near zero with no continuing maintenance after installation.
**Mode:** `general`. **Triangulated:** Fig 11 plus Fig 12 :119 (a part that survives its own use is a catalyst, not the default).

### F6 : Isolation substitutes a habitat; it does not remove one

Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."
Fig 11 :67 "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."
Fig 11 :68 "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"
Fig 11 :69 "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"
Fig 11 :70 "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."
Fig 11 :39-40 "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

Ex-situ breeding, artificial propagation and offset habitats substitute a habitat. They have two silent failure modes: what was not reproduced, and what came along uninvited. A captive population that is demographically secure but has lost its interactions proves wiring, never capability. Snyder et al. 1996 (opened 2026-11-23) corroborate at the world level: "Captive breeding should be viewed as a last resort in species recovery and not a prophylactic or long-term solution" and "it should not displace habitat and ecosystem protection."

**Refuter:** show a substituted habitat that maintains the species' wild capability and its interaction set with no reintroduction of contact.
**Mode:** `general` (isolation law), `specified` (captive-breeding case). **Triangulated:** Fig 11 plus Snyder et al. 1996 as secondary corroboration.

### F7 : The ground is a measurement, not agreement

Fig 2 :29-30 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."
Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"
Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."
Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."

The ranking of strategies is settled by field measurement of species and interaction persistence, by frames that do not share an evidence pool. Endorsement by a conservation organisation with a reputation tied to a strategy is a stake-inducing channel, not an anchor: Fig 10 :59 "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying". Because extinction is irreversible, the cost of being wrong is high, so independence must be bought in proportion.

**Refuter:** show a strategy comparison settled by agreement among stakeholders sharing the same monitoring data and the same institutional frame, with no independent measurement.
**Mode:** `general`. **Triangulated:** Fig 2 plus Fig 10, different figures and different failure genera.

### F8 : The two goals are a union, so unbundle before searching

Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
Fig 9 :56-57 "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."
Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

A species-only lever and a relationship-only lever each clear their own region. The intersection, where both conditions bind, must be treated as a specific compound with its own loop. Cost, feasibility and public support are budget filters, never witnesses for which strategy is best. Polak et al. 2015 (opened 2026-11-23) corroborate at the world level: "Planning simultaneously for species and ecosystem targets delivered the most efficient outcomes for both sets of targets, while planning first for ecosystems and then filling the gaps to meet species targets was the most inefficient conservation strategy."

**Refuter:** show a single lever that closes both slots in the intersection without a dedicated compound.
**Mode:** `general`. **Triangulated:** Fig 9 plus Fig 1; world source secondary.

### F9 : Describe the residual before picking the strategy; the figures return a shape

Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"
Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"

Choosing "protected areas" or "ecosystem-based management" first and then mapping the residual onto it is the named inversion, echo without shadow. The canonical README states the limit directly: the framework reports "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." So this audit does not declare a winning strategy. It declares the shape any winner must have.

**Refuter:** produce a figure line that names a specific strategy as the best one across habitats and residuals.
**Mode:** `general`. **Single-source** for the figure rule (Fig 9), confirmed by the canonical README.

### F10 : Most effort lands above the broken link

Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
Fig 3 :29-30 "Most improvement effort in practice lands above the break, because that is where the measurements already exist."

Species counts are a well-instrumented link. Interaction persistence is the middle link with no adequate instrument, which is where chains usually break. Effort spent refining species counts cannot rescue the interaction link. Simmons et al. 2020 note that "interactions are easier to miss than species during sampling", corroborating the missing instrument at the world level.

**Refuter:** show an interaction-persistence instrument as well-deployed and as cheap as a species count, so the middle link is measured.
**Mode:** `general`. **Triangulated:** Fig 3 plus Fig 6 :32, with world corroboration secondary.

### F11 : Only methods cross scales, with a measured gap

Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
Fig 4 :25 "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."
Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."

A strategy result from one site is a description of one occasion. What can be carried to another site is the monitoring method and the head start, with a round-trip gap R_T measured, not assumed zero. McDonald-Madden et al. 2016 corroborate the need for structure-aware assessment: "indices of species importance common in food-web and network theory can be a poor guide to ecosystem management, resulting in significantly more extinctions than necessary."

**Refuter:** show a strategy result that transfers between sites with R_T = 0 and no method adaptation.
**Mode:** `general`. **Single-source** for the figure rule (Fig 4); world source secondary.

### F12 : Instruments are improved from instruments already returning something

Fig 6 :32 "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."
Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
Fig 8 :53 "Residual first, frame second."

Interaction monitoring is extended from coarse indicators already returning a weak signal (visitation rates, occupancy, network position), not specified from an account of the ideal network. An interaction that no current instrument resolves is residual, not absent. The frame is chosen after the residual is described.

**Refuter:** show an interaction-monitoring instrument specified from a pure description with no predecessor returning a signal.
**Mode:** `general`. **Triangulated:** Fig 6 plus Fig 8.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

"A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument." The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A strategy closes R on both slots with a single averaged metric and no per-slot residual, that is, aggregation does not throw away a live signal (breaks Fig 1 :19-22, F2).
2. Relationships are fully specified by a species list plus abundances, so the bond carries no information beyond the atoms (breaks Fig 12 :5, :46-47, F1).
3. A strategy that changes no contact with the habitat (pure ex-situ or synthetic substitution) demonstrably maintains wild capability and the interaction set (breaks Fig 11 :39-40, :66-70, F6).
4. A measurement shows one named strategy dominating all others on both slots across habitats, with low-overlap independent replication; then the shape-not-a-name limit is a budget limit, not a structural one (breaks F9).
5. A standing actor list is found whose valence is invariant across all residuals in the decision (breaks Fig 11 :49-51, F4).

If any fires, the shape is narrowed or withdrawn. Until one is run, nothing here is `grounded`. These were written before section 7, so they cannot be charged with being fitted to the conclusion.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Do not publish a single ranked list of named strategies. Publish the shape and the per-slot scoring rule (F1, F2, F9).
2. Enumerate the habitat to second and third order before scoring any strategy (F3).
3. Treat species as atoms and relationships as bonds, and refuse any metric that records only the atoms (F1, F12).
4. Prefer in-situ, contact-preserving strategies over substitutions, and treat captive or synthetic approaches as last resorts with two declared silent failure modes (F6).
5. Budget management as permanent work, because introduction is available and exclusion is not (F5).
6. Unbundle the species goal from the relationship goal and score the intersection as its own compound (F8).
7. Settle with field measurement and independent frames; treat stakeholder endorsement as a declared stake, never an anchor (F7).
8. Use cost, feasibility and public support as budget filters only (Fig 9 :60).
9. Send the residual and the refuter first through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`; offer the theory only after a verdict is recorded (Fig 10 :52).

### Round trip gap R_T

Condensing the figures into "protect habitat and interactions, measured at the ground" drops four constraints: per-slot scoring (F2), bond typing (F1), the introduction/exclusion asymmetry (F5), and the substitution failure modes (F6). R_T is therefore nonzero and is recorded qualitatively, not numerically, because no ground reading exists. Treating the condensation as identical to the figures would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives: a best conservation strategy is one that minimises per-slot residual over an enumerated habitat, treating species as atoms and relationships as bonds, preserving in-situ contact, budgeting permanent maintenance, scoring the species-relationship intersection as a compound, and settling at a measured ground with independent frames. No named strategy is licensed by the figures. Strains after re-census: none hidden; one, the introduction-power versus introduction-safety question, was dissolved by re-census. The verbatim figure lines used, with their `Fig N :line` citations, are listed in `09_watson_answer.md` and re-checked in `11_audit.md`.
