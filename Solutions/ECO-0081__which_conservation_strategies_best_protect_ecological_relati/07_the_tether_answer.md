# The Tether's answer on conservation strategies

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the per-slot predictions. Not `grounded`.** A shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A strategy is asked to keep a species persisting and to keep the relationships it depends on persisting. Those are different slots, species as atoms and relationships as bonds. The residual is the vector of gaps between prediction and field observation over both slots, measured per slot and never averaged (Fig 1 :19). The two goals are a union, not a partition: a single lever clears only the region where one condition holds (Fig 9 :54-58). So the question is not one ranking problem and must be unbundled before it is searched.

## 2. The shape

1. **Enumerate the habitat by contact, never compose it.** List the species, conditions and relations, then what contacts those, outward until the cascade stops mattering (Fig 11 :43-44, :47). Score no strategy before that list exists.
2. **Score per slot, never averaged.** Species and relationships are separate slots, and an averaged biodiversity index throws away the only signal that says where the failure is (Fig 1 :19, :22, :28).
3. **Count bonds as well as atoms.** A species list records half the problem; the bond carries the rest (Fig 12 :5, :46-47, :94). Two strategies with the same species list and different interaction wiring are isomers, a checkable claim with a falsifier in `06`.
4. **Preserve in-situ contact.** Capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone; an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing (Fig 11 :39-40). Isolation substitutes a habitat, and the two silent failure modes are what was not reproduced and what came along uninvited (Fig 11 :66-70).
5. **Budget permanent work.** Introduction is available and exclusion is not, so a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state (Fig 11 :61, :63).
6. **Treat species and relationship goals as a union.** Unbundle before searching and score the intersection as its own compound (Fig 9 :54-58). Cost, feasibility and public support are budget filters, never witnesses (Fig 9 :60).
7. **Settle at a measured ground with independent frames.** The anchor is a measurement that does not move when a frame pushes on it (Fig 2 :29-30). Agreement among frames sharing an evidence pool is disqualifying for a verdict (Fig 2 :40); buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42). A stakeholder with a reputation tied to a strategy declares that interest (Fig 10 :59).
8. **Carry methods, not recordings, across sites.** Only methods cross up and only head starts come down; measure the round-trip gap rather than assuming it is zero (Fig 4 :22, :30).
9. **No named winner.** The figures return a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32). Any name would have to come from a provenanced import or a ground measurement.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any named strategy, protected area, ecosystem-based management, restoration, invasive control, assisted migration or captive breeding, is the best one. That name is not derivable from the figures.
- Not that species counts are useless. They are a well-instrumented first-order link; they are simply not sufficient for the relationship slots (Fig 3 :27).
- Not that ex-situ work is never justified. It is a substitution with two declared silent failure modes and should be a last resort, corroborated secondarily by Snyder et al. 1996.
- Not that cost or public support decide the verdict. They decide the budget (Fig 9 :60).
- Not `grounded`. No field measurement was taken in this derivation.

## 4. What would settle it : the ground

A source-discriminating instrument and a field measurement, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

- **Interaction instrument:** does a monitoring method already return a weak signal on interaction persistence that can be improved, rather than specified from nothing? Fig 6 :32 "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."
- **Per-slot test:** under a candidate strategy, does the species slot and each relationship slot trend down or stay flat? A residual that persists and does not trend down is the one to point a refusal at (Formula step 2).
- **Isomer test:** do two strategies over the same species set with different interaction wiring return different per-slot residuals at the ground?
- **Order test:** does a third-order cascade overturn a first-order species gain, as the DDT case did?
- **Substitution test:** does a substituted habitat maintain wild capability and the interaction set, or do the two silent failure modes appear?
- **Standing falsifiers from `01` section 5:** an averaged metric that preserves a live slot signal; a species list plus abundances that fully predicts interaction persistence; a standing actor list invariant across residuals; a named strategy dominating on both slots with independent replication.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The enumeration of the habitat is never complete, and an interaction that no instrument resolves is residual, not absent: Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." The six-term set and the species-and-interaction slot split are a visible choice, not a neutral discovery (*They're Scanning Books*). The answer stops at the limit of available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity" (*How Intelligence Actually Works*).

A specific winning strategy could be named only by importing a value from outside the figures, with a source gate and a shadow test, or by a ground reading. Until then the shape is the deliverable.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name a winning conservation strategy. It gives the shape any winner must have. Species are atoms and relationships are bonds, so a species list alone records half the problem; the residual must be scored per slot and never averaged. The habitat is enumerated by contact and followed outward, because a third-order cascade can overturn a first-order species gain, as DDT did to the eagles. Management is asymmetric: you can add an actor and cannot subtract the habitat, so it is permanent work, and captive or synthetic substitution hides two silent failure modes. Species persistence and relationship persistence are a union, so cost and public support are filters for the budget, not witnesses for the verdict. The shape is settled by field measurement and independent frames, not by agreement. The slot for the winning name stays open until a ground reading fills it.
