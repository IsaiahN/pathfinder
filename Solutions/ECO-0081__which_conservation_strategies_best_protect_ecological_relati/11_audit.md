## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :19-28 (per-slot), Fig 11 :43-70 (habitat, cascade, asymmetry, substitution), Fig 12 :5, :117 (atoms and bonds), Fig 9 :54-58 (union).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07` section 2 and `08` section 1: enumerate the habitat, score per slot, count bonds, preserve contact, budget maintenance, unbundle the union, settle at a ground.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete strategy ranking, for example "strict protected areas with a species monitoring index beat single-species recovery".

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **bond typing** (Fig 12 :46-47), which a species index does not record;
- the **union intersection** (Fig 9 :55-58), which a single lever leaves standing;
- the **asymmetry and substitution rules** (Fig 11 :61, :66-70), which a set-and-forget reserve design ignores;
- the **independent-ground requirement** (Fig 2 :40, :42), which a stakeholder-designed monitoring index fails.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a structure must sit between the species count and the relationship result, and the habitat must be enumerated before scoring. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the figures" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
