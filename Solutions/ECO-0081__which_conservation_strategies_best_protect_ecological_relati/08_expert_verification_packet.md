# Expert verification packet : the Tether on conservation strategies

**What this is:** a claim in a form a conservation scientist or practitioner can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the per-slot predictions. Not `grounded`.** No field measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status of this packet:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A best conservation strategy cannot be ranked by a species count, because species are atoms and relationships are typed bonds between them, and a formula that records only the ingredients has recorded half the problem. The residual is a vector over slots, measured per slot and never averaged. The habitat is enumerated by contact and followed outward, since a third-order cascade can overturn a first-order species gain. A manager can add an actor and cannot subtract the habitat, so management is permanent work, and isolation substitutes a habitat rather than removing one. Species persistence and relationship persistence form a union, so the intersection needs its own compound, and cost is a budget filter rather than a witness. The figures return this shape; they do not name a winning strategy.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 : framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 11 and 12

- **Statement:** species and relationships are different kinds in the composition, so a single averaged biodiversity score is structurally blind to the relationship slots.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :5 "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Also Fig 12 :46-47 "A recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself." Also Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Also Fig 11 :47 "relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency".
- **Refuter:** show that a species list plus abundances fully predicts interaction persistence, so the bond carries no information beyond the atoms; or show an averaged metric that preserves a live slot signal no per-slot reading shows.

### C2 : ecology-facing; checkable by a conservation scientist against real systems

- **Statement:** the order of effects is by dependency out to third order, and a third-order interaction loss can overturn a first-order species gain. Two strategies over the same species set with different interaction wiring are isomers and can therefore differ in outcome.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :44 "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." Also Fig 11 :52-53 "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." Also Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- **What to check:** is there a real case where a strategy improved the target species at first order and lost a relationship two or three steps out? Does the US EPA CADDIS record of DDT and eggshell thinning fit the figure's cascade description? Are there two sites with the same species list and different wiring whose outcomes differ?
- **Refuter:** show a strategy whose ranking is invariant under adding second- and third-order contacts; or show two strategies over the same species set with different wiring returning identical per-slot residuals at the ground.

### C3 : management-facing; checkable against practice

- **Statement:** management is asymmetric and permanent; isolation substitutes a habitat and hides two failure modes.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :61 "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." Also Fig 11 :63 "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state." Also Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Also Fig 11 :68 "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails" and Fig 11 :69 "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts".
- **What to check:** does a reintroduction or ex-situ program that stops maintenance still hold its interaction set? Does Snyder et al. 1996 support the last-resort reading?
- **Refuter:** show a substituted habitat that maintains wild capability and the interaction set with no continuing reintroduction of contact; or show a strategy whose relationship residual stays near zero with no maintenance after installation.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a theory of everything and not a final ranking. No figure line names a winning strategy.
- Not that species counts are useless. They are the measured link; relationships are the under-instrumented link (Fig 3 :27).
- Not that habitat protection alone is sufficient, and not that any named approach is excluded.
- Not that cost, feasibility or public support decide the verdict. They are budget filters (Fig 9 :60).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken in this derivation.
- Not that the species-and-interaction slot split is nature's own list. It is a visible choice and is recorded as such.

## 5. What would settle it

A field measurement and a source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. Does an interaction-monitoring method already return a weak signal that can be improved, rather than being specified from nothing (Fig 6 :32)?
2. Does a candidate strategy drive each slot residual down, or does one residual persist and not trend down?
3. Does a third-order cascade overturn a first-order species gain?
4. Does a substituted habitat hold its interaction set without continued contact?
5. Do two strategies over the same species set with different wiring return different per-slot residuals?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, :22; Fig 11 :43-44, :47, :61, :66, :68-69; Fig 12 :5, :117. These are short passages.
2. Check C2 against a case you know: was there a first-order species gain and a second- or third-order relationship loss?
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish an intact interaction set from a species-only recovery, or say why none is possible.
4. Check C3 against a reintroduction you know: was the habitat substituted, and which of the two silent failure modes appeared?
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
