**Subject:** One checkable claim about conservation strategies, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a checkable output and would like you to test it against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question in effect terms.** A conservation strategy is asked to do two jobs: keep a target species persisting, and keep the relationships it depends on (pollination, predation, competition, dependency) persisting. Those are different slots. The residual is the gap between what a strategy predicts for each slot and what the field returns. It is a vector, not a single score.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the per-slot predictions, not `grounded`):**
>
> 1. A species list records only the atoms. Relationships are the bonds, and the bond carries information the species do not. Any ranking that averages species and relationships into one index has thrown away the only signal that says where the failure is.
> 2. The habitat must be enumerated by contact and followed outward. Scoping to direct contact is how a distant effect becomes the dominant residual, as with DDT reaching eagles several steps out.
> 3. A manager can add an actor and cannot subtract the habitat. Managed ecosystems are permanent work, not solved states.
> 4. Isolation is substitution of a habitat, not removal. Captive or synthetic approaches inherit two silent failure modes: what you failed to reproduce, and what you unintentionally introduced.
> 5. Species persistence and relationship persistence are a union, not a partition. A single lever clears only the region where one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing.
> 6. Cost, feasibility and public support are filters for the budget, never witnesses for the verdict.
>
> This is a shape any best strategy must have. It is **not** a claim that protected areas, ecosystem-based management, or any other named strategy is the winner. The figures return a shape, not a name.
>
> **What would refute it (written down before this message):**
> 1. an averaged metric that preserves a live slot signal no per-slot reading shows;
> 2. a species list plus abundances that fully predicts interaction persistence;
> 3. a substituted habitat that maintains wild capability and the interaction set with no reintroduction of contact;
> 4. a field measurement showing one named strategy dominating on both slots across habitats with independent replication;
> 5. a standing actor list whose valence is invariant across all residuals.
>
> If any holds, I will narrow or withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical figures README: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Simmons et al. 2020, estimating the risk of species interaction loss: https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.3000843
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and that the output is only as good as its validator. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> [your name]
