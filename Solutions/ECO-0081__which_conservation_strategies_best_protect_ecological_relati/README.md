**Question:** Which conservation strategies best protect ecological relationships as well as individual species?

**Source:** ecology.txt line 100

**Section:** 9. Human activity, conservation, and restoration

## What this package answers, in effect terms

A conservation decision chooses a strategy and predicts two things: that a target species persists, and that the relationships on which it and the wider community depend (pollination, predation, competition, dependency) persist. The residual is the gap between each prediction and what the ground returns, measured per slot, never averaged. The question asks for a ranking of strategies by how small that gap gets across both kinds of slot. The figures return the shape any best strategy must have. They do not return the name of a single winning strategy, and this package does not pretend otherwise.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan and no receptiveness column.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **Agreement is not an anchor.** Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." A list assembled for likely agreement is a list selected to agree.
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures exactly the correlation that makes their agreement worthless.
3. **A verifier must not hold the interiors.** Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, not the source.

Legally: unsolicited bulk email to scraped personal addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one. Public opt-in venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv` avoid both.
