## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical README says the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)". Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence", and :135: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

From *The Library of Babel*, read directly: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question." And: "With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."

So for this question a **shape** is: the statement that randomness changes coverage, cost and residual but not the kind of reach, plus the two explicitly open slots, `NEW KIND(???)` and `ONTIC(???)`. A name would be asserting that randomness is ontic, or that P equals BPP, or that it is a seventh primitive. All three are forbidden by the shape limit, the first because the framework calls it open, the second because it needs a ground, the third because it would add a member to a closed set.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*, direct: use the vocabulary already held and closest to the thing, "connect and correlate combinations of the existence of your known words and their relative proximity to commonly recurring new terms that you are unfamiliar with." Recurse until the space narrows.
- Fig 6 :30: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* (via exemplar): "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."
- *Library of Babel*, direct, on stopping: "You will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place, as there is nowhere else to search and nothing else that can describe the answer better." Here the stop is when the four domains no longer exchange anchors.

The residual was described before any frame was chosen. This matters: describing randomness first as "a thing that makes systems unpredictable" would have selected chaos theory as the frame and made every other field seem irrelevant. Describing it as "a change of reach, of cost, or of residual" let the four domains separate.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." The README: the framework "stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*They're Scanning Books When They Should* (via exemplar): "The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space." Also: "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible." The six are a visible guess, not nature's own list. Naming ontic randomness as *the* answer would turn a visible choice into an invisible one.

## 4. The specific temptation in this question

The Hard Problem is About Source (via exemplar) gives the mechanism. An instrument reads green and "cannot comment on the source"; one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK can give the same reading. "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." And: "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy." It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved it.

Here the reading is "the output looks random." Several sources give that reading: a compounded remainder (chaos), detail discarded at a crossing (noise), a pseudo-random generator the observer cannot invert, or a genuinely indeterministic process. The reading does not determine the source. Fig 6 :27: "Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same." What is owed is a discriminating instrument, not a name. That is exactly why F6 is a `gap` and not a guess.

## 5. Q2 answered: what licenses a particular structure in the slot?

Nothing in the framework. Fig 13 :143-144 settles this explicitly: "genuine indeterminacy is the open case, unresolved." The shape says randomness is a residual effect; it does not say which world the residual belongs to. A particular answer is licensed only by a ground reading or by a provenanced import with a shadow test. Until one of those, the answer is the searcher's own contribution and must be recorded as such.

## 6. Two consequences for this outreach

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, read directly: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige." Gathering famous names to agree that randomness is ontic would be the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask is a budget to try the refuter.

**Agreement can be wrong in a specific way.** Same essay, direct: "You may all agree and be wrong. You may set your clock to someone else's, who was setting your clock to yours, and be off by many deviations over time." This is Fig 2's coherent drift in plain language, and it is why the packet asks what would fail rather than what would be believed.

## 7. My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

Same essay, direct: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post. ... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of the claim and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on.

## 8. Correction carried forward from the exemplar

The exemplar corrected an earlier overstatement of "shape, not a name": the phrase governs a primitive *missing* from `Γ`, not the selection of atoms already held. I keep that correction here. Randomness, chaos and noise are not missing primitives to be smuggled in; they are derived effects of composition and loss (seat map substrate law). What is genuinely missing is an instrument that discriminates ontic from epistemic randomness, and the framework says so. Retracting the overstatement does not change the `gap`; it locates it correctly.
