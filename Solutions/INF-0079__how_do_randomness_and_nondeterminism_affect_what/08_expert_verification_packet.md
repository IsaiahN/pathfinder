# Expert verification packet - randomness, computation, prediction

**What this is:** a claim in a form a complexity theorist, information theorist or philosopher of probability can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion.

**Mode: `specified` on C1 to C3, `general` on the ontic stop. Not `grounded`.** No computation or measurement in this folder settles the claim.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question is a union of two conditions with different anchors. In computation, randomness and branching do not add a kind, so the compositional reach does not move; what changes is coverage, resource cost and the independence required of searchers. In prediction, randomness is the residual a turn leaves: an amplified remainder is chaos, and detail discarded at a crossing is noise. Fully deterministic dynamics can therefore be unpredictable beyond a finite horizon, and a failure to predict is not by itself evidence of a new primitive. There is a formal limit on proving incompressibility inside a fixed frame, and the question of whether the randomness is ontic is explicitly open in the framework. What is offered is this shape, three refuters written in advance, and the ground readings that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 13 and 6

- **Statement:** randomness and nondeterminism rearrange a closed set; they do not add a primitive or move the wall of what is composable.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :115 "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists." Fig 13 :116-118 "infinite effort inside a finite reach." Fig 6 :21 "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Fig 6 :25 "An import adds no atom to the world."
- **What to check:** is a random choice or a nondeterministic branch an operation on arrangements over held atoms, with no new atom? Does the figures' atom accounting really permit no other reading?
- **Refuter:** exhibit a randomized or nondeterministic machine computing a function outside the deterministic compositional reach, or derive a seventh kind from rearranging the six. If either fires, C1 is withdrawn.

### C2 - computation-facing; checkable against complexity theory

- **Statement:** the figure's "wall" is compositional expressibility, not a resource-bounded oracle model. Randomness changes search coverage, cost and independence, not the kind of reach. The two currencies, bits and actions, do not add.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :49 "more search at the same reach: is it in here and unfound? The closure does not change. Same atoms, same wall, more of the space actually visited." Fig 12 :125-126 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 6 :53-54 "searchers drawing on the same evidence are one searcher wearing many names."
- **What to check:** does the nondeterministic Turing machine article's reach claim (deterministic and nondeterministic machines solve the same problems, time may differ) match C1? Does the randomized algorithm article's resource reading (randomness as a resource, P versus BPP open) match C2 rather than contradict it? Is the vocabulary strain between compositional closure and resource-bounded solvability honestly named rather than smoothed?
- **Refuter:** show that correlated searchers buy reach at the same rate as independent ones, or that random sampling changes the kind of reach without an import, or that bits and actions can be added in a single unit that settles new predictions.

### C3 - prediction-facing; checkable against dynamical systems and probability

- **Statement:** randomness in prediction is a residual effect with two mechanisms, not a primitive. Whether the loss is ontic is open in the framework.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :28 "They alternate, and the alternation is where both the recursion and the randomness come from." Fig 13 :31-32 "a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise." Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." Fig 13 :143-144 "genuine indeterminacy is the open case, unresolved."
- **What to check:** does the chaos literature's finite predictability horizon under deterministic dynamics match the amplified-remainder mechanism? Does the Stanford Encyclopedia entry's separation of chance from randomness match the framework's open slot? Is there any figure line that settles ontic indeterminacy? There is not; this is the point.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a randomness in computation that is neither an amplified remainder nor discarded detail at a crossing, or produce a ground reading that settles ontic versus epistemic randomness. The second reverses the `gap` and is welcomed as a correction.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a determinism thesis, and not an indeterminism thesis. The ontic question is `gap`.
- Not that randomized algorithms never outperform deterministic ones under bounded resources. P versus BPP is open in the world literature and is not settled here.
- Not that Chaitin's theorem proves randomness does not exist. It limits proof within a fixed system.
- Not that all randomness is chaos. Chaotic amplification and discarded crossing detail are distinct mechanisms (isomers over the same atoms).
- Not one averaged answer across the four domains. Each has its own anchor.
- Not `grounded`. This packet carries a simulated derivation, not a measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

Ground readings, not agreement:
