## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*. Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."** Fig 13 :134-135: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence, It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the form a narrowed answer can take before the ground fills it. It constrains; it does not supply the value. For this question:

- **Shape:** a residual indexed by slot (economy state crossed with country crossed with identifying route), each component anchored to a measured output response, with overlapping conditions treated as compounds and a refuter per component.
- **Not the shape:** "the multiplier is 1.5", or "it is larger in recessions and smaller in open economies" as a figure-derived numeric claim. Those are secondary readings from the world literature, not outputs of the figures.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary already held that is closest to the thing, then recurse on new terms that overlap or correlate with it. *"With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."*
- The effect description here is: "one number does not predict the response across slots." That is a checkable claim about forecasts, not a preference for a theory.
- *Library of Babel* on malformed questions: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually."* This is exactly the Fig 9 union reading of recession, expansion and country.

## 3. What licenses a slot's value

Only two things, both outside the shape:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* Here that means an identified spending change plus the measured output response for that slot.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs**, with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :51). A published estimate can travel into the shape as a secondary reading only with its dataset, method and year recorded.

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the instrument warning: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"* and *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."* An estimated output response is a reading that can be produced by several structural sources (accommodative monetary policy, high slack, low leakage, or a large propensity to consume). Naming the source from the reading alone is the metamerism error. *"the answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* In economics that better instrument is an identification strategy that separates the conditions, not another average of the same reading.

## 4. The specific forbidden error for this question

Turning the world literature's range into "the Tether's multiplier" would be a name where the framework returns a shape, and it would be a source named from a projection. The literature's spread (0.8 to 1.5 in one survey, roughly 1 in an IMF downturn estimate, roughly 2 for post-2010 austerity, 0.7 to 0.9 higher in downturns in a meta-regression) is **the pattern of slots**, not a wandering around one true constant. Collapsing it into a headline number repeats the Fig 1 error: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

## 5. Q2 answered: what licenses a numeric value?

**Nothing in the figures.** A value is licensed only by a ground reading for that slot, or by a provenanced import with a shadow test, recorded as secondary. Until one of those, the number is the searcher's contribution and must be recorded as such. This is not a ban on using published estimates; it is a ban on presenting them as figure-derived.

## 6. Two consequences for this folder

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* A citation-weighted list of famous multiplier papers is a budget allocator, not a verdict. The ask in `02_outreach_email.md` gives the shape a budget to be tried and requests a refutation.

**This audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is not a test of the multiplier and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on.

## 7. Correction recorded

An earlier reading of this section treated "shape, not a name" as if it forbade mentioning any numeric estimate. That was overstated, and it is recorded here rather than erased. The canonical phrase governs a **missing** thing, one not held in the frame. Published multiplier estimates are held in the economics frame; they can be cited as secondary readings with provenance. What remains forbidden is presenting one as the framework's answer or as the output of the figures. Both claims are now visible so neither is lost.
