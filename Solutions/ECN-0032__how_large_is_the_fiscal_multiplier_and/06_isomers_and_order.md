# Isomers and order: what regime-dependent multipliers are

**Mode: `general` for the structural analogue, `specified` for the falsifiable claim.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The literature reports that the same fiscal instrument (a change in government purchases) produces different output responses in different regimes and countries. Two questions are owed:

1. Are the recession and expansion multipliers **isomers** under Fig 12's definition, or merely differently measured versions of one thing?
2. In what dependency order must the derivation run, so that a step is not taken above a broken link?

## DOMAIN CENSUS (supporting derivation)

Atoms held in the economics frame: the fiscal authority, government purchases `G`, households, firms, output `Y`, and the measurable response `ΔY/ΔG`. Conditions in contact: slack, the monetary reaction, debt and fiscal space, openness and exchange-rate regime, spending composition, confidence. Bonds available: the seven typed relations of Fig 12, plus negation. No new primitive is available without import, and none is claimed.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are they isomers?

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fig 12 :95-96: *"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."*

A recession multiplier and an expansion multiplier written with the same symbols (`ΔG`, `ΔY`, households, firms, fiscal authority) have the same ingredient set. What differs is the bond: under slack with an accommodative monetary reaction, the bond from `G` to `Y` runs through spare capacity and income; under a binding expansion with an offsetting monetary reaction, the corresponding bond runs through crowding-out and prices. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. That is Fig 12's isomer, not a scalar multiplied by a regime dummy.

**Scope limit, named.** This is an analogue at the level of the economics frame's own atoms. It is **not** a claim that government spending or output is one of Fig 13's six substrate terms. The Fig 13 mapping was re-censed out in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §3 because it strained the text.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if the two regimes can be written as the same molecule with a single scalar coefficient and no change in the bond type, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands instead (Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*).

### Q2 - What order does the derivation run in?

Fig 3 :20-21: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**

Fig 3 :26-27: *"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments. A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

The dependency order for this question:

1. **Perception.** Is there an identified change in purchases that is not predictable from real-time forecasts? Without it there is no anchor.
2. **Vocabulary.** Do we have terms for the conditions that the situation affords: slack, monetary reaction, openness, debt, composition? Without them a regression can only return an average.
3. **Objective.** What counts as progress: a residual that stops arriving per slot, not a tighter estimate of one constant.
4. **Planning.** Which estimator and which comparison across slots; chosen only after links 1 to 3 are in place.
5. **Learn and carry.** Which result can be carried to another country or episode, and which cannot because its conditions were not reproduced (Fig 11 :67-69: *"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."*).

A meta-regression that pools 1,800 estimates is work at step 4 or 5. If step 1 or 2 is broken in the underlying studies, that work cannot diagnose the break; it can only reprint it.

**Falsifier:** show an estimator that produces a stable, interpretable slot vector from spending changes that were fully anticipated in real time. If it does, the dependency order claim fails.

### Q3 - What is the union structure?

Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**

The compounds are not `recession OR expansion OR country`. They are intersections:

- slack AND lower bound binds AND monetary accommodation: one compound;
- slack AND high debt AND open economy: another;
- expansion AND normal policy AND closed economy: a third.

A lever that clears the single-condition region (say, adding a slack control) leaves the intersections standing, because the second and third conditions still bind (Fig 9 :55-56). This is why a single "state-dependent multiplier" still fails: it clears one condition and leaves the compound.

**Falsifier:** show that one condition plus an additive adjustment removes all residual structure; then the union claim fails and the partition reading stands.

### Q4 - Two currencies, held apart

Fig 12 :125-126: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add. Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**

A slot-indexed table is expensive to describe (bits) and may be cheap to test if the data already exist. A single headline number is cheap to describe and expensive to settle, because it must survive every regime it claims to cover. Neither price predicts the other, so both must be read. Summing them into one "best estimate" is the error the figure names, not the fix.

## CONCLUSION

Over the economics frame's shared atoms, a recession multiplier and an expansion multiplier are Fig 12 isomers: same ingredients, different regime bond, different substance. That is an analogue with `general` mode, not a claim about the six-term substrate. The derivation must run in Fig 3 dependency order, with identification before vocabulary before objective before estimator; a pooled average above a broken identification link is work at a step that cannot use it. The union structure is intersections of conditions, so clearing one condition leaves the compounds. Each claim has a falsifier written before the run.
