# Outreach draft: check the output before deciding about the frame

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter to a public submission through the venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** nothing in this email asks for agreement, trust, or endorsement. The recipient's verdict on stage 1 is recorded before any framework material is shown. If the verdict changes after stage 2, it is not pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).

---

**Subject:** Check one claim about fiscal multipliers before you decide whether the frame is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the testable parts):** the question "how large is the fiscal multiplier?" asks for one coefficient. A residual analysis says that is the wrong object. The figures index a residual by object slot, not by average: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated"* (Fig 1 :19), and *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average"* (Fig 1 :22). So the multiplier should be reported as a vector indexed by state of the economy, country, and identifying route, with each component anchored to its own measured output response. The variation across recessions, expansions and countries is a union of overlapping conditions, not noise around one constant: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound"* (Fig 9 :57-58).
>
> **What this does not claim.** It does not supply a number. The framework returns *"a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (Fig 9 :32). The published estimates I have read (roughly 0.8 to 1.5 in one survey, around 1 in downturns in an IMF study, higher in recessions in regime-switching work, roughly 2 for austerity in the Great Recession) are secondary evidence about the slots, not outputs of the figures. I am not asking you to accept any of them on my say-so.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written down before this email:**
> 1. One constant multiplier fitted on one set of slots predicts held-out output response as well as or better than a slot-indexed vector.
> 2. One condition, with no compound interactions, accounts for all the cross-country and cross-regime variation with no residual structure left.
> 3. Two estimates drawing on the same dataset and the same identifying assumptions are treated as independent witnesses and that changes the verdict.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw the shape claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **A question I would value:** for the episode and country you know best, what would you expect to see if the slot-indexed claim were true? I would like your answer to that before I show you any of the source material.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract in the solution folder): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Auerbach and Gorodnichenko, "Fiscal Multipliers in Recession and Expansion," NBER WP 17447: https://www.nber.org/papers/w17447
> - Ramey, "Can Government Purchases Stimulate the Economy?", Journal of Economic Literature 49(3), 2011: https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/jel.49.3.673
> - Baum and Poplawski Ribeiro, "Fiscal Multipliers and the State of the Economy," IMF WP 12/86: https://www.imf.org/en/publications/wp/issues/2016/12/31/fiscal-multipliers-and-the-state-of-the-economy-40146
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the figures and formula are public. Run them on a problem you know well and can check against a ground, and write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Two-stage offer, stated plainly

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual, the shape claim and the three refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict *before* showing any framework material.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the source links and the formula, with the note that the figures are the authority and that a reply after stage 2 is not corroboration.
3. **Stage 3, optional.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

Nothing is sent by this agent. You host and send. Do not use this text for a list selected for likely agreement; that manufactures the disqualifying correlation (Seat Map :163).
