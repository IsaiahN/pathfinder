## 0. The question as an effect, and the sought-for shape bound as an ordinary term

Question: how can the benefits of increasingly capable AI be distributed without concentrating power and opportunity?

**Effect statement of the residual.** Capability produces a benefit surplus. In the observed world that surplus tends to accrue to a small set of actors, and the controlling of the capability becomes the controlling of opportunity. The default prediction, that benefit distribution and power concentration trade off against each other, fails to resolve: we observe both broad diffusion of AI outputs and deepening concentration of the inputs (compute, data, talent, capital). The residual is the persistent gap between "benefits are spread" and "power and opportunity are not concentrated." We want the shape of an answer.

**Arity, symmetry, scale.** Arity: at least three conditions, producing benefits, allocating them across slots, and keeping power and opportunity from concentrating. Symmetry: "distribute benefits" is a transfer or allocation (symmetric in the sense that a unit can move), while "concentrate power" is a threshold or comparison (ordered, who holds more), so the two are not the same kind of relation. Scale: production is first order (a generator closing a residual), distribution is a cross-scale membrane question, and concentration is a population or room-chain question.

**The sought-for shape, written down and bound before the search** (Formula step "Direction": a frame cannot check the direction of its own question, so make the shape an ordinary term). Write:

> A distribution is acceptable only if the benefit residual is read per slot (not aggregated), the decision frames range against a ground that does not update on their own reports, the thing that crosses to the many is a method or head start rather than a recording of one occasion, the capability is carried by a reproduced habitat and not by the isolated model, and a seat exists that can refuse a verdict. The specific policy that does this is not named by the framework.

This shape term is what the findings below accrue residual against. It is written before the conclusion, as required, and is refusable by the same test as any other term.

## 3. Findings

Mode on every substantive claim is stated. `T` means triangulated across two figure lines or figure families with low overlap. `S` means single-source on the figure side. `G` means gap, no figure line governs.

### F1 — "Benefits" and "power and opportunity" are two currencies, not one stock (decisive, `specified`, T)

Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add. Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds." Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

The question fuses two different things. Benefit units are a transferable currency (what can be delivered to a slot). Power and opportunity are reach (what a slot can compose or access, the currency of closure). Spreading the first does not automatically spread the second. A Gini coefficient over benefit units is an aggregate that can fall while the capacity to act stays concentrated in a few slots, which is exactly the hidden-signal failure Fig 1 :22 names. The world literature corroborates at the level of effect: Acemoglu (NBER 2024, opened 2026-11-24) reports that *"there is also no evidence that AI will reduce labor income inequality. Instead, AI is predicted to widen the gap between capital and labor income."* Malhotra and Vogel (Roosevelt Institute 2025, opened 2026-11-24) put the split in policy language: *"With respect to means, predistribution policies strive to enhance capabilities and balance power, whereas redistribution policies reallocate resources."* These corroborate the two-currency split from economics and political economy; they do not decide it.

**Verdict:** the question is a union compound with two currencies and separate anchors. Verdicts per currency stay separate and are never averaged into one "distribution score."

**Refuter:** a demonstrated measure in which transferring a benefit unit to a slot also monotonically increases that slot's reachable composition set at the same rate, with an anchor that does not update on the measurement. Then the two currencies add and F1 collapses.

### F2 — Capability is agent-and-habitat; distributing the model is not distributing the capability (decisive, `specified`, T)

Fig 11 :39: "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."

An "increasingly capable AI" is not a portable object. It is an AI together with data, compute, complementary skills, instruments, and institutions. Handing a frontier model to a slot that lacks the habitat does not change that slot's contact, so by Fig 11 :40 it changes nothing capability-wise. What can cross the membrane is a method (a re-applicable generator) or a head start (something that makes the next search cheaper), not a recording of one occasion (Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."). The GovAI benefit-sharing report (Dennis, Manning, Clare et al. 2025, opened 2026-11-24) distinguishes *"sharing AI resources"*, *"expanding access to AI systems"*, and *"transferring a portion of the financial proceeds from AI commercialization or AI-driven economic growth"*; the first two are habitat or head-start transfers, the third is a pure benefit-unit transfer. The ecological-psychology corroborator is lower overlap: Nye and Silverman (Springer 2012, opened 2026-11-24) define an affordance as *"an action possibility formed by the relationship between an agent and its environment"*, which is the same relational claim as Fig 11 :39 in a different field.

**Verdict:** distribution that only moves benefit units or model outputs leaves the capability concentration intact. The lever is contact and habitat: instruments, data access, skills, the ability to check outputs, and the institutional seat that can refuse. `specified`, ungrounded.

**Refuter:** an experiment in which giving an isolated model (with no habitat change) to previously unequipped slots measurably increases their reachable composition set against a non-updating ground. That would break Fig 11 :40 at the ground and force a re-census.

### F3 — Concentration is a named collapse, and the remedy is triangulation, not negotiation (decisive, `specified`, T)

Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool" Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."

"Concentrating power" is not a vague political slogan here. It is the structural condition of Fig 2: frames range against each other instead of against an anchor, so they converge on whoever had the most influence, or they never talk and agree because one evidence pool is talking. A benefit-sharing forum whose members all read the same industry reports is one pool; its agreement about "fair distribution" is a convention, not a verdict about the world. If that convention is then allowed to fix an allocation outcome, Fig 10 :47 says it has stopped being a convention and become an ungrounded verdict. The remedy is independent frames ranging against an anchor that does not update, with independence bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42). OECD's 2024 AI Futures report (opened 2026-11-24 via the public report text) names the same object in policy terms as *"Excessive power concentration"* and notes that *"Access to important inputs like AI talent and compute are also distributed unequally worldwide"*.

**Verdict:** "without concentrating power" is re-grounded as a structural condition on the decision process (independent frames, non-updating anchor, refusal seat), not as an equality of outcome figure. `specified`, triangulated across Fig 2 and Fig 7 with low overlap between the anchor rule and the room-chain rule.

**Refuter:** a procedure that produces a distribution verdict at a ground that updates on the verdict itself, and can be shown to remain correct under that feedback. Then the non-updating-anchor requirement is refuted.

### F4 — Distribution must be read per slot; the aggregate is how the live concentration hides (strong, `specified`, T)

Fig 1 :19 and :22 (above). Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Fig 9 :30: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."

The middle link of Fig 3's chain is where this question breaks. We can perceive capability (benchmarks, adoption rates), and we can plan redistributions (taxes, transfers), but the link that says what would count as "not concentrating power" at a given slot has no agreed instrument. An aggregate living-standard or inequality index is a frame-internal summary that can improve while one slot loses all real access. The correct object is a per-slot reading against a counter that does not move when the reading changes. From *How Intelligence Actually Works* (opened 2026-11-24): *"The ground must be immovable by persuasion and not bi-directional like the frames are. The ground doesn't update on anything that the frames do."*

**Verdict:** per-slot verdicts, never averaged. The "one number" version of the question is refused as a frame-internal quantity. `specified`, triangulated across Fig 1 and Fig 3.

**Refuter:** a case where the aggregate distribution measure and the per-slot reach measure can be proven to move together for all slots under all residuals, with no live slot hidden. Then aggregation is harmless here.

### F5 — A one-off settlement cannot hold as capability rises (strong, `specified`, T)

Fig 10 :72: "When the question changes, the room should change. A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom." Fig 11 :63: "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state." Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

The question says "increasingly capable." That is a moving question. A distribution mechanism that was correct at capability level C does not remain correct at C+1 if its room stays fixed. The framework's answer is not a one-time allocation but a maintained arrangement with the seat able to re-read the per-slot residual and refuse. Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." An institution that distributes today can concentrate tomorrow without changing its own rules, because the residual changed.

**Verdict:** the answer is a maintained process, not a terminal allocation. Any claimed final settlement is refused (no last room, consistent with the canonical figures README). `general` on the structure, `specified` on the application.

**Refuter:** a demonstrable capability plateau at which the question stops moving for a bounded domain. Then a one-off settlement is admissible for that domain and F5 narrows.

### F6 — Importing a policy needs provenance and a shadow test; a wrong atom costs more than primitives (moderate, `specified`, S for the cost claim)

Fig 6 :24: "Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives." Fig 8 :50: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them" Fig 9 :27: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"

Every concrete policy (a tax, a public compute pool, a data trust, a UBI, an antitrust rule) is an import from the economics or policy frame into this derivation. It must carry provenance (where it came from, what gap it explains) and pass a shadow test (does it explain the per-slot residual we described?). Without that, any policy will seem to fit, which is Fig 9 :27's exact failure. TheWTO 2025 World Trade Report (opened 2026-11-24 via public report text) states the effect: *"without deliberate efforts by policymakers to broaden access and opportunity, the gains from AI may still be unevenly distributed"*; that is corroboration that access and opportunity are the operative conditions, not a figure verdict.

**Verdict:** no policy is named by the framework. Policies are bindings from other frames, recorded as such, with provenance. `specified` ungrounded. The "which policy" question is D5, a `gap`.

**Refuter:** a figure line that actually selects a tax rate, bound, or named institution. None was found after the census in section 1 and the re-census in section 7.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion, as a design instrument)

From the exemplar's discipline and the seat map's rule that *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device"*: the shape claim is refuted if any of the following fires.

1. **Two-currency failure:** a measure shows that moving a benefit unit to a slot raises that slot's reachable composition set one-for-one against a non-updating anchor, for all slots. (Breaks F1.)
2. **Agent-only capability:** an isolated model transfer, with no habitat or contact change, measurably increases the receiving slots' capability against the ground. (Breaks F2.)
3. **Agreement as verdict:** a distribution procedure whose frames share one evidence pool and whose anchor updates on their reports produces a verdict that survives independent re-testing. (Breaks F3.)
4. **Aggregate equivalence:** aggregate and per-slot reach measures are proven to move together for all residuals with no hidden live slot. (Breaks F4.)
5. **Terminal settlement:** a capability plateau is demonstrated for which a fixed one-off allocation remains correct as the residual changes. (Breaks F5.)
6. **A figure line governs a policy:** a verbatim figure line is found that selects a specific allocation rule, bound, or institution. (Breaks F7's `gap`.)

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the census is rerun. Until one is run, nothing here is `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Ask the question in effect terms first: which slot's residual, in which currency, against which anchor (F1, F4).
2. Treat the capability object as agent-and-habitat; design for contact introduction, not model handout (F2).
3. Require independent frames and a non-updating anchor for any distribution verdict; do not accept a stakeholder forum's agreement as a verdict (F3).
4. Maintain the arrangement; expect the room to change as capability changes (F5).
5. Name no policy as a figure result. Record any concrete policy as a provenanced binding with a shadow test (F6, F7).
6. Send the generator (the shape claim and its refuter), not a recording of this derivation (Fig 4 :25, Fig 10 :52).

## 7. Strains, re-census, and the three step-7 questions

**Applied in order, SEARCHED first.**

- **SEARCHED (cheapest, assumed by the other two).** I searched the verbatim figure text for any line selecting an allocation rule, ratio, or policy. None found. Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom." More searching inside the framework cannot manufacture a policy atom. This is a genuine `gap`, not proof no policy exists (Fig 8 :39).
- **INWARD (extend the instrument from one already returning something).** The instrument already returning something is the per-slot reach reading (Fig 1 :19). It is sharpened by asking what counter would not update on the distribution program's own reports. That is an instrument design task, still unbuilt here. Report `unreached`, not `unreachable in principle`.
- **OUTWARD (import in effect terms, with source gate and shadow test).** The effect description is the shape term in section 0. The frames that already hold pieces of it are AI-benefit economics (Acemoglu), predistribution theory (Hacker, Malhotra and Vogel), ecological psychology (affordance), and the capability approach (Sen, Nussbaum). Each is an import with provenance recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv`, and each is corroboration only. Fig 6 :24 prices an ungated import above starting from primitives, so none is promoted into the verdict.

**Strain S1, Fig 12 two currencies versus one "distribution" question.** Dissolved by re-census: Fig 12 governs the price of a composition; the question bundles two different currencies that the figure says do not add. No contradiction.

**Strain S2, Fig 11 "improvement that does not change contact changes nothing" versus the real fact that more capable models do change outcomes.** Dissolved by re-census: Fig 11 governs capability of an agent-and-habitat at a slot; a better model in the same habitat can change the task outcome without changing the receiving slot's capability.
