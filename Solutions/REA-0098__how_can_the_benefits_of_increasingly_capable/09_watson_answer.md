## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about distributing the benefits of increasingly capable AI without concentrating power and opportunity. Option A: treat this as one redistribution dial and ask for the right setting or a named policy. Option B: treat it as a union of conditions with separate anchors, keep benefit units and reach as two currencies, name the shape an acceptable distribution must have, and record the policy as a `gap` until a ground settles it. What each leads to: A averages away the live per-slot signal, confuses a transfer with a capability, lets a shared-evidence forum's agreement count as a verdict, and produces a policy with no provenance; B delivers checkable per-domain verdicts, six refuters written in advance, and an honest stop at the instrument limit.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry, and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency, and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Benefit surplus spreads while control of capability concentrates. Arity: at least three conditions (production, allocation, non-concentration) plus a habitat condition, so a union compound, not a partition; a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either (Fig 9 :55 to :57). Symmetry: transfer of benefit units versus an ordered `⋛` comparison of reach, so not symmetric. Scale: first order at production, membrane scale at transfer, population scale at concentration.
- **Domain 1, benefit production.** Actors: AI systems, tasks, developers, compute, data. Currency: residuals closed against the ground. Anchor: the task outcome, a measurement that does not move when a frame pushes on it. Verdict within this domain is about capability, not about allocation.
- **Domain 2, allocation across slots.** Actors: people, households, regions, firms; benefit units. Currency: benefits delivered per slot. Anchor: a non-updating counter of who got what. This is where the per-slot rule governs and aggregation fails.
- **Domain 3, power and opportunity.** Actors: incumbents, challengers, the refusal seat. Currency: reach, what can be composed or accessed. Anchor: a ground that does not update. Concentration is the Fig 2 mutual-update or common-cause collapse; the remedy is triangulation.
- **Domain 4, habitat and institutions.** Actors: rules, markets, skills, instruments, infrastructure. Currency: introductions of actors and conditions. Anchor: reproduced contact, maintained work. No standing list of good actors survives a residual change.
- **Domain 5, the universal policy.** Actors: legislatures, regulators. Currency: policy instruments. Anchor: none available in this run. Not a domain yet; recorded as `gap`.
- **Order by dependency.** Perception (per-slot residual) gates vocabulary (terms for what benefits afford); vocabulary gates the objective (what counts as non-concentration, a comparison); objective gates planning; planning gates learn and carry (Fig 3 :20). The chain breaks at vocabulary/objective, where no instrument is held (Fig 3 :27).

Strain test applied: Fig 12's two-currency claim and the single word "distribution" were initially read as a contradiction; re-census assigns Fig 12 to pricing compositions and the question to a bundle of two currencies, so no conflict remains (see `11_audit.md`).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The question as posed is a union of conditions, not a single redistribution dial. Benefit units and reach are two currencies that do not add, so spreading benefits does not by itself prevent concentration. Capability belongs to an AI-and-its-habitat, so the transferable objects are methods, head starts, and habitat conditions, not isolated models or recordings of one success. Concentration of power is the named case where frames range against each other and the anchor moves, so distribution without concentration requires independent readings against a ground that does not update and a seat able to refuse a verdict. Every effect is read per slot, not as an aggregate, and the arrangement is maintained work rather than a final allocation because the question moves as capability rises. No figure line returns a tax rate, bound, income floor, or named institution; that is a declared `gap`. Mode `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the policy, not `grounded`, candidate. The whole folder is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The shape claim states its mode, three checkable sub-claims, and six refuters written before the run, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The larger avoidable cost is spending a reader's independence by sending the full framework before their verdict, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." A wrong imported policy costs more than starting from primitives (Fig 6 :24). Both costs are reduced by leading with the shape claim and the refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. Concrete policies are bindings, not primitives, so a failed policy can be superseded without withdrawing the shape. No term has to be deleted; a refutation adds a refusing term and the reach falls safely (Fig 6's amendment). The `gap` stays open rather than being filled with a wrong atom.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A per-slot instrument with a non-updating anchor showing that benefit transfers raise reach one-for-one would collapse the two-currency verdict; an isolated model transfer that changes receiving-slot capability with no contact change would break Fig 11 :40; a procedure whose frames share one evidence pool and whose anchor updates on their reports that survives independent re-testing would break the triangulation verdict; a verbatim figure line selecting a specific policy would close the `gap`. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
