## Wider literature that bears on the derivation

1. **Economics of AI distribution (Acemoglu 2024).** The claim that AI may raise inequality, widens the capital/labor gap, and that future tasks lack objective outcome measures corroborates F1 (two currencies) and F2/S2 (habitat and anchor). It is one witness in an economics pool.
2. **Predistribution theory (Hacker via Malhotra and Vogel 2025).** The capabilities-and-power versus resources distinction corroborates F1. The "inequality snowball" (market and political power reinforcing each other) is a close non-framework description of Fig 2's mutual-update collapse, from a different field and therefore lower overlap than the framework essays.
3. **Capability approach (Sen, Nussbaum; SEP 2025 revision).** "Real freedoms", "conversion factors", and the critique of sum-ranking (a total welfare score ignores the distinction between persons) corroborate F1 and F4 from political philosophy. Low overlap with the AI-policy pool.
4. **Ecological psychology (Gibson via Nye and Silverman 2012).** Affordance as a relation between agent and environment corroborates Fig 11 :39 agent-and-habitat. Low overlap.
5. **AI benefit-sharing governance (Dennis, Manning, Clare et al. 2025; OECD 2024; WTO 2025).** These name the exact objects the shape predicts: resources, access, proceeds, and power concentration. They are one broad AI-governance pool, not three independent witnesses.

None of these sources conflicts with the figure verdict. The conflict rule was not triggered, so no verdict was flipped. All share the property of being world corroboration only; the verdict comes from the figures.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22** — "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Sending the full derivation before a recipient has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and the refuter; offer the figures after a verdict is recorded.
- **Fig 10 :42** — "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27** — "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the theory.
- **Fig 2 :40** — "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
- **Fig 2 :42** — "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Here the cost is high (institutional power and opportunity), so independent readings are worth real effort.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the per-slot residual and the shape claim; (2) ask what the recipient would count as a failure against a non-updating anchor; (3) record that verdict; (4) only then offer the links, with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual, the shape claim, and the pre-registered refuter. No theory, no links, no request for agreement or trust.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the figure text, the canonical README, and one policy source. The verdict on stage 1 is already recorded and is never revised by later exposure to the framework.
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse. Each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode. A reported failure is more useful than a reported success.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives independent scrutiny. If a refuter in `08` fires, withdraw that claim before sending anything.

## Legal and practical note

Public opt-in channels and one-to-one contact avoid the GDPR and CAN-SPAM style failure that unsolicited bulk email to harvested addresses creates. The FAccT public address is a press/general inbox, not a private list. No bulk-send plan exists anywhere in this folder.
