## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Are redistribution and predistribution different bonds or different amounts?

- Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Malhotra and Vogel (Roosevelt Institute 2025, opened 2026-11-24) write: *"With respect to means, predistribution policies strive to enhance capabilities and balance power, whereas redistribution policies reallocate resources."* That is a bond distinction: one operates on reach and capability (a comparison/production on the habitat), the other on benefit units (a transfer).
- Over the full shared atom set `{benefit units, reach, habitat, anchor, refusal seat}`, a redistribution-first arrangement and a predistribution-first arrangement are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance. The formula that lists only "distribution instruments" cannot tell them apart, which is Fig 12 :5's flat-form collapse.
- As they are actually practised, the two share more than one atom but not all: redistribution typically omits the habitat and refusal-seat atoms. By Fig 12 :124 ("functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."), the weaker claim is that both belong to a **functional group** of distribution mechanisms with a shared `input → allocation → outcome` skeleton, differing in which atoms occupy the middle slot.
- **Verdict:** type, not degree, is the variable. This corroborates F1 from `01` at the bond level. `specified`, triangulated (Fig 12 :5/:117 + the policy brief, low overlap between a figure definition and a policy source).

### Q2 — What order are the four conditions in? (dependency, not importance)

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it." Mapped to this question:

| link | content | consumes | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 perception | read the per-slot residual in both currencies | the ground | measurable with an instrument that does not update (unbuilt here) |
| 2 vocabulary | terms for what a benefit affords and what a slot can compose or access | link 1 | partly absent: "power" has no settled instrument (Fig 3 :27) |
| 3 objective | say what counts as not concentrating, in our own terms | link 2 | the broken link; a `⋛` comparison on reach per slot |
| 4 planning | a route to that objective under incomplete information | link 3 | policy imports live here, with provenance |
| 5 learn and carry | name what worked, verify it, carry it to the next capability level | link 4 | maintained work (Fig 11 :63) |

Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." More compute for distribution modeling (link 4) cannot rescue the absent objective (link 3). Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

- **Verdict:** the chain breaks at vocabulary/objective. Effort spent on policy design before the per-slot objective is instrumented lands at a step that cannot use it. `general` structure, `specified` for this question.

### Q3 — Which bond is "concentration"?

Fig 12 defines `⋛` as "comparison. a threshold relation between two quantities." Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation." Fig 2 :39: "Correlation has valence." Fig 2 :40 marks agreement about the world from a shared pool as disqualifying for a verdict.

"Concentration of power" is a threshold relation on reach across slots, so it is a `⋛` comparison, not a `+` conjunction of benefits. "Distribution of benefits" is a transfer that, at the slot, reads as a `−` (a moved quantity) and a `+` (both parties hold something). Because `⋛` is ordered, a pooled average cannot choose the direction of the comparison. That is the bond-level reason F4 refuses the aggregate.

- **Verdict:** "benefits" and "power" are literally different glyphs in the figure notation. This is a figure-internal result, `general`, single-source on the glyph mapping (Fig 12 + Operators table), corroborated at the world level by the two-currency policy literature but not decided by it.

### Q4 — The habitat analogue: seed, ground, and the firewall

Fig 4 :34-35: "A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes." Fig 11 :39: capability is agent-and-habitat. From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (opened 2026-11-24, lower-overlap simulated analogue): *"If prestige could buy actions, the system would collapse into a monoculture. The highest-prestige agent would monopolize compute and repeat its past success, and the system would stop exploring."* The essay reports building a "PrestigeFirewall" that separated social capital from action budget. That is a run-level analogue of Fig 2's mutual-update collapse and Fig 10 :47's convention-that-decides-an-outcome: a distribution system in which reputation can buy reach is the collapse, and separating the two is the engineering remedy.

- **Verdict:** the analogue is corroboration only, from a field (agent systems) with low textual overlap with the figures but the same author's evidence pool. It does not decide F3. `specified` shape, single-source for the specific firewall mechanism.

### Q5 — Is there a standing "best distributor"?

Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."

So no institution is permanently pro-distribution. A regulator that disperses benefit units today can concentrate reach tomorrow if the residual changes. And distribution is an introduction into a habitat (adding instruments, access, refusal seats), not a subtraction from one; you cannot subtract the habitat. Fig 11 :63: "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."

- **Verdict:** the question's "increasingly capable" forces the maintained-process answer (F5). `general` structure.

## FALSIFIER (written before the conclusion)

The isomer and order claims are refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A concrete redistribution and a concrete predistribution can be written with identical atoms and identical bonds (same molecule), so Fig 12 :117's different-substance condition fails.
2. The dependency order is reversible: a distribution verdict can be settled and verified before the per-slot objective is instrumented, with no hidden frame-internal measure.
3. "Concentration" is shown to be a `+` conjunction of benefit holdings rather than an ordered `⋛` comparison of reach, so the aggregate is sufficient and Fig 12 :112's goal/comparison mapping is wrong for this domain.
4. An isolated agent improvement is shown to change receiving-slot capability without changing contact (breaks Fig 11 :40 and the habitat analogue).

If any fires, the corresponding structural claim is withdrawn and the census in `01` section 1 is rerun.

## CONCLUSION

Redistribution and predistribution are isomers over the shared distribution atom set (or a functional group when atoms are missing), so type not degree is the variable. The dependency chain breaks at vocabulary/objective, where no instrument for reach-per-slot exists; policy design above that break cannot rescue it. "Concentration" is an ordered `⋛` comparison, not a sum of benefit units, which is why the aggregate is refused. No standing best distributor survives a changed residual, so the answer is maintained introduction into a habitat rather than a terminal allocation. Four falsifiers are written down in advance.
