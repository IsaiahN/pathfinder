## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The bridge between a catalytic function that recurs chemically and a heritable sequence that specifies it. Arity: at least three conditions in contact (persisting catalysis, heritable sequence, closing habitat) plus a fourth compound (the mapping). Symmetry: catalysis is symmetric across uses (a part survives its own use), while record-to-product is ordered production. Scale: crosses from the molecular stratum to the inherited-lineage stratum, a membrane crossing. Fig 9 :55 makes it a union, not a partition: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- **Domain 1, prebiotic chemistry and catalysis.** Actors: ribozymes, peptides, nucleotide cofactors, metal ions, mineral surfaces. Currency: reaction throughput and activation barrier. Anchor: chemical kinetics, whether the reaction is measurably faster. Union contributor, not a partition cell.
- **Domain 2, genetic inheritance.** Actors: replicating polymers, sequences, adaptors (tRNA), ribosome, aaRS. Currency: heritable sequence information and copying fidelity. Anchor: replication and sequence readout. Union contributor.
- **Domain 3, habitat and selection.** Actors: protocells, compartments, environments, gradients. Currency: differential persistence and reproduction. Anchor: the ground, what survives contact. Union contributor.
- **Domain 4, the bridge (overlap of 1, 2 and 3).** Actors: the code, translation, adaptors, recruited cofactors. Currency: mapping from sequence to function. Anchor: nameable in principle (an assay asking whether a sequence yields a working catalyst), absent in fact for the historical route. Union compound with its own loop.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3 :20).** Settle the persisting catalytic part before recording it; record the arrangement before installing the mapping; install the mapping before pricing any alphabet import; close the habitat with selection last. Fig 3 :27 applies to the historical link: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

Strain test applied: the catalyst/functional-group/isomer strain (S2) is dissolved by declaring the atom set or defaulting to the functional group; the recording strain (S3) is dissolved by distinguishing a replay of one occasion from a recorded re-applicable method, with an assay owed. Both remain visible in `01` §4. No figure line was forced to carry a quantity it does not govern.

**Domain 3, habitat (triangulated: Fig 11 plus Fig 2).**

- Fig 11 :40 "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." A more stable catalyst that changes no contact does not move the transition.
- Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Kinetics and replication are anchors; consensus is not.

**Domain 4, the bridge (single-source on the figure side, with world corroboration; historical route = gap).**

- Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :135 "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it." The literal route and the first encoded catalyst are `gap`.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the history does not. A catalyst is a part that survives its own use. Encoding is the recording of that arrangement so re-derivation becomes a lookup and the actions were spent once. The transition crosses a membrane that carries a re-applicable method up and a head start down, and a settled catalyst becomes an atom for the inherited composition, so no new primitive is needed for the encoding step. The catalytic function is a functional group; ribozyme and protein enzyme are isomers only over a declared shared atom set (S2, conditional). The bond from record to product is production, not identity. A new alphabet enters as an import with a source gate and a shadow test if the starting closure lacks it. Selection runs in an enumerated habitat with no maintainer in the relevant period. The literal route, the first encoded catalyst and the date are `gap`, of both the unreachable-at-this-budget kind (no discriminator for the historical link) and the shape-not-a-name kind. This is a `specified`, ungrounded, candidate result per domain, with a `general` structural shape.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states modes, three checkable sub-claims and seven refuters before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a historical narrative instead of a shape plus a refuter. Fig 2 :42 prices that: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." It is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The two live strains are removable without touching the core: S2 by declaring the atom set or defaulting to the functional group, S3 by running the method/replay assay. No primitive has to be withdrawn if a refuter fires; only the finding it breaks is retracted. A sent claim can be superseded by the corrected packet.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A bracketing assay on a closed system showing sequence-specified catalytic persistence arising without the recorded-arrangement step would break C1; a derivation of the new alphabet inside the starting closure would break C3; a measured maintainer seat with an outcome-fixing convention would break the unmaintained-habitat reading; an anchor that moves when frames agree would break Fig 2 :29. A shared atom set declared in advance plus a bond-difference result would upgrade S2 from conditional to `specified`. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
