## 0. The claim under audit

The ordinary-language question is: *How did early catalysts become genetically encoded?* Read in effect terms, it asks for the route by which a catalytic function that existed only as a recurring chemical event came to be carried as a heritable, heritably reproducible sequence that specifies it.

The bound sought-for shape, written down before the verdicts (formula "Direction"): a route counts as an answer only if it names

1. the catalytic structure that survives its own use,
2. the arrangement whose recording turns re-derivation into a lookup,
3. the cross-scale filter that carries a method up and a head start down,
4. the anchor that does not update on what a frame thinks, and
5. any atom imported into the starting closure, with source gate and shadow test.

This term is now ordinary and can be refused. Nothing below grants it standing by assertion.

The sources do not supply a history. They supply constraints. Wikipedia's *Abiogenesis* says the transition was "not a single event, but a process of increasing complexity" and that "the derivation of the LUCA from simple components is far from understood." So even the factual record is openly a set of partial mechanisms, not a settled narrative. The figures add the structural constraints that any candidate route must satisfy.

## 1. Domain census before choosing any figure line

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.**

- **Arity.** The residual relates at least two conditions: a catalyst that persists across uses, and a heritable sequence that specifies it. A third condition (a compartment whose differential persistence closes the loop) is in contact with both. This is a **union** of overlapping conditions, not a single question with two opposite answers (Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."). Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." So one lever will clear the single-condition regions and leave the intersections standing.
- **Symmetry.** Catalysis, in the figures' own definition, is symmetric across uses: the catalyst survives its own use (Fig 12 :119). Encoding is ordered: a gene produces a product that did not exist before (production, ⇒), so swapping gene and product changes the thing. The residual therefore has both a symmetric and an ordered factor; a pooled "how did encoding arise" reading would erase that difference.
- **Scale.** The residual crosses from the molecular/chemical scale to the inherited/inherited-in-a-lineage scale. That is a membrane crossing (Fig 4 :19-20: "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."), not a single-scale optimization.

**One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor.**

| domain | actors | currency | anchor (nameable?) | partition or union |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 prebiotic chemistry / catalysis | ribozymes, peptides, nucleotide cofactors, metal ions, mineral surfaces | reaction throughput and activation barrier | yes: chemical kinetics, whether the reaction is measurably faster | contributes one condition of the union |
| D2 genetic inheritance | replicating polymers, sequences, adaptors (tRNA), ribosome, aaRS | heritable sequence information, copying fidelity | yes: replication and sequence readout | contributes a second condition |

**No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.** D4 is included only because an anchor can be named in principle: an assay that asks whether a given sequence yields a working catalyst. The historical route itself has no instrument, which is why the literal route is recorded as a gap rather than promoted to a domain. Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." That is a description of the D4 historical link, and it is the reason for the honest stop.

**Partition or union.** Union, not partition (Fig 9 :54-57). A catalyst can be useful without being encoded; a sequence can be inherited without encoding a catalyst; a compartment can persist without either. The question asks about the intersection. Unbundle before searching again.

**Dependency order (Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance.").**

1. A catalytic structure must be settled as a persisting pattern before it can be a recorded arrangement (Fig 12 :104).
2. A replicating polymer with nonzero fidelity must exist before a sequence can carry a record.
3. A mapping (adaptor, code, translation) must exist before a sequence can specify a catalyst.
4. A habitat must close the loop with selection before the arrangement can be kept rather than merely occurred.
5. Any atom not in the starting closure must be imported with provenance before the new alphabet can be composed over (Fig 6 :14, Fig 8 :50-52).

Effort spent above the break cannot rescue one below it (Fig 3 :19). The historical link (step 3 and its timeline) is the one with no instrument.

## 3. Findings F1 to F10

### F1: A catalyst is a persisting structure, not an event (triangulated)

Fig 12 :116 and :119:

> "Four structures a compositional system exhibits, and each names something the flat form cannot write."
> "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."

This is the framework's own definition of the subject of the question. A catalyst is not "a thing that speeds a reaction" in the flat sense; it is a **part that survives its own use**, and the fact of survival is exactly what a list of ingredients fails to record. The prebiotic condition is therefore not "catalysis existed" but "reusable catalytic parts existed and were distinguishable from spent ones."

**Strain test.** Fig 12 :116 lists catalyst among *four structures a compositional system exhibits*, alongside isomer, cycle and functional group. These are not among the six substrate terms of Fig 13 (space, time, energy; spectrum, gradient, network). A reader who tries to make "catalyst" a seventh primitive breaks Fig 13's minimality. **Re-census:** catalyst is a *derived structural pattern* that a compositional system can exhibit, not a primitive. Fig 12 :119 itself groups it under structures a system exhibits. Strain dissolved; the fact is kept visible because it changes what kind of claim F1 is (a pattern claim, not a substrate claim).

World corroboration (secondary, low overlap with the framework): the *Ribozyme* article reports that Cech and Altman received the 1989 Nobel Prize for "the discovery of catalytic properties of RNA," that the ribosome is "fundamentally a ribozyme," and that the active site contains no protein residues within a stated distance (Noller-derived result reported there). The *Cofactor* article defines a prosthetic group as a nonpolypeptide unit "that is regenerated in each enzymatic turnover." Regeneration across uses is the same shape as Fig 12 :119's "survives its own use." These are two distinct biochemistry facts, not two versions of one claim.

**Refuter:** show that a catalytic part in the figure sense does not persist across uses, or that "survives its own use" is not what distinguishes a catalyst from a spent part in the record. If catalysis is a one-occasion event, F1 falls.

### F2: Genetic encoding is a recorded arrangement that turns re-derivation into a lookup (triangulated)

Fig 12 :107-110:

> "where the arrangement is recorded, re-deriving it is a lookup and the actions were spent once."
> "Where it is not, the result carries forward and the deriving does not, so every lineage pays again."

Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance." Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."

This is the figure-derived answer to the *why* half of the question. A gene is not merely a copy of a catalyst; it is the **record of the arrangement** such that an offspring lineage pays a lookup where an unencoded lineage would pay the finding-out again. Without such a record, "the result carries forward and the deriving does not": a useful catalytic shape recurs only where chemistry or selection happens to rebuild it, and every generation re-pays the cost. Encoding is therefore the substrate on which the cost of establishing an arrangement is spent once rather than repeatedly.

Fig 13 :130 makes the identity explicit:

> "A term carries the atoms it was built from and the bonds that joined them. That record is not documentation of the term. It is the term."

So a genetic sequence is not a description appended to a catalyst. Under this reading, the sequence *is* the term at the inherited stratum: it carries the atoms and the bonds, and the lineage is what makes the term checkable.

World corroboration (secondary): the *Genetic code* article defines the code as rules that "translate information encoded within genetic material ... into proteins," with the ribosome linking amino acids in an order specified by mRNA using tRNA adaptors. Crick's 1955 adaptor hypothesis, reported there, is precisely the requirement for a mapping that can carry a sequence to a product. The *Cofactor* article reports the proposal that adenosine-based cofactors "may have acted as adaptors" and names the process exaptation: "adapting a pre-evolved structure for a novel use." That is a concrete route by which a persisting part becomes a bridge rather than a new atom.

**Refuter:** show that an inherited sequence does not turn a re-derivation into a lookup, that is, that each generation must re-establish the catalytic arrangement from scratch even with the sequence present; or show that the sequence carries atoms but not the bond information that makes the arrangement the substance. If so, F2 is wrong and "encoding" is only a correlation with catalysis.

### F3: Crossing the membrane carries a method up and a head start down (triangulated)

Fig 4 :22-25:

> "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
> "upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."
> "downward: something that makes the next search cheaper. Never a replay of what worked once."
> "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."

Fig 4 :19-20: "The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."

The transition from chemical catalysis to inherited catalysis crosses the membrane between the molecular scale and the lineage scale. What can cross upward (toward the inherited stratum) is a **method**, that is, a catalytic arrangement that can be reapplied and verified. What comes down is a **head start**, that is, a sequence that makes the next generation's search cheaper without being a replay of one occasion. A gene that reliably regenerates a working catalyst is a generator: it crosses as a method. A dead copied sequence that no longer produces a catalyst is a recording: Fig 4 :25 says it "looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." The difference is not in the alphabet but in whether the ground pays.

Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." The round trip here is: abstract the catalytic arrangement into a sequence, concretise the sequence back into a catalyst, and measure whether it still pays. The gap R_T is the loss across that trip, and it is exactly where fidelity and mutation live.

**Strain test.** A gene is normally a *recording*, and Fig 4 :25 disqualifies recordings carried upward as knowledge. Yet F2 says a gene *is* a record. Apparent contradiction. **Re-census:** there are two different readings of "recording." A recording of one occasion (a dead replay) is disqualified. A recorded *arrangement* that functions as a re-applicable method is precisely what Fig 4 :23 permits upward. The discriminator is whether the ground pays on reuse, and it needs an instrument (see S3). The strain is named, not hidden, and it sends us to an assay, not to a softer reading.

**Refuter:** show that what crossed the membrane was a recording of one occasion with no re-applicability, and that no head start came down; or show that the kernel changed with size, breaking Fig 4 :19-20.

### F4: A settled catalyst becomes an atom for the inherited composition (triangulated)

Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."

The recursion is the engine of the answer. A catalytic arrangement that has been settled against the chemical ground becomes an operand at the next stratum. At that stratum it can be bonded into a sequence, a regulatory relation, a network (to use Fig 13's structure terms) without being re-derived. This is why the question "how did catalysts become encoded" is not a question about making new catalysts. It is a question about **what a settled catalyst can be composed over**. No new primitive is required for that step.

Fig 12 :106: "Nothing composes over what was never admitted as a part." Settlement is necessary. Fig 12 :107-110 (quoted in F2) shows it is not sufficient: whether the cost is re-paid depends on the substrate, that is, on whether the arrangement is recorded across the boundary that matters.

World corroboration (secondary): the *Abiogenesis* article reports that LUCA "already had hundreds of genes encoded in the DNA genetic code" and a full translation suite, and that life "consists of reproduction with (heritable) variations" under the NASA formulation "a self-sustaining chemical system capable of Darwinian evolution." That is a mature instance of the recursion, not the origin of it; it is used only as shape corroboration.

**Refuter:** show that a settled catalyst cannot serve as an atom for a higher-stratum composition, or that the inherited layer must re-derive every catalyst it uses. Either breaks the recursion.

### F5: The catalytic function is a functional group, not automatically an isomer (single-source on the figure definition, with a live conditional)

Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

The catalytic outcome is fixed by a substructure (an active site, a fold, a cofactor binding site). That makes "catalyst" a **functional group**: the group says what kind (catalyst), the qualifier says which member (this ribozyme, that protein enzyme, this metal cluster). Ribozymes and protein enzymes can realize the same functional group with different monomers. They are **not** isomers as usually written, because they do not share an atom set: RNA nucleotides versus amino acids. Under Fig 12 :117, isomer requires *same atoms*.

The stronger claim survives only under a re-census: if both are written over a shared abstract atom set (monomer position, bond, fold, active-site geometry), then they are the same atoms under different arrangements, hence isomers in the figure's sense. Fig 13 :121: "Different routes can arrive at the same substance." World corroboration for the weaker form: the *Ribozyme* article notes that ribozymes "mimic the mechanism used by their protein counterparts" (for example self-cleaving RNA versus RNase A) despite four nucleotide choices versus twenty amino acid side chains. Different alphabet, same kind of outcome: a functional group.

**Strain (live, conditional).** The isomer reading is strained unless the shared atom set is declared. It is not smoothed over. The default verdict is the functional-group claim; the isomer claim holds only under the declared abstraction and needs the falsifier below.

**Refuter (written before the conclusion):** if either a ribozyme or a protein enzyme can be shown to require an atom the other cannot be written over even at the abstraction level (for example a catalytic chemistry with no analogue in the other alphabet), the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim stands.

### F6: A new alphabet enters as import, with a source gate and a shadow test (triangulated)

Fig 6 :14 calls import "the only operator that moves the wall." Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Fig 6 :22: "NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world. IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure."

Fig 8 :50-52:

> "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."

If the starting closure holds RNA-like polymers and nucleotide cofactors, an amino-acid alphabet is not derivable by rearrangement. It must be imported from a frame whose closure differs. Two candidate routes in the world literature are reported in the sources opened: (a) amino acids first served as cofactors or side chains attached to RNA, then peptides, then proteins, as summarized in the *RNA world* article ("amino acids, peptides, and finally proteins initially were cofactors for ribozymes"), and (b) cofactors are "fossils of nucleic acid enzymes" (White 1976 as reported there), with the *Cofactor* article adding that adenosine-based cofactors may be remnants of the RNA world and that exaptation adapted a pre-evolved structure for a novel use. Both are **claims in the world record**, not things the figures produce. They corroborate the *shape* of an import (a held structure repurposed) without naming the historical event.

Fig 6 :24: "Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives." A single-origin story imported at low provenance is expensive. Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom." Endless rearrangement of RNA cannot manufacture the peptide alphabet; something has to enter from outside the closure.

**Refuter:** show that the amino-acid alphabet is derivable by composition from the starting closure without an atom entering, or produce a candidate import with no source gate and no shadow test and insist it carries no cost. The first breaks the closure law; the second ignores the price.

### F7: Nobody chose; selection ran, and the habitat must be enumerated (triangulated)

Fig 11 :36: "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."
Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."
Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

The question's "how did" invites a designer or a single clever step. The figures refuse the invitation. In the unmaintained habitat there is no seat; what survived is what the ground kept. The right move is to enumerate what was in contact with the residual (catalytic molecules, replicating polymers, compartments, gradients, cofactors, mineral surfaces), then what contacts those, outward until the cascade stops mattering, and to rank each actor relative to this residual rather than carry a standing list of "good" molecules. A cofactor that is a benefactor to a ribozyme can be a malfactor to a protein enzyme; valence is not carried.

Fig 11 :39: "And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." A more stable ribozyme that does not change its contact with a compartment or a replicating polymer changes nothing at the transition.

World corroboration (secondary): the *Abiogenesis* article reports the prevailing view as autocatalysis and ribozymes in an RNA world, with the caveat that "the transition from non-life to life has not been observed experimentally." The *Ribozyme* article reports in vitro selection (SELEX, directed evolution) producing ribozymes for chosen tasks, and that "almost any nucleic acid can evolve into a catalytic sequence under appropriate selection" (as reported in the *RNA world* article). That is selection running in a test tube, a direct instance of the shape, not a claim about Deep Time.

**Refuter:** show a maintained seat acting on the prebiotic habitat in the relevant period with an outcome-fixing convention, or exhibit an actor whose valence is intrinsic to the residual. Either would break the unmaintained-habitat reading.

### F8: The anchor is kinetics and sequence readout; the channel can decay (triangulated)

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."
Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does."

For D1 the anchor is a measurement: does the reaction run faster with the part present. For D2 the anchor is replication and readout: does the sequence persist and get read. Neither anchor changes because a community agrees on the RNA world. What can decay is the **channel**: copying fidelity, adaptor specificity, compartment inheritance, the mapping from sequence to function. Fig 10 :28 lists "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings" as the ways contact is lost while the ground does not move. In this question those instantiate as mutation, mistranslation, and loss of compartment inheritance.

Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." A consensus of papers asserting the same origin scenario is one evidence pool talking, not an anchor. Fig 7 :13: "agreement compounds, evidence does not."

**Refuter:** show an anchor for prebiotic catalysis or encoding that moves when a frame pushes on it (for example, a "catalyst" defined by consensus rather than by rate enhancement), or show that the ground decays while the channel stays fixed.

### F9: Keeping verdicts separate, never averaged (structural)

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

A single answer to "how did early catalysts become encoded" would average across at least four slots: ribozyme self-replication, cofactor recruitment, translation/adaptor origin, and compartment inheritance. Each has a different anchor and a different closure. The verdicts in `07_the_tether_answer.md` are therefore reported per domain and never combined into a probability or a single narrative. A "95% of enzymes are protein-encoded" statistic is a filter for where to look, not a witness for how the transition happened.

**Refuter:** exhibit a measurement that is simultaneously the anchor for catalysis, for heritability, for compartment persistence and for the sequence-to-function mapping. If none can be named (and none is named here), averaging is structurally disallowed.

### F10: The literal route is a gap, reported as such (unreachable at this budget and shape-not-a-name)

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."
Fig 13 :135: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."
Fig 13 :134: "It can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands, so something has to be imported before it is asked again."
Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
Fig 9 :44: "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room."

No figure line returns the historical sequence of events, the first encoded catalyst, the date, or the geochemical setting. The figures constrain the shape of any answer and stop. The world literature is openly partial: the *Abiogenesis* article says the derivation of LUCA from simple components is "far from understood"; the *RNA world* article says even proponents agree there is "still not conclusive evidence to completely falsify other paradigms and hypotheses." The honest verdict is therefore `gap`, of two kinds:

- **Unreachable at this budget and with these instruments:** the historical link at step 3 of the dependency order has no discriminator. More reading of the same literature adds spelling, not evidence (Fig 7 :13, Fig 6 :53-54).
- **Shape-not-a-name:** even with a perfect derivation, the framework returns a kind of thing (a persisting catalytic structure, a recorded arrangement, a head start across a membrane, an imported alphabet), never the name of the molecule or the event.

**This is a verdict, not silence.** "Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence."

**Refuter / what would close it:** a source-discriminating instrument or dataset that records a transition from unencoded catalytic persistence to sequence-specified catalytic persistence in a closed system, with bracketing (a before and after measured against the same ground). Until such a reading exists, F10 stands.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the seat map, "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Catalyst definition breaks.** A catalytic part in the figure sense does not persist across its own use; spent and regenerating parts are indistinguishable in the record (breaks Fig 12 :119).
2. **Recording breaks.** An inherited sequence does not turn re-derivation into a lookup, so every lineage still pays the full establishment cost (breaks Fig 12 :107-110).
3. **Membrane breaks.** What crossed the scale boundary was a replay of one occasion with no re-applicability, or the kernel changed with size (breaks Fig 4 :22-25, :19-20).
4. **Recursion breaks.** A settled catalyst cannot become an atom for the inherited composition, or the inherited layer must re-derive every catalyst (breaks Fig 12 :104).
5. **Import breaks.** The new alphabet is derivable inside the starting closure, or a candidate import is accepted without a source gate and shadow test (breaks Fig 6 :14, :21-22, Fig 8 :50-52).
6. **Anchor breaks.** A frame is allowed to update the anchor for catalysis or heritability, so agreement settles the question (breaks Fig 2 :29, :35).
7. **Ground discriminator fires.** A measurement shows a sequence-specified catalytic persistence arising without the recorded-arrangement step, or in a maintained habitat with an outcome-fixing convention (breaks F2 and F7 at the ground).

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the shape is revised. Until one is run, nothing here may be labelled `grounded`.
