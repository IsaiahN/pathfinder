# Expert verification packet: the Tether on catalyst encoding

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in origins of life, prebiotic chemistry or molecular evolution can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the sub-claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the historical route. Not `grounded`.** No measurement at a prebiotic transition is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status of this packet:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A catalyst is a part that survives its own use. Genetic encoding is the recording of the catalytic arrangement so that a later lineage pays a lookup instead of re-deriving the catalyst; where the arrangement is not recorded, every lineage pays again. The transition crosses a scale membrane that carries a re-applicable method up and a head start down, and a settled catalyst becomes an atom for the inherited composition, so no new primitive is needed for the encoding step itself. Ribozymes and protein enzymes are members of one functional group, not automatically isomers; the bond from record to product is production; an amino-acid alphabet enters as an import with a source gate and a shadow test if the starting closure lacks it; selection runs in an enumerated habitat. The literal historical route is reported as a `gap`, with a bracketing assay named as the instrument that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: the recording claim (framework-internal plus chemistry-facing)

- **Statement:** a catalyst is a persisting part, and encoding is the arrangement recorded so that re-derivation becomes a lookup. A gene that reliably regenerates a working catalyst is a generator; a dead copy is a replay of one occasion.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is." Fig 12 :107-110: "where the arrangement is recorded, re-deriving it is a lookup and the actions were spent once. Where it is not, the result carries forward and the deriving does not, so every lineage pays again." Fig 4 :23: "upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success." Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."
- **What to check:** is the figure definition of catalyst faithful to the chemical fact (regeneration across turnovers, reported for prosthetic groups in the opened *Cofactor* article), and does an inherited sequence actually change the per-generation cost of producing the catalyst? The second part is the S3 assay: express the sequence, repeat across generations, measure whether re-derivation becomes a lookup.
- **Refuter:** a catalytic part that does not persist across its own use; or an inherited sequence that does not turn re-derivation into a lookup; or a case where the same sequence is a method in one context and a replay in another with no change in the ground. Any one fires C1.

### C2: the functional-group and bond claim (chemistry-facing, checkable by a specialist)

- **Statement:** ribozyme, protein enzyme and metal cluster are members of one catalytic functional group. They are isomers only over a declared shared atom set, which this packet does not declare by default. The bond from record to product is production (⇒), not identity and not bare sequence.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :77: "A produces B; B did not exist before." Fig 12 :69: "substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No." Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."
- **What to check:** (1) do the three classes share an outcome-fixing substructure in the sense the figure defines? The opened *Ribozyme* article reports that ribozymes "mimic the mechanism used by their protein counterparts" (self-cleaving RNA versus RNase A), despite four monomers versus twenty side chains. (2) Can a shared atom set be declared and a bond difference shown to change the substance? If yes, the isomer claim becomes `specified`; if not, the functional-group claim stands. (3) Does the gene-product relation really fail the identity test, so that swapping loses something, and is the product absent before the record fires?
- **Refuter:** a catalyst requiring a catalytic atom the other class cannot be written over even at the declared abstraction (kills the isomer claim); or a gene and product shown to be intersubstitutable with no loss (kills production); or a case where the product exists before the record fires (kills production).

### C3: the import and habitat claim (framework-internal plus evolution-facing)

- **Statement:** if the starting closure lacks amino-acid monomers, they enter as an import, not as a composition; an import needs a source gate and a shadow test. Selection runs in an unmaintained habitat, which must be enumerated from the residual rather than composed from a standing list of important molecules.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Fig 6 :22: "NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world. IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure." Fig 8 :50-52: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ." Fig 11 :36: "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there." Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."
- **What to check:** (1) could the amino-acid alphabet be derived from the starting closure without adding an atom? If yes, the import claim fails. (2) Is there a provenance and a shadow test for the candidate import, for example cofactor recruitment or exaptation as reported in the opened *RNA world* and *Cofactor* articles? (3) Was there a maintainer seat in the relevant period? If yes, the unmaintained reading of Fig 11 :36 does not govern.
- **Refuter:** a derivation of the alphabet inside the closure; an import accepted with no source gate or shadow test; or a maintained seat with an outcome-fixing convention. Any one fires C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a history. No first encoded catalyst, no date, no setting, no RNA-first verdict.
- Not that the RNA world is settled. The opened source reports that even proponents agree there is no conclusive evidence against other paradigms.
- Not that ribozymes and protein enzymes are strict isomers. That is conditional on a declared shared atom set and carries a falsifier.
- Not that cofactor fossils, exaptation, the adaptor hypothesis or in vitro selection are derivations from the figures. They are world-frame corroborations of shape, provenanced in `10_source_ledger.csv`.
- Not that a gene is automatically a method. That is the S3 assay, still owed.
- Not a theory of everything, not a final room, not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by an instrument.

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminator, not more agreement. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument reading green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. **Bracketing assay:** measure a closed system before and after unencoded catalytic persistence becomes sequence-specified catalytic persistence, with the same ground on both sides. This would close the `gap` on the historical route.
2. **Method/replay assay (S3):** express the sequence, repeat across generations, measure the round-trip gap. This decides whether the arrangement is recorded or re-paid each time.
3. **Anchor test:** name the anchor for each condition (kinetics, replication/readout, differential persistence) and check it does not move when frames agree.
4. **Import shadow test:** show a gap the starting closure cannot close and a provenance for the incoming alphabet.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read the four short passages: Fig 12 :119, Fig 12 :107-110, Fig 12 :123 and Fig 12 :77. About two minutes.
2. Check C2 against their own knowledge: do ribozymes, protein enzymes and metal clusters share an outcome-fixing substructure, and is the gene-product relation production rather than identity? About eight minutes.
3. Check C1 against the S3 question: would expressing the sequence repeatedly turn re-derivation into a lookup in the system they know? About five minutes.
4. Name one instrument or dataset that would bracket the transition, or say why none is possible. About three minutes.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
