## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, and Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the figure lines above, chiefly Fig 9 :55-57 (union), Fig 11 :40 (contact), Fig 11 :43-44 and :52-53 (habitat and cascades), Fig 1 :27 (self-scoring).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07`: separation must be absolute, measured at the ground, boundary-complete, slot-resolved, kept as two conditions, and paired with demand measures; the empirical rate is a gap.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete world claim, for example "country X decoupled its CO2 emissions from GDP between 2005 and 2020".

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- **the boundary width.** The shorthand says boundary-complete; a national CO2 series is territorial and first-order. Ritchie 2019 shows the sign can change under a consumption boundary.
- **the condition count.** The shorthand says two conditions; a CO2 series speaks only to damage-by-climate, not to material throughput or biodiversity loss.
- **the time window.** The shorthand says sustained; a 15-year window can hide a rebound or a reversal outside it.

What survives cleanly: an intensity ratio is not an absolute decline, the frame cannot score itself with its own metric, and an aggregate can hide a live slot. That residue is worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no instrument was run in this derivation; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is a condensed statement of the metalaws" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
