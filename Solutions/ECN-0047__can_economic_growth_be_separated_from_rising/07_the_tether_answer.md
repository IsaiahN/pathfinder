# The Tether's answer on growth, resource use and environmental damage

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks whether a rising output can be separated from rising physical throughput and damage. The residual is the gap between a claimed separation and what the ground returns per slot. It is a **union compound**, not one hard question (Fig 9 :57): resource use and environmental damage are two conditions with different instruments, and the production versus consumption boundary is a second, orthogonal split. Answering with a single yes or no is answering a different question.

## 2. The shape, per domain

1. **Separation must be absolute, not a ratio.** Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Energy is *"conserved, finite, directionless alone"* (Fig 13 :14); a gradient is *"a difference that can be spent"* (Fig 13 :22). A falling intensity spends the same gradient more efficiently. Unless absolute throughput and damage fall, the contact has not changed. This is D1.
2. **The economic frame cannot score itself.** Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* GDP and intensity ratios are outputs of the frame whose success they are asked to grade. Fig 2 :37-38: instruments *"can be wrong on axes they do not measure."* The verdict belongs to a physical measurement (Fig 1 :23). This is D2.
3. **The compound must be unbundled and the habitat enumerated.** Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 11 :44: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."* A production-based inventory is a first-order reading. Consumption-based accounting and cascade enumeration are not alternative opinions but wider contact. This is D3.
4. **Slots must stay separate.** Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* An economy-wide ratio can be near zero while one resource or ecosystem carries a live residual (Fig 1 :13). This is a reporting constraint on D3.
5. **Policy can introduce but not subtract.** Fig 11 :61: *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* Fig 11 :63: *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."* A cap or a demand measure can change contact; it does not remove the habitat. Efficiency gains paired with demand measures are the admissible policy form. This is D4.
6. **The empirical yes/no is a `gap`.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The figures return the constraints, not the global rate.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "decoupling is impossible". No figure line returns that verdict, and world sources report real absolute reductions in some countries (IPCC B.5).
- Not "efficiency is worthless". Efficiency is a rearrangement inside a finite reach (Fig 13 :113); it matters, but it is not the same as an absolute decline.
- Not "GDP is useless". It is an instrument that can be wrong on axes it does not measure (Fig 2 :37-38), not a forbidden quantity.
- Not "offshoring explains every reported fall". That is an empirical claim about a boundary, to be settled by a consumption-based account (Ritchie 2019), not by the figures.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken in this derivation.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating account, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists." The questions for the instrument:

- Does absolute material and energy throughput fall, globally, for multiple decades, while output grows? A counter settles this.
- Does measured environmental damage fall on the same window and boundary? A different instrument settles this, and the two must not be averaged.
- Does the result survive a consumption-based and cascaded boundary, including indirect and third-order effects (Fig 11 :52-53)?
- Are there live slot residuals hidden by the aggregate (Fig 1 :13, :22)?
- Do rebound effects erase the absolute saving after the efficiency gain (Jevons source, secondary)?

## 5. The honest stop

**Reached on the shape, unreached on the rate.** The figure derivation stops where the instruments stop. Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* The round trip gap R_T between the figure-derived shape and the concrete world claim is recorded qualitatively in `11_audit.md`. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and the verdict is `candidate` until the ground pays.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer yes or no to whether growth can be separated from resource use and damage, because the question is a bundle of at least two conditions, not one. It returns the shape any admissible answer must have: the decline must be absolute, not a falling intensity per unit of output, because an improvement that leaves physical contact unchanged changes nothing; it must be measured at the ground rather than scored with the economic frame's own metric; it must be read across consumption as well as production boundaries and across indirect cascades; and the two conditions must stay separate, since an aggregate can hide a live slot. Efficiency is not dismissed, but on its own it is a rearrangement inside a finite reach. Which countries, if any, have actually achieved this is left to a counter, not to the framework. The shape, its pre-registered refuter and the instrument that would close it are what this offers; the rate is owed to the ground.
