# Expert verification packet: the Tether on growth and decoupling

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in growth and sustainability can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No measurement was taken in this derivation. This is a shape and a route, not an empirical result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question is a union compound: resource use and environmental damage are two conditions with different instruments, and the production versus consumption boundary is a second split. From the figure rule that an improvement which does not change contact changes nothing, a falling intensity per unit of output is not separation. Separation would have to register at the ground as an absolute, sustained decline in physical throughput and damage, measured across consumption and production boundaries and across indirect cascades, with the two conditions kept separate and slot-level residuals reported. The figures return that shape and no global decoupling rate. Which countries, if any, have achieved it is owed to measurement.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 9 and 11

- **Statement:** the question must be unbundled, and an intensity improvement is not a change of contact.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 11 :40 *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Fig 13 :14 *"conserved, finite, directionless alone"*.
- **Refuter:** a single instrument in a single unit that settles both conditions with no residue, or a measured case where a falling intensity with unchanged absolute throughput is accepted at the ground as separation. Under either, C1 falls.

### C2 — measurement-facing, checkable by an analyst against real accounts

- **Statement:** a production-based territorial inventory enumerates first-order contact only. Consumption-based accounting and cascade enumeration are wider readings of the same habitat, not rival opinions, and the boundary choice can change the result.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :43 *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 11 :44 *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."* Fig 11 :52-53 *"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*
- **What to check:** take one country reported as decoupling on a production basis and rerun the same years on a consumption basis, then add one upstream material cascade. Does the sign hold? World secondary corroboration: Ritchie 2019, "If only territorial emissions were falling whilst consumption-based emissions were rising, this would suggest it was 'offshoring' emissions elsewhere."
- **Refuter:** a complete, unauthored production-based account that already contains all indirect and third-order effects. Under that, the boundary distinction collapses and C2 falls.

### C3 — framework-internal derivation about the verdict metric

- **Statement:** the economic frame cannot grade its own contact with the physical ground using a quantity it produces; the verdict requires a physical measurement.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :27 *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Fig 1 :23 *"The ground enters as the only metric."* Fig 2 :37-38 *"instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."*
- **Refuter:** a frame-independent physical anchor that is also the quantity the economic frame optimises, such that Fig 1 :27 does not apply. If such a quantity is shown, C3 falls.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a yes or no on whether decoupling has happened. That is a `gap` in this derivation (Fig 9 :32).
- Not that decoupling is impossible. IPCC AR6 WGIII B.5 reports "At least 18 countries have sustained production-based GHG and consumption-based CO2 emission reductions for longer than 10 years"; these "have only partly offset global emissions growth".
- Not that efficiency gains are worthless, nor that GDP is useless, nor that offshoring explains every reported fall.
- Not that resource use and damage can be averaged into one indicator. Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by this derivation.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating account, not an argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists." Concretely:

1. A sustained, multi-decade, global absolute fall in material and energy throughput while output grows, measured by counters.
2. The same for a damage indicator, with a different instrument, never averaged with the first.
3. Both surviving a consumption-based and cascaded boundary, with slot-level residuals still visible.
