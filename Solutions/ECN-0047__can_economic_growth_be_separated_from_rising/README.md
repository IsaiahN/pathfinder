## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is whether a rising output can be composed with a falling physical contact. Option A says yes: growth can be separated, so the two trajectories can diverge. Option B says the separation is only apparent when it is measured as a ratio, while the absolute contact keeps rising. What each leads to: A licenses a claim that the problem is being solved by efficiency and innovation alone; B requires that separation register at the ground as an absolute, boundary-complete, sustained decline in throughput and damage before it is credited.

**What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run:** a shape with three constraints. (1) The decline must be absolute, not a falling intensity per unit of output. (2) It must be measured at the ground across the full habitat and its cascades, with production and consumption boundaries both read. (3) The two conditions, resource use and environmental damage, must be kept separate and never averaged into one indicator. Binding this shape as a term lets it accrue a residual like anything else, so the preference can be refused by the same test as every other claim (THE_FORMULA, "Direction").
