## What the wider literature bears on the shape

1. **The intensity-versus-scale point** is directly stated by IPCC AR6 WGIII B.2: intensity improvements were "less than emissions increases from rising global activity levels". That is F2 in world terms. It is a different evidence pool from the figures, so it is genuine secondary corroboration, not one pool talking twice.
2. **The absolute case exists but is local** (IPCC B.5, Segovia-Martin et al. 2023). This is a possible conflict with a naive "separation is impossible" reading. The conflict was returned to the domain census and did not flip the verdict: the figure shape says what would count as separation, not that it cannot occur. The figure and the world source are answering different questions, so there is no strain after re-census.
3. **The boundary point** is directly supported by Ritchie 2019: a territorial fall can coexist with a consumption rise. That is F5.
4. **The rebound point** is supported by the Jevons source and Segovia-Martin et al.: efficiency gains can be offset by demand response, so a ratio improvement need not become an absolute decline. That is F6.
5. **The two IRP reports share an evidence pool** (same institution, overlapping assessments), so they count as one witness, not two. The two essays by the framework author also share a pool with the figures and carry no independent weight.
