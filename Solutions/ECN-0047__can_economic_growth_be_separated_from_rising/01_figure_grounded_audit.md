## 0. The residual, in effect terms

Let the claim be: output Y rises while physical throughput T and environmental damage D fall, or at least while the ratio T/Y falls. The residual is the gap between that claimed separation and what the ground returns per slot. In symbols borrowed from the framework: R_s = |Γ(b,a) - o'|_s for each resource slot, ecosystem slot and boundary slot s, never as one global number.

- **Arity.** At least three quantities (Y, T, D) plus a rate and a boundary. It is not a two-place relation. It is a **union compound** of two conditions, resource use and damage, each with its own loop (Fig 9 :57).
- **Symmetry.** Asymmetric and ordered. Throughput produces output (production bond ⇒), and a policy intervention precedes a change in contact (sequence bond →). A falling intensity is a comparison (⋛) between two ratios, not a symmetric join. The two conditions are not interchangeable.
- **Scale.** Micro (firm and technology, where intensity improvements are made), national (where accounts are drawn), global (where absolute throughput and planetary boundaries register). Time matters: a rebate or rebound appears after the efficiency gain, so a short window can show separation that a long window does not.

**Boundary rule.** If no nameable anchor exists for a proposed domain, it is not a domain yet. The anchor here must be a measurement that does not move when an economist changes a model (Fig 2 :29), not the model's own ratio.

## 3. Findings

### F1 — The question is a union compound, not one hard question (framing, decisive)

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

"Resource use" and "environmental damage" are two conditions. A falling material footprint with rising biodiversity loss is not a partially solved problem, it is the intersection standing. A carbon-only indicator is the single lever that clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections untouched. Fig 4 of the essays records the same point: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"* (Library of Babel, read 2026-11-23).

**Mode:** `general` for the union rule, `specified` for its application to T and D. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 9 plus the essay). **Refuter:** a single instrument in a single unit that settles both conditions at once, with no residue left in either.

### F2 — Only a change of physical contact counts; a falling intensity is not separation

Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Fig 13 :14: *"conserved, finite, directionless alone"*. Symbols table :89: *"Conserved, finite, and directionless on its own."* Fig 13 :22: *"a difference that can be spent"*.

Efficiency is a rearrangement inside a finite reach. It spends the same gradient more slowly per unit of output, or it lowers the effective price and calls forth more output. Unless the absolute flow changes, the contact has not changed. Fig 13 :113: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not."* Fig 13 :118: *"infinite effort inside a finite reach."*

**Mode:** `specified` (the structural claim is general, its application to throughput is specified). **Status:** triangulated (Fig 11, Fig 13 and the Symbols table, low overlap between capability language and substrate language). **Refuter:** a measured case in which a falling intensity, with absolute throughput and damage unchanged, is accepted at the ground as separation. F2 does not say absolute decoupling is impossible; it says relative decoupling is not the same claim.

### F3 — The economic frame cannot score itself with its own metric

Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Fig 1 :23: *"The ground enters as the only metric."* Fig 2 :37-38: *"instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."*

GDP is produced by the economic frame and cannot be the metric that grades the frame's contact with the biosphere. The same instrument may be perfectly good for the question it was built for and wrong on exactly the axes this question asks about. Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* A market is listed as an anchor in that line, but the market price of a resource is not a counter of tonnes. The anchor this question is constitutively about is physical throughput and damage (Fig 2 :36).

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 1 and Fig 2, different argument routes). **Refuter:** an account that is independent of the framing frame, that measures throughput in physical units, and that does not move when a model or a price changes.

### F4 — Aggregation hides the live slots

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 1 :13: *"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"*.

An economy-wide intensity ratio can fall to zero residual in the aggregate while one resource, one ecosystem or one region carries a live residual. This is not a side objection to decoupling, it is the named failure of the average. A fall in total material intensity can coexist with a rising rare-metal extraction slot, and the slot-level signal is the one that determines whether the separation is real.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 1 :13, :19, :22). **Refuter:** a slot-resolved account in which every live slot is reported and none is swamped by an aggregate. This is a reporting requirement, not a verdict.

### F5 — The habitat must be enumerated across boundaries and cascades

Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 11 :44: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."* Fig 11 :52-53: *"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*

The figure's own example is an environmental-damage case, so the mapping needs no analogy. A production-based inventory enumerates first-order contact and misses the imported goods, the extracted materials upstream, and the waste downstream. Consumption-based accounting is not a different opinion, it is a wider enumeration of the same habitat. Where the habitat is substituted rather than merely narrowed, Fig 11 :66 applies: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."*

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 and the Symbols table's enumerated habitat definition). **Refuter:** a boundary that can be shown to include all material cascades without having been authored to make the answer come out a particular way.

### F6 — Two currencies: an intensity ratio and an absolute reduction do not add

Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 12 :126: *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."* Operators table :46: *"progress is a subtraction, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot say how far along it is"*.

A cheaper description (output per unit of input) and an actual removal of throughput (less input) are different readings. Summing them is the error the figure names. Under an ordered bond the reduction is a subtraction (−) and the target is a comparison (⋛); neither is a conjunction (+) with the efficiency gain.

**Mode:** `specified` for the mapping, `general` for the two-currency rule. **Status:** single-source for the figure definition, with the physical mapping declared as a **strain** and kept visible: Fig 12's two currencies are description cost and verification cost, not money and joules. The structural point (two incommensurable readings) survives; the exact identification is ours. **Refuter:** a single unit in which an efficiency ratio and an absolute physical reduction are genuinely commensurable.

### F7 — The empirical yes/no is a gap at this budget, not a hidden verdict

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*. Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

The figures narrow the admissible shape of an answer. They do not return a global number for whether absolute, boundary-complete decoupling has been achieved. That is owed to the ground, and the WATSON world sources are the secondary place to look, never an overruling of the figure verdict. A model-to-model condensation that skips the measurement has an unmeasured round trip gap R_T and is dead reckoning.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 9, Fig 8, Fig 13, Fig 4). **Refuter:** a figure line that directly settles the empirical question with a global rate, or a complete instrument that closes the gap.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the standing instruction that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Ground refutation.** A sustained, multi-decade, global, absolute decline in resource throughput and in environmental damage while output grows, measured with consumption-based and cascaded boundaries, is demonstrated. This would not refute the structural shape but would refute the `gap` claim at F7 and move the empirical part toward `grounded`.
2. **Substrate refutation.** A re-census derives energy or the gradient from the other substrate terms, or shows that an internal efficiency change with no physical contact change can count as settlement. This breaks F2.
3. **Compound refutation.** A single instrument in a single unit settles resource use and damage jointly, with no categorically different residue. This breaks F1.
4. **Boundary refutation.** A complete, unauthored enumeration of the habitat is produced in which production-based accounting already contains all indirect and third-order effects. This breaks F5.
5. **Metric refutation.** A frame-independent physical anchor is found that is also the quantity the economic frame optimises, so the frame does score itself with a quantity it produces and Fig 1 :27 does not apply. This breaks F3.

Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with F1 and F2. The question is a union compound and only absolute contact counts. Do not answer with a single yes or no (F1).
2. Keep the two conditions and the boundary choices separate. Never average resource use and damage into one indicator (F1, F4).
3. Ask for the instrument, not for agreement. The honest ask is what would count as a failure of the shape claim (Fig 9 :27, Fig 2 :35).
4. Use the public institutional venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`, one-to-one, and record the verdict before any theory is shown (`02_outreach_email.md`).
5. Treat Fig 4's round trip as mandatory. Any condensation of the figures into a one-line slogan must carry its measured gap R_T, or it is dead reckoning (Fig 4 :30).
