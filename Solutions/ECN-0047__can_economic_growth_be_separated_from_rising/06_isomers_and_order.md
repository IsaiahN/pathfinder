# Structure, order and analogues for the decoupling question

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

Decoupling is used as though it named one thing. It does not: relative decoupling, absolute decoupling, territorial decoupling, consumption-based decoupling, direct decoupling and cascade-complete decoupling are different arrangements of the same atoms. The residual is whether these are variants of one claim or different substances, and what order they must be evaluated in.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: a quantity of output Y, a physical throughput T, a damage D, a boundary B, a time window W, and an intervention I. Bonds held: ⇒ production (throughput produces output), − subtraction (absolute reduction), ⋛ comparison (a threshold between rates), → sequence (intervention before outcome). No new atom is available without an import from the measurement frame.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Are relative and absolute decoupling isomers?

Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

Written as molecules:

```
relative decoupling   =  (Y ⇒ T_with_less_intensity) ⋛ (earlier Y/T)
absolute decoupling   =  T_now − T_earlier  with  Y_now > Y_earlier
```

They share the atoms Y and T but the bond differs. Relative decoupling uses a **comparison** (⋛) between two ratios; absolute decoupling uses a **subtraction** (−) between two physical stocks or flows. Under Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers** at the level of the shared atom set {Y, T}, and the difference is decisive in behaviour: a country can be a perfect relative decoupler and a failing absolute decoupler in the same year. The word "decoupling" is the flat formula that hides the bond.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a single measurement can be written that is both a falling ratio and a falling absolute throughput with no residual difference under any boundary, the isomer claim fails.

### Q2 — Are they a functional group instead?

Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

When the atom set is widened to include damage D, boundary B and window W, relative and absolute decoupling no longer share all atoms. They become two members of a **functional group** of claims of the form "output-related quantity compared with a physical quantity under a declared boundary and window". The qualifier (relative or absolute, territorial or consumption) says which member. This is the weaker and safer reading, and it is the one the outreach packet uses, because it does not require claiming a shared atom set that a checker might dispute.

### Q3 — What order must the evaluation follow?

Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

The dependency order is not the order in which the terms are usually discussed:

1. **Unbundle** the two conditions and the two boundary choices (Fig 9 :54).
2. **Describe** the residual in effect terms before picking the frame (Fig 9 :27).
3. **Enumerate** the habitat across cascades (Fig 11 :43).
4. **Measure** the physical reading at the ground (Fig 1 :23).
5. **Compare** the rates only after steps 1 to 4 (⋛ is a comparison bond, Operators table :51).
6. **Subtract** the absolute quantities (Operators table :46: *"progress is a subtraction, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot say how far along it is"*).

Most published arguments start at step 5. A falling ratio is then optimised before the boundary and the absolute quantity have been read. That is effort landing above the break in Fig 3's chain.

### Q4 — Analogues as the question needs them

- **DDT cascade (Fig 11 :52-53).** The figure's own example: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, residual three steps out. It maps to extraction, manufacture, trade, consumption and waste without an analogy being invented.
- **Metamerism (*The Hard Problem is About Source*).** One green reading, several possible sources. A falling intensity reads the same for a genuinely lower throughput and for a higher throughput with faster growth. The instrument, not the argument, discriminates.
- **Jevons rebound (world source, secondary).** An efficiency gain lowers the effective cost and can call forth more use. In bond terms, ⇒ from efficiency to output can dominate the − of per-unit saving. This is a world analogue, corroborating Fig 11 :40; it does not decide the verdict.

## CONCLUSION

Relative and absolute decoupling are isomers over the shared atom set {Y, T}: same atoms, different bond, different behaviour. Once damage, boundary and window are included they are better described as members of one functional group. The evaluation order is set by dependency, not by rhetorical convenience: unbundle, describe, enumerate, measure, compare, subtract. The strongest single falsifier is a measurement that collapses the ratio and the absolute decline into one reading with no boundary-dependent residue.
