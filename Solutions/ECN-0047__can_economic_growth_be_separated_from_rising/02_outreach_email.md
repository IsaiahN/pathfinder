# Outreach draft: check the shape before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` have been checked against the verbatim figure text by the recipient. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one output on growth and resource use before you decide about the theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a figure-grounded derivation of a question in your field: can economic growth be separated from rising resource use and environmental damage? I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. I want you to check one output against what you already know. If it does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question is not one hard yes/no. It is a union of at least two conditions, absolute resource throughput and environmental damage, each with its own boundary and its own ground. From the framework's rule that an improvement which does not change contact changes nothing, a falling intensity per unit of output is not separation. Separation would have to register at the ground as an absolute decline in physical throughput and damage, measured across consumption as well as production boundaries and across indirect cascades, with the two conditions kept separate. The figure text returns this shape and no global rate. It does not tell us whether separation has actually been achieved, and I am not claiming it does.
>
> **What I am explicitly not claiming:** that decoupling is impossible; that any country's reported falls are offshoring; that efficiency gains are worthless; or that GDP is a bad measure for everything. I am claiming only that a ratio is not an absolute decline and that an aggregate can hide a live slot. The economic frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces, so the verdict has to come from a physical measurement, not from the frame's own indicator.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before this message:**
> 1. a sustained, global, absolute decline in both resource use and damage while output grows, measured with consumption-based and cascaded boundaries;
> 2. a single instrument in a single unit that settles both conditions with no residue;
> 3. a demonstration that an efficiency change which leaves absolute throughput unchanged is accepted at the ground as separation;
> 4. a complete production-based account that already contains all third-order effects.
>
> If any of these holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the links are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers, local path: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in the Project Pathfinder workspace.
> - Canonical usage rule, local path: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`.
> - The eight-step loop and the mode rule, local path: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The framework's own limit is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [your name]
