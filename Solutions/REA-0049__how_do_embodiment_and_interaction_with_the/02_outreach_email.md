**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about embodiment and representation, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** "what a system can represent" should be read as its **reach**, the set of things expressible from what it currently holds, not as the size of an inner store. From that binding, three things follow about physical interaction:
>
> 1. A frame cannot widen its own reach from inside. Only contact with something whose closure differs adds a new representational primitive. So interaction widens what can be represented by supplying access, not by rearranging the same inventory more thoroughly.
> 2. Capability belongs to the agent and its habitat together. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves the agent in isolation. A clean test harness is a different habitat, so a synthetic success proves wiring and not capability.
> 3. The sensorimotor apparatus is an instrument, and instruments are sharpened from worse instruments that were already returning a failing reading. Nobody specified a telescope from an account of Mars. The pose is a precondition of the frame transform, not a decorative detail.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the body literally inserts grounded concepts into a store; that interaction is mutual updating (the physical ground does not update on what a model thinks); that the four E-labels are isomers of one another; that this run has measured any of it. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Which atoms a given body actually imports is left open.
>
> **What would refute it, written down before this message:**
> 1. A frame's reach grows while its contact with every differing closure is held fixed, or a new atom appears by composition alone.
> 2. An agent-only change moves the ground, or capability is shown to belong to the agent with habitat held fixed.
> 3. An instrument is specified from a description alone, with no prior returning instrument to sharpen.
>
> These are the checks I wrote first. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
