# The Tether's answer: embodiment, interaction, and representational reach

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the framing, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

"How does interaction change what a system can represent?" becomes: how does contact change the reach, `closure(Γ)`, and the settlement of terms, given that the reach and the library are different quantities (Fig 6 :42; FORMULA step 8). The question is a **union compound**, not one hard problem: reach-change, settlement-channel and instrument-sharpening can all be present at once, and a lever that clears one leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :54-57).

## 2. The shape

1. **Interaction changes reach and access, not library size.** Fig 6 :14: **"the only operator that moves the wall"** is IMPORT. Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* Fig 6 :25-26: **"An import adds no atom to the world."** and **"The world does not gain primitives; a frame gains access. Composition explores what the operators reach; instruments extend what can be represented at all."** So the world does not get new primitives; the frame gets access, its closure expands, and things become representable that were unreachable. Composition alone cannot do this: Fig 13 :118, *"infinite effort inside a finite reach."*

2. **Capability is agent-and-habitat, not a property of the agent.** Fig 11 :39-40: **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."** and **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."** The habitat is enumerated, never composed (Fig 11 :43); isolation substitutes a habitat rather than removing it, so a test harness proves wiring and not capability (Fig 11 :70). The figure's own stated refuter is carried forward: "an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim" (Fig 11 :72).

3. **The sensorimotor apparatus is an instrument, grown not specified.** Fig 6 :32: **"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."** Fig 6 :33: *"Nobody specified a telescope from an account of Mars."* The Symbols table makes the body literal: the frame transform `F` "Cannot be run without a pose." A body supplies the pose as a precondition, not as a metaphor.

4. **The second job of contact is to keep settlement possible.** Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does."** Fig 2 :33: **"The anchor must not update."** A term is expressible and, separately, settleable. FORMULA step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." A system with a large library and a stale channel holds candidates, not settled terms.

5. **The body-facing representational link is affordance vocabulary, and it is the least measured link.** Fig 3 :7: *"it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked."* Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** Effort spent above this link cannot rescue it (Fig 3 :19). Fig 12 :115: *"having terms for what a situation affords was taken for a dictionary and it is a grammar."*

6. **The three mechanisms are a functional group, not a single mechanism, and the contact bond is ordered.** See `06_isomers_and_order.md`. Embodied, embedded, enactive and extended labels are isomers only when written over a shared atom set with the arrangement stated. "Body and world mutually constitute representation" is the wrong bond: Fig 2 :33 forbids an anchor that updates.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that the body inserts grounded concepts into an inner store. That is the content-addition reading, re-censed away in `01` §0 and §6.
- Not that interaction is mutual updating. The physical ground does not change its answer because a frame thinks differently.
- Not that the four E-labels are isomers as ordinarily stated. They share a functional group until the atom set and bond are declared.
- Not that this run has measured anything. No instrument was built or read. The world sources are corpus sources, and four framework essays share an author with the figures.
- Not a name for which atoms a given body imports. Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

A discriminator, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy"; the colour case closed "not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** The discriminators owed here:

- **Reach-vs-library test:** hold contact with all differing closures fixed and vary internal library size. If reach still grows, F1 breaks.
- **Agent-only test:** hold the habitat fixed and improve the agent. If the ground moves, the capability claim breaks (Fig 11 :72).
- **Instrument lineage test:** find a case where a sensor was specified from a description with no prior returning instrument to sharpen. If one exists, F4 breaks.
- **Settlement test:** find a term settled and composable over for a frame with no live contact with the ground. If one exists, F2 breaks.
- **Affordance-link test:** measure the affordance vocabulary link and show it invariant under changes in physical interaction. If so, F5 breaks.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The reach is finite in kinds and unbounded in arrangement (Fig 13 :113-118), and the set of atoms declared is a visible guess, not nature's own list. The shape stops where the available instruments stop: we can say that contact, habitat, and instruments are the three conditions under which representational reach changes, but not which atom a particular body supplies, nor how large the gain is. The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that a body stores more concepts. It says that "what a system can represent" is its reach, and a frame cannot widen its own reach from inside: only contact with a closure that differs adds a new primitive. Physical interaction does three separable jobs. It imports atoms into the frame, widening reach; it keeps the channel to the ground live so a term can be settled at all; and it supplies sensorimotor instruments that are sharpened from cruder instruments already returning a failing reading. Capability belongs to agent and habitat together, not to the agent alone, so improving an agent while holding contact fixed changes nothing, and a clean test harness proves wiring rather than capability. The body-facing link is the vocabulary of affordances, which is also the least instrumented link in the chain. What is offered is the shape, five written refuters, and the instruments that would close it. Which atoms a given body actually imports stays open until the ground settles it.
