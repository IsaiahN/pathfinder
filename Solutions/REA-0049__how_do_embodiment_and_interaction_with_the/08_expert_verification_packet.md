## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"What a system can represent" should be read as its reach, `closure(Γ)`, not as the size of an inner store. Interaction changes that reach by contact, through three separable mechanisms that must not be averaged: import (only contact with a differing closure adds a representational primitive), settlement (the ground settles a term, and the channel to it can go stale), and instrumentation (the sensorimotor apparatus is an instrument sharpened from a worse instrument already returning something). Capability is a property of agent and habitat together, so improving an agent while holding contact fixed changes nothing, and a substituted habitat such as a test harness proves wiring rather than capability. The framework does not name which atoms a given body imports. It offers the shape, written refuters and the discriminators that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — reach and import (framework-internal plus cognitive-science facing)

- **Statement:** a frame cannot widen its own reach from inside. Only contact with a frame whose closure differs adds a new atom. The world gains no primitives; the frame gains access.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :14 — **"the only operator that moves the wall"**; Fig 6 :21 — **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."**; Fig 6 :25-26 — **"An import adds no atom to the world."** and **"The world does not gain primitives; a frame gains access. Composition explores what the operators reach; instruments extend what can be represented at all."**; Fig 8 :45 — **"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."**
- **What to check:** is the reach/library distinction coherent as stated, and does the world-source analogue (enactive sensorimotor coupling, niche construction) actually describe a gain of access rather than a gain of internal content?
- **Refuter:** a frame's reach grows while contact with every differing closure is held fixed, or a new atom appears by composition alone. Either breaks C1.

### C2 — capability is agent-and-habitat (ecological-psychology and organizational facing)

- **Statement:** capability is not carried by the agent alone; an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing; a test harness is a substituted habitat, so a synthetic success proves wiring and not capability.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39-40 — **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."** and **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**; Fig 11 :43 — **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**; Fig 11 :70 — **"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."**
- **What to check:** does Gibson's affordance as "what it offers the animal ... implies the complementarity of the animal and the environment" support the agent-and-habitat reading? Does Suchman's situated action support the claim that a detached plan is not the capability? Or is the body's role merely causal, as the SEP entry's critics argue?
- **Refuter:** the figure's own: "an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim" (Fig 11 :72). Also: capability demonstrated with habitat held fixed and ground unmoved.

### C3 — instruments are grown, not specified, and the body-facing link is the least measured (engineering and methodology facing)

- **Statement:** a sensorium is an instrument improved from a worse instrument already returning a failing reading; it is not specified from a description. The body-facing representational link is the affordance vocabulary link, which has the fewest instruments.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :32 — **"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."**; Fig 6 :33 — **"Nobody specified a telescope from an account of Mars. A wandering point in the sky was already a reading, and every sharper instrument was built on the residual the last one produced."**; Fig 3 :7 — **"it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked"**; Fig 3 :27 — **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**
- **What to check:** does behavior-based robotics (Brooks, "Intelligence without representation") actually instantiate this, or does it instead show that representation is unnecessary for the behaviour in question? The second is not a refutation of representability, but the distinction must be made explicitly.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a sensor specified from a description alone with no prior returning instrument, OR measure the affordance vocabulary link and show it invariant under changes in physical interaction.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that the body stores or inserts grounded concepts.
- Not that interaction is mutual updating. Fig 2 :33: **"The anchor must not update."**
- Not that the body is metaphysically constitutive of mind. The figures claim capability-and-contact, not a thesis in the constitution debate, which the SEP entry records as live.
- Not that the four E-labels are isomers as ordinarily stated; see `06_isomers_and_order.md`.
- Not that this is a ground reading. Four framework essays share an author with the figures; the external sources are corpus sources. No measurement was taken.
- Not a name for which atoms a body imports. Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*
- Not that a community's agreement settles any of the above. Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**
