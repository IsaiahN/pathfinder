# Expert verification packet - the Tether on prebiotic concentration

**What this is:** a claim in a form a prebiotic chemist, geochemist or origin-of-life researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** No measurement at the origin of life is claimed here. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A dilute aqueous reservoir is a state with no magnitude, so concentration is not more starting material but a cross-kind composition: a spendable difference over space. That difference cannot be manufactured inside the closed dilute frame; it must be imported from a frame whose closure differs (light, thermal gradients, freeze-thaw, wet-dry, tides or a mineral surface), and the import adds no new molecule, only access to a difference that can be spent. The local excess must be held in a substituted habitat enumerated from what is in contact with the molecules, and restored by a cycle, because diffusion and hydrolysis erase a one-off event. Once settled, the concentrated mixture becomes an atom the next composition can read. The framework returns this four-part shape, with the mechanism slot open and four falsifiers written in advance.

## 5. What would settle it

Ground readings, not agreement:

1. **Concentration-factor test:** measure the local excess and the downstream yield in the same coupled setting. Does crossing the threshold change what the next composition produces?
2. **Closure test:** a closed, well-mixed dilute reservoir with no imported difference or boundary. Does a local excess persist? The shape predicts no.
3. **Substitution test:** remove the boundary while holding the difference constant. Does the excess vanish? The shape predicts yes.
4. **Cycle test:** one drying or freezing event versus repeated cycling. Does only the latter accumulate products? The shape predicts yes.
