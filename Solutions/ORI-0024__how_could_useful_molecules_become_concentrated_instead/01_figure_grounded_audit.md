## 0. The residual, in effect terms

The question is not "which prebiotic scenario is true." It is: **by what effect does a population of useful molecules move from a dilute, approximately uniform distribution in water to a local concentration above the threshold at which the next composition becomes favourable?**

- **Supplied state:** monomers (nucleotides, amino acids, fatty acids, HCN derivatives) delivered at low molarity into a large aqueous reservoir. Water is a competing nucleophile, so hydrolysis beats condensation at low concentration (Budin and Szostak, 2010; Campbell et al., 2019, both opened).
- **Required state:** local concentration high enough that weak interactions, template copying, precipitation or membrane assembly can proceed. Budin and Szostak report the requirement explicitly: prebiotic self-assembly and template-directed polymerization *"necessitate high (~mM) monomer concentrations due to the weak interactions involved."*
- **Residual R:** the gap between the two. In effect terms, it is a **flat distribution where a difference is required**: a state (space) with no magnitude, no "more here, less there." Fig 13 :29: *"A state with no structure has no magnitude: three of what, ordered how, varying across what?"*

R is **per slot**, not a global average: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* (Fig 1 :19) and *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* (Fig 1 :22). The slot here is a locality (a pore, a brine vein, a drying film, a droplet), not the whole ocean. Asking "what is the concentration of the ocean" throws away the only signal that says where to look.

**Arity / symmetry / scale.**

- *Arity:* two conditions, supply-at-low-concentration and requirement-at-high-local-concentration, so a **union compound** (Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*), not one hard problem. Unbundle before searching.
- *Symmetry:* asymmetric. *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* (Fig 11 :61). You can add a concentrating mechanism; you cannot subtract the aqueous habitat or the second law. A gradient also carries direction, not mere amount: *"space has directedness"* (Fig 13 :47).
- *Scale:* first order, a structure over a state gives a local concentration (Fig 13 :75-76). Second order, the concentrated molecules become atoms for the next composition (Fig 13 :104). Third order, the cycle that restores the difference becomes a route (Fig 12 :121).

**Partition or union.** Union, not partition. Spending effort on ever-better molecule synthesis (above the break) cannot rescue a missing retention mechanism (below it).

## 3. Findings

### F1 - Dilution is a state with no magnitude, not a shortage of material (primary, triangulated)

Fig 13 :29: *"A state with no structure has no magnitude: three of what, ordered how, varying across what?"*
Fig 13 :74: *"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."*
Fig 13 :75-76: *"Energy and a gradient compose a flow. A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."*

A uniform dilute reservoir is space (a state) carrying no structure that says "more here." Concentration is therefore not "more molecules in the same state"; it is a **cross-kind composition**: a structure (a gradient or an ordering) bonded to the state space. That composition is legal under Fig 13 :74, and it is a distinct term under Fig 13 :76. The practical consequence is that adding more source material (bigger pond, more meteorites) does not by itself close R. It changes the state, not the structure over it.

**Refuter:** demonstrate a sustained local excess in a closed, well-mixed reservoir with no structure bonded to space, no external source, boundary or cycle. If that happens, Fig 13 :29 does not govern concentration and this finding is withdrawn.

**Triangulation:** Fig 13 :29 (substrate) and Fig 8 :32 (*"no search, however long, adds an atom"*), two figures with low overlap on the claim that rearrangement inside a closure does not change what the closure can hold. The reading that "atom" means primitive, not molecule, is stated explicitly in §2.

### F2 - The gradient is irreducible and must be imported from a different closure (primary, triangulated)

Fig 13 :46: *"What makes a gradient a gradient is that it can be spent"*
Fig 13 :47: *"space has directedness"*
Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*
Fig 6 :25: *"An import adds no atom to the world."*
Fig 8 :32: *"no search, however long, adds an atom"*

An ordering spread over space gives only a distribution, *"a fact that stays put"* (Fig 13 :46). What concentration needs is a spendable difference with direction. Because that difference is one of the six irreducible terms (Fig 13 :41: *"Each is indivisible: no arrangement of the other five yields it."*), a closed dilute frame cannot manufacture it by rearranging what it holds. It must arrive from a frame whose closure differs: sunlight, a geothermal gradient, freeze-thaw, wet-dry cycling, tidal forcing, a mineral surface with differential affinity. Crucially, that import *"adds no atom to the world"*: the sun and the vent do not create new prebiotic molecules, they give the local frame access to a difference it can spend.

This is the figure-grounded reason the question is not answered by "there was more HCN" or "more meteorites fell." It is answered by naming what the source frame supplies: a difference that can be spent and a direction along which to spend it.

**Refuter:** a controlled closed system that sustains a local concentration above its equilibrium bulk value with no imported difference of any kind (no thermal, chemical, radiative, surfaces or mechanical asymmetry). That breaks Fig 6 :23.

**Triangulation:** Fig 13 :41/:46-47 (gradient irreducible and directed) and Fig 6 :23/:25 (import from a different closure adds no atom to the world). Low overlap: one is a claim about the primitive set, the other about closure dynamics.

### F3 - The concentrated state needs a boundary, and isolation is substitution, not removal (single-source on the figure, corroborated in the world)

Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."*
Fig 11 :61: *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."*
Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*
Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*

A local excess is not self-maintaining. The aqueous habitat remains (you cannot subtract it), so the concentrated state must be held in a **substituted habitat**: an ice eutectic vein, a mineral pore, a drying film on a mineral surface, a lipid vesicle, a coacervate droplet. Each is not "removal of water" but a different set of contacts with the same ground. Because contact is a fact about the world, introduction is available and exclusion is not; a boundary can be added, but the reservoir and diffusion cannot be deleted. The maintenance is ongoing: the channel decays even though the ground does not, so the boundary or cycle must be continually renewed.

The correct procedure is enumeration, not invention. Fig 11 :44: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."* For this residual that list includes water, salts, minerals, ice, tides, light, geothermal flow, amphiphiles and surfaces, then their contacts. A mechanism is then *read off* the habitat, not composed from an armchair.

**Refuter:** a concentrated state that persists indefinitely with no boundary, compartment, adsorption site or restoring cycle, in a system still in contact with the bulk reservoir. That breaks Fig 11 :66.

**Status:** single-source within the figures (Fig 11 only), corroborated by four independent world sources (Attwater et al. 2010, ice eutectic; Budin and Szostak 2010, mineral adsorption and thermal diffusion columns; Damer and Deamer 2020, hot-spring films; Matsuo and Kurihara 2021, coacervate droplets). These sources share no evidence pool with the framework.

### F4 - Concentration must become an arrangement the next composition can read (triangulated)

Fig 12 :14: *"the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not"*
Fig 12 :15: *"nothing downstream can tell them apart"*
Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."*

A list of molecules present is not the state of the system. The flat-formula failure (Fig 12 :6 to :16) is exactly the dilution error: a dilute mixture and a concentrated mixture of the same monomers have the same ingredient list, and nothing downstream can tell them apart *unless the arrangement is recorded*. Concentration is that arrangement. Once a local composition settles (a polymer forms, a vesicle assembles) it becomes an atom for whatever composes over it, so concentration is the upstream link that makes the next level possible. This is why concentration is *"the arrangement is the substance"* (Fig 12 :94) in a chemical, not merely physical, sense: the same molecules in a brine vein and in the open ocean are different substances for the next reaction.

**Refuter:** show that local concentration makes no difference to whether the next composition proceeds under otherwise identical chemistry. That would break the arrangement claim and make this entire upstream link idle.

**Triangulation:** Fig 12 :14-15/:104 (arrangement and recursion) and Fig 13 :76 (a gradient over space is a distinct term, not a different ingredient list). Low overlap.

### F5 - The cycle is part of the mechanism, not an optional extra (single-source, corroborated)

Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*

Because exclusion is unavailable and the channel decays (F3), a one-off concentration event is erased by diffusion and hydrolysis. What persists is a cycle: wet/dry, freeze/thaw, tidal fill/drain, heating/cooling. The output of one pass (a slightly longer oligomer, a retained lipid) re-enters as the input to the next. A chain cannot do this, so any account that describes a single evaporation or a single freezing event as "the mechanism" has described a link, not a route. Damer and Deamer (2020) make the same point in ground terms: continuous cycling keeps polymers in a steady state with synthesis exceeding hydrolysis.

**Refuter:** a one-shot concentration event that leaves a persistent, composing local excess without any return loop. That breaks Fig 12 :121.

### F6 - The union must be split before searching (primary)

Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*
Fig 9 :4: *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort"*; Fig 9 :5: *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*

"The concentration problem" is a bundled name for at least three conditions with different grounds: (a) produce a spendable difference over space, (b) hold a local excess against the reservoir, (c) couple the concentrated molecules into a next composition. A single lever (more source material, better clay) clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the other conditions still bind. The residual is therefore unbundled in §1 before any mechanism is named.

**Refuter:** show that one variable controls all three conditions with a single monotone lever and no trade-offs. Then the union reading is wrong and the search can be rebundled.

### F7 - The framework returns a shape, not a name (primary, triangulated)

The figures cannot say whether the historical mechanism was evaporation, eutectic freezing, thermophoresis in vent pores, adsorption on clay, or encapsulation in vesicles. That is a question for the ground: experiment, geology, isotopes. What the figures can say is the **shape** any answer must have: an imported spendable difference, a cross-kind composition over space, a substituted habitat that holds it, a cycle that restores it, and a handoff to a next composition. Naming one mechanism would be a binding from the world frame, provenanced as such, not a derivation. This is not a weakness of the answer; it is the answer's honesty condition.

**Refuter:** exhibit a figure rule that selects one concrete mechanism (not just the shape) for this residual. None exists in the text; if one is produced, this finding is revised.

**Triangulation:** Fig 9 :32 and the canonical figures README (*"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*), two distinct documents.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required by the discipline: *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM"* (Fig 5 :65). The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A closed, well-mixed dilute aqueous reservoir at thermodynamic equilibrium is shown to sustain a local concentration above its bulk value with no imported difference, no surface, no boundary and no cycle. This breaks Fig 6 :23 and Fig 13 :29.
2. A concentration increase is produced with no spendable difference and no cross-kind composition, that is, a pure scalar scaling of a state with no gradient directedness. This breaks Fig 13 :46-47 and :74.
3. A local excess persists indefinitely in contact with the bulk reservoir, with no substituted habitat, adsorption site or restoring cycle. This breaks Fig 11 :66 and Fig 12 :121.
4. A seventh primitive is required to explain concentration, so the six-term set is not sufficient. This breaks Fig 13 :41 and the minimality claim.

If any fires, the shape claim is withdrawn. Until one is run, nothing here is labelled `grounded`.

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is the residual a shortage of material?** No. It is a state with no magnitude (Fig 13 :29). Adding material changes the state, not the structure.
2. **What is missing?** A spendable difference over space (Fig 13 :46), which is an irreducible primitive, not derivable from ordering plus space (Fig 13 :41, :45-47).
3. **Where does it come from?** A frame whose closure differs (Fig 6 :23): energy, not new matter. The import adds no atom to the world (Fig 6 :25).
4. **What holds it?** A substituted habitat, enumerated (Fig 11 :43, :66). Introduction is available, exclusion is not.
5. **What keeps it?** A cycle, because the channel decays (Fig 12 :121; Fig 10 :24). A chain cannot close.
6. **What is it for?** It becomes an arrangement the next composition reads; settled products become atoms (Fig 12 :14-15, :104).
7. **Can the framework name the mechanism?** No. It returns a shape (Fig 9 :32). The ground decides which concrete route occurred.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives after re-census: **a spendable difference imported from a different closure, composed over space, held in a substituted habitat, restored by a cycle, and handed to the next composition.** The specific naming of one prebiotic mechanism is refused. Strains after re-census: **one, resolved** (the "atom" strain of §2, fixed by treating the gradient as the irreducible primitive). Standing open item: which mechanism, if any, was historical. That is a `gap` for the figures and a task for the ground, not a silence (Fig 13 :134).
