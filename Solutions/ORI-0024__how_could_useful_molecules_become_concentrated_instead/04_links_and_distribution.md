## Four things in the wider literature that bear directly on the shape

1. **Concentration is a physical, not only a chemical, operation.** Budin and Szostak (2010) show that temperature gradients, ice and mineral surfaces can produce large enrichments without a new synthetic pathway. This corroborates F2: the difference is imported, not made from more starting material.
2. **Equilibrium concentration is set by phase, not by starting dilution.** Attwater et al. (2010) show that a 50-fold or 200-fold dilution of the starting mixture does not dilute the eutectic phase; it shrinks its volume. This is a ground-level instance of F1: the structure, not the amount, sets the local magnitude.
3. **A cycle is required, and a one-off event is erased.** Campbell et al. (2019) and Damer and Deamer (2020) both show that repeated wet-dry cycling, not a single drying, is what accumulates products. Overdilution by rain destroys the yield. This corroborates F5 and the exclusion asymmetry of F3.
4. **A boundary both concentrates and couples.** Matsuo and Kurihara (2021) show droplets concentrating RNA and lipids internally, which corroborates F3 and F4: a substituted habitat holds the excess and makes it readable by the next composition.

None of these sources conflicts with the figure verdict. Where they agree, they do so from different fields (prebiotic chemistry, physics, geology, biology) with no shared evidence pool with the framework, so the agreement is genuine but still secondary: the figures decide.
