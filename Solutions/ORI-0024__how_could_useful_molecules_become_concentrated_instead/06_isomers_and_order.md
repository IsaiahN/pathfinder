# Concentration as arrangement, bond and cycle

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The residual is a gap between bulk dilution and a local concentration above the threshold for the next composition. Three questions are owed about structure and order: (1) is concentration a new ingredient or a new arrangement? (2) which bond and which order make it consequential? (3) are the proposed mechanisms isomers, that is, different arrangements of the same held terms, or different substances with different atoms?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: the six of Fig 13 (space, time, energy as states; spectrum, gradient, network as structures). Bonds held: the seven plus negation (Fig 12; Operators table). No new primitive is available without an import. Currency: local concentration and retention. Anchor: a measured concentration factor and a measured downstream yield.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Is concentration an ingredient or an arrangement?

Fig 12 :14: *"the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not"*
Fig 12 :15: *"nothing downstream can tell them apart"*
Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."*

A dilute solution and a concentrated solution of the same monomers have the same ingredient set. Under the flat-formula collapse (Fig 12 :6-16), nothing downstream can distinguish them, and nothing signals that anything was lost. So concentration is not an ingredient; it is the arrangement that carries the information the ingredient list omits. The next composition reads the arrangement, not the list. Fig 13 :76 confirms this at the substrate level: *"A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."*

**Falsifier:** if two mixtures with identical ingredients but different local concentrations give identical downstream yields under identical conditions, then the arrangement carries no information here and this finding fails. This is directly testable and is in fact what the world sources deny (Budin and Szostak 2010, ~mM requirement; Attwater et al. 2010, activity retained after 50-fold dilution in ice).

### Q2 - Which bond makes concentration consequential?

Fig 12 :46-47 defines a bond as the typed relation between operands, not recoverable from them. Applied here:

- The **gradient** over space is the spendable difference (Fig 13 :22, :46). It is the structure that supplies magnitude.
- The **network** relation is what holds molecules in contact (Fig 13 :24: *"that entities relate, and influence travels"*), whether as mineral pores, ice-brine channels or a vesicle membrane.
- The **cycle** (Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*) is what turns a one-off concentration into a persistent route.
- The **comparison** ⋛ is relevant to fractionation: the Operators table :49 defines it as *"a threshold relation between two quantities."* Selecting a narrow range of molecules by thermal diffusivity or adsorption affinity is threshold-like. It is **not** conjunction: the flat formula's `+` records co-presence and cannot express a cut.

So the mechanism is a molecule in the bond sense: atoms (the six) joined by typed relations (gradient, network, cycle, threshold), and the bond, not the atom list, carries the concentration effect.

**Falsifier:** if the concentration effect can be written as a mere sum of co-present ingredients with no typed relation, the bond analysis is wrong and the flat formula is sufficient.

### Q3 - Are evaporation, freezing, thermophoresis, adsorption and encapsulation isomers?

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

Read at the level of the six held terms, the candidate mechanisms are **isomers**: each uses the same atoms (a state, a structure, a boundary relation, a cycle) but arranges them differently. Evaporation bonds the gradient to space through removal of solvent; freezing bonds it through exclusion of solute from a growing crystal; thermophoresis bonds a temperature difference to a molecular diffusivity; adsorption bonds it to a surface affinity; encapsulation bonds it to a closed membrane. Same atoms, different bonds, different substance. The ingredient lists look almost identical, which is why the literature presents them as rival "settings" rather than as variants of one shape.

At a coarser reading they are members of one **functional group**: *"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* The kind is "imported spendable difference plus substituted habitat plus cycle"; the qualifier is the physical carrier. This is the right level for the Tether answer, which gives the group and refuses to name the member.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if any candidate mechanism requires an atom the others do not (for example, a seventh primitive such as a specialised "vitality" or a new conserved quantity), the isomer claim fails and the mechanisms are different substances in the strong sense. No world source read here requires such an atom, so the isomer reading stands as `specified`.

### Q4 - What order is the union?

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*
Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."*

Order is dependency, not chronology of discovery. The chain is:

```
external difference (D2) -> local excess (D1) -> retention in substituted habitat (D3)
    -> cycle restores the difference -> concentrated mixture becomes an atom
    -> polymer / vesicle / copied strand settles -> next level
```

Each link consumes what the previous one produces. Effort spent improving a link above the break (better monomer synthesis) cannot rescue a link below it (no retention). Once a concentrated composition settles, it becomes an atom at the next level, which is why the order is recursive rather than a one-way pipeline.

**Falsifier:** demonstrate that the next composition can proceed at bulk dilution with no intermediate settled concentration. Then the handoff link is idle and the order collapses.

## CONCLUSION

Concentration is an **arrangement**, not an ingredient (Fig 12 :14-15, :94). Its bonds are the gradient, the network relation, the cycle and the threshold comparison (Fig 13 :22, :46; Fig 12 :121; Operators :49). The candidate prebiotic mechanisms are **isomers** over the same six held terms, or, at the level the framework actually answers, members of one **functional group** (Fig 12 :117, :123). The order is dependency order (Fig 3 :20), with a settled concentrated composition becoming an atom for the next level (Fig 12 :104).

This rescues the part of the question that is figure-tractable: the shape, the bond types and the order. It refuses the part that is not: which isomer was historical. That remains a ground question.
