## Wider literature that bears on the question but was not used to decide it

These entries were opened through public search listings and are recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv` with the accessed date. They are listed here as reading directions, not as authorities for the verdict.

- Judea Pearl, "Causal inference in statistics: An overview", *Statistics Surveys* 3 (2009), 96 to 146. The paper stresses "the assumptions that underly all causal inferences". https://projecteuclid.org/journals/statistics-surveys/volume-3/issue-none/Causal-inference-in-statistics-An-overview/10.1214/09-SS057.full
- Peter Spirtes, "Introduction to Causal Inference", *Journal of Machine Learning Research* 11 (2010). On statistical equivalence classes: "all of the DAGs in a statistical equivalence class fit the data and the background assumptions equally well, and are equally simple." https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume11/spirtes10a/spirtes10a.pdf
- Amin Jaber, Jiji Zhang and Elias Bareinboim, "Causal Identification under Markov Equivalence: Completeness Results", ICML 2019. Quote used: "a PAG can be learned directly from data, and the scientist does not need to commit to a particular, unique diagram." http://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/jaber19a.html
- Jonas Peters, Dominik Janzing and Bernhard Scholkopf, *Elements of Causal Inference* (2017): "we require further assumptions to obtain identifiability results." https://web.math.ku.dk/~peters/jonas_files/ElementsOfCausalInference.pdf
- Rory J. Maizels and James Briscoe, "Gene regulatory networks: from correlative models to causal explanations", *Nature Reviews Genetics* (2026): "GRNs, which should provide mechanistic explanations, are increasingly reduced to statistical correlations". https://www.nature.com/articles/s41576-026-00939-1
- Gilles Monneret and colleagues, "Identification of marginal causal relationships in gene networks from observational and interventional expression data", *PLOS ONE* (2017): the paper notes that "even with a sufficient amount of interventional data, i.e. roughly one knock-out for each gene, a directed acyclic graph (DAG) cannot generally be accurately estimated". https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0171142

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22** "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Sending the full framework before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and its refuter; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who has absorbed the whole framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27** "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what they would expect to see if the claim were true before giving them any source.
- **Fig 2 :40** "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." A panel of correlation-only methods all trained on the same matrix is one witness.
- **Fig 9 :60** "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." A venue's impact factor or a reviewer's reputation is a budget filter, never a witness.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its four refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and what instrument would settle it; (3) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer, with the third stage

1. **Stage 1: validate the output.** Send the residual, the shape claim and the refuters. Record their verdict before showing any theory. This is Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source" and Fig 2 :45 "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the source links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, by Fig 2 :40.
3. **Stage 3: offer the formula as a method to reuse.** Fig 4 :22 permits sending a generator. Each user must state their own ground, write their own refuter before the run, and declare `general`, `specified` or `grounded`.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. If any refuter in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` fires, stop and correct the claim before sending anything.

## Hosting

The folder is the archive. There is no uploaded packet, no mailing list, and no automated send. If the material is hosted, prefer an opt-in download page that lets a recipient decline contact over an attachment pushed to a list. This is a practical choice with a figure-grounded reason: it keeps the contact channel available rather than consuming it.
