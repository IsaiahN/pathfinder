# The Tether's answer: causal influence from partial network correlations

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the barrier shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A researcher holds correlations over the nodes of a network that was only partly measured. They want directed causal influence: which node changes which, in which direction, with what sign. The residual is the difference between what the flat correlation reading predicts and what the network would do under an intervention. Under Fig 9 :54 this is a **union compound**: a direction condition and a coverage condition that bind at the latent common cause. Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound." It gets one loop per condition, not an average.

## 2. The shape

1. **A correlation reading is symmetric; causal influence is ordered.** Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." The Operators table puts `+` under the symmetric test and `→` under the ordered test. Two directed networks with the same correlations are isomers whose difference shows up only under intervention. Direction is not in the reading.
2. **A hidden common cause makes nodes agree without influencing each other.** Fig 2 :18 to :21: "one evidence pool / they never talk, and still agree / so the agreement is the pool talking, / not two frames confirming each other." Partial measurement is what leaves that pool unnamed.
3. **Correlation is valence, not a verdict.** Fig 2 :39: "Correlation has valence." Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." A panel of correlation-only estimators sharing one matrix is one witness.
4. **More search at the same reach cannot add the missing node.** Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means." And from inside the frame, depth and absence look the same: Fig 6 :27 "Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."
5. **The reachable answer is a shape, not a name.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." The shape is the class of graphs compatible with the reading plus the list of discriminating readings that would narrow it. A particular graph is licensed only by the ground or by a provenanced import, never by the reading alone.
6. **What is owed is contact.** Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." A known-target intervention, a formerly latent node brought into the measurement, or a time-resolved reading that supplies the state between two structures. An imported structural assumption can narrow the class, but only with a source gate and a shadow test.
7. **Do not name the answer that the framework does not hold.** The README says the framework returns a shape, not a name. The correction in `05` §5b applies: naming a member of an equivalence class is a binding from the causal frame onto held concepts, permitted with provenance, but it is not the reading's own output.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that causal inference from observational data is impossible. Constraint-based discovery, Markov equivalence classes and identification algorithms over partial ancestral graphs all narrow the class without an intervention. The claim is narrower: the unique name is not owed by the partial reading.
- Not that absence of a correlation proves absence of an edge. Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
- Not that the unique graph is universally unidentifiable. Under additional assumptions (time order, functional form, no unmeasured confounding) a specific query may be identifiable. Those assumptions are imports and are priced as such.
- Not that a consensus of methods corroborates. Siegenthaler and Gunawan found that "network inferability depends on the available information contained in the data, but not on the inference method."
- Not grounded. No intervention was performed and no latent node was measured in producing this answer.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A discriminator, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

- **Intervention test:** does intervening on node `X` change `Y` in the way the chosen graph predicts, or in the way a rival in the same class predicts? This is the ground for the direction condition.
- **Latent-source test:** does bringing a formerly unmeasured common cause into the reading collapse the apparent edge between two nodes? This is the ground for the coverage condition.
- **Cycle test:** does the system close a feedback loop that a directed acyclic representation excluded by construction? Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
- Pre-registered falsifiers from `01` §4: symmetry broken without contact; idle latent; agreement predicting interventional outcomes; closure escaped by search.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 6 :27 says depth and absence look the same from inside; Fig 8 :39 says a search that finds nothing is not a proof of absence. So the answer stops at the class, records which discriminator would close it, and declares the assumptions used to get this far. *They're Scanning Books When They Should* warns: "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible." A visible choice can be revised; an invisible one cannot. The lineage is the checkable part. "Indiscernibility is not identity."

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that causal influence cannot be learned from data. It says that a correlation matrix is the wrong kind of record for the answer: it is symmetric where causal influence is ordered, it lists ingredients where the bond carries the meaning, and a partially measured network leaves the common cause unnamed. What is reachable is a shape, the class of directed structures compatible with the reading and a ranked list of the extra readings that would cut that class. What is not reachable from the reading alone is a unique graph, a sign that survives intervention, or the identity of a hidden source. Agreement among correlation-only methods is one evidence pool, not corroboration. The ground is a known-target intervention or a newly measured node, and the answer stops at the class until one of those pays. This is a `specified` shape from a simulated derivation, not a measurement.
