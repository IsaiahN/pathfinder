## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about inferring a network's causal influence from observed correlations when the network is only partially measured. Option A: a unique directed graph with signs and strengths is recoverable from the partial correlation reading. Option B: only a narrowed class of structures is recoverable, and discriminating within it requires new contact or a provenanced import. What each leads to: A reads a symmetric projection as if it carried an ordered bond, spends the independence of the people checking it by asking them to agree with a name, and mistakes a hidden shared source for an edge. B reports a shape, states its falsifiers, and records which discriminator would close it. The figures decide between A and B; the world sources below corroborate or conflict.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain giving actors, currency and anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between the correlation reading over measured nodes and the directed influence structure one wants. Arity: two conditions, direction and coverage, bound at the latent common cause, so a union compound (Fig 9 :54), not one hard question. Symmetry: correlation is symmetric in its node pairs; causal influence is an ordered bond. Scale: node pair, whole graph, and measured subnetwork versus containing network.
- **Domain 1, the observation frame.** Actors: measured nodes, samples, association estimates. Currency: co-variation. Anchor: the assay that produced the readings. Question type: partition of measured from latent, and description of what the channel carried.
- **Domain 2, the causal-generating frame.** Actors: all nodes, directed edges, structural assignments, interventions. Currency: response under intervention. Anchor: a known-target perturbation whose outcome is read. Question type: identifiability of a query from a distribution plus assumptions.
- **Domain 3, the inference and verification frame.** Actors: researchers, estimators, candidate graphs. Currency: agreement and residual among candidates. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all three domains draw on one evidence pool, so this domain's independence is zero.
- **Partition or union.** Union: direction and coverage overlap at the latent common cause. Keep two loops, never average their verdicts (Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated.").
- **Order by dependency.** Describe the channel first; then ask whether the vocabulary can express an ordered edge and a latent node; then bind the objective (class or name); then choose the route (search, instrument, import); then carry with provenance (Fig 3 :20).

Strain test applied: Fig 2 :40 forbids agreement between frames sharing a pool as a verdict, but it does not forbid one frame's reading from narrowing a class. Fig 12 :5 and Fig 8 :54 support the narrowing. Re-census resolves the apparent conflict; see `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §6.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

Not in general. From a partial correlation reading, a unique causal influence on the network is not recoverable; a narrowed class of structures is. The reasons are structural: the reading is symmetric where the bond is ordered (Fig 12 :117), a hidden shared source produces agreement without influence (Fig 2 :21), and more search inside the same closure cannot add a node that was never measured (Fig 6 :21). Agreement among correlation-only estimators is one evidence pool, not corroboration. What closes the residual is an intervention with a known target, a formerly latent node brought into the reading, a time-resolved measurement, or a provenanced assumption that survives its shadow test. The shape is the class plus the ranked discriminator list, not a single graph and not a vague warning about correlation. This is a `specified`, ungrounded result from a simulated derivation.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable if the shape is reported as a shape. The costly error is reporting a name and having an intervention fail on it, which wastes a budget and can harm a system. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The avoidable version of the cost is spending a reviewer's independence by sending a recording instead of a generator.
- **undoable:** yes for the inference, which can be withdrawn and reissued as a narrower class. No for consequences already produced by acting on a wrong edge; an intervention that changed the world cannot be un-run. That asymmetry is why the refuter is written before the claim is applied.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A known-target intervention that selects one member of the class, a measurement of a formerly latent common cause that collapses an apparent edge, a time-resolved reading that reveals a feedback cycle, or a derivation showing that a symmetric reading determines a unique ordered bond without contact. None of these has been run here, so the verdict remains `candidate`.
