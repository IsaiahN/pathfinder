# Union, order and analogues for institutional endurance

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The folder-level question treats "reversal" as one thing. It is not obviously one thing. Two questions are owed: (1) is durability a single property or a union of overlapping conditions, and (2) what order do the conditions come in, so that a lever applied at one link does not get wasted on another.

## DOMAIN CENSUS (repeated briefly, derived from the residual line alone)

Actors: rule text, agencies, beneficiaries, professions, office holders, researchers. Currency: compliance, resources, support, legitimacy, residuals. Anchor: the ground in D2 (behaviour and outcomes), the accumulated stake in D3. D1 and D4 have no anchor of their own, so they are channels rather than domains. Union of D2 and D3; D1 and D4 are carried by them. Order by dependency: perceive whether the reform still operates, then name what would count as endurance, then plan the maintenance lever, then carry the method.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 Is reversal one thing or a union?

- Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
- Fig 9 :57: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."**
- Reversal has at least four subtypes: formal repeal, defunding, unenforcement, reinterpretation until the rule no longer does anything. Each can occur with the others absent.
- Fig 9 :55-56: **"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**
- ⇒ Repeal clears the formal region. It does not clear a still-resourced and still-defended implementation routine. Conversely, defunding a live constituency's program leaves the statute on the books and the symbolic fight intact.
- **Conclusion:** the residual is a union compound and must be unbundled before a mechanism is searched for. `general` for the figure rule, `specified` for the four subtypes.

### Q2 What is the dependency order, and where can effort be wasted?

- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
- Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**
- Link 1 (perception): can anyone tell that the reform is still operating? Without a measure, "endurance" is a legal-text reading, which is the metamerism error of `05` §3.
- Link 2 (vocabulary): are there terms for what the reform affords and what would count as progress? Fig 3 :7: "it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked".
- Link 3 (objective): can the maintainer say what would count as success in its own terms? Operators table :52: "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one". Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."
- Links 4 and 5 (planning, carrying): a route under incomplete information, then "name what worked, verify it, and carry it to the next problem" (Fig 3 :15).
- Most reform effort lands on the statute, which is easy to measure. That is effort above the break when the break is at links 1 to 3.
- **Falsifier:** if a reform with no measure of continued operation and no stated objective nevertheless endures at the same rate as one with both, the dependency order is wrong for this domain.

### Q3 Are enduring and reversed reforms isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- If the atom set is held as {rule, agency, budget, constituency, measure, narrative}, then an enduring reform and a reversed one can share every atom and differ only in the arrangement: in one, the constituency is bonded to the measure and the measure to the agency, so the loop closes; in the other, all atoms are present but the constituency is bonded only to the founding government, so the chain ends at the handover.
- Under Fig 12's definition that is an **isomer** claim: same atoms, different bond, different substance. It is a real checkable claim, not a metaphor, provided the atoms are enumerated rather than composed (Fig 11 :43).
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if an enduring reform requires an atom absent from a reversed one, or vice versa, the isomer claim fails and the weaker functional-group claim stands instead. Single-source for the figure definition; world check not yet run.

### Q4 What order is the union?

- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"**
- Each reform that has been settled against the ground becomes an atom at the next level. A sequence of settled reforms can then compose into a regime property, which is why "an institutional culture of compliance" is a derivative, not a primitive.
- Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**
- The order is therefore: settle contact first (D2), create the stake that maintains the channel second (D3), allow the norm to follow third (D4), and only then expect the legal text (D1) to carry any weight on its own.
- Fig 4 :34: **"The level a method lands on remakes it. A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."**
- The same reform design landing in a different habitat becomes a different institution. This is the figure's reason for refusing a universal institutional blueprint.

## CONCLUSION

Reversal is a union compound, not one outcome. The dependency order puts ground contact before stake and stake before norm and text. Over a fixed atom set, enduring and reversed reforms are an isomer claim with a stated falsifier; the weaker functional-group claim is the fallback if the atom sets differ. No new primitive is introduced, and the figures do not name which bond arrangement is best. That remains a binding from the world frame.
