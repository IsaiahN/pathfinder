# Outreach draft: check the shape claim before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the refuter in this message is understood and the verdict is recorded before any framework material is shown. If the shape claim fails on contact, there is nothing else to look at.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about why reforms survive a change of government

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about institutional reform. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output fails, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** A reform is enacted under one government. Later the office changes hands. Some reforms keep being obeyed and resourced; others are repealed, defunded, unenforced, or quietly stop being used. The formal text does not sort them. The question is what property predicts the difference.
>
> **The shape claim (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A reform endures to the degree that it has stopped being a text held by the outgoing office and has become three things: a maintained channel to an outcome that does not move with the government (an actual behaviour, a measurement, a budget that pays or does not); an introduced set of actors whose stake survives the original sponsors (an agency, a constituency, a set of expectations already accumulated); and a loop in which the reform's own operation regenerates the support that keeps it going. Reversal is what happens when the channel is simply not maintained after the handover. The drift then looks principled from inside the new government, which is why nobody there notices it.
>
> This is a **shape**, not a name. It does not say "an independent court" or "constitutional entrenchment" or "a broad beneficiary coalition". Those are bindings from the research literature onto the shape, and until their provenance is shown they carry no weight here.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** Not that the literature is wrong. Not that path dependence, veto points, increasing returns or policy feedback are refuted. They are secondary corroboration and they share one evidence pool. Not that this derivation is a measurement. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, and it cannot settle the question it reports on.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim.** I wrote these before sending this:
> 1. A reform with no maintained outcome channel and no introduced stake consistently endures across many handovers.
> 2. A reform with all four features listed above is consistently reversed.
> 3. Repeal, defunding, unenforcement and reinterpretation turn out to be one lever with no residual difference between them.
> 4. A bare legal text, with no method and no constituency, reliably reproduces its behaviour in a different habitat.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the public sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula (public repo, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Arrival of the Fittest: The Tether Architecture: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
