# Expert verification packet: the Tether on institutional reform endurance

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher of institutions can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the rules applied, not `grounded`.** No cross-country measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The unit of endurance is a reform together with its habitat, not the reform text. Reversal after a change of government is primarily the failure of the channel that kept the rule in contact with an outcome that does not move with political power. Endurance requires three conditions: an independent outcome channel that is maintained, an introduced set of actors whose stake survives the original sponsors, and a loop in which the reform's operation regenerates its own support. A norm whose reference point is the incoming government's narrative is a frame, not an anchor, and will drift with that government invisibly. The statute is a recording carried by these conditions, not a generator. The figures return this shape; they do not name which concrete institutional arrangement occupies the channel slot.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 Implementation and contact, checkable against cases

- **Statement:** a reform whose outcome channel is not maintained after handover will drift or be hollowed out, even if its text remains in force. Repeal is one subtype of reversal, not the whole class.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does"; Fig 10 :27-28 "the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"; Fig 11 :40 "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
- **What to check:** can you name a case where measurement, inspection or funding was withdrawn at handover and the reform nevertheless kept changing its target outcome in the intended direction? Can you separate repeal from unenforcement empirically?
- **Refuter:** a documented multi-case pattern of no-channel, no-stake reforms that endure. That breaks Fig 10 :24 and Fig 11 :40 for this domain.

### C2 Habitat and stake, checkable against the same cases

- **Statement:** endurance is a property of rule plus habitat. The reform can introduce an agency, a constituency or accumulated expectations, but cannot exclude the informal habitat it lands in, so maintenance is permanent work.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39 "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"; Fig 11 :61 "Introduction is available; exclusion is not."; Fig 11 :63 "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state." Supporting: Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
- **What to check:** is the beneficiary constituency an effect of the reform, or a pre-existing group that merely captured it? Does the reform's own operation actually reproduce the support that defends it, or is that an ex post story?
- **Refuter:** a controlled comparison with rule text held constant, habitat varied, and endurance statistically independent of habitat. That breaks the capability claim for this domain. Cycle closure is single-source and analogue; it fails if a reform reproduces its defenders yet always reverses, or never reproduces them and never reverses.

### C3 Norm, text and union, checkable against the measurement instrument

- **Statement:** a norm anchored to the incoming government's narrative cannot settle anything; a bare legal text is a recording, not a method; and "reversal" must be unbundled before a lever is searched for.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :18 "a declared meaning is not a grounded one"; Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."; Fig 4 :25 "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."; Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- **What to check:** do your outcome measures distinguish a repealed rule from one that is still on the books but unused? Do they distinguish a rule that lost its constituency from one that kept it?
- **Refuter:** a single lever that removes all four reversal subtypes at once with no residual difference; or a bare text, with no method and no constituency, that reliably reproduces its behaviour in a different habitat.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a name: no claim that courts, entrenchment, veto points, coalitions or infrastructure are the answer. Those are bindings from the world frame with a provenance record.
- Not a refutation of North, Pierson, Jacobs and Weaver, Tsebelis, Acemoglu or the policy-feedback literature. They are secondary corroboration and share one evidence pool (Fig 2 :40).
- Not that durability is one property or that one lever explains all reversal.
- Not that a reform can be made permanent (Fig 11 :63).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken in this derivation.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. A cross-reform dataset with a discriminator that separates repeal, defunding, unenforcement and reinterpretation, and that measures outcome contact, introduced stake and loop closure independently of the legal text. Specific refuters are listed under C1 to C3 and in §3b of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`. If the full-feature reforms reverse and the no-channel reforms endure, the shape is wrong.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 10 :24, :27-28, Fig 11 :39, :61, :63 and Fig 9 :55. Six short passages.
2. Name one reform from your own field that fits C1 and one that contradicts it.
3. Say whether the beneficiary constituency in your case was created by the reform or captured it.
4. Say what your outcome measure would read if the rule existed on paper but nobody acted on it.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
