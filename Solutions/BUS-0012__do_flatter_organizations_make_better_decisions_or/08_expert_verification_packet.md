# Expert verification packet - the Tether on flatter organizations

**What this is:** a claim in a form an organization scholar or practitioner can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** No organization was measured. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, so it cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"The flatter organizations make better decisions, or create coordination problems and unclear accountability" is a false binary. Decision quality and coordination with accountability are two conditions with different anchors, so their verdicts must be kept separate and never averaged. Decision quality is read per decision slot against a ground that does not update on the organization's own report. Coordination is the channel that carries information to that slot. Accountability is a seat able to refuse a verdict about a decision it did not author. Flattening changes the bond among held atoms, not the loop, and changes contact with the ground only if a contact edge actually moves. Over a fixed atom set, tall and flat are isomers. No figure line selects a number of layers; that is a declared gap. What the framework returns is this shape, with refuters written in advance.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - The union claim, checkable by reading Figures 9 and 1

- **Statement:** the question must be split into two conditions with different anchors; averaging them destroys the signal.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 - **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** Fig 9 :57 - **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."** Fig 1 :19 - **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22 - **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**
- **Refuter:** a ground-anchored measurement in which decision quality and accountability always move together under a single condition across every slot and habitat, with no categorical residue left over. Then the union collapses to one loop and this claim fails.

### C2 - Organization-facing, checkable against a delayering case

- **Statement:** flattening changes the bond (who relates to whom, where information travels), not the per-slot loop; a structural change counts only if it changes contact, and coordination work is redistributed rather than deleted.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :20 - **"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."** Fig 13 :24 - **"that entities relate, and influence travels"**. Fig 11 :40 - **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."** Fig 10 :24 - **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**.
- **What to check:** list the contact edges before and after a delayer. Did information reach the deciding slot from the same ground, or from a pooled internal report? Did decision quality improve per slot or only as an org-wide average? Is the coordination work gone, or carried by a new role or ritual?
- **Refuter:** a per-slot decision gain that survives with no contact edge changed, or with channel fidelity falling and no substitute coordination structure. That is the agent-only improvement Fig 11 :40 says cannot happen. Also: if a reorganisation called flattening necessarily changes the atom set, the isomer claim in `06_isomers_and_order.md` fails and only the functional-group reading stands.

### C3 - The accountability claim, checkable against the decision rights map

- **Statement:** unclear accountability is a structural seat failure, not a matter of titles. A verifier that can reconstruct a decision cannot verify it, and a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :42 - **"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."** Fig 10 :47 - **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**. Fig 10 :59 - **"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."**
- **What to check:** for one recent high-impact decision, name the person or body that could have refused it without having authored it. If the only checker is the author, or a reporting convention settled the outcome, the seat is vacant by this definition. Purpose-built substitute roles (coaches, client owners, narrow authority slices) count as seats if they can refuse.
- **Refuter:** an organization that measures high accountability with no seat able to refuse a verdict and with verifiers fully able to reconstruct the claims they check. That would show the seat condition is not necessary here and falsify this sub-claim.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that flat is better or flat is worse. No directional verdict is asserted.
- Not a span of control, a layer count, or a Dunbar-derived headcount. That is a declared `gap` (Fig 9 :32 and Fig 13 :134), not a withheld answer.
- Not that shared mental models, alignment or consensus settle decision quality. They are conventions (Fig 2 :40 and :41).
- Not that removing a layer removes the functions it performed. The functions still have to be fulfilled somewhere.
- Not that any named organization was studied by this derivation, and not that the literature cited here was pooled as corroboration across different fields without declaring overlap.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement paid for any claim in this packet.

## 5. What would settle it

An instrument, not agreement. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."*

1. Per-slot outcome anchor: what did the ground return, in units that do not move when the organization grades itself?
2. Channel fidelity per edge: where does information decay after a delayer, and does it reach the deciding slot?
3. Refusal test: name a seat that could refuse a verdict on a decision it did not author.
4. Contact delta: which contact edges actually moved with the reorganisation?
5. Chain diagnosis: which link in perception, vocabulary, objective, planning and learn and carry stops the chain? Fig 3 :27 - **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :55 and :57, Fig 1 :19, and Fig 10 :24 and :42. Five short passages.
2. Check C2 against one delayer you know: were contact edges and coordination substitutes enumerated, or only layer counts?
3. Check C3 against one high-impact decision: who could have refused it without authoring it?
4. Name one instrument that would anchor per-slot decision quality in your setting, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
