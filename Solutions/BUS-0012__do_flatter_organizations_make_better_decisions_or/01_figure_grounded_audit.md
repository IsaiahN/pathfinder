## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

`R = |Γ(b,a) - o'|`, the gap between what an organization's decision model predicts and what the ground returns. Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Slots here are individual decisions, teams, and time periods. The question asks: does reducing hierarchy depth shrink `R` (the "better decisions" option) or grow `R` (the "coordination problems and unclear accountability" option)?

The two options are not two answers to one question. Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** A flattened structure changes the channel to the local ground *and* removes or redistributes the coordination and verification structure. These are two conditions, and a case in both is categorically different from a case in either.

**Shape bound before the run.** An acceptable answer must, for each slot: (a) name the anchor that settles decision quality, (b) name the channel that carries information to that slot, (c) name the seat that can refuse a verdict, and (d) state what would reverse the call. This is the derive-first discipline of Fig 5 :65: *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."*

## 1. Census of R: arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity.** Relational, at least three places: decision model `Γ`, action `a`, observation `o'`. Indexed by slot and ordered by time. A fourth place, the network edge between two slots, carries coordination.
- **Symmetry.** "Flatter" is an ordering, a spectrum in the six-term sense (Fig 13 :20: *"ordering, so more and less can be said"*). "Better" is a threshold comparison, an ordered bond, not a symmetric join (Operators table :51: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*). "Coordination" is a network relation (Fig 13 :24: **"that entities relate, and influence travels"**). None of the governing bonds is symmetric, so an arrangement is a different substance, not a spelling of the same one.
- **Scale.** The single decision loop (Fig 1), the organizational network across the membrane between one agent and many (Fig 4), and the time channel across which contact is kept or lost (Fig 10 :24). The transform between scales loses information (Fig 4 :27), so a claim at one scale does not transfer to another without a measured round trip.

## 4. Findings F1 to F10

### F1 - The question is a union, not a binary (decisive for the framing)

Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**
Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**

Asking "better decisions OR coordination problems" treats two conditions as alternatives. The figures require unbundling: D1 has an outcome anchor, D3 has a refusal anchor. Their verdicts are kept separate and never averaged.
**Mode:** `general` for the union rule, `specified` for the application to this question.
**Refuter:** a single ground-anchored measurement in which decision quality and accountability always move together in the same direction across every slot and every habitat, with no residue left over. Then the union collapses to one loop and this finding fails.

### F2 - Decision quality is per slot against an anchor that does not update

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**
Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**
Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

An org-wide "decision quality" score that averages across teams and quarters is exactly the signal-killing move Fig 1 names. The anchor must be something that does not move when the organization reports a success: a customer decision, a market result, a patient outcome. A flat team's shared belief that it decided well is Fig 2's mirror.
**Mode:** `specified`.
**Refuter:** a reported decision-quality gain that survives per-slot indexing and is paid by an anchor that does not update on the organization's own report. Until such a measurement exists, "flat organizations make better decisions" is a candidate, not a verdict.

### F3 - Flattening changes the cast and the bond, not the loop

Fig 4 :20: **"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."**
Fig 13 :24: **"that entities relate, and influence travels"**

Depth is a parameter of the network structure, one of the six structures already held. It changes who carries the loop and how influence travels. The loop itself (perceive, plan, act, predict) is the kernel that is invariant with size. So the question is not "does the loop work better when flat" in the abstract; it is "which link does the new bond structure feed, and does the residual at that slot fall?"
**Mode:** `general` for kernel invariance, `specified` for organizations.
**Refuter:** a depth change that measurably alters the per-slot loop itself, for example removing perception or prediction at a slot rather than reassigning who performs it. That would falsify the kernel claim here.

### F4 - The channel decays, not the ground: coordination failure is a different quantity

Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**
Fig 10 :26: **"saturated metrics, pooled readings"**
Fig 10 :47: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**

"Coordination problems" map to D2: information reaches the wrong slot, arrives stale, or is pooled from a shared source. This is not the same quantity as D1's decision quality. Removing layers shortens one path and can load another (wider spans, more cross-team edges), so the channel can fail in a new place. A reporting convention that is treated as if it were the outcome is Fig 10 :47's failure: the convention has decided the outcome and stopped being a convention.
**Mode:** `specified`.
**Refuter:** coordination failure that persists and grows while channel fidelity, measured per slot against a non-updating anchor, stays high and non-declining. If that is observed, coordination is not a channel failure and this mapping is wrong.

### F5 - Accountability is a seat, and the seat must be able to refuse

Fig 10 :42: **"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."**
Fig 10 :52: **"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."**
Fig 10 :59: **"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."**

"Unclear accountability" is not vagueness about titles. It is the structural condition in which no seat can refuse a verdict because the people judging a decision are the people who made it, or because a convention has been promoted into a verdict. Flattening that removes every outside seat can produce exactly this, even when decisions are fast. Conversely, a flat organization that builds purpose-built substitute seats (coaches, client owners, salary groups with one narrow slice of authority each) has not removed accountability; it has redistributed it.
**Mode:** `specified`.
**Refuter:** an organization that measures high accountability with no seat able to refuse a verdict and with verifiers fully able to reconstruct the claims they check. That would show the seat condition is not necessary and falsify this finding.

### F6 - Capability is agent-and-habitat, so an org-only change counts only if it changes contact

Fig 11 :39: **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**
Fig 11 :40: **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

Redrawing the org chart is an agent-side change. It matters only if it changes contact with the ground: who hears the customer, who can see the failed outcome, who can refuse. A flat structure that removes a listening layer without moving contact has changed the drawing and nothing else.
**Mode:** `specified` for organizations, `general` for the capability rule.
**Refuter:** a flattening that improves per-slot `R` with the habitat held strictly constant and with no measured change in any contact edge. That is the agent-only improvement this figure says cannot happen.

### F7 - Internal agreement in a flat team is one evidence pool, not corroboration

Fig 2 :31: **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."**
Fig 2 :40: **"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**
Fig 7 :12 and :13: *"same closure, handed along;"* / *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*

Flatter organizations often pride themselves on alignment and shared mental models. Those are conventions about meaning, which Fig 2 :41 permits as *"efficient for a convention"*. They are disqualifying as a verdict about decision quality when the agreeing people read the same report. Fig 7 :12 to :13 is the same law at the room scale.
**Mode:** `specified`.
**Refuter:** a flat group whose decision quality improves while every member draws on one evidence pool, with quality independently anchored at the ground. That would show shared reading does not corrupt the verdict here.

### F8 - Diagnose which link stops the chain before crediting the structure

Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**
Fig 3 :29: *"Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."*

Flattening is a structural intervention, but decision failure can be at perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, or learning. Effort spent above the break cannot rescue one below it (Fig 3 :19). If the vocabulary or objective link is missing, adding autonomy pushes decisions down to people who cannot say what would count as progress.
**Mode:** `specified`.
**Refuter:** a measured improvement in per-slot `R` when the vocabulary and objective links are demonstrably absent and unmeasured. That would break the dependency order.

### F9 - A unique optimal number of layers is a `gap`

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**
Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"**
Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

No figure line selects a span of control or a layer count. The framework returns a shape, not a name: any working structure must keep a channel to a non-updating ground, keep a seat able to refuse, and carry influence across nodes. Which number of layers does that in a given habitat is a ground question requiring an instrument, not a figure question requiring more search. This is `unreachable`, not silence, and not an excuse to invent a number.
**Mode:** `general` for the gap.
**Refuter:** a figure line that derives a unique optimal layer count, or a ground measurement that settles it across habitats with the same per-slot residual. Until one of these fires, the honest answer to "how flat" is a declared gap.

### F10 - Strain recorded and resolved: hierarchy levels are not the six-term set

My first pass tried to map hierarchy levels directly onto Fig 13's six terms, reading each level as a structure. That strains Fig 13 :74: **"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."** It only fits with a forced reading, so by the strain test it governs another quantity.
**Re-census:** Fig 13 governs the minimum set for any composed system. Hierarchy depth is a parameter of the network structure, which Fig 13 :24 already holds. It is not a seventh term and not a new primitive. The strain dissolves; the earlier reading is kept here as evidence, not erased. See §9 and `11_audit.md`.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required by the derive-first discipline: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is:

> The binary is a union. Flattening improves decisions where it maintains a channel to a non-updating ground and a seat able to refuse, and it creates coordination and accountability failures where it removes the coordinating structure or the refusal seat. No figure line selects a layer count.

It is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. One ground-anchored measurement shows decision quality and accountability moving in the same direction across all slots in every habitat under a single condition, leaving no categorical residue. The union then collapses to one loop (against F1).
2. A per-slot reduction in `R` survives while channel fidelity falls and no outside refusal seat exists. Contact is then not the mechanism (against F4 and F5 and F6).
3. A figure line is found that derives a unique optimal layer count, closing the F9 gap.
4. A decision improvement is measured below a demonstrably missing vocabulary or objective link (against F8).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Do not answer the either/or. Unbundle D1 from D3 and report two verdicts, never an average (F1).
2. Ask for per-slot, ground-anchored measures before accepting any "better decisions" claim (F2).
3. Treat flattening as a bond change on a held network atom, not as a new primitive or a moral virtue (F3, F10).
4. Measure the channel separately from decision quality. Accountability is a seat with the power to refuse, not an org-chart label (F4, F5).
5. Diagnose which link stops the chain before crediting the structure (F8).
6. Publish the F9 gap rather than filling it with a number. Fig 4 :30: **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."**
7. Offer the refuter before the theory. Fig 10 :52: **"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."**

## 8. Verdict of this audit

The figures do not say "flat is better" and do not say "flat is worse." They say the effect is per slot (Fig 1 :19), depends on contact (Fig 11 :40), is carried by a channel that decays separately from the ground (Fig 10 :24), and requires a seat that can refuse (Fig 10 :42). Those are four different quantities in a union, not one score. Decision quality and coordination/accountability must be reported separately. The layer count is a declared gap. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate. Strains after re-census: none live; one retracted strain (F10) preserved in §4 and §7.
