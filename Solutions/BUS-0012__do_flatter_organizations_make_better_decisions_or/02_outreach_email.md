**Subject:** Check one output about flat organizations before you decide whether the theory is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "do flatter organizations make better decisions, or do they create coordination problems and unclear accountability" is a false binary. Those are two conditions with different anchors, not two answers to one question. Decision quality has to be read per decision slot against a ground that does not update when the organization reports a success. Coordination and accountability are a separate channel-and-seat problem: information has to reach the slot with fidelity, and somebody outside the decision has to be able to refuse the verdict. Removing layers changes both, in different directions, so the two verdicts must never be averaged.
>
> What the framework returns is a shape: any structure that works must keep a channel to a non-updating ground, keep a seat able to refuse, and carry influence across nodes. It does **not** return a number of layers, and I am not claiming one.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that flat organizations are always better, that shared mental models prove anything about decision quality, that a wider span is a coordination failure by itself, or that this derivation has measured any organization. It has not. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency, so it cannot settle the question it reports on.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. one ground-anchored measurement in which decision quality and accountability always move together under a single condition, across every slot and habitat;
> 2. a per-slot decision gain that survives while channel fidelity falls and no outside refusal seat exists;
> 3. a figure line that derives a unique optimal layer count;
> 4. a decision improvement measured below a demonstrably missing vocabulary or objective link.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step formula (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
