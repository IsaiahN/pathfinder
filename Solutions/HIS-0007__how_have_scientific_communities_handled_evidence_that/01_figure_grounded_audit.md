## 0. The residual, in effect terms

A community holds a reading E. Two questions can be asked of it:

1. Does E range against an anchor that does not move when the community changes its mind? If yes, E is reliable (settled against the ground).
2. Can practitioners in a given frame re-derive E with the means that frame holds? If no, E is difficult to reproduce in that frame.

The residual R is the gap between those two answers: a term that has paid against the ground but cannot be composed over inside the reproducing frame. It is a three-place effect, not a property of E alone: (reading, ground, frame). Arity: three, with two independent conditions. Symmetry: asymmetric; reliability and reproducibility are different axes and cannot be traded against one another (F1). Scale: per claim or per object slot, and separately across a chain of communities over time (F1 slot rule, F5 room chain).

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run: a procedure with five parts, (a) label the specific slot that fails, (b) keep the claim as candidate rather than false, (c) describe the missing condition in effect terms, (d) choose one of SEARCHED / INWARD / OUTWARD, (e) carry the method and record the lineage. That is the sought-for shape, written down so it can be refused (Fig 5 :62-66).

## 1. Domain census, before choosing any figure line

R by arity, symmetry and scale: three-place, asymmetric, per slot and per handoff. One line per domain with actors / currency / anchor. No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | partition or union |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 ground contact | phenomenon, instrument, observation | the residual R (prediction minus observation) | the ground, which does not update (Fig 2 :29-30) | partition boundary: what counts as a reading at all |
| D2 reproducing frame (closure) | lab, protocol, calibration, practitioner, tacit habit | reproducibility inside `closure(Γ)` | none inside the frame; the ground is reachable only from outside by import (Fig 6 :23) | part of a union; the frame is not an anchor |
| D3 verification arrangement | peers, reviewers, replication teams, editors, funders | agreement and residuals | the ground, never agreement (Fig 2 :35, :45) | part of a union |
| D4 historical transmission | records, protocols, apprentices, instruments handed down | methods versus recordings crossing the membrane | the ground persists; the channel decays (Fig 10 :24) | part of a union |

Union, not partition. "Reliable but hard to reproduce" is a case in two conditions (ground contact and within-frame reach). Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound." It needs its own loop, not two separate problems solved one after another. The four domains above are not four independent questions: D2, D3 and D4 are the three closures that overlap on the one residual, so they form a union. D1 is the anchor domain against which all three are ranged.

Order by dependency (Fig 3). 1 perception: does the reading register at all? 2 vocabulary: does the reproducing frame have terms for the instrument and the procedure? 3 objective: what would count as progress, per slot? 4 planning: which different frame to seek? 5 learn and carry: transmit method, not recording. The likely break is between 2 and 3, which Fig 3 :26 names as the link that "has neither [obvious failures and tests], so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments." Effort spent at links 4 and 5 cannot rescue a break at 3 (Fig 3 :19).

Strain test applied before findings. A line that only fits with a strain governs another quantity and sends us back to census. Two candidate strains were found and re-censused: (i) Fig 8 :32 idempotence seemed to forbid import, but Fig 6 :23 governs what moves the wall while Fig 8 governs what composition inside a wall can do, so they are different quantities and agree; (ii) Fig 7 :13 "agreement compounds, evidence does not" seemed to forbid replication entirely, but Fig 7 :36 governs which room is next, not whether a differing room can be selected, so no strain remains.

## 2. Findings, with verbatim figure lines and refuters

### F1: Reliability and reproducibility are two quantities, not one (triangulated)

Fig 5 :69: "settling is necessary for a term to be composed over, and it is not sufficient."
Fig 5 :70: "A term is settled when the ground pays for it. A term is usable when something can compose over it."
Fig 5 :72: "a library that grows is not a reach that grows."

Second, low-overlap source: Fig 12 :107: "Settling compresses the description and not what it cost to establish."

A reliable but difficult to reproduce result is exactly a settled term that is not usable in this frame. The community's error is to read "settled" as "composable here". They are two conditions, and Fig 5 names one of them. Fig 12 :107 adds the other half: the cost of establishing the arrangement is not carried in the compressed description, so a new lineage cannot simply read the result off the record.

World corroboration is deliberately weak here: the Open Science Collaboration (2015) found replication success was predicted by strength of original evidence, which is a statistical association in one pool, not the figure's structural distinction. It is corroboration, not a second witness.

Refuter: show a settled term that is automatically composable in every frame without method, instrument or habitat transfer, or show that the two conditions of Fig 5 :70 are one condition under another name.

### F2: Repeating the same frame cannot add what the frame lacks (triangulated)

Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."
Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."

These are two figures with low overlap: Fig 8 proves why composition cannot leave a closed set; Fig 6 says what alone can move the wall. Together they explain why asking the same community to repeat the same protocol more times does not repair reliability gap. If the missing thing is an atom (an instrument, a calibration, a skill), more of the same closure cannot manufacture it. Fig 6 :34: "Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."

Refuter: exhibit a case in which repeated same-closure replication added a new atom to the closure, or show a composition that left `closure(Γ)` without contact with a differing frame.

### F3: What must be carried is the method, not the recorded result (triangulated)

Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."
Fig 13 :130: "A term carries the atoms it was built from and the bonds that joined them. That record is not documentation of the term. It is the term."

Fig 4 governs scale crossing; Fig 13 governs what a term is. Low overlap. A published result is a recording; what makes it reproducible is transporting the method (instrument, circuit, calibration, protocol, conditions) as a head start. Fig 12 :108-109 adds: where the arrangement is not recorded, "the result carries forward and the deriving does not, so every lineage pays again."

The historical record agrees, weakly and separately: the 1989 cold fusion episode released almost no experimental detail, and the Nature Materials editorial (2019) says "This frustrated efforts to replicate the work"; the 2026 arXiv history says most attempts "could not be replications in this sense" because experimenters had no detailed protocols. Two different pools, one direction.

Refuter: show a case in which a bare result, with no transported method, instrument or skill, was reliably reproduced by a frame that had not held the method before. That would falsify the directional rule of Fig 4 :22.

### F4: A replication is a substituted habitat (single-source, flagged by the figure itself)

Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."
Fig 11 :67: "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."
Fig 11 :68: "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"
Fig 11 :70: "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."

This is the mechanism behind "difficult to reproduce". A replication lab is a substituted habitat. It inherits actors it did not choose, and the conditions it failed to carry stay invisible until the goal fails. This figure line is single-source. Fig 11 :71 itself warns: "The isolation law and the capability claim fit cases already known and have not been tested forward, so they can still come back wrong." I do not upgrade it by agreement with the cold fusion cases; those cases share the same historical pool as each other and are not independent witnesses.

Refuter: exhibit a successful replication that carried none of the original habitat conditions (instrument batch, calibration, ambient condition, practitioner skill), which would break the isolation law at the ground.

### F5: Agreement is not a verdict; a chain of agreeing copies is a mirror chain (triangulated)

Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
Fig 7 :36: "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."
Fig 7 :13: "agreement compounds, evidence does not"
Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."

Fig 2 governs the anchor and independence; Fig 7 governs selection between rooms. Low overlap. A community that answers non-reproduction by asking more people in the same closure to agree is doing mirror-chain work. The legitimate move is to pick a next room that differs from the last and is chosen against the residual the last one surfaced (Fig 7 :24-26), then range it separately against the ground (Fig 2 :46).

World sources conflict here, and the conflict is recorded rather than pooled. Collins ( Physics Today, 2021) says disputed cases were settled by "broad agreement" about which experiments were competent; Franklin ( Studies in History and Philosophy of Science, 1994) says the critics "exchanged both data and analysis programs" and reasoned from them. Under the figures both descriptions are partial: Franklin's exchange is an import of a differing closure (Fig 6 :23), not agreement; Collins's agreement records how the chain chose a room (Fig 7 :36), not what settled the ground (Fig 2 :45). No verdict flips.

Refuter: exhibit a case in which agreement among frames that draw on one evidence pool changed the ground's answer, with the anchor shown to have moved because of that agreement.

### F6: The ground does not decay; the channel does (triangulated)

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."
Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does"
Fig 10 :25: "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"

Fig 2 governs what an anchor is; Fig 10 governs how contact is lost. Low overlap. Evidence once reliable can become hard to reproduce because the transmission channel drifted (records, training, instrument calibration), not because the ground changed. The community's job is to keep the anchor reachable (Fig 10 :29), and the output of a saturated channel is not a verdict but "you have lost touch" (Fig 10 :28).

Refuter: exhibit a case in which the ground itself changed so that no instrument could recover the original reading while the channel was demonstrably intact. This would strain the anchor claim and force a re-census of D1.

### F7: Failure to reproduce is not refutation (triangulated)

Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
Fig 8 :41: "different facts, and they issue different instructions. Underivable: go outside."
Fig 8 :42: "already in the closure and was not found, so the remedy is more search, not another atom."
Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"

Formula step 7 keeps SEARCHED, INWARD and OUTWARD distinct: SEARCHED asks whether the missing thing is in the closure and merely unfound; INWARD sharpens an instrument already returning something; OUTWARD imports from a frame whose closure differs. A community that calls non-reproduction "false" has collapsed unreachable-at-budget into falsehood. A community that imports a new atom against a budget limit has made the opposite error, which Fig 8 :43 prices as worse than starting from primitives.

Refuter: exhibit a case in which a wrong imported atom demonstrably cost more than the extra same-frame search would have, with the accounting recorded. That would narrow the verdict, not reverse the distinction.

### F8: Keep the verdict per slot; a field rate is a filter, not a witness (triangulated)

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

A 36 percent reproducibility rate for a field is a budget filter. It does not decide any particular claim. The Open Science Collaboration (2015) itself found that subjective replication ratings and significance criteria gave different answers on the same studies, which is a warning against letting one aggregate hand down a verdict.

Refuter: show that a field-level rate settled a particular claim without any separate range against the ground for that claim.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter (written before the WATSON world search)

"The falsifier written before the run is a design instrument." The shape claim in §4 is refused if any of the following holds:

1. A documented community case in which a settled reading was automatically composable and reproducible in every frame, without method, instrument or habitat transfer. Breaks F1, F2, F3 and F4.
2. A case in which repeated same-closure replication added a new atom to the closure. Breaks Fig 8 :32.
3. A case in which agreement among frames drawing on one evidence pool moved the anchor. Breaks Fig 2 :35 and :40.
4. A case in which the ground changed while the channel was intact, leaving no nameable anchor. Breaks F6 and forces a new census of D1.

These were committed before the world sources were read. The world search produced no case that fired any of them.

## 4. Recommendation: what a community should do with such evidence

Mode: `specified`, a procedure rather than a measurement.

1. Keep it as candidate, not false. The ground paid for it (Fig 5 :70); reproduction is a separate condition (F1).
2. Name the slot. State which claim, instrument or procedure failed, per slot, not a field average (Fig 1 :19, Fig 9 :60).
3. Describe the missing condition in effect terms, before choosing a frame. What would you expect to see if the missing atom were present? (Fig 9 :27-28, Fig 6 :30).
4. Run the three questions in order. SEARCHED first, cheapest; INWARD second, sharpen an instrument already returning something; OUTWARD third, import from a frame whose closure differs, with a source gate and a shadow test (Formula step 7; Fig 6 :32-34). Do not read INWARD against OUTWARD.
5. Carry methods, not recordings. Publish the circuit, calibration, protocol, batch and conditions as a head start (Fig 4 :22, Fig 13 :130).
6. Pick a differing next room. The next replication should use a different closure or instrument and be chosen against the residual the last one surfaced (Fig 7 :36). Keep the frames independent (Fig 2 :31).
7. Record the lineage. Provenance shows how a ruling was reached, not that it was right; the ground decides (Fig 10 :62-65). A reader must be able to locate the error, not merely feel it.

### Verdict of this audit

The shape survives: a reliable but difficult to reproduce result is a settled term that a frame cannot compose over; the remedy is a differing frame, a carried method and a recorded lineage, never a vote among mirrors. Every claim stays `specified` or `general`. The one `single-source` finding (F4) is labelled as such and is explicitly not upgraded by correlated world sources. After re-census, no hidden strain remains; the open item is the figure's own admission at Fig 11 :71 that the isolation law has not been tested forward.
