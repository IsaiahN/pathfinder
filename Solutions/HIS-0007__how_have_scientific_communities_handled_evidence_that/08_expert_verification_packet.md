# Expert verification packet: the Tether on reliable but hard-to-reproduce evidence

**What this is:** a claim in a form a historian or sociologist of scientific practice can check. Mode, derivation and refuters come before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the procedure, `general` on the metalaw statements, not `grounded`.** No historical case is claimed to have been measured. This is a shape and a route.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Reliable but difficult to reproduce" is a union compound of two conditions: a reading that has paid against an anchor, and a frame that cannot re-derive it. Settling is necessary for a term to be composed over and is not sufficient, so such a reading is a candidate, not a false claim. Repeating the same protocol in the same closure cannot add the missing atom; the wall moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs. What must travel is the method, not the recorded result, and a replication is a substituted habitat whose omissions stay invisible until the goal fails. Agreement among readers of one evidence pool is not a verdict, and a field-level replication rate is a filter for the budget, not a witness for a particular case.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 5, 8 and 13

- **Statement:** a settled reading is not automatically usable, and a search inside one closure cannot add what that closure lacks. Non-reproduction is therefore not refutation.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :69 "*settling is necessary for a term to be composed over, and it is not sufficient.*" Fig 5 :70 "*A term is settled when the ground pays for it. A term is usable when something can compose over it.*" Fig 8 :32 "*Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom.*" Fig 8 :39 "*So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence.*" Fig 13 :134 "*Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence*".
- **Refuter:** show a settled reading that was automatically reproducible in every frame with no transfer of method or habitat, or show a composition that left a closed set without contact with a differing frame.

### C2: history-facing; checkable against a case the expert knows

- **Statement:** the travel item is the method (instrument, calibration, protocol, material conditions, skill), not the published result; a replication is a substituted habitat.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :22 "*Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down.*" Fig 4 :25 "*A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion.*" Fig 11 :66 "*Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another.*" Fig 11 :68 "*what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails*".
- **What to check:** in the case you know, what was omitted when the result was sent? Was it a protocol, a batch, a calibration, an ambient condition, or a practitioner's skill? Did the omission stay invisible until the replication failed?
- **Refuter:** exhibit a successful replication that carried none of the original habitat conditions. That breaks Fig 11 :66. Note that Fig 11 :71 itself flags this law as not tested forward, so I hold it as `single-source`, not as settled.

### C3: verification-facing; checkable against the social arrangement

- **Statement:** agreement among frames drawing on one evidence pool cannot corroborate a claim about the world; the next reproducing frame must differ from the last and be chosen against the residual it surfaced. A field rate cannot decide a case.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :35 "*Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you.*" Fig 2 :40 "*agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool*" Fig 7 :36 "*A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced.*" Fig 9 :60 "*Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict.*"
- **Refuter:** exhibit a case where agreement among people reading the same evidence moved the anchor, with the anchor shown to have changed because of that agreement.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that a hard-to-reproduce reading is true. "Candidate" is a status, not a verdict.
- Not that consensus, institutional reputation or a count of replications settles a claim. Fig 2 :45: "*Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it.*"
- Not that a field-wide reproducibility rate decides a particular case.
- Not that the transmission channel is immutable. Fig 10 :24: "*the ground does not decay, the channel does.*"
- Not `grounded`. No measurement of a historical case is asserted here.
- Not that the Collins and Franklin accounts have been reconciled. Their conflict is recorded; the figures decide without pooling them.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument or a genuinely differing frame, not another argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "*cannot comment on the source*" and "*An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy.*" Here:

1. Which slot failed (instrument, calibration, material batch, ambient condition, skill, or a genuine null)?
2. Is there an instrument already returning something that can be sharpened in place (INWARD), or must the description travel to a frame that already holds the missing condition (OUTWARD)?
3. Does a second frame with a different closure, ranged separately against the same anchor, return a reading?
4. Was the method carried as a head start and the lineage recorded, so an error can be located rather than merely felt?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 5 :69-70, Fig 8 :32 and :39, Fig 4 :22 and :25, Fig 11 :66 and :68, Fig 2 :35 and :40, Fig 9 :60. Ten short passages.
2. Pick one historical case you know well. Write down which slot failed to reproduce and what was omitted when the result was transmitted.
3. Name the instrument or differing frame that could discriminate the source of the reading, or say why none is available.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. If you disagree, state which of C1, C2 or C3 fails and on what ground.
