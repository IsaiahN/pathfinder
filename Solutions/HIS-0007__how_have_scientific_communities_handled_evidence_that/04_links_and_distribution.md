## Wider literature relevant to the residual

Opened through the search integration (direct PDF fetch was blocked on some, which is recorded honestly in the ledger):

- Harry Collins, "Gravitational waves and scientific realism" (2016, ORCA, Cardiff): "*replicating the experiments could not prove whether some claimed phenomenon was real or not*" because one could not tell whether the replication was performed adequately.
- Allan Franklin, "How to Avoid the Experimenters' Regress" ( Studies in History and Philosophy of Science, 1994): reports that Weber's critics "*exchanged both data and analysis programs*" and re-analysed their own data with Weber's algorithm. This **conflicts** with Collins's social-agreement account; we do not pool them.
- Gilbert, King, Pettigrew and Wilson, comment on the Open Science Collaboration ( Science, 2016): "*the data are consistent with the opposite conclusion*". Same evidence pool, opposite field-level reading, which is exactly why a rate is a filter and not a witness.
- Bloor's review of Galison ( Social Studies of Science, 1991): the question is how an experiment comes to be seen as having "*established a fact, rather than being a mere artefact*".
- Isaiah N, "Decoding Tacit Knowledge: Why Context, Subtext, & Pretext Matter" (2026): relays Polanyi's "*we can know more than we can tell*" and Collins's relational, somatic and collective kinds; directly relevant to F3 and F4.
- Isaiah N, "The Library of Babel: Locating The Book of Books" (2026): "*you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question.*"
- Isaiah N, "The Hard Problem is About Source" (2026): "*If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information*" and "*An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy*."

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22** "*Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down.*" Send the shape claim and its refuter as a method to check, not the whole architecture as a recording.
- **Fig 10 :42** "*A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it.*" A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** "*describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit.*" Ask what they would expect to see in a hard-to-reproduce case before giving them the article.
- **Fig 2 :40** "*agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool*" and Fig 7 :13 "*agreement compounds, evidence does not*": a list selected for receptiveness produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 9 :51** "*evidence bearing on the residual is admissible; evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel*" so the first message contains no biography of the framework and no appeal to its author.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the residual and pre-registered refuter; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) record that verdict; (4) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1:** send the residual, the shape claim and the refuter. Ask what would refute it. Record the answer before any theory is shown (Fig 10 :52, Fig 2 :45).
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the three local links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

**Hard precondition:** the stages only work if the stage-1 claim survives. `08_expert_verification_packet.md` is the artifact to send; check it against a case you know before using `02_outreach_email.md`.
