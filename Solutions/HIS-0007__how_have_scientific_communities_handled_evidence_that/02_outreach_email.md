# Outreach draft: check the shape before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** the three sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` must survive your own check before you send this. The first contact carries only the residual and the pre-registered refuter.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about unreliable-to-reproduce evidence, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I work on the history and practice of scientific evidence. I have one output I would like you to check against cases you know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output fails, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** "reliable but difficult to reproduce" is not one problem. It is a case in two conditions: a reading that has paid against an anchor, and a frame that cannot re-derive it. Under the figures, settling is necessary for a term to be composed over and is not sufficient. So such a reading is a **candidate**, not a false claim. Repeating the same protocol in the same frame cannot add the missing instrument or skill; the wall moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs. A replication is a substituted habitat, so what was not carried across stays invisible until the goal fails.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that every such reading is true, that a field-level replication rate decides any particular case, that consensus settles anything, or that this is a historical finding. It is a shape a case must have, not a measurement of one.
>
> **What would refute it, written before I looked at cases:**
> 1. a settled reading that was automatically reproducible in every frame, with no method, instrument or habitat transferred;
> 2. a case where repeated same-closure replication added a new atom to the closure;
> 3. a case where agreement among people reading the same evidence moved the ground;
> 4. a case where the ground itself changed while the channel was intact, leaving no nameable anchor.
>
> A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed. If you do reply, I will record your verdict before showing you any source.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the public links. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local project file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local project file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop (local project file): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a case you know well. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
