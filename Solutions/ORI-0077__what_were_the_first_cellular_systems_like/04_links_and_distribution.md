## Three findings in the wider literature that bear directly on this question

1. **Population, not individual.** Kandler's pre-cells, Woese's progenotes and Glansdorff's
   heterogeneous community are three names for the same shape reached from different starting
   points: no discrete first cell, frequent exchange, cellularization as a process. They appear
   across three Wikipedia articles that cross-reference one another, so they are **one evidence
   pool with three spellings**, not three independent witnesses (Fig 2 :40).
2. **A model that couples the conditions.** West et al. (2017) is a distinct frame, a computational
   biophysics model from UCL rather than a phylogenomics review. It couples membrane, proton
   gradient and heredity in one feedback loop, which is why the three conditions are a union in
   `01` §1 and why their verdicts are not averaged. Its own caveat is recorded: the parameter
   values *"should not be interpreted literally, but thought of as indicative of conditions that
   promote protocell functioning."*
3. **A live conflict about setting.** Weiss et al. (2016) infer a thermophilic vent autotroph;
   Berkemer and McGlynn infer a mesophilic, heterotrophic hot-spring setting; Martin et al. (2026)
   propose LUCA as the successor to separate archaeal and bacterial ancestors. This conflict is
   the reason the name is withheld. A conflict sends the derivation back to the census; it never
   flips the figure verdict (Fig 9 :32, *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*).

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22** - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending a reconstructed
  anatomy before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the
  shape and its refuter; offer the links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who
  has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** - *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."*
  Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the article.
- **Fig 2 :40** - *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the
  agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* - the Wikipedia articles on LUCA, pre-cells and the
  Darwinian threshold share references. Do not count their agreement as corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply.** (1) The shape claim and its four refuters. (2) Ask what they
would count as a failure. (3) Record their verdict. (4) Only then the three links, with the note
that the figures, not the summary, are the authority. (5) Never pool later agreement as evidence.

## The two-stage offer

- **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52 (*"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"*)
  and Fig 2 :45 (*"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*). The reader
  checks the output without holding the framework's interior.
- **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Once a reader has absorbed the theory, their agreement
  stops being evidence. Their verdict on stage 1 must be recorded before any theory is shown.
- **Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted.** Fig 4 :22. The formula is a generator, so giving

**Hard precondition.** Stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. As drafted it is a
shape, not a reconstruction; check `08_expert_verification_packet.md` before sending and answer the
four refuters honestly.
