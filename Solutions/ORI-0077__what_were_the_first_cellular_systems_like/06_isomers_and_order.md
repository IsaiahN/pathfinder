# Structure, order and analogues: how a cell composes, and how a regime crosses

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see
`05_what_shape_means.md` §5).

## RESIDUAL

The shape claim in `01` says a cell is a cross-kind composition and that the first cellular systems
arose in an unmaintained regime before a threshold made vertical heredity effective. Two supporting
questions are owed: (1) can a minimal cell be written as a first-order molecule over the six-term
set, and (2) is the move from pre-cell regime to LUCA an **isomer**, a **functional group**, or a
**crossing of role** under Fig 12's definitions?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: Space, Time, Energy (states) plus Gradient, Network, Spectrum (structures). Bonds held:
the seven, including `→` sequence, `⇒` production, `−` subtraction and `⋛` comparison; negation is
the eighth operator and is not a bond. No new primitive is available without import. The empirical
frame contributes fatty acid membranes, FeS nanocrystals, proton gradients, ribosomes and shared
genes; these are atoms of domain 2, not new members of the six.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Can a minimal cell be written at first order?

- Fig 13 :74: **"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."**
- Fig 13 :75: **"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."**
- A first-order molecule is `state → structure → state`, one crossing. Three candidates are legal
  and each is a different first-order term:
  - `Space → Network → Energy`: a boundary that relates parts and retains a resource.
  - `Energy → Gradient → Space`: a directed flow spent across a boundary. Fig 13 :76: *"Energy and
    a gradient compose a flow."* Also: *"A gradient over space and one over time are different
    terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."*
  - `Space → Spectrum → Energy`: an ordering over states that can be inherited.
- These are not three cells. They are three legal first-order molecules. A cell is their **union at
  second order**, because each first-order molecule must be settled before another composes over
  it (Fig 12 :104).

**Physics check, not a figure line.** West et al. (2017) model exactly this coupling: a fatty acid
membrane (space + network), a geologically sustained proton gradient (energy + gradient), and
rudimentary heredity through FeS chelation and partitioning (spectrum + network). Their model is a
laboratory computation, not a fossil. Mark `specified`, unmeasured at the ground. Triangulated for
legality under Fig 13; single-source for the physics mapping.

**Falsifier:** show that one of the three first-order molecules cannot be written over the held six,
or that a cell needs an atom outside them. The isomer and functional-group claims below then fail.

### Q2 - Are pre-cells and LUCA isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- If a pre-cell and LUCA are written over the **same full atom set**, differing only in which bond
  holds and which state each structure mediates, they are isomers: same atoms, different
  arrangement, different substance.
- As the empirical frame actually writes them, the pre-cell has no stable chromosome and no proper
  cytoplasmic membrane (Kandler, reported in Wikipedia *Pre-cell*), while LUCA has a lipid bilayer,
  a genetic code and ribosomes (Wikipedia *LUCA*). They do **not** share the full atom set as held;
  the weak claim is that they share a **functional group**, the cross-kind composition itself.

**Falsifier:** if a pre-cell can be shown to lack an atom LUCA requires (no spectrum, no energy
flow), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. If it can be shown to
possess a seventh atom, the substrate fails and both readings are withdrawn.

### Q3 - What order is the crossing?

- Fig 13 :4-5: **"the two kinds are in lockstep, a crossing is a change of role rather than a step upward, and no member of the pair is senior to the other."**
- Fig 11 :4-9 sets three panels: unmaintained, maintained, isolated. The pre-LUCA regime is panel 1:
  *"nothing selects; what survives is what the ground kept."* LUCA is not panel 2 in the strong
  sense, because no seat maintains it; the threshold is the point where vertical heredity makes
  selection effective on individual entities (Wikipedia *Darwinian threshold*). So the crossing is
  a **change in what carries the heredity**, not a maintainer arriving.
- Fig 10 :70: **"The tree of life is what a non-invertible transform looks like from outside."** The
  web-to-tree change is a lossy transform, as a member of one kind becoming an operand for the other
  is lossy (Fig 4 :27: *"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."*).

**Falsifier:** show a seat maintaining the pre-LUCA habitat, or show the web-to-tree transform is
invertible. Either breaks the crossing reading.

### Q4 - Why a route cannot be replaced by an event

- Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**
- Fig 12 :12: *"three different changes, one written formula"* (Melt, Freeze, Boil under the same
  notation). A single event "the first cell appeared" has the same defect: the ingredients are
  recorded and the arrangement is not.
- Kandler's cellularization is an empirical route, not a figure derivation: membrane, genome
  stability, walls, cytoskeleton (Wikipedia *Pre-cell*). The figure supplies the requirement that
  a route be writable; the empirical record supplies the candidate steps.

**Falsifier:** a ground reading showing the transition was one unbonded event with no intermediate
arrangements.

### Q5 - Does the eight-operator set help?

- Operators table :57: negation, *"is the point that it is false, rather than absent? Yes."* The
  discrete "first cell" is not absent from the record; it is **refused**. A vocabulary with `−`
  but without `¬` can say what is missing from an arrangement and cannot say that an arrangement is
  wrong, so it can only accumulate (Operators table :74). This solution uses negation to refuse the
  ordinal "first cell" rather than to subtract it from a list.
- Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."** The positive feedbacks in West et al. (2017)
  are cycles in this sense; a linear origin story cannot close them.

**Falsifier:** show the refusal is actually a subtraction (a first cell that is merely missing from
the fossil record), in which case `−` is the right operator and this reading is wrong.

## CONCLUSION

A cell is not a first-order molecule but the union of at least three legal first-order molecules,
each settled before the next composes. Pre-cells and LUCA are isomers only under the stronger
"same full atom set" writing; as the empirical frame writes them they are a functional group. The
regime change is a crossing of role (web to tree, horizontal to vertical) and a lossy transform, not
a step upward and not the arrival of a maintainer. A route, not an event, is what the bond notation
can carry. Negation is used to refuse the discrete first cell, not to subtract it from the record.

This rescues what is defensible without naming a setting: no seventh term, no import that moves the
wall, no first individual. `PRE-LUCA(???)` stays marked.
