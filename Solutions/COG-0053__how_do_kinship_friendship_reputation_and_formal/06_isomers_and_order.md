# Bond typing, union order, and analogues for the four mechanisms

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §5).

## RESIDUAL

The interaction among kinship, friendship, reputation and formal rules has to be said in structural terms. Three questions are owed: (1) what *structures* are the four under the six-term substrate, (2) what *bond types* join them, and (3) are different arrangements of the same four **isomers**, and in what order does the union run?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: the six of Fig 13 (space, time, energy as states; spectrum, gradient, network as structures) plus the seven bonds and negation. The four mechanisms are **settled molecules** built from those atoms, not primitives: a kinship network is a network over persons in a shared space and time; a reputation ordering is a spectrum over actions; a formal sanction is a directed gradient that can be spent. No new primitive is available without import.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — What structures are the four?

- Fig 13 :74 "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."
- Fig 13 :24 "that entities relate, and influence travels" -> kinship and friendship are a **network** structure.
- Fig 13 :20 "ordering, so more and less can be said" -> reputation is a **spectrum** structure.
- Fig 13 :22 "a difference that can be spent" -> a formal rule with a sanction is a **gradient** structure.
- The states they cross through are the shared situation, resource and outcome (space, time, energy at this level). A relation of kin to kin is a molecule `person - network - person`; a reputation judgment is `action - spectrum - actor`; a sanction is `violation - gradient - outcome`.
- **Falsifier:** if any of the four can be shown to be a bare state with no structure in the middle, the six-term typing fails for that mechanism and the whole mapping is withdrawn. Mode `specified`, single-source for the mapping (Fig 13), triangulated with Nowak's separate thresholds as corroboration only.

### Q2 — What bond types join them?

- Operators table: "+, ∥, ≡" symmetric; "→, ⇒, −, ⋛" ordered.
- Kinship and friendship: symmetric. "swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No." A and B are kin in the same sense as B and A.
- Reputation: ordered under comparison ⋛. "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes." More trustworthy versus less.
- Formal rules: ordered under sequence → and production ⇒. A rule is laid down before it is applied, and a sanction produces an outcome that did not exist before.
- Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Writing all four as one conjunction "+" is collapse 6: it records the ingredients and loses the arrangement.
- **Falsifier:** if swapping operands of reputation or of enforcement leaves every observable outcome unchanged, they are symmetric bonds and this typing is wrong.

### Q3 — Are they isomers?

- Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Over the shared atom set {kinship, friendship, reputation, formal rules}, two societies can hold the same four and arrange them differently:
  - **Reputation first:** gossip and standing emerge locally; formal rules later codify the standing already assigned. Sequence: reputation → rules.
  - **Rules first:** an external authority installs enforcement; reputation and local trust are built afterwards around compliance. Sequence: rules → reputation.
- Same atoms, different bond order, different substance. These are Fig 12 isomers, and the difference is decisive in behaviour (one is seen as legitimate, the other as imposed; Cox et al. 2010 find externally imposed rules that do not match local custom trigger low monitoring and high extraction).
- The weaker reading is a **functional group** (Fig 12 :124): "The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." All four arrangements share the group "mechanism that closes a cooperation slot"; the bond order says which member.
- **Falsifier:** if both orders produce the same behavioural outcomes in comparable settings, the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim survives. Written before the world check, not after.

### Q4 — What order is the union?

- Fig 9 :57-58 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."
- Dependency order: perception (distinguish cooperators) -> vocabulary (kin / friend / reputation / rule) -> objective (what counts as cooperation) -> planning -> carry. Reputation depends on a communication channel; formal enforcement depends on monitoring that reputation cannot supply under anonymity. So D1 (face-to-face) -> D2 (reputational) -> D3 (formal), with D4 composed over all three.
- Each crossing runs through a state: Fig 13 :75 "Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state." A network of kin and an ordering of reputation meet in a shared situation; a reputation ordering and a sanction gradient meet in a shared outcome.
- There is also a **cycle**, not just a chain. Fig 12 :121 "The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Cooperation produces reputation, reputation produces more cooperation; a sanction produces compliance, compliance reproduces the sanction's legitimacy. Whether these loops close with a positive or negative sign is measured, not assumed.
- **Falsifier:** a case where the dependency order reverses (formal enforcement installed before any local reputation or kin network, and remaining stable with no local reconstruction) would break the dependency claim, leaving only the isomer claim.

### Q5 — Is any part a catalyst?

- Fig 12 :119 "A part that survives its own use." Gossip is the standing example: using reputation to judge someone also regenerates the gossip network that supplies reputation. A norm enforced by the community is spent and regenerated in the same act.
- A spent-and-not-regenerated part behaves differently across uses, and nothing in a flat list records which a term is. This is why "add a mechanism" is not a linear operation: adding a catalyst changes the loop, adding a spent part does not.
- **Falsifier:** if a mechanism is observed to be consumed by its own use with no regeneration, it is not a catalyst and must be modelled as a resource with a budget.

## CONCLUSION

The four mechanisms are settled molecules typed as network, spectrum and gradient structures crossed through shared states. Their bonds are not uniform: kin and friendship are symmetric, reputation is a comparison, formal rules are sequence and production. Over a shared atom set they form genuine **isomers**, with reputation-first and rules-first as two distinguishable substances whose behavioural difference is a checkable prediction. The union runs in dependency order and contains closing cycles, with at least one catalyst. This is a `specified` structural claim, not a ground reading.
