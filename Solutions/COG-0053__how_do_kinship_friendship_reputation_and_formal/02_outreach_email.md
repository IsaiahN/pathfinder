**Subject:** One checkable claim about how kinship, friendship, reputation and rules combine

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question I started with:** how do kinship, friendship, reputation and formal rules interact to sustain cooperation? The word "interact" is the whole problem. A list of four causes does not answer it.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the four are not four independent causes that add up. They are four *slots* in one overlapping compound, joined by different kinds of relation. Kinship and friendship are mutual bonds between paired people. Reputation is an ordering (who is more or less trustworthy). Formal rules are a directed, spendable difference (a sanction that can be applied). What scales up from a face-to-face group to an institution is a **method**, monitoring and graduated sanctioning, not a recording of who cooperated once. Because each slot closes under its own condition, raising one does not substitute for the others: more kin warmth does not fix anonymity; more reputation does not fix a stranger whose reputation is unknown.
>
> This is a claim about **shape**, not a measurement. It does not say which mechanism dominates in your society or dataset. That is something the ground has to settle.
>
> **What would refute it, written down before I wrote to you:**
> 1. one of the four, with no bond to the others, shown by measurement to sustain cooperation at every scale including large anonymous institutions;
> 2. the four shown to draw on a single evidence pool, so they are one mechanism with four names;
> 3. formal rules shown to transfer no method across scale, only a record of past cooperation;
> 4. the effect of a mechanism shown to be the same in every context.
>
> If any holds, I retract the shape. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that this is a theory of everything; that it settles any real society; that the four are the only relevant mechanisms; that "more rules" is generally good. The framework's rule is that a mechanism's effect is relative to the residual it is being used against, so the same sanction can sustain cooperation in one setting and crush it in another. I am also not asking you to trust me or the framework. The ask is only that you try to break the output.
