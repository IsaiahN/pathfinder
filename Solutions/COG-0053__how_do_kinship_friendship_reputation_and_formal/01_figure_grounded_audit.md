## 0. The residual in effect terms

Observed: cooperation persists in groups where a one-shot, well-mixed self-interest frame predicts defection. The residual R is the gap between that prediction and the observation. It is not one number.

- **Arity.** Four candidate terms are in play: kinship, friendship, reputation, formal rules. R is therefore a four-slot residual.
- **Symmetry.** The slots are not symmetric. Kinship and friendship are mutual relations between paired actors. Reputation is an ordering over actors (more or less trustworthy). Formal rules are directed, enforceable differences. Treating all four as interchangeable instances of "social capital" destroys the information that distinguishes them.
- **Scale.** Kinship and friendship operate at small, face-to-face scale. Reputation operates at community scale through gossip. Formal rules operate at institutional scale through monitors and sanctions. R is a cross-scale residual.
- **Per-slot, never aggregated.** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." An aggregate "amount of social capital" is how the live signal saying *which* slot is broken disappears. So the question is not *how much* of each mechanism, but *which bond* holds in which slot at which scale.

**Bound sought-for term.** From the outset (see README): the interaction is a set of priced, typed bonds inside one union compound. This is written down before the run, as Formula step "Direction" requires, so it is refusable rather than an invisible preference.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

R by arity / symmetry / scale is stated above. One line per domain, with actors / currency / anchor. A domain without a nameable anchor is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor (nameable?) |

| D2 Reputational / informational | third parties, gossip network | reputation, an ordering over actors | yes: knowledge of past behaviour, who is helped |

| D4 Composition (the question itself) | the four mechanisms as terms | the bonds between them | yes: measured cooperation across scales (public-goods and field data) |

**Partition or union?** Union. The mechanisms overlap. Kin are often friends; reputation overlays both; formal rules codify an existing reputation norm. Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57-58 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound." D4 is therefore a compound with its own loop and its own ground, not the sum of D1, D2 and D3.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Perception (can actors tell cooperators from defectors?) enables vocabulary (terms for kin / friend / reputation / rule), which enables the objective (what counts as cooperation), which enables planning and carrying. Reputation depends on a communication channel; formal rules depend on monitoring that reputation cannot supply once anonymity rises. So the dependency order is D1 to D2 to D3, with D4 composed over all three. Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."

**Strain test applied up front.** A line that only fits with a strain governs another quantity. All figure lines below are checked against these four domains before being used. One strain is found and named in section 4; it is not smoothed over.

## 3. Findings

### F1 — The four mechanisms are ingredients; the interaction is the bond (decisive, `general`)

Fig 12 :5 "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."

Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance."

Fig 12 :111 "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."

The question asks *how they interact*. Under Fig 12 that is not a question about the four items; it is a question about the typed relations between them. A list ("kinship helps, friendship helps, reputation helps, rules help") is collapse 6, the flat formula. It records the ingredients and not the arrangement, so nothing downstream can tell the arrangements apart and nothing signals that anything was lost.

- **Refuter:** show a composition of the four that carries no more information than the four-item list, i.e. swapping the relations among them changes nothing observable.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 12 + Formula "Composition" section, low overlap within the framework). Corroborated in a different field by Cox et al. (2010), who find the Ostrom design principles work as a *configuration* rather than singly.

### F2 — It is a union compound, not four separate causes (`general`)

Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."

Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."

Fig 9 :57-58 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."

Kinship overlaps friendship, reputation overlays both, and formal rules can codify reputation. A lever that strengthens only one mechanism clears the region where only that condition holds and leaves the intersections standing because the second condition still binds. So there is no single lever "more trust" or "more enforcement". There is a compound, and it has its own loop.

- **Refuter:** exhibit a case where the four conditions are independent (no overlap in any observed group), which would make the partition reading correct and D4 unnecessary.
- **Status:** single-source for the figure definition (Fig 9); triangulated with Nowak (2006), whose five mechanisms each carry a *different* rule (r, w, q, k, n/m), and with Cox et al. (2010), whose review finds the principles "well supported empirically" as a configuration.

### F3 — Bond types differ; symmetric for kin and friendship, ordered for reputation and rules (`specified`)

The Operators table splits the eight operators: "+, ∥, ≡" are symmetric; "→, ⇒, −, ⋛" are ordered. Kinship and friendship are mutual (swap the operands, little changes): the symmetric case. Reputation is an ordering: Fig 13 :20 "ordering, so more and less can be said." Formal rules are a directed difference: Fig 13 :22 "a difference that can be spent." Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

So "interact" needs a bond type. Calling all four "conjunction" is the flat-formula error again, and it overcharges or undercharges by log₂(k!) under a symmetric bond (Fig 12 :92).

- **Refuter:** show that swapping the operands of reputation or of formal enforcement leaves the outcome unchanged, which would demote them to symmetric bonds.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 12 + Operators table + Fig 13 substrate, three figure routes). The specific binding of each mechanism to a bond remains `specified`, not grounded.

### F4 — The mechanisms sit at different scales; the membrane passes methods, not recordings (`specified`)

Fig 4 :21 "The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction."

Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."

Fig 13 :74 "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."

Kinship and friendship are a **network** structure: Fig 13 :24 "that entities relate, and influence travels." Reputation is a **spectrum** structure (ordering). Formal rules are a **gradient** structure (directedness that can be spent). Two states never bond directly; composition runs across kinds. What scales up from a face-to-face group to an institution is a *method*, monitoring and graduated sanctioning, not a recording of who cooperated once.

This predicts the direction found by Marlowe et al. (2008): reputation fails as anonymity rises, and third-party punishment rises with population size, ending in "an institutionalized system of third-party punishment involving police, judges and jailers."

- **Refuter:** a case where formal rules simply replace kinship and friendship, cooperation holding at the same level with no method transferred across the membrane.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 4 + Fig 13, low internal overlap; corroborated cross-culturally by Marlowe et al. 2008).

### F5 — Valence is relative to the residual; the same mechanism can help or hurt (`general`)

Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."

Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."

There is no standing list of good mechanisms. Reputation and punishment can sustain cooperation or enforce conformity that destroys it. Marlowe et al. (2008) give the exact case: third-party punishers include those enforcing norms most people would call harmful. So "add formal rules" is not a monotone improvement; the valence depends on the residual being settled.

- **Refuter:** a mechanism whose valence is the same for every residual and every society, which would make valence a property carried by the mechanism.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 + Marlowe et al. 2008 cross-cultural data + Cox et al. 2010 on externally imposed rules failing when they do not match local conditions).

### F6 — No single mechanism closes R at all scales (`specified`)

Fig 8 :45 "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."

Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."

Nowak (2006) gives the non-circular reason: each of the five mechanisms is governed by a different threshold. Kin selection needs r > c/b; direct reciprocity w > c/b; indirect reciprocity q > c/b; network reciprocity b/c > k; group selection b/c > 1 + n/m. Raising relatedness does nothing for the anonymity of a large institution; raising reputational knowledge does nothing when strangers' reputations are unknown. The surplus lives where the closures differ. Fig 8 :51 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."

- **Refuter:** one mechanism shown, by measurement, to sustain all observed cooperation across all group sizes with no bond to the others.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 8 + Fig 6 + Nowak 2006, genuinely low overlap between a metatheory figure and evolutionary game theory).

### F7 — The anchor is cooperation itself, not agreement among theorists (`general`)

Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."

Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

The ground that settles "does cooperation persist" is an outcome: a public-goods contribution rate, a compliance record, an infant-survival measure. Two reviews that cite the same meta-analysis are one witness. Fig 7 :13 "agreement compounds, evidence does not."

- **Refuter:** a verdict for this question settled by consensus of researchers without any behavioural or outcome measurement.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 2 + Fig 7). This is a rule about the checking arrangement, not a prediction about which mechanism wins.

### F8 — The framework returns a shape, not a name (`general`)

Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."

From the canonical figures README: "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)" and "Indiscernibility is not identity." So the derivation can say the arrangement must have typed bonds, must be a union compound, and must pass methods across the scale membrane. It cannot say *which* mechanism dominates in a given society. That is settled by a ground reading or by a provenanced import with a shadow test.

- **Refuter:** a derivation from the six-term set alone that selects a unique mechanism for a given society with no ground reading.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 9 + figures README, two different presentation layers).

## 4. Strain test and re-census

**Strain found and named.** Fig 13 :75 "Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state." Read literally against the four mechanisms, "kinship and friendship are both networks, so two structures would join directly". Re-census resolves it: Fig 13 governs the six primitives, whereas the four mechanisms are *molecules* built from those primitives (a network of related individuals is a settled composition, not a primitive). Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it." So a composition of kin-bonds and a composition of rule-bonds can meet through a state (a shared situation, a resource, an outcome), exactly as the figure requires. Same rule as the exemplar's Fig 13 / derivative-rule re-census: different objects, no contradiction. **Strain dissolved by re-census, not hidden.**

**Second strain, still open.** Calling reputation a spectrum (an ordering) and formal rules a gradient (directedness) is a `specified` binding from the substrate onto social facts. The six-term set licenses *a* structure in each slot, not *which*. Fig 13 :35 "Space, time and energy are not three readings of one quantity." By the same token the social structures are not three readings of one quantity. The binding needs a shadow test, which this simulated run does not supply. **Named, not smoothed.**

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the seat-map rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A single one of the four mechanisms, with no bond to the others, is shown by measurement to sustain cooperation at all scales, including large anonymous institutions. (breaks F1, F2, F6)
2. The four mechanisms are shown to draw on one evidence pool, so that they are one mechanism under four names rather than a union with surplus. (breaks Fig 8 :49 "Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names.")
3. Formal rules are shown to transfer no method across the scale membrane, only a recording of particular past cooperation. (breaks F4)
4. The valence of a mechanism is shown to be invariant across residuals and societies. (breaks F5)
5. A ground reading selects a unique dominating mechanism for a given society without a provenanced import. (breaks F8)

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Lead with the bond claim, not the four-item list (F1). Ask a checker what would change if the relations among the four were swapped.
2. Treat the compound as the unit of analysis; do not average mechanism effects (F2, Fig 1 :19).
3. State bond types explicitly: symmetric for kin and friendship, ordered for reputation and rules (F3).
4. Ask the scale question directly: what method crosses from a face-to-face group to an institution, and does it arrive (F4).
5. Declare valence as context-relative; never recommend "more rules" as a monotone fix (F5).
6. Anchor on measured cooperation, and record overlap between sources so one pooled review is not counted twice (F7).
7. Keep the slot for "which mechanism dominates here" open until a ground reading or provenanced import closes it (F8).
