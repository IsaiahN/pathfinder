## Two findings in the wider literature that bear directly on the claim

1. **The mechanisms are not interchangeable.** Nowak's five rules are five different inequalities in five different parameters. This is the world-facing reason for the figure claim that the four mechanisms are bonded in different ways and close different slots. It is a *different* evidence pool from the figures (evolutionary game theory versus the metatheory), so it is weak corroboration rather than circular agreement.
2. **Scale changes which mechanism can do the work.** Marlowe et al. find that third-party punishment rises with population size, and explain that as anonymity defeats reputation: "with greater anonymity in larger populations, there is a greater likelihood of interacting with a stranger whose reputation is unknown." Roberts, Dunbar and Roberts show the same pressure at the primate scale: stable bonds are with selected kin and non-kin, and communication is what allows bonding on a larger scale. Together these corroborate Fig 4's membrane rule (methods cross up, recordings do not) and Fig 11's valence-relativity rule.

A conflict note: Fehr et al. argue strong reciprocity "cannot be rationalized as an adaptive trait by the leading evolutionary theories" including kin selection, reciprocal altruism and indirect reciprocity, while Nowak's framework treats punishment as an enhancer of mechanisms rather than an independent one. That conflict is *not* resolved by flipping a verdict. It sends us back to the census: the two papers are measuring different things (ultimate adaptive account versus mechanism taxonomy), so they occupy different domains and the figure verdict is unchanged. Recorded here rather than smoothed.
