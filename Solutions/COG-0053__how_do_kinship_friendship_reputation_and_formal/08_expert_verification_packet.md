# Expert verification packet — the Tether on cooperation mechanisms

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher on social institutions and cooperation can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the per-domain predictions. Not `grounded`.** No behavioural measurement is claimed. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Kinship, friendship, reputation and formal rules are not four independent causes that add up. They are four slots in one overlapping compound, joined by **typed bonds**: kin and friendship are symmetric mutual bonds, reputation is an ordering (more or less trustworthy), and formal rules are a directed, spendable difference (a sanction). Each slot closes under its own condition, so raising one does not substitute for another. What scales from a face-to-face group to an institution is a method, monitoring and graduated sanctioning, not a recording of who cooperated once. A mechanism's effect is relative to the problem it is used against, so the same sanction can sustain cooperation in one setting and crush it in another. The framework does not name which mechanism dominates in a given society; it returns a shape, falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close the slot.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 12 and 13

- **Statement:** the interaction is a bond, not a list; the four mechanisms are typed network / spectrum / gradient structures crossed through shared states.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :5 "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Also Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance." And Fig 13 :74 "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."
- **What to check:** is the bond claim actually doing work, or is it a restatement of "the four interact"? Look for an observable that changes when the relations are swapped while the four items are held fixed.
- **Refuter:** show a composition of the four that carries no more information than the four-item list; or show one of the four is a bare state with no structure in the middle, which breaks the six-term typing.

### C2 — world-facing; checkable against the cooperation literature

- **Statement:** each mechanism closes under a different condition, so they are not interchangeable and do not substitute for one another at scale. Reputation fails where a stranger's reputation is unknown; formal third-party enforcement rises with population size.
- **Source, verbatim figure line:** Fig 8 :45 "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." Corroboration (secondary, different evidence pool): Nowak (Science, 2006) derives five different thresholds (r, w, q, k, n/m); Marlowe et al. (Proc R Soc B, 2008) report "Our results show that people in larger, more complex societies engage in significantly more third-party punishment than people in small-scale societies." and explain it by anonymity defeating reputation.
- **What to check:** is the substitution claim falsified by any existing dataset? Does a rise in kin-based or reputation-based cooperation actually track third-party enforcement, or is the relationship not monotone? Note that Fehr et al. (2002) argue strong reciprocity is not reducible to kin selection, reciprocal altruism or indirect reciprocity; that is a domain difference, recorded in `04_links_and_distribution.md`, not a flipped verdict.
- **Refuter:** one mechanism, unbound, meeting the cooperation target at every scale including large anonymous institutions; or cross-cultural data showing no rise in third-party enforcement with anonymity.

### C3 — framework-internal derivation with a world check

- **Statement:** the compound has an order and a cycle; reputation-first and rules-first are isomers over the same atom set, with different behavioural outcomes, and a mechanism's valence is context-relative.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :57-58 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound." Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
- **What to check:** do reputation-first and rules-first arrangements differ observationally (legitimacy, compliance cost, monitoring effort)? Cox et al. (2010) find externally imposed rules that do not match local custom suffer low monitoring and high extraction, which is the predicted shape, but this has not been checked as an isomer test.
- **Refuter:** both orders producing the same behavioural outcomes in comparable settings (kills the isomer claim, leaves the functional-group claim); or a mechanism whose valence is invariant across societies (kills the context-relativity claim).

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that the four mechanisms simply "all help", and not an average of their effects.
- Not that more of any one is always better; each bond closes under its own condition.
- Not that formal rules replace kinship, friendship or reputation. They compose over them.
- Not a name for the dominant mechanism in any society; the framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement in this derivation settles the arrangement.
- Not a theory of everything, and not a claim that reputation is ground. "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it." (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, opened 2026-11-22.)

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. The five pre-registered refuters are in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §5. The cheapest discriminating check is C2: does third-party enforcement substitute for reputation in a large anonymous setting, or compose with it? If it substitutes, Fig 4's membrane claim fails. If it composes, the shape stands on that point.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 12 :5 and :94, Fig 13 :74, Fig 8 :45, Fig 11 :49. Five short passages.
2. Check C2 against the cooperation literature you know: are the mechanisms substitutable at scale, or not? Name one dataset that would discriminate.
3. For C3, name one observable that would differ between a reputation-first and a rules-first arrangement. If you cannot, say so; that is a failure of the claim.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. No agreement is requested, and later agreement will not be pooled as corroboration.
