## What an answer would look like (Direction bound as an ordinary term)

Before deriving, the sought-for shape is written down and bound so it can accrue a residual like anything else. For this question the sought-for shape is: a rule that ranks candidate conservation actions per object slot (site, species, interaction, function), that keeps the cost of describing an action separate from the cost of testing it, that lets an ecological measurement rather than expert agreement settle which action worked, and that keeps every cut ranked and reversible. A priority list is not the answer; a list is the output of running the rule on a particular habitat with a particular ground.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the rule.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that the rule tells a manager which species or site to save.

Three figure-grounded reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." (Fig 2 :35). A recipient list selected for receptiveness is a mirror, not a frame.
2. "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool" (Fig 2 :40). Selecting for likely agreement manufactures the disqualifying correlation.
3. "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." (Fig 10 :42). Sending the full framework before asking for a verdict spends the only thing the outside frame contributes.
