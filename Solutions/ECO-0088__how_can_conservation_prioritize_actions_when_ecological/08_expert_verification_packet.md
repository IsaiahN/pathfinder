# Expert verification packet: the Tether on conservation prioritization under uncertainty and scarcity

**What this is:** a claim in a form a conservation scientist or practitioner can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the rule, `general` on the structural claim, not `grounded`.** No ecological measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Conservation prioritization under uncertain outcomes and a limited budget should not be run as one aggregate score. It should be run as a five-part rule: keep the prediction-to-observation residual per object slot (site, species, interaction, function) and never sum the slots; keep two costs in separate accounts, bits for describing and modelling an action and actions for testing it; enumerate the habitat outward to second- and third-order contacts and re-derive the list when the residual changes; use cost and feasibility as filters for the budget and never as witnesses for whether an action worked; and range independent checks against a measured outcome that does not move because reviewers agree. An action is accepted when its cost plus the residual it leaves is less than the residual before, and the assumption to refuse is the one whose residual persists and does not trend down. The output is the shape of a defensible list, not a list; a concrete list needs an enumerated habitat and a live ground.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 9 and 12

- **Statement:** a single aggregate priority score is structurally disqualified. The residual is per slot, and the two budget currencies do not add.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 12 :125 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."
- **What to check:** in your own planning system, can one scalar score preserve the identity of a live slot, and can description effort and field effort be converted into one unit without changing which actions are affordable?
- **Refuter:** exhibit a lossless aggregate score over slots; or a conversion between the two costs that leaves the budget boundary and the chosen actions unchanged. If either succeeds, C1 fails.

### C2 - ecology-facing; checkable against the habitat and the cascade

- **Statement:** the habitat must be enumerated, not composed, and the enumeration must reach beyond direct contact. There is no standing action list across a change of residual.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :44 "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." Fig 11 :52 "contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."
- **What to check:** does your prioritization scope include second- and third-order contacts? Can a "best practice" action remain best after the focal residual changes? The DDT case in the figure is a real ecological caution, not an analogy.
- **Refuter:** show a direct-contact-only plan that avoided a documented third-order failure; or a standing action list that stayed valid across a change of residual. If either holds, C2 fails.

### C3 - verification-facing; checkable against the monitoring channel

- **Statement:** agreement among frames is not a verdict. The ground settles each frame separately, and the channel to the ground decays even though the ground does not.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool" Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does"
- **What to check:** in a past decision, was the claim that an action worked established by independent measurements, or by convergence of people reading the same reports? When monitoring stopped, did the program report "no change" when it had in fact lost touch?
- **Refuter:** show a decision about which action worked settled by agreement with no independent ground reading. If that is possible, C3 fails.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a priority list. No site, species or action is named. The list is `unreachable-at-this-budget`, not `gap`.
- Not an ethical verdict on triage. Declining to add an actor is not subtracting the habitat; the ethical disagreement in the literature is recorded in `01` §7 and is not settled by a figure.
- Not a claim that cost is irrelevant. Cost is a filter for the budget, never a witness for the verdict.
- Not a claim that local or Indigenous knowledge is an oracle or a footnote. It is one frame that must be ranged against the ground with the others.
- Not a derivation of conservation science from machine learning or animal behaviour. Those are imported with a source gate and a shadow test (`06` Q4) and they corroborate only.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

A run on a specified habitat with a live ground. Enumerate the slots and the cascade; price bits and actions separately; fit an instrument already returning something; keep every cut ranked and reversible; let the measured outcome decide whether each action paid. Then run the five refuters in `01` §4. Surviving that run would move the rule to `grounded` for that habitat. Nothing less does.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19 and :22, Fig 12 :125 and :130, Fig 9 :60 and Fig 11 :44 and :51. Six short passages.
2. Name one scalar score in your own planning system and say what live slot information it discards.
3. Name one monitoring channel in your system and say how you would know it had gone stale rather than the ecosystem stable.
4. Name one second- or third-order contact your current scoping omits.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
