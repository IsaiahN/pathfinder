# The Tether's answer: prioritizing conservation actions under uncertainty and scarcity

**Mode: `specified` on the five-part rule, `general` on the structural claim. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not an ecological measurement.

## 1. The residual it answers

A manager must choose among candidate actions when the outcome of each action is not yet paid by the ground and the budget bounds how much can be described and tried. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." The residual is many slots (sites, species, interactions, functions), it is not symmetric across them (Fig 11 :49), and the effects cascade beyond direct contact (Fig 11 :52). The question is a union of two conditions, uncertainty and scarcity, with a compound at their intersection (Fig 9 :55). It is not one hard optimization.

## 2. The shape: a five-part rule

1. **Keep the residual per slot and refuse to sum the slots.** Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A single biodiversity score hides the one live slot that matters. Show which slots are live before ranking anything.
2. **Keep two costs in separate accounts.** Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Bits price the description, the data, the model, the expert elicitation. Actions price finding out whether the thing works, the survey, the fence, the translocation. The budget boundary is set in each, and "Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it" (Fig 12 :130).
3. **Enumerate the habitat outward and re-derive when the residual changes.** Fig 11 :44: "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." There is no standing list of good actions: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual" (Fig 11 :51). Intervention is asymmetric: "Introduction is available; exclusion is not" (Fig 11 :61), so a protected site is permanent work, not a solved state (Fig 11 :63).
4. **Use filters for the budget, witnesses for the verdict.** Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Cost, feasibility and acceptability decide what gets tried first. Only a measured ecological outcome decides whether it worked. Keep every cut ranked and reversible (Fig 9 :41), because "a wrong cut removes the answer and speeds up" (Fig 9 :40).
5. **Range independent frames against a ground that does not update.** Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." Field experiment, monitoring, remote sensing, local and Indigenous knowledge and models each range separately; agreement among frames that share evidence is the pool talking, not corroboration (Fig 2 :40). Monitoring is the channel and it decays: "the ground does not decay, the channel does" (Fig 10 :24). A dead channel reports "you have lost touch" (Fig 10 :28), not "no change".

**Acceptance and refusal.** An action is accepted by the bargain in `THE_FORMULA.md` step 3: accept φ iff cost(φ) + left(R,φ) < cost(R), with SUPPORT (a live slot), REACHABILITY (it composes from what the program has) and NOVELTY (it is not an action already in the library). The term to refuse is the bound priority assumption whose residual "PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN" (Fig 5 :59), not the outcome anyone disliked. Refusal is negation, a unary operator the operators table defines, so the record can carry the correction without deleting anything.

**Dependency order.** Settle the objective's instrument before optimizing the selection algorithm. Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." The objective link in conservation is the least instrumented one.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a priority list. No site, species or action is named, because no habitat was enumerated and no ground was supplied. The list is `unreachable-at-this-budget`, not `gap` (Fig 13 :134).
- Not "spend the budget on the most charismatic species", and not its mirror "spread the budget so nobody loses". The triage-isomer distinction in `06` Q3 is a structural point, not an ethical verdict.
- Not a claim that cost is irrelevant. Cost is a filter for the budget. It is not a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).
- Not a claim that expert or community agreement is the ground. Agreement is variation; selection between frames is where the ground votes (Fig 7 :35). Local and Indigenous knowledge is one frame among those that must be ranged, not an oracle and not a footnote.
- Not a derivation of one discipline from another. Bayesian experimental design and explore/exploit theory are imported with a source gate and an independence debit (Fig 6 :23-24); they corroborate the shape and do not decide it.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken, so nothing here is settled.

## 4. What would settle it, and what would break it

**To settle the rule:** run it on a specified habitat. Enumerate the slots and the cascade, price bits and actions separately, fit a monitoring instrument that already returns something (Fig 6 :32), record which cuts were made and keep them reversible, and let the measured outcome decide. Then check the five refuters in `01` §4. A rule that survives a live ground on a real habitat moves from `specified` to `grounded` for that habitat, not in general.

**To break it:** any of the five pre-registered refuters firing. In short: a lossless aggregate score; a cost conversion that preserves the budget decision; agreement settling an outcome without a ground; a direct-contact-only plan that avoids a third-order failure; a ranked list with no refusal that keeps working as conditions change.

**Instrument note.** From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source", and the colour case "was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." A population count rising after an action does not by itself identify the cause. What is owed is a discriminator, not a better story.

## 5. The honest stop

The rule is a generator, not an answer. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Concrete prioritization needs an enumerated habitat, a priced budget in two currencies, and a live anchor. None is in the question. The framework reports that the list is out of reach with what is held and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity." This is a simulated derivation; it cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not produce a priority list. It produces the shape a defensible list must have. Keep the residual per site, species, interaction and function, because an average hides the one live slot that matters. Keep two costs in separate accounts: bits to describe an action, actions to test it, and never add them. Enumerate the habitat outward to the cascade and re-derive the list when the residual changes, since no standing list of good actions survives a change of residual. Use cost and feasibility as filters for what gets tried, never as witnesses for what worked, and keep every cut ranked and reversible. Range experiments, monitoring, remote sensing and local knowledge separately against a measured outcome that does not move because reviewers agree. Accept an action only when its cost plus the residual it leaves is less than the residual before, and refuse the assumption that keeps being wrong and is not improving. The concrete list stays open until a habitat and a live ground are supplied. What is offered is that shape, five refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it.
