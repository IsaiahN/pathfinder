# Union, order, and the triage question

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question bundles at least two conditions: outcomes are uncertain, and resources are limited. Is that one hard prioritization problem, or a union of overlapping conditions with a compound at the intersection? And once split, what is the dependency order: must the objective be settled before the action is ranked? A second question follows from the world literature: "triage" and "spread the budget widely" are often presented as opposite strategies. Are they isomers under Fig 12 (same atoms, different arrangement), or are they different atoms altogether?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held in the conservation frame: sites, species, interactions, functions, actions, measurements, budgets, time. Bonds held: sequence (→), comparison (⋛), conjunction (+), subtraction (−), and negation (¬) for refusal. No new primitive is available without import.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Partition or union?

- Fig 9 :4-5: "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."
- Fig 9 :55-56: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."
- A lever that clears the *scarcity* condition (more funding, better cost efficiency) leaves the *uncertainty* condition binding. A lever that clears the *uncertainty* condition (a better monitoring instrument) leaves the budget boundary where it was. So the two are not one scalar problem.
- ⇒ The compound has its own loop and its own ground. **Verdict: union, with a compound at the intersection.** Triangulated: Fig 9 :4-5 and Fig 9 :55-56 are the same figure, so this is `single-source` at figure level; the conservation literature's separation of money and knowledge (McDonald-Madden 2008) is a low-overlap corroboration, not a second figure.

### Q2 - What is the dependency order?

- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
- Links: (1) perception: can we observe the slot at all? (2) vocabulary: do we have terms for what an action affords? (3) objective: can we say what counts as progress per slot in our own terms? (4) planning: a route to that objective under incomplete information. (5) learn and carry: name what worked, verify it, carry it.
- Fig 3 :25-27: the middle link "has neither" obvious failures nor obvious tests, so "it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments. A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
- In conservation, objective-setting is often the untested middle link: "protect biodiversity" is a fundamental objective that is vague and underspecified until means objectives make it measurable (McCarthy 2014, WORLD in `09`). Optimizing the selection algorithm above that break is effort spent above the break.
- ⇒ **Verdict: settle the objective's instrument before ranking actions.** Single-source on the figure (Fig 3), corroborated from the conservation decision-theory pool.

### Q3 - Are triage and portfolio-spread isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Written over the same atoms `{slots, actions, budget, monitor, ground}`, triage and spread share every atom. What differs is the arrangement: triage concentrates effort on the slots where the residual is live and the action can move it; spread distributes effort to keep short-term persistence across all slots. That is Fig 12's isomer condition: same atoms, different bond, different substance. Both are members of the same functional group (allocate a budget against live residuals); the qualifier says which member.
- But the carbon-copy reading fails. Triage as *giving up on a species* is not the same molecule: it adds a refusal (¬) and removes the standing of that slot to be composed over, which Fig 5 :55 describes as "What falls is the reach." Bottrill et al. 2008 define triage as efficient allocation, not abandonment; the PNAS 2016 paper sets that against "injurious neglect." The two literatures are using the same word for different bonded arrangements.
- ⇒ **Verdict: isomers only under the narrow, same-atoms reading; otherwise a functional group with a ¬ added.** Single-source on the figure definition, with the distinction itself coming from the world sources.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if triage can be written without an atom that spread requires, or if the two can be interconverted by re-labelling alone with no change in the bond and no change in behaviour at the ground, the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim stands.

### Q4 - Explore versus exploit: an imported analogue with a source gate

- Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."
- The explore/exploit literature (Valentin et al. 2024, human cognition; Rainforth et al. 2023, mathematics) is a frame whose closure differs from conservation planning. It predicts that a decision-maker with a finite budget must trade learning about options against using the best known option.
- The figure's own version is F7 in `01`: accept an action iff cost(φ) + left(R,φ) < cost(R), and revise the term whose residual persists and does not trend down. Learning is not a separate luxury; it is a candidate action with its own cost and leftover residual.
- **Source gate:** provenance recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv` with author, venue, year, accessed date and quote. **Shadow test:** does the imported frame explain a gap the conservation frame left? Yes: McCarthy 2014 notes that learning "will usually come at the cost of immediate management actions," which is exactly the leftover term the bargain prices.
- **Debit against independence:** the imported frame is machine learning and cognitive science, low overlap with ecology. Importing it is legitimate, but it adds no atom to the conservation world (Fig 6 :25: "An import adds no atom to the world."), and it is not allowed to decide the verdict. Figures decide.

### Q5 - Which term is the wrong one?

- Fig 5 :58-60: "every term currently bound and generating a prediction is a candidate for being the wrong one. And they rank by a quantity already recorded - a term whose residual PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN is the one to point the refusal at."
- The candidates are enumerable: the current priority list, the monitoring method, the objective definition, the assumption that a threat can be addressed spatially. Each predicts something and each has a residual history.
- The term to refuse is the one that keeps being wrong and is not improving, not the outcome a stakeholder disliked. Negation is a unary operator (Operators table :70-73), so the plan can carry the refusal without deleting the term from the record. Fig 6 :42-43: "A refutation adds a term that refuses; it removes nothing, and the reach falls because something was added rather than because anything was deleted."
- ⇒ **Verdict: a prioritization rule without a ¬ operator can only accumulate failed assumptions.** Single-source on the operators table and Fig 5 amendment.

## CONCLUSION

The question is a union with a compound at the intersection, not one hard problem. Its dependency order runs from an instrumented objective to a ranked plan, and effort above a broken objective link cannot rescue it. Triage and spread are isomers under the narrow same-atoms reading and a functional group otherwise; the "give up" version adds a refusal and is a different molecule. Explore/exploit is an admissible import with a source gate, a shadow test and an independence debit, and it corroborates the acceptance bargain rather than deciding it. The refusal target is the bound term whose residual persists and does not trend down.
