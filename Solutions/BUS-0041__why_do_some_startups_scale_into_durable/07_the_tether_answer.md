# The Tether's answer on startup durability

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Two startups begin with similar ideas and resources. The gap between what each predicts and what the market returns stays small across years of growth in one case and grows until the venture dies in the other. R is indexed per startup and per time, never averaged (Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."; Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."). The contact is a **union compound**, not one hard problem: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either (Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."). It has its own loop and its own ground, the market (Fig 2 :29).

## 2. The shape

1. **Durability is a property of venture-and-habitat, not a stock the venture owns.** Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Holding ideas and resources equal does not hold the habitat equal.
2. **Two conditions must both hold.** C1, a maintained channel to a paying ground; C2, reach renewed by contact with a closure that differs from the startup's own (Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."). Each has its own failure mode; one lever does not clear both (Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones.").
3. **Success is a label applied after selection ran.** Fig 11 :36: "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there." A durable startup is a lineage that met its ground, and "winning traits" read off survivors do not prospectively name the cause.
4. **A strength can flip sign when the residual changes.** Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use." So there is no permanent list of good founders or good practices.
5. **Contact cascades outward.** Fig 11 :52: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles." The decisive condition is often several steps out from the startup, which is why first-order enumeration misses it.
6. **Startup isolation is substitution, not removal.** Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." A sandbox or accelerator proves wiring in a substituted habitat, not capability in the target one.
7. **The framework does not name the winning move.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence," and Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Which specific practice wins next is a declared `gap`, not a hidden answer.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a list of success factors. Effectuation, product-market fit, timing and the lean loop are bindings from the business evidence pool, provenance owed; none is a figure-derived name.
- Not "the founder is irrelevant." The founder is part of Γ and of the loop; the claim is only that Γ alone cannot carry durability (F1).
- Not "the market is always right in the short run." The market is the anchor for *whether it paid*, not for every claim a venture makes (Fig 2 :37-38, instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure).
- Not a derivation of one startup's fate. No prospective measurement here settles `left(R,φ)`.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing in this folder is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. The falsifiers, written before the run, are the instruments in effect terms:

1. **Habitat substitution test.** Does durability survive a substitution that removes payment with Γ held fixed? If yes, capability was agent-only and the shape fails.
2. **Prospective discriminator.** Is there a property, fixed before outcome, that predicts durability with an instrument that discriminates among the several sources of a high reading? If yes, success was not merely a post-selection label.
3. **Two-condition test.** Can one intervention restore both C1 and C2 with no categorically different residue? If yes, the union collapses to one condition.
4. **Closure test.** Can reach be renewed by internal rearrangement alone with no new atom and no differing closure while payment continues? If yes, the closure rule fails.
5. **Higher-order test.** Is the decisive failure fully explained by direct first-order contact with no second- or third-order actor? If yes, the cascade claim narrows.
6. **Valence test.** Does a named capability keep invariant valence across a documented change of residual, measured against the ground? If yes, the valve claim fails.

Standing refuters from the record: a direct payment with no habitat; a room that substitutes for the ground; a single lever that clears a union.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The vocabulary of entrepreneurship research is a set of bindings, not nature's own list. Declaring them makes the choice visible, not neutral. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity." No number of additional success stories closes the gap; they are more books in the same library (Fig 7 :13 "agreement compounds, evidence does not").

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give you a list of traits that make startups win, and it does not claim to settle which startup will survive. It says that durability is a property of venture-and-habitat, not a stock the venture owns; that two conditions must both hold, a maintained channel to a paying ground and reach renewed from a differing closure, each with its own failure mode; that "success" is a label applied after selection ran rather than a property read off the survivor; that a strength can flip sign when the problem changes; and that a sandbox is a substituted habitat, not the real one. What it offers is the shape any durable-startup answer must have, six falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The specific winning move stays open until the ground settles it.
