# Outreach draft - validate the output first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the effect restatement and the refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` sections 0 and 4 survive a first check. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one output before you decide whether the account is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question in effect terms:** why does a startup's gap between what it predicts and what the market returns stay small across years of growth in one case and grow until the venture dies in another, when the two began with similar ideas and resources? That gap is measured per venture and per time, not averaged across a portfolio. The anchor is the market, which pays or withholds.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** durability is not a stock a startup owns. It is a property of venture-and-habitat. Two conditions must both hold: a maintained channel to a paying ground, and reach renewed by contact with a frame whose closure differs from the startup's own. Each has its own failure mode, and they are not one lever. A strength can flip sign when the problem the venture faces changes, so there is no standing list of good founders, good teams or good ideas. "Success" is a label applied after selection ran, not a property you can read off the survivor before the fact.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that effectuation, product-market fit, timing, or any other business-school term *is* the mechanism. Those are bindings from the entrepreneurship literature onto categories, and convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents; only the record of where each came from separates them. The framework returns a shape, not a name. It does not tell you which move will work next.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a venture whose durability is unchanged after a habitat substitution that removes the market's payment, with its internal ideas and resources held fixed;
> 2. a prospectively measured property, fixed before the outcome, that predicts durability with a source-discriminating instrument;
> 3. one intervention that restores both conditions with no categorically different residue left over;
> 4. reach renewed purely by internal rearrangement with no new atom and no differing closure, while the market keeps paying.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figures README: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Full text of the figures with line numbers (local copy in the solution folder): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - The Formula, the eight-step loop: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - Public entrepreneurship research venues, for checking rather than endorsing: see `03_public_contact_points.csv` and `04_links_and_distribution.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a startup problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and its essays warn that outputs can be "contrived to fit the formula." A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
