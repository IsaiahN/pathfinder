## 0. The claim under audit and the residual in effect terms

**Question:** Why do some startups scale into durable businesses while others with similar ideas and resources fail?

**Residual stated as an effect.** A venture's library Γ predicts a market return Γ(b,a); the market returns o′; R = |Γ(b,a) - o′|. R is indexed per startup-slot and per time, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."; Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."). Durable scaling = R stays small and keeps shrinking over a long chain of slots. Failure = R grows until the slot dies. The phrase "similar ideas and resources" fixes the starting Γ as roughly equal across the two compared slots, so whatever separates them must be sought in the *loop, the habitat, and the maintenance of contact*, not in a larger initial Γ.

**Arity / symmetry / scale of R.**
- Arity: a four-place relation among venture Γ, action, habitat conditions, and time. It carries an ordered bond (a route through slots) and a comparison bond (payment versus no payment), not a symmetric join.
- Symmetry: asymmetric. Credit assignment runs backwards from outcome to source, and the label "successful" is applied after the fact rather than read off the venture before the fact (Fig 11 :36 "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there.").
- Scale: the single venture loop, the population of ventures selected against the market, and the time channel across a venture's life. The membrane between scales is lossy (Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked.").

**Question type (Fig 9):** a union, not one hard problem. Two conditions must both hold; a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either (Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either.").

**Direction bound before the run (Formula, "Direction"):** the sought-for shape is "durability is a two-condition conjunction with two distinct failure modes and a valve whose sign changes with the residual." It is written down here so it can accrue a residual and be refused by the same test as everything else.

## 3. Findings

### F1 — "Similar ideas and resources" is an agent-only reading (decisive)

Fig 11 :39-40:

> "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."
> "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

The question's own premise, that two startups with similar ideas and resources diverge, is exactly the agent-only picture the figure refuses. If the starting Γ is held equal, the difference cannot be in Γ alone; it must be in the habitat the venture is in contact with and in the loop that keeps or loses that contact. Fig 4 :34 adds the other half: "A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."

- **Mode:** `specified` for the startup application; `general` for the figure's capability claim.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 capability rule and Fig 4 seed/landing rule, low overlap between habitat and membrane).
- **Refuter:** a venture whose durability is unchanged across a habitat substitution that removes the market's payment, with all internal Γ held fixed. If it still scales, capability was agent-only after all.

### F2 — "Success" is a label applied after selection ran, not a property read off the venture

Fig 11 :36:

> "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."

A survived startup is a lineage that met its ground. Reading "winning traits" off survivors and naming them as the cause is the source error the figure names. This is the mechanism that survivorship-bias literature rediscovers in the business pool (see `09_watson_answer.md`, World section).

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Status:** single-source for the figure (Fig 11); the low-overlap corroboration is the survivorship-bias source, which is secondary only.
- **Refuter:** a prospectively measured venture property, fixed before the outcome, that predicts durability with a source-discriminating instrument. Then the label is doing work before selection, and this finding is withdrawn.

### F3 — The market is the anchor; internal agreement cannot settle

Fig 2 :29 and Fig 2 :35:

> "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."
> "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

Payment from customers is the anchor for "did it scale." Internal agreement among founders, advisors, or a sympathetic investor room is a frame, not a ground. A room does not upgrade the label.

- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 2 anchor rule and Fig 7 room rule).
- **Refuter:** a venture whose internal metrics stay green while customer payment disappears, and which nevertheless keeps scaling. That would show the anchor is internal agreement, not the market.

### F4 — Two conditions, union not partition

Fig 9 :57 and Fig 9 :55:

> "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
> "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."

The two conditions are C1 (a maintained channel to a paying ground) and C2 (renewed reach through contact with a differing closure). Each has its own failure mode. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing, because the second condition still binds (Fig 9 :56).

- **Mode:** `general` for the union rule, `specified` for C1/C2.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 9 union rule and Fig 3 dependency chain).
- **Refuter:** one intervention that restores both conditions with no categorically different residue left over. Then it was one condition, not a union.

### F5 — Reach must be renewed from a differing closure

Fig 6 :23 and Fig 8 :32:

> "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."
> "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."

A startup can exhaust its initial idea and resources by rearranging them; that is search inside a fixed closure and adds no new atom. Scaling into durability requires contact with a differing closure (a new market, a new customer type, a new stakeholder, new knowledge) that supplies an atom it did not hold. Under Fig 12 :117, two ventures with the same starting atoms but different bonds are isomers: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 6 import and Fig 8 idempotence, plus Fig 12 isomer for the binding).
- **Refuter:** reach renewed purely by internal composition with no new atom and no differing closure, with the market still paying. That breaks the closure rule.

### F6 — Contact cascades; second- and third-order effects decide

Fig 11 :52:

> "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."

A startup's habitat is not just its immediate customers. It includes what contacts those customers, outward until the cascade stops mattering (Fig 11 :44). A change several steps out (a platform rule, a regulator, an input supplier, a macro cycle) can be the residual that matters and is invisible to first-order enumeration.

- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Status:** single-source for the figure (Fig 11 habitat cascade); corroborated at low overlap by the socio-ecological scale literature, which is secondary.
- **Refuter:** a failure fully explained by direct first-order contact with no second- or third-order actor in the causal chain.

### F7 — The middle dependency link has no instrument

Fig 3 :27 and Fig 3 :19:

> "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
> "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

Perception and planning have obvious failures and tests. Having terms for what a changed situation affords, and being able to say what would count as progress there, has neither (Fig 3 :26). So measurement effort piles up at the measurable links (growth metrics, funding) while the break sits at vocabulary and at carry. Vanity metrics are the named failure: a reading that "gives the rosiest picture possible" is not the ground (Fig 1 :24-27).

- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 3 chain and Fig 1 frame-internal disqualification).
- **Refuter:** a venture whose chain demonstrably works at perception, vocabulary and objective and fails only at planning. That would falsify the "usually the middle" claim.

### F8 — Valence flips with the residual; a strength becomes a rigidity

Fig 11 :49-51 and Fig 12 :119:

> "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
> "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."
> "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."

A founding routine that paid when the residual was X can become a rigidity when the residual becomes Y. The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another. A "good team" is not a standing list.

- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 valence and Fig 12 catalyst/functional group).
- **Refuter:** invariant valence of a named capability across a documented change of residual, measured against the ground. Then valence is a property after all.

### F9 — Isolating a startup is habitat substitution, not removal

Fig 11 :66:

> "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."

A test market, a sandbox, an accelerator, or a synthetic solve does not remove the habitat; it swaps in another one with actors the venture did not choose. "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability" (Fig 11 :70). What you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails; what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts (Fig 11 :68-69).

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Status:** single-source for the figure (Fig 11).
- **Refuter:** capability preserved across a substitution with every goal-required condition reproduced and no new actor acting. Then substitution is harmless and the law is too strong.

### F10 — What wins next is a gap, unreachable with the library as it stands

Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134, Fig 8 :39:

> "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"
> "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"
> "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

No figure line, after a genuine re-census, governs *which* specific move will make a given venture durable next. The framework returns a shape (the two conditions and their failure modes), not a name. Naming effectuation, product-market fit, or timing as *the* answer is a binding from a world frame onto held atoms, not a derivation from the figures.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Status:** `gap`, declared. Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per Seat Map :287: "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A venture's durability is unchanged after a habitat substitution that removes the market's payment, with internal Γ held fixed (breaks F1).
2. A prospectively measured property, fixed before outcome, predicts durability with a source-discriminating instrument (breaks F2).
3. One intervention restores both conditions with no categorically different residue (breaks F4).
4. Reach is renewed purely by internal composition with no new atom and no differing closure while the market keeps paying (breaks F5).
5. A failure is fully explained by direct first-order contact with no higher-order actor (breaks F6).
6. A named capability keeps invariant valence across a documented residual change (breaks F8).

The answers to these are not recorded here; they are offered to an outside frame to test before any theory is shown. If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the residual, not with a list of success factors. Two startups with similar Γ and divergent fates are a union compound with two failure modes, not a search for a winning trait (F4).
2. State the mode on every sentence. Nothing here is `grounded` (F1 to F10).
3. Put the derivation before any binding to business vocabulary, with provenance, so the import/convergence distinction is checkable (F10; Fig 8 :50 "But shared atoms are not the signature. Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence.").
4. Do not send a list of famous founders or professors who agree. Ask for a refutation (Fig 2 :35, Fig 10 :42).
5. Figure 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Send the generator, the derivation and the refuter, not a recording of which startups won.
