## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not the *level* of a startup's early success but the *duration for which the gap stays small while the venture keeps growing*. In effect terms: a venture's library Γ (its ideas, routines, founding team and network) predicts a market return Γ(b,a); the market returns o′; R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| is measured per startup-slot and per time, not averaged across startups (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22). Durable scaling means R stays small and keeps shrinking across a long sequence of slots. Failure means R grows until the slot dies. The anchor is the market, named as an anchor in Fig 2 :29.

- Option A, "durability is a stock the startup owns (the idea, the founder, the resources)": leads to the prediction that the difference between two similar startups is located inside the venture considered alone.
- Option B, "durability is a relation that must be maintained": leads to the prediction that the difference is a property of venture-and-habitat, and that it fails when contact with the paying ground or renewal of reach fails (Fig 11 :39, Fig 11 :40).
- What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a *shape* naming the conditions that must both hold for a slot's R to stay small across growth, plus an instrument that would discriminate a failure of one condition from a failure of the other. That shape is the sought-for term; until it is bound it predicts nothing and no check in the loop can see it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B in `specified` mode, kept per domain and never averaged.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the entrepreneurship question, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of startup success.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." (Fig 7 :35) and "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool" (Fig 2 :40). A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
