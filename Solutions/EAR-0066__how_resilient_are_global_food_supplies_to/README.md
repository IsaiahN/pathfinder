**Question:** How resilient are global food supplies to simultaneous harvest failures in major producing regions?

**Source:** `earth systems.txt` line 88. **Section:** 7. Food, agriculture, and land use.

**Field for public contact points:** food security research.

**Residual in effect terms.** The gap, for each crop x producing-region x consuming-population slot, between food that is physically available and economically obtainable when several major breadbaskets fail in the same season, and the food needed to avoid a declared harm. The gap is per slot, never a global average. The question asks whether that gap stays small and recovers, or widens and cascades.

**What an answer would look like (bound as an ordinary term).** A shape: per-slot verdicts naming which conditions must hold for the gap to stay small, which imports would widen the reach, which couplings convert one failure into many, and an instrument that would settle each slot. A number is not required and is not returned.

## Honest headline verdict

The figures do not return a resilience percentage. They return a separator. Simultaneous harvest failure is a **union of overlapping conditions** whose regions are coupled by a common cause, not N independent draws (Fig 2 :6-7, Fig 9 :54-57; corroborated by Anderson et al. 2019). Resilience must be indexed per slot (Fig 1 :19, :22; corroborated by Kuhla et al. 2026). The current reach cannot add resilience by more optimisation inside it; only an import from a closure that differs at the evidence level buys genuine independence (Fig 6 :21, :23; Fig 8 :32, :48). Verdicts for production, trade, governance and nutrition stay separate and are never averaged. The literal magnitude is a declared `gap`, reported as unreachable at this budget rather than as silence.
