**Subject:** One checkable claim about simultaneous breadbasket failures

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the mechanism, not `grounded`):** "global food supply resilience" is the wrong object. The thing that fails is a per-crop, per-region, per-population slot: does food stay physically available and economically obtainable above a declared need threshold when several major breadbaskets fail in the same season? Three consequences follow.
>
> 1. The failures are not independent draws. Several breadbaskets often fail together because one climate mode organised drought and heat across them. Treating them as independent overstates how diversified the system is.
> 2. The current efficiency program cannot add the missing buffer. More optimisation inside the same set of actors, routes and rules visits more of the same space; it does not add a new option. Only a genuine difference in exposure, supplier base, input source, reserve trigger or instrument widens the set.
> 3. Verdicts for production, trade and price, policy, and nutrition outcomes stay separate. A stock-to-use ratio or diversification index is a way of allocating attention, not proof that people remained food secure.
>
> **What would refute this, written before I sent it:**
> 1. A single global scalar that predicts every slot's threshold outcome across simultaneous failures, with an anchor that does not move when people change their minds.
> 2. Near-zero cross-breadbasket failure correlation under strong climate modes, so geographic diversification buys full independence.
> 3. An efficiency change inside the existing set that raises measured buffer in all four domains and survives a ground test.
> 4. A monitoring instrument that resolves the per-slot gap without pooled metrics or saturation, with a published held-out failure rate.
>
> If any holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I will send the three source links in a second message. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
>
> If you want to use the method: write down what would refute your own result before you run it, and state whether your claim is general, specified or grounded. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
