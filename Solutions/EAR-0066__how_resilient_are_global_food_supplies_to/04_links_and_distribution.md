## 2. Wider literature that bears on the question

The seven substantive world sources span climate science (Anderson, Gaupp, Chen), agricultural economics and agent-based trade modelling (Kuhla), food-security measurement and resilience studies (Béné, Zurek), and food-systems policy review (Zhang). They were chosen for low overlap, not for agreement. Overlap declaration:

- Anderson 2019 and Chen 2024 share the breadbasket-yield field and several authors or institutions; they are one broad climate-crop evidence pool on the question of whether shock frequency is rising, not two independent witnesses.
- Gaupp 2020 and Kuhla 2026 share the Gaupp failure-probability estimates (Kuhla draws its failure distributions from Gaupp), so Kuhla's production-side forcing is not independent of Gaupp. Kuhla's trade-and-consumption results are its own contribution.
- Béné 2023, Zurek 2022 and Zhang 2025 overlap in the food-system-resilience literature and partly share authors and institutions; they count as one pool for the measurement claim.
- The lower-overlap corroborators are the climate-mode physics (a common cause measured independently of the figures) and the agent-based propagation result (a model-based demonstration of heterogeneous per-slot outcomes). The essays share the framework author's pool completely and carry no independent witness weight.

No world source conflicts with the figure verdict after the re-census in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 7 S4. The Gaupp/Chen disagreement about the direction of the frequency trend was returned to the census and resolved as two different quantities, not used to flip the verdict.

## 3. Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 4 :22** — *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending a full resilience index before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the per-slot shape and the refuter.
- **Fig 10 :42** — *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** — *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to observe if the common-cause claim were true before giving them the sources.
- **Fig 2 :39-40** — *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Selecting recipients by likely agreement manufactures the disqualifying correlation.
- **Fig 6 :24** — *"Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence."* Show where each claim came from. Do not present a borrowed statistic as a derivation.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the per-slot shape claim and refuter; (2) ask what the recipient would count as a failure; (3) record that verdict; (4) only then the source links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.
