# Union, bond and cascade order in a simultaneous harvest failure

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the distinction between a union and a partition. Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading.**

## RESIDUAL

The question asks "how resilient", which presupposes one object with one magnitude. The residual is instead a per-slot gap whose value depends on which conditions bind together. Two questions are owed: (1) is a simultaneous failure a union of overlapping conditions or a partition of separate ones, and (2) what is the dependency order of the couplings, so that a lever is applied at the link that actually breaks?

## DOMAIN CENSUS (supporting derivation)

Atoms available in the food-system frame: production, stock, trade route, supplier base, policy rule, reserve, information instrument, demand. Bonds available: the seven typed relations of Fig 12, of which four matter here.

- `+` conjunction: two conditions hold at once. Fig 12 :55-59 identifies it as *"both hold at once"*, symmetric, tested by swapping operands.
- `⋛` comparison: a threshold between two quantities. Fig 12 :85-89: **"a threshold relation between two quantities"**, tested by which is larger. Availability versus need; price versus purchasing power.
- `→` sequence: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"* (Fig 12 :70-74). Production shortfall precedes price response precedes policy response precedes outcome.
- `−` subtraction: *"A with B removed or absent"* (Fig 12 :78-83). A buffer is availability with a shock removed; where the arrangement is not recorded, the deriving must be paid again (Fig 12 :107-110).

No new atom is available without import. The import candidates are a genuinely different climate exposure, a different supplier base, a reserve with an independent trigger, and a source-discriminating instrument.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Union or partition? (decisive for order)

Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
Fig 9 :55-57: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**

A simultaneous failure that (a) hits an import-dependent region, (b) with a small supplier base, and (c) during an export-ban wave is a case in three conditions, not a harder case of any one. The intersection is categorically different: for that slot, adding storage does not fix the missing supplier, and adding suppliers does not fix the ban. Fig 9 :58-59 adds the second-order value: *"it stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound"* and the residue becomes describable. This is why the per-slot census in `01` is the right unit, and why the answer is a set of per-domain verdicts rather than a percentage.

**Falsifier:** if the three conditions could be cleanly separated so that each lever cleared its own disjoint set of regions with no overlap, the partition reading would win and a single aggregate index could be defended. World evidence rejects that separation (Kuhla et al. 2026: production concentration, import dependency and small supplier base coincide in the same high-risk regions), but the verdict is from Fig 9, not from the world.

### Q2 — What is the coupling bond? (structure)

Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**
Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**
Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Two food systems can hold the same ingredients, production, stocks, trade and reserves, and differ entirely in resilience because the bond differs. Consider two systems over the same atom set:

- System A: one climate mode drives all breadbaskets; reserves release when the world price crosses a fixed trigger; suppliers are the same few exporters.
- System B: breadbaskets load on different modes; reserves release on a local availability signal; suppliers do not share the shock.

Same atoms, different bonds, different substance. They are **isomers** under Fig 12 :117. Counting atoms (how many exporters, how many tonnes of stock) cannot tell them apart. That is why an input-count resilience index fails the figure's own test.

**Falsifier:** if the behaviour of A and B were identical across all slot shocks despite the different arrangements, the bond carries no information and the isomer claim fails; then only the atom count matters.

### Q3 — What is a functional group here?

Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

The substructure `production → stock → access` fixes the kind of thing a food system is. The qualifier, which crop, which region, which population, says which member. A "resilient food system" is not a member of a kind with a fixed value; it is a functional group instantiated many times, and each instance takes its own verdict. This is the structural reason a standing list of good policies fails Fig 11 :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

**Falsifier:** exhibit a substructure whose presence fixes the outcome in every slot regardless of the qualifier, including the small-supplier import-dependent case. If one exists, a standing prescription is legitimate and this finding falls.

### Q4 — Catalyst, cycle and the sign of the feedback

Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**
Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**

- A **reserve** is a catalyst only if it is replenished; a one-off stock is a spent part. Kuhla et al. 2026 note high stock-to-use ratios buffer risk but that storage is costly and poorly designed stockholding can crowd out private storage. The accounting must record which kind the reserve is; Fig 12 :119 says nothing records it by default.
- An **export ban** is a positive feedback: price spike triggers restriction, restriction removes supply, supply removal raises price. Kuhla et al. 2026: *"abrupt export bans can amplify international market stress and trigger cascading restrictions"*; Chen et al. 2024 cite the 2008/09 crisis. This loop closes.
- An **early-warning release** is a negative feedback: signal triggers release before the spike, release lowers the spike. Whether it closes depends on reliable data and timely implementation (Kuhla et al. 2026). Fig 12 :121 gives the test, not the answer.

**Falsifier:** show that a ban does not re-enter as an input to further restrictions (no loop), or that a reserve survives repeated use without replenishment (catalyst vs spent distinction empty). Then the feedback analysis collapses to a linear chain and lever order stops mattering.

### Q5 — Dependency order and the broken link

Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**
Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**
Fig 11 :52-54: **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**

Order: measure the yield anomaly (D1); translate through trade and stock into availability and price (D2); add the policy response (D3); read the household outcome (D4). The habitat must then be enumerated outward: fertilizer and energy input suppliers, feed, transport, storage, trade rules. A shock three steps out is the one that matters in import-dependent regions. The link most likely broken is between vocabulary and objective (Fig 3 :25-27): the field has yield and price terms but no settled objective instrument for the outcome slot, which is exactly what Béné et al. 2023 report.

**Falsifier:** a working instrument at D4 that is already used to choose the D1 to D3 levers, with published error rates. Then the chain is intact and the "nothing below the break" warning is inapplicable.

## CONCLUSION

The simultaneous-failure question is a **union** of overlapping conditions (Fig 9), not a partition. Food systems over the same atom set but with different couplings are **isomers** (Fig 12 :117), so an atom-count index cannot see the difference. The functional group `production → stock → access` fixes the kind; the crop, region and population qualify the member (Fig 12 :123). Reserves are catalysts only if replenished, bans are positive feedback cycles, and early warning is a negative feedback whose closure is empirical (Fig 12 :119, :121). The chain is ordered production to trade to policy to outcome, the habitat extends outward to inputs and rules, and the broken link is the outcome instrument (Fig 3, Fig 11). Each claim has a falsifier above.
