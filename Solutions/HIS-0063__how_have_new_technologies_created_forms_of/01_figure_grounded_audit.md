## 0. The residual in effect terms

The question is usually told as a chronicle: telescope, microscope, accelerator, detector, each one "revealing" something. A chronicle is a recording, and Fig 4 :25 warns what a recording is: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* So state the residual as an effect instead.

**R, stated in effect terms.** An instrument returns a reading with positive mass in some object slot. The theory held at the time has a library Γ whose closure cannot compose a term that predicts that reading. The reading is real contact with the ground, but the theory has no vocabulary that reaches it. The question asks for the mechanism of that mismatch: why does new technology produce evidence that older theory cannot easily interpret, and what would close the gap.

**What an answer would look like (the sought-for shape, bound as an ordinary term).** A shape with three parts: (i) an account of how an instrument extends what can be registered; (ii) an account of why the old closure cannot compose the new reading; (iii) a route that closes the gap without inventing a primitive. Binding this shape as a term lets it accrue a residual like anything else, which is the discipline `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction" asks for: *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM - write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."*

**Arity, symmetry, scale.** Arity: at least two conditions, an instrument returning something and a theory failing to compose it. Symmetry: asymmetric, the instrument changes contact while the theory is a frame inside its own closure; the direction runs from instrument to reading to failed composition, and more theory inside the closure does not reverse it. Scale: first order at perception, second order at the comparison between a theory and its evidence, and cross-scale where a method carries between levels.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - New technology extends representation, it does not add facts to the world (decisive for the first half)

Fig 6 :25-26:

> **"An import adds no atom to the world."**
> **"The world does not gain primitives; a frame gains access. Composition explores what the operators reach; instruments extend what can be represented at all."**

Fig 6 :32-33:

> **"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."**
> **"Nobody specified a telescope from an account of Mars. A wandering point in the sky was already a reading, and every sharper instrument was built on the residual the last one produced."**

Seat Map :403, from the appendix that carries the ordering rather than only the laws:

> **"IMPORT is the only operator that adds an atom; instrumentation is the only thing that extends what can be represented at all."**

This is the direct answer to "how have new technologies created forms of evidence." They extend what can be represented at all. They do not create new kinds in the world, and they are not built from a description of what they will find. The telescope was built on a wandering point that was already a reading. The cloud chamber was built on a fogged plate that was already an anomaly, and *They're Scanning Books* puts the historical point precisely: *"The great accidental discoveries, radioactivity from a fogged plate, the cosmic background mistaken for antenna noise, are the walk producing an unpredicted sample."*

**Mode.** `general` on the structural claim, `specified` when bound to any one instrument. The world source SEP confirms the instrument-inclusive turn independently (see F9).

**Refuter.** Show an instrument whose new reading is composable in the old closure with no residual, or show an instrument designed from a description alone with no prior reading returning something. Either breaks this finding.

**Triangulation.** Fig 6 :26 and Seat Map :403 are two documents with different purposes (the loop and the verification arrangement), so low overlap. Corroborated independently by SEP (Boyd and Bogen, 2026), which says *"scientific observation is rarely unaided perception and instead usually employs sophisticated instrumentation."* Result: **triangulated**.

### F2 - Why the older theory cannot interpret it: closure is idempotent (decisive for the second half)

Fig 8 :32:

> **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."**

Fig 6 :21-22:

> **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."**
> **"NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world. IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure."**

Fig 13 :115:

> **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."**

The older theory cannot easily interpret the new evidence for a structural reason, not a sociological one. Its closure can rearrange what it already holds but cannot add the atom the reading needs. More careful work inside the theory is more search at the same reach (Fig 6 :48-49), which *"Fails by spending budget on a region already swept."* This is why the historical pattern is not "scientists were slow." It is that the interpretive term was not in the library, and no amount of composition inside it would produce one.

The historical record fits the shape. On the electron: Schuster's 1890 charge-to-mass estimate *"produced a value that was more than a thousand times greater than what was expected, so little credence was given to his calculations at the time. This is because it was assumed that the charge carriers were much heavier hydrogen or nitrogen atoms"* (Wikipedia, *Electron*, 2026). The reading was returned; the old library had no light carrier to compose it with, so the anomaly was parked. On Jupiter's moons: *"a planet with smaller planets orbiting it did not conform to the principles of Aristotelian cosmology, which held that all heavenly bodies should circle the Earth, and many astronomers and philosophers initially refused to believe that Galileo could have discovered such a thing"* (Wikipedia, *Galileo Galilei*, 2026). And Clavius's observatory *"confirmed the observations and, although unsure how to interpret them, gave Galileo a hero's welcome"*, which is the residual in one sentence: confirmation at the ground, no vocabulary yet.

**Mode.** `general` for the closure law, `specified` for its binding to a given episode.

**Refuter.** Show closure is not idempotent (a composition inside a closed set that adds a new atom), or show a historical case where the old theory composed the new reading immediately and permanently with no new term and no import.

**Triangulation.** Fig 8 :32 plus Fig 6 :21-22 plus Fig 13 :115, three different figures with different offices (union surplus, closure, minimal set), low overlap. **Triangulated**.

### F3 - The break is at the middle link, and that link has no instrument

Fig 3 :25-27:

> **"Perception has obvious failures and obvious tests. Planning has obvious failures and obvious tests. The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."**
> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

This refines F2. The failure is not that the instrument did not register; D1 is working. It is that the vocabulary link between the reading and what it affords has no instrument of its own. Fig 3 :22-23 adds the warning: *"A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can. Which is why a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing."* A historian who demands interpretation before the vocabulary exists is taking a reading below the break.

The observer's defect is a second, named failure at this link. Seat Map :275: **"The observer's defect. The instrument itself carrying the genus it was built to catch. A new instrument's first output is a claim about the instrument, not about the system, and a known-positive fixture proves sensitivity, never specificity."** So a new reading may be an artifact, and the first check is on the instrument, not on the theory. Schuster's estimate was *"largely correct"* but given no credence; conversely, Martin Horky took doubled stars through the telescope as *"evidence that the instrument was deceptive when viewing the heavens"* and so cast doubt on the moons (Wikipedia, *Galileo Galilei*, 2026). Both errors live at the instrument-to-vocabulary link.

**Refuter.** Show a case where the middle link already had a calibrated instrument and an agreed vocabulary before the new evidence arrived, so that the break was elsewhere.

**Status.** **Triangulated**: Fig 3 :27 and Seat Map :275 are different documents naming different failures at the same link.

### F4 - The gap is a union compound, and it must be unbundled before search

Fig 9 :5-6 and :57:

> **"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."**
> **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."**

The two conditions are: the instrument returns a reading (D1), and the theory cannot compose it (D2). A single lever clears the region where only one holds. Sharpening an instrument that already registers does not give the theory a term; adding a term that cannot be settled by a reading does not produce evidence. Fig 9 :55-56: *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."* The residue after unbundling is smaller and categorically different, and *"the residue is finally describable"* (Fig 9 :59).

**Refuter.** A case where one lever (either more instrument or more theory alone) closes both conditions at once, with the record to show it.

**Status.** `general`, **triangulated** with Fig 9 :5 and Fig 9 :57 in the same figure is not two witnesses, so mark **single-source** on the figure and corroborate with the Seat Map's own union rule at :211: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either; it stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."* With that, **triangulated**.

### F5 - The two remedies are opposed tracks: INWARD and OUTWARD

Fig 6 :28-31:

> **"Which makes reachability two questions, not one. The instruments are opposed."**
> **"inward: is our representation adequate to hold it? The instrument extends access."**
> **"outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels."**
> **"Neither answer tells you much about the other. Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other."**

Fig 6 :34-35 and :40:

> **"What is unavailable is a sensor for something that has never registered anywhere, at any resolution. That is the whole of it."**

INWARD sharpens an instrument that is already returning something; OUTWARD imports a frame whose closure predicts the residual's shape. Fig 8 :27-28 gives the order for OUTWARD: **"describe R, then find the frame that already predicts its shape."** Fig 9 :27-28 gives the reason: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."**

The history supplies clean instances of both tracks. The cloud chamber is INWARD: Wilson *"began to develop expansion chambers for studying cloud formation and optical phenomena in moist air. Very rapidly he discovered that ions could act as centers for water droplet formation in such chambers"*, and the instrument went on to reveal the positron and the muon (Wikipedia, *Cloud chamber*, 2026). The positron is also OUTWARD in its origin, because Dirac's theoretical proof preceded the chamber observation: *"This method was also used to prove the existence of the positron in 1932, in accordance with Paul Dirac's theoretical proof, published in 1928"* (same source). Both tracks are real and must be kept separate.

**Refuter.** Show that the outward description can replace the inward instrument for a case where nothing has ever registered, or that inward sharpening can supply a missing atom. Formula step 7 forbids both, so either would break the framework, not just this reading.

**Status.** **Triangulated** across Fig 6 :28-31 and Formula step 7, with independent world corroboration.

### F6 - Strain and re-census: the direction can reverse

Fig 6 :37-40:

> **"It tells a question that nothing could ever answer apart from one that is merely hard. It is not a rule about theory."**
> **"theory routinely precedes its instrument, and is welcome to. Neptune was calculated before it was seen, and the prediction is what told anyone where to point"**

At first reading, F1 (instruments extend representation) strains against this line (theory precedes instrument). The strain test says: if a line only fits with a strain, it governs another quantity, so re-census. Re-census resolves it. Fig 6 :26 governs **representation and reach** (what can be registered at all). Fig 6 :38 governs **temporal order of discovery** (which comes first, the prediction or the detector). Different quantities, so no strain after re-census. The honest claim is therefore not "technology always comes first." It is:

- sometimes a new instrument extends representation and produces a reading the old theory cannot compose (Galileo's moons, the electron, the cloud chamber);
- sometimes a theory points the instrument at a reading it already predicts (Neptune, Dirac's positron, gravitational waves predicted by Einstein and detected by LIGO in 2015).

Fig 6 :39 allows a jump: *"instruments do not have to improve by small steps. A jump in resolution is fine"*; what is forbidden is a sensor for something that has never registered anywhere.

**Mode.** `specified` for each historical direction, `general` for the two-track structure.

**Status.** After re-census, **no hidden strain**. The reverse-direction claim is **single-source** on the figure (Fig 6 :38) with independent world corroboration from the gravitational-wave and cloud-chamber sources.

Fig 13 :133-135:

> **"reached at all. A system with a closure can answer that before it searches, and what it returns is a shape rather than a name."**
> **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"**
> **"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."**

Fig 9 :32 and :42:

So the answer to a history question of this kind is not a list of great instruments and not a name for the missing concept. It is the narrowed shape of the relation: a union compound at the perception-to-vocabulary link, remedied inward or outward, settled only at the ground. Fig 8 :39: **"a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** A period in which no theory could interpret a reading is a verdict of unreachability at that library, not silence and not a proof that interpretation is impossible.

**Refuter.** Produce a framework output that names the missing primitive and is then settled by the ground without any new term being minted or imported. By Fig 13 :135 that should be impossible; if it happens, this finding is wrong.

**Status.** **Triangulated** across Fig 13 :133-135 and Fig 9 :32, plus the canonical README's *"a shape, not a name"*.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach, Seat Map :287: **"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."**

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. An instrument is exhibited whose new reading is composed by the prior theory with no residual and no new term, in a case where the prior theory had not already predicted it. This breaks F1 and F2.
2. Closure is shown to be non-idempotent, that is, composition inside a closed set produces a new atom. This breaks F2 and the barrier-theorem reading.
3. The perception-to-vocabulary link is shown to have possessed a calibrated instrument and agreed vocabulary in a case where the reading still could not be interpreted. This breaks F3.
4. A historical case is documented in which a single lever, either instrument refinement or theory alone, closed both conditions at once. This breaks F4.
5. The framework is shown to name a missing primitive that the ground then settles without any mint or import. This breaks F7.

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with F1 and F2 as a single mechanism: instruments extend representation; closure cannot compose what it does not hold. That is the answer to the question in effect terms.
2. Keep the two directions separate (F6). Do not claim technology always precedes theory; the record says otherwise.
3. Unbundle the compound before searching for further examples (F4), and describe the effect before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28). Otherwise any episode will seem to fit.
4. State the mode on every sentence (F8). A historical pattern is a `specified` structure, not a `grounded` verdict from this derivation.
5. Send the refuter with the claim, and ask for a failure rather than agreement (Fig 10 :52, Seat Map :287).
6. Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** Send the derivation and the refusal condition, not a chronicle of famous instruments.

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is the mismatch one question?** No, it is a union of two conditions (Fig 9 :57), so unbundle before search.
2. **What does the instrument do?** It extends what can be represented at all (Fig 6 :26), built from a worse instrument already returning something (Fig 6 :32).
3. **Why can the theory not compose it?** Closure is idempotent (Fig 8 :32); composition cannot add an atom (Fig 6 :21).
4. **Where is the break?** The perception-to-vocabulary link, which has no instrument (Fig 3 :27), plus the observer's defect (Seat Map :275).
5. **What closes it?** INWARD or OUTWARD as opposed tracks (Fig 6 :28-31), after SEARCHED first (Formula step 7).
6. **What is settled?** Only the ground settles a term (Fig 9 :42). This derivation settles nothing; it characterizes the shape.
7. **What is owed?** A shape, a refuter, and provenance (Fig 13 :133, Seat Map :287), not a name and not a list of famous instruments.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives: new technology extends the representable, the older theory's closure cannot add the atom that would compose the new reading, the break sits at the perception-to-vocabulary link, and the two remedies (sharpen the instrument, import a frame) are opposed and neither settles the case without the ground. The reverse direction also survives: theory can precede its instrument. Strained after re-census: **none**, with one earlier strain dissolved at F6 and one naive reading retracted above.
