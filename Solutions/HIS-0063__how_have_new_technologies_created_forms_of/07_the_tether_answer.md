# The Tether's answer: new technology and uninterpretable evidence

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive mechanistic claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a historical verdict. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §8), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

An instrument returns a reading the holding theory cannot compose. Old theory digests facts; new instruments return readings. The question asks why the second so often outruns the first. In effect terms the mismatch is a **union compound of two conditions** (Fig 9 :57, *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*): a reading is returned, and the theory lacks a term that composes it. It is not one hard problem and not a failure of attention. Fig 9 :5 says such disagreements *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*

## 2. The shape

1. **New technology extends representation; it does not add atoms to the world.** Fig 6 :26: *"instruments extend what can be represented at all"*; Fig 6 :25: *"An import adds no atom to the world."* An instrument is built from a worse instrument already returning something (Fig 6 :32), and the telescope was not specified from an account of Mars (Fig 6 :33). This is the "how" of the first half of the question.
2. **The older theory cannot interpret the evidence because its closure is idempotent.** Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."* The interpretive term is not in the library, and no amount of work inside it manufactures one. The electron episode shows the shape: a charge-to-mass estimate a thousand times too large was parked because the library held only heavy carriers.
3. **The break is at the perception-to-vocabulary link, not at the measurement and not at the final theory.** Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Clavius's observatory confirmed the Jovian moons while being *"unsure how to interpret them"*: ground contact without vocabulary, in one sentence.
4. **A reading does not determine its source.** From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: the instrument reads green but *"cannot comment on the source"*; *"It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."* So a period of non-interpretation is the state before a discriminating instrument exists, which is why naming the missing concept from the reading alone is premature.
5. **There are two directions, and they select different first questions.** Instrument-first (`instrument → reading ⇒ term owed`) runs SEARCHED then OUTWARD. Theory-first (`term → instrument → reading`) runs INWARD. Fig 6 :38: *"theory routinely precedes its instrument, and is welcome to."* They are isomers over a shared atom set (see `06_isomers_and_order.md`), not two versions of one story. They must not be averaged.
6. **The two remedies are opposed tracks.** Fig 6 :31: *"Neither answer tells you much about the other. Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other."* INWARD sharpens an instrument already returning something; OUTWARD imports a frame whose closure predicts the residual's shape. Fig 8 :27-28: *"describe R, then find the frame that already predicts its shape."*
7. **The framework returns a shape, not a name.** Fig 13 :133: *"what it returns is a shape rather than a name."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."* The shape narrows; the ground settles. Fig 9 :42: *"narrowing gets you close, the ground decides."*

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that technology always comes first. Theory routinely precedes its instrument (Fig 6 :38), and both directions are real.
- Not that any period of inability to interpret was irrational, or that historians were slow. The reason is structural (Fig 8 :32), and the observer's defect (Seat Map :275) means the first check is often on the instrument, not the theory.
- Not a name for the concept that resolved any particular episode. That is owed by the historical record, not by this derivation.
- Not that every new reading is genuine evidence. A new instrument's first output is a claim about the instrument, and sensitivity is not specificity (Seat Map :275).
- Not a general law that instruments can be built for anything. Fig 6 :40: *"What is unavailable is a sensor for something that has never registered anywhere, at any resolution."*
- Not `grounded`. No reading was taken by this derivation, and no historical case was settled here.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A case record, not a further argument. For any episode, the settling items are:

- the reading, with date and instrument, and whether something was already registering before the instrument was sharpened (Fig 6 :32-35);
- the holding library at the time, stated so that closure's inability to compose the term is checkable rather than asserted (Fig 8 :32);
- the route that closed the gap, with provenance: a mint settled by the ground, or an import with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :12-13, Fig 8 :51-52);
- an instrument that discriminates among the sources of the reading, since a reading alone cannot (Hard Problem is About Source).

Standing falsifiers from `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4: a reading the prior theory composes immediately with no residual; a non-idempotent closure; a calibrated vocabulary link that still fails; one lever closing both conditions; a missing primitive named and then settled with no mint or import.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The figure edge is real: *"What is unavailable is a sensor for something that has never registered anywhere, at any resolution. That is the whole of it"* (Fig 6 :40). The five essays' agreement with the figures is one evidence pool, not corroboration. The five independent world sources corroborate the shape but do not settle it, because this derivation did not take a reading. The answer stops where the available instruments and records stop, and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity"* (figures README).

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a chronicle of great instruments and does not claim to name the concept that resolved any historical puzzle. It says that a new instrument extends what can be registered at all, while an older theory's closure can only rearrange what it already holds, so the new reading has no term to compose it. The break sits between having the reading and having vocabulary for it, a link the figures say has no instrument of its own. There are two real directions, instrument-first and theory-first, and they need different first moves: find a frame that already holds the term, or sharpen an instrument already returning something. The framework names neither the missing concept nor a final theory. It offers the shape any answer must have, four refuters written in advance, and the kind of record that would close it. The case stays open until the ground settles it.
