# Two directions, one compound: order and the isomer question

**Mode: `specified` on the historical binding, `general` on the ordering shape.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §8).

## RESIDUAL

The audit found two real historical directions: instrument-first (a reading arrives, then vocabulary is owed) and theory-first (a term predicts, then the instrument is pointed and a reading settles it). Two questions follow. (1) Can both be written over the same atoms with the same bonds, so they are **isomers** under Fig 12's definition? (2) What is the order of the compound that contains both, and which link depends on which?

Bonds available: the seven, of which three matter here. → sequence (*"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*, Fig 12 :72); ⇒ production (*"A produces B; B did not exist before"*, Fig 12 :77); ⋛ comparison (*"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, Fig 12 :87). Negation ¬ is available for a refusal, but it is not a bond (Fig 12 :134-136).

This derivation is an ordering of **loop states**, not a composition of the six physical terms. Applying Fig 12's isomer definition here is an analogue at the level of arrangements of operations, not a claim about physical substances. That boundary is kept visible; forcing it would be the strain the strain test forbids.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - The two directions as ordered molecules

**Direction A, instrument-first.**

```
instrument → reading  ⇒  (term owed)
```

The instrument produces a reading (⇒, since the reading did not exist before the instrument was pointed). The reading precedes the term (→, since the term needs the reading to have happened). Galileo's moons fit: the telescope returned moving points, and only then was vocabulary owed; Clavius's observatory *"confirmed the observations and, although unsure how to interpret them"* (Wikipedia, *Galileo Galilei*, 2026). The electron case fits: the 1890 charge-to-mass reading preceded the light-carrier term, and was parked because *"it was assumed that the charge carriers were much heavier hydrogen or nitrogen atoms"* (Wikipedia, *Electron*, 2026).

**Direction B, theory-first.**

```
term → instrument → reading   (settlement)
```

The term precedes the pointing (→), and the instrument produces the reading that settles it (⇒). Fig 6 :38 governs this directly: *"theory routinely precedes its instrument, and is welcome to. Neptune was calculated before it was seen, and the prediction is what told anyone where to point."* The cloud chamber source gives the matching case: the positron observation in 1932 was *"in accordance with Paul Dirac's theoretical proof, published in 1928"* (Wikipedia, *Cloud chamber*, 2026). Gravitational waves give a second: predicted from general relativity, detected by LIGO in 2015 (Wikipedia, *Gravitational wave*, 2026).

**Both use all five atoms.** Direction A needs a recognizer to treat the fogged plate or the moving points as anomalous. Direction B needs a recognizer to accept the predicted reading as a settlement rather than noise. Neither direction can drop an atom the other requires.

### Q2 - Are they isomers?

Fig 12 :117:

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fig 12 :123:

> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

Over the shared atom set `{reading, term, instrument, ground, recognizer}`, the two directions are **isomers in the figure's sense**: same atoms, different bond placement, different behaviour in time. The difference is decisive. In direction A an unexpected reading drives the search and the term is owed by the residual; in direction B the term drives the pointing and the reading is owed by the prediction. Both would look identical in a flat list that recorded only "instrument, theory, evidence," which is exactly the flat-formula collapse Fig 12 :14-16 names: *"the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not"* and *"nothing downstream can tell them apart"*.

The weaker claim is that they share a **functional group**: both are `term ⋛ reading` at some level, with the qualifier saying which direction ran. I keep the stronger isomer claim because the falsifier below is checkable, and record the weaker one as the fallback.

### Q3 - What is the order of the union?

Fig 12 :104:

> **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"**

A reading settled against the ground becomes an atom at the next level. A term settled the same way does too. So the compound *"a new technology produced evidence an older theory could not interpret"* is **second order**: it composes two first-order directions that have each settled, and it needs no new primitive. Fig 6 :26 still governs what it costs: *"instruments extend what can be represented at all"*, which adds no atom to the world (Fig 6 :25).

The comparison bond ⋛ between the two directions is a **threshold relation between two quantities** (Fig 12 :87). At second order the operands are settled molecules now serving as atoms, so the type question is the same one the exemplar named and left open: whether a settled molecule counts as a quantity at that level needs an instrument, not an argument. Recorded as a strain, not smoothed.

**Dependency order (Fig 3).** Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* The order for this compound is:

1. perception (a reading registered),
2. vocabulary (a term that composes it or predicted it),
3. objective (what would count as interpreting it),
4. planning (which instrument to sharpen or which frame to import).

SECOND-ORDER COMPARISON sits above all four and consumes them. Effort spent at step 4 before steps 1 and 2 arrive is the Fig 3 :19 failure: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* More precisely here, effort at step 4 before step 2 arrives is effort on a link whose input never arrived, which Fig 3 :22-23 says *"is a reading of nothing."*

### Q4 - Which of the three step-7 questions applies to each direction?

Formula step 7 separates SEARCHED, INWARD and OUTWARD, and asks SEARCHED first. Applied in order:

- **Direction A (instrument-first).** The reading exists, so SEARCHED can run: is the composing term already in the library and unfound? Schuster's parked estimate is the case where it was not; the term (a light charge carrier) had to be imported from a frame whose closure held it. That is OUTWARD, with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :12-13).
- **Direction B (theory-first).** The term exists, so the reading is sought. INWARD applies: sharpen an instrument already returning something. Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* LIGO is the clean case: a predicted strain of less than one part in 10^20 required a jump in resolution, which Fig 6 :39 permits (*"instruments do not have to improve by small steps. A jump in resolution is fine"*), provided something was already registering.

The two directions therefore select **different first questions**. That is the practical value of keeping them separate: a direction-A case answered with "build a better detector" and a direction-B case answered with "find a new concept" both spend budget on the wrong instrument.

## FALSIFIER, WRITTEN BEFORE THE RUN

1. If direction A can be written without a recognizer (the reading interpreted itself), or direction B without a ground (the prediction settled itself), the shared-atom set is wrong and the isomer claim fails; the functional-group reading stands instead.
2. If a case is found where the two directions genuinely share all five atoms but produce the same behaviour in time (same order, same settlement conditions), they are not isomers under Fig 12 :117; they are one direction described twice.
3. If a direction is shown to require a sixth atom that the other lacks, the isomer claim fails by the same test the exemplar used for its own isomer claim.
4. If direction B is shown to be impossible (no theory ever preceded its instrument), Fig 6 :38 is wrong and Q4's split collapses.

## CONCLUSION

The two historical directions are isomers over the shared set `{reading, term, instrument, ground, recognizer}`: same atoms, different bond placement, decisive difference in behaviour. The compound question is second order, composed from settled first-order directions, and costs no new primitive. The dependency order runs perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, with the second-order comparison above all four. The two directions select different first questions under Formula step 7, which is why they must never be averaged into a single story of "technology drives theory" or its reverse. One strain remains live and is named: the operand type of ⋛ at second order.
