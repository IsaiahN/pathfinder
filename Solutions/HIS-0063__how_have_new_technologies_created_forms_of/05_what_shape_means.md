## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README gives the limit: the framework returns *"a shape, not a name."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :133: *"what it returns is a shape rather than a name."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."* And on how the vocabulary is bootstrapped: *"If you don't know the right terms to search for, you will have to use the existing vocabulary you do know that is closest to the words that you wish to describe and grasp the shape of."*

For this question a **shape** is therefore the narrowed form of the mechanism you can state before you can name the missing interpretive concept. It constrains; it does not fill. Examples:

- A shape: "an instrument extends what can be registered, and the old closure cannot compose the reading, so the break sits between perception and vocabulary."
- A name: "the missing concept was X, and Galileo should have used it."

The first is checkable against a record. The second pretends to a settlement the derivation did not perform.

## 2. How a shape is obtained here

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. The essay describes the search as bootstrapping: each new term narrows the space and deepens the vocabulary.
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."*
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."*
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: *"you will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place, as there is nowhere else to search and nothing else that can describe the answer better."* The paradox is that by then you have composed the answer through the search.

Applied here: the effect description is the two-condition compound (a reading is returned, and no term composes it), not the names of particular instruments. That is why `01` begins with the residual and census rather than a chronicle.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays."* The README: the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."*
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :51-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*They're Scanning Books* sharpens the second: *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space. Importing a term extends what can be said; importing an axis extends what can be perceived at all."* And: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible, which is worth a great deal, because a visible choice can be revised and an emergent one cannot."*

So naming the interpretive concept that resolved a historical episode is licensed only by the settled record of that episode, or by a provenanced import from the frame that supplied it. A narrative that says "instrument X revealed concept Y" is doing a provenance move; unless the record is shown, it is an unmarked import.

## 4. The source reading: the essay most directly on point for this question

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism of "evidence the old theory cannot interpret" in instrument terms. Metamerism: an instrument reads green, and *"the instrument is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source."* Several underlying causes can create one reading: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* The full set of ways to produce a reading is not closed: *"The full set of ways to produce a reading is not known to be closed or determined."*

The crucial sentence for a history question is the closing: *"It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."* Earlier: *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* And: *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it."*

This is Fig 6 :32 (*"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something"*) told from the instrument side. It also supplies the honest stop: a period in which the old theory could not interpret the reading is not a failure of reasoning, it is the state before a discriminating instrument exists. The reading determines neither the source nor the holding, which is why a name is premature.

**Correction to my own §3 above (kept visible).** I initially wrote that shape-not-a-name forbids naming any missing concept. That overstates it. The README's phrase governs a **missing** thing, one not held in Γ. The essays make the same point at the representation level: the limit is on axes no primitive captures, not on selecting among held terms. For this question the missing interpretive concept really is missing from the old theory's library, so the limit applies. But the rule should be stated as "the framework returns a shape for a primitive not held," not as a blanket ban on naming. Both statements are recorded so neither is quietly lost.

## 5. Instruments extend the sensory window, not the conceptual one

*They're Scanning Books* adds the qualification the history needs: *"Every instrument is a crystallized hypothesis about what is worth measuring, and the hypothesis comes from human awareness. Instruments extend the sensory window but not the conceptual one, they reach what we cannot see but can imagine measuring."* And: *"Change the sensors, and not just the data changes, the shape of the compression changes: the categories, the primitives, what counts as similar to what."*

This triangulates Fig 6 :26 (*"instruments extend what can be represented at all"*) with an important boundary: an instrument can reach what we cannot see but can imagine measuring, not what we have no primitive for at all. Fig 6 :40: *"What is unavailable is a sensor for something that has never registered anywhere, at any resolution."* So "new technology creates new evidence" is a `general` shape with a stated edge, not a claim that any instrument can be designed from nothing.

The same essay supplies the two historical directions used in `06`:

- *"The great accidental discoveries, radioactivity from a fogged plate, the cosmic background mistaken for antenna noise, are the walk producing an unpredicted sample."*
- *"Radioactivity was not discovered when the plate fogged; it was discovered when someone recognized the fogging as anomalous and did not stop."*
- *"Discovery under consequence = a random walk plus a model that can be surprised."*

That is Fig 6 :32 and Fig 3 :27 in narrative form: the reading exists before the vocabulary, and the recognizer is what turns noise into evidence.

## 6. Contact and effect terms

*Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"a measurement with no question is noise, and a reading with no encounter is projection."* It also quotes Fig 6 :30 (*"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms"*) and Fig 2 :45 (*"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it"*). For outreach this means the first contact must carry the residual and its refuter, not the framework's interior. That is the whole design of `02_outreach_email.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md`.

## 7. Prestige is not action

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly."* And: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."*

A list of famous instruments or famous historians cannot settle this shape. What can settle it is a case record: the reading, the date, the theory's closing, and the import that eventually composed it.

## 8. My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

Same essay: *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself. An external validator, or ground, is always needed to tether the veracity of the output to reality."* And: *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."*

So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of the historical claim and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on.

## 9. Answer for this question

**"Shape" here means the checkable form of the instrument-to-theory mismatch, stated before the missing interpretive concept is named.** That form has three parts: an instrument extends what can be registered (Fig 6 :26); the older theory's closure cannot add the atom that would compose the reading (Fig 8 :32); and the break sits at the perception-to-vocabulary link, where there is no instrument of its own (Fig 3 :27). The two remedies, sharpen the instrument or import a frame, are opposed tracks (Fig 6 :28-31). The framework returns that much and stops. A name for what resolved any particular episode is owed by the historical record, not by the derivation.
