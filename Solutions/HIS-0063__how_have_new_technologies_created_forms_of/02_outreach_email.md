# Outreach draft: check the shape before you decide about the history

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the mode labels and the refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are intact. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one claim about instruments and evidence before you decide whether the framing is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific shape I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with me. If the shape does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** when a new instrument returns a reading that an older theory cannot interpret, the mismatch has a describable shape. The instrument extends what can be registered at all; the older theory's closure can rearrange what it already holds but cannot add the term the reading needs. The break is not at the measurement and not at the eventual theory. It sits at the link between having a reading and having vocabulary for what it affords, a link the figures say has no instrument of its own. So the case is a compound of two conditions, not one hard problem.
>
> **What this does not say:** it does not say technology always comes first. Theory routinely precedes its instrument, and the prediction then tells people where to point the detector. It does not name which missing concept any historical case needed. It returns a shape, not a name. It is not a claim that any period of perplexity was irrational.
>
> **What would refute it:**
> 1. an instrument whose new reading the prior theory composed immediately with no residual, where the theory had not already predicted it;
> 2. a demonstration that a closed theoretical library can add a new primitive by composition alone;
> 3. a case where the perception-to-vocabulary link already had a calibrated instrument and agreed vocabulary, and the reading still could not be interpreted;
> 4. a single lever, instrument or theory alone, that closed both conditions at once.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, I withdraw the shape. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract, branch `general-agent`): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in the project folder.
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Full architecture (Medium): https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
> - Worked essay on instruments and sources (Substack): https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/the-hard-problem-is-about-source
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a case you know well and can check against a record. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a frame cannot check the direction of its own question, so a written refuter is the only thing that makes the result checkable. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Regards, [name]
