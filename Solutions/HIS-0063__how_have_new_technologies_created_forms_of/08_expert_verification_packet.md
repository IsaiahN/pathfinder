# Expert verification packet: new technology and uninterpretable evidence

**What this is:** a claim in a form a historian of science, philosopher of science or historian of technology can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the mechanistic claim, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No historical case is claimed to be settled by this packet. It offers a shape and a route.

**Status of this packet: a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.** It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

When a new instrument returns a reading that a holding theory cannot interpret, the mismatch has a checkable shape. The instrument extends what can be registered at all; the theory's closure can rearrange what it holds but cannot add the term the reading needs. The break is not at the measurement and not at the eventual theory; it sits at the link between having a reading and having vocabulary for what it affords. The case is a compound of two conditions, not one hard problem. There are two directions, instrument-first and theory-first, and they select different first moves. The framework returns that shape and stops; a name for the missing concept is owed by the historical record.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal plus history-facing; checkable by reading Fig 6 and a case record

- **Statement:** instrumentation extends what can be represented at all, and an instrument is built from a worse instrument already returning something.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :26, *"The world does not gain primitives; a frame gains access. Composition explores what the operators reach; instruments extend what can be represented at all."* Fig 6 :32, *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* Fig 6 :33, *"Nobody specified a telescope from an account of Mars. A wandering point in the sky was already a reading, and every sharper instrument was built on the residual the last one produced."* Corroborated independently by Boyd and Bogen, SEP 2026: *"scientific observation is rarely unaided perception and instead usually employs sophisticated instrumentation."*
- **What to check:** in a case you know, was there a reading already returning something before the instrument was sharpened, or was the instrument designed from a description with no prior reading?
- **Refuter:** exhibit an instrument whose new reading the prior theory composed immediately with no residual and no new term, where the theory had not predicted it; or exhibit an instrument built from a description alone with no prior reading returning something.

### C2 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 8 and Figure 3

- **Statement:** the old theory cannot interpret the reading because closure is idempotent, and the break sits at the perception-to-vocabulary link.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :32, *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."* Fig 3 :27, *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Seat Map :275, *"The observer's defect. The instrument itself carrying the genus it was built to catch. A new instrument's first output is a claim about the instrument, not about the system, and a known-positive fixture proves sensitivity, never specificity."*
- **What to check:** in the case, was the interpretive term genuinely absent from the holding library, so that more work inside it could not produce the term? Was the first check on the instrument (artifact versus signal), or was the theory blamed first?
- **Refuter:** show closure is not idempotent; or show a case where the perception-to-vocabulary link already had a calibrated instrument and agreed vocabulary and the reading still could not be interpreted; or show one lever (instrument or theory alone) closing both conditions at once.

### C3 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 6 and Figure 12

- **Statement:** the two historical directions are isomers over a shared atom set, and they select different first questions; the compound is second order and needs no new primitive.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :38, *"theory routinely precedes its instrument, and is welcome to. Neptune was calculated before it was seen, and the prediction is what told anyone where to point."* Fig 12 :117, *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :104, *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"*
- **What to check:** can instrument-first and theory-first both be written over `{reading, term, instrument, ground, recognizer}` with different bond placement? Does dropping the recognizer from either direction break it?
- **Refuter:** show one direction requires an atom the other lacks (which kills the isomer claim and leaves the functional-group claim of Fig 12 :123 standing); or show a case where the two directions produce identical behaviour in time; or show no theory ever preceded its instrument.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a chronicle of famous instruments and not a name for the concept that resolved any case. Fig 13 :133: *"what it returns is a shape rather than a name."*
- Not that technology always comes first. Fig 6 :38 allows the reverse direction; both are in the packet.
- Not that a period of non-interpretation was irrational. The reason is structural, Seat Map :275 adds a separate instrument-level failure.
- Not that every new reading is evidence. Sensitivity is not specificity (Seat Map :275).
- Not that instruments can be built for anything. Fig 6 :40: *"What is unavailable is a sensor for something that has never registered anywhere, at any resolution."*
- Not `grounded`. No measurement is claimed here.
- Not corroboration from the essays. They share an author with the figures and are one evidence pool.

## 5. What would settle it

A case record, not an argument:

1. The reading with date and instrument, and whether something was already registering before sharpening (Fig 6 :32-35).
2. The holding library at the time, stated so the absence of the composing term is checkable (Fig 8 :32).
3. The route that closed the gap, with provenance: a mint settled by the ground, or an import with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :12-13, Fig 8 :51-52).
4. An instrument that discriminates among the sources of the reading, since the reading alone cannot (Hard Problem is About Source).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 6 :25-26, :32-33, :38-40 and Fig 8 :32, Fig 3 :27. Five short passages.
2. Pick one case you know well. Say which direction it follows: did the reading come first, or the prediction?
3. Name the term that was absent from the holding theory, and say whether it was minted from the ground or imported from another frame.
4. Try one refuter from §2. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
5. Return a one-line correction on shape if the fit is wrong. No reply is needed if you would rather not engage.
