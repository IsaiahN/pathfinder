# Solution HIS-0063

**Question:** How have new technologies created forms of evidence that older theories could not easily interpret?

**Source:** history of science and knowledge.txt line 78

**Section:** 7. Methods, technologies, and ways of knowing

**Mode:** `specified` on the substantive mechanistic claims, `general` on the structural shape. Nothing here is `grounded`. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading, so it cannot settle the question it reports on.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

## Question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not which instruments were important, but what happens at the contact between a new instrument and an older theory. Option A: new technology simply adds facts that the old theory slowly digests. Option B: new technology extends what can be registered and represented, producing a reading the old theory cannot compose because its closure cannot add the atom the reading needs. What each leads to: A makes interpretation a matter of time and goodwill, so it predicts no structural limit and no refuter; B predicts a specifiable shape (a union compound at the perception-to-vocabulary link), a named refuter, and an honest stop until the ground settles it. An answer would look like that shape plus the route to closing it, so this answer binds that shape as an ordinary term and tests it rather than treating it as an unexamined preference.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to believe the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the project's own words:

1. **A room does not upgrade the label.** Seat Map :283: *"A room does not upgrade the label. A room reaching a conclusion without the ground is agreement, and agreement among frames is a weighted average of influence. Adding people raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor."*
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** Seat Map :163: *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that makes their agreement worthless.
3. **Asking people to trust before they check spends their independence.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Seat 4 "must not hold the interiors" (Seat Map, Seat 4).

Legally, scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one.
