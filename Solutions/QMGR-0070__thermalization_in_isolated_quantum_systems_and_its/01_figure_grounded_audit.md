## 0. The question in effect terms

Question: thermalization in isolated quantum systems and its relationship to black hole dynamics.

- **Option A:** assert a direct identity, that a black hole is simply the fastest thermalizing isolated quantum system and the two are one mechanism. This yields a strong-sounding sentence and no checkable residual, because the anchor at the quantum-gravity scale is not named and the difference between the two domains is averaged away.
- **Option B:** treat the question as a union compound of two domains with different closures and different anchors, derive the shape of their relation, keep the verdicts separate, and leave the slot open where no instrument resolves it. This yields a checkable shape, a stated refuter, and an honest gap.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run: a cross-kind composition shared by both domains, with each domain's ground status stated separately, and a comparison at a second order rather than a merged primitive. This is the sought-for shape made refusable, as `THE_FORMULA.md` Direction requires.

## 1. Residual, then domain census

**Residual R, in effect terms.** Two contact failures are bundled in the question. (i) A closed quantum system evolves unitarily, which is time reversible, yet a generic isolated many-body system approaches thermal equilibrium with no external bath. (ii) A black hole is expected to evolve unitarily, yet semiclassical gravity gives thermal Hawking radiation and an information problem. The residual is the gap between each fine-grained description and its thermal coarse-grained description, and the further gap between the two frames that describe them.

- **Arity:** two conditions, so a union compound, not one hard question. Fig 9 :55 **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** Fig 9 :32 **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**.
- **Symmetry:** the flow from an initial state to equilibrium is directed, while the underlying law is time symmetric. The relevant structure is a gradient, a difference that can be spent, not a mere ordering.
- **Scale:** first order inside each domain (Hilbert space and spectrum for the many-body system, horizon and entropy for the black hole), second order at the relation between them.

**Partition or union.** Domains 1 and 2 are a union with an overlap in domain 3. They are not a partition: a single lever that clears thermalization alone leaves the black-hole information condition standing, and vice versa. Fig 9 :55 applies. The overlap is a compound with its own loop, so it is kept as domain 3 rather than hidden.

**Dependency order (Fig 3).** Settle domain 1 first, because its anchor is visible. Then name the anchor gap in domain 2. Only then attempt the second-order relation (Fig 12 :104), and only after that prepare outreach. Fig 3 :27 **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** Effort spent on the relation before the anchors are sorted lands above the break and cannot be used.

## 3. Findings F1 to F10

### F1: The question is a union compound, not one question (decisive for method)

Fig 9 :55 **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** Fig 9 :6 **"the verdict: is φ* in here at all?"**

The two conditions are not one hard problem. A single lever that explains why a closed non-integrable gas thermalizes does not by itself resolve whether information escapes a black hole, because the second condition adds a horizon and an anchor problem. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged. Refuter: a demonstration that both failures are the same condition under one predicate, with the same anchor, such that clearing one clears the other.

### F2: The cross-kind rule governs the shape of both domains

Fig 13 :74 **"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."** Fig 13 :75 **"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."**

Read each domain as a first-order molecule. Isolated thermalization reads energy and spectrum over a state, with a gradient of entanglement as the directed structure. Black hole dynamics reads energy and horizon structure, with entropy ordering and a gradient of information flow. A direct bond of the two thermal endpoints would join two structures or two states directly, which Fig 13 :75 forbids. Strain test: forcing the identity claim joins two structures at first order, so the line strains, which means it governs a different quantity and the claim must be recast as a second-order comparison. Refuter: derive both domains as the same first-order molecule over one state pair with no second-order step, and show the framework admits it.

### F3: The relation is second order, and adds no primitive

Fig 12 :104 **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."** Fig 6 :14 **"the only operator that moves the wall"**

Each domain's first-order result is settled in its own frame, so each becomes an atom at the next level. Their comparison is therefore a composition of settled molecules, not a new field and not a seventh member. Fig 5 :33 **"The three guards are a product, not a checklist."** There is no residual that licenses a new term here, so the mint is inert. Refuter: show that a new primitive is required to state the relation, and that it is not a composition of the six.

### F4: Isomer or functional group, kept separate from the identity claim

Fig 12 :117 **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** Fig 12 :123 **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

Over the full shared six-term atom set, the two domains are isomers in the figure's sense: same atoms, different arrangement, different behaviour. As they are usually described they share only energy and time and a directed structure, which makes the weaker functional-group claim. This is a structural claim, not a physics result, and it is `specified`. Refuter: if either domain requires an atom the other does not, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group claim stands. See `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

### F5: The anchors are unequal, and this is the honest gap

Fig 2 :29 **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."** Fig 3 :27 **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

Domain 1 has a visible anchor: experiment and numerical diagonalization. Domain 2 has theoretical constraints, but at the quantum-gravity scale no instrument returns a reading that would settle the microscopic dynamics. Fig 8 :39 **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** So the correct verdict on the cross-frame identity is `gap` at the grounded level, not a refusal of the relation and not a claim that no relation exists. Refuter: name a ground-visible anchor for the quantum-gravity-scale claim and show it settles the shape.

### F6: World results corroborate; they are not imports that add an atom

Fig 6 :23 **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."** Fig 8 :51 **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents."**

Fig 8 :32 **"no search, however long, adds an atom"** applies to searches inside a frame. Refuter: exhibit a world result that requires a new kind not composable from the six, with provenance and a shadow test.

### F7: Filters are not witnesses

Fig 9 :60 **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."** Fig 2 :42 **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."**

The choice of six sources was a budget filter across low-overlap fields, not a vote. Refuter: show two cited sources draw on the same result and relabel the overlap.

### F8: Verification must not spend independence

Fig 10 :42 **"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."** Fig 10 :52 **"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."** Fig 2 :35 **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."** Fig 7 :13 **"agreement compounds, evidence does not"**

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the seat reasoning rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument.

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A measurement or theorem shows that a direct state-state or structure-structure bond is required to compose the two domains, with no intervening opposite-kind term (breaks F2 and Fig 13 :74-75).
2. A demonstration that the two domains do not share the six-term atom set, because a seventh primitive is needed that is not a composition of the six (breaks F3 and F4).
3. A ground reading at the quantum-gravity scale that settles whether the same cross-kind composition holds, and returns a different shape (breaks F5).
4. A figure re-census showing that the comparison bond cannot take settled molecules as operands at second order (breaks F3 and the Operators table type constraint).
5. A result showing the two conditions share one anchor, such that clearing one clears the other (breaks F1).

If any fires, the corresponding part is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of the cross-frame claim may be labelled `grounded`.
