# Expert verification packet: acting under unobtainable information

**What this is:** a claim in a form a decision theorist, risk analyst or judgment researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so the reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the discipline, `general` on the structural splits. Not `grounded`.** No decision outcome was measured. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

When the observation that would discriminate the options cannot be read, a decision system should act as follows. Split the case first: an atom in the closure and merely unfound calls for more independent search; genuine absence, where nothing has registered at any resolution, calls for a characterized import rather than a sharper instrument inside the same frame. Keep the live, per-slot residual rather than an aggregate. Refuse a false held premise before minting terms over it. Preserve contact with whatever can settle the question, and buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong. Report unreachable as a verdict, with what would have to be true for the answer to differ, rather than naming the missing thing. The framework does not select a named decision criterion; that is an honest gap. It offers the shape any right act must have, refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 8, 9 and 13

- **Statement:** the missing-information condition is a union of two conditions with different instructions, and the correct report when nothing closes it is a verdict plus reversal conditions, not a manufactured name.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :40-43: *"Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions."* Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*
- **Refuter:** one rule handles both conditions with identical instructions and no loss; or a system names the missing primitive from inside its closure and the name is settled without new contact.

### C2: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 5, 6 and 10

- **Statement:** search cannot add an atom, the residual must remain per slot, a false premise owes a refusal, and internal refinement alone does not change contact.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :32: *"no search, however long, adds an atom."* Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 5 :53: *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so."* Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does."* Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*
- **Refuter:** search inside a fixed closure adds a genuinely new atom; or a near-zero aggregate with one live slot is safe without attending to that slot; or an explaining term removes a persistent flat residual that a refusal could not; or an internal-only refinement changes contact.

### C3: world-facing; checkable by a decision theorist against the named criteria

- **Statement:** maximin, maximax, minimax regret, the Hurwicz index, the Laplace rule and info-gap robustness are arrangements over shared atoms (outcomes, states, comparison, weighting), so figure lines cannot rank them. Which is right here is a gap to be closed by a ground, not by the framework.
- **What to check:** is that characterization faithful? Is there a criterion that requires an atom the others lack? The Laplace rule's equal-probability assumption is the standing candidate. If such a criterion exists, the isomer or functional-group claim in `06` Q3 fails and it is a different kind of thing, which would make the gap easier to close, not harder.
- **Source, verbatim for the shape limit:** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- **Refuter:** a figure line that selects one named criterion, or a demonstrated ground payment that discriminates them on this problem with a provenanced import and a shadow test. Either closes the gap; the second is the legitimate way.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a named decision rule. No figure line selects maximin, regret, Hurwicz, Laplace or info-gap.
- Not "do not act." The discipline licenses an act; it forbids manufacturing the discriminator, not deciding.
- Not that a failed search proves impossibility. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
- Not that a cost estimate is a verdict. Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a measured decision outcome.
- Not corroborated by the essays. They share an author and an evidence pool with the figures, so their agreement is expected.

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminator or a ground payment, not more agreement:

1. **Classification test:** the same system, given a case known to be budget-limited and one known to be absent, is shown to need the same instruction. That breaks C1.
2. **Proto-instrument test:** information that never registered at any resolution becomes available by refining an existing instrument alone. That breaks C2's instrument clause.
3. **Aggregate test:** a near-zero aggregate with one live slot is safe without attending to the slot. That breaks C2's per-slot clause.
4. **Criterion test:** a named decision rule is imported with provenance and a shadow test and pays against the ground on this problem. That closes C3's gap.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 8 :40-43, Fig 9 :32 and :57, Fig 13 :134-135, Fig 1 :19, Fig 5 :53, Fig 10 :24 and Fig 11 :40, ten short passages.
2. Check C3 against the classical criteria you know. Is the shared-atom characterization faithful? Name one criterion that needs an atom the others lack, or say none exists.
3. Name one instrument or decision problem that would discriminate the two conditions in C1, or say why none could.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. Do not send a statement of agreement; it cannot be used as evidence and it spends the independence being bought.
