**Subject:** One output to check on acting under ignorance, before any theory

> Hello,
>
> I am working on a narrow question in decision under ignorance: what a decision system should do when the information that would discriminate the options cannot be had. I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** when the discriminating observation cannot be read, a decision system should
>
> 1. split the missing-information condition before acting. "Merely hard and unfound" and "no instrument could ever register it" are two conditions with different instructions, and a single rule for both loses the distinction;
> 2. search first where search can pay, but not treat a failed search as proof that nothing is there;
> 3. keep the live, per-slot signal rather than collapsing to one reassuring aggregate;
> 4. refuse a held premise that is false before adding more terms over it;
> 5. keep the channel to whatever can settle the question open, and buy a genuinely different second frame in proportion to the cost of being wrong;
> 6. report "unreachable" as a verdict, together with what would have to be true for the answer to differ, instead of naming the missing thing.
>
> This is a **shape of right action, not a measurement and not a named decision rule**. It does not select among maximin, minimax regret, the Hurwicz index, the Laplace rule, or robust-satisficing. Those are bindings from the decision-theory frame, and I am not claiming the framework derives any of them.
>
> **What would refute this output.** I wrote these down before writing the derivation:
>
> 1. one rule handles both missing-information conditions with identical instructions and no loss;
> 2. a search confined to a fixed closure adds a genuinely new atom, or a failed search proves absence;
> 3. information that never registered anywhere becomes available by refining an existing instrument alone, with no new frame contacted;
> 4. an aggregate near zero with one live residual is safe without ever attending to that slot;
> 5. an explaining term, rather than a refusal, removes a residual that persists and does not trend down.
>
> If any of these holds, I withdraw the corresponding point. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send the source links. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a decision problem you know well and can check against a real outcome. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state whether your output is `general`, `specified` or `grounded`. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thank you for your time.
