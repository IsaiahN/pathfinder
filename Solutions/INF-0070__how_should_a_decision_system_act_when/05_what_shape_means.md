## 1. A shape is a constraint you can state before you can name it

The README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

From *How Intelligence Actually Works*: *"the framework if applied effectively provides us a tool that cuts down to the shape of a thing, problem or question based on the given information available at this point in time."*

For this question, a **shape** is therefore the discipline a right act must have, statable before any decision criterion is named: split the missing-information condition, do not mistake a failed search for proof of absence, keep the live per-slot residual, refuse false premises, keep contact, and report unreachable as a verdict. That is a shape. "Use the Hurwicz criterion with alpha equals 0.4" is a name.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already hold that is closest to the thing, look for new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."* The residual in `01` §0 is written in effect terms for this reason.
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* This is why the outreach email sends an effect description, not the architecture.
- *How a Society of Agents Would Work*: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* The essay carries the per-slot rule into the multi-agent case; it is the same constraint at a different scale.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: you are done when *"the finish line will also be the starting place"*, and the paradox is that by then you have *"composed the answer through the process of locating it."*

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* The README: the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."*
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*They're Scanning Books*: *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space."* And: *"A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six."* Also: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."*

So the named decision criteria are visible choices made in another frame. Their provenance can be recorded; they do not become figure-derived by being plausible.

## 4. The specific forbidden error here: naming the rule from a reading

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. An instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source"*: one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* And the resolution was not an argument: *"What the Spectrometer Example Reveals: The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* Also: *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."*

Applied here: a decision problem returns an aggregate (expected utility, regret, robustness curve). Several criteria can produce readings that look alike on the same problem. Naming one as "the right rule under ignorance" from that reading is the metamerism error: a source named from a projection, where several sources could give the same projection. What is owed is a discriminator, not a name. Fig 6 :34: *"Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."*

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."*

## 5. Q: does "shape, not a name" forbid maximin, regret, Hurwicz or info-gap? CORRECTION to §4

I overstated the rule in the first draft of this essay section, and the correction is kept visible.

- The README's phrase is *"what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Its subject is a primitive not held in Γ.
- Maximin, minimax regret, the Hurwicz index, the Laplace rule and info-gap robustness are **already held terms in the decision-theory frame**. None is missing from that frame.
- Therefore **naming them is not forbidden**. What is forbidden is (a) claiming the framework derived or selected one, (b) inventing a seventh principle, or (c) saying the binding moves the wall. Fig 6 :14: *"the only operator that moves the wall"* is import, and a binding onto held atoms adds no atom.
- My §4 metamerism point is **not retracted**. It still holds against naming a criterion from a single reading of an aggregate. What changes is the reason: the missing discriminator, not a blanket ban on names. Both claims are recorded so neither is quietly lost.

The honest status is the one in `01` F11: the framework returns the shape of right action, the named criteria are bindings with their own provenance, and **which one is right here is a genuine gap** until a ground settles it.

## 6. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Someone let prestige stand in for proof... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."* A list of famous decision theorists assembled to agree with the shape claim is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask is a budget to try the claim against a ground, and a refutation if it fails.

**Agreement inside a shared pool is one witness.** *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"A shared mindset lets two people drift together without either one noticing"* and *"Two frames that overlap almost completely are 'one frame wearing two names.'"* Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* This applies to this whole folder: the eight essays share an author, so their agreement with the figures is expected and carries little independent weight.

## 7. My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula"*, however *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* LLM output is *"simulacra."* So `01` and `09` are simulated derivations, checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are **not** tests of the claim and must be labelled as such. They cannot settle anything they report on.

*Arrival of the Fittest: The Tether Architecture* adds the limit in its own words: *"Because it is a metatheory, it cannot be validated, nor can it validate itself."* And its own "Three mappings offered and declined" section records that resemblance is not alignment: *"there is no prior, no likelihood and no posterior anywhere in the mechanism."* The same caution applies to mapping a decision criterion onto the loop.
