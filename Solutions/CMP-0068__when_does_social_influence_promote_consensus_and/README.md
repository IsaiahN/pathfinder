**Question:** When does social influence promote consensus, and when does it intensify polarization or fragmentation?

## What this answer is

The figures are a domain-agnostic metatheory, so they do not return a critical confidence bound, a number of bridging ties, or a sociological effect size. What they do return is a **structural discriminator**: the outcome of social influence is governed by (1) whether frames range against a ground that does not itself update, (2) whether the frames draw on independent evidence, and (3) whether a persistent disagreement is one question or a union of conditions. The verdict is a shape with a pre-registered refuter, not a settled empirical result.

**This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.** It cannot settle the question it reports on. See `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` preamble and `05_what_shape_means.md` §6.

## Restatement in effect terms

Given a set of frames whose opinions update under mutual influence, what observable difference separates runs that converge on a shared position from runs that split into hostile clusters or durable multiplicity? Option A: treat consensus as an intrinsic good produced by more influence and more contact. Option B: treat consensus as a `triangulation` outcome that requires a non-updating anchor and independence, and treat polarization as a named failure mode with two distinct mechanisms plus a correct case where disagreement should not be closed. The derivation records why B survives the figures and A does not.
