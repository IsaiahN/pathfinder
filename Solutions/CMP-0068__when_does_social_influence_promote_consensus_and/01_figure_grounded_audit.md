## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

The question asks for a discrimination, not a single number. Let R be:

> Given frames whose opinions update under mutual influence, what observable difference separates runs that converge on a shared position from runs that split into hostile clusters or durable multiplicity?

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term (Formula step "Direction"): a **discriminator** with named conditions such that, if you can measure the conditions, you can say which outcome class a run is in, plus a list of cases where the outcome class is not a defect. Written down before the run, this shape is refusable: a discriminator that does not separate cases is a failed discriminator.

Two naive answers are refused at the gate:

- **A: "more influence and more contact produce consensus."** This assumes the thing to be discriminated. Fig 7 :12-13 "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not" says more of the same contact compounds agreement without adding evidence.
- **B: "polarization is always a failure of influence to overcome."** Fig 9 :4-5 says a disagreement that does not shrink with effort can be several well-formed questions. The correct move can be to split rather than to push harder.

## 1. Domain census

R by arity, symmetry and scale, before any figure line is chosen.

- **Arity.** At least two conditions are being discriminated (consensus versus polarization/fragmentation), and polarization and fragmentation are themselves distinct outcomes. This is a **union compound**, not one hard question. Fig 9 :55-58: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Each condition needs its own loop; their intersections form compounds with their own loops.
- **Symmetry.** Influence travels through relations (Fig 13 :24 "that entities relate, and influence travels"), and influence strength is not symmetric: under the no-anchor collapse the group converges on "whoever had the most influence" (Fig 2 :15). So the operator is a directed, weighted relation, not a symmetric join.
- **Scale.** First order inside one group of frames; second order between groups or rooms (Fig 7). A result at one scale must be carried up as a method, not a recording (Fig 4 :22-25).

One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor. No nameable anchor means it is not yet a domain.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1. Frame updating against a ground** | individual frames, opinions, weights on private history versus inherited prior (Symbols table: "How far the belief leans on private history versus inherited prior.") | opinion updates by weighted averaging and by contact with a truth signal | the ground: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." (Fig 2 :29) | partition case: does a non-updating anchor exist for this residual? |
| **D2. Evidence pool and independence** | sources, media diets, homophilous ties, shared cues, correlated samples | overlap of evidence between frames; effective sample size | independence is measurable: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." (Fig 2 :31) | partition case: are the frames drawing on one pool or on different ones? |
| **D3. Between-room composition and disagreement type** | groups/rooms, their closures, the residual each surfaces | agreement within a room, residual between rooms | the ground's pressure and the bracket's fidelity across the handoff (Fig 7 :38-39) | union case: is a persistent disagreement one question or several? |

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** The chain runs D2 (is the evidence independent?) and the anchor test in D1 (does the ground update?) first, because both are prerequisites for interpreting an update; then D1 (what the update produces); then D3 (what happens when rooms are composed). "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not" (Fig 3 :19). A reading of D3 taken before D2 has been assessed is a reading of nothing, because agreement in a single-pool group has not yet been classified.

**Partition or union.** D1 and D2 are partitions with a genuine overlap: a group can have a non-updating anchor and still share one evidence pool, which is a compound with its own loop (Fig 9 :54). D3 is a union: consensus and fragmentation are overlapping conditions of the same contact process, not two points on one scale.

## 3. Findings

### F1 : Genuine consensus is triangulation, not averaged influence (decisive, triangulated)

Fig 2 :29-30:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."**

Fig 2 :33:

> **"The anchor must not update."**

Fig 2 :35:

> **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

Fig 2 :45:

> **"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."**

**Derivation.** Social influence promotes consensus in the specific sense of a correct, stable shared position when two conditions hold: each frame can range against something that does not move when the frame pushes on it, and the frames are not merely averaging one another. The anchor is not a third party to the disagreement and not a tie-breaker in a vote; "Each frame is corrected by it separately" (Fig 2 :46). If either condition fails, agreement can still occur, but it is no longer triangulation.

**World corroboration (secondary).** Hegselmann and Krause 2006 report a Funnel theorem: *"a consensus on the truth will be reached if all individuals posses an arbitrarily small inclination for truth seeking."* Couzin et al. 2005 find that *"groups can make consensus decisions, even though informed individuals do not know whether they are in a majority or minority"* and that a very small proportion of informed individuals is enough for great accuracy. Both are low-overlap fields (social epistemology and animal behaviour) and both support the anchor condition: a small, non-updating contact with the ground is sufficient to pull a group to an accurate consensus.

**Refuter.** A case in which a group reaches accurate, stable consensus with no contact, however indirect, to anything external that fails to move when the group pushes on it; or a measurement showing that a non-updating ground contact reduces accuracy when other conditions are held.

**Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 2 plus Fig 13's independence condition, with two low-overlap world fields corroborating). Mode `specified` at the structural level; the world results are empirical support, not settlement of this derivation.

### F2 : Mutual update without an anchor is coherent drift, and whoever has the most influence wins (decisive, single-source in the figures)

Fig 2 :13:

> **"no anchor; each updates on the others"**

Fig 2 :14-15:

> **"they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence"**

Fig 2 :34:

> **"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."**

**Derivation.** This is the polarization-by-influence mechanism. It does not require hostile intent or extreme opinions. It requires only that the anchor be removed or captured, so that updates are among frames only. The outcome is a shared position whose content is set by influence weight, and the drift is *coherent*, which is why participants experience it as agreement. This is why "more consensus" is not evidence of more truth: the same observable (agreement) is produced by both the grounded panel and this one. The discriminator must be the anchor, not the agreement.

A second route into the same collapse: if the group's own convention is promoted to the role of the ground, it has stopped being a convention. Fig 2 :43 "Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."

**World corroboration.** Bail et al. 2018 found that *"Republicans who followed a liberal Twitter bot became substantially more conservative posttreatment"*, an experimentally induced move away from the source position. Lord, Ross and Lepper 1979 found that exposing opposing factions to the *same* evidence *"may be not a narrowing of disagreement but rather an increase in polarization."* These are two different mechanisms (counterarguing under motivation, and biased assimilation of a common pool) and both are consistent with drift under influence rather than triangulation.

**Refuter.** A case of stable agreement whose content tracks an external, independently measured ground while the only updating is among frames, with no frame contacting that ground; or a demonstration that influence weight does not predict the settled position under the no-anchor condition.

**Status:** `single-source` for the figure mechanism (Fig 2 only), but corroborated by two low-overlap empirical fields. The mechanism is named in the figures; the magnitudes are not.

### F3 : One evidence pool produces agreement that is the pool talking, not corroboration (decisive, triangulated)

Fig 2 :18:

> **"one evidence pool"**

Fig 2 :19:

> **"they never talk, and still agree"**

Fig 2 :20-21:

> **"so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other"**

Fig 2 :31-32:

> **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."**

Fig 6 :53:

> **"searchers drawing on the same evidence are one searcher wearing many names."**

**Derivation.** The second collapse is a common cause, not mutual updating. Frames can agree perfectly while never interacting, because a shared source writes the same content into each of them. Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." This matters here because polarization researchers often observe *within-camp* agreement and call it consensus. Under the figures, high within-camp agreement is exactly what a single pool produces, so it is weak evidence for a healthy consensus. Homophily (McPherson et al. 2001: *"Homophily limits people's social worlds in a way that has powerful implications for the information they receive, the attitudes they form, and the interactions they experience."*) is the network mechanism that keeps the pool shared.

The statistics analogue is not an analogy. Fig 2 :44 "The effective-sample-size correction for correlated observations is standard in survey statistics and meta-analysis; the collapse is the same arithmetic read as a warning." Killip et al. 2004 write that *"In accounting for the similarities among clustered subjects, there is a net loss of independent data."* A thousand people reading one feed is closer to one observer than to a thousand.

**Refuter.** A case in which two frames demonstrably drawing on the same single source produce independent, non-redundant correct information about the residual; or a measurement in which effective sample size does not fall as within-cluster correlation rises (would break the statistics corroboration, not the figure line).

**Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 2, Fig 6, Fig 13, plus survey statistics and network sociology). Strongest empirical bridge in the solution.

### F4 : More homogeneous contact compounds agreement and drowns novelty (decisive, triangulated)

Fig 7 :12-13:

> **"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"**

Fig 7 :14:

> **"blending: novelty swamped to the mean"**

Fig 7 :35:

> **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**

Fig 7 :36:

> **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."**

**Derivation.** This is why scaling up a discussion inside the same closure does not move it toward truth. A room's internal agreement is variation within that room; it is not a verdict. Progress per handoff needs two factors: "the bracket's fidelity across the handoff" and "the ground's pressure on the room" (Fig 7 :38-39). If the next room is booked like the last one, the chain is "one long room: motion, no progress" (Fig 7 :32). Applied here: adding more deliberants who share a pool, or more rounds of the same averaging rule, increases apparent consensus without adding evidence. Bridging to a different closure is what can add evidence, and it is debitable against independence (Fig 6 :24).

**World corroboration.** Centola and Macy 2007: *"Complex contagions require social affirmation from multiple sources."* Their result that long nonredundant ties can *impede* diffusion when reinforcement is needed shows that it is the structure of independent confirmation, not the raw amount of contact, that governs the outcome. Lorenz 2006 shows non-monotonicity: *"After the first bifurcation from consensus to polarisation consensus strikes back for a while."* So neither "more contact" nor "less contact" is a monotone lever.

**Refuter.** A controlled comparison in which adding frames from the same closure, with no new evidence source, reliably increases accuracy on an independent ground; or a case where a "room" added from an identical closure produces a genuinely new prediction.

**Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 7 and Fig 8, with network-science and opinion-dynamics corroboration). Note the strain with the classical averaging result, resolved in §6.

### F5 : A persistent disagreement can be the right outcome (decisive, triangulated)

Fig 9 :4-5:

> **"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."**

Fig 9 :54:

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**

Fig 9 :55-56 (the union case):

> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**

**Derivation.** The question's framing assumes fragmentation is a failure. The figures say that a disagreement that does not shrink under effort is diagnostic of a union of conditions. The correct intervention is then to split the issue, not to increase influence. Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." A cohesion score is a filter for the budget; it cannot tell you whether the shared position is right.

**World corroboration.** Winklmayr et al. 2020 find that *"stalemates improve collective accuracy by selectively filtering out incorrect decisions"* and that in complex environments, where information is both shared and independent, this effect is stronger. A group that fails to agree can be filtering a correlated cue. This is a genuine low-overlap corroboration (animal collective decision making).

**Refuter.** A case where splitting a persistent disagreement into its subsets destroys accuracy relative to forcing a single shared position; or a measurement showing that non-shrinking disagreement is always explained by a single condition rather than a union.

**Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 9 plus Fig 8's union/partition distinction, with an independent animal-behaviour field corroborating). This is the finding that most changes the answer to the user's question: consensus is not the only good outcome.

### F6 : The same influence has different valence in different residuals (triangulated)

Fig 11 :49:

> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**

Fig 11 :50-51:

> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**

Fig 11 :51:

> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

**Derivation.** A confident speaker, a shared message, a charismatic minority, or a homogeneous network has no fixed sign. In a residual where the ground is reachable and evidence is independent, such an actor can accelerate triangulation (Couzin et al.'s informed minority). In a residual where the anchor has been captured and the pool is shared, the same actor becomes the "whoever had the most influence" of F2. This is why the user's question cannot be answered with a context-free list of good or bad influence mechanisms. The answer has to be conditional on the residual, and the residual has to be described in effect terms before a frame is chosen (Fig 9 :27-28).

Contact cascades matter for scale: Fig 11 :52-53 "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." A local intervention on a feed can set a second-order effect on a different issue.

**Refuter.** A demonstration that one influence mechanism has the same sign across residuals with different ground contact and different evidence independence; or a case where actor valence is intrinsic rather than residual-relative.

**Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 11 plus Fig 2's two collapses, with world sources supplying the two signs).

### F7 : The anchor is legitimate only when the question is constitutively about it (single-source, with an important limit)

Fig 2 :36:

> **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."**

Fig 2 :37-38:

> **"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."**

**Derivation.** The discriminator in F1 is not a licence to install any convenient ground. For "does this group share an accurate picture of X," an external measurement of X is the right anchor. For "is this shared picture acceptable to the people who hold it," human judgment is the right anchor. Conflating the two is a category error that produces either a technically accurate but rejected consensus, or a comfortable but wrong one. Fig 2 :46 "The anchor is not a third party to the disagreement and it is not the tie-breaker in a vote."

**Refuter.** A case where a technically valid external measurement settles a question that is constitutively about human acceptance, or vice versa, and the result is treated as final.

**Status:** `single-source` (Fig 2). Mode `general` on the rule; its application to any specific topic is `specified` and unmeasured.

### F8 : What the figures do not give (gap, stated plainly)

The figures return no critical confidence bound, no threshold number of independent sources, no effect size, and no claim about any particular platform. Lorenz 2006's non-monotonic bifurcations and the HK 2023 amendment ("the transitions are in fact wild, chaotic and non-monotonic") show why a single numeric lever would be misleading even if it could be derived. The framework returns a shape: the conditions to measure and the failure modes to look for. "Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence" (master plan §1, citing Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."). This is a `gap` at the level of numeric parameters, not a failure of the derivation at the level of structure.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Required before outreach. Seat Map :287 "And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."

The discriminator is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A group reaches accurate, stable consensus while drawing on a single evidence pool that itself updates with group opinion, with no independent ground contact. (Breaks F1 and F3.)
2. Adding a non-updating ground and genuinely independent evidence systematically *increases* polarization or fragmentation with other conditions held. (Breaks F1.)
3. The anchor/independence discrimination does not separate consensus from polarization cases better than chance across a pre-declared sample. (Breaks the whole discriminator.)
4. A persistent disagreement that meets Fig 9 :4-5's description is shown to be one condition and closing it improves ground-measured accuracy. (Breaks F5.)
5. A single influence mechanism retains the same sign across residuals that differ in ground contact and evidence independence. (Breaks F6.)

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the three-outcome discriminator, not with "consensus is good." The user's question is answered by distinguishing triangulation, coherent drift, and one-pool agreement.
2. State the mode on every sentence: structural conditions are `specified`, the shape is `general`, nothing is `grounded`.
3. Treat agreement itself as uninformative until independence and anchor status are measured. Fig 2 :40 and Fig 7 :35.
4. Include the "do not close it" case (F5). It is the part of the answer most likely to be missed and it is directly figure-derived.
5. Price independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."). Cheap internal deliberation is not a substitute for a second, low-overlap frame.
6. Send the generator, not the recording: the discriminator, its refuter and the figure lines. Fig 4 :25 "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."
