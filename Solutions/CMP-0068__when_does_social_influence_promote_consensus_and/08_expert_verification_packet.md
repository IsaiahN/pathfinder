# Expert verification packet: the Tether on social influence, consensus and polarization

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in opinion dynamics or collective decision making can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the structural discrimination, `general` on the shape. No measurement is claimed.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Agreement alone cannot tell you whether social influence promoted consensus or polarization. The figures distinguish three sources of the same observable. When frames range separately against a ground that does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks, and draw on independent evidence, influence promotes genuine consensus by triangulation. When the anchor is removed or begins updating, frames converge on whoever had the most influence, and the drift is coherent enough to feel like agreement. When frames share one evidence pool, they can agree without talking, and that agreement is the pool talking rather than corroboration. A fourth case is normative: a disagreement that does not shrink with effort can be a union of conditions, so the right move is to split it, not push harder. The framework returns these conditions and their operational tests, not a numeric threshold.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 : framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 2
- **Statement:** a non-updating anchor plus independence is what separates triangulation from the two collapses; agreement is not the discriminator.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29-30 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 2 :31-32 "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."
- **Refuter:** a case of accurate, stable consensus with no ground contact, or with all frames drawing on one updating pool; or a demonstration that the anchor/independence test does not separate cases better than an agreement score.

### C2 : framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 2, 6 and 8
- **Statement:** the two collapses are mechanically distinct. Mutual update with no anchor converges on influence weight. A shared pool produces agreement without interaction. Both are consistent with polarization and with apparent consensus.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :13 "no anchor; each updates on the others" and Fig 2 :14-15 "they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence". Fig 2 :19 "they never talk, and still agree" and Fig 2 :20-21 "so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other". Fig 6 :53 "searchers drawing on the same evidence are one searcher wearing many names."
- **Refuter:** a case where the two mechanisms produce distinguishable signatures in the same data with no ground manipulation, or a case where one mechanism never produces the predicted outcome across a declared sample.

### C3 : framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 9 and 12
- **Statement:** consensus and fragmentation are a union of overlapping conditions, not a single scale, and over the shared atom set they are isomers in Fig 12's sense: same atoms, different bond arrangement, different substance.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :4-5 "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- **Refuter:** a case where agreement maps one-to-one onto ground accuracy (kills the union claim), or where the two outcomes require different atom sets (kills the isomer claim).

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a critical confidence bound, effect size, or platform-specific finding.
- Not "diversity always helps" or "more contact always converges." Lorenz 2006 reports non-monotone transitions; Centola and Macy 2007 show reinforcement must come from independent sources.
- Not that agreement is always suspect. Fig 2 :41 "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it" governs a different object from :40.
- Not a derivation of social psychology or network science from the figures. World sources are imports with provenance.
- Not grounded. No measurement in this packet settles any case.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. The decisive manipulations are the anchor test (does the reference move when the group pushes on it?), the independence test (effective sample size under within-camp correlation), and the mechanism-separation test (does the discriminator beat an agreement score on a pre-declared sample?). Winklmayr et al. 2020 supply a concrete case where the absence of consensus improves accuracy, which is the world-side version of C3.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :13-15, :18-21, :29-35 and :40-45; Fig 9 :4-5; Fig 12 :117. Six short passages.
2. Check C1 against one dataset you know: can you identify a non-updating reference and an independence measure, or only an agreement score?
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish the two collapses in that dataset, or say why none is available.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
