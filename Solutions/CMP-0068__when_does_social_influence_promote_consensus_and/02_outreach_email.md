**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` have been re-checked against the verbatim figure text. The offer collapses on contact if a stage-1 claim does not survive.

---

**Subject:** Would this discriminator separate consensus from polarization in your data?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the figure set makes a structural distinction between three ways a group can end up agreeing or disagreeing.
>
> 1. **Triangulation.** Frames range against an anchor that does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks, and they draw on independent evidence. The framework says "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." This is the case where social influence promotes genuine consensus.
> 2. **Coherent drift.** With no anchor, "they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence." The drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices. If the anchor itself starts updating, the grounded case turns into this one.
> 3. **One evidence pool.** "they never talk, and still agree, so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other." Agreement here is not corroboration. A shared, updating pool plus biased assimilation can intensify polarization even while people report agreement.
>
> A fourth case is where disagreement should **not** be closed. "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." What looks like fragmentation can be a union of conditions that needs unbundling, not a failure of influence.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** a numeric confidence bound, a critical number of bridging ties, that diversity always helps, or that any of this is an empirical result about Twitter or any other platform. The framework returns a shape and a set of discriminators, not an effect size. A `specified` structural claim is not a measurement.
>
> **What would refute it, written before sending:**
> 1. A case where frames reach accurate, stable consensus while drawing on a single shared evidence pool that itself updates with the group's opinions, with no independent ground contact.
> 2. A case where adding independence or an external ground systematically increases polarization and fragmentation with other conditions held.
> 3. A demonstration that the anchor/independence discrimination does not separate outcomes better than chance across your cases.
>
> If any holds, I retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical figures README: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
