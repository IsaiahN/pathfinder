# The Tether's answer: when influence promotes consensus versus polarization

**Mode: `specified` on the structural discrimination, `general` on the shape. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading. This cannot settle the question for any particular group.

## 1. The residual it answers

Given frames whose opinions update under mutual influence, what observable difference separates runs that converge on a shared position from runs that split into hostile clusters or durable multiplicity? The observed fact of "agreement" is insufficient, because the same reading is produced by triangulation, coherent drift and a shared evidence pool. The discriminator must look upstream of the agreement.

## 2. The answer in three outcome classes

1. **Consensus by triangulation.** Social influence promotes genuine consensus when frames range separately against a ground that does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks, and when they draw on independent evidence. Fig 2 :29-30 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 2 :31-32 "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." In this condition, a small amount of contact with the ground can be enough: HK 2006's Funnel theorem and Couzin et al. 2005's informed minority are the world-side versions.
2. **Polarization or drift by influence weight.** With no anchor, or with an anchor that itself updates, "they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence" (Fig 2 :14-15). The drift is coherent, "which is why nobody notices" (Fig 2 :34). Exposure to opposing views need not correct; Bail et al. 2018 measured a backfire, and Lord et al. 1979 measured increased polarization from identical evidence. A convention promoted to a ground produces the same collapse: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one" (Fig 2 :43).
3. **Agreement or fragmentation from one evidence pool.** Fig 2 :19 "they never talk, and still agree" and Fig 2 :20-21 "so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other" High within-camp agreement is weak evidence of a healthy consensus when the camp shares a source. Homophily and the effective-sample-size correction are the network and statistical forms of the same fact (Fig 2 :44). This can produce apparent consensus inside a camp and fragmentation between camps from the same mechanism.

**Plus a fourth, normative case:** a persistent disagreement that does not shrink with effort can be the right outcome. Fig 9 :4-5 "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." Winklmayr et al. 2020 found that stalemates filter incorrect decisions. Fragmentation is not always a failure to be engineered away.

## 3. What the answer explicitly does not say

- No critical confidence bound, no number of bridging ties, no effect size, no platform-specific result. Those are empirical parameters, and the framework returns a shape (F8).
- Not "more diversity always helps." Lorenz 2006 found non-monotone transitions, and Centola and Macy 2007 showed that reinforcement must come from independent sources.
- Not "consensus is always good" or "agreement is always suspect." Fig 2 :41 says agreement about a shared meaning is the product; :40 says agreement about the world from one pool is worthless. Different objects.
- Not a derivation of social psychology or network science from the figures. The world sources are imports with provenance.
- Not grounded. No measurement in this folder settles any case.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

The discriminator is refutable, not a matter of taste. Operational tests:

- **Anchor test:** does the reference the group uses change when the group pushes on it? Track it independently of group opinion. A non-updating reference is required for triangulation (Fig 2 :33-34).
- **Independence test:** do the frames draw on different evidence? Estimate effective sample size under within-cluster correlation rather than counting heads (Fig 2 :31, :44; Killip et al. 2004).
- **Mechanism-separation test:** in a pre-declared sample, does the anchor/independence discrimination separate consensus from polarization cases better than an agreement score alone? This is the test that would fire refuter 3 in `01`.
- **Split test:** for a non-shrinking disagreement, do the subsets correspond to distinct conditions with distinct grounds? If so, split (Fig 9 :54-58).
- **Valence test:** does the same influence mechanism change sign when ground contact and evidence independence change? Fig 11 :49-51 predicts it does.

A ground reading, not more agreement, moves any of this to `grounded`. "Cost if wrong" and "reversed by" are stated in `09_watson_answer.md`.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached at the level of parameters, not unreachable at the level of structure.** The figures return the conditions to measure and the failure modes to name. They do not return a threshold. The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable: three domains, eight findings, one live interpretive strain, one declared gap, five pre-registered refuters. "Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence." The essays caution that a minimum set is *"a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes"*, so the discriminator is a checkable generator, not a final theory.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that more influence makes consensus, and it does not say that disagreement is always a defect. It says that agreement has three different sources. When frames range separately against a ground that does not move, influence promotes genuine consensus by triangulation. When the anchor is removed or starts updating, frames converge on whoever had the most influence, and the drift is coherent enough to feel like agreement. When frames share one evidence pool, they can agree without talking, and that agreement is the pool talking, not corroboration. A fourth case matters: a disagreement that does not shrink with effort can be several well-formed questions, so the right move is to split it rather than push harder. What the framework offers is the discriminator, the operational tests for anchor and independence, the refuters written in advance, and an honest stop before any threshold the figures do not supply.
