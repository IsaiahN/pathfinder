## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Union or partition?

- Fig 9 :4-5: *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*
- Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*
- The four domains overlap at two seams: the signal/carrier pair (physics and society both move a pattern through a channel) and the aboutness pair (biology and cognition both have a model that is about something). Overlap means union, not a list of independent questions (Fig 9 :55-56).
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if the four domains share no condition, so that a lever in one changes nothing in the others and there is no overlap to unbundle, the union reading fails and the correct treatment is a partition of four separate questions. *Status: `specified`, census-level, not measured.*

### Q2 — What order does the work run in?

- Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*. Describe the residual in effect terms first.
- Formula step 2 owes a refusal before a mint: the sought-for universal quantity is a preference, not a term held, so it must be bound as an ordinary term and allowed to accrue a residual before any search (Formula "Direction").
- Fig 2 :29: name the anchor of each domain before comparing them; a currency without a nameable anchor is not a domain yet.
- Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."* Only after each domain's measure is settled against its own anchor can it serve as an atom for a cross-domain comparison.

- **Falsifier:** if a domain's anchor cannot be named, that domain is not yet a domain and the census must be re-run; the four-domain shape then fails as stated.

### Q3 — The energy analogue (external, low overlap)

The SEP entry says the emerging picture of information theory is *"not unlike the concept of energy: there are various formal sub-theories about energy (kinetic, potential, electrical, chemical, nuclear) with well-defined transformations between them."* It also says *"the same formulas"* are used to describe fundamentally different phenomena.

This is a genuine low-overlap analogue: one word, several sub-theories, defined transformations, and no single definition that does the work of all of them. It corroborates Option B from outside the framework.

But note the boundary, honestly: in the Tether's six-term set, Energy is a single **state** (Fig 13 :13-14, *"conserved, finite, directionless alone"*), and the physical sub-theories are bindings onto that state. The analogy is between *how a word is used* in physics and *how a word is used* across the four domains, not an identity between information and energy. *Status: single-source for the analogue, corroborative only.* **Falsifier:** if the energy sub-theories are in fact reducible to one quantity with no transformation loss, the analogue stops supporting Option B.

### Q4 — Homomorphism versus isomorphism

The good regulator theorem states the theorem as *"every good regulator of a system must be a model of that system"*, then immediately qualifies it: the mapping constructed is a **homomorphism**, *"meaning the model can lose information about the entity that is modeled."*

That is the exact structural analogue of Fig 4 :27 *"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."* A homomorphism preserves some structure and loses detail; an isomorphism preserves all distinctions and is invertible. The unity claim, if it means one currency, would require the four cross-domain maps to be isomorphisms. The theorem's own result is a homomorphism, and the figures expect loss.

*Status: triangulated* (Fig 4 :27 plus the Conant and Ashby page, two closures with low overlap: cross-scale transforms and cybernetic regulation). **Falsifier:** exhibit an isomorphism between any two of the four domain currencies that preserves every distinction on both sides, with a measured `R_T = 0`. That would break F6 of `01` and reopen Option A.

### Q5 — Are the four notions isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- Candidate binding over the six-term set: entropy is an **ordering** (spectrum-like) over Energy states; free energy is a **difference that can be spent** (Fig 13 :22 *"a difference that can be spent"*) over a Network; prediction error is a gradient over an ordered hypothesis space; a price is an **ordering** (spectrum-like) over a Network of exchanges.
- If all four can be written over the same atom set `{Space, Time, Energy, Spectrum, Gradient, Network}` and differ only in arrangement, they are **isomers** under Fig 12 :117. That is a real, checkable structural claim.
- If one domain requires an atom the others do not (for example, if cognition cannot be written without a seventh primitive, or society without a state not in the six), the isomer claim fails and the weaker **functional group** reading stands: all four are `state/structure/state` crossings, with different atoms in the slots.
- **Strain, named:** the candidate bindings above are bindings from the four scientific frames onto held atoms. They are imports in the provenance sense (Fig 8 :50-52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."*). They are **not** derived from the six-term set, and their `R_T` has not been measured. So the isomer claim is stated as a hypothesis with provenance flagged, not as a result.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if any of the four cannot be written over the shared atom set without adding a primitive, the isomer claim is withdrawn and the functional-group claim recorded instead.

## CONCLUSION

The four conditions are a union to be unbundled in a fixed order: describe, name anchors, refuse, settle, compare. The external analogue of energy and the homomorphism result both corroborate several sub-theories with lossy transformations rather than one quantity. Over the six-term set, the four notions may be isomers (same atoms, different arrangement) or may only share a functional group; the isomer version is the stronger claim and has a stated falsifier. The bindings that would decide it are provenanced imports with an unmeasured `R_T`, so nothing here is `grounded`.
