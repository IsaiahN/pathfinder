## Restating the question as an effect

The word *information* is used in four closures. The decision is not lexical; it is about what a single notion would commit us to.

- **Option A, one notion.** One quantity is the currency in all four domains, and one kind of anchor settles it in each. Consequence: a bit of entropy, a regulatory model, a prediction error, and a price signal become commensurable; a cross-domain transform should be lossless or the unity claim needs a loss term it does not yet have; a single lever should shrink the question.
- **Option B, one kernel and four currencies.** The same loop runs in all four domains, but actors, currency and anchor change at each one, and the transforms between them are lossy. Consequence: what is single is the *shape* of the loop, not the currency; each domain keeps its own anchor; a cross-domain bridge must declare its round-trip gap `R_T` instead of assuming zero.

What an answer would look like was written down before the run and bound as an ordinary term, as the formula's "Direction" requires: *an answer is either (i) a quantity Q with one anchor A such that Q is the currency in all four domains and the transfer between domains is invertible, or (ii) a proof that no such Q exists while a shared loop does*. That binding is the sought-for shape made refutable. It is stated again in `01` section 4 before the conclusion, so it can fail.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to believe the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons in the figures' own words:

1. **A room does not upgrade the label.** *"A room reaching a conclusion without the ground is agreement, and agreement among frames is a weighted average of influence. Adding people raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor."* (local seat-map text)
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** Fig 2 :40 *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; and agreement about a shared meaning is "efficient for a convention" (Fig 2 :41), not a verdict. Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures exactly the agreement that would make their answer worthless.
3. **Asking for trust before checking spends independence.** Fig 10 :42 *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, never the framework's interiors.

Legally: unsolicited bulk email to harvested addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one. Public opt-in venues avoid both.
