## 0. The residual in effect terms

The residual `R` is the question itself, restated as a decision with consequences. *"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* (Fig 4 :19-20). The problem is whether the *currency* in that sentence is one across physics, biology, cognition and society, or four.

- **Option A, one notion.** A quantity `Q` is the currency in all four domains, and one sort of anchor settles `Q` in each. This commits us to commensurability: entropy bits, a regulator's internal model, a prediction error and a price signal are readings of one thing. It also commits us to an invertible transfer, or to a loss term the claim has not yet supplied.
- **Option B, one kernel and four currencies.** The loop is single; the actors, currency and anchor change at each domain; the transforms between them are lossy. What is single is a shape, not a measure.

What an answer would look like was bound before the run as an ordinary term: either `(i)` a quantity `Q` with one anchor `A` such that `Q` is the currency in all four domains and the transfer is invertible, or `(ii)` a demonstration that no such `Q` exists while the shared loop does. Both outcomes are checkable. A search that cannot tell them apart has not answered the question.

**Question type first.** *"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* (Fig 9 :4-5). The four domains are each well formed; the shared word is what makes them look like one question. Fig 9 :57 adds: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."* Four conditions give a compound with its own loop. This is the first finding, not a preliminary.

## 1. Domain census (before any figure line is chosen)

`R` by arity, symmetry, scale:

- **Arity:** four conditions. A union compound, not a single hard question (Fig 9 :4-5, :57).
- **Symmetry:** the measures called *information* do not share a symmetry. Shannon entropy is symmetric under permutation of outcomes; mutual information is symmetric in its two arguments; semantic information is directional (aboutness); Landauer's bound ties a bit to an energy cost; a price signal is directional in time and local to a place. A single notion would have to be invariant under all of these, or explain why the asymmetries are decoration.
- **Scale:** each domain is a level with its own currency. Cross-domain claims are cross-level transforms, so they inherit a round-trip gap (Fig 4 :27-30).

One line per domain. The rule from the plan is strict: no nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | nameable anchor | closure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Physics | bit, channel, memory, erasure event | entropy in bits, thermodynamic cost `kT ln 2` | a measurement / an experiment (Fig 2 :29) | physical theory |
| Biology | organism, environment, Markov blanket, regulatory network | variational free energy, surprisal, self-information | a maintained viable state / an assay outcome | life science |
| Cognition | agent, generative model, sensory states | prediction error, model evidence, information gain | *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."* (Fig 2 :37) | mind / brain |
| Society | individuals, prices, institutions, dispersed knowledge | price signal, dispersed knowledge, communication | a market (Fig 2 :29) | social / economic order |

All four have nameable anchors. All four are therefore domains under the rule. None is dissolved into another by this census.

**Partition or union.** Union, not partition. The domains overlap at the signal/carrier pair and at the meaning/aboutness pair, so they are not a list of separate questions. *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing."* (Fig 9 :55-56). Unbundle before searching again (Fig 9 :54).

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); refuse the one-notion route before minting (formula step 2: a refusing term is owed, not an explanation); settle each domain's currency against its own anchor before attempting a cross-domain comparison (Fig 12 :104, a settled molecule becomes an atom at the next level). A cross-domain unity claim that is asserted before the four anchors have been named is out of order.

**Strain test applied during the census.** If a figure line only fits by stretching it, it governs another quantity and the census is re-run. Two strains were found and are recorded in section 6 rather than smoothed over.

## 3. Findings

### F1 — One kernel, four currencies (decisive)

Fig 4 :19-20: *"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."*

Fig 10 :68: *"Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between. That is why there is no top rung and no regress: a generator crosses, an instance does not, and the level it lands on remakes it."*

These are two low-overlap routes: the membrane (scales, methods crossing up) and the ground maintainer (seats, conventions at a seam). Both say the invariant is the loop, not the currency. *Status: triangulated.* The consequence for this question is Option B: a single information currency across four domains is not licensed; a single loop is. **Refuter:** exhibit a quantity `Q` that is the currency in all four domains, with the same anchor type in each, and no lossy transform between them. That breaks both lines at once.

A boundary note, not a retraction: F1 does **not** say that the four domains are unrelated. It says the relation is a transform, not an identity. Identity would require the loss term to be zero, which is exactly what F6 puts in question.

### F2 — Each domain has its own anchor

Fig 2 :29-30: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."*

Fig 2 :37: *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."*

The figure itself lists a measurement and an experiment (physics), a market (society), a patient outcome (biology or medicine), and then distinguishes judgment as the right anchor for a question about people and as *only an instrument* for a question about code correctness (Fig 2 :37). The anchor is legitimate *"when the question is constitutively about it"* (Fig 2 :36). Four questions, four anchors. A single notion of information would need one anchor that is constitutive for all four, and none is named. *Status: triangulated* (Fig 2 :29 and :36-37 are different clauses with different jobs: definition of an anchor, then legitimacy test). **Refuter:** name an anchor that is constitutive for all four domains, such that a measurement in physics, an assay in biology, a judgment in cognition and a market in society are all readings of that same anchor rather than four different instruments.

### F3 — Four conditions make a compound, not one harder question

Fig 9 :4-5: *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*

Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

The question is not one hard puzzle about a mystery substance. Each domain has a working notion with a working anchor; the difficulty is the bridge. Treating the four as one question is the aggregation error. *Status: triangulated* (Fig 9 union/partition, Fig 1 per-slot residual, low overlap). **Refuter:** show that the four domains' information notions *do* shrink together under a single lever, with one operation that reduces the residual in all four at once and does not leave an intersection standing.

### F4 — A single aggregate would hide where to look

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*

Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

If the four currencies are averaged into one *information* number, the residual that says which domain is failing disappears. This is not a stylistic objection to unification; it is the named failure of the metric. *Status: single-source* (Fig 1 only; Fig 9 :4-5 corroborates the split but not the per-slot metric). **Refuter:** give an aggregation rule over the four domains that preserves the per-domain residual that tells you where to look, and show it does not reduce to an average.

### F5 — A shared meaning is a convention, and declaring it does not ground it

Fig 2 :41: *"agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"* (listed under *"efficient for a convention"* in the same figure). This is the line that decides what a single notion of information would be if we had it: a convention, not a verdict about the world.

Fig 10 :18: *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"*; Fig 10 :49: *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."*

The two clauses do different work. Fig 2 :41 says agreement on a word produces a convention. Fig 10 says a seat may author such a convention at a seam, but the declaration has no truth value and does not touch the ground. So stipulating that *information* means one thing across all four domains would be authoring a convention, and would not settle anything about the four currencies. *Status: single-source for the convention claim* (Fig 2 :41); Fig 10 :18 and :49 are a **strained** fit for this question, since in their figure they govern the seat at a seam, not semantic information in general. Re-census in section 6 narrows them to the cross-domain seam, where they do apply. **Refuter:** show a ground reading in which one and the same quantity is settled in all four domains by the same instrument type, so the shared meaning is a verdict rather than a convention.

### F6 — The cross-domain transform is lossy; the gap is measurable, not zero

Fig 4 :27: *"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."*

Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

Any claim that carries *information* from physics to biology or from cognition to society is a cross-scale transform. The framework says the transform discards detail, is not reversible, and has a gap `R_T` that must be measured. A single notion of information that transfers losslessly is exactly what the figure rules out, unless `R_T = 0` is measured rather than assumed. *Status: triangulated* (Fig 4 :27 and :30 are two clauses with different jobs; Fig 10 :68 corroborates from the seat side). **Refuter:** demonstrate a lossless, invertible map between two of the four domain currencies with a measured `R_T = 0` against a ground, for example a bijection between entropy bits and a regulator's free-energy measure that preserves every distinction on both sides.

### F7 — Shannon information is a composition inside a selection rule, not the universal quantity

Fig 5 :45: *"This is minimum description length, Rissanen 1978, which is itself a composition of Kolmogorov complexity and Shannon coding put to work on model selection."*

In the figures, Shannon coding appears as one ingredient of a bargain for accepting a new term. It is a composition put to work on a specific job, not a total account of the word. The SEP entry corroborates from outside: the three influential *definiens* (probability, computation, truth) have *"next to nothing in common"* and some conflict. *Status: single-source for the figure line* (Fig 5 :45); the world corroboration is secondary and high-overlap with no figure. **Refuter:** derive the four domain currencies from Shannon coding alone without adding an interpreting agent, a truth condition or an anchor, and show the derivation preserves what each domain's measure does.

### F8 — The six-term set refuses "one reading of one quantity"; this is an analogy, not a direct governance

Fig 13 :35: *"Space, time and energy are not three readings of one quantity. Neither are spectrum, gradient and network. Each answers a question the other two cannot"*. Fig 13 :37: *"their agreement would carry no information, and one of them would be redundant rather than minimal."* Fig 13 :39: *"Six members that overlapped would be fewer members wearing six names."*

The set's own construction rule is that distinct frames are not collapsed into one quantity; if they were, their agreement would carry no information. Applied to the four domains, this is a structural echo of F1. **Strain:** Fig 13 governs the six primitives, not the four domains of this question. Applying it here is an analogy and must not be used as the decisive witness. Re-census: the direct governance is Fig 4 :19-20 and Fig 10 :68; Fig 13 is corroborative only. *Status: single-source / strained, downgraded to corroboration.* **Refuter (for the analogy):** show that the four domains *are* four readings of one underlying fact in the same sense that Fig 13 denies for space/time/energy, so their agreement is redundant rather than informative.

### F9 — What the framework returns is a shape

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*; Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*.

The canonical usage rule adds that the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"* and stops where instruments cannot distinguish. So the answer available here is the shape of a unified notion (one kernel, four currencies, lossy transforms, four anchors), not a named universal quantity. *Status: triangulated* (Fig 9 :32 and the canonical README). **Refuter:** produce the name, with a ground reading that settles it, instead of the shape.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

"A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument." The shape claim in this folder is: **one kernel, four currencies, four anchors, lossy transforms, no single universal quantity.** It is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Commensurability.** A quantity `Q` is exhibited that is the currency in all four domains, with the same anchor type in each (breaks Fig 4 :20 and Fig 10 :68).
2. **Invertible transfer.** A map between two domain currencies is shown to be lossless and invertible with a measured `R_T = 0` against a ground (breaks Fig 4 :27-30).
3. **Single lever.** One operation reduces the residual in all four domains simultaneously and leaves no intersection standing (breaks Fig 9 :4-5, :55-57).
4. **Redundant agreement.** The four notions are shown to be readings of one underlying fact, so their agreement carries no information (breaks the F8 analogy and the Fig 13 :37 principle).

If any fires, the shape claim is withdrawn and Option A recorded as the surviving reading. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`. A search that finds no refuter is not a confirmation: *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."* (Fig 9 :44).

## 5. Recommendation

1. Send the shape claim and the four refuters first, before any theory (Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*).
2. Keep per-domain verdicts separate. Never average them into an *overall* answer about information; the average is where the signal goes (Fig 1 :22).
3. State the mode on every sentence. *"an unmeasured quantity is a specification of what to measure and is worth having, it is not a verdict."* (local formula text)
4. Treat any cross-domain bridge as an import with a source gate and shadow test, and debit its `R_T`. Fig 8 :50-52: convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents; only the record separates them.
5. Use the public submission and contact points in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. Ask for a refutation, not belief. Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

### Route check (formula step 7, kept distinct)

- **SEARCHED:** inside the four domain closures, each has a working measure and an anchor. Nothing in the search for a universal `Q` inside one domain can prove that `Q` is absent from the other three: *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room."* (Fig 9 :44). This was asked first.
- **INWARD:** sharpen the instrument in one domain, for example ask what an assay actually discriminates. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it."* That can name a domain's currency more precisely; it does not create a universal one.
- **OUTWARD:** import only with a source gate and shadow test. A candidate universal theory must characterize `R` in effect terms first (Fig 9 :26-28), then show a frame whose closure predicts its shape; the atom it adds is debited against independence.

The three were not conflated. SEARCHED was cheapest and its answer was assumed by the other two.

Strains after re-census: two, both named, neither hidden. Mode: `general` on the kernel, `specified` on the classification, not `grounded`. Candidate, not accepted.
