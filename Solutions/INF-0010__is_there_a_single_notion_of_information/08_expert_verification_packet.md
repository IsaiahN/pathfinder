# Expert verification packet — the Tether on one notion of information

**What this is:** a claim in a form an information theorist, philosopher of information or cognitive scientist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the kernel, `specified` on the four-domain classification, not `grounded`.** No measurement at the cross-domain interface is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

One loop runs in physics, biology, cognition and society, but the currency and the anchor change in each, and the transform between domains is lossy. What is therefore shared is a shape: one kernel, four currencies, four anchors, and a round-trip gap `R_T` that must be measured. No universal quantity is named. A shared definition of *information* across the four would be a convention, not a verdict, and declaring it would not ground it. The packet offers the shape any candidate answer must have, four refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 4, 10 and 2

- **Statement:** the kernel is invariant across domains; currency and anchor are not. Four distinct anchors are named, and none is constitutive for all four.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :19-20 — *"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* Fig 10 :68 — *"Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between."* Fig 2 :29 — *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."*
- **Refuter:** exhibit a quantity Q that is the currency in all four domains with the same anchor type in each, or name one anchor that is constitutive for all four. That breaks Fig 4 :20 and Fig 10 :68.

### C2 — cross-domain; checkable by anyone working on unification

- **Statement:** every bridge between two of the four domains is a lossy transform with a measurable round-trip gap; a lossless invertible bridge is not available by assertion.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :27 — *"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."* Fig 4 :30 — *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* From the cybernetics literature, the good regulator result is a homomorphism that *"can lose information about the entity that is modeled."*
- **What to check:** take any proposed mapping, for example Shannon entropy to a regulator's variational free energy. Does it preserve every distinction on both sides? Is the round trip identity? If not, what did it discard?
- **Refuter:** a demonstrated lossless, invertible map between two domain currencies with measured `R_T = 0` against a ground. That breaks C2 and reopens Option A.

### C3 — framework-internal, with a convention consequence

- **Statement:** agreement on a shared meaning is a convention, and declaring a meaning does not ground it. So stipulating one universal definition of information would not settle the cross-domain question.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :41 — *"agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*, listed under "efficient for a convention". Fig 10 :18 — *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"*; Fig 10 :49 — *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."* Fig 9 :32 — *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*.
- **Refuter:** a ground reading in which one and the same quantity is settled in all four domains by the same instrument type, so that the shared meaning is a verdict rather than a convention.

**Strain declared, not hidden:** Fig 10 :18 and :49 govern a seat authoring a convention at a seam. For this question the seam is the cross-domain bridge, so they apply to the *declaration* of one notion, not to a domain's own measure. C3 rests primarily on Fig 2 :41; the Fig 10 lines are a narrowed, corroborative fit. See `01` section 6.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that the four domains are unrelated. They share a loop; they do not, on this reading, share a currency.
- Not that *information* is a useless or empty word. It is a working term with an anchor in each domain.
- Not that Shannon entropy *is* biological or cognitive information. Such bindings are provenanced imports with an unmeasured gap.
- Not a new primitive, not a derivation of one domain from another.
- Not that the six-term substrate is nature's own list. It is a human's visible guess and remains revisable.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Commensurability:** one quantity settled in all four domains by the same instrument type (would promote Option A).
2. **Round trip:** a measured `R_T` between two domain currencies; zero breaks C2, nonzero corroborates it without settling the word.
3. **Single lever:** one operation shrinking the residual in all four domains at once, leaving no intersection (Fig 9 :55-57).
4. **Source discriminator:** an instrument that tells whether four equal readings of *information* come from one source or four, as the spectrometer did for metamerism.
5. **Redundant agreement:** a demonstration that the four notions are readings of one underlying fact, so their agreement carries no information (Fig 13 :37).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 4 :19-20 and :27-30, Fig 2 :29 and :41, Fig 10 :18 and :68, Fig 9 :32. Eight short passages.
2. Check C1 against your own domain: what is the anchor in your field, and is it the same kind of anchor as in the other three?
3. Check C2 against one mapping you know well. Name what the map discards and whether the round trip is identity.
4. Say which of the five settle-tests in section 5 you could actually run, and which are only arguments.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
