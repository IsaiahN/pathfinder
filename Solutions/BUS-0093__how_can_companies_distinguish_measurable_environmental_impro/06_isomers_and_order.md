# Flat formulas, isomers and dependency order

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the shape.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6). No emissions inventory was performed.

## RESIDUAL

A reported reduction and a displaced harm can have the same total. The supporting derivation owes three things: (1) what the headline number records and what it drops; (2) whether two equal totals are the same substance; (3) in what order the check must be run.

## DOMAIN CENSUS (supporting)

Atoms held: the two conditions (reduction inside boundary, stability outside), the slots (region, supplier, gas, category, stage, time), and the bonds `→` `⇒` `−` `⋛`. No new primitive is available without an import from the physical or accounting frame. The union compound is not a list of two problems; it is one compound with its own loop.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - The headline number is a flat formula

Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* The collapse named in the figure is "the flat formula": the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not, so nothing downstream can tell entries apart and nothing signals that anything was lost.

A corporate total of 100 kt CO2e records the ingredients. It does not record that 10 kt left own operations and 10 kt appeared at a supplier. The word *"isomer"* in Fig 12 :117 names the consequence: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

- **Result:** two companies with equal totals can be isomers under Fig 12. Same atoms, different arrangement, different substance.
- **Refuter:** exhibit two arrangements with identical atoms and identical bonds that nonetheless differ in total system harm. The isomer reading then collapses.
- **Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 12 flat-formula collapse and Fig 8 closure, low overlap).

### Q2 - The ordered bonds carry what the number drops

Operators table: `→` sequence means *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*; `⇒` production means *"A produces B; B did not exist before"*; `−` subtraction means *"A with B removed or absent"*. A displacement is not a symmetric relation. It has a source and a destination: activity moved from firm to supplier (sequence), harm created in a region where it did not exist (production), local benefit achieved by removing harm from the boundary rather than from the world (subtraction).

Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* The headline number names a state. The question asks for a route, so it needs bonds.

- **Result:** the discrimination must be written as an arrangement with ordered bonds, not as a total.
- **Refuter:** show that displacement can be fully represented by a symmetric conjunction of quantities with no ordered bond. That removes the route and breaks the finding.
- **Status:** `general` structural claim.

### Q3 - Is it a union or a partition?

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

The two conditions here are (i) reduction inside the boundary and (ii) stability outside it. A single lever, buying a better carbon accounting platform, clears the region where only (i) holds and leaves the intersection standing, because (ii) still binds. Unbundle before searching. The Library of Babel essay gives the same instruction in other words: *"If your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually"*.

- **Result:** the answer is a compound with its own loop; condition (ii) is not an afterthought to (i).
- **Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 9 and the Library of Babel essay; different fields, low overlap).

### Q4 - Dependency order

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."* Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*

Ordered links for this question:

1. **Anchor.** What settles the question and does not move when the boundary is pushed (Fig 2 :29-30).
2. **Vocabulary.** Terms for what can move across the boundary: supplier, region, stage, gas, category. Fig 3 :7 *"it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked"*.
3. **Objective.** What counts as progress: the reduction survives an outward redraw of the boundary. This is a comparison, `⋛` (Operators table).
4. **Planning.** The procedure: enumerate outward, measure per slot, get independent instruments.
5. **Learn and carry.** Record the lineage: which atom was imported, from which frame, with what shadow test, and what the round-trip gap was.

Fig 3 :29-30: *"Most improvement effort in practice lands above the break, because that is where the measurements already exist."* Corporate reporting effort lands on link 3 (declaring a target) and link 1 of the measurement chain (own operations) because those are instrumented. The break is between per-slot measurement and independent triangulation.

- **Refuter:** show that independent triangulation can be done before per-slot residuals exist and still discriminate A from B. Then the order is wrong and this finding fails.
- **Status:** `general` on the order, `specified` on where the break lies.

### Q5 - The round-trip gap `R_T`

Symbols table: `R_T` is *"gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))"*, *"The gap between what was sent up and what came back down"*, measuring *"what the coarser description cannot hold."* Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

Round trip on this folder's own condensation:

- **Sent up (x):** the five-constraint shape (anchor, enumeration, per-slot residual, independence, declared comparison).
- **Abstraction (T_A):** "use a boundary-invariant, full-lifecycle, independently checked measurement."
- **Concretisation (T_E):** "adopt GHG Protocol Scope 3 plus third-party assurance plus the territorial-versus-consumption test."

**`R_T` is nonzero.** Three constraints do not survive the trip back down:

1. the **anchor test** is replaced by "use a standard"; a standard is a convention and can decide an outcome, which Fig 10 forbids (*"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*);
2. the **per-slot rule** is replaced by category reporting, which can be aggregated;
3. the **independence requirement** is replaced by "third-party assurance", which may be three readers of the same report (Fig 2 :31).

One thing survives cleanly: the need to look outside the fence line. `R_T` is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints, not measured numerically, because no ground reading exists. Treating the shorthand as identical to the shape would set `R_T` to zero and commit the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.

## CONCLUSION

The headline number is a flat formula: it records ingredients and not arrangement. Equal totals can be isomers, different substances that the notation cannot distinguish. The correction is not a bigger number but an arrangement with ordered bonds, explicit slots and a comparison at a declared threshold, run in dependency order after the anchor is fixed. The condensation of this shape into "use a standard and assure it" carries a nonzero round-trip gap, which is why the outreach packet sends the shape and its refuter rather than the shorthand.
