# Expert verification packet: real improvement versus shifted harm

**What this is:** a claim in a form a measurement or reporting specialist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** No emissions inventory was performed. This is a shape and a route, not a result, and it is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A reported environmental improvement inside a corporate boundary cannot, by itself, discriminate total harm reduction from displacement of harm to suppliers, other regions or other lifecycle stages. A procedure that can discriminate them must have five properties: a boundary-invariant physical anchor; an outward enumeration of the habitat by slot (supplier, region, stage, gas, category, time); residuals kept per slot and never aggregated; at least two low-overlap instruments pointed at the same thing; and an ordered threshold comparison at a declared convention with a recorded lineage. The figures give this shape. They do not name a reporting standard, and no company is asserted to have displaced harm.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - Anchor and boundary (framework-internal, checkable by reading Figure 2)

- **Statement:** a reported metric that changes when the reporting boundary changes is a frame, not the ground, and cannot settle whether harm fell.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :30 *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* Fig 1 :27 *"A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Fig 10 :18 *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one."*
- **What to check:** redraw the reporting boundary outward in a real inventory. Does the reported improvement survive, and does it match a boundary-independent physical inventory? If it does, the metric has an anchor after all.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a company-reported metric invariant under boundary redraw and matching a boundary-independent physical inventory across all enumerated slots. Then C1 fails and is withdrawn.

### C2 - Slots and contact cascade (partly framework-internal, partly measurement-facing)

- **Statement:** the discrimination must be per slot and the habitat enumerated outward; a single total erases the signal of displaced harm.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 11 :43 *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 11 :52 *"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* Fig 10 :26 *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings."*
- **What to check:** in a real inventory, does a single Scope 1 to 3 total hide a supplier or region slot whose emissions rose as own operations fell? The world source for this test is Russell and Akopian (WRI / GHG Protocol, 2019): *"none of them explicitly considered how their products might increase emissions."*
- **Refuter:** show that a single aggregated number preserves the per-slot signal under boundary redraw with no slot-level data, or that a first-order measurement captures a third-order harm without enumerating the actors between. Then C2 fails.

### C3 - Independence and the comparison (framework-internal)

- **Statement:** two readers of one inventory are one frame, and the procedure's output is a threshold comparison, not a third number.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :31 *"Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."* Fig 2 :40 *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Fig 2 :42 *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Operators table entry for `⋛`: *"a threshold relation between two quantities."*
- **What to check:** do the two proposed checks draw on different evidence (for example, a supplier's metered data and a consumption-based input-output inventory), or are they two audits of the same spreadsheet? Is the threshold for "cancels" declared before the comparison is run?
- **Refuter:** show that two readers of the same report count as two independent frames under Fig 2's definition, or that agreement within one evidence pool carries verdict weight. Then C3 fails.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that any company's improvement is false. No ground reading was performed.
- Not that a named standard (Scope 3, ISO 14064, a net-zero standard) is the required answer. That is a binding from the accounting frame onto the shape, provenanced as such, not a figure derivation.
- Not that reporting is worthless. It is a necessary frame and an insufficient verdict.
- Not that adding assurance providers creates independence. It can be three readers of one report.
- Not grounded. Everything here is `specified` or `general` and remains candidate.
- Not a claim that the ecology analogue (DDT) is independent corroboration. Fig 11 already names it.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument:

1. **Boundary-invariance test:** does the improvement survive an outward redraw of the boundary and match a boundary-independent physical inventory at every slot?
2. **Per-slot residual test:** is there a positive residual at a supplier or region slot that cancels the local reduction? The leakage ratio (increase outside divided by reduction inside) can exceed 100 percent.
3. **Consumption-versus-territorial test:** Our World in Data states that if territorial emissions fall while consumption-based emissions rise, that *"would suggest it was 'offshoring' emissions elsewhere."*
4. **Threshold convention:** declare before the run what counts as cancellation, and record it; a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29-31, Fig 1 :19 and :22, Fig 11 :43 and :52, and the Operators entry for `⋛`. Six short passages.
2. Name one real inventory in which a scope total hid a slot-level increase, or say why such a case cannot exist.
3. Name one instrument that can discriminate reduction from displacement at a supplier boundary, or say why none is available.
4. Say which of C1 to C3 fails and why. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
