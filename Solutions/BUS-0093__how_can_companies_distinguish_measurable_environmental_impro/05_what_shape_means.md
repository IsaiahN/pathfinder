## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical usage rule states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question:

- *"A company needs a boundary-invariant anchor"* is a shape.
- *"A company should adopt standard X"* is a name.

The first narrows the space of procedures that could work. The second picks one and, unless provenance is shown, mistakes a binding from the accounting frame for a figure result.

## 2. How the shape was obtained here

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary already held that is *closest* to the thing, then look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it. *"With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."* This is why the residual was stated as "a reading that cannot discriminate reduction from displacement" and not as "Scope 3 under-reporting".
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: *"you will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place, as there is nowhere else to search and nothing else that can describe the answer better."* Here that point is reached when the five constraints (anchor, enumeration, per-slot residual, independence, comparison with declared threshold) cannot be removed without losing the discrimination.
- *Library of Babel* on bundles: *"If your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually"*. This is the union compound of `01` §1: reduction inside the boundary and stability outside it are two conditions, not one.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays."* The canonical rule says the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."* Here that would be a boundary-invariant physical inventory across every enumerated slot that fires no off-slot residual.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8), with a source gate and a shadow test. Buying a consumption-based dataset is such an import: it adds an atom to the firm's frame, and its provenance and the gap it explains must be recorded.

*They're Scanning Books When They Should* sharpens the warning: *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space."* And: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."* A reporting boundary is a declared primitive of the corporate frame. Declaring it makes the choice visible; it does not make it the ground.

## 4. The source-discrimination problem

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism, reported in the exemplar's canonical reading: a spectrometer reads green and *"cannot comment on the source"*; one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK can give the same reading. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* It was the instrument, not the argument, that resolved it.

That maps exactly onto this question. A fall in the reported number is the reading. Its source is either reduction or displacement. The reading does not determine the source. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument (a boundary-invariant per-slot inventory), not a name and not an argument about sincerity.

## 5. Prestige is not action

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (reopened this session): *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* And the same essay, on the nature of the anchor: *"The Tether ties alignment to the ground. Not to human preferences. Not to a reward model trained on human feedback. To the thing that settles a question regardless of what any frame thinks. An outcome. A measurement. A board that pays or does not."*

A disclosure badge, a well-known assurance provider or a prestigious ESG rating is a prestige proxy. It can give the measurement a budget to be tried. It cannot make the improvement real. The correct ask of an outside expert is a refutation of the procedure shape, not an endorsement of the framework.

## 6. My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

Same essay: *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself. An external validator, or ground, is always needed to tether the veracity of the output to reality. Anything else is drift."* And: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post. It will often be better than the generic outputs that you will usually get otherwise too (intelligence is essentially arrangement). However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."*

So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a **simulated derivation**, checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of any company's emissions claims and it cannot settle the question it reports on. Its mode is `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the shape. This limitation is carried into `07`, `08`, `09` and `11`.

## 7. Correction to my own §3

I initially wrote that the framework forbids naming any standard at all. That overstates the rule. The canonical phrase governs *"what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*, the subject being a primitive or procedure not held in the frame. Naming a standard that already exists in the accounting frame is a **binding**, not the invention of a missing atom. What remains forbidden is claiming that the binding is a figure derivation or that it moves the wall (Fig 6: *"IMPORT adds an atom"*, this one adds none). Both the narrower and the earlier reading are recorded here so neither is quietly lost.
