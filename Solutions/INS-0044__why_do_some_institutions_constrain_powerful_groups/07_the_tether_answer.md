# The Tether's answer: why some institutions constrain powerful groups

**Mode: `specified` on the institutional claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Two institutions can carry the same written impartiality clause and the same oversight bodies. In one, a powerful group's action is corrected; in the other, the group's advantage is reproduced across generations. The residual is per check-slot and per group-slot (Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*), not a national score. It is a union compound of conditions (Fig 9 :57 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*), not one hard question with two opposite answers.

## 2. The shape

1. **The difference is anchor status, not rule content.** Fig 2 :25 *"pinned: it does not update on A or B"*; :34 *"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."* A check that updates on the group it measures is panel 1. A check both parties range against is panel 3. Fig 10 :19: *"install what can be violated"*, and :47 *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."*
2. **Reproduction is a chain of mirrors handing one closure along.** Fig 7 :12-13 *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"*; :36 *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."* Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Breaking the chain requires a room selected against the residual, not another member like the last.
3. **Independence is low overlap of evidence.** Fig 8 :48-49 *"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus"* and *"Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."* Separate letterhead is a filter; separate evidence is the condition. Fig 10 :86: *"Whoever is measured by a reading cannot own it."*
4. **Capability is agent-and-habitat.** Fig 11 :39-40 *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"* and *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Reforming the people in office without changing whom they answer to does not move the residual. Contact cascades (Fig 11 :52), so first-order enumeration misses third-order effects.
5. **The channel decays, not the ground.** Fig 10 :24 *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*. A rule that remains on the books can coexist with a channel that has gone stale, pooled or saturated (Fig 10 :25-28). Keeping the anchor reachable is an office, not a one-time design.
6. **Stake is declared, not abolished.** Fig 10 :59 *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"*; :60 *"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it."* A checker with a career to protect is not disqualified by having an interest; it is disqualified when the interest in the reading is undeclared and therefore runs unchallenged.
7. **The two outcomes are isomers.** Same formal atoms, different bonds, different substance (Fig 12 :117). A constraint is an ordered chain ending in a subtraction and a comparison, with negation available; reproduction is a cycle that has closed, with a spent catalyst (Fig 12 :119, :121). See `06_isomers_and_order.md`.
8. **Which specific arrangement will bind is a `gap`.** Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*; Fig 13 :134 *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*. The missing instrument is characterized in effect terms: a discriminator that tells, before the ground settles it, whether a checker is genuinely low-overlap and whether its channel is reachable. We do not hold it.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that rules are irrelevant. Rules are what can be violated (Fig 10 :19); the verdict is about anchor status, not rule count.
- Not that transparency alone breaks a reproduction loop. Transparency is the perception link; Fig 3 :19 *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*
- Not that formal independence guarantees independence. Fig 8 :48-49 governs evidence overlap, not letterhead.
- Not that a country or a named reform is the answer. That is the F9 gap, and naming one would be a binding with no source gate.
- Not that this is an aggregate law. Verdicts are per domain and per slot, never averaged.
- Not `grounded`. No institutional outcome was measured in this derivation.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A ground reading, not more agreement. The questions that would fire a falsifier:

- **Pinned-anchor test:** a documented case with a check that cannot be rewritten, registers and sanctions violations, and shows no coherent drift, yet reproduces advantage at the same rate. That breaks claim 1.
- **Overlap test:** a documented case with shared evidence and personnel yet constraint outcomes matching the low-overlap case, with no convergent independent derivation. That breaks claim 3.
- **Rule-sufficiency test:** a controlled comparison where formal rule content alone, with anchor status and contact held fixed, lowers intergenerational reproduction. That breaks the union claim (Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict."*).
- **Contact test:** a case where improving only officeholder character, with contact unchanged, lowers reproduction. That breaks claim 4.
- **Instrument:** a discriminator that can tell low-overlap checking and channel reachability before outcomes settle them would close the F9 gap and move the relevant claim from `candidate` toward `grounded`.

Standing falsifiers from `01`: derive one of the six held terms from the other five; admit a state-state or structure-structure composition that cannot be refused; show a case where formal rule content alone suffices with anchor and contact fixed.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The six-term substrate and the vocabulary binding onto institutions are visible choices, not nature's own list: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible"* (*They're Scanning Books*). The line of reasoning stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity"* (*How Intelligence Actually Works*). A reform that looks like a constraint at the resolution of an aggregate index may still be a reproduction loop.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not rank constitutions and does not name a country. It says that the difference between an institution that constrains a powerful group and one that reproduces its advantage is not the formal rule on paper but the status of the checking anchor: whether it stays pinned rather than updating on the group, whether the people checking draw on separate evidence rather than one pool, and whether the channel to the actual outcome survives a generation. Where the anchor updates, the drift is coherent and insiders notice nothing; where checkers share a pool, their agreement is the pool talking. Reproduction is a closed cycle handing the same closure to the next generation; constraint is a chain with a subtracting and refusing link inserted. The conditions form a union, so one lever clears single-condition cases and leaves the intersections standing. What it returns is the shape any candidate answer must have, four refuters written in advance, and the name of the missing instrument. Which specific arrangement will bind stays open until a ground settles it.
