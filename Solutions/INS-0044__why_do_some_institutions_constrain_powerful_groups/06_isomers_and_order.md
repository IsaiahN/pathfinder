# What a constraint is made of: bonds, cycles and order

**Mode: `specified` on the institutional application, `general` on the bond definitions.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The same formal ingredients, an impartiality clause, an oversight body, a sanction, a news media, an electorate, can be arranged into an institution that constrains a powerful group or into one that reproduces the group's advantage. If the ingredients are the same, the difference is carried by the arrangement. Two questions are owed:

1. Which bond types compose a constraint, and which compose a reproduction loop?
2. What is the dependency order among the links, so that effort is not spent above a break?

## DERIVATION

### Q1. Is a constraint a different substance from a reproduction loop, or just a different quantity?

Fig 12 :117:

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fig 12 :94-96:

> **"The arrangement is the substance."**
> **"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."**

The written constitution is the flat formula: it lists the ingredients. The bond is what is not recorded. Two systems with the same list can therefore be isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different behaviour. This is why an audit that reads only the ingredients misses the difference entirely. Fig 12 :5 makes the general point: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*

**Falsifier (pre-registered):** if two institutions can be shown to have identical formal atoms *and* identical bond arrangements while producing opposite constraint outcomes, the isomer claim fails and the difference must come from an unenumerated habitat condition (D4), which would be a different finding, not a repair of this one.

### Q2. Which bonds compose each arrangement?

From the Operators table and Fig 12 :55-89:

- **Conjunction (+, symmetric):** oversight body AND statute AND budget both hold. This is the ingredient list. It is necessary and insufficient; it is the flat case.
- **Sequence (→, ordered):** allegation → investigation → sanction. The order matters: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"* (Fig 12 :72). A sanction that precedes an investigation is a different substance.
- **Production (⇒, ordered):** a disclosure produces an investigation that did not exist before. *"is there a B before A fires? No."* (Operators table :39).
- **Subtraction (−, ordered):** the sanction removes the advantage. *"A with B removed or absent"* (Operators table :43). Fig 12 :112: *"progress is a subtraction"*.
- **Comparison (⋛, ordered):** the sanction is larger than the group's capacity to absorb it. *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* (Operators table :48). This is the decisive bond: a check that can be violated but whose sanction is smaller than the gain from violating it does not constrain.
- **Negation (¬, not a bond):** the check refuses a claim the powerful group holds. Fig 12's amendment :55-56: *"What it removes is not a term but a term's standing to be composed over. Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach."*

A constraint is therefore `disclosure ⇒ investigation → sanction − advantage`, with the sanction verified by ⋛ against the group's absorption capacity, and with ¬ available so a held claim can be refused rather than merely accumulated around. A reproduction loop replaces ¬ with +: the group's advantage keeps composing with more advantages, and nothing can be refused. Fig 12's operators-table note :57-58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."*

**Falsifier:** if a functioning constraint can be written with + and ⋛ only, with no negation anywhere (no refusal of any claim), the claim that the absence of ¬ is what makes a vocabulary accumulate is wrong for this domain.

### Q3. What is the cycle, and what decides its sign?

Fig 12 :121:

> **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**

This is the generational finding of F3 in `01`. The output of one generation (wealth, network access, familiarity with the rules) re-enters as the input to the next. If the sign is positive, the loop closes and advantage compounds. If the sign is negative, at least one link subtracts from what re-enters and the loop opens. A chain, by contrast, cannot close: it just hands something along. So "reproduced across generations" is not one long chain but a cycle that has closed; "constrained" is a chain in which a subtracting and refusing link has been inserted.

Fig 12 :119 adds the second structural object:

> **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**

A norm, an independent press or an audit office that survives its own use is a catalyst. A captured checker is spent in its first use and regenerates as a different thing. Nothing in the written rule records which one it is, which is exactly why the per-slot residual of Fig 1 :19 is required.

**Falsifier:** if a reproduction loop can be shown to close with no re-entry of output as input, that is, by a one-off transfer rather than a cycle, the cycle claim fails and the finding collapses into a stock-and-flow account, which the figures do not license here.

### Q4. Which structures are held, and which are a functional group?

Fig 12 :124:

> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

Across the four domains, the shared functional group is `state → structure → state`:

- D1: jurisdiction (space) through a rule (structure) to an outcome over time.
- D2: resources (energy) through an influence network (structure) to future jurisdiction.
- D3: an allegation (state) through an audit/investigation (structure) to a verdict (state).
- D4: contact at one stratum through a cascade (structure) to contact at the next.

The group says *what kind* of thing each domain is; the qualifier says which member (which check, which group). Fig 13 :22 and :24 supply the two structures that do most of the work: *"a difference that can be spent"* and *"that entities relate, and influence travels."* Concentrated resources are a spendable difference; networks are what let influence travel. This is a binding from the social frame onto held atoms, declared as such, and Fig 13 :34-35 warns that the two kinds are not interchangeable: *"Neither kind generates anything alone. Neither denotes anything alone."*

**Falsifier:** if the four domains cannot each be written as `state → structure → state` without inventing a new primitive, the functional-group claim fails and the domains must be re-censused as unrelated.

### Q5. What order do the links run in?

Fig 3 :20-21:

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**

For this question the chain is perception → vocabulary → objective → planning → learn and carry. The middle link is the one with the fewest instruments: Fig 3 :25-26 *"having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."* The institutional consequence is sharp: a body that can count violations (perception) but cannot say what would count as its own success (objective) has a broken middle link. It will optimise the measurement it already has. Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*

Order by dependency is also why `→` must not be read as a rhetorical arrow in `01`. Fig 12 :72 defines it as a real ordering constraint. The repository's own exemplar had to retract an ordering it had added without license; here the ordering is licensed by Fig 3, not by the six-term set.

**Falsifier:** if a reform at the planning link can be shown to restore a broken objective link without any change to vocabulary or measurement, the dependency order claim is wrong for this domain.

### Q6. Why one lever leaves the intersections standing

Fig 9 :55-56:

> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**

Fig 9 :57:

> **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"**

The four domains form such a union. An impartiality clause (D1) addresses the anchor condition; independent media (D3) addresses the overlap condition; civic and educational contact (D4) addresses the channel; all three must hold at once. A lever that clears only D1 reduces the pile but leaves the compound, and the residue becomes *categorically different*, not merely smaller (Fig 9 :58-59). This is the structural reason the "why some, why others" question cannot be answered by a single variable.

## CONCLUSION

The two institutional outcomes are **isomers** over a shared atom set: same formal ingredients, different bonds, different substance. A constraint is a chain in which a disclosure produces an investigation, an ordered sequence reaches a subtraction of the advantage, a comparison verifies the sanction is large enough, and negation is available so a held claim can be refused. Reproduction is a cycle whose sign has closed: output re-enters as input, a catalyst is spent in its own use, and a vocabulary without ¬ can only accumulate. The four domains form a functional group with the same `state → structure → state` signature, and they are a union rather than a partition, so one lever clears single-condition regions and leaves intersections standing. The dependency order is perception → vocabulary → objective → planning → learn and carry, and the middle link is where the least instrumentation exists.

This rescues the defensible part of the question without naming a country or a reform: the bonds and the cycle are checkable structural claims; the binding of social vocabulary onto held atoms is a declared choice; the specific institutional arrangement that would close the gap is not derived.
