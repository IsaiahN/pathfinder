## QUESTION

Why do some institutions constrain powerful groups while others reproduce their advantages across generations?

Restated as an effect: why does the residual R = |Γ(b,a) − o′| stay open for one check-slot and close for another, and why does it re-open in each new generation for some groups but not others? R is indexed per check and per group (Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*). The anchor is the ground, that is, what actually happens to the powerful group's action. Option A: attribute the difference to formal rule content. Option B: attribute it to anchor status, evidence overlap and channel reachability, with rule content as the thing that can be violated. A sends a rule-count verdict that fits both outcomes and cannot distinguish them. B sends a checkable shape with four refuters and an honest gap. B is what the figures govern.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Arity: a four-place relation, (check, powerful group, action, generation). Symmetry: the decisive relation is a threshold comparison (⋛), *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* (Operators table :48), not a symmetric join: the sanction must be larger than the group's capacity to absorb it. Scale: a single decision loop, an institutional loop and a generational chain, with a lossy membrane between them (Fig 4 :27 *"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."*).
- **D1 Institutional frame.** Actors: branches, agencies, courts, statutes and budgets. Currency: formal authority and compliance. Anchor: the ground, what actually happens to the group's action. Status: admitted.
- **D2 Powerful group as a frame.** Actors: firms, wealth holders, families and professional networks. Currency: money, access, appointments and information. Anchor: its own closure and reproduction across generations. Status: admitted.
- **D3 Checking seat.** Actors: auditors, judges, journalists, watchdogs, researchers and citizens. Currency: evidence and residuals. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Status: admitted.
- **D4 Habitat and contact cascade.** Actors: education, finance, media, professions, family and donors. Currency: contact. Anchor: the ground, which does not decay. Status: admitted.
- **X1 Good rules as a free-floating property.** No nameable anchor, because the rule scores itself. Not a domain yet; absorbed into D1 as a frame-internal quantity.
- **X2 Culture as sole cause.** No anchor independent of D4. Absorbed into D4 as a habitat condition.
- **Union, not partition.** Fig 9 :57 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*. Constraint requires an unmoved anchor AND low-overlap checking AND a reachable channel. A single lever clears single-condition regions and leaves intersections standing (Fig 9 :55-56).
- **Order by dependency.** Perception → vocabulary → objective → planning → learn and carry (Fig 3 :20-21). The middle link has the fewest instruments (Fig 3 :25-27).

Strain test applied: mapping the six-term substrate onto social vocabulary is a `specified` binding, not a derivation; if a line fits only through that mapping it governs the social frame and returns to D1 to D4, not to physics. After re-census no figure line strains against another.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

Constraint versus reproduction is decided by anchor status and contact, not by formal rule content. The check must not update on the group, must be ranged against by both parties, and must be able to register and sanction a violation; the checkers must draw on separate evidence and declare what stake cannot be removed; the channel from check to outcome must survive a generation. Reproduction is a closed cycle handing the same closure to the next cohort with coherent drift; constraint is a chain with a subtracting and refusing link inserted. The four domains form a union, and the two outcomes are isomers over a shared atom set. The specific arrangement that will bind in a given case is a `gap`, reported as unreachable with the missing instrument described in effect terms. This is `specified`, ungrounded, candidate. Verdicts per domain are kept separate and never averaged.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, refuters and lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a recording instead of a generator (Fig 4 :25 *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*). Fig 2 :42 *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter and recording the verdict before any theory is shown.
- **undoable:** yes. The shape can be revised without discarding the method. A broken anchor claim, a failed overlap test or a contact result that contradicts D4 removes the affected claim and leaves the census intact. The four refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are written so withdrawal is a recorded action, not a silent edit.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A documented pinned-anchor case with unchanged reproduction, a shared-pool case with constraint as strong as the low-overlap case, a controlled rule-content effect with anchor and contact fixed, or a character-only improvement with contact unchanged would each break the corresponding claim. A discriminator that reports low overlap and channel reachability before outcomes would close the F9 gap. A derivation of one of the six held terms from the other five, or a figure re-census that admits a state-state bond without an intervening structure, would break the substrate and require a full rerun. Prestige, a large panel of agreeable readers and a governance index are not such instruments.
