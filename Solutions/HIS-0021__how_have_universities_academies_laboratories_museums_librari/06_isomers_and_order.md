## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Are two institutions with the same channels isomers?

Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
Fig 12 :5 "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance."

Take a fixed atom set `{representation, library, import, membrane, ground, seat, habitat, room chain}`. A university and an academy can both occupy library, import, membrane, seat and habitat. If they differ only in which bond mediates which channel, they are **isomers**: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. A library that sits between a researcher and a differing closure (import) is a different substance from the same library used to carry a recording down to the next generation (membrane), even though the atom list is identical.

This is the structural reason a list of "institutional features" cannot predict the effect. The ingredient list is not the molecule; the bond is the half nothing was recording. Fig 13 :121 "Different routes can arrive at the same substance." The converse also holds: same ingredients under a different bond give a different substance.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two institutions with identical channel sets and demonstrably different bond arrangements produce the same effect on the same residual against the same anchor, the isomer claim fails and they are one institutional type with two names.

### Q2 — Is the order in "institution X led to result Y" licensed?

Fig 12 :33-34 (Operators table :29-34) defines `→` as sequence: "A then B; B needs A to have happened", with the test "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes." The Operators table warns: "the only bond a flat chain can express, so every term today is a sequence whether or not it should be."

Two cases must be separated:

- **Co-presence.** An academy, a library, a correspondence network and a journal can all be active at once in the Republic of Letters. Their relation is `+` conjunction ("both hold at once", Operators table :12-15), a symmetric bond. Fig 12 :92 "Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term". Counting "which came first" here overcharges by log₂(k!) and manufactures a false sequence.
- **Real succession.** Where a settled molecule becomes an atom for the next level (Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it"), a genuine `→` is licensed: a method must be settled before it can be carried as a head start. That is an order in the dependency chain (Fig 3 :20-21), not a historical date ordering.

Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." carries a direction but not a ranking: it is a filter with a direction, not a wall (Fig 4 :21). Historians who write "the academy caused the discovery" are usually asserting a `⇒` production (Fig 12 :76 "A produces B; B did not exist before") where the evidence supports only `+` co-presence. The operator must be chosen by the six tests, not by narrative convenience. Fig 12 :90 "Six tests, and they separate the seven bonds."

**Falsifier:** if swapping two co-present institutions changes nothing about the residual, yet the historical account requires a sequence, the sequence is an artifact of notation, not a result.

### Q3 — Catalyst, cycle and functional group in the historical record

Fig 12 :119 "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."
Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
Fig 12 :123 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

- **Catalyst.** A method or an instrument that survives its own use is a catalyst. An institution that publishes a method without the derivation spends the method; an institution that trains students in the derivation regenerates it. This is why "how many papers" and "how many practitioners" are different readings. Fig 12 :107-110 notes that where the arrangement is not recorded, "every lineage pays again."
- **Cycle.** Fabien Simon (EHNE) reports that the Republic of Letters' journal "institutionalized the art of the letter, and established a system of reciprocity: publication was a reward for correspondants, who were encouraged to send new letters to share their experiences, thereby sustaining the journal." That is a positive cycle with the output re-entering as input. Fig 7 :12-13 "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not" is the negative case: a cycle with no ground pressure runs in place. The sign of the loop decides which one you have.
- **Functional group.** Across all six institution types, the substructure that fixes the effect is a **contact group**: an instrument returning something, a library that can compose over it, and a reference that does not update. The group says what kind of effect is possible (D1+D2+D5); the qualifier says which institution supplies each member in a given period. This is a structural claim, not a historical finding.

**Falsifier:** a case where the contact group is present, the method is regenerated, the cycle is positively signed, and no change in what could be known follows against the ground. That would break the functional-group claim.

### Q4 — What the two readings cost

Fig 12 :125 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."
Fig 12 :126 "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."

An institutional history can be cheap in description (a one-line story: "the academy did it") and enormous in actions (the archives needed to settle it). Or it can be expensive to state (eight channels, per-slot verdicts) and cheap to refute (one counterexample to a channel). The two prices do not add. Fig 12 :130 "Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it." A ranking of institutions by "impact" sums incommensurable currencies and is therefore not a verdict.

## CONCLUSION

Over a fixed channel set, two institutions that differ only in bond arrangement are **isomers** (Fig 12 :117), so a feature list cannot determine the effect. Co-presence is `+` (symmetric), while a real succession is `→` or `⇒` only when a settled molecule becomes an atom at the next level (Fig 12 :104); a flat narrative silently converts the first into the second. The historical record supplies a genuine cycle (the correspondence/journal reciprocity) and a functional group (instrument plus library plus non-updating reference). All three are structural shapes with stated falsifiers, not historical attributions. No new primitive is needed, and nothing here is `grounded`.
