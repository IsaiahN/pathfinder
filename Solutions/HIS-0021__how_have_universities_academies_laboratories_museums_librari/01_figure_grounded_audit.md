## 0. The residual under audit, in effect terms

The question is: "How have universities, academies, laboratories, museums, libraries, and government agencies shaped what could be known?"

Restated as an effect, "shaped what could be known" is not one quantity. It is a bundle of changes to the reach and to the contact of a knowing frame:

1. what instruments can represent at all;
2. which atoms (terms, instruments, observations) and bonds (methods, conventions, standard classifications) sit in the library Γ;
3. whether the wall of closure(Γ) moves through contact with a frame whose closure differs;
4. what crosses the membrane between one generation or one site and the next, methods or recordings, head starts or replays;
5. whether the anchor that settles claims stays reachable and non-updating;
6. whether a seat able to refuse a verdict exists and is not authored by the claim it checks;
7. which actors, conditions and relations are in contact with the residual and can cascade into it;
8. whether the next room in a chain differs from the last and matches the residual the last one surfaced.

Option A reads the question as a single lever: institutions "decide what is true," so the historian should rank institutions by how much they determined belief. Option B reads it as a union of mechanisms with different anchors and returns a per-domain shape, refusing any ranking by influence. What each leads to: A turns agreement into a verdict and an updating reference into an anchor, which is the named collapse; B returns checkable mechanisms, separate verdicts and a refuter, and leaves the historical attribution open. The figures govern B, as shown below.

## 1. R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity.** The residual has at least eight channels (listed in §0). It is a **union**, not a partition. Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55-57 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds." The six institution names are not six channels; each is an actor that spans several channels at once. A laboratory is an instrument site, a ground site and a habitat actor. A university is a membrane, an import site and a seat.
- **Symmetry.** The effect is **ordered and residual-relative**, not symmetric. Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." The same academy that keeps a ground reachable for one question can be a mirror chain for another. A pooled "institutions helped science" verdict has the wrong symmetry.
- **Scale.** The effect operates inside one scale (a lab bench, a library, a room) and across scales (generation to generation, local academy to Republic of Letters). Fig 4 :21 "The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction." The residual also registers per slot: Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." and Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." So the verdict must be per institution and per channel, never an average over six institution types.

**No nameable anchor means it is not a domain.** Each of D1 to D8 has one. D3's anchor is not the importing institution's prestige but the provenance and shadow test (Fig 8 :12 "source gate: provenance", :13 "shadow test: explains a gap"). A famous academy is not an anchor. D5's anchor is the measurement, not the society that published it (Fig 2 :29-30). D8's anchor is the ground's pressure, not the reputation of the room (Fig 7 :37-39).

**Partition or union.** Union, with overlap. D3 and D4 overlap in libraries and academies; D5 and D6 overlap in academies and agencies; D1 and D7 overlap in laboratories. These are compounds with their own loops, so one lever does not clear the intersections (Fig 9 :55-57). The correct move is to unbundle, then run one loop per domain, not to average.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** D1 perception (what can be represented) -> D2 vocabulary (what is in Γ) -> D5 objective (what counts as progress, read against an anchor) -> D6 planning (a seat able to refuse) -> D4 carry, D3 import, D7 habitat, D8 room chain. Fig 3 :20-21 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it." Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Historically this matters: improving an academy's publication rate (above the break) cannot rescue a channel in which no anchor is reachable (D5). Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

## 4. Findings

### F1 — The question is a union, and the six names are habitats, not channels (decisive)

Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
Fig 9 :55-57 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."
Fig 11 :45 "actors: what acts on it, and what it acts on"
Fig 11 :46 "conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"
Fig 11 :47 "relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"

The six listed institutions are **actors enumerated from the habitat**, not six independent variables. Fig 11 :43-44 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed. List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." The relevant conditions include funding, patronage, censorship, war and print; the relations include correspondence, competition, training and regulation. These are read off the world, not composed from the six names (Fig 11 :48 "You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual.").

**Refuter:** show that the eight channels reduce to one variable with one anchor such that a single lever clears all intersections. Until then, per-domain verdicts are kept separate and never averaged.

**Triangulation status:** triangulated across Fig 9 (union/partition) and Fig 11 (habitat enumeration), two figures with low conceptual overlap.

### F2 — What institutions shape is reach and contact, not the ground

Fig 2 :29-30 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."
Fig 11 :91 "The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."
Fig 6 :26 "The world does not gain primitives; a frame gains access. Composition explores what the operators reach; instruments extend what can be represented at all."

An institution can widen what is representable (D1), enlarge Γ (D2), move the wall by importing from a differing closure (D3), and keep or sever the channel to an anchor (D5). It cannot make a claim true by deciding it. Fig 2 :45 "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." and Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

This is the honest replacement for the tempting answer "institutions constructed scientific truth." That phrase promotes a frame to the anchor, which is panel 1 of Fig 2. The figure-governed claim is narrower and stronger: institutions changed the reach and the contact; the ground still decided which claims paid.

**Refuter:** a documented case in which an institutional decree changed what the ground returned, with the anchor shown to move when pushed. (A decree can change what gets published or funded; that is D2/D5, not the ground.)

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 2, Fig 6 and Fig 11. Low overlap.

### F3 — Instruments extend what can be represented, and they are found, not specified

Fig 6 :32 "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."
Fig 6 :33 "Nobody specified a telescope from an account of Mars. A wandering point in the sky was already a reading, and every sharper instrument was built on the residual the last one produced."
Fig 6 :34-35 "The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve."
Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

Laboratories, observatories and museums-as-collections are instrument habitats. Their effect is not to add a primitive to the world but to add access: a new reading that was already there at some resolution but unresolved. This is why the historical record shows instruments being improved from prior instruments rather than deduced from a verbal specification. It also explains the second-order effect: a new instrument creates a residual in the existing Γ, which drives a new mint.

**World corroboration (secondary only):** Pardo Tomás 2020 writes that through these networks travelled "instruments and apparatus indispensable for all these observations and for experiments in chemistry or physics," and that "Science is not an imaginary entity in a void: it is material culture." Clark and Chalmers 1998, from human cognition, write that "the general paraphernalia of language, books, diagrams, and culture" are delegated operations, and that "Cognitive processes ain't (all) in the head!" Both predict that material supports are part of the knowing system, matching Fig 6 :26.

**Refuter:** a case where an instrument was specified from a pure description with no prior reading and no ancestor instrument, and nonetheless worked first time.

**Status:** triangulated (Fig 6 + Fig 3) for the figure claim; world corroborators are secondary and from different fields.

### F4 — The wall moves only through contact with a differing closure, and that contact has a cost

Fig 6 :14 "the only operator that moves the wall"
Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."
Fig 8 :45 "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."
Fig 8 :46 "Composition crosses the boundary between them, and that surplus is where imports live."
Fig 8 :12 "source gate: provenance"
Fig 8 :13 "shadow test: explains a gap"
Fig 6 :24 "Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."

Academies, universities, correspondence networks, libraries and museums are the classic contact zones. Their legitimate wall-moving effect is import from a closure that differs, subjected to a provenance gate and a shadow test. This is exactly what the Republic of Letters supplied: a low-overlap network whose value was the differing closures it connected, not the agreement it produced. Fig 8 :48-49 "How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."

The cost clause matters historically. Import from a live peer is debited against independence. A famous academy whose members all read the same reports is one frame wearing many names (Fig 8 :50 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents."), so its agreement cannot corroborate an import. The same mechanism that gives the surplus can destroy the evidence for it.

**World corroboration:** Fabien Simon (EHNE, 2017/2026 edition) writes that "Universities and colleges—places for the transmission of knowledge—served as such. More or less formalized scientific academies represented its bulwarks, from the Florentine Accademia del Cimento (1657) to l'Académie des sciences (1666) and the Royal Society (1660)." Pardo Tomás 2020 notes that "new spaces arose in the spaces where book, maps and scientific journals were circulated: aristocratic salons, the rooms of academies and societies, cabinets of curiosities, gardens and theatres." These are contact zones, matching D3.

**Refuter:** a case in which a closure added a new atom by composition alone, with no contact with a frame whose closure differed and no instrument built from a prior reading.

**Status:** triangulated (Fig 6 + Fig 8), two figures with low overlap on the operator versus the source of the atom.

### F5 — Only methods cross the membrane; a growing library is not a growing reach

Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
Fig 4 :23 "upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."
Fig 4 :24 "downward: something that makes the next search cheaper. Never a replay of what worked once."
Fig 4 :25 "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."
Fig 5 :72 "a library that grows is not a reach that grows."
Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"

Universities, academies, journals and museums are the membrane stations. They shaped what could be known when they carried a re-applicable method or a head start. They failed to shape it when they carried a recording of one occasion, a set of conclusions without the derivation, or a settled result without the arrangement that produced it. Fig 12 :5 "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." A catalogue that lists instruments and results but not the bond (the method, the arrangement) records half of itself.

Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." The dead-reckoning trap here is to count volumes acquired or degrees awarded and call that a growth of reach. Those are frame-internal quantities. Fig 1 :26 "predicates minted, and credibility accrued" and Fig 1 :27 "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."

**Refuter:** a case where a verbatim replay of one past success, with the method discarded, reliably generated a new result in a new habitat. The test harness case is the standing counterexample in the other direction: Fig 11 :70 "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."

**Status:** triangulated (Fig 4 + Fig 5 + Fig 12).

### F6 — An anchor that updates on the institution's own reports is not an anchor

Fig 2 :33 "The anchor must not update."
Fig 2 :34 "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."
Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"
Fig 2 :41 "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"
Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does"
Fig 10 :26 "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"

This is the sharpest institutional finding. Laboratories and academies can keep an anchor reachable (measurement, experiment, counter). Government agencies, standard setters and prestige hierarchies can instead let the reference update on their own publications, ratings or categories. When that happens, agreement within the system is the pool talking, and the drift is coherent, which is why it is invisible from inside. Fig 7 :12-13 "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not."

Two distinct goods must not be confused. Fig 2 :41 says agreement about a shared meaning is efficient for a convention. Shared standards, nomenclatures and unit systems are legitimate conventions; their product is coordination. They become defects only when a convention decides an outcome: Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." A government classification that fixes what counts as a disease, a species or a qualified expert has crossed that line, and the institution holding it is no longer a ground maintainer.

**World corroboration:** Bowker and Star 1999 describe classification as "a set of boxes (metaphorical or literal) into which things can be put in order to then do some kind of work - bureaucratic or knowledge production," and state that "Building the ICD involved building the state as much as developing medical knowledge." This is D5/D6: a bureaucracy turning uncertain findings into official categories. Merton 1942 says "organized skepticism is both a methodological and an institutional mandate," the normative form of a refusal seat. Both are secondary corroboration; neither decides the figure verdict.

**Refuter:** a case where an institution's published rating moved the ground itself, demonstrated by pushing the rating and observing the ground move. If only belief moved, it was a frame.

**Status:** triangulated (Fig 2 + Fig 10 + Fig 7).

### F7 — A seat able to refuse is a prerequisite, and a vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate

Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."
Operators table :58 "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"
Fig 12 :112 "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."
Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"
Fig 10 :59 "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"
Fig 10 :60 "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."

Universities and academies shaped what could be known partly by providing a seat whose occupant can refuse a claim, and partly by failing: a chair, a journal or an agency that publishes only confirmations has removed the negation operator from its vocabulary. Fig 12 :112 shows that a vocabulary can name a plan, a goal and progress and still not name "this is false"; the operators table names the missing unary operator. Negation is not a bond: it refuses a claim rather than rearranging parts.

Merton's "organized skepticism" is the world-source analogue, not the authority. His universalism, that truth claims "are to be subjected to preestablished impersonal criteria," is also a seat rule, but it is a sociology-of-science description, not a figure line.

**Refuter:** a case where an institution with no refusal seat reliably corrected a claim solely by accumulating more confirmations, with the correction traceable to the confirmations rather than to an outside ground.

**Status:** single-source on the figure side for the unary-operator form (Operators table), triangulated with Fig 10 for the seat form. Kept separate, not upgraded.

### F8 — Contact cascades, and valence flips when the residual changes

Fig 11 :40 "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
Fig 11 :39 "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"
Fig 11 :52-54 "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."
Fig 11 :61 "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."
Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."

Institutional effects are properties of the researcher-and-habitat pair, not of the institution alone. Funding, censorship, print, patronage and rival institutions are conditions and relations in contact with the residual, often two or three steps out. This is why a "great academy" can be a benefactor for one residual and a malfactor for another (Fig 11 :49, :51). Isolation (exile, closure of an academy, loss of a library) substitutes a habitat; it does not subtract one, and it carries two silent failures: what you failed to reproduce and what you unintentionally introduced (Fig 11 :67-69, cited in `06`).

**World corroboration:** Fogarty 2024, in evolutionary biology (genuinely low overlap), defines niche construction as the process whereby "organisms actively modify, or construct, aspects of their own environments and, in doing so, alter the natural selection pressures to which they are subject," and notes persistent modifications can form part of an offspring's niche. That is a mechanism-shape analogue of D7 and D4, not evidence about historians.

**Refuter:** a case where an institution's effect on what could be known was identical across two residuals with different anchors, with the contact enumeration held fixed.

**Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 + Fig 4 on substitution), though the analogous world source is low-overlap corroboration only.

### F9 — Rooms must differ and match the residual they surfaced

Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
Fig 7 :36 "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."
Fig 7 :37 "Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:" (the two factors named on the following lines are the bracket's fidelity and the ground's pressure on the room)
Fig 7 :6 "the limit: no last room"
Fig 10 :72 "When the question changes, the room should change."

A succession of universities or academies that all read the same corpus and cite the same authorities is a mirror chain: motion without progress. Progress requires that the next room differ at the evidence level and that it be summoned by the residual the last one surfaced. This is a constraint on institutional succession, not on institutional size. A small heterodox shop can be a differing room; a large academy can be one long room.

**Refuter:** a chain in which every room was a close copy of the last and the ground nevertheless voted at each handoff, with the ground's pressure shown independent of the differences.

**Status:** triangulated (Fig 7 + Fig 10).

### F10 — No figure line names which institution caused which historical result (declared `gap`)

Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"
Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"
Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
Fig 9 :44 "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."

After a genuine census, no figure line selects a named institution for a named advance, a percentage of influence, a ranking of the six types, or a date at which an institution "caused" a discovery. The framework returns a shape, not a name, and the record of which institution contacts which residual is a historical ground question requiring archives, instruments and dated sources that this derivation does not hold. This is reported as `gap` and `unreachable at this budget`, not as silence and not as underivable in principle. Fig 8 :39 is explicit that a search finding nothing is not a proof of absence.

This is the honest stop. It is not an invitation to manufacture a ranking from prestige, and it is not a claim that institutions did not matter. It is a claim about what the figures can return.

**Status:** `gap`, triangulated across Fig 9, Fig 13 and Fig 8 on the limit of what a closure can return.

### F11 — The world sources corroborate mechanisms, not the verdict; overlap is declared

The substantive world sources read were: Merton 1942/1973 (sociology of science), Pardo Tomás 2020 and Fabien Simon (history of science / Republic of Letters), Bowker and Star 1999 (classification and bureaucracy), Gooday 2008 and Schmidgen 2021 (laboratory history), Clark and Chalmers 1998 (human cognition), and Fogarty 2024 (evolutionary biology). Full details, quotes, accessed date and overlap are in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

None conflicts with the figure verdict; the conflict rule was not triggered. But the three Republic of Letters / circulation sources share an evidence pool and count as one witness. The two laboratory-history sources count as one witness. The management-like claim from Merton and Bowker/Star share a science-studies pool. Only Clark and Chalmers (cognition) and Fogarty (evolutionary biology) are genuinely low-overlap, and they corroborate mechanism shapes (external carriers, niche construction), not the historical attribution. Agreement inside one pool does not become corroboration by being repeated (Fig 2 :40).

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Required before any outreach: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device (Seat Map, a local framework file). The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. The eight channels reduce to one variable with a single anchor such that one lever clears all intersections (breaks F1).
2. An institution's decree is shown to move the ground itself, with the anchor pushed and observed to move (breaks F2, F6).
3. A closure adds a new atom by composition alone, with no differing frame and no prior instrument reading (breaks F3, F4).
4. A verbatim replay of one past success, with the method discarded, generates a new result in a new habitat (breaks F5).
5. An institution with no refusal seat reliably corrects claims by accumulating confirmations alone, with the correction traceable to the confirmations (breaks F7).
6. Identical contact enumeration yields identical institutional effect across two residuals with different anchors (breaks F8).
7. A chain of close-copy rooms consistently receives the ground's vote independent of their differences (breaks F9).
8. A figure line is found that names a specific institution for a specific historical result with a stated ground (closes F10's `gap`).

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn. Until one is run, nothing here is `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Lead with the union finding (F1) and the reach-versus-ground distinction (F2). Do not send a ranking of institutions.
2. Run the loop per domain and keep verdicts separate. Never average D1 through D8 into an "institutional impact" score (Fig 1 :19, :22).
3. State mode on every claim. The historical attribution is `gap`; the mechanisms are `specified`; the structure is `general`.
4. Put the refuter before the sources in any outreach (Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source.").
5. Use the public institutional venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`, one-to-one, asking what would refute the shape (Fig 2 :35; Fig 7 :35).
