# Outreach draft — check the shape before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the verdict fields in `07_the_tether_answer.md` and the three sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` are stated in the first message and the question "what would refute this?" is asked before any source link appears. The check must survive contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about institutions and what could be known

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a historical question and have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on mechanisms, `general` on the shape, not `grounded`).** If a research community holds a library of terms, instruments and methods, then an institution changes what could be known only by acting on a short list of channels, and each channel has a different anchor. It can extend what instruments can register at all. It can add atoms to the library. It can move the wall of what is composable, but only by contact with a group whose library differs, and that contact has to pass a provenance and a shadow test. It can carry a re-applicable method to the next generation, or a recording of one occasion that looks like knowledge and is not. It can keep a measurement reachable as a reference that does not move when the institution pushes on it, or it can let the reference drift with its own reports. It can hold a seat able to refuse a claim, or lose the operator for refusal and accumulate forever. The same institution can help one question and harm another, because its effect is relative to the question, not a property it carries.
>
> **What this is not.** It is not a claim that institutions decided what was true. A decree can change what gets published or funded; it cannot change what the ground returns. It is not a ranking of universities, academies, laboratories, museums, libraries and agencies by influence. The figures I am working with return a shape, not a name, and they give no percentage and no historical attribution. Nothing here is a measurement.
>
> **What would refute it, written before I sent this:**
> 1. show that the channels reduce to one variable with one anchor, so that a single lever clears all of them;
> 2. show an institution's decree moving the ground itself, with the reference pushed and observed to move;
> 3. show a library adding a genuinely new primitive by rearranging what it already held, with no differing group and no prior instrument reading;
> 4. show a verbatim replay of one past success with the method discarded reliably producing a new result in a new setting;
> 5. name a figure line that selects a particular institution for a particular historical discovery with a stated ground.
>
> If any of those holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would value from you:** not endorsement, but which of these channels you would expect to find in the sources for the period you know, and what you would count as a failure of the claim. Please answer that before looking at any framework material, so your verdict is recorded while you still stand outside it.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send three public links. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the formula is in the links. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it; a reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
