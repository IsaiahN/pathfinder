## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 9 :54 (union), Fig 11 :91 (habitat versus ground), Fig 6 :23 (import) and Fig 7 :35 (room selection).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07` and `09`: eight channels with different anchors, union not partition, reach and contact changed but not the ground, no ranking returned.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete historian's answer would be: the Royal Society did X in year Y, the academy did Z, and together they account for a share of the observed change.

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **per-slot indexing** (Fig 1 :19), which a global "institutional impact" narrative drops;
- the **source gate and shadow test** on imports (Fig 8 :12-13), which a list of well-known institutions substitutes with prestige;
- the **method-versus-recording distinction** at the membrane (Fig 4 :23-25), which a count of volumes or degrees erases;
- the **gap itself** (Fig 13 :134), which a concrete narrative fills with a named cause.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: institutions changed reach and contact, and the ground still settled. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no archive or instrument reading exists in this derivation; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the formula as identical to the figures would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
