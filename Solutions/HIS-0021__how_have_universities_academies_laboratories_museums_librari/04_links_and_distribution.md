## Wider literature that bears on the question

The sources above are deliberately split across low-overlap fields. The three Republic of Letters and circulation sources (Pardo Tomás, Simon, and the library-history abstract found in search) are **one evidence pool**, not three witnesses. The two laboratory-history sources (Gooday, Schmidgen) are one pool. Merton and Bowker and Star share a science-studies pool. Only Clark and Chalmers (human cognition) and Fogarty (evolutionary biology) are genuinely low overlap, and they corroborate mechanism shapes, not the historical attribution. No source conflicted with the figure verdict, so the conflict rule was not triggered.

What each predicts here:

1. **Material carriers.** Pardo Tomás and Clark and Chalmers predict that instruments, books and external records are part of the knowing system, matching the instrument and library channels (Fig 6 :26, Fig 4 :22).
2. **Contact zones.** Simon predicts that academies, universities and libraries mattered as nodes connecting differing closures, matching import (Fig 6 :23) and the union surplus (Fig 8 :45).
3. **Categories as institutional products.** Bowker and Star predict that government and professional classification turns uncertain findings into official boxes, matching the convention-outcome crossing (Fig 10 :47).
4. **Sites and habitats.** Gooday and Schmidgen predict that laboratories were not fixed, placeless spaces but changing sites embedded in other institutions, matching the habitat and membrane reading (Fig 11 :43-44, Fig 4 :21).
5. **Refusal as an institution.** Merton predicts that organized skepticism is an institutional mandate, matching the refusal seat (Fig 10 :42), though he grounds it sociologically rather than in a unary operator.
