# Expert verification packet — the Tether on institutions and what could be known

**What this is:** a claim in a form a historian of science can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the mechanism claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on historical attribution. Not `grounded`.** No archive measurement or dated provenance chain is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a historical result. Simulated derivation, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question is a union of eight channels with different anchors, not a single lever, and the six named institution types are habitat actors spanning those channels rather than six independent variables. Institutions shaped what could be known by changing reach and contact: what instruments could register, what a library could compose, whether a differing closure could be contacted, what method crossed to the next generation, whether a non-updating reference stayed reachable, whether a refusal seat survived, which actors were in contact with the residual, and whether the next room differed from the last. Institutions did not change what the ground returned. A decree changes publication or funding, not the ground. Verdicts per channel are kept separate and never averaged, and no figure line returns a ranking or a historical attribution.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 9, 11 and 2

- **Statement:** the residual is a union of overlapping channels, the six institution names are habitat actors, and institutions change reach and contact but not the ground.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 11 :43-44 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed. List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." Fig 11 :91 "The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."
- **Refuter:** reduce the channels to one variable with one anchor such that one lever clears all intersections; or show an institution's decree moving the ground itself with the anchor pushed and observed to move.

### C2 — history-facing; checkable against the sources in `10_source_ledger.csv`

- **Statement:** the instrument, library, import, membrane and cycle channels are visible in the historical record, and the Republic of Letters is a real low-overlap contact zone with a positive correspondence cycle, not a single institution acting alone.
- **What to check:** Pardo Tomás 2020 describes letters, books, journals and instruments travelling together as "material culture" through "aristocratic salons, the rooms of academies and societies, cabinets of curiosities, gardens and theatres." Fabien Simon (EHNE) says "Universities and colleges—places for the transmission of knowledge—served as such. More or less formalized scientific academies represented its bulwarks" and that the journal "established a system of reciprocity: publication was a reward for correspondants, who were encouraged to send new letters." Gooday 2008 and Schmidgen 2021 show the laboratory was not a placeless, fixed type, which is what the habitat reading predicts. Fig 6 :32 "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something." Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
- **Refuter:** show a library adding a genuinely new primitive by composition alone with no differing closure and no prior instrument reading; show a verbatim replay of one past success with the method discarded reliably producing a new result in a new setting; or show that the three circulation sources are independent witnesses rather than one evidence pool.

### C3 — framework-internal derivation

- **Statement:** a growing library is not a growing reach; a convention that decides an outcome stops being one; and a chain of like-minded rooms is motion, not progress.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :72 "a library that grows is not a reach that grows." Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Fig 7 :36 "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."
- **Refuter:** a case where cataloguing more volumes grew the reach with no new settled term and no differing closure; or a chain of close-copy rooms consistently receiving the ground's vote independent of their differences. Bowker and Star 1999 are the world analogue for a convention deciding an outcome: "each standard and category valorizes some point of view and silences another." That is corroboration only, not the authority.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- No ranking of the six institution types, no percentage, no "most important institution." Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."
- No claim that institutions decided what was true, and no claim that a decree moved the ground. Fig 2 :33 "The anchor must not update."
- No claim that all institutional agreement is corruption. Shared standards are efficient for a convention (Fig 2 :41); they become a defect when they decide an outcome (Fig 10 :47).
- No derivation of one institution from another and no new primitive.
- No claim that a named institution's effect is settled by this packet. The historical attribution is a `gap`, reported as unreachable at this budget, not silence (Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence").
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by an archive measurement.
