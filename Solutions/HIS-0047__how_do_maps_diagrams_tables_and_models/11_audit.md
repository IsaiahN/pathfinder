## 1. Independent domain census

I redid the census from the question and its effect statement, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the systematic difference in reasoning when the encoding changes and the data do not. Arity: at least three relata, representation / ground / reasoner, so this is a frame-to-ground relation, not a label swap. Symmetry: the data-to-representation map is many-to-one and not invertible; the arrangement is not recoverable from the ingredients. Scale: per slot in the cognitive frame, between frames at the community scale. Question type: Fig 9 requires unbundling a union before search.
- **Domain A, notation.** Actors: marks, cells, nodes, edges, axes, glyphs, typed bonds. Currency: expressibility and reach. Anchor: the arrangement encoded. Type: partition of expressible versus inexpressible. Order: first, since a bond must be carryable before reasoning can use it.
- **Domain B, cognition.** Actors: library, slots, belief, weighting, residual. Currency: per-slot residual and inference cost. Anchor: the ground described by the data. Type: union compound. Order: second.
- **Domain C, ground.** Actors: measurements, dataset, world. Currency: measurement. Anchor: the ground, which must not update. Type: anchor, not a frame. Order: zeroth.
- **Domain D, verification.** Actors: investigators, peer frames, publications. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Type: union of frames; surplus only where closures differ. Order: last. Independence in this simulated run is zero: all four domains draw on one evidence pool.
