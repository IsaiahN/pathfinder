# The Tether's answer: how representations shape reasoning

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape; `specified` on the domain placements and on the world-facing cognitive claim. Not `grounded` for the general claim.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading. Specific historical instances are grounded in the cited sources and marked as such.

## 1. The residual it answers

A representation is assumed to be a transparent window: same data in, same results out, the display merely prints what has already been settled. The residual is the systematic gap between that prediction and the fact that swapping representation while holding data fixed changes what is noticed, inferred, hypothesised and mistaken. Under Fig 9 :54 this is a **union compound**, not one hard question: "when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." It needs one loop per domain, with verdicts kept separate.

## 2. The shape, per domain

**Domain A, representation as frame.** A map, diagram, table or model is a frame with a signature, not a transparent channel. Symbols table :111: "A frame is any bounded system with a library: an agent, a person, a discipline, a model. It is a theory with a signature rather than a coordinate system, so frames differ in what they can express and not in how they label it." Its reasoning power is the typed bond it carries. Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Operators table :4 to :5: "The bond carries information the operands do not, so a term that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself." Its failure mode is silent loss: Fig 12 :15 "nothing downstream can tell them apart, and nothing signals that anything was lost."

**Domain B, reasoner.** The effect is per slot. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." A global claim of "same information" throws away the signal, Fig 1 :22. The representation acts at the middle link of the dependency chain: Fig 3 :7 "it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked" and Fig 3 :10 "it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms." Fig 12 :115: "having terms for what a situation affords was taken for a dictionary and it is a grammar." This placement is `specified` and unmeasured.

**Domain C, ground.** A representation extends reach but adds no atom. Fig 6 :29: "inward: is our representation adequate to hold it? The instrument extends access." Fig 6 :26: "An import adds no atom to the world." And it does not settle the ground: Fig 10 :18 "a declared meaning is not a grounded one." Fig 6 :33: "Nobody specified a telescope from an account of Mars." Representations are instruments improved from worse instruments that were already returning something.

**Domain D, verification.** One representation does not force one conclusion. Fig 2 :31: "Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are." Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." Combining two representations buys a surplus only where their closures differ. Fig 8 :51: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ." Otherwise they are "two names for one frame" (Fig 8 :15).

**What the framework does not do.** It returns a shape, not a name. No figure line names a particular visualization, so the answer does not say which chart is best. A causal claim about a specific representation in a specific task needs a ground (controlled experiment) or a provenanced import with a shadow test.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that representations manufacture truth. Fig 6 :26 bars it.
- Not that a better chart would have settled a contested historical fact. Fig 10 :18 bars it; the Snow case confirms it (below).
- Not that all representations are equally reasoning-shaping. Fig 12 :99 "A bond costs what it could have been, and nothing more": a flat display carries nothing in its bond term.
- Not that agreement among readers of the same display corroborates. Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."
- Not `grounded`. The general cognitive claim has not been settled by an experiment in this run.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A controlled representation-swap experiment, not a further argument. Hold the data, the bond structure, the ground, the priors and the slots fixed; vary only the encoding; measure the change in inference reachability and in which slots carry residual. The literature already specifies the design: Larkin and Simon 1987 distinguish "informationally equivalent" from "computationally equivalent" representations; Cheng 2004 reports that solutions with diagrams "were up to six times easier than informationally equivalent sentential representations" while a tabular representation was not better overall; Stapleton, Jamnik and Shimojima 2016 give a formal account of "observational advantages." These are predictions and designs, not this run's measurements.

For the historical half, the ground is the checkable record: the pump-handle episode, the water-company comparison table (Tulchinsky 2018, Table 5.1), the cesspit excavation reported by Whitehead 1865, and the revisionist reading in Brody et al. 2000. The standing refuters are in section 3 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`; written before the conclusion.

## 5. The honest stop

**Reached for the shape, unreached for the name.** The figure-derived shape is stable across re-census: frame, bond, per-slot residual, no new atoms, ground unmoved, surplus only at differing closures. The specific cognitive mechanism and the ranking of visualization techniques are not reached. The six-term set was never the substrate of notation (that reading was retracted in `01`, section 6), and the canonical rule is "a shape, not a name." "Indiscernibility is not identity": two informationally equivalent encodings are not thereby the same frame.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> A table of numbers is an ingredient list; a map, diagram or model adds the typed relation between the ingredients, and that relation is information the numbers do not carry. Under the Tether's figures, a representation is a frame with a signature, so it changes what a reasoner can express and which object slots carry live residual, rather than merely printing results. Its leverage sits at the vocabulary and objective links of the dependency chain, where a situation's affordances become sayable. Its limit is equally firm: it extends reach without adding atoms and without settling the ground, so a legend declares a meaning but does not establish one. One map does not force one conclusion; two readers of the same report draw on one evidence pool, and two representations help only where their closures actually differ. What this offers is the shape any answer must have, five falsifiers written in advance, and the controlled experiments and historical records that would close it. It does not name a best chart, and it makes no claim that has been settled by measurement in this run.
