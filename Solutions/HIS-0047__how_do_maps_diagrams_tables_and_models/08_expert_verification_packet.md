# Expert verification packet: representations as reasoning frames

**What this is:** a claim in a form a specialist in visualization, diagrammatic reasoning or history of science can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the domain placements and the world-facing cognitive claim. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading. Specific historical instances are grounded in the cited record.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A map, diagram, table or model is not a transparent window onto settled results. It is a frame with a signature whose typed bonds fix what can be composed, and the bond carries information the ingredient list does not. The reasoning effect is per item rather than global, so a "same information" verdict hides it. The leverage sits at the vocabulary and objective links of the dependency chain, where a situation's affordances become sayable. The limit is firm: a representation extends reach, adds no atom to the world and does not settle a contested ground. One representation does not force one conclusion, and two representations add reach only where their evidence pools differ. What is offered is the shape any answer must have, five refuters written in advance, and the experiments and records that would close it. No best chart is named and no general cognitive claim is asserted as measured.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading the Symbols table and Figure 12

- **Statement:** a representation is a frame; the difference it makes is the bond, which an ingredient list does not carry and loses silently.
- **Source, verbatim:** Symbols table :111 "A frame is any bounded system with a library: an agent, a person, a discipline, a model." Fig 12 :5 "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Fig 12 :15 "nothing downstream can tell them apart, and nothing signals that anything was lost." Operators table :4 to :5 "The bond carries information the operands do not, so a term that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself."
- **Refuter:** exhibit a pure relabeling that preserves all bonds and expressive power yet changes reasoning in a way the framework cannot predict; or show a flat ingredient-only display from which all four structures of Fig 12 :116 ("Four structures a compositional system exhibits, and each names something the flat form cannot write.") are as readily writable as from a bonded display.

### C2: cognitive-facing; checkable by a representation researcher against the experimental literature

- **Statement:** informationally equivalent representations differ in which inferences are cheap; the effect is per slot and is placed at the vocabulary and objective links, not at raw perception alone. Not all formats help equally.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 3 :7 "it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked" and Fig 3 :10 "it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms." Fig 12 :115 "having terms for what a situation affords was taken for a dictionary and it is a grammar." Fig 12 :99 "A bond costs what it could have been, and nothing more."
- **What to check:** does the controlled representation-swap literature support a per-item, vocabulary-level placement? Larkin and Simon 1987 distinguish informational from computational equivalence; Cheng 2004 reports diagrams up to six times easier than informationally equivalent text while tables were not better overall; Stapleton, Jamnik and Shimojima 2016 formalize observational advantage. These corroborate the shape but do not by themselves establish the Fig 3 placement.
- **Refuter:** a controlled swap holding bonds, ground, priors and slots fixed yields no systematic change in inference reachability; or representation effects are shown to occur only at perception and never at vocabulary or objective. Either would relocate the effect and change the remedy.

### C3: history-facing; checkable against the Snow record

- **Statement:** a representation confirms or reveals but does not settle a contested ground, and one map supports several readings depending on the frame that holds it.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :31 "Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are." Fig 10 :18 "a declared meaning is not a grounded one." Fig 8 :51 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."
- **What to check:** Brody et al., *The Lancet* 2000, "The map did not give rise to the insight, but rather it tended to confirm theories already held by the various investigators." Science History Institute 2022: the ghost map "could be used to argue that living near a pump that emitted a foul odor caused cholera. Snow needed to prove that the water had to be ingested for cholera to spread." Whitehead 1865: "I recorded, tabulated, and pondered over, every evening." Tulchinsky 2018, Table 5.1, gives the rate comparison that did the settling.
- **Refuter:** show that the map alone, without the water-company comparison and the cesspit excavation, settled the ground; or show that the same map compelled a unique conclusion in all contemporary investigators. Either would break Fig 2 :31 and Fig 10 :18 as applied to this case.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that a representation manufactures truth or adds a ground atom (Fig 6 :26 "An import adds no atom to the world.").
- Not that a better chart would have settled a contested historical fact (Fig 10 :18).
- Not that all representations are equally reasoning-shaping (Fig 12 :99).
- Not that agreement among readers of one display corroborates the claim (Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool").
- Not a name for a best visualization. Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."
- Not `grounded`. No controlled experiment was run in this derivation.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A controlled representation-swap experiment holding data, bond structure, ground, priors and slots fixed, measuring change in inference reachability and in which slots carry live residual. This moves the general cognitive claim toward `grounded`.
2. A demonstration that a flat ingredient-only display supports all four structures of Fig 12 :116 as readily as a bonded display. This breaks C1.
3. A representation shown to create a new ground fact rather than access to an existing arrangement. This breaks Fig 6 :26.
4. Two frames sharing one evidence pool producing genuinely independent corroboration. This breaks C3 and Fig 8 :51.
5. For the historical half, the checkable record is already available: the pump-handle episode, the cesspit excavation (Whitehead 1865), the water-company table (Tulchinsky 2018, Table 5.1) and the revisionist reading (Brody et al. 2000).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Symbols table :111, Fig 12 :5, :15 and :116, Fig 1 :19, Fig 2 :31, Fig 10 :18. Seven short passages.
2. Check C2 against one study you know well. Does the reported effect sit at perception, or at what the representation lets the reasoner say and aim at? If you cannot tell, say so. That is itself useful.
3. Check C3 against the Snow record. Did the map supply the answer, or confirm a theory that other instruments settled?
4. Name the one refuter above you would try first, and say what result would make you withdraw the shape.
5. Send the refutation or the protocol. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
