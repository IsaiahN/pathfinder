## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, whether a representation merely displays already-settled results or shapes reasoning. Option A (display only): representation is a lossless transparent channel; same data force the same conclusions; the format is inert. Option B (frame): representation is a frame whose typed bonds fix reachable inference per slot; it extends reach without adding atoms; it does not settle the ground; its social effect depends on evidence-pool independence. What each leads to: A predicts no systematic reasoning change from a representation swap and treats disagreements as noise; B predicts per-slot changes in what is noticed and inferred, predicts silent loss in flat displays, and predicts that one map supports several readings. Verdict recorded from the figures before the world sources were weighted.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Systematic variation in reasoning outcomes attributable to encoding rather than content. Arity: at least three relata, representation / ground / reasoner. Symmetry: many-to-one and not invertible, since the arrangement is not recoverable from the ingredients (Fig 12 :95, :96). Scale: per slot in cognition, between frames at the community scale. Fig 9 :54 requires unbundling this union before search.
- **Domain A, representation as frame.** Actors: marks, cells, nodes, edges, axes, glyphs, typed bonds. Currency: expressibility and reach. Anchor: the arrangement encoded (Fig 12 :94). Question type: partition of expressible versus inexpressible. Order: first, a bond must be carryable before reasoning can use it.
- **Domain B, reasoner.** Actors: library Γ, slots s, belief b, weighting w, residual R_s. Currency: per-slot residual and inference cost. Anchor: the ground the data describe. Question type: union compound (Fig 3 perception, vocabulary, objective, planning). Order: second, consumes A's expressible set.
- **Domain C, ground / data.** Actors: measurements, dataset, world. Currency: measurement. Anchor: the ground, which must not update (Fig 2 :29 to :30, :33). Question type: anchor, not a frame. Order: zeroth, the reference all frames range against.
- **Domain D, verification / history of science.** Actors: investigators, peer frames, publications. Currency: agreement and residuals. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Question type: union of frames; surplus only where closures differ. Order: last. In this simulated run all framework-derived seats draw on one evidence pool, so their agreement is internal consistency, not corroboration.

Dependency order by Fig 3 :20: ground, notation, cognition, verification. Strain test applied: Fig 13's six-term set was retracted as a governor of notation (it governs the substrate of a composed physical system), and Fig 12's bond pricing is not applied to reaction time (two currencies, Fig 12 :126).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

A representation is not a transparent window. It is a frame whose typed bonds fix what can be composed and therefore which residuals register per slot. Its reasoning power is the arrangement it carries, which an ingredient list does not; its characteristic failure is to lose that arrangement silently; its leverage is at the vocabulary and objective links of the dependency chain; its limit is that it extends reach without adding atoms and without settling the ground; and combining two representations buys a surplus only where their closures differ. Verdicts are kept per domain and are never averaged into a single "visualization works" claim. The display-only hypothesis loses on domains A, B and D and retains only its true half in domain C: a representation adds no atom. This is a `general` shape with `specified`, unmeasured placements; specific historical instances are grounded in the cited record. The strongest single outreach point is the independent historical one: the map confirmed a theory, it did not supply the answer.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states modes, refuters and provenance before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real avoidable cost is treating a projection as a source, that is, citing a map or a chart as if it settled a contested ground. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The remedy is to lead with the shape claim and its five refuters and to keep verdicts per domain.
- **undoable:** yes. The domain placements and the analogue mappings are removable without touching the figure shape; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already strip the unsupported names. A sent claim can be superseded, and no primitive has to be withdrawn.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A controlled representation-swap experiment holding bonds, ground, priors and slots fixed (or a demonstration that such swaps change nothing in inference reachability) would settle the cognitive claim; a demonstration that a flat ingredient-only display supports all four structures of Fig 12 :116 would break the bond verdict; a representation shown to create a new ground fact would break Fig 6 :26; two frames sharing one evidence pool producing genuinely independent corroboration would break Fig 2 :31 and Fig 8 :51. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
