# Outreach draft: check the output before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the visual-stage output described below survives the recipient's own check. State the mode on every claim. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.

---

**Subject:** Check one figure-grounded output on representations and reasoning

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** Under a display-only view, a representation is a transparent channel: same data, same conclusions, format inert. The residual is the systematic fact that swapping a representation while holding data fixed changes what is noticed, inferred, hypothesised and mistaken. This is a bundle of overlapping conditions, not one hard question, so I have kept four verdicts separate rather than averaging them.
>
> **The output (`general` shape, `specified` placements, not `grounded`).** A map, diagram, table or model is a frame with a signature, not a window. Its reasoning power is the typed relation it carries between items; an ingredient list does not carry that relation, and the loss is silent. The effect is per item rather than global, so a "same information" verdict hides it. The leverage sits at the vocabulary and objective links of the dependency chain, where a situation's affordances become sayable. The limit is firm: a representation extends reach, adds no atom to the world, and does not settle a contested ground. One map does not force one conclusion, and two representations help only where they draw on genuinely different evidence.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** Not that a representation manufactures truth. Not that a better chart would have settled a contested historical fact. Not that all representations are equally reasoning-shaping. Not that agreement among readers of one display corroborates anything. Not that this is `grounded`: the general cognitive claim needs controlled experiments, and I have not run one.
>
> **What would refute the shape, written before this message:**
> 1. a pure bond-preserving relabeling that changes reasoning in a way the framework cannot predict;
> 2. a flat ingredient-only display that supports all four structures the flat form is said not to write as readily as a bonded display;
> 3. two frames drawing on one evidence pool that produce genuinely independent corroboration;
> 4. a controlled representation swap, holding bonds, ground, priors and slots fixed, that yields no systematic change in inference reachability;
> 5. a representation shown to create a new ground fact rather than access to an existing arrangement.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is withdrawn or relocated. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would actually like from you:** tell me what you would count as a failure of the shape claim, before I show you the source. Your verdict on this stage is recorded before any theory is shown, and I will not pool later agreement as corroboration.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figures text and formula (local framework extract, line numbered): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures README, canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Diagrams and Diagrammatical Reasoning, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/diagrams
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
