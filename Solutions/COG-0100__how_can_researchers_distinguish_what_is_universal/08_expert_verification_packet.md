# Expert verification packet: the Tether on universal mind versus learned form

**What this is:** a methods claim in a form a cross-cultural researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the separator shape, `specified` on each method domain, `gap` for the feature list. Not `grounded`.** No feature assignment is claimed as settled by measurement. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "universal or learned?" is a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition, so the verdict belongs to each feature and population separately and is never averaged. The invariant item is a functional group of cognitive machinery; variation is a change of member or bond, and where an institution adds an atom the relation is composition plus import. The central difficulty is source discrimination: shared machinery, exposure-dependent learning, historical diffusion and an institution-fixed convention can produce the same behavioral reading, so repetition in the same kind of sample cannot separate them. The separator uses low-overlap frames (cross-species presence, developmental exposure, documented transmission lineage, exogenous variation), a non-updating anchor, provenance, and a refutation written before the run. The figures return that shape and the dependency order of its instruments. They do not return a list of universal features; that is a reported `gap`.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 9, 1 and 12

- **Statement:** the two classes form a union, the residual is per slot, and the invariant item is a functional group whose members and bonds vary. No pooled universal percentage is licensed.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54, "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 1 :19, "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 12 :123, "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- **Refuter:** show a clean partition with no feature exhibiting both invariant machinery and variable binding, or show that a pooled assignment reproduces every per-slot assignment. Either defeats this sub-claim.

### C2: methods-facing, checkable against comparative practice

- **Statement:** the same behavioral reading has multiple sources, and provenance plus a source-discriminating observation, not further replication, is what separates them. Low-overlap frames are required; two studies reading one dataset are one witness.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :51, "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them." Fig 2 :31, "Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are." Fig 8 :50, "But shared atoms are not the signature. Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence." Supporting source, opened 2026-11-22: Isaiah N, *The Hard Problem is About Source*, "the instrument is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source."
- **What to check:** take one contested case (for example, a widely replicated categorisation effect). Can the researcher name the four candidate sources, state what observation would discriminate them, and show that their frames do not share one evidence pool? If the only discriminator offered is "more samples," the sub-claim stands and the study is not a source test.
- **Refuter:** show two source classes always producing distinguishable behavioral readings without provenance or a source-discriminating instrument, or show two "independent" frames drawing on one dataset.

### C3: dependency and refusal, checkable by reading Figures 3 and 12 and the Operators table

- **Statement:** the separator is ordered by dependency, breaking at comparability and the definition of a source; a method that cannot refuse a universal claim can only accumulate them.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 3 :27, "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Fig 12 :111, "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." Operators :58, "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate." Fig 2 :29, "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."
- **Refuter:** demonstrate a working instrument that fixes emic/etic comparability and source definition across two low-overlap frames without first moving the break, or show that a moving anchor still settles a source verdict.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a list of universal cognitive features, and not a universal percentage. The list is a reported `gap`.
- Not that any named feature is innate, or purely learned.
- Not that the figures name an instrument for cognitive form. Fig 6 :32 constrains its shape ("An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something") but names none; D7 in `01` is a live gap.
- Not that the WEIRD literature is refuted. It is one evidence pool, not two witnesses and not a verdict on any feature.
- Not a new "culture bond." Operators :68 refuses an eighth operator without a derived test.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A source-discriminating observation for a named pattern that separates shared machinery, learning, diffusion and institution-fixed convention, with the four classes written down before the run.
2. A ground reading that moves a specific feature slot from `gap` to `specified`: the assignment survives an intervention or exogenous variation, with an outcome that does not update on the researcher's reading.
3. A derivation of the source discriminator from an instrument already returning something, per Fig 6 :32 and :34.
4. The falsifiers in `01` section 4: clean partition, new primitive, no effect of per-slot indexing, always-distinguishable sources, moving anchor, collapsed independence.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :54, Fig 1 :19, Fig 12 :123 and :117, and Fig 8 :51. Five short passages.
2. Take one comparison you know well. Name the four candidate sources and say what observation would discriminate them. If "replicate in another sample" is the only answer, C2 is confirmed.
3. Say where your own construct would fail the round trip into another language and back (Fig 4 :30). That locates the break in C3.
4. Name one instrument already returning something that could be sharpened into a source discriminator, or say why none is available.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
