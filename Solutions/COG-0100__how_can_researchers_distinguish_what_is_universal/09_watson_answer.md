# WATSON answer: distinguishing the universal from the learned in human cognition

**Mode: `general` for the separator shape, `specified` per domain, `gap` for the feature list. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. Figures are primary and decide; world sources are secondary corroboration only.

## QUESTION

How can researchers distinguish what is universal about human minds from what people learn through culture, history and institutions? Restated in effect terms: given a cognitive pattern, a population and an observed form, what procedure assigns an origin to that form among shared machinery, exposure-dependent learning, historical diffusion and an institution-fixed convention, and what would refute the assignment? Source: `cognitive science.txt` line 121, section 10 (Methods, comparison, and theory). Option A: treat the question as a partition and find a single criterion. Option B: treat it as a union and build a per-slot separator. Each leads to a different method: A produces a pooled percentage and one lever; B produces separate verdicts and a source-discriminating instrument requirement.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Residual R is three-indexed `(feature, population, candidate source)`, non-symmetric, cross-societal to cross-species in scale. Seven domains, each with actors, currency and a nameable anchor. The domains are the candidate separating instruments, because the question asks for a method.

| id | domain | actors | currency | anchor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | Cross-population variation and reaction norm | populations, environments | presence and form across varied habitats | comparative cross-population datasets |
| D2 | Cross-species and phylogeny | species, populations | presence across related species with no shared learning history | ethological and phylogenetic records |
| D3 | Development and ontogeny | infants, caregivers, exposure histories | acquisition trajectory, critical periods | developmental experiments |
| D4 | Comparability, emic and etic | speakers, researchers, instruments | category boundaries, emic versus etic terms | cross-language naming and psychophysical measurement |
| D5 | Historical transmission and diffusion | institutions, traditions, documents | transmission lineage of the form | documentary and archaeological records |
| D6 | Causal identification and intervention | treatments, instruments, outcomes | change in outcome when a candidate cause is moved | experimental or quasi-experimental outcomes that do not update on the frame's belief |
| D7 | Source-discriminating instrumentation | instrument, reading, candidate sources | discrimination among sources yielding the same reading | a spectrometer-like source discriminator |

**Union, not a partition.** Presence can be universal while form is learned and diffused, so one lever leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :54). Dependency order (Fig 3 :20): perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry. The break is at vocabulary and objective, where comparability and the definition of a source lack an instrument (Fig 3 :27). No eighth domain was admitted; the search for a "culture bond" produced no derived candidate. Strain test: Fig 13 :74 (cross-kind composition) and Fig 13 :20 (spectrum as ordering) were re-censused out as governing lines for the universal/learned sort; they govern other quantities.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The figures decide the method, not the list. Universal and learned form a union of overlapping conditions; the verdict belongs to each feature and population separately and is never pooled; the invariant item is a functional group whose members and bindings vary; the same behavioral reading has several sources that only provenance, low-overlap frames and a source-discriminating observation can separate; the instrument order breaks at comparability; the causal arrow needs a change rather than a correlation; and a refusal condition must be written before the run. The figures do not decide which cognitive features are universal. That is `gap`, stated plainly, of the unreached-at-this-budget kind rather than the underivable-in-principle kind: a source-discriminating instrument would close a slot, but none is specified here. Mode: `general` on the separator, `specified` on each domain method, not `grounded`.

- **cost if wrong:** a learned form mislabelled universal exports an instrument built on one population into another and misdiagnoses the cases it meets; a universal form mislabelled learned spends the search budget hunting variation that is not there and misses the shared substrate. Henrich et al. rate the first cost high. The separator itself costs little to withdraw because it carries its own refuter. The real avoidable cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a recording instead of a generator (Fig 2 :42, "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong.").
- **undoable:** yes. The separator is a method plus a refuter, not a settled list. Withdrawing a domain verdict removes the standing to compose over it, not a term from the record (Fig 6 amendment on reach). Each domain can be rewritten independently, and a promoted feature slot can be returned to `gap` if its discriminator fails.
- **reversed by:** (1) a clean partition with no overlapping feature defeats the union; (2) a variation requiring a new cognitive primitive defeats the functional-group claim; (3) per-slot assignments coinciding with a pooled assignment defeat the slot rule; (4) a demonstration that source classes are always distinguishable without provenance defeats the metamer claim; (5) an anchor that moves when frames push on it removes a domain's ground; (6) two frames marked independent and found to share one pool lose the triangulation mark; (7) a ground measurement that names a feature and survives the anchor test promotes that slot from `gap`. Until one fires, the verdict stands as candidate.
