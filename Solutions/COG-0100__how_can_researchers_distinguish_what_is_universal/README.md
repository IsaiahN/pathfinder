## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to believe the result.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words, cited in `02` and `04`:

1. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that would make their agreement worthless.
2. **A room does not upgrade the label.** Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." More agreeing researchers add vocabulary, not an anchor.
3. **Asking for trust before checking spends independence.** Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, never the framework's interiors.

Legally, scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR/CAN-SPAM-style rules in many jurisdictions. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one.

## The one-line result

The figures do not return a list of universal cognitive features (that is a declared `gap`), but they do return a `general` separator: split the union, keep the verdict per feature slot, treat the invariant as a functional group and the learned part as a binding, and discriminate among the several sources that can produce the same reading using low-overlap frames, a non-updating anchor, provenance, and a pre-registered refusal. That separator is `specified` per domain and is the answer this question can actually be given.
