# Union, functional group, isomer, and order in the universal/learned question

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the bond grammar, `gap` for the feature list.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 8).

## RESIDUAL

"How can researchers distinguish what is universal about human minds from what people learn through culture, history and institutions?" hides four separable questions: (1) is the split a partition or a union, (2) when two populations differ, is the difference a new member, a new bond, or a new atom, (3) in what order must the separating instruments be applied, and (4) what does an institution-fixed category do to the comparison? An answer that collapses these into one percentage is the aggregation failure in Fig 1.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Partition or union?

- Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

A pattern can be universally present and learned in form. The two classes therefore overlap. Treating them as a partition would force a false choice and would let one lever (more replication) appear to solve a problem it only narrows. **Verdict: union.** The four source classes U, L, H, I from `01` F4 are overlapping conditions, not four bins. Triangulated: Fig 9 split rule plus Fig 1 per-slot rule plus the field's own observation that invariance and variance are studied together (Cross-cultural psychology: "both their variability and invariance, under diverse cultural conditions"). Low overlap between the figure census and the field definition.

### Q2: Are the source classes isomers, functional-group variants, or imports?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Fig 12 :46: "A bond is the typed relation between two operands."
- Fig 13 :115: "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."

Take the shared machinery as the atom set. Two populations that differ only in which member fills a slot and how it is bonded are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different behavioral substance. This is the strong, checkable form of the universal claim.

Take a case where one population adds an institution, an artefact, or a novel practice. The atom set is no longer fixed. The relation is not isomerism but composition plus import (Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs"). An import needs a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :12, :13), otherwise the new element is recorded without provenance and the source question is begged.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two populations can be shown to differ in cognitive form while their atom set, member selection and bond all match, then neither isomerism nor functional-group variation explains the difference and this reading fails. Equally, if a putatively novel institution is derived from the existing atom set without contact with a differing frame, the import reading fails for that case. This is the live strain S3 from `01` section 8; it is named, not hidden.

### Q3: What order is the union?

- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."
- Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
- Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

The chain runs perception -> vocabulary -> objective -> planning -> learn and carry. The break is at vocabulary and objective: emic/etic comparability and the operational definition of "source" have no stable instrument. Applying a sophisticated causal design (planning, above the break) to a construct whose meaning did not travel (vocabulary, at the break) spends effort on a link whose input never arrives (Fig 3 :22: "A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can").

The bond grammar matches. Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond." The method needs: sequence (exposure before form), comparison (threshold between candidate sources), subtraction (what changes when the source is removed), and negation (refusal of a universal claim). Operators :51: "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one." Operators :58: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."

**Verdict:** the order is dependency order, and the first instrument owed is at the vocabulary/objective link. Triangulated: Fig 3 chain plus Fig 12 bond grammar, low overlap.

### Q4: What do the low-overlap analogues actually say?

This is where the world sources do real work, because they are separate fields with their own ground.

- **Animal behaviour (Tinbergen):** phylogeny (the history of a species over generations) and ontogeny (developmental explanations for changes in individuals) are *different questions*, not competing answers. Translating: cross-species presence and developmental acquisition are complementary separators, and asking one to answer the other is a category error. This corroborates the union reading (Q1) from a field that never studies human culture. At the ground: ethological experiments.
- **Genetics and ecology (reaction norm):** "a reaction norm ... describes the pattern of phenotypic expression of a single genotype across a range of environments." The universal object is the *function* mapping environment to outcome, not a fixed phenotype. This corroborates F3 (invariant substructure, variable binding) from a field with a different currency. At the ground: controlled multi-environment trials.
- **Statistics (causal inference):** causal claims require "the response of an effect variable when a cause of the effect variable is changed"; "there is no inherent causality in phenomena that correlate." This corroborates F9 and the need for the sequence and negation operators in Q3. At the ground: randomized and quasi-experimental designs.

The three are genuinely low overlap with one another and with the cross-cultural corpus. Agreement among them is weak corroboration of the *shape*, not of any feature verdict. They are not three witnesses to a universal feature list, since none supplies one.

### Q5: What does an institution-fixed category do?

- Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"
- Fig 10 :48: "a convention nothing can check is a constant the seat authored, carrying the seat's authority"
- Fig 10 :49: "Declaring a meaning does not ground it."
- Fig 10 :59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."

When a category used in a comparison is itself produced by the institution being studied, it has stopped being a neutral measuring convention. It fixes the outcome before the ground is consulted. The method must therefore enumerate, not assume, which categories are conventions and which carry a claim about the world, and must declare the unavoidable interest in the reading. This is source class I in `01` F4.

**Falsifier:** a locally fixed category shown to be checkable against an independent ground and not to determine the outcome would not belong to class I; the classification should be withdrawn for that case.

## CONCLUSION

The universal/learned split is a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition (Q1). Over a fixed atom set the variants are isomers; when an atom set changes, the relation is composition plus import, and the fixed-atom condition is a live strain with a stated falsifier (Q2). The instruments must be applied in dependency order, because the break sits at comparability and source definition, and the bond grammar requires sequence, comparison, subtraction and negation (Q3). Three low-overlap fields corroborate the shape, not a feature list (Q4). An institution-fixed category is a verdict masquerading as a convention and must be declared (Q5).

This framing rescues what is defensible in the field's own universal/learned debate without pretending the figures can produce the list: the union, the per-slot verdict, the invariant-as-functional-group, the instrument order, and the refusal condition need no new operator and no named primitive.
