# Supporting derivation: structure, order and analogues

**Mode: `specified` on the union and the order, `general` on the structural analogy.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The question "when should an autonomous system seek human input rather than act independently?" looks binary. Two supporting derivations are owed:

1. Are the two options a partition, an isomer pair, or a union of overlapping conditions? (structure)
2. What is the order of the decision, and is that order forced by the figures or chosen by the author? (order)

A third, animal-behaviour analogue is offered as secondary corroboration, never as a witness for the figure verdict.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: the six terms of Fig 13 and the operators of the Operators table. No new primitive is available without import. The cast for this question: object slots, the library Γ, the instrument, the residual per slot, external frames (nature, corpus, peer frame), a seat that can refuse, and a ground that does not update. Currency: composition inside the closure, settlement cost in bits and actions, and independence when a peer frame is imported. Anchor: the ground of the task for D1 and D3, human judgment itself for D2, and the ground never agreement for D4.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Partition, union or isomer?

- Fig 9 :4-5: "First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."
- Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- Fig 9 :56: "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."
- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- "Act independently" and "seek human input" do **not** share the same atom set. The first has Γ, its atoms and bonds, and an internal instrument. The second adds an external frame, a provenance record and a source gate. So they are **not isomers** over a fixed atom set; they are a union of conditions with overlapping slots.
- The weaker isomer claim survives if the comparison is made inside one frame over a fixed atom set: the same slot can be resolved by SEARCHED (more sweep of the same closure) or by INWARD (a sharper instrument built from a worse one already returning something). Fig 6 :49: "more search at the same reach: is it in here and unfound? The closure does not change. Same atoms, same wall, more of the space actually visited." Fig 6 :32: "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something." These two share the atoms and differ in the relation to the wall; that is an isomer pair in the structural sense, but it does not include D2 or D3 and cannot be used to collapse them.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a case shows that "act" and "seek" are two spellings of one operation over the same atom set, with the same wall and the same anchor, the union claim fails and the isomer reading stands alone.

### Q2 - Is the order forced, or chosen?

- Formula step 7 names three questions and says: "SEARCHED is it in here and unfound? ... Asked FIRST - it is cheapest and the other two assume its answer." The other two are "INWARD extend the instrument, from one already returning something" and "OUTWARD IMPORT. Characterize R in effect terms, then find the frame whose closure predicts its shape."
- Fig 6 :31: "Neither answer tells you much about the other. Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other." So SEARCHED is ordered before INWARD and OUTWARD by cost, while INWARD and OUTWARD are parallel tracks, not a sequence. The claim "always exhaust inward before outward" would exceed the figure. What is forced is: search the closure before supposing it lacks an atom; keep the two extension tracks separate; characterise `R` before looking for a frame.
- Fig 9 :43: "The verdict tells you to leave. The lookup tells you where to go. Elimination is what you do after you arrive." The order is verdict, lookup, elimination. In this question: decide whether the atom is in the closure (verdict), describe the residual in effect terms and find the frame (lookup), then test the imported answer against the ground (elimination).
- Fig 2 :36-38 adds a prior classification that is not in Formula step 7: if the question is constitutively about a person, the human is the anchor, so there is no import to look up. That classification must precede the three questions; otherwise a system would run OUTWARD on a question whose ground is already a person.
- **Falsifier:** if a case exists where OUTWARD is cheaper than SEARCHED and the latter's answer is not assumed by the former, the stated order is a convention rather than a structural constraint. Record it as a correction rather than smoothing it.

### Q3 - What order is the decision chain?

Fig 3 :4-15 names five links: perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry. Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."

Mapped to this question:

| link | in this question | failure signature |
|---|---|---|
| 1 perception | can the system tell what is at the slot and tell it apart from what was there before | stale percepts, dropped transients (Fig 10 :25) |
| 2 vocabulary | does it have terms for what the situation affords, including "a structure must sit here" or "this is a person question" | a dictionary where a grammar is needed (Fig 12 :115) |
| 3 objective | can it say what would count as progress here in its own terms | a residual that persists and does not trend down with no named target (Fig 5 :59-60) |
| 4 planning | is there a route to the objective under incomplete information | escalating to a human before SEARCHED is answered |
| 5 learn and carry | name what worked, verify it and carry it to the next problem | replaying a recording of one human answer as if it were a method (Fig 4 :25) |

Fig 3 :25-27: the middle link, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, "has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments. A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." The "when to seek human input" decision usually breaks at link 3, not at the escalation button. Asking whether to escalate before the objective is stated is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22-23: "A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can. Which is why a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing.")

### Q4 - The analogue: declining when the information is incomplete

Opened world source, secondary: the Wikipedia metacognition page reports Beran, Smith and Perdue 2013, that chimpanzees "were more likely to check what was in the container first when the food was hidden" and show "effective information-seeking behavior when information is incomplete"; and Foote and Crystal 2007, that rats "were more likely to decline to take the test as the difficulty of noise discrimination increased". The same page reports that "not all metacognition is accurate" and that greater confidence is associated with less accurate metacognitive judgment.

The shape-analogy is: a per-item uncertainty signal can govern whether the agent answers or seeks more information, and the choice is made before the answer. This corroborates the per-slot routing rule and the warning that a confidence score alone cannot discriminate its own source. It does **not** corroborate the human-as-anchor rule, because the cited animals have no human ground. Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." Field: animal behaviour and human cognition; low overlap with the human-factors and philosophy sources, so weak but genuine corroboration of the shape only.

- **Falsifier:** a controlled case where an agent with a non-updating, per-item uncertainty signal fails to seek more information when information is incomplete, or seeks it when complete. That would refute the analogy, not the figure verdict.

### Q5 - What the operators say about "when"

- Operators table :51: "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one." The "when" in the question is a threshold relation, ⋛, not a conjunction. Fig 12 :87: "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."
- Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond." The escalation decision uses a comparison (⋛), a sequence (→, the order of questions) and possibly a negation (¬, a refusal of a held term). It is not a single scalar.
- Operators table :58: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate." A system that can only add human inputs and never refuse one will accumulate callbacks without changing reach. Fig 6 :41-43: "the wall moves outward on import. It also moves inward, and this figure is silent on that." and "What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall." A refusal removes standing to compose over, not the term itself.

So "when to seek input" and "when to refuse an input already held" are two questions with two operators. A vocabulary with only ⋛ can decide when to escalate and cannot carry the correction once escalation turns out to be wrong.

## CONCLUSION

"Act" and "seek" are not isomers over one atom set and not a partition. They are a union of four overlapping conditions, and the one genuine isomer pair in the neighbourhood is SEARCHED versus INWARD inside a fixed closure. The order is partly forced by cost (SEARCHED first), partly parallel (INWARD and OUTWARD are separate tracks), and partly prior to the loop (the anchor classification at Fig 2 :36-38). The chain usually breaks at the objective link, before escalation is even a meaningful question. ⋛ makes "when" a comparison, → makes the route a sequence and ¬ is required to refuse an input that turned out to be a wrong atom. One live type strain remains: ⋛ is defined for two quantities, and whether a per-slot residual and a cost of independence are quantities of the same kind at that level is a question for an instrument, not an argument. The animal analogue corroborates per-slot uncertainty monitoring only.
