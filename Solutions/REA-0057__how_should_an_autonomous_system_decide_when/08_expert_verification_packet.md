# Expert verification packet: when should an autonomous system seek human input?

**What this is:** a claim in a form a human-factors researcher, AI-safety engineer, HRI researcher or oversight practitioner can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain boundary and decision rule, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No deployment measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. The whole packet is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading, so it cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

An autonomous system facing "act or seek human input" is not facing one dial with a right threshold. The decision is indexed per object slot and splits into four overlapping conditions with different anchors: the answer is in the system's closure and merely unfound; the instrument can be sharpened in the system's own terms; the question is constitutively about a person, so the human is the ground; or the closure lacks an atom and a human frame with a differing closure may be worth importing from. The order is SEARCHED, then INWARD and OUTWARD as separate tracks, with any import gated by provenance and a shadow test, debited against the human frame's independence, and separated from the verification seat. Agreement is a budget filter, never a witness. No figure line returns a universal numeric threshold; that is a declared gap, not an estimate.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 2 and 9

- **Statement:** the decision is per slot and the human has two different roles (ground for constitutively human questions, witness with a differing closure otherwise), so a single global threshold cannot govern both.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 - "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 2 :36-38 - "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it. Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure." Fig 9 :54 - "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- **Refuter:** a demonstration that all four conditions collapse to one monotone quantity with one threshold that settles every slot across deployments; or a measurement that human judgment moves when told the system's answer and still settles a constitutively human question.

### C2 - decision-procedure; checkable against the formula and Figure 6

- **Statement:** "seek human input" is the OUTWARD import track, ordered after SEARCHED and parallel to INWARD, with a source gate, a shadow test and an independence debit.
- **Source, verbatim:** Formula step 7 - "SEARCHED is it in here and unfound? ... Asked FIRST - it is cheapest and the other two assume its answer." Fig 6 :12-13 - "source gate: provenance" and "shadow test: explains a gap". Fig 6 :24 - "Import from a live peer is debited against that frame’s independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives." Fig 6 :31 - "Neither answer tells you much about the other. Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other."
- **What to check:** is there a case where OUTWARD is cheaper than SEARCHED and SEARCHED's answer is not assumed by OUTWARD; and does an unprovenanced human answer ever move the wall and pass a source-discriminating instrument?
- **Refuter:** either result breaks the order claim or the gate claim respectively. A case where novelty alone is the correct trigger for escalation would also break Fig 5's bin assignment.

### C3 - seat and contact; checkable by an oversight practitioner or HRI researcher

- **Statement:** the importer should not be the verifier, and adding a human changes capability only if it changes contact with a ground that does not update on either frame's report.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :42 - "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Fig 10 :86 - "Outside the slice being graded. Whoever is measured by a reading cannot own it." Fig 11 :40 - "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 11 :70 - "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."
- **Refuter:** a controlled deployment in which the importer also verifies and the verification still passes a non-access check; or one in which adding a human with no live ground contact improves settled outcomes at a slot. Either would break C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not "always ask a human" and not "never ask a human". Fig 2 :36-38 gives the split.
- Not a universal confidence percentage, handoff count, latency budget or named role. Fig 9 :32 - "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134 - "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence".
- Not that agreement among reviewers corroborates anything. Fig 2 :40 - "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool".
- Not that a human in the loop is automatically a ground or an increase in capability. Fig 11 :70.
- Not that the animal metacognition analogue proves anything about machines. It corroborates per-slot uncertainty monitoring only.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken in this derivation.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument at the slot, not a further argument:

1. **Anchor versus instrument test:** does the human's verdict move when told the system's answer, on a constitutively human question, and does it still settle it?
2. **SEARCHED completeness:** is the atom absent from `closure(Γ)`, or merely unfound?
3. **Shadow test:** does the human answer explain the residual described before the human was chosen?
4. **Non-access test:** can the importer also verify without reconstructing the claim?
5. **Contact test:** in a controlled deployment, does adding the human change contact with a ground that does not update on either frame's report?

Six standing refuters are pre-registered in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 5, written before the conclusion.
