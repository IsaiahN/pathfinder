# The Tether's answer: how should an autonomous system decide when to seek human input rather than act independently?

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain boundary and decision rule, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading. No deployment was measured.

## 1. The residual it answers

A system predicts at a slot, observes, and holds a residual `R`. The question asks whether the next move is internal composition or an import from a human frame. The residual is indexed per slot (Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated.") and a global near-zero with one live slot is a legal state (Fig 1 :13). The question is therefore not "what is the system's overall confidence?" but "where does this particular answer live, and which anchor settles it?"

The four conditions form a union, not a partition (Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."). Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.

## 2. The shape

1. **Route per slot, not by a global dial.** A single threshold clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because a second condition still binds (Fig 9 :56). Aggregating across slots is how the live signal disappears (Fig 1 :22).

2. **Classify the question before choosing a human.** If the question is constitutively about a person, "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure." (Fig 2 :36-38). In the first case the human is the ground and "The anchor must not update" (Fig 2 :33). In the second the human is a witness, and an instrument can be wrong on axes it does not measure. These are two domains with different anchors.

3. **Order the questions.** SEARCHED first: "Asked FIRST - it is cheapest and the other two assume its answer." (Formula step 7). Then INWARD, extending an instrument "from one already returning something" and sharpening "in our own terms" (Fig 6 :29). Then OUTWARD: Fig 6 :30 "outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Formula step 7 names the same track OUTWARD IMPORT and requires R to be characterised in effect terms before the frame is sought. INWARD and OUTWARD are parallel tracks; "Neither answer tells you much about the other. Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other." (Fig 6 :31). Seeking human input is OUTWARD, not the first move.

4. **Do not seek on novelty.** Fig 5 :11-14 sorts the gap into held, new, mis-attached and mechanism broken; the amendment adds refuted (Fig 5 :53). Novelty is the curiosity drive's bin, not the import bin. A system that escalates on anything new will ask a human about slots that an extended perception could resolve.

5. **Gate the import.** Fig 6 :12-13 names "source gate: provenance" and "shadow test: explains a gap"; Fig 6 :24 says "Import from a live peer is debited against that frame’s independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives." A human answer without provenance and a shadow test carries zero evidentiary weight, because "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them" (Fig 8 :50-52).

6. **Describe the residual before picking the person.** Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Fig 8 :53: "Residual first, frame second." State which atom, which ground contact and which closure difference would have to be true of the human frame before contacting it. Otherwise the room is authored and the selection is a preference dressed as a verdict (Fig 10 :73-75).

7. **Separate the importer from the verifier.** Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Fig 10 :86: "Outside the slice being graded. Whoever is measured by a reading cannot own it." An interest in the outcome is unavoidable; "an interest in the reading is disqualifying" and "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it." (Fig 10 :59-60).

8. **Do not count agreement as corroboration.** Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." A room of agreeable humans is a convention, not a ground.

9. **Test contact, not presence.** Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 11 :70: "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability." A human who is a reflex button, or who cannot see the residual, has not increased capability.

10. **No universal number.** No figure line returns a confidence percentage, a handoff count, a latency budget or a named role that always decides. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". The threshold is a `gap`, declared and left for a local ground to settle.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "always ask a human." For "is this code correct" the human is only an instrument (Fig 2 :37-38).
- Not "never ask a human." For a question constitutively about a person the human is the legitimate anchor (Fig 2 :36).
- Not "ask the most agreeable people." High correlation disqualifies a verdict (Fig 2 :40).
- Not "more human oversight is more capability." Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."
- Not a theory of everything, and no final room. The union stays a union with its own loop.
- Not `grounded`. No deployment, instrument or controlled comparison was run for this derivation.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument at the slot, not a further argument. The discriminations owed:

1. **Anchor versus instrument test.** On a constitutively human question, does the human's verdict move when the human is told the system's answer, and does it still settle the question? If it moves and still settles, the anchor test at Fig 2 :33-34 breaks.
2. **SEARCHED completeness.** Is the missing atom actually absent from `closure(Γ)`, or merely unfound? A wrong import costs more than starting from primitives (Fig 6 :24).
3. **Shadow test.** Does the human answer explain the specific residual described before the human was chosen, or only the general situation? Fig 8 :19-21: an undirected union with no described residual is mute.
4. **Non-access test.** Can the person who supplied the answer also verify it without reconstructing the claim? Fig 10 :42.
5. **Contact test.** In a controlled deployment, does adding the human change contact with a ground that does not update on either frame's report? Fig 11 :40, :70.
6. **Standing refuters from `01` section 5.** All six, including the collapse of the four domains to one monotone threshold and the discovery of a figure line that does return a number.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The four domains and their anchors are "a human's careful guess" at the structure of this decision, made visible by being written down. Another sensorium's classification of who may settle a slot need not be these four. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments, and the lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity." A synthetic solve proves wiring, not capability. No part of this folder is a ground reading.

## 6. One-paragraph version

> The Tether does not give a confidence percentage for when an autonomous system should ask a human. It gives a decision rule indexed per slot. First classify the question: if it is constitutively about a person, the human is the ground and must not update on what the system thinks; otherwise the human is at most a witness with a differing closure. Then search the system's own library before supposing it lacks the answer, sharpen an instrument that already returns something, and only then consider importing from a human frame. That import needs a provenance check and a test that it explains the specific residual, and it spends the human's independence, so the person who supplied the answer should not also verify it. Agreement among agreeable reviewers is a convention, not corroboration, and adding a human to the loop changes capability only if it changes contact with a ground that does not update. The universal threshold is not returned by the figures; it is a declared gap that each deployment must settle with its own instrument.
