## What in the wider literature bears on this question

1. **Automation bias is a failure of the human side of the loop, not the machine side.** The Wikipedia page reports commission errors (following an aid without checking other sources) and omission errors (failing to notice an aid's failure), and that making people accountable reduced commission errors but training did not reliably fix omission errors. This bears on condition D4: a human who is always available and always believed is a mirror, not an anchor. Fig 2 :35 - "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." The effect is corroboration only; the figure line decides.
2. **Meaningful human control is tracking plus tracing, not presence.** Santoni de Sio and van den Hoven say a person pushing a button in reflex is not meaningful control. This bears on F9: a human in the loop changes contact only if the system tracks reasons and facts and the outcome can be traced to a human who could refuse. Fig 11 :70 - "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability." Their paper is philosophy and design requirements, so it corroborates the shape of the seat condition; it does not measure a deployment.
3. **Animals and people decline when information is incomplete.** The metacognition page reports chimpanzees seeking more information when food was hidden and rats declining hard perceptual trials. This is the lowest-overlap corroborator in the set: a per-item uncertainty signal can govern whether to answer or to seek more information, and the choice is made before the answer. It corroborates the per-slot routing claim, not the human-specific anchor claim. Field: animal behaviour and human cognition.
4. **Partial access yields a shape without a name.** The tip-of-the-tongue page reports that features of an inaccessible target are available even when the target is not. That is a human analogue of Fig 9 :32 - "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." It is an analogy, not a ground reading of a machine.
5. **Regulation prices oversight by risk and autonomy.** EU AI Act Article 14 requires oversight measures commensurate with risk, level of autonomy and context of use, and requires the ability to override and stop. That is an institutional instance of Fig 2 :42 - "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong", applied at the deployment level rather than derived by the system. The Act is a convention with legal force; it is not the figure's ground.

Overlap declaration: the automation-bias page, the Wikipedia metacognition pages and the meaningful-human-control paper draw on partly different evidence pools (human factors, cognitive and animal studies, philosophy of responsibility), so their agreement is weak corroboration rather than one witness, but none of them measured this system. EU AI Act and NIST are institutional conventions, not independent witnesses about the figure verdict. No world source conflicts with the figure verdict, so the conflict rule was not triggered.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1:** send the residual, the four-domain shape, the ordering SEARCHED, INWARD, OUTWARD, and the pre-registered refuter. Ask what else would refute it. Record the verdict before showing any framework material.
2. **Stage 2, opt in only:** if the recipient is curious, offer the verified figure text and the canonical usage rule. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
