**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the four-domain split in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F1 and F4 is checked against the verbatim figure text by the recipient. The stage-1 output must survive or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** Check one decision rule before you decide whether the framing is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** an autonomous system facing "act or seek human input" is not facing one dial with a right threshold. It is facing four different conditions with four different anchors, and the answer should be indexed per object slot, never averaged into one global confidence. Roughly:
>
> 1. If the answer is already in the system's library and merely unfound, act. More internal search is the cheapest move and nothing is owed to an outside frame.
> 2. If the instrument can be sharpened from one already returning something, sharpen it in the system's own terms before going outside.
> 3. If the question is constitutively about a person ("is this a good answer for this person?"), the human is the ground, not a source of advice. That ground must not update on what the system thinks.
> 4. If the system's closure genuinely lacks an atom, a human frame with a different closure may be worth importing from. That import needs a provenance check, a test that it explains the specific residual, and it spends the human's independence for that check. The importer should not also be the verifier.
>
> The order matters: internal search first, instrument second, human third. Seeking human input on novelty, or asking a room of agreeable people, are both failures of the rule.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that there is a universal confidence percentage at which to escalate; that adding a human to the loop always increases capability; that any particular person or job title should always decide; or that agreement among several reviewers is corroboration. The framework returns a shape and a decision procedure, not a number, a name or a headcount. The specific threshold is a declared gap, not an estimate I have filled in.
>
> **What would refute the output:**
> 1. All four conditions collapse to one monotone quantity with one threshold that settles every slot across deployments.
> 2. Human judgment moves when told the system's answer and still settles a constitutively human question.
> 3. An unprovenanced human answer moves the wall and passes a source-discriminating instrument.
> 4. The person who supplied the answer also verifies it and the verification still passes a non-access check.
> 5. Adding a human with no live ground contact improves settled outcomes on a live slot in a controlled deployment.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will narrow or retract the output. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figure text, not my summary of it, is the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract, the citation form is `Fig N :line`): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Public human oversight and human-computer interaction venues are listed in the companion contact file; use a submission route, not a personal address.
>
> **If you want to use the method:** write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - [name]
