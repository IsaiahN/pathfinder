# WATSON answer: how should an autonomous system decide when to seek human input rather than act independently?

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain boundary and decision rule, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.**

**Status: SIMULATED DERIVATION, not a ground reading.** This WATSON run synthesizes material already in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, `05_what_shape_means.md`, `06_isomers_and_order.md`, `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md`. It is checked for internal consistency against the figures. By its own rule (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: LLM output is "simulacra" and "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself") it cannot settle the question it reports on. No deployment, instrument or controlled comparison was run.

## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the choice between acting independently and seeking human input. Option A: one global confidence threshold at which the system escalates. Option B: a per-slot decision rule that classifies the question, orders three questions, gates any import and separates the seats. What each leads to: A sends a single lever that clears the region where one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, treats a human as a ground for questions where the figure says the human is only an instrument, and buys agreement that is disqualified as a verdict. B sends a checkable procedure, written refuters and an open numeric slot until a local ground settles it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap `R = |Γ(b,a) - o'|` per slot `s` (Symbols table :38-40). Arity: four conditions, not two opposite answers. Symmetry: the system-human relation is not symmetric; the human can be a non-updating anchor, a peer frame with a differing closure, or a seat that can refuse. Scale: first order inside one frame (one slot, one action), second order at the membrane where a primitive is imported. Fig 9 :4-5 makes a union of well-formed questions rather than one hard question when rules work well on different subsets.
- **Domain 1, the system's own closure.** Actors: object slots, Γ, atoms, bonds, the seven operators and negation, the live mass `R⁺_s`. Currency: composition inside `closure(Γ)` and settlement cost in bits and actions (Fig 12 :125-127). Anchor: the ground of the task, which does not move when the system thinks. Question type: partition boundary, what is in the closure and what is not. Order: first, because SEARCHED is cheapest and the other questions assume its answer.
- **Domain 2, human as legitimate ground.** Actors: the human judge, the system output, the person the answer is for. Currency: verdicts about whether an answer is good for a person. Anchor: human judgment itself, legitimate when the question is constitutively about people (Fig 2 :36). Question type: separate union member with its own anchor, not a subcase of Domain 1 or 3. Order: before Domain 3, because if the human is the ground there is no import to make.
- **Domain 3, human as a differing-closure peer frame.** Actors: the human's corpus, experience, instruments and ground contact, described in effect terms. Currency: an imported primitive with provenance, paid against independence. Anchor: the ground still decides; the human frame is a witness, not the anchor. Question type: union, overlapping Domain 1 on the residual and Domain 4 on the seat. Order: after SEARCHED and INWARD.
- **Domain 4, the verification seat.** Actors: the person assigned to oversee, refuse, override or stop, not the author of the claim. Currency: conventions authored, verdicts transmitted, never verdicts issued (Fig 10 :43-46). Anchor: the ground, never agreement; outside the slice being graded (Fig 10 :86). Question type: union with Domains 2 and 3, so the roles must be separated when one person could occupy two. Order: checked when the human is chosen, not after.
- **Order by dependency.** Classify the anchor first; then SEARCHED; then INWARD and OUTWARD as separate tracks; then importer and verifier separated; then contact tested at the live slot.

Strain test applied: reading the Fig 2 :36-38 passage as a blanket 'always ask a human' rule strains it, because the same passage limits the human to an instrument for 'is this code correct.' A line that only fits with a strain governs another quantity. Re-census produced Domains 2 and 3 with different anchors. No figure was softened (see `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 2).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The question is a union of four conditions with different anchors, not one dial. Route per slot; classify whether the human is the ground or a witness; search the closure before supposing an atom is absent; sharpen an instrument that already returns something; import from a differing closure only with provenance, a shadow test and an independence debit; keep the importer separate from the verifier; count agreement as a budget filter only; and test whether the human changes contact with a ground that does not update. No universal number, headcount or named role is returned. This is `specified`, ungrounded, candidate, with a declared `gap` on the numeric threshold.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable but not free. Escalating when the atom is already in `closure(Γ)` imports an atom that was not needed, and Fig 6 :24 prices a wrong atom above starting from primitives; it also spends a human frame's independence for no new access (Fig 2 :42, "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."). Failing to escalate when the atom is genuinely absent leaves the residual persisting and the channel decaying while the ground does not decay (Fig 10 :24). The two costs are in different currencies, bits to describe and actions to settle, and do not add (Fig 12 :125-127). The avoidable cost is spending a reviewer's independence by sending a recording rather than a generator (Fig 4 :25).
- **undoable:** yes, for the claim. A wrong import can be refused without deleting the term; what falls is its standing to be composed over, and the reach is recomputed from derivation (Fig 6 :41-43). A procedure can be superseded by a corrected one and no primitive has to be withdrawn. What is not recoverable is a spent reviewer's independence or a room that has already converged: "A mirror cannot correct you" (Fig 2 :35). So the artifact is undoable and the social cost is partly not.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An anchor-versus-instrument test showing the human's verdict moves when told the system's answer and still settles a constitutively human question; a completeness test showing the atom absent from the closure before import; a shadow test showing the human answer explains the described residual; a non-access test allowing the importer to verify; a controlled deployment showing that a human with no live ground contact improves settled outcomes at a slot; a figure re-census returning a universal numeric threshold. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`. A pre-registered refuter with six failure conditions was written in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 5 before the conclusion.
