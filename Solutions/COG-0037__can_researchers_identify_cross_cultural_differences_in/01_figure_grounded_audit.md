## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

A study administers a logical-reasoning test to two cultural groups and observes a score difference. The observation `o'` is one reading. Two candidate sources compete to explain it:

- **S_ability:** a genuine difference in reasoning, the quantity the study wants to measure, and
- **S_familiarity:** a difference in familiarity with the test format, items, language, instructions, or testing situation, an artifact of the instrument.

The residual is not "which group reasons better." It is: **the source of a reading is not determined by the reading.** Both sources can produce the same score gap. Calling the gap "reasoning" is naming a source from a projection.

**Bound ordinary term (Formula step "Direction", Fig 5 :65-66: *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else"*):** a legitimate answer has the form *a source-discriminating design in which the score gap is indexed per item and per person, is unchanged under an explicit familiarity manipulation, and is confirmed by at least one low-overlap instrument ranging against a local, non-updating anchor*. Anything that does not have that form is not yet an answer to this question; it is a reading awaiting a source.

R by arity, symmetry and scale:

- **Arity:** at least two conditions (ability, familiarity), and they overlap, so this is a **union compound**, not a two-item partition (Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones"*).
- **Symmetry:** the reading is a symmetric function of two sources, so it cannot select an arrow between them. The comparison bond ⋛ is "a threshold relation between two quantities" (Operators table :48-49), not a source discriminator.
- **Scale:** first order at the item and person slot; second order at the group comparison; third order at the study design that chooses the instrument. The chain is ordered by dependency (Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance"*), so a better group-level conclusion cannot rescue a broken item-level measurement (Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not"*).

## 3. Findings F1..F10

### F1 — One reading does not determine its source (decisive, `specified`, triangulated)

Fig 2 :18-21 describes the common-cause collapse:

> **"one evidence pool"** / **"they never talk, and still agree"** / **"so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other"**

*The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened 2026-11-22) states the general form: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"* and, of the instrument that reads green, *"it cannot comment on the source."* The resolution there was instrumental: *"It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."*

Applied here: a score gap is a reading. "Reasoning ability" and "test familiarity" are two sources that can produce it. Reading the source off the score is the metamerism error. Fig 6 :34-35 sets the rule for what would close it:

> **"The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve."**

Triangulated across Fig 2 (common cause), Fig 6 (instrument rule), and the source essay (low overlap with the two others in vocabulary, same framework evidence pool). Verdict: a single test score cannot settle the attribution. `specified`, ungrounded.

**Refuter:** show a score gap whose source is determined by that score alone, verified against a local non-updating ground, with no second instrument and no familiarity manipulation. That would refute the source-indistinguishability claim.

### F2 — The residual must be indexed per slot; group means are the aggregation failure (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 1 :19:

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**

Fig 1 :22:

> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

Symbols table :43-44: *"R is measured per slot, never as a single global number."*

A culture-level mean is an aggregate over item slots and person slots. If a handful of items carry the familiarity mass and the rest do not, the mean dissolves the live signal, and Fig 1 :28 applies: *"The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."*

Low-overlap corroboration: *Simpson's paradox* (opened 2026-11-22) defines the effect as *"a trend appears in several groups of data but disappears or reverses when the groups are combined"* and warns it is *"particularly problematic when frequency data are unduly given causal interpretations."* That is Fig 1 :22 in statistical notation, reached from mathematics, not from the framework.

The closest existing instrument is differential item functioning. Opened 2026-11-22: *"DIF becomes pronounced when individuals from different groups, who possess the same underlying true ability, exhibit differing probabilities of giving a certain response."* This is the per-slot source discriminator Fig 1 demands. The same page adds: *"differences on Y conditional on group membership alone is inadequate for establishing bias"*, which is F1 in psychometric form.

**Refuter:** show that a group mean preserves every item-level and person-level residual, including sign reversals and DIF hotspots. If it does, the per-slot requirement is withdrawn for this question.

### F3 — It is a union compound, not a partition (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 9 :54-57:

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
> **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."**

Ability and familiarity are not two boxes into which items can be sorted. They overlap: a familiar item is easier partly because familiarity reduces processing load, which is itself part of reasoning performance. A single lever (a "culture-fair" test) clears the region where only familiarity binds and leaves the intersection standing. So one averaged conclusion ("group A reasons better") is a verdict over a compound, which the figures forbid. Verdicts are kept per condition and not pooled.

**Refuter:** show a clean partition of items into ability-only and familiarity-only sets with an empty intersection and no second-order effect. Then the union reading is withdrawn.

### F4 — The test is a substituted habitat with two silent failures (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 11 :39-40:

> **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"**
> **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

Fig 11 :66-70:

> **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."**
> **"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"**
> **"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"**
> **"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."**

Transporting a test from the habitat in which it was standardised into another cultural habitat is a substitution, not a neutral reuse. What fails to reproduce (local familiarity, language, the meaning of the task) is invisible until the comparison fails; what is unintentionally introduced (test-taking conventions, evaluator cues) is invisible until it acts. This is the structural reason "familiarity" travels with the instrument and cannot be wished away by relabelling the test "culture-fair."

Low-overlap corroboration: *Clever Hans* (opened 2026-11-22): *"The horse was responding directly to involuntary cues in the body language of the human trainer, who was entirely unaware that he was providing such cues."* The source was found by varying conditions, *"the horse gave the right answer only when the questioner knew what the answer was and the horse could see the questioner"*, not by reading more taps. Same shape as F1 and F4, reached from animal behaviour.

**Refuter:** show a score gap that is invariant under substitution of habitat, format, language, and administrator knowledge. Then the habitat-substitution analysis is withdrawn.

### F5 — Familiarity has valence relative to the residual, not as a standing nuisance (`general`, single-source on the figure side)

Fig 11 :49-51:

> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**
> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

"Test familiarity" cannot be assigned a fixed sign. For a residual about arithmetic reasoning, schooling-driven familiarity may raise the score and count as a benefactor to measurement validity; for a residual about novel problem solving, the same familiarity may count as a malfactor. What counts as a confound is a relation to the residual, not a property of the item. This blocks the usual move of subtracting a fixed "familiarity effect" from every group.

**Refuter:** show a familiarity manipulation whose direction and magnitude are invariant across residuals. Then valence-relative treatment is unnecessary for this question.

### F6 — The anchor must be local and must not update on the score (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 2 :29-30:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."**

Fig 2 :36-38:

> **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."**
> **"It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."**

Fig 2 :33-35:

> **"The anchor must not update."**
> **"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."**

The question is constitutively about reasoning as it manifests in a habitat, so reasoning performance against a local, non-updating task is the anchor. The imported standardised test is an instrument for that anchor, and familiarity is exactly an axis an instrument can be wrong on. If the scoring key is normed on the pooled group means it is being asked to compare, the anchor updates on the frame's own output and the comparison is Panel 1 (coherent drift), not triangulation. Fig 10 :24 adds: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; saturated or pooled readings are a lost channel, not a verdict.

**Refuter:** show a local anchor whose stated value changes when the scoring key changes, yet which still settles the comparison. That would break the anchor test.

### F7 — Independence must be bought; agreement within one evidence pool is not corroboration (`general`, triangulated)

Fig 2 :31-32:

> **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."**
> **"Independence is what makes agreement mean something."**

Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

Two studies using the same items, the same translation, and the same norming sample are one study wearing two names (Fig 8 :48-49: *"Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names"*). A genuine second frame must draw on different evidence: a different task format, a locally constructed anchor, an administrator blind to the hypothesis, or a familiarity manipulation. Fig 8 :45: *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."* Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*

**Refuter:** show two frames with measurably disjoint evidence producing the same attribution, with the provenance of each record kept separate. That is convergent derivation, and it would mean one frame suffices in practice, weakening the independence requirement.

### F8 — The line between "ability" and "familiarity" is not drawable from inside the test frame (`general`, triangulated)

Fig 9 :27-28:

> **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**

Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."* Fig 9 :32:

> **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**

Fig 8 :53: *"Residual first, frame second."* If the researcher picks "reasoning ability" as the explanation before describing the residual in effect terms, any test will seem to confirm it. The effect description that travels is: *holding item difficulty and local anchor fixed, a manipulation of exposure changes the score by delta*. That is checkable by anyone. "Reasoning ability" is a cause name, and cause names are borrowed from a frame.

**Refuter:** show an effect description that uniquely determines the cause name across all candidate frames. Then describe-the-effect-first ceases to be a constraint.

### F9 — The specific attribution is a `gap`, reported as unreachable at this budget, not silence (`gap`)

Fig 13 :134-135:

> **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"**
> **"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."**

Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 9 :44: *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."*

No figure line names a culture, an item, or a numerical fraction of the gap as "familiarity" versus "ability." That requires a source-discriminating instrument operated against a local ground. This run operated none. The gap is of the **unreachable-at-this-budget** kind, not **underivable in principle**: DIF, measurement invariance, and familiarity manipulations already return something that fails to resolve (Fig 6 :34-35), so importing a new atom would be unnecessary and wrongly priced (Fig 6 :51-52). The framework hands back a design shape and an owed instrument, not a culture verdict.

**Refuter:** operate a discriminator that assigns the source at the item and person slot against a non-updating local anchor. The gap then closes and is replaced by a per-slot verdict.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Source discrimination fails to be required.** A score gap is shown to determine its source, verified against a local non-updating ground, with no second instrument and no familiarity manipulation (breaks F1).
2. **Aggregation is lossless.** A group mean is shown to preserve every item-level and person-level residual, including sign reversals and DIF hotspots (breaks F2).
3. **Habitat substitution is inert.** The gap is invariant under changes of format, language, administrator knowledge, and local anchor (breaks F4 and F6).
4. **The union is a partition.** Items split into ability-only and familiarity-only sets with an empty intersection and no second-order effect (breaks F3).
5. **Valence is fixed.** A familiarity manipulation has the same direction and magnitude across different residuals (breaks F5).
6. **Independence buys nothing.** Two frames with measurably disjoint evidence add no source-discriminating power over one frame (breaks F7).

If any fires, the shape claim is withdrawn or narrowed in public, with the earlier reading preserved in the retraction log below. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the residual and its refuter (Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"*). Show the source-indistinguishability claim and the six refuters before any framework material.
2. Enumerate the habitat, do not compose it (Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed"*). List what contacts the score: items, format, language, instructions, administrator, schooling, test familiarity, local tasks, and the two-step cascade outward.
3. Measure per slot (Fig 1 :19). Use DIF and measurement invariance as the existing instruments, and add a familiarity manipulation as a second, low-overlap frame.
4. Keep the anchor local and non-updating (Fig 2 :29, :33). Never norm the key on the very group means the key is being asked to compare.
5. Do not send a recording of a past successful study (Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion"*). Send the design method and its refuter.
6. Report the specific attribution as a `gap` until a discriminator fires. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* so a `gap` is a verdict, not an omission.

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is a score gap a verdict?** No. It is one reading of two possible sources (Fig 2 :18-21; source essay).
2. **Can the source be read off the score?** No. An instrument that reads green cannot comment on the source; the spectrometer, not an argument, resolved it.
3. **What unit?** Per item and person slot (Fig 1 :19, :22); DIF is the existing instrument.
4. **Are the conditions a partition?** No. Union with an unassignable shared fraction (Fig 9 :54-58).
5. **Is a new construct owed?** No. Ability and familiarity are bindings onto held vocabulary; inventing a seventh construct would fail the MDL bargain (Fig 5 :43).
6. **Is the test an import that moves the wall?** No. It is a substituted habitat (Fig 11 :66-70); importing another "culture-free" test against a budget limit adds an atom that was not needed (Fig 6 :51-52).
7. **What closes it?** A source-discriminating instrument and a local non-updating anchor, with low-overlap frames; then per-slot verdicts, never a group average (Fig 7 :35; Fig 2 :42).

### Updated verdict

The **shape** survives: a source-discriminating design with per-slot residuals, a union reading of the two conditions, a local non-updating anchor, low-overlap frames, and a `gap` on the specific attribution until an instrument fires. The **folk framing** does not: a culture-free test, a standing list of familiarity effects, or an averaged group verdict are all refused. Strains after re-census: **none hidden**, three handled in section 7.

## 7. Strains, re-census, and what was carried forward

- **S1: Fig 2 :36-38 versus the choice of anchor.** *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."* Is reasoning ability an anchor or an instrument here? Re-census: the question is constitutively about reasoning as it manifests in a person's habitat, so local reasoning performance is the anchor; the standardised test is the instrument and familiarity is an unmeasured axis. No strain after re-census.
- **S2: Fig 11 :40 versus the premise that ability is agent-only.** Not a figure conflict: the premise is the thing being corrected. Kept visible as a retracted premise in section 6, not erased.
- **S3: Fig 8 :45 union surplus versus Fig 2 :40 agreement worthlessness.** One quantity read twice. Independence decides both whether low-overlap frames create surplus and whether their agreement means anything (Fig 8 :47-49). No strain.
- **After re-census: none hidden, one live design constraint** (realising genuinely low-overlap frames in a single study is hard), and one declared `gap` (D4).
