# Outreach draft: check the separator before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the six refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 are written into whatever artifact accompanies this. The recipient must be able to name a failure before they are shown any theory.

---

**Subject:** A failure condition for the "test familiarity versus reasoning ability" confound

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one checkable methodological claim I would like you to test against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a cross-cultural difference in logical-reasoning test scores does not, by itself, tell you whether the difference is reasoning ability or familiarity with the test. One reading has at least two candidate sources, and the reading does not discriminate between them. So a group mean is the wrong unit: the signal that would tell you where to look is per item and per person, and aggregation dissolves it. "Test familiarity" is not a fixed nuisance with a fixed sign either; whether it helps or hurts depends on the question the study is asking.
>
> **What would close it:** a source-discriminating design. That means (1) the residual is indexed per item and per person, (2) a familiarity manipulation changes exposure while a local, non-updating task stays fixed, and (3) at least one second instrument that does not share the first instrument's items or norming sample gives the same per-slot pattern. Without that, the attribution stays open. I am not claiming any culture reasons better or worse, and I have no name for which item or group is "familiarity" in any particular study.
>
> **What I wrote down before sending this:** the claim fails if a group mean preserves every item-level residual; if the score gap is invariant under changes of format, language, administrator knowledge and local anchor; if ability and familiarity form a clean partition with an empty intersection; if a familiarity manipulation has the same effect across different residuals; or if two frames with genuinely disjoint evidence add no discriminating power over one. Any one of those is a useful failure. A one-line "this fails because..." is more helpful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the source links. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula, verbatim with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - The source-discrimination essay: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/the-hard-problem-is-about-source
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the formula is public. Run it on a problem you can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Best,
> [name]

## Two-stage offer, recorded

1. **Stage 1:** send the claim, the separator, and the six refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict **before** showing any framework material.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if curious, offer the three source links. The figures, not the summary, are the authority.
3. **Stage 3, opt-in only:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user names their own ground and writes their own refuter before running it.

Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration. By Fig 2 :40, agreement inside one evidence pool is the pool talking.
