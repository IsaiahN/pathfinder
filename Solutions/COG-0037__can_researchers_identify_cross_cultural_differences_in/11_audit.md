## Independent domain census

I redid the census before reading the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** One score gap with two candidate sources. Arity: two overlapping conditions, so a **union compound** (Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones"*), not a two-box partition. Symmetry: the reading is symmetric in the two sources, so it cannot select an arrow; ⋛ is *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* (Operators table :48-49), a comparison, not an attribution. Scale: first order at item and person, second order at group, third order at the design that chooses the instrument.
- **Domain 1, measurement and instrument.** Actors: items, scoring key, format, language, administrator, the two groups. Currency: response versus prediction per slot. Anchor: reasoning performance in the local habitat, which does not update on the key. Union: sources overlap in every item.
- **Domain 2, the two conditions.** Actors: ability-bearing and familiarity-bearing processes. Currency: score mass per slot. Anchor: no single anchor for the compound; each condition has its own ground. Union.

- **Domain 4, the specific attribution.** Actors: a named culture, item or fraction. Currency: a source label. Anchor: none operated. `gap`, unreachable at this budget.

## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :19/:22 (per-slot residual), Fig 2 :29/:33-38/:40 (anchor, drift, evidence pool), Fig 9 :54-58 (union), Fig 11 :66-70 (substituted habitat), Fig 13 :134 (gap).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07` section 2: one reading, two sources; per item and person; union; substituted habitat; local anchor; low-overlap frame; D4 open.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the concrete study design a researcher would actually run: a specific test, a specific local task, a specific familiarity manipulation, a specific second instrument, and a specific per-item verdict.

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **identity of the slot** (Fig 1 :19 says per slot but does not say which item or person counts as the live one; only an instrument fixes that);
- the **non-updating anchor condition** (Fig 2 :29), which a concrete norming procedure can quietly violate by pooling the compared groups;
- the **union intersection** (Fig 9 :57-58), which a concrete two-condition coding tends to force into a partition;
- the **provenance of the second frame** (Fig 8 :50-51), which a concrete replication can lose by sharing items and norming samples.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a source cannot be named from one reading, so a discriminator is owed and the attribution stays open. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the figures" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
