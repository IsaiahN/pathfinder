# The Tether's answer on cross-cultural reasoning differences

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain findings, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the specific source attribution. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A cross-cultural logical-reasoning test produces a score gap. Two sources compete for it: reasoning ability and test familiarity. The reading does not discriminate them. The question asks whether the attribution can be made without the confusion. It is a **union compound**, not one hard problem with two opposite answers (Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones"*), so it has its own loop and its own ground, and verdicts are kept per condition and never averaged.

## 2. The shape

1. **A single score gap does not determine its source.** Fig 2 :18-21 names the common-cause collapse: *"one evidence pool"*, *"they never talk, and still agree"*, *"so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other."* The instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source"* (*The Hard Problem is About Source*). What closes it is a discriminator, not an argument (Fig 6 :34-35).
2. **The residual is per item and per person, never a group mean.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* DIF is the existing slot-level instrument.
3. **Ability and familiarity are a union with an overlapping intersection.** Fig 9 :57-58: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."* One lever, a "culture-fair" test, leaves the intersection standing.
4. **The test is a substituted habitat with two silent failures.** Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Fig 11 :68-69: *"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"* and *"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"*. Capability is *"a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"* (Fig 11 :39).
5. **Familiarity has residual-relative valence, not a fixed sign.** Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* No standing list of familiarity effects survives a change of question.
6. **The anchor must be local and must not update on the score.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :36: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* A key normed on the group means it is asked to compare has become another frame, and Panel 1 coherent drift follows (Fig 2 :34).
7. **Independence must be bought.** Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* The same items and norming sample are one frame wearing two names (Fig 8 :49); a second instrument with disjoint evidence is required.
8. **The specific attribution is a `gap`.** No figure line names a culture, item, or fraction as the source. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*; Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* This is unreachable at this budget, not underivable in principle, because discriminators already exist (Fig 6 :34-35).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that cross-cultural reasoning differences are unreal. The shape refuses a source attribution from one reading; it does not deny the phenomenon.
- Not that any culture reasons better or worse. No group verdict is returned.
- Not that a "culture-free" test exists or that relabelling a test removes familiarity.
- Not that familiarity has a fixed effect that can be subtracted from every score.
- Not a new construct. Ability and familiarity are bindings onto held vocabulary; inventing a seventh term fails the bargain (Fig 5 :43).
- Not `grounded`. No source-discriminating instrument was operated and no measurement was taken.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further definition. The questions that would close it:

- **Per-slot DIF:** do items with equal local-anchor difficulty show different response probabilities by group? If not, that part of the gap is not item-specific familiarity.
- **Familiarity manipulation:** holding the local task fixed, does changing exposure ( evaluative versus game-like framing, retest, format familiarisation) move the score? The *Stereotype threat* page records real instances: *"the framing of the directions given to the children may have a greater effect on performance than socioeconomic status."*
- **Low-overlap frame:** does a second instrument with different items and a local anchor reproduce the per-slot pattern? If yes, that is convergent derivation at maximum independence, not adopted import (Fig 8 :50-51).
- **Anchor test:** does the local task's stated value stay fixed when the scoring key changes? If it moves, the anchor has become a frame (Fig 2 :33-34).
- **Standing falsifiers** from `01` section 4: lossless aggregation, habitat-invariant gap, empty-intersection partition, fixed-sign familiarity, or no gain from a second disjoint frame.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable in principle.** The six-term set and the figures say what a design must look like, not which culture or item is the source. The canonical rule: *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*; *"Indiscernibility is not identity."* A source-discriminating instrument at the item-and-person slot, ranged against a non-updating local anchor, would close D4. Prestige or agreement is not such an instrument (Fig 2 :40; *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Only the ground can settle it."*).

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that cross-cultural reasoning differences are imaginary, and it does not name a culture or a test item as the culprit. It says that one score gap has at least two candidate sources, reasoning ability and test familiarity, and the reading cannot tell you which source produced it. So the unit must be the item and the person, not a group mean; the two conditions form an overlapping union, not two tidy boxes; a standardised test is a substituted habitat with two silent failures; and familiarity has no fixed sign, because whether it helps or hurts depends on the question. What would settle the attribution is a source-discriminating design: a local task that does not move when the scoring key moves, an explicit familiarity manipulation, and a second instrument that shares neither the first instrument's items nor its norming sample. Until that runs, the framework returns the shape of the design and six refuters written in advance, and it leaves the attribution open. Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence.
