# Expert verification packet — the Tether on cross-cultural reasoning differences

**What this is:** a claim in a form a cross-cultural methods researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural split, `gap` on the specific attribution. Not `grounded`.** No measurement at the item-and-person slot is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A cross-cultural logical-reasoning score gap does not by itself determine whether the difference is reasoning ability or test familiarity. The signal that would discriminate the sources is per item and per person, not a group mean; the two conditions form a union with an overlapping intersection, not a partition; a standardised test is a substituted habitat with two silent failures; and familiarity has valence relative to the residual rather than a fixed sign. The attribution stays open until a source-discriminating design runs: a local, non-updating anchor, an explicit familiarity manipulation, and a second instrument with low overlap in evidence. The framework returns that shape and six refuters, not a culture-level verdict.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — Per-slot residual, not group mean (framework-internal plus psychometric; checkable)

- **Statement:** a group mean is the wrong unit for locating the source; the residual lives at the item-and-person slot.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Symbols table :43-44 *"R is measured per slot, never as a single global number."*
- **What to check:** does the DIF literature actually operate at that unit? The opened page says *"DIF becomes pronounced when individuals from different groups, who possess the same underlying true ability, exhibit differing probabilities of giving a certain response."* Does a pooled mean hide a sign reversal? *Simpson's paradox* (opened): *"a trend appears in several groups of data but disappears or reverses when the groups are combined."*
- **Refuter:** show that a group mean preserves every item-level and person-level residual, including sign reversals and DIF hotspots. The per-slot requirement is then withdrawn.

### C2 — One reading, two sources, union not partition (framework-internal plus world; checkable)

- **Statement:** ability and familiarity overlap in every item; the compound needs per-condition verdicts and an empty-intersection proof before it can be treated as a partition.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57-58 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."* Fig 2 :18-21 *"one evidence pool"*, *"they never talk, and still agree"*, *"so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other."*
- **What to check:** does the familiarity literature support overlapping conditions? The opened *Flynn effect* page has a section titled "Test familiarity": *"children who take the very same IQ test a second time usually gain five or six points"*, and Neisser: *"Test scores are certainly going up all over the world, but whether intelligence itself has risen remains controversial."* The opened *Stereotype threat* page records instruction-frame effects on the same Raven's test.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a clean partition of items into ability-only and familiarity-only sets, with an empty intersection and no second-order effect. The union reading is then withdrawn.

### C3 — Substituted habitat and non-updating local anchor (framework-internal; checkable)

- **Statement:** a test transported into a new cultural habitat is a substitution, and the anchor for a question about reasoning must be local and must not move when the scoring key moves.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :66 *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Fig 11 :68-69 *"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"*, *"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"*, Fig 11 :70 *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."* Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :36 *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."*
- **What to check:** can the researcher name a local task whose value is independent of the pooled norm? If the scoring key is normed on the means being compared, has the anchor become a frame? Fig 2 :34: *"everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."* Low-overlap analogue: *Clever Hans* (opened), where correct responses fell from 89 percent to 6 percent when the questioner's knowledge and visibility were varied.
- **Refuter:** show a gap invariant under changes of format, language, administrator knowledge and local anchor, or a local anchor whose value remains valid while moving with the key. C3 then fails.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that cross-cultural reasoning differences are unreal, and not any group-level verdict.
- Not that a culture-free test exists, or that relabelling a test removes familiarity.
- Not a standing, fixed-sign "familiarity effect" that can be subtracted from every score (Fig 11 :49).
- Not a new construct, a seventh term, or a unified theory of intelligence.
- Not `grounded`, and not the result of a measurement. No figure line names the source of a particular gap (Fig 13 :134, Fig 9 :32).
- Not a request for agreement. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument:

1. **Per-slot DIF:** do items matched on a local-anchor difficulty show different response probabilities by group?
2. **Familiarity manipulation:** holding the local task fixed, does changing exposure move the score? The *Stereotype threat* page records the manipulation already working.
3. **Low-overlap frame:** does a second instrument with disjoint items and norming sample reproduce the per-slot pattern? If so it is convergent derivation (Fig 8 :50-51), not adopted import.
4. **Anchor test:** does the local task's value stay fixed when the key changes? If not, the anchor is a frame (Fig 2 :34).
5. **Falsifiers:** lossless aggregation, habitat-invariant gap, empty-intersection partition, fixed-sign familiarity, or no gain from a second disjoint frame. Any one fires against this packet.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, :22; Fig 9 :55, :57-58; Fig 11 :66, :68-70; Fig 2 :29, :34, :36; Fig 8 :49-51. Nine short passages.
2. Check C1 against the DIF definition and Simpson's reversal: is the per-slot unit the right one?
3. Check C2 against the Flynn "Test familiarity" section and one instruction-framing study: do the conditions overlap in practice?
4. Name a local, non-updating anchor for a reasoning task in one of the cultures in question, or say why none is available.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
