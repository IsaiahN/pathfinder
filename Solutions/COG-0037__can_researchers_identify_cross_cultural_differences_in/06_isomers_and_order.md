## RESIDUAL

Can a cross-cultural score gap be attributed to reasoning ability rather than test familiarity? Three supporting questions are owed: (1) is the condition structure a partition or a union, (2) in what order must the instruments run, and (3) what analogues from other fields carry the shape without borrowing the conclusion?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Partition or union? UNION

- Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- Fig 9 :57-58: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*
- Ability and familiarity overlap in every item: a familiar item is easier partly because familiarity reduces load, and reduced load is part of reasoning performance. So a lever that removes familiarity leaves an intersection where familiarity still binds through ability.
- ⇒ The compound needs its own loop and its own per-slot verdicts. One averaged conclusion is a verdict over a compound, which the figures forbid.
- **Falsifier:** a clean partition of items into ability-only and familiarity-only sets with an empty intersection and no second-order effect. If found, the union reading is withdrawn.

### Q2 — What is the measurement unit? PER SLOT

- Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
- Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
- Symbols table :43-44: *"R is measured per slot, never as a single global number."*
- The slot is the item-and-person pair. DIF operates exactly there: the opened Wikipedia page says *"DIF becomes pronounced when individuals from different groups, who possess the same underlying true ability, exhibit differing probabilities of giving a certain response."*
- A group mean is a second-order composition over slots. Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"* — a mean may settle at its level, but the per-slot signal is what drives the search.
- **Falsifier:** show a mean that preserves every item-level sign reversal (the Simpson case). Then aggregation is lossless and the per-slot requirement is dropped.

### Q3 — In what order do the instruments run? SEARCHED, then INWARD, then OUTWARD

`THE_FORMULA.md` step 7 names three questions, and the first is asked before the other two because it is cheapest and they assume its answer:

1. **SEARCHED — is it in here and unfound?** Fig 6 :49: *"more search at the same reach: is it in here and unfound? The closure does not change. Same atoms, same wall, more of the space actually visited."* For this question, the answer is largely yes: DIF, measurement invariance, retest gains, and framed-instruction experiments already exist inside the research closure. *Simpson's paradox* (opened) supplies the aggregation warning, and the *Flynn effect* page supplies a "Test familiarity" section with a concrete retest gain. So importing a brand-new construct would be wrongly priced (Fig 6 :51-52).
2. **INWARD — is the representation adequate?** Fig 6 :29: *"inward: is our representation adequate to hold it? The instrument extends access."* Here: refine the instrument so it can hold the source distinction at the item-and-person slot; DIF and local anchoring are refinements from worse instruments already returning something (Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."*).
3. **OUTWARD — does another frame already hold it?** Fig 6 :30: *"outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Here: import the familiarity-manipulation frame from social psychology (Steele and Aronson's instruction manipulation; Desert et al.'s evaluative versus game-like Raven's instructions) with provenance and a shadow test, not as a new atom.
- INWARD and OUTWARD are opposed and never read against each other (Formula step 7). Running OUTWARD because INWARD is hard is the dead-reckoning error.
- **Falsifier:** show that SEARCHED returns nothing, that no existing instrument returns anything that fails to resolve. Then importing a new atom becomes legitimate and the order is reversed.

### Q4 — Are the two readings isomers, and does it matter?

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- Two studies of the same group using the same items but different administration frames are **not** isomers: they share atoms and bond alike. They are one frame wearing two names (Fig 8 :49).
- Two studies over the same atom set (items, persons, tasks, anchors) but with a different bond between the reading and the anchor, for example one normed on pooled means and one ranged against a local task, **are** isomers under Fig 12 :117: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. The score formula cannot show which; the behaviour (does the gap survive the familiarity manipulation?) decides.
- **Falsifier:** show that the arrangement makes no behavioural difference, that both bond choices give identical per-slot residuals under manipulation. Then the isomer reading collapses to a relabelling and the bond carries nothing.

### Q5 — What is the refusal?

The claim "the gap is reasoning ability" is not a missing term to be minted; it is a held binding that is false until discriminated. Fig 5 :53: *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"*; Operators table :57: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."* Formula step 2 routes REFUTED before MINT. The refusal is written and pre-registered in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4; it refuses the attribution, not the possibility of a real difference.

## CONCLUSION

The condition structure is a **union** with an unassignable shared fraction, the measurement unit is the **item-and-person slot**, and the instrument order is **SEARCHED first, INWARD second, OUTWARD third**, with the familiarity frame imported only after the effect is described in effect terms. Over a fixed atom set, a pooled-norm design and a locally-anchored design are **isomers**, differing by bond rather than by ingredients, and the difference is decisive in behaviour. The refusal of the naive attribution precedes any new construct. Each claim has a falsifier written above.
