## What is being decided, in effect terms

A cross-cultural researcher observes a difference in test scores between two groups. Two readings compete for the same observation: a genuine difference in reasoning (the thing the study wants), and a difference in familiarity with the test format, materials, language, or testing situation (an artifact of the instrument). Option A treats the score gap as evidence about reasoning. Option B requires a source-discriminating design before any attribution is made. A leads to a verdict that may be the instrument talking; B leads to a shape, a set of refuters, and an open attribution until a local ground settles it. What an answer would look like is bound as an ordinary term in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 0, per Formula step "Direction".

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the derivation.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that a particular culture or a particular test item is the source of an observed gap. No figure line returns that name.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words: agreement selected for likely assent manufactures the correlation that makes agreement worthless as a verdict (Seat Map :163); a verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it (Fig 10 :42); and the framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32). The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, never the framework's interiors.
