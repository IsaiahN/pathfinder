## The question restated as an effect

A regulator, a modeller and a forecaster must attach numbers to a spread of possible outcomes. The spread has two sources in the question as posed: the mechanism is poorly known (it may even be the wrong mechanism), and given a mechanism the outcomes vary randomly. The decision at stake is what to report and what action to take. Three options lead to different consequences:

| option | what the consumer receives | what action it licenses |
|---|---|---|
| one scalar (variance, entropy, a single interval) | a number that hides which source produced it | hedge, collect more data and change the model all look the same |
| two additive components that are summed back into one number | an appearance of decomposition with the conflation restored | the original error, with extra notation |
| a structured, per-slot report that holds the sources apart and scores them against the ground | what is varying, where, what is merely unknown, what is outside the model class, and how independent the evidence is | collect data for one source, import a frame for the other, and refuse a verdict where the ground has not paid |

The answer binds the sought-for shape as an ordinary term before the derivation: **a report is acceptable only if it lets a reader tell, per slot, reducible variation from structural ignorance, and only if the two are not summed into one figure and are ranged against a ground that the model cannot move.** That binding is written down here and in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 0 before any figure line is used, so it can accrue a residual and be refused by the same test as everything else (Formula step "Direction").

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No numerical estimator presented as the answer. The figures constrain the shape of a report; they do not name a unique estimator. Saying otherwise would turn a shape into a name.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **A room does not add an anchor.** Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Agreement about a decomposition is not evidence that the decomposition is right.
2. **Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation.** Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."
3. **Asking for trust before a verdict spends the reader's independence.** Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."
