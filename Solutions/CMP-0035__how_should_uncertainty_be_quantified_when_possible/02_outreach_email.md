**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. Not for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its five refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` sections 0 and 4 are stated exactly as written there. The offer collapses on contact if the output cannot survive validation.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about reporting uncertainty with mechanism ignorance

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output about uncertainty quantification that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to agree with it, and I am not asking you to read any theory first. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question (in effect terms):** outcomes depend on two things at once, a mechanism that is poorly known and random variation given the mechanism. A single number cannot tell a reader which of these produced the spread, so the actions open to the reader (collect more data, change the model class, or hedge) all look the same.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** an acceptable uncertainty report has five properties. It is indexed per outcome or regime, not a single global width. It keeps reducible spread (parameters, candidate mechanisms inside the class) separate from irreducible spread given the mechanism. It carries a distinct, non-probabilistic marker for the case where the mechanism class itself is wrong or missing. It records how much the evidence behind candidate models overlaps, so that agreement among models sharing an assumption is not counted as corroboration. And it is scored against an observed outcome by a rule the model cannot move, with a stated mode and cost if wrong. The two spreads and the structural marker are not added together. The justification is a cost rule: bits of description and actions of measurement are in different units and summing them is the named error.
>
> **What this is not claiming:** not that there is a unique numerical estimator; not that the aleatoric/epistemic two-way split is exhaustive; not that a flat residual proves the mechanism is outside the model class (an instrument may simply have saturated); not that an ensemble's narrow spread means small uncertainty; not that any frame can certify it has quantified everything. The framework here returns a shape, not a name.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim. I wrote these down before sending this:**
> 1. A single scalar induces the same action and the same expected loss as the decomposed report across conditions fixed in advance.
> 2. On a held-out, ground-scored forecast task, splitting the components does not improve a strictly proper score over the best single predictive distribution fitted on the same data.
> 3. A case called structural absence is fully resolved by more samples inside the same model class, with no new atom, instrument or frame, and without instrument saturation.
> 4. The model's own reported uncertainty is a sufficient statistic for its calibration error out of sample.
> 5. Frames with high measured evidence overlap still contribute information not available from their shared pool, under a measure fixed before the test.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the corresponding part. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If you have a verdict, and only then:** I can send the source material. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority. I also use the formula as a method others can run on their own problem, with their own refuter written first.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
