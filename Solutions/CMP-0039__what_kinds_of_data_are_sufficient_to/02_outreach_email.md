**Subject:** What would you count as sufficient data to identify a causal effect here?

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on one narrow question and would like your check on the answer before I show you where it came from. I am not asking you to agree with anything, and I am not asking you to read a theory first.
>
> **The question in effect terms:** with feedback, a hidden common cause, and unmeasured variables present, what kinds of data are enough to identify that X causally influences Y, rather than merely that X and Y move together?
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a four-part sufficient set.
> 1. Data in which the setting of a variable is an anchor, meaning the setting mechanism does not change in response to the system. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor.
> 2. Evidence from frames that do not draw on the same hidden source. Two datasets sharing one confounder are one witness with two names.
> 3. Data that resolve the loop, either through time ordering that separates the two passes of a cycle, or through an edge held severed by intervention.
> 4. An imported atom that measures or replaces the unmeasured variable, with its provenance recorded.
>
> The claim is that observational data alone, however large, are a filter on the hypothesis space and never the witness for a direction. The framework's own wording for what comes out is "a kind of thing, never the answer itself", so I am sending a shape of sufficient data, not a universal recipe.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any one dataset always identifies every effect; that a large sample is sufficient; that a significant coefficient is an anchor; or that the hidden variable can be named from the projection alone. I am not asking you to trust the framework. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
>
> **What would refute this, written down before I sent it:**
> 1. a procedure that returns the causal direction from a joint distribution alone, with no intervention, instrument, or temporal asymmetry;
> 2. an intervention whose setting depends on the hidden confounder that still identifies the effect;
> 3. a cyclic influence identified from a single time slice with no interventional or temporal asymmetry.
>
> If any of these holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop and mode rule: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the formula is in the last link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thank you for checking.
> [name]

## Two-stage offer, stated explicitly

1. **Stage 1:** the recipient checks the four-part sufficient set against what they know, without holding the framework's interior. Their verdict is recorded before any theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in:** only if they are curious, offer the three source links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because they have now seen the interior (Fig 2 :40).

No stage 3 ask is made in the first message. The formula is offered only after an explicit request to reuse it.

One-to-one only. No bulk send, no automation, no misleading subject line, and an opt-out line ("no reply is needed") in every message.
