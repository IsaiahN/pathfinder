# Outreach draft - validate the output first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in
`03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and the pre-registered refuter in
`01_figure_grounded_audit.md` have been checked once more against the verbatim figure text. If the output
does not survive, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** Check one discriminating observation before you decide whether the concept claim is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking
> you to agree with it, and I am not asking you to read the theory first. If the output does not
> survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (`specified`, not `grounded`):** we want evidence that a machine formed concepts rather
> than learned useful associations. Both hypotheses can drive error to near zero on the training
> distribution. So the question is not about accuracy; it is about what observation would separate two
> internal sources that project to the same behavior.
>
> **The output, stated as a shape:** no fixed-distribution behavioral result can settle this, because a
> concept and an association that produce the same outputs are identical in the contents and only a
> record of where each came from separates them. An adequate discriminator has four properties:
>
> 1. it is reported **per item or per slot**, not as one average score;
> 2. it is taken **under changed contact** with a ground that does not move when the machine answers, not
>    on a held-out set drawn from the same distribution;
> 3. it is **triangulated** with an internal check: does the machine hold a composed term with a typed
>    bond and a recorded lineage, and can it refuse a regularity when the ground refutes it, rather than
>    only accumulating another weight?
> 4. the four conditions are reported **as separate verdicts**, never averaged into one concept score.
>
> A benchmark score, a named concept feature, or a room of agreeing readers does not satisfy any of the
> four. A recent JMLR paper on concept-based models is a useful caution here: such models *"are known to
> suffer from information leakage, whereby models exploit unintended information encoded within the
> learned concepts"*.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that this is a test of consciousness, that a single benchmark now exists
> that does all four things, that the framework names which instrument will work, or that any machine
> has been shown to have concepts. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Naming a specific probe as
> *the* concept test is my binding from the machine-learning frame, not a figure-derived result.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written down before this message:**
> 1. a proof that training behavior determines internal source uniquely, so concept and association
>    cannot be behaviorally equivalent;
> 2. a system with no compositional bond, no lineage record and no refusal operator that nevertheless
>    passes every changed-contact probe, with an independent internal inspection confirming the absence;
> 3. a system with full compositional lineage and working refusal that fails every changed-contact probe
>    while a matched association-only system passes;
> 4. a ground reading, not an agreement, that reverses any of the four conditions.
>
> If any holds, I will narrow or withdraw the claim. A one-line *"this fails because..."* is more useful
> to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my
> summary of them. I am sending them now only because you asked; your verdict on the four properties
> above was recorded first.
> - Figure text with line numbers (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical figures README (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Eight-step formula and mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know
> well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it,
> and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## The three stages, kept distinct

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual, the four properties and the four refuters. Ask what the recipient
   would count as a failure. Record their verdict before showing any framework material.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, then and only then offer the three source links.
   Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes
   their own refuter and states their own mode.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage 1 output survives. If a checker shows that
one of the four conditions is unnecessary or insufficient, the shape is narrowed in
`01_figure_grounded_audit.md` before any further contact.
