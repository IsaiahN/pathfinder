# Are concepts and associations isomers? The union compound and its dependency order

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading
(see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The primary audit left two supporting questions open. (1) Under Fig 12, are the two hypotheses
(concept / association) an isomer pair, so that one test over a shared atom set could separate them?
(2) If the question is a union compound, in what dependency order must the four conditions be checked?
Both are answered here with their own falsifiers.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms and terms available: a library Γ with primitives and typed bonds; a per-slot residual `R`; a
ground that does not update; a habitat that can change; a lineage record. No new primitive is available
without import (Fig 13 :115: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."*).
The two hypotheses are not two frames with different evidence pools in this simulated run; both are
descriptions of one machine, so any agreement between them is one pool.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are H_C and H_A isomers under Fig 12?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- A concept under the figures is a molecule: atoms joined by a typed bond, with a lineage and a refusal operator.
- An association under the figures is a flat correlation: ingredients recorded, bond unrecorded (Fig 12 :5 *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*).
- Therefore the two do **not** share the same atom-and-bond set. One has a bond; the other does not.
  By Fig 12's own definition they are **not isomers**. The stronger uniformity claim fails.
- The weaker claim survives: they are two members of a **functional group** (predictive systems) whose
  qualifier is "bonded with lineage" versus "flat correlation". Fig 12 :124: *"the group says what kind;
  the qualifier says which member."*
- A closer isomer analogue does exist, but at a different level: two *machines* that output the same
  behavior (same "formula") while one holds a composed term and the other a flat weight. The difference
  is invisible in the formula and decisive under intervention. This is an analogue, not an identity:
  the two internal arrangements still do not share all atoms, because the bond is absent on one side.
  Mark `specified`, single-source for the figure definition, unmeasured at the ground.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a machine can be written with the same primitive set under
  two different typed bonds, one yielding "concept" behavior and one yielding "association" behavior,
  then the two are genuine isomers and the stronger uniformity claim holds. If the association side
  requires no bond at all, the isomer claim fails and the flat-versus-bonded claim stands.

### Q2 - What order are the four conditions? (Fig 3 dependency chain)

Fig 3 maps the chain as perception to vocabulary to objective to planning to learn and carry. Map the
four conditions onto it:

| Fig 3 link | condition from the primary audit | status if missing |
|---|---|---|
| 1 perception | per-slot residual (F2): which item fails, not the average | there is no signal to grade |
| 2 vocabulary | does the machine hold terms for what a situation affords, composed across kinds? (F5) | the search cannot reach the term |
| 3 objective | can it say what would count as progress and refuse a held regularity? (F5, Operators :58) | nothing can be settled or refused |
| 4 planning | changed-contact transfer under intervention (F3) | no route to the objective |
| 5 learn and carry | lineage record, origin and seq stamped (F5, Fig 13 :127-128) | the result cannot be carried up |

- Fig 3 :25-26: **"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."** This is exactly the concept/association middle of the chain. It is where the question actually lives, and it is the link with no instrument.
- Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** So a claim that the middle link works because no failure has been reported is invalid.
- Fig 3 :18: **"Where a given chain stops is a reading, never a property of the chain."** Do not assign a fixed "level of intelligence" to a machine from where its chain stops today.
- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."** Effort spent on link 4 (a better transfer benchmark) cannot rescue a broken link 2 (no composed vocabulary). This is the reason the primary audit refuses to let a benchmark result settle the question.
- **Falsifier:** if a changed-contact test (link 4) is shown to work on a system that demonstrably has no vocabulary for what a situation affords (link 2), the dependency order is wrong and must be re-censused.

### Q3 - Does any one test terminate the question?

Fig 7 :6 names *"the limit: no last room"*; Fig 7 :30-31: *"every room closes some of the gap; no finite number of them ever arrives"*; Fig 13 :90: *"One chain is one route through a space that has no last route."*

So even a successful discriminator is a room, not a final room. It closes part of the gap and hands a
residual to the next check. This is not skepticism about the test; it is the framework's own limit on
what any test can claim. A single "concept detected" headline is a last-room claim and is refused.

### Q4 - Is the order recoverable from the artifact?

Fig 13 :127-128: *"Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available. The space has no last route and every turn begins from a remainder the last one left, so the order is not recoverable from the artefact. It is recoverable from the lineage."*

Hence the fourth condition: a lineage record is not documentation added to the result, it is part of what
would make the result checkable. A concept claim with no recorded origin is a claim whose route cannot be
recovered. Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level"* - a term settled at one level becomes an operand at the next, which is why the internal check and the transfer check must be read at different levels and not averaged.

## CONCLUSION

H_C and H_A are **not isomers** under Fig 12's definition (same atoms, different bond), because one side
has no typed bond. The defensible weaker claim is that they are members of one functional group with
different qualifiers. A same-atoms/different-bond version is a real isomer claim with a stated falsifier,
but it needs an instrument and is not yet measured. The four conditions form a dependency chain whose
middle link (vocabulary and objective) is the one with no instrument; this is the part of the question
that deserves the first probe, not the last. No single test is a last room, and the order is recoverable
only from the lineage.
