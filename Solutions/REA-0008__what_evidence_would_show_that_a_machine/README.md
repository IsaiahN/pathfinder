**What an answer would look like (bound as an ordinary term, Formula step "Direction").**
An answer is *a discriminating observation*, not a definition: a description of an observation O such that
O would be possible if the machine formed concepts and impossible (or far less likely) if it learned only
useful associations, with O indexed per slot and taken under changed contact with a ground that does not
move. Binding this in advance makes the sought-for shape refusable by the same test as every other term.
The residual R here is the discrimination residual: prediction under the concept hypothesis minus
observation under the association hypothesis. On fixed-distribution behavior R = 0, which is the whole
problem.

**What is deliberately not here**

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree that concepts exist.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **Agreement is not a verdict.** Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* A list of people selected because they already accept the concept hypothesis is a convention, not corroboration.
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that makes their agreement worthless.
3. **A verifier must not hold the interiors.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, never the framework.

Legally: public opt-in submission routes avoid the GDPR and CAN-SPAM failure that comes with scraping personal addresses for unsolicited bulk outreach.

**Suggested next step.** Take `01` and `08` to the public venues in `03`. The audience that can help is the one with a public, checkable record of contact with this residual, reached through an opt-in channel, and positioned to refute it. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
