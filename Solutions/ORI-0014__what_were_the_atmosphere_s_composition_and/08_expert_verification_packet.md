# Expert verification packet: early atmosphere composition and pressure

**What this is:** a claim in a form a geochemist, paleobarometrist or atmospheric modeler can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape; the numerical claims are `grounded` by their authors, not by this derivation.** No new measurement is claimed here. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Any answer to "what were the atmosphere's composition and pressure during the relevant period?" must be a cross-kind composition, not a single gas species; composition and pressure are two coupled slots with different anchors and must be unbundled before either is searched; and the figures do not name the gases or the pressure. The numbers come from the rock record: for the Archean, an anoxic atmosphere dominated by N2 and CO2 with minor H2, CO, CH4, H2S and SO2, at a total pressure well below the modern bar, plausibly a few tenths of a bar near 2.7 Ga, with CO2 uncertain across roughly three orders of magnitude. For the pre-4.0 Ga window the honest result is `gap` on measured values plus a live thermodynamic scenario. What is offered is the shape, the written refuters and the lineage of anchors, not a new measurement.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 13 and Figure 9
- **Statement:** the answer must be a composition across kinds; the figures do not name a gas or a pressure; composition and pressure form a union to be unbundled.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :3 "No system functions without any of them, none is built from the others, and everything else is a composition across the two kinds." Also Fig 13 :74 "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one." Also Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself" and Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- **What to check:** does the six-term set contain any gas species or pressure value? It does not. So a gas list cannot be a figure derivation.
- **Refuter:** derive a gas species or a pressure value from the six terms, or show that a single-species atmosphere is a legal composition under Fig 13 :74.

### C2 — proxy-facing; checkable by a geochemist against the published proxies
- **Statement:** three independent paleobarometers agree on a thin Archean atmosphere, and the anoxia slot is a negation supported by independent redox proxies.
- **What to check:** Som et al. (2016) report 0.23 ± 0.23 bar (2σ) at 2.74 Ga from vesicles in sea-level basalt; fossil raindrop imprints give <0.52 to 1.1 bar at 2.7 Ga; Marty et al. (2013) give pN2 < 1.1 bar, possibly 0.5 bar, from N2/36Ar in fluid inclusions at 3.5 to 3.0 Ga. Catling and Zahnle (2020) tabulate these with ages and bases. Anoxia rests on S-MIF (<10^-6 present O2), detrital uraninite and redox-sensitive metals, not on a single sample.
- **Refuter:** show that the three paleobarometers share a calibration, a rock or a single reinterpretation, so their agreement is one evidence pool (R3); or show O2 at or above 10^-5 present atmospheric level before oxygenic photosynthesis in two independent proxy systems (R2).

### C3 — model-facing; checkable by an atmospheric modeler
- **Statement:** CO2 and pressure are coupled, so a mixing ratio is not an answer without a total pressure, and the published disagreement can be a different arrangement rather than a different gas set.
- **What to check:** Lehmer et al. (2020) reproduce micrometeorite oxidation with >70 percent by volume CO2, which is ">0.7 bar" in a 1 bar atmosphere but ">0.16 ± 0.16 bar" in a 0.23 bar atmosphere. Catling and Zahnle (2020) give paleosol CO2 estimates spanning 0.003 to 0.75 bar. Ohmoto and Ferry (2026) reach a different gas set from an assumed mantle fO2 (IW minus 2), not from a rock proxy.
- **Refuter:** a proxy whose reading is invariant to total pressure over 0.1 to 5 bar would make the coupling irrelevant; or a derivation of the Ohmoto and Ferry gas set from the same rock proxies, on the same ages, would collapse the conflict into one frame.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that the figures derive N2, CO2, methane or any pressure. Those are imports with provenance.
- Not that the N2-CO2 consensus is settled and Ohmoto and Ferry (2026) are wrong. Different anchors; the conflict stays live.
- Not a single number for the whole "relevant period." The proxies have different ages.
- Not that the pre-4.0 Ga window had no atmosphere. Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
- Not `grounded` by this derivation. The numbers are grounded by their authors; the shape is `specified`.
- Not a claim that the six-term set is nature's own list. It is a visible guess, recorded as such.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument.

1. A paleobarometer or gas proxy that breaks the Lehmer degeneracy between a 1 bar, >0.7 bar CO2 atmosphere and a 0.23 bar, >0.16 bar CO2 atmosphere.
2. Dated Hadean material with preserved fluid inclusions or vesicles, closing the `gap` at the oldest end.
3. An independent redox proxy in a different rock system that confirms or fires the anoxia negation.
4. A forward prediction of a proxy shape from the thermodynamic scenario, so convergent derivation can be told from adopted import (Fig 8 :50-52).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 13 :3, :74, Fig 9 :32, :54 and Fig 2 :29, :35. Six short passages.
2. Check C2 against the published proxy values. Are the three paleobarometers independent instruments, or one evidence pool?
3. Check C3: does the micrometeorite reading actually admit both pressure solutions? If not, the coupling claim is wrong.
4. Name one measurement that would distinguish the N2-CO2 scenario from the H2-CH4-NH3 scenario in the 4.5 to 3.9 Ga window, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
