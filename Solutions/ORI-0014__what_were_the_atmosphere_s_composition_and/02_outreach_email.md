# Outreach draft: check one output before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` survive your own check. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.

---

**Subject:** Would you check one shape claim about the early atmosphere before we talk about theory?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output to check against what you already know about the early atmosphere. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output fails, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question, in effect terms:** what were the atmosphere's composition and pressure during the window when prebiotic chemistry could operate and life appeared, roughly 4.5 to 3.5 billion years ago?
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the framework does not return a gas list. It returns the shape any answer must have. An atmosphere is a composition across two kinds, states and structures, so the answer must be a gas vector, not a single species. Composition and pressure are two conditions with different anchors, so they must be unbundled before either is searched; the same mixing ratio at 0.23 bar and at 1 bar is a different climate. And the figures do not name which gases occupy the slots. That part comes from the rock record, not from the framework. This is a claim about the form of an answer, not a measurement.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the figures derive N2 or CO2 or methane; that they pick out 0.23 bar; or that they decide the current disagreement between a thin N2-CO2 atmosphere and a reducing H2-CH4-NH3 atmosphere. Those are world claims with their own anchors. A model chapter and a rock proxy are not two witnesses to the same thing.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before I ran this:**
> 1. two independent proxies, sharing no rock, isotope system or calibration, showing one gas made up more than 99 percent of the atmosphere at one age with no detectable trace species;
> 2. two independent proxy systems showing O2 at or above 10^-5 present atmospheric level before oxygenic photosynthesis;
> 3. the three paleobarometers being shown to share a single evidence pool, so their agreement is one reading repeated rather than three instruments;
> 4. a preserved Hadean rock or fluid inclusion returning a gas abundance or a barometric reading, which would close the gap I currently report as `gap`.
>
> I wrote these down before searching the literature. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers: local `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` (public repo: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures)
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Compound primary source on the Archean atmosphere: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7043912/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so a result contrived to fit the formula is not a test.
>
> — [name]
