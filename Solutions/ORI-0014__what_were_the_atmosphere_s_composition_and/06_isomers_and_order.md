## DERIVATION

### Q1 — What kind of structure is an atmosphere?

- Fig 13 :9-14: Space is "near, far, between, contained"; Time is "the ordered succession of differences"; Energy is "conserved, finite, directionless alone". Fig 13 :19-24: Spectrum is "ordering, so more and less can be said"; Gradient is "a difference that can be spent"; Network is "that entities relate, and influence travels".
- An atmosphere occupies space (contained over a surface), changes over time (outgassing, escape, biological innovation), and carries energy (greenhouse absorption). It has a spectrum (ordering of gas abundances), gradients (pressure and temperature differences that can be spent), and networks (photochemical and biogeochemical reaction cycles).
- Fig 13 :74: "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one." ⇒ the atmosphere is a legal cross-kind composition. But the six alone cannot produce N2 or 0.23 bar. They produce the **form** of the composition, not its contents.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a gas species or a pressure value can be derived from the six terms alone, the shape claim that they are imports is false.

### Q2 — What order must the derivation run in?

- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
- Dependency order for ORI-0014: (1) preserved rock and proxy exists; (2) total pressure is bounded; (3) composition is constrained conditional on pressure; (4) climate consistency is checked; (5) synthesis.
- The order is not optional. Lehmer et al. (2020) show that a CO2 estimate above 70 percent by volume yields ">0.7 bar" CO2 in a 1 bar atmosphere but ">0.16 ± 0.16 bar" in a 0.23 bar atmosphere. If step 2 is skipped, step 3 returns a number with no meaning. Effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it.
- Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." The Hadean has no preserved rocks, so step 1 is `gap` there (Fig 13 :134) and steps 2 to 5 cannot be run as measurements. They can only be run as scenario calculations, which is what Ohmoto and Ferry (2026) do.
- **Falsifier:** a single instrument that returns both total pressure and the full gas vector for one age, with no cross-calibration, would collapse steps 2 and 3 into one and make the dependency order moot.

### Q3 — Are the competing scenarios isomers or functional-group variants?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Scenario A (Kasting 2013; Catling and Zahnle 2020): N2 and CO2 dominant, minor H2, CO, CH4, H2S, SO2, O2 < 10^-6 present atmospheric level, total pressure roughly 0.1 to 1 bar.
- Scenario B (Ohmoto and Ferry 2026): H2, CH4 and NH3 rich, CO2 poor, higher computed pressures in the magma-ocean stage.
- As written, A and B share almost no gas atoms. By Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**. They are two different substances built from different imported atom sets. Calling the disagreement "the same atmosphere with different parameters" is the flat-formula error (Fig 12 :14-16: "the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not").
- The **isomer claim worth testing** is narrower: hold the gas set fixed (N2, CO2, CH4, H2, H2O) and vary only the total pressure and hence the partial pressures. Same atoms, different arrangement, different climate outcome. That is a real Fig 12 isomer claim, and the Lehmer result is its cleanest case.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if bringing two atmospheres to the same partial pressures (not the same mixing ratios) makes their climates and proxy readings identical, then pressure is not a bond that changes the substance and the isomer claim fails. The functional-group reading would stand instead: both are members of "an anoxic atmosphere over liquid water".
- Triangulated for the definition (Fig 12 and the Operators table give independent statements about bonds and arrangement); single-source for the climate consequence (Lehmer et al. 2020).

### Q4 — Analogues: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance

- Modern Earth: N2-O2, 1 bar, oxidizing. Venus: CO2-N2, about 92 bar, hot. Titan: N2-CH4, about 1.5 bar, organic haze. Jupiter: H2-He with CH4 and NH3, no surface. These are not different gases in every case; they are different arrangements and pressures of overlapping ingredient sets.
- Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance." Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world." A settled atmosphere at one epoch becomes an atom for the next epoch's composition; the early anoxic atmosphere is an operand for the Great Oxidation, not a different kind of thing.
- Ohmoto and Ferry explicitly use a planetary analogue: the Late-Hadean atmosphere was "similar to those of today's atmosphere on Jupiter". This is an argument by arrangement, not by identity. It is legitimate as an analogue (it gives a shape) and illegitimate as a name (it does not give the Earth's pressure or abundance). Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself".
- **Falsifier:** an analogue that matches the gas set but fails the pressure and temperature constraints should not be used as evidence for substance identity. Jupiter matches H2-CH4-NH3 but not surface conditions on Earth; its use is therefore illustrative only.

### Q5 — Order, negation and the anoxia slot

- Operators table :57-58: "this does not hold" and "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."
- The anoxia constraint refuses a claim. It is not the subtraction of O2 from a list (Symbols table: "A with B removed or absent" is subtraction, which is the absence of an operand). This matters for Q3: a vocabulary with subtraction but no negation can write "an atmosphere without O2" and cannot write "the claim that O2 was present is false". The world literature does the latter with S-MIF, detrital uraninite and redox-sensitive metals.
- **Falsifier:** two independent proxy systems showing O2 at or above 10^-5 present atmospheric level before oxygenic photosynthesis. This fires the negation, changes the answer categorically, and drops the "anoxic atmosphere" functional group.

## CONCLUSION

An atmosphere is a legal cross-kind composition, but its gases and its pressure are imports, not figure outputs. The dependency order is rocks, then pressure, then composition, then climate. The two published scenarios are not isomers as written because they use different gas sets; the real isomer claim holds the gas set fixed and varies pressure, and the micrometeorite proxy shows why it matters. Analogues give shapes, not names. The anoxia slot is a negation, not a subtraction, and its falsifier is stated.

Nothing in this derivation settles the composition or pressure. It constrains the form of the answer and records where the ground must be consulted.
