## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The phrase "the beginning of time" bundles three conditions with different anchors: the earliest era the standard model explains and instruments confirm (anchor: CMB, BBN, distances); the boundary the model extrapolates to (anchor: a mathematical proof of incompleteness); and a first event in time (no anchor held). The first is settled epoch by epoch. The second settles a limit of the theory, not what occupies it: the extrapolated `t = 0` moves when the theory changes, so it is frame-internal rather than an anchor. The third is a reification of a sequence threshold that presupposes the ordered time it is asked to explain. Candidate boundary frames are provenanced imports with no source gate or shadow test; at best they stand as isomers over a shared atom set. The framework returns the shape `BEGINNING(???)`, not a name.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - The extrapolated boundary is frame-internal, not an anchor

- **Statement:** the `t = 0` term is produced by the same model that is being tested and changes with the theory; instruments anchor the explained era, not the boundary.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :27 - *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Fig 2 :29 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :30 - *"you push on it, it is not an anchor."*
- **What to check:** is there any observation that fixes a first event independently of the model whose extrapolation produces it? If yes, the boundary is an anchor after all.
- **Refuter:** an anchor for `t = 0` that does not move when the theory changes and returns a stable first event.

### C2 - Past-incompleteness settles a boundary of the theory, not a first event

- **Statement:** the BGV result and the classical singularity theorems establish past-incompleteness under stated hypotheses. A proof of incompleteness returns a kind of thing, not a named first event, and relativistic cosmology predicts its own invalidity near a dynamic singularity.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :37 - *"It tells a question that nothing could ever answer apart from one that is merely hard. It is not a rule about theory."* Fig 9 :32 - *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :135 - *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*
- **What to check:** does either theorem add a premise that identifies the boundary with a physical first event, rather than proving incompleteness? SEP *Singularities and Black Holes*: *"Cosmic time 'zero' is a label attached to no event."*
- **Refuter:** a derivation that yields a first event from geodesic incompleteness with no added premise.

### C3 - Boundary proposals are imports without shadow tests; their isomer status is unmeasured

- **Statement:** the no-boundary proposal, loop bounce, past-eternal NEC-violating construction and conformal cyclic model are provenanced candidate frames, not settled terms. The CMB artifact does not record which one produced it. Where they share the full atom set and differ only in arrangement they are isomers; where they do not, the functional-group reading stands.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :14 - *"the only operator that moves the wall"*; Fig 8 :50 - *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents."*; Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*; Fig 13 :129 - *"Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available."*
- **What to check:** is there a large-angular-scale or non-Gaussianity trace that discriminates two of these proposals, or a proof that their atom sets differ? World check: Lehners's review states *"the big bang singularity is avoided"*; Easson and Lesnefsky report *"we present a simple, explicit class of inflationary solutions that are smooth, nonsingular, and geodesically complete for all time, including into the past."* These disagree on the boundary and cannot both be pooled as one witness (Fig 2 :40).
- **Refuter:** a discriminating observation (settles the claim), or a demonstration that the candidates do not share the atom set (drops the isomer claim to the functional-group claim).

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that the universe is finitely or infinitely old. Neither verdict is settled here.
- Not that the singularity theorems prove creation or a first event.
- Not that inflation occurred, that a bounce occurred, or that any named boundary frame is the answer.
- Not a theory of everything; no final room. Fig 7 :6: *"the limit: no last room."*
- Not `grounded`. No boundary measurement is claimed.
- Not that "before the Big Bang" is meaningful in the ordinary temporal sense. The sequence bond presupposes the substrate.
