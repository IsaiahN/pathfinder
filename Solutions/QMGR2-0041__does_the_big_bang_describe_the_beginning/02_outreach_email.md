# Outreach draft - check the boundary claim before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** the boundary claim and its refuter must be stated as a `specified` prediction, not a result. If the email implies a measurement has settled whether time began, the two-stage offer collapses on contact (F2 and F3 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`).

---

**Subject:** Check one boundary claim before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the framework's loop scores a prediction against an observation per slot, never as one global number. Applied to the early universe, the slots are epochs: nucleosynthesis at about one second, last scattering, the Hubble flow, and an extrapolated boundary at `t = 0`. The first three are paid by instruments. The fourth is produced by the same model that is being tested and moves when the theory changes: add quantum gravity or a bounce and the boundary changes or disappears. The framework's rule is that an anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. So the earliest explained era is anchored; the extrapolated boundary is not. The shape claim is `BEGINNING(???)`, not a named first event.
>
> The question as usually asked bundles three different conditions: the earliest era the model explains, the boundary of the model, and the claim that time itself began. The first has instruments, the second has a mathematical proof of incompleteness, the third has no anchor we hold. A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either, so they should not be averaged into one sentence.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the singularity theorems prove the universe began; that the Big Bang is a first event rather than an epoch; that any named boundary proposal, no-boundary, bounce, conformal cyclic, past-eternal, is the answer; and that the CMB carries the route that produced it. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Past-incompleteness settles the shape of a boundary, not what occupies it. Agreement in the cosmology literature is not a witness here, especially among papers drawing on the same CMB data pool.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. an anchor for `t = 0` that does not move when the theory changes and returns a stable first event;
> 2. a derivation that gets a physical first event from geodesic incompleteness with no added premise;
> 3. one instrument that settles the explained era and the absolute origin together, with no categorically different residue;
> 4. an observation at large angular scales that discriminates two candidate boundary frames, or a trace in the CMB that uniquely fixes the route.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - The eight essays and the thirteen figures, including the derivation of the six-term set: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-intelligence-actually-works
> - The architecture paper with the figures and the citation list: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
> - Figures and formula for direct download (branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
