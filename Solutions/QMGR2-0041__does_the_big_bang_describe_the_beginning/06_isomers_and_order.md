## DERIVATION

### Q1 - What bond is "beginning"?

- **Sequence.** Fig 12 :72: **"A then B; B needs A to have happened"**. A beginning is the lower threshold of an ordered series. It is not a member of the series; it is the cut where the series starts to return values.
- **Comparison.** Fig 12 :87: **"a threshold relation between two quantities"**. "Begin" compares a boundary value against the running coordinate. The Operators table :51 puts it plainly: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one."*
- **Census consequence.** Both bonds need an ordered succession of differences to run on (Fig 13 :12, *"the ordered succession of differences"*). The claim "time began" asks the bond to explain its own substrate. That is F6, not a proof that time is eternal; it is a type observation about the grammar of the question.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** a model in which sequence and comparison operate without an ordered time substrate. If one is demonstrated, F6 fails and C3 becomes a well-formed condition with an anchor to be found.

### Q2 - Are the candidate boundary frames isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- The candidates under consideration (no-boundary wavefunction, loop quantum bounce, past-eternal NEC-violating construction, conformal cyclic rescaling) share the functional group "a mechanism that removes or relocates a classical past boundary." On that reading they are a functional group, not isomers: *"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- The stronger isomer reading: if two candidates are written over the same full atom set (Space, Time, Energy, Spectrum, Gradient, Network) and differ only in which structure mediates which state pair at the boundary, then they are isomers under Fig 12 :117. This is the claim worth testing, not a claim already established.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two candidates require different atoms, or if an observation discriminates them at large angular scales, the isomer claim fails or is settled respectively. Until then the weaker functional-group claim stands. The status is `specified` for the figure definition, `single-source` for the physics application.

### Q3 - What is the dependency order of the question?

- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**

### Q5 - Which analogues bear, and which are refused?

- *How Intelligence Actually Works*, video frame versus photograph: **"the frame and the photograph are indistinguishable as artifacts, and they carry different warrants."** The CMB is the frame; the boundary route is the video that was discarded. This analogue governs F7.

## CONCLUSION

"Beginning" is a sequence boundary with a comparison test, both of which presuppose an ordered time substrate; it is not a term that can explain that substrate. The candidate boundary frames stand at best as a functional group, with the isomer reading a real but unmeasured stronger claim. The dependency chain breaks at the vocabulary/objective link, so further epoch fitting does not settle C3. The union must be unbundled before any lever is chosen. All claims are `specified` or `general`; none is `grounded`.
