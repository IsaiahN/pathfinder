# The Tether's answer on ranking, encounter and trust

**Mode: `specified` on the mechanism, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on magnitude. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading. No platform was instrumented and no experiment was run here.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question joins two conditions in one sentence. Fig 9 :5: "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." So:

- `R_E`, encountered: the difference between the slate a user would see under a selection referenced to a non-updating ground and the slate they actually see under an engagement-referenced selection. Ground: the logged slate, per item slot (Fig 1 :19).
- `R_T`, trusted: the difference between what an independent judgment would license and what the platform's engagement proxy reports. Ground: human judgment, because the question is about people (Fig 2 :37).
- The magnitude of either: `gap`. No figure line returns a number, and no instrument was run here.

## 2. The shape, per domain, never averaged

1. **A metric the ranking produces cannot anchor the ranking.** Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." Fig 1 :26 names *"credibility accrued"* in the same list. Fig 2 :30: "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." Change the ranking and engagement moves; it is downstream of the frame. This is `general`, structural.
2. **A loop that ranges against its own output is coherent drift, not correction.** Fig 2 :34: "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices." Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." The user and the platform model update on each other. This is `specified`, falsifiable by showing a reference that does not update.
3. **Exposure contracts toward the user's existing closure.** Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Selection toward predicted engagement selects against such contact, except when novelty itself happens to pay. Fig 4 :25 warns that a replay "repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one." From inside the contracted feed, depth and absence look the same (Fig 6 :27). `specified`, triangulated with Fig 8 :48 and Cinelli et al. 2021.
4. **In-feed agreement is a common-cause symptom until independence is shown.** Fig 2 :19: "they never talk, and still agree". Fig 2 :20 to :21: "so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other." Fig 2 :40 disqualifies such agreement as a verdict about the world while allowing it as a convention. Fig 8 :49: "one frame wearing two names." `specified`, triangulated.
5. **Cross-closure exposure is priced, not promised.** Fig 6 :24: "Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives." Fig 11 :69: "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts." Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." So "show them the other side" can backfire, and Bail et al. (2018) found exactly that for one population. `specified`, single-source on the figure price with a low-overlap empirical corroboration.
6. **A better model with unchanged contact changes nothing.** Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." The candidate pool, platform rules and the user's other sources are the habitat; the model alone is not the outcome. `specified`, corroborated by the randomised result that changing the algorithm changed exposure but not attitudes.
7. **Ranking is an ordered comparison, and the loop is a cycle.** Fig 13 :20: "ordering, so more and less can be said." Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction." Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes." The sign is the objective, not the architecture. See `06_isomers_and_order.md`. `general` for the structure, `specified` for the binding.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a number. No effect size, no percentage, no direction for trust. D4 is `gap`.
- Not "ranking changes what people trust." The best randomised evidence changed exposure substantially and found no significant change in political attitudes, political knowledge or trust in institutions over three months (Guess et al., 2023). That is a partial firing of the pre-registered refuter R4 and is recorded as such (audit §3).
- Not "ranking has no effect on trust." Fig 8 :39: "a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Three months at that duration is a limit, not a proof.
- Not "diversity fixes it." Fig 6 :24 prices the import; Fig 11 :49 makes its valence relative to the residual.
- Not that the metric tensor, gradient, spectrum or any other substrate atom *is* the algorithm. "Ranking" is a binding of held figure terms onto a software artifact, recorded as a binding (audit §5).
- Not grounded. No instrument, experiment, audit or measurement was run in producing this answer.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. The Hard Problem is About Source: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding tests are:

1. **Encounter:** per item slot, against a chronological control, measure the logged slate. This is the D1 ground. It is a measurement that exists; it has not been run here.
2. **Trust:** an independent human-judgment instrument, checked on the axes it measures, with the direction registered in advance. The platform's engagement dashboard is not that instrument (Fig 2 :37 to :38).
3. **Frame-internal test:** show the engagement reading is unchanged under a different ranking, using an instrument the ranking cannot move. If it is unchanged, audit F1 falls.
4. **Independence test:** show agreement survives removal of one evidence pool. If it does, audit F5 falls.
5. **Standing falsifiers:** derive a new figure-held primitive from the others; show the loop can range against a reference it cannot move while still correcting; measure a direct exposure effect with no ranking intervention.

The world result is relevant but not decisive: a three-month null on attitudes and a large effect on exposure. It confirms D1 and leaves D2 `candidate` at that duration.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say "algorithms make people believe things," and it gives no number. It splits the question: what people encounter has a ground in the logged slate, while what they trust has a ground in independent human judgment. A platform that scores itself with clicks, dwell time or accrued credibility is a frame scoring itself with a quantity it produces, so that metric cannot anchor the loop. Selection toward predicted engagement contracts exposure toward what the user's history already predicts, because no frame escapes its own closure without contact with a different one; agreement inside the resulting feed is the pool talking, not independent confirmation. Showing opposing views is a priced import whose sign is not given in advance, and a better ranking model with unchanged contact changes nothing. The randomised evidence to date confirms a large effect on exposure and a three-month null on attitudes and trust, which the answer records as a live partial refutation rather than hiding. What is offered is the shape any checkable answer must have, the anchor tests, and falsifiers written in advance. The magnitude stays open until the ground settles it.
