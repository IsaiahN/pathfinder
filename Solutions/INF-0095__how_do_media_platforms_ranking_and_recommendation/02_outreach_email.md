# Outreach draft: check the anchor test before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` have been re-read and the mode labels are intact. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Standing of what is sent:** a `specified` shape from a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

**Subject:** One anchor test about recommender systems, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about ranking and recommendation. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The question "how do ranking and recommendation systems alter the information people encounter and trust" is two questions bundled into one sentence. The framework's split is:
>
> 1. **What people encounter** has a ground: the logged slate, a record of which items were shown in which order. That record does not change when the ranking changes; it records the change.
> 2. **What people trust** has a different ground: independent human judgment, because the question is about people. Engagement, clicks, dwell time and "credibility accrued" are not that ground. They are quantities the ranking frame produces. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces.
>
> The shape claim is that a loop which ranges against a metric it produces is a frame scoring itself, and that selection toward predicted engagement contracts exposure toward what the user's history already predicts, unless contact with a materially different frame is deliberately maintained. In-feed agreement is then a common-cause symptom until independence is shown: users who never talk can still agree because the same pool served them.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming a number for the effect. No figure in the framework returns an effect size, and no instrument was run here, so the magnitude is marked `gap`. I am not claiming that ranking changes what people trust. The best randomised evidence I could find changed exposure substantially and did not change political attitudes or trust in institutions over three months. I record that as a real partial refutation, not as a silence. I am also not claiming that showing opposing views reliably fixes anything; the framework prices such contact and says its sign is not given in advance.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. Show that the engagement quantity is measured by an instrument the ranking frame cannot move, and that it returns the same reading under a different ranking.
> 2. Show, per item slot against a chronological control, that engagement ranking does not shift encountered items toward what the user's prior history predicts.
> 3. Show that agreeing users draw on low-overlap evidence pools and that their agreement survives when one pool is removed.
>
> I wrote these down before running any search. If any one holds, the corresponding part of the claim falls and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would actually like from you:** tell me what *you* would count as the ground for "what a person encountered" and for "what a person trusted" in your own evaluation practice. If your answer differs from mine, that is the useful result.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - The verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`. Start at Fig 1 :19, Fig 2 :29, Fig 6 :23.
> - The canonical usage rule (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`.
> - The eight-step loop and the mode rule (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The framework's own position is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and that outputs can be contrived to fit the formula. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks for reading,
> [your name]
>
> Opt-out: reply "no" and I will not contact you again. This is a one-to-one message, not a list send.

## Stage order

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual, the split, the shape claim and the three refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as ground. Record their verdict before showing any framework material.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the three local documents. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.
