# The Tether's answer: how to tell what drives an ecosystem decline (EAR-0049)

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the procedure, not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading. It cannot settle which driver governs any actual decline.

---

## 1. The residual it answers

An ecosystem declines across a set of slots. We want to know whether climate, direct human pressure, or their interaction chiefly drives it. The residual is per slot: `R_s = |predicted decline − observed decline|` under a candidate driver. The honest answer has two layers. The figures govern the **shape of a sound attribution**. They do not name a winner for any case, because naming one requires an instrument that was not read here.

## 2. The shape

1. **Keep the decline per slot.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* An ecosystem-wide index throws away the only signal that says where each driver acts.

2. **Describe the decline before choosing a driver.** Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Characterise which species, sites, functions and seasons are failing, in effect terms, then range candidates against that description.

3. **Split before searching; unbundle the union.** Fig 9 :5: *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* When the split lands on an overlap, Fig 9 :54 says *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :57-58: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."* The interaction is that compound, not a third peer and not a remaining error term.

4. **Require an anchor that does not update.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* A manipulation, a natural experiment or a declared counterfactual comparison. Two time series that move together are one evidence pool, not two witnesses (Fig 2 :20-21: *"so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other"*).

5. **Buy independence between frames.** Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* The same dataset tuned two ways is one frame with two names.

6. **Enumerate contact outward.** Fig 11 :52-53: *"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* Direct human pressure can act through several intermediates and with a lag; a first-order-only scope misses it.

7. **Keep filters out of the verdict.** Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* Model fit, significance and weights decide what to try first. The ground decides what is true.

8. **Record the stop.** Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."* The case-level question stays open here because no instrument was read, and that stop is reported rather than papered over with a guessed driver.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "the driver is climate", "the driver is humans", or "the driver is the interaction" for any named ecosystem. Which one governs is a ground question.
- Not that correlation between a driver series and a decline series settles attribution. It does not, because several sources can create the same reading.
- Not that the three candidates are a partition of three peers. They are a union with a compound (F4 of the audit).
- Not that the framework can name the bond type of the ecological interaction. It cannot from these figures; that is a recorded `gap` (Q2 of `06_isomers_and_order.md`).
- Not that averaging across slots is a harmless convenience. It is the named way a live signal disappears.
- Not grounded. No measurement was taken for this package.

## 4. What would settle it, and what a refuter would look like

The ground, not more agreement. Concretely:

- A manipulation or exclusion experiment that changes one candidate while holding the others, with the result recorded per slot. This is the anchor of point 4.
- A factual-versus-counterfactual design in which the control world is declared and does not move when a frame pushes on it. Extreme event attribution, opened 2026-11-22, uses exactly this structure.
- A fingerprint-style discrimination in which unique signatures for all candidate causes are compared with observed patterns and internal variability, rather than one cause fitted and the rest assumed.
- An instrument that discriminates among sources that produce the same decline reading. *The Hard Problem is About Source*, opened this session, notes that the colour case was resolved *"not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."*

The pre-registered refuters are in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4. If any fires, the affected part of the procedure shape is withdrawn, not reinterpreted.

## 5. The honest stop

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* What this derivation returns is the kind of thing a warranted attribution must be. The specific driver for a specific decline remains unreached here. That is `unreachable at this budget`, which is a different fact from `underivable in principle`: attribution science demonstrably produces ground readings for particular events, so the question is answerable with the right instrument. The lineage is recorded so a later reader can locate where the stop happened. Fig 10 :65-66: *"Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right: the ground does that, and a wrong ruling stays wrong however clean its record."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The figures do not tell you whether a particular ecosystem is declining because of climate, because of direct human pressure, or because of their interaction. They do tell you what a trustworthy answer has to look like. Keep the decline per species and site instead of averaging it into one index. Describe the decline in effect terms before you pick a driver, or any driver will seem to fit. Split a bundled question before searching, and treat the overlap where climate and human pressure both bind as its own compound rather than a leftover. Range each candidate against an anchor that does not move when a frame pushes on it, such as a manipulation, a natural experiment or a declared counterfactual. Make sure the candidate frames draw on genuinely different evidence, and enumerate the contact cascade outward, because a pressure can act three steps from where it was applied. Keep fit statistics in the budget column and let the ground hand down the verdict. What this gives you is the shape of a sound attribution, written refuters in advance, and the instruments that would close it. Which driver actually governs a real decline stays open until those instruments run.
