# The Tether's answer: what evidence is sufficient to establish that a cultural difference is real?

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the cross-cultural application, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7). It cannot establish that any particular cultural difference is real.

## 1. The residual it answers

A between-group difference `Δ` is a reading whose source is unassigned. World (G), measurement channel (M) and contact habitat (P) all predict it. The question asks for the evidence that would settle the source. The figure-governed answer is that **no single test is sufficient**; sufficiency is structural, a conjunction of conditions on the discriminator and its anchor.

## 2. The shape of sufficient evidence

1. **Describe the residual before choosing a method.** Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*. Bind what "sufficient" would look like as an ordinary term (formula Direction; Fig 5 :62-66), so the preference can be refuted.
2. **Split the union and name the compound.** Fig 9 :54-57: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Translation, response channel and habitat are overlapping conditions; one lever leaves the intersections standing.
3. **Measure the seam.** Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* A declared translation equivalence is not a grounded one (Fig 10 :18, :49).
4. **Read per item and per group.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
5. **Enumerate the habitat.** Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* A convenience panel is a substituted habitat: *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."* (Fig 11 :70)
6. **Keep the anchor outside the channel and unable to update.** Fig 2 :29-30 defines the anchor; :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* For a question constitutively about people, blind informed judgment about outcomes is a legitimate anchor (Fig 2 :37), while the scale remains an instrument that *"can be wrong on axes they do not measure"* (Fig 2 :38).
7. **Buy independence with low-overlap methods.** Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; :42 *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Two self-report scales in two languages are one method wearing two names.
8. **Use thresholds as filters, never as verdict witnesses.** Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."*
9. **Record the lineage and stop honestly.** Fig 13 :126: *"The array cannot be checked by inspection, so the record is what makes it verifiable."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*; Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

**Verdict per domain** (kept separate, never averaged): D1 translation seam, triangulated, a measured round trip is necessary; D2 response channel, triangulated, a gate is not the ground; D3 contact habitat, single-source on the figure side, enumerate contact; D4 substantive claim, triangulated, the ground decides and for questions about people a blind outcome anchor is legitimate; D5 verification, triangulated, low overlap plus a non-updating anchor; named sufficient test, `gap`, method choice is an import with provenance.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that a passed invariance test proves a difference real. A gate is not the ground.
- Not that a failed invariance test proves a difference unreal. It is a channel failure, not a world failure (Fig 10 :24 *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*).
- Not that any named statistic, cutoff, method or sample size is sufficient by itself. No figure line governs one (Fig 9 :32).
- Not that many agreeing studies establish a source. Agreement in one evidence pool is one witness.
- Not that the framework can settle a specific cross-cultural claim. No dataset was read or measured here.
- Not that translation equivalence is impossible. A round trip with a measured gap can clear a specific item; what is refused is declaring equivalence without measuring it.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground, not agreement

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer, not an argument, resolved it. Concretely, a specific claim is settled when:

1. A discriminating check was installed before the comparison and could have failed.
2. It did not fail on the axis it was built to test (for example, a round trip with gap below a preset resolution, or an external criterion the response channel does not author).
3. A second method with genuinely low overlap returns the same source assignment, and neither method draws on the other's evidence.
4. The habitat was enumerated and the relevant conditions reproduced.
5. Readings were per item and per group, and the anchor did not move when the frame was pushed.

Standing refuters, written before this conclusion (full list in `01` section 4): a reading that determines its source with no discriminator; low-overlap methods that diverge with no resolver; a check that cannot fail; a habitat condition that flips a settled assignment; a figure line naming a sufficient test; a pooled mean that hides or invents a live per-slot signal.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable in principle.** The gap is about a governing figure line naming a sufficient test, not about the field's ability to build discriminators. Where instruments stop discriminating, report `unreached` with the lineage and the dropped constraints enumerated (Fig 4 :31-32, Fig 13 :134). *They're Scanning Books* warns that *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."* The six-item discriminator in section 2 is a visible choice, not nature's list. *Indiscernibility is not identity.*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> A between-group score difference does not tell you where it came from. Culture, translation, response style and sampling can all produce the same reading, which is why a passed invariance test is a gate and not a verdict. What the figures require is structural: describe the difference in effect terms before choosing a method, split the overlapping sources into their compounds, measure the translation round trip, read each item within each group, enumerate who was actually in contact with the claim, keep an anchor that does not move when the frame is pushed, and get a second method that draws on genuinely different evidence. Thresholds decide where to spend the next hour of checking, never whether the difference is real. No figure line names a sufficient test, so the honest output is a discriminator shape with refuters written in advance, and an `unreached` verdict where instruments stop discriminating. A failure is more useful to me than an endorsement.
