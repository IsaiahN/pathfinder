# Solution COG-0092 — figure-grounded package

**Question:** What evidence is sufficient to establish that a cultural difference is real rather than an artifact of measurement?

**Source:** `Questions/cognitive science.txt` line 113 (question 92). **Section:** 10. Methods, comparison, and theory.

**Field:** cross-cultural research methods and evidence standards.

**Mode:** `general` on the structural shape of sufficient evidence; `specified` on the cross-cultural prediction (which conditions a study would have to discriminate and which anchors it would have to keep reachable). Nothing here is `grounded`: no dataset was read or measured in this run, and no expert has replied. Every substantive file states that this is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading, so it cannot settle the question it reports on.

**Status:** done. **Registry status:** pending (owned by the parent session).

**Boundary.** Nothing in this folder was modified outside it. Figure text was read from `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`; the canonical usage rule from `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`; the loop from `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`. World sources were opened on 2026-11-22 and are recorded with author, venue, year, accessed date and a quote in `10_source_ledger.csv`. Public contact points are institutional and opt-in only.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals, and no receptiveness ranking.
- No bulk-send plan and no list of people selected for likely agreement.
- No claim that a cutoff value, a scale name or a statistical test is *the* sufficient test. The figures return a shape and a refuter, not a named instrument (Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*).
