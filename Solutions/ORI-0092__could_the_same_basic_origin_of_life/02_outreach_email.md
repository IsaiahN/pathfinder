**Subject:** Would this shape survive a very different ocean and atmosphere?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on any named pathway, `gap` on the specific route).** The question "could the same basic origin-of-life pathway work on planets with very different oceans, atmospheres, and minerals?" is two questions, not one:
>
> 1. Does the *operation sequence* transfer? Produce a feedstock, activate it, polymerise, encapsulate, select, copy with variation.
> 2. Does the *same concrete chemistry* transfer? The same molecules, the same bond order, the same mineral catalysts.
>
> The figure-derived shape says these come apart. A method can be re-applied; a recording of one success cannot. A habitat is read off the world, never composed from the pathway that worked somewhere else, and moving a problem to a new habitat substitutes the habitat you inherit actors you did not choose. So my claim is that the **generator transfers and the concrete pathway does not**. What recurs on a different planet is an isomer or a functional analogue, not the same molecule in the same arrangement.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** not that life is common, not that a particular prebiotic route is universal, not that a named pathway would close the gap on Mars, Europa, or an exoplanet, and not that any of this is measured. The framework returns a shape, not a name. I do not know which chemistry would work on a named planet, and I am recording that as an open gap rather than filling it.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before this message:**
> 1. one concrete pathway closing the life-gap in two habitats with disjoint solvent, atmosphere, and mineral sets, measured, with no hidden condition introduced by the experiment;
> 2. a claimed universal method that fails to re-close the gap in a fresh habitat once every required condition was reproduced and measured;
> 3. an actor whose effect on the outcome is unchanged across a demonstrated change of habitat (for example a mineral that helps in one setting and is inert or destructive in another would be a normal result; invariance would be the refutation);
> 4. expert agreement alone, with no new ground reading, correctly changing the state of the gap.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources I actually read. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Canonical figures and formula (figures 1 to 13, Operators and Symbols tables): `local: C:\Users\Admin\Desktop\Project Pathfinder\FRAMEWORK\reference\03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` and `https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures`
