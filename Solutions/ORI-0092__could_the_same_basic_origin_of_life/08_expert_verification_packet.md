# Expert verification packet - the Tether on ORI-0092

**What this is:** a claim in a form a planetary-origin researcher can check. Mode, derivation, and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on any named pathway, `gap` on the exact route for a named planet. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "could the same basic origin-of-life pathway work on planets with very different oceans, atmospheres, and minerals?" is a union of two questions: does the operation sequence transfer, and does the concrete chemistry transfer. The figure-derived verdict is that the generator crosses and the instance does not. A new habitat is enumerated from the new planet, never composed from the pathway that worked elsewhere, and the valence of any actor is relative to the residual being closed. What recurs on a different world is therefore an isomer (same feedstocks, different bond order) or a functional group (same operations, different atoms), not an identical replay. Which chemistry would actually close the gap on a named world is a declared `gap`. What is offered is the shape, the falsifiers written in advance, and the measurements that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal plus physics-facing; checkable by reading Figure 11 and Figure 4

- **Statement:** a method can be re-applied across habitats; a recording of one success cannot. The new habitat is enumerated, never composed, and isolation is substitution.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :43 - *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 11 :66 - *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Fig 11 :67 - *"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."* Fig 4 :22 - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Fig 10 :68 - *"a generator crosses, an instance does not, and the level it lands on remakes it."*
- **What to check:** is a pathway's habitat in fact a function of the planet and not of the pathway? Are there cases where moving the residual leaves the actor set unchanged?
- **Refuter:** demonstrate that H2's habitat is composable from P alone, or that a claimed universal method fails to re-close R in a fresh habitat after every required condition was reproduced and measured.

### C2 - chemistry-facing; checkable by a prebiotic chemist against known reaction networks

- **Statement:** "same pathway" has only two figure-licensed readings: isomer (same atoms, different bond, different substance) or functional group (same operation shape, different atoms). Identical atoms in identical bond order across disjoint habitats is not licensed.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 - *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :124 - *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Fig 13 :121 - *"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."* Fig 13 :124 - *"the same ingredients under a different bond are a different substance."*
- **What to check:** do two candidate networks that share a feedstock set but reorder the chemistry actually behave differently in a measured respect? If the feedstocks differ, is the only shared structure the operation shape?
- **Refuter:** one concrete pathway closing R in two habitats with disjoint solvent, atmosphere, and mineral sets, measured, with no unenumerated condition introduced by the experiment. Or a demonstration that permuting bond order leaves every measured outcome unchanged (then the bond carries no information here and the isomer claim is dead).

### C3 - measurement-facing; checkable by a mission or experimental scientist

- **Statement:** a laboratory solve under familiar conditions is a substituted habitat and proves wiring, not capability; and the effect of a catalyst is relative to the residual, not a property it carries.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :70 - *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."* Fig 11 :49 - *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :68 - *"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"*; Fig 11 :69 - *"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"*.
- **What to check:** in a proposed H2 experiment, which conditions are measured from H2 and which are silently imported from the laboratory protocol? Does the same catalyst change sign under a documented change of residual?
- **Refuter:** an actor whose valence toward R is invariant across a documented change of habitat, or an H2 experiment that closes R with every required condition measured and none imported.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that life is common or rare (a different question, ORI-0093).
- Not that cyanosulfidic chemistry, hydrothermal chemistry, or any named route is universal.
- Not identical replay of Earth chemistry on another world. That is exactly the claim being refused.
- Not a second genesis claimed to have been observed. No observation is asserted here.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken in this derivation.
- Not a claim that expert agreement settles anything. Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating reading, not an argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a color instrument *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"the answer is unreached until a better instrument exists"*; it was the spectrometer that resolved it. Here:

1. An independently originated biosphere with its chemistry measured (does it use the same atoms in the same bond order under a disjoint habitat?).
2. A controlled pathway run under a second planet's measured solvent, atmosphere, and mineral inventory, with all conditions reproduced and none imported.
3. A catalyst-valence measurement across two documented habitats.
4. A seed test: the same operation sequence planted in two measured habitats, with fidelity of execution checked.
