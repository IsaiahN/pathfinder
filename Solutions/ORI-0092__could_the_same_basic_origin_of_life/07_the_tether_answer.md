# The Tether's answer on ORI-0092

**Mode: `general` on the structural claim, `specified` on any named pathway, `gap` on the exact pathway for a named planet. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Given a pathway P that closed R (the gap from prebiotic chemistry to a living, evolving system) in habitat H1, would P close R in habitat H2 with a different ocean, atmosphere, and mineral inventory? The question is a **union compound**, not one hard question (Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*). It splits into C1 (does the operation sequence transfer?) and C2 (does the concrete chemistry transfer?). R is indexed per planet (Fig 1 :19), never averaged.

## 2. The shape

1. **Only the generator crosses.** A method can be re-applied; a recording of one success cannot. Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Fig 10 :68: *"a generator crosses, an instance does not, and the level it lands on remakes it."* The operation sequence is the generator; the achieved living system in H1 is the instance.
2. **The new habitat is enumerated, not composed.** Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* You read H2 off H2. Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* You inherit actors you did not choose (Fig 11 :67).
3. **Actor valence is relative to the residual.** Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* A mineral that helps in H1 can be inert or harmful in H2. There is no standing list of good catalysts (Fig 11 :51).
4. **What recurs is an isomer or a functional group, not the identical arrangement.** Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* If the feedstock set is shared but bond order differs, the two pathways are isomers. If even the feedstocks differ, they share only the functional group (Fig 12 :124: *"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*).
5. **A laboratory solve is a substituted habitat.** Fig 11 :70: *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."* Showing the pathway works under H1-like conditions proves the wiring, not that it closes R in H2.
6. **The exact pathway on a named planet is a `gap`.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*; Fig 13 :135: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*. The framework returns the shape any answer must have and the falsifiers, then stops where the instruments stop.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that life is common, and not that it is rare. That is a different question, ORI-0093.
- Not that any named pathway (cyanosulfidic, hydrothermal, lipid-first, RNA-first) is the one that would work on Mars, Europa, Enceladus, or an exoplanet.
- Not that identical chemistry in two habitats is impossible. It is `specified` as unlikely under Fig 11, and R1 in §5 would settle it.
- Not a claim that theory alone decides. The ground does.
- Not `grounded`. No second genesis has been observed and no controlled H2-condition solve has been run here.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating reading, not a further argument. Concretely:

- **Second-genesis test.** An independently originated biosphere observed with its chemistry measured. If its pathway uses the same atoms in the same bond order as Earth life under a disjoint habitat, R1 fires and literal portability is real.
- **Habitat-substitution test.** Run a candidate pathway under a second planet's measured solvent, atmosphere, and mineral inventory, with every required condition reproduced and measured, and with no condition silently imported from the laboratory (Fig 11 :68-69). If R closes, C2 survives for that pair.
- **Valence test.** Measure the same catalyst's effect on R across two documented habitats. Invariance breaks Fig 11 :49-51.
- **Seed test.** Plant the same operation sequence in two measured habitats. Different outcomes with equal fidelity of execution support the seed reading; identical outcomes refute it.
- **Threshold test.** Measure whether R actually reaches zero (⋛) in H2, not merely that some precursor formed.

## 5. Pre-registered falsifiers

Written before the conclusion, per the seat-map rule:

1. **R1:** one concrete pathway closes R in two habitats with disjoint solvent, atmosphere, and mineral sets, measured.
2. **R2:** a claimed universal method fails to re-close R in a fresh habitat after all required conditions were reproduced and measured.
3. **R3:** an actor's valence toward R is invariant across a documented change of habitat.
4. **R4:** a habitat is shown composable from the pathway alone, or moving R leaves the actor set unchanged.
5. **R5:** expert agreement, with no new ground reading, correctly changes the state of R.

Any one fires → the claim is withdrawn or narrowed. None has fired in this simulated run.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The figure set can say what a portable answer must look like and what would have to be true for the answer to differ. It cannot name the chemistry on a named planet, because naming it would mean already holding it (Fig 13 :136: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*). *They're Scanning Books* warns that *"A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six."* Another habitat need not use our axes. The lineage, the refuter, and the stop are recorded so a later reader can locate the error. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that one origin-of-life pathway works everywhere, and it does not name a pathway for any particular world. It says the question is two questions. The operation sequence, the generator, can cross between habitats; the concrete arrangement, the instance, cannot. A new planet's habitat has to be enumerated from that planet, since moving the problem substitutes a habitat and you inherit actors you did not choose, and the effect of any given mineral or energy source depends on the residual you are trying to close. So what would recur on a very different world is an isomer or a functional analogue, same feedstocks arranged differently or the same operations filling different slots, not the Earth recipe replayed. A laboratory result under familiar conditions proves the wiring and not the capability. Which chemistry would actually work on a named world is an open gap; the answer stops where the instruments stop. What is offered is the shape any answer must have, four falsifiers written in advance, and the measurements that would close it.
