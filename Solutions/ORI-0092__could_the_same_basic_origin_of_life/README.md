## Question

Could the same basic origin-of-life pathway work on planets with very different oceans, atmospheres, and minerals?

**Source:** `Questions/origins of life.txt` line 127, section 10, Life beyond Earth and broader implications.

**Restated as an effect.** Given a pathway P that closed residual R, where R is the gap between a prebiotic world and a living, evolving system, in habitat H1, does P close R in habitat H2 whose solvent, atmosphere, and mineral inventory differ? Option A: assert that the same concrete pathway is portable. Option B: keep the figure-derived shape, namely that the method or generator can cross while the concrete arrangement cannot, and that what recurs in H2 is an isomer or functional analogue found by re-enumerating H2. A sends an ungrounded generalisation and misdirects instrument design; B sends a checkable shape, stated refuters, and an open slot until a ground settles it.

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term (formula step "Direction").** A candidate answer has three parts: (1) a substrate-independent operation sequence that could be re-run, (2) a habitat enumeration for H2 read off H2 rather than copied from H1, and (3) a ground test that would show whether R closed. Writing this down before the run makes the preference refusable by the same test as everything else.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to believe the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. A room does not add an anchor. *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"* (Fig 7 :13).
2. Selecting for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation. *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40).
3. Showing the source before taking the verdict spends the reader's independence. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42).
