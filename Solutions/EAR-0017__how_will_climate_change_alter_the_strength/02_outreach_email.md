# Outreach draft - check the shape claim before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` (F1 to F4) survives a reader's check. If it does not, there is nothing else to look at.

---

**Subject:** Would this monsoon claim fail in your hands?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one checkable claim about monsoon projections and I would like you to test it against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the claim fails, that is the useful outcome and I will retract it.
>
> **The claim (`general` on the shape, `specified` on any regional application, not `grounded`):** "the monsoon response to climate change" is not one question. It is a union of three conditions, strength, location and timing, in each of several regional systems. The figures I am working from give a structural rule: a cross-kind composition requires a structure between two states, and a gradient over space and a gradient over time are different terms because the state it is bonded to is part of the term. So location and timing cannot be treated as extra columns on a strength number. The habitat also has to be enumerated two steps out: aerosols, ocean heat uptake, cloud feedback, ice and topography, not only the land-sea contrast. This is a shape, not a measurement, and it does not tell you the sign or the magnitude of any regional change.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the figures produce a number, that a large model ensemble is many independent witnesses, or that paleo proxies name a missing mechanism. A model ensemble is a filter for effort; gauges, reanalysis and proxies are the witnesses. I am not asking you to trust the source.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before this message:** (1) a monsoon shown to compose as a direct bond between two states with no structural intermediary; (2) a single global index that predicts every regional strength, location and timing change without a per-region residual; (3) strength, location and timing collapsing to one term under the symmetry test; (4) model agreement, with no independence test and no ground reading, accepted as settling a regional projection. If any of these holds, I withdraw the claim.
>
> **What I would like from you:** one line on which of those four you think fires, or what you would count as a failure that I have not listed. A refutation is more useful to me than an endorsement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figure text with line numbers: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` (local extract, cited as Fig N :line)
> - Full derivation and retraction log: `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` in this folder
> - The verification packet with the three checkable sub-claims: `08_expert_verification_packet.md`
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the eight-step formula is in the figures link once you opt in. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - [name]
