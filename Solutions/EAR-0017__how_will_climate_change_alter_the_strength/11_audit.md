## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed zero. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 13 :74-76, Fig 11 :44 and :52-53, Fig 1 :19, Fig 2 :35 and :40, Fig 9 :54 and :32.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07_the_tether_answer.md`, namely the monsoon is a union of three observables per region, a structure sits between the surface states, the habitat must be enumerated two steps out, and the ground settles the numbers.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** any single-sentence public form of that shorthand, for example "monsoons strengthen under warming" or "climate change shifts monsoons".

**R_T is nonzero.** Four constraints held by the shorthand do not survive the trip back down:

- per-slot indexing is lost when the sentence becomes global;
- the distinction among strength, location and timing is lost when it becomes one verb;
- the two-step habitat and its remote drivers are lost when the sentence names only the land-sea contrast;
- the independence requirement on witnesses is lost when a model consensus is reported as the answer.

Conversely, two things survive cleanly: a structure must sit between the surface states, and the space-bonded and time-bonded gradients are distinct terms. Those are the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically because no ground reading exists here; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the formula or the slogan as identical to the figure set would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a measured gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` states.
