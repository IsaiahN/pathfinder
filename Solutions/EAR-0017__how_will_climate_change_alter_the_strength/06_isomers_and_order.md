# Isomers, functional groups and order in the monsoon question

**Mode: `general` on the structural claims, `specified` on their regional application.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The audit leaves three questions: are strength, location and timing isomers, a functional group, or unrelated quantities; in what order must the loops run; and does the union need unbundling before the search. This file answers those without adding any new atom.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held for a monsoon: states (space, time, energy as land-ocean thermal contrast, season, available energy) and structures (gradient, spectrum as moisture ordering, network as teleconnections). Bonds held: the seven plus negation. No new primitive is available without import. Anchors: the figures for structure, the ground for any number.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are strength, location and timing isomers?

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

Fig 13 :76: *"A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."*

As the question states them, strength, location and timing do **not** share one atom set under one bond. Location is a gradient bonded to space; timing is a gradient bonded to time; strength is the magnitude of the resulting moisture and energy flux. They are different molecules over a shared substrate, not isomers. **Not isomers.**

Across regions the answer differs. The South Asian, East Asian, West African and American monsoons each satisfy the same substructure, a thermal gradient between two surface states with a cross-kind structure between them. That is Fig 12's functional group: the group says "monsoon", the qualifier says which member. They are not isomers either, because their atoms differ (Tibetan Plateau heating, West African topography, American land-sea configuration). This is a **functional-group claim**, and it is triangulated between Fig 12 :124 and the regional treatment in Moon and Ha (2020, opened), which computes onset and retreat separately for nine domains.

The stronger isomer claim would require both systems written over the full shared atom set and differing only in arrangement. No source read here licenses that, so it is not asserted.

### Q2 - What is the dependency order?

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*

Fig 3 :24-26: the middle link, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, *"has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."*

The order for this question:

1. **Perception.** Do gauges, reanalysis and proxies resolve each observable in each region? (Fig 3 :27, *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*)
2. **Vocabulary.** Thermal contrast, moisture convergence, energy flux equator, aerosol forcing, ocean heat uptake.
3. **Objective.** Define strength, location and timing as measurable indices with explicit thresholds. This is the missing middle link.
4. **Planning.** Run scenario-conditioned projections only after the index exists.
5. **Learn and carry.** Verify on the historical period before carrying the index forward.

Most monsoon effort lands at step 4, where the measurements already exist. The audit's recommendation to bind the objective first (Fig 3) is therefore a dependency claim, not a preference.

### Q3 - Is the union a partition or a compound?

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*

Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*

Strength, location and timing are overlapping conditions on one circulation. A single lever (for example, "increase greenhouse gases") changes all three but does not derive any one from the others. The region x observable pairs must be run as separate loops. This is a union, not a partition; treating it as a list of independent problems would lose the intersections, and treating it as one problem would lose the differences.

### Q4 - Where do cycles enter?

Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*

Fig 13 :32: *"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise."*

Cloud feedback, ice albedo, soil moisture and ocean heat uptake re-enter the monsoon as input. The composition is therefore a cycle, not a plain chain. The sign of the loop decides whether a change closes (a persistent shift) or damps. Internal variability is the compounding remainder. This is why timing, the most seasonal observable, is the least certain in the IPCC AR6 SPM (opened): delayed onset has high confidence over North and South America and West Africa, while delayed retreat over West Africa is only medium confidence. A chain model cannot produce that asymmetry; a cycle model with a sign can.

## FALSIFIER, WRITTEN BEFORE THE RUN

If a single figure-governed bond derives strength, location and timing as one term under Fig 12's symmetry test, Q1 and Q3 fail and the union collapses. If the dependency order can be reversed, with projections made before the objective index exists and no residual left over, Q2 fails. If all regional monsoon changes can be written as an open chain with no feedback re-entry and the residual still closes, Q4 fails. Any of these fires sends the census back to `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 1.

## CONCLUSION

Strength, location and timing are **different molecules over a shared substrate**, not isomers. Regional monsoons are members of a **functional group** (Fig 12 :124), not isomers of one another. The loops must run perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, carry, in that dependency order (Fig 3 :20). The question is a **union of overlapping conditions**, so it must be unbundled before search (Fig 9 :54). Feedbacks make each local loop a cycle (Fig 12 :121), which is the structural reason the near-term timing result is low confidence. Each claim has a stated falsifier. None is a ground reading.
