# The Tether's answer on monsoons under climate change

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the scenario-conditioned world numbers, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The historical monsoon and the historical forcing are observed, but the future monsoon does not resolve to one value. It resolves to a distribution whose sign is region-dependent and whose magnitude depends on the emissions scenario and on the rate of aerosol decline. IPCC AR6 (opened) reports that the mid-century decline in South and Southeast Asian monsoon rain was dominated by anthropogenic aerosols, while the later recovery is driven by greenhouse gases; those are two different forcings on the same system. The residual is therefore a union of conditions, not one hard problem.

## 2. The shape (figure-governed, `general`)

1. **Unbundle before answering.** Strength, location and timing are overlapping conditions on one circulation (Fig 9 :54, :57). They are distinct molecules over a shared substrate, not one scalar (Fig 13 :76). Each region x observable pair gets its own loop. Verdicts stay separate and are never averaged.
2. **No global averaging of the residual.** *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated"* (Fig 1 :19). A global monsoon index is a filter for effort, not a witness for a regional decision (Fig 9 :60).
3. **A structure sits between the surface states.** Cross-kind composition forbids a direct bond of two states (Fig 13 :74-75). The monsoon reads as land-ocean states with a thermal gradient structure between them, and the seasonal reversal is a second gradient over time. Location and timing are different terms because the state the gradient binds to is part of the term (Fig 13 :76).
4. **Enumerate the habitat two steps out.** Fig 11 :43-46 and :52-53 require the conditions, actors and relations to be read off the world, and warn that the residual that matters can be three steps out. For monsoons that means aerosols, ocean heat uptake, cloud feedback, ice and topography, not only the land-sea contrast. Chen et al. (2024, opened) trace the northward shift of South Asian rain to Eurasian cloud feedback and Southern Ocean heat uptake, both remote from the monsoon itself.
5. **The ground decides, not the ensemble.** Fig 2 :35 and :40, Fig 7 :13. Abramowitz et al. (2019, opened) show why CMIP models share biases. Gauges, reanalysis and paleo proxies are the anchors; models are working frames. Paleo is a low-overlap import used with provenance (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8).
6. **Feedbacks make the loop a cycle.** Fig 12 :121 and Fig 13 :32. Internal variability is the compounding remainder, which is why near-term timing is the least certain of the three observables.

## 3. What the world sources add (secondary, `specified`)

These are not figure outputs. They are opened world sources, reported here because they are the ground's current answer, with author, venue and year in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

- **Strength.** IPCC AR6 WG1 Chapter 4 reports that global land monsoon precipitation *"will likely increase"* by around **1.3 to 2.4 percent per degree of global warming** while Northern Hemisphere monsoon circulation weakens. Moon and Ha (2020) find the Northern Hemisphere increasing more than the Southern. Chen et al. (2024) resolve the apparent paradox: a weakening component and a northward-shift component partly offset, and the shift drives most of the South Asian rainfall increase.
- **Location.** IPCC AR6 Chapter 4 reports a north-south asymmetry (NH increase greater than SH) and an east-west asymmetry (Asian-African increase, North American decrease). Chen et al. (2024) attribute the robust South Asian northward shift to extratropical clouds and Southern Ocean heat uptake. Moon and Ha (2020) find the South African monsoon decreasing in the near term while Indian and East Asian rain increases.
- **Timing.** IPCC AR6 SPM B.3.3: *"The monsoon season is projected to have a delayed onset over North and South America and West Africa (high confidence) and a delayed retreat over West Africa (medium confidence)."* Moon and Ha (2020) project extended rainy seasons over most domains except the American monsoon, with Indian and Indo-Chinese lengthening mainly through delayed retreat and East Asian through earlier onset.

These three bullets are **scenario-conditioned and reversible by the ground**. They are not what the figures produced.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a single global monsoon verdict. The global index is a budget filter.
- Not a derivation of the sign or magnitude of any regional change from the six terms and seven bonds. That is a `gap` at the figure level (F7).
- Not a claim that a large model ensemble is many independent witnesses. It shares code, parameterisations and evaluation data.
- Not a claim that paleo proxies name a missing primitive. They constrain a held term.
- Not a claim about one scenario without naming it. All numbers above are conditioned on SSPs as reported by the sources.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement in this folder settles anything.

## 5. What would settle it - the ground

- A per-region, per-observable index with explicit onset and retreat thresholds, checked against gauges and reanalysis (the missing middle link of Fig 3).
- An independence test on any ensemble used as a witness, with out-of-sample validation (Abramowitz et al., 2019).
- A source-discriminating comparison between the thermodynamic and dynamic mechanisms, of the kind He et al. (2025) attempt with paleo proxies: an instrument that distinguishes sources rather than reading one green.
- Standing falsifiers from `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4: a direct state-state monsoon bond; a global index with no per-region residual; collapse of the three observables to one term; model agreement accepted without ground contact.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The figures give the shape any answer must have and record where the instruments stop: the regional numbers are left to the ground. A search that finds no figure line for a number is not a proof that no number exists (Fig 8 :39, *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*). The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not predict one number for the world's monsoons. It says the question is a union of three conditions, strength, location and timing, in each of several regional systems, so a global average is a filter and not a witness. A monsoon composes as land and ocean states with a thermal gradient structure between them, and a gradient over space (location) is a different term from a gradient over time (timing). The habitat must be enumerated two steps out: aerosols, ocean heat uptake, cloud feedback, ice and topography, because the residual that matters is often remote from the monsoon itself. Models are working frames that share evidence; gauges, reanalysis and paleo proxies are the ground. The figures return this shape, not the sign or magnitude of any regional change, and they carry four refuters written in advance. The regional numbers come from the assessment literature and stay scenario-conditioned until the ground settles them.
