# Expert verification packet - the Tether on galaxy simulation accuracy

**What this is:** a claim in a form a galaxy-simulation researcher or an observational astronomer can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No measurement of any code's error is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a number.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Accuracy is not one number. It is a residual between what a simulation predicts for ordinary baryons and what a telescope returns, indexed per component (gas, stars, dust), per object and per regime. Averaging across slots discards the signal that says where to look. Convergence is a gate, not a ground: the sky is the anchor, and suites that calibrate to the same observables are one evidence pool, not two confirmations. Two codes with the same ingredients but different subgrid feedback are isomers, invisible in the ingredient list and decisive in gas flows; the live residual sits at the uninstrumented middle link, chaos limits object-level claims to ensemble statements, and calibration carries a round-trip gap that must be reported. What is offered is the shape any answer must have, falsifiers written in advance, and the class of source-discriminating instrument that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal and methodological; checkable by reading Figures 1, 2 and 4

- **Statement:** accuracy must be reported per slot, against an independent observational anchor, with the calibration round-trip gap stated. A single global number, code consensus and pure convergence are all disqualified.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 - *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 2 :29 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Fig 2 :40 - *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Fig 4 :30 - *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*
- **Refuter:** a single global accuracy number that predicts, prospectively and per slot, which component will diverge next in a held-out galaxy; or two suites with independent solvers, subgrid physics and calibration data that agree on a held-out observable and survive an independent measurement; or a calibration statistic that is lossless over all other observables so that R_T is zero.

### C2 - astronomy-facing; checkable against the simulation literature

- **Statement:** two suites with the same atom set but different subgrid feedback are isomers under Fig 12 :117, *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Their matching stellar mass functions do not establish matching baryon physics, because gas flows, CGM properties and dust differ.
- **What to check:** Crain and van de Voort 2023 report that *"Similar galaxy populations can emerge in simulations with dissimilar subgrid feedback implementations. However, these models generally predict markedly different gas flow rates into, and out of, galaxies and their haloes."* Ludlow et al. 2020 report that *"even simulations starting from the same initial conditions, but carried out using different hydrodynamical codes, do not converge to similar solutions"* and that convergence is easier for stellar masses than for gaseous winds and the CGM. van de Sande et al. 2018 find that *"the areas of discrepancy and agreement are different in the various simulations."* Are those characterizations faithful to the papers?
- **Refuter:** two codes with different subgrid feedback that also agree within observational uncertainty on gas inflow and outflow rates, CGM absorption and dust attenuation. Then the bond difference stops being decisive in behaviour and the isomer claim fails.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation, with an instrument limit

- **Statement:** the live residual sits at the uninstrumented middle link, and chaos caps object-level claims. Fig 3 :27 - *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Fig 13 :32 - *"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise."* Ludlow et al. 2020 report that chaotic galaxy-to-galaxy variations account for up to 50 percent of scaling-relation variance, which is the figure's shape observed at a different scale.
- **What to check:** is there a direct observation of individual star-formation and feedback events at the subgrid scale in a representative volume? If there is, the middle link is instrumented. Can any suite predict one identified galaxy's star-formation history to observational error over a Hubble time without stochastic divergence?
- **Refuter:** either of the above demonstrated. If the subgrid link is instrumented and the residual does not change, or if single-object prediction succeeds, C3 is false. Note the honest reporting rule: the astronomy instance is a low-overlap corroboration of the figure's shape, not a second independent witness for the figure itself.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not one accuracy number, and not a verdict that any named simulation is accurate or inaccurate.
- Not that any code is best. Prestige and community size are contact costs, not evidence (Seat Map :163).
- Not that convergence proves validation, or that agreement among suites corroborates the world.
- Not a derivation of a feedback prescription, no new primitive, and no imported atom that moves the wall (Fig 6 :21).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by an independent measurement.
- Not that CGM absorption is "the answer". It is one instance of the source-discriminating class; the framework returns a kind of thing, never the answer itself (Fig 9 :32).

## 5. What would settle it

An independent, source-discriminating observation, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. Does a held-out observable discriminate two feedback bonds that both match the galaxy population?
2. Does a single global number predict per-slot divergence prospectively?
3. Does agreement hold between suites with independent solvers, subgrid physics and calibration data on a held-out observable?
4. Does convergence alone settle accuracy with no observational comparison?
