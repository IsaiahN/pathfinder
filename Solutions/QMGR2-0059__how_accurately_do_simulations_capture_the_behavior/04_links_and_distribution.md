## What the wider literature bears on the question

1. **The review and the comparison paper agree on the shape, not on a number.** Crain and van de Voort 2023 list enduring successes (clustering, mass, size, star-formation rate, scaling relations) and outstanding challenges (circumgalactic gas, subgrid sensitivity). van de Sande et al. 2018 show that the areas of agreement and discrepancy differ between EAGLE, HYDRANGEA, Horizon-AGN and Magneticum. Neither hands us one accuracy percentage. That is the per-slot residual of Fig 1 :19 stated in the literature's own vocabulary.
2. **Calibration is not prediction.** Crain and van de Voort state that calibration characteristics *"cannot be considered as predictions of the simulation"*, and the review notes that *"Simulations are therefore often better suited to offering qualitative insight rather than quantitative predictions"*. This is Fig 1 :27 (a frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces) corroborated from inside astronomy.
3. **Code convergence and physical fidelity are different.** Ludlow et al. 2020 show that codes starting from the same initial conditions do not converge to similar solutions, and that convergence is easier for stellar masses than for gaseous winds and the CGM. This is the union of section 1.3 of `01` observed: more resolution clears the numerical condition and leaves the subgrid condition standing.
4. **The engineering field corroborates the split at low overlap.** NAFEMS and Oberkampf distinguish verification from validation as *"two completely different issues"*. This is a different evidence pool from astronomy, so it is a genuine structural witness for F3, but not a witness for any astronomical number.
5. **Source discrimination is the owed instrument.** The metamerism essay and the CGM conclusion of Crain and van de Voort point the same way: a reading that several subgrid causes can produce does not name its source; a different instrument is required. This is Fig 6 :32-33 and Fig 8 :39.

No world source conflicts with the figure verdict after re-census. The apparent conflict between code agreement (the review's framing) and code divergence (Ludlow) was resolved by re-census R2: agreement is about calibration targets, divergence is about physical implementation. Agreement from a shared calibration pool is one witness, not two (Fig 2 :40).
