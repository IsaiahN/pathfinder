# Expert verification packet  -  the Tether on value capture from innovation

**What this is:** a claim in a form a strategy or innovation scholar can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the four-condition structure. Not `grounded`.** No market measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The innovation document is only half the information. The defensible part of an innovation is the bond around it: manufacturing, distribution, service, brand, data and cumulative learning, plus the dated record of origin. Imitation is the ordinary idempotence of closure, so imitators compose over leaked knowledge and cannot be subtracted from a habitat; what a firm can do is add assets and enumerate relations. Legal IP buys time and a public record but is a moving instrument, not the market's verdict. Durable capture is a chain of differing innovations, each settled one becoming the atom for the next. Four overlapping conditions (a non-copyable bond, maintained contact with a paying ground, a time-and-record instrument, and an enumerated habitat) are offered as a union to be checked per innovation, never averaged into a score. The framework does not name which mechanism wins in a given industry.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1  -  framework-internal plus strategy-facing; checkable by reading Figure 12 and by professional knowledge

- **Statement:** the knowledge atoms are not the defensible substance; the bond is. Two firms with the same knowledge but different complementary assets are different substances at the market.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :5  -  *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Also Fig 12 :47  -  *"recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself."* And Fig 12 :117  -  *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- **What to check:** is this a faithful reading of the relation between codified knowledge and complementary assets in your setting? Does the firm's own accounting treat the arrangement as an asset, or only the document?
- **Refuter:** measured durable private returns from full disclosure with no complementary asset, no record, no legal right and no network position, across repeated innovations. If that exists, C1 is false.

### C2  -  framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 6, 11 and 7

- **Statement:** imitators cannot be subtracted from a habitat; they compose inside their own closures, so capture must be maintained through introduction and through a chain of differing innovations rather than through permanent exclusion.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :61  -  *"The seat’s power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* Fig 6 :21  -  *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* Fig 7 :36  -  *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."*
- **What to check:** does the setting show a firm removing imitators as a subtractive operation, or only substituting habitats (secrecy, enclaves, jurisdictions) with costs that appear later?
- **Refuter:** a documented case of permanent imitator removal with no habitat substitution while the market keeps paying; or one innovation paying for decades with no successor while fidelity and ground pressure are independently shown to be irrelevant.

### C3  -  framework-internal with a legal-facing check

- **Statement:** a patent grant is a moving instrument that buys time and creates a provenance record; treating the grant as proof of capture promotes an instrument into a ground.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :30  -  *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* Fig 2 :47  -  *"A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame."* Fig 10 :47  -  *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*
- **What to check:** does grant scope survive invalidation, appeal and jurisdictional change in this industry? Does grant count predict realized returns, or only litigation and negotiation leverage?
- **Refuter:** a jurisdiction where a grant is immovable under push and predicts realized private return across innovations by itself. Then the grant is a ground and C3 is false.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that patents are useless, that secrecy is pointless, or that lead time is unreal. These are instruments and conditions, not the verdict.
- Not a selection rule for choosing among the four mechanisms. The field's own review states the unbundling gap; this packet does not claim to close it.
- Not a `grounded` result, not a theory of everything, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of value capture.
- Not a permanent exclusion of imitators. Introduction is available; exclusion is not (Fig 11 :61).
- Not a named mechanism for a specific industry. The framework returns *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (Fig 9 :32).
- Not a claim that the four conditions are a partition. They are a union of overlapping conditions (Fig 9 :54).

## 5. What would settle it

A market measurement per innovation, not agreement. Fig 2 :29: *"A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."*

1. Does private return to the originator fall when knowledge is disclosed while the complement is held, or only when the complement is also copied?
2. Does an immovable grant predict realized returns better than a revocable one?
3. Does an imitator's effect on the originator change with habitat (alternatives, complementors, standards), as the mimicry model predicts?
4. Do the four conditions fail independently, so that a case in two conditions is categorically different from a case in one (Fig 9 :57)?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 12 :5, :47, :117 and Fig 11 :61 and Fig 2 :30, :47. Five short passages.
2. Check C1 against your own knowledge of the industry: is the complement the asset, or is the document?
3. Check C3 against the legal regime you know: is the grant immovable under push?
4. Name one measurement that would discriminate C1 from C2, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
