## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not whether an innovation creates value but **who the market pays for it, and for how long**. In effect terms:

- **b** is the firm's belief about what customers will pay for, formed from its own history and inherited priors.
- **a** is what the firm does: invest, disclose, patent, keep secret, build manufacturing, build distribution, license, form an alliance, move first.
- **Γ(b,a)** is what the firm's library of knowledge and assets predicts it will receive.
- **o′** is what the market actually returns: price, share, profit, survival.
- **s** is the innovation, indexed per innovation and per time. R is never averaged across a portfolio (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22).

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** (Fig 1 :19)
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** (Fig 1 :22)

The observed residual is that predicted private return exceeds realized private return while the innovation still creates value for customers, imitators, suppliers or complementors. The phenomenon to explain is the **distribution and duration of that gap**, not its existence.

- Option A, "value sits in the knowledge, so protect the knowledge": leads to an exclusion-first strategy (patents, secrecy, legal enforcement) and predicts that tightness of protection orders outcomes.
- Option B, "value sits in the arrangement and the habitat, while knowledge diffuses": leads to a strategy of holding a non-copyable bond, maintaining contact with a paying ground across a chain of differing innovations, and keeping a record, and predicts that protection alone does not order outcomes.

What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a **shape** naming conditions whose conjunction keeps a slot's R small, plus an instrument that discriminates a failure of one condition from a failure of another. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no loop check can see it. It is bound in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 0, so the refuter in section 4 can fire on it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B in `specified` mode, kept per domain and never averaged.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the business question, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of value capture.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
