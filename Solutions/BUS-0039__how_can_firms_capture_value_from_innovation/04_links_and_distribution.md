## 2. What the wider literature bears on the question

**One evidence pool, one witness.** Teece 1986, Cohen, Nelson and Walsh 2000, and James, Leiblein and Lu 2013 are all in the innovation and strategy literature. They agree that knowledge leakage shifts returns toward complementary assets and that firms use bundles of mechanisms rather than patents alone, but they draw on the same conferences, the same citation chain and overlapping survey lineages. Per Fig 2 :31, *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Their agreement is one witness, not three.

- Teece 1986: *"when imitation is easy, markets don't work well, and the profits from innovation may accrue to the owners of certain complementary assets, rather than to the developers of the intellectual property."* This corroborates F2 at the level of the finding, but the abstract does not supply a mechanism at the level of the bond. It is corroboration, not a verdict.
- Cohen, Nelson and Walsh 2000: patents least emphasized, secrecy and lead time most emphasized, with patenting used for blocking and negotiation. This corroborates F4 (the grant is an instrument) and F6 (lead time is a handoff). It also conflicts with any naive reading in which legal IP is the primary capture route; that conflict returned the derivation to the census and was re-mapped as condition C3.
- James, Leiblein and Lu 2013: *"we have yet to develop a robust theory that allows us to unbundle the characteristics of institutions, industries, firms, and individual technologies that affect the selection of particular value capture mechanisms."* This corroborates F8 and, importantly, states the same `gap` the figures state: the field has a mechanism list, not a selector. Do not present the four conditions as a solved selection rule.

**Low-overlap witnesses**, each from a different field and each opened directly:

1. **Philosophy of knowledge.** Polanyi's *"we can know more than we can tell"* corroborates the claim that a codified disclosure does not carry the whole arrangement (F2, F3). It says nothing about markets, so it corroborates the mechanism shape only.
2. **Engineering / cryptography.** Kerckhoffs' principle, *"Design your system to be secure even if the attacker has complete knowledge of all its algorithms"*, corroborates F3 from a field where the failure of secrecy is formalised. The analogue is exact in shape: robustness comes from a changeable key, not from hiding the design. It is an analogue, not firm-level evidence.
3. **Animal behaviour / ecology.** Honma et al. 2008 find that *"the availability of alternative prey rather than the precise method of predator learning critically determines the relationship between Model and Mimic."* This corroborates Fig 11 :49-51: the effect of an imitator depends on the habitat, not on a standing property of the imitator. Low overlap with management strategy.

**Framework-internal essays** are used for the canonical reading of "shape" in `05_what_shape_means.md` only. Their author wrote the figures, so they are not independent world witnesses. That overlap is recorded in the ledger.

## 3. Distribution rules

Figure-grounded reasons for how material is offered:

- **Fig 4 :22:** *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Send the generator, the residual and the refuter, not the conclusion. A recording carried upward *"looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion"* (Fig 4 :25).
- **Fig 10 :52:** *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Seat 4 must not hold the interiors. A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28:** *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"* (quoted across two lines). Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the article.
- **Fig 2 :40:** agreement from a shared pool is worthless as a verdict (quoted in `01`). Later agreement about the framework is never pooled as corroboration.
- **Fig 2 :42:** *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Buy it from reviewers in STR, TIM and adjacent fields who have not seen the derivation.
- **Fig 10 :59:** *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* Declare the stake; do not ask for belief.

**A hard practical constraint.** The AOM page states: *"Artificial Intelligence: Work submitted to AOM must be created by the authors and not the product of artificial intelligence tools unless appropriate to the research question and properly cited."* This whole solution is a simulated derivation produced with an LLM and is labelled as such everywhere. It must **not** be submitted as an authored empirical or theoretical article. The legitimate use is as a private checking instrument: post the residual and the refuter, invite a human to run the check, and let that human author whatever they conclude. Any submission must be rewritten and owned by a human author who can defend the derivation, with the AI contribution disclosed under the applicable policy.

## 4. The two-stage offer, compressed

**Stage 1.** Send `08_expert_verification_packet.md` sections 1 to 3 only: the one-paragraph claim, the three checkable sub-claims with their verbatim figure lines and refuters, and the non-claims. No provenance bundle, no essay links.

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If the recipient is curious and has already recorded a verdict on stage 1, offer the three public links: the figure text, the formula and usage rule, and the essays. Their later agreement is a conversation, not corroboration (Fig 2 :40).

**Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method, never as an answer (Fig 4 :22). Each user writes a refuter before running it and states the mode of what they get.

**Stop rule.** If stage 1 fails, the theory is not offered. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.

**Hosting.** Nothing here is hosted or sent by this agent. The human operator hosts the packet on an opt-in page and sends one to one. Prefer a download page that lets a recipient decline contact over an attachment pushed to a list.
