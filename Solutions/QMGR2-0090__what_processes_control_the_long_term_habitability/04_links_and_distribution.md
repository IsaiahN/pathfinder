## Wider literature that bears on the shape

1. **The negative-feedback lineage.** Walker et al. 1981 established the silicate-weathering thermostat; Foley 2015 and Gillmann et al. 2024 coupled it to the interior and to plate tectonics; Cawood et al. 2025 placed it in a comparative planetology frame. This is a single scientific programme with several groups, so its internal agreement is strong but its independence from the planetary-science evidence pool is low. Cite it as one witness with a long lineage, not as many witnesses.
2. **The systems/regulation lineage.** Lenton et al. 2020 and the Tellus 2026 piece reframe habitability as feedback between life and environment, explicitly removing teleology. This is a genuinely lower-overlap frame (Earth system science and history of science) and is the best world corroboration of Fig 11's agent-and-habitat shape.
3. **The ecology lineage.** "Feedbacks in ecology and evolution" (2022) argues organism-environment feedbacks across population, community and planetary scales. Lowest overlap with the planetary-interior literature; corroborates Fig 11's contact cascade and Fig 12's cycle.
4. **The definitional baseline.** NASA 2026 and NASEM 2023 both define habitability as conditions sustained through time. They anchor the effect-term description of the residual in the field's own words.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22-25** — *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down. A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Sending the full framework before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and the refuter; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** — *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** — *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the article.
- **Fig 2 :40** — *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* A send selected for people likely to agree produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 2 :42** — *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* The cost here is moderate: a wrong shape would misdirect measurement effort on exoplanets. Independent checkers are worth seeking through the public opt-in venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its refuter; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) record their verdict before any theory is shown; (4) then the three public links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (5) offer the formula as a method with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

## The two-stage offer

- **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52 — *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"* — and Fig 2 :45 — *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* The recipient checks the output without holding the framework's interior.
- **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. Their verdict on stage 1 is recorded first and never pooled as corroboration.
- **Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted.** Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* The formula is a generator.
- **Stage 3 still needs a ground per problem.** `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": a frame *"cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it holds."* Each user must write down what would refute their result before running it.
