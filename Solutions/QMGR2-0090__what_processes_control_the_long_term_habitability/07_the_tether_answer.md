# The Tether's answer: what processes control the long-term habitability of planets?

**Mode: `specified` for the shape, `general` for the structural cross-domain claims, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a measured process list. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

We want to know which processes determine whether a planet stays habitable over geological time. In effect terms, the residual is the gap between what a present-day snapshot predicts about future habitability and what actually happens to it. Fig 9 :54-55 names the structure: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* and *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Habitability is a compound of stellar, interior, surface and biological conditions, not one hard question. It has its own loop and its own ground.

## 2. The shape

1. **Habitability is a property of the coupled system, not of the planet alone.** Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* So the controlling processes are the ones that maintain contact across the strata, and the answer cannot be read from mass and orbit alone.
2. **The process set is enumerated from the world, not composed from the figures.** Fig 11 :48: *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."* The figures do not contain carbon, water or plate tectonics. Those enter as provenanced imports (Fig 6 :23).
3. **The conditions form a union compound with cascading contact.** Fig 11 :52: *"And contact cascades."* A process can be harmful at first order and load-bearing at second order. Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49), so no standing list of "good" processes survives a change of residual.
4. **Recycling loops and their sign govern persistence.** Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* The carbonate-silicate thermostat is the world's instance: a negative loop on Earth, a broken or positive loop on Venus. Fig 13 :24 adds the network: *"that entities relate, and influence travels."*
5. **Energy must be a spendable gradient, and its source moves.** Fig 13 :14: *"conserved, finite, directionless alone"*; Fig 13 :22: *"a difference that can be spent."* The star supplies the gradient, but its luminosity rises with time, so long-term habitability is tracking a moving anchor rather than sitting in a fixed zone. Fig 13 :88-89: *"The residual of the turn just closed is what the next turn composes against, so no two passes begin from the same place."*
6. **The ground is persistence of life, not agreement.** Fig 2 :29-30: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* A habitable-zone model is a frame; persistence of life is the anchor. Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
7. **A planet runs in the unmaintained mode.** Fig 11 :56: *"Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept."* Regulation in the world literature (Lenton et al. 2020) is feedback, not an author. There is no seat that can subtract habitat actors; introduction is available and exclusion is not (Fig 11 :61).
8. **The figures return a shape, not the process list.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* The canonical limit is a shape, not a name. Naming a master lever is a provenanced import, and its provenance is the whole issue.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a list of named master processes. Carbon, water, weathering, tectonics and the magnetic field are imports with provenance, not figure derivations.
- Not a theory of everything or a final room. Fig 7 :6: *"the limit: no last room."*
- Not that habitability is a state or a yes/no property. It is a maintained trajectory; time is a keystone (NASEM 2023).
- Not that agreement among papers or experts settles it. Fig 2 :40 disqualifies shared-pool agreement for a verdict.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement here settles which process is necessary or sufficient for a given world.

## 5. What would settle it — the ground

A discriminator, not another argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (via exemplar): an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding tests are:

- A world whose long-term habitability is measured to be fully intrinsic, with no stellar or biospheric contribution (breaks the coupled-system claim).
- A measured case where the governing conditions are independent, with no feedback or cascade (breaks the cycle claim).
- A case where the anchor is a model proxy that moves when frames change (breaks the ground claim).
- A planetary process derived from the six terms alone that predicts a measured habitability boundary (breaks the shape-not-a-name limit).
- The standing refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4, written before the conclusion.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The four-domain habitat census is a visible guess at the coupling axes, not nature's own list; declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral. Fig 6 :34-35: *"Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."* The world instruments (spectrographs, isotope records, climate models) are extended from worse ones that were already returning something. The derivation stops at the limit of what those instruments distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."*

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name a master process for planetary habitability, and it does not claim a final answer. It says that habitability is not a property of a planet alone: it is a maintained property of the contact between the star, the interior, the surface and any biosphere, and the ground that settles it is whether life actually persists. The conditions form a coupled union with cascading effects, and what keeps a world in a habitable state is the recycling loops and spendable gradients across those strata, not an ingredient list. The framework returns that shape and stops. The specific processes, carbon cycling, water, tectonics, magnetic shielding, biological feedback, are imports from planetary science with their provenance recorded, not derivations from the figures. What it offers is the shape any answer must have, falsifiers written in advance, and the discriminators that would close it. The anchor stays open until the ground settles it.
