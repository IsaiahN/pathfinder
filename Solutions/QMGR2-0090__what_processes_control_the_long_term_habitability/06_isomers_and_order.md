# Isomers and order: Earth, Venus, and the structure of a habitability claim

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The primary audit established the shape: habitability is a maintained cross-scale contact property. Two structural questions remain. (1) Are the divergent outcomes of Earth and Venus a difference of **arrangement** rather than a difference of ingredients? (2) What is the **order** of the dependency chain, and does the order of derivation matter? Both are owed before the shape can be offered as checkable.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Are Earth and Venus isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Fig 12 :96: *"Two entries can share every ingredient and be inverses of one another."*

Earth and Venus are drawn from the same nebular reservoir and share the same broad inventory: silicate rock, volatiles including CO2 and water, and stellar insolation (NASEM 2023; Cawood 2025). What differs is the **arrangement**: orbital distance, tectonic regime, presence of liquid water, operation of the carbonate-silicate loop, retention of the atmosphere. The outcomes are inverses in the figure's sense, one clement and one runaway.

- Under Fig 12's definition the two are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in an ingredient list (mass, bulk composition) and decisive in behaviour (habitable versus uninhabitable).
- The weaker reading is a **functional group**: both are rocky planets in the same stellar neighbourhood, with the qualifier "distance from star and carbon cycling mode" picking the member. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if Earth and Venus can be shown to have different primitive inventories, not merely different arrangements of a shared inventory, the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim stands. Equally, if a one-to-one mapping of ingredients to outcomes predicts both planets' states without reference to the bond, the arrangement carries no information and the claim fails. Triangulated for the definition (Fig 12 plus the two world sources); the mapping itself is `specified`, unmeasured.

### Q2 — What order is the chain, and does the order of derivation matter?

- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**
- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
- Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**
- Fig 13 :28: **"They alternate, and the alternation is where both the recursion and the randomness come from."**

Dependency order for habitability: star (energy source) → water and carbon availability → interior recycling and magnetic shielding → surface thermostat → biospheric feedback → persistence. Each link consumes what the one above produces. Effort spent above a broken link arrives where it cannot be used: a perfect atmospheric model cannot rescue a star that has left the main sequence.

Two order rules from the formula:

1. **Refuse before mint** (Formula step 2). The claim "habitability is a planet-only property" is refused before any process is proposed. Negation is not a bond (Operators table :70-78), so refusal is a separate operator, not a composition.
2. **Describe the residual before choosing a frame** (Fig 9 :27-28). The effect-term description of habitability comes before the choice of planetary science or ecology as a corroborating frame.

- **Falsifier:** show that the dependency order can be reversed without changing the predicted habitability outcome in a measured case. Then Fig 3's ordering is not load-bearing here and the chain collapses to a list. `general` for the rule; `specified` for this particular order.

### Q3 — Is "Gaia" a derivation or an import? (correction to `05` §2)

- Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*

**Correction.** I initially treated "Gaia" as a usable world name for the shape. It is not. Gaia is a world frame with a contested history, and Lenton et al. 2020 explicitly strips the teleology that the name carries. Adopting it would present an import as a derivation. The shape is stated in effect terms only; Gaia is cited as a low-overlap corroborating frame, never as the framework's answer. Both the first reading and the correction are visible here.

- **Falsifier:** demonstrate that the Gaia frame predicts the shape independently of the planetary-science evidence pool and with no term borrowed from the figures. That would upgrade it from corroboration to an independent witness.

### Q4 — Analogues and their limit

Fig 11 offers three habitats: unmaintained (pure contact), maintained (a seat that can introduce actors), and isolated (a substituted habitat). Applying them to planets:

| analogue | figure | what it illuminates | where it breaks |
|---|---|---|---|
| garden / managed ecosystem | Fig 11 :58-63 | maintenance is permanent work; introduction available, exclusion not | a planet has no authoring seat; the unmaintained panel governs (Fig 11 :56) |

| electromagnetic analogue | Fig 12 :95-98 (isomerism in chemistry) | same composition, different arrangement, different substance | chemistry's bonds are typed and priced; planetary "bonds" are inferred, not given |

The limit matters: analogies are a bridge for vocabulary, not evidence. Fig 8 :30: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."* Each analogue is recorded with its break.

## CONCLUSION

Earth and Venus are well read as **isomers over a shared planetary inventory**: same atoms, different bond, different substance, with the difference invisible in an ingredient list. The habitability chain is **ordered by dependency** (star, water and carbon, interior, surface, biosphere, persistence) and the order of derivation is fixed by two rules (refuse before mint; describe the residual before choosing the frame). "Gaia" is an import, not a derivation, and the habitat analogues break precisely where a planet has no authoring seat. The supporting derivation rescues the structural content of the shape without naming a master process or importing a seventh primitive.
