## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

Question: what processes control the long-term habitability of planets?

Restated as an effect: for a planet at time *t*, which processes determine whether the conditions life needs (liquid water, a usable energy supply, nutrients, clement physical conditions) remain available at *t + Δt* over geological Δt, and which processes extinguish them? An answer would let us say which quantities to measure on an exoplanet to predict whether it stays habitable, and which interventions or natural changes would move a world across the habitability boundary.

R by arity, symmetry and scale:

- **Arity:** many conditions, not one. Long-term habitability is a conjunction of stellar, interior, surface and biological conditions. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* This is a **union compound**, not one hard question.
- **Symmetry:** not conserved and not reversible. Habitability is **maintained**, like a garden, and requires continual work from coupled flows. Fig 11 :63: *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."* Loss is easier than maintenance, matching Fig 11 :61's asymmetry (introduction available, exclusion not).
- **Scale:** multi-stratum. Stellar luminosity (gigayears), interior convection and volatile cycling (megayears to gigayears), surface climate and hydrology (seasons to megayears), biological feedback (generations to megayears). The coupling is the object of study, not the individuals.

Binding the sought-for shape as an ordinary term (Formula, "Direction"): an answer would look like **a set of processes, each named, each with a ground-visible anchor, each shown to be load-bearing for the persistence of the habitability conditions, and each placed in a dependency order.** I bind that shape here so it can accrue a residual and be refused (Fig 5 :65-66).

## 3. Findings F1..F10 (verbatim figure lines before conclusions)

### F1 — Habitability is a property of the coupled system, not of the planet alone (decisive)

Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*
Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

Mapping to this question is a **binding**, not an invention: the "agent" slot is the biosphere, the "habitat" slot is star plus interior plus surface, and "capability" is the capacity to persist in a habitable state. The figure decides the shape: you cannot answer the question from a planet's mass and orbit alone, nor from life alone. You need the contact between them.

- **Mode:** `general` for the structural claim; `specified` for the binding to a planet.
- **Triangulation:** Fig 11 (framework-internal) plus NASEM 2023 (planetary science) plus Lenton et al. 2020 (Earth system science). Low overlap between the three fields, so this is a `triangulated` shape claim.
- **Refuter:** show a world whose long-term habitability is fully determined by intrinsic planetary properties with no contribution from stellar evolution or biospheric feedback, measured against the persistence-of-life ground.

### F2 — The process list is enumerated from the world, never composed from the figures

Fig 11 :44: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."*
Fig 11 :46: *"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"*
Fig 11 :47: *"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"*
Fig 11 :48: *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."*

The figures do not contain "carbonate-silicate cycle" or "plate tectonics." Those are read off the world. Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Importing them requires a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 8), and each import is debited against independence.

- **Mode:** `specified` for the imported list; `general` for the enumeration rule.
- **Status:** `single-source` for the enumeration rule (Fig 11), but corroborated by the world sources' own practice (NASEM 2023 enumerates star, interior, atmosphere, water, nutrients, energy, time).
- **Refuter:** produce the process list by composing the six terms without reading the world, and show the resulting list predicts a habitability boundary that the ground pays.

### F3 — Contact cascades; first-order enumeration misses the governing process

Fig 11 :52: *"And contact cascades."* (the figure's worked case is DDT reaching eagles three steps out)
Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*

A process that looks harmful at first order (volcanic CO2) can be load-bearing at second order (it feeds the thermostat that stabilises climate). A process that looks beneficial (a thick atmosphere) can be fatal at second order (runaway greenhouse on Venus). Valence is relative to the residual.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Triangulation:** Fig 11 plus Foley 2015 (whole-planet coupling) plus Cawood 2025 (divergent terrestrial evolution), two independent planetary-science groups.
- **Refuter:** show a habitability process whose effect is identical at first and second order in a measured case, so the cascade rule adds nothing.

### F4 — The thermostat is a cycle and a network, not a list of ingredients

Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*
Fig 13 :24: *"that entities relate, and influence travels"*
Fig 13 :76: *"Energy and a gradient compose a flow. A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."*

The carbonate-silicate thermostat is the figure's cycle: volcanic CO2 (output) is weathered and subducted (re-entry) and returns as outgassing. Its sign is negative on Earth, so it closes; on Venus it fails and the loop runs away. Walker, Hays and Kasting 1981 named the negative feedback; Foley 2015 and Gillmann et al. 2024 show the coupling to tectonics and interior. The network bond (Fig 13 :24) carries the influence between climate, mantle and biosphere that the ingredient list does not.

I initially called the thermostat a **catalyst** (Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."*). **Retracted as primary:** a catalyst is a part that survives its use; the thermostat's load-bearing property is that the *output re-enters as input*, which is the cycle. The catalyst reading is kept only for the biosphere's amplification of weathering, where a part (plant-driven weathering) does survive its own use. Both readings are now recorded.

- **Mode:** `specified` for the thermostat binding; `general` for cycle and network.
- **Status:** `triangulated`: Fig 12 plus Fig 13 plus three independent world sources (Walker 1981, Foley 2015, Gillmann 2024) with distinct methods.
- **Refuter:** show a planet with a stable, long-lived climate that has no material recycling loop, only a one-way flux balance.

### F5 — Energy is directionless alone; a spendable gradient is required, and its source moves

Fig 13 :14: *"conserved, finite, directionless alone"*
Fig 13 :22: *"a difference that can be spent"*
Fig 13 :88-89: *"The residual of the turn just closed is what the next turn composes against, so no two passes begin from the same place."*

Stellar energy supplies the gradient (day/night, equator/pole, surface/space) that life spends. But the star's luminosity rises over main-sequence time, so the boundary moves; the "continuous habitable zone" of the NASA source is the figure's compounding remainder in astronomical clothing. Long-term habitability is therefore tracking a moving anchor, not sitting inside a fixed zone.

- **Mode:** `general` for energy/gradient; `specified` for the stellar-luminosity binding.
- **Status:** `triangulated`: Fig 13 plus NASA Astrobiology 2026 plus NASEM 2023.
- **Refuter:** show a biosphere persisting billions of years with a fixed energy flux and no spendable gradient anywhere in its habitat.

### F6 — The ground for habitability is the persistence of life, not agreement among models

Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*

A habitable-zone model is a frame. A spectral retrieval is a frame. The anchor is whether life actually persists. In this simulated run all the seats share one evidence pool (the same literature), so their agreement is one pool talking, not corroboration. This is recorded rather than hidden.

### F8 — Long-term means a compounding remainder; positive feedback can run away

Fig 13 :31-32: *"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos"*
Fig 13 :74: *"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."*

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per Fig 5 :65-66, a predicted shape is bound as an ordinary term so it can be refused. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A world's long-term habitability is shown, against the persistence-of-life ground, to be fully determined by intrinsic planetary properties with no contribution from contact with a star or a biosphere (breaks F1, Fig 11 :39-40).
2. The governing conditions are shown to form an independent list with no coupling, no feedback and no cascade (breaks F3 and F4, Fig 11 :52, Fig 12 :121).
3. The anchor turns out to be frame agreement or a spectral proxy that updates when frames change, rather than an outcome that does not (breaks F6, Fig 2 :29-30).
4. A planet is shown to require an authoring seat able to subtract habitat actors (breaks F7, Fig 11 :56, :61).
5. A specific planetary process is derived from the six terms alone, with no world reading, and predicts a measured boundary (breaks F9, the canonical shape-not-a-name limit).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.
