# Expert verification packet: steering and self-organization

**What this is:** a claim in a form a governance, complex-systems, or institutional-design researcher can check. Mode, derivation, and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No experiment or institutional record is claimed to have been measured.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Steering and self-organization are two modes of one habitat, not opposites. When no seat maintains anything, selection runs and what survives is what the ground kept. When a seat exists, it works by enumerating contact, introducing actors and instruments, and leaving the ground to decide. Steering preserves self-organization exactly in that maintained form. It destroys it in three ways: moving the anchor, letting a convention decide an outcome, and substituting a model or sandbox for the habitat. Capability belongs to the agent and the habitat together, so improving the center in isolation changes nothing. "Desirable" is not supplied by the framework; it must be bound as an ordinary term, checked per slot, and made refusable. What is offered is the shape any such arrangement must have, with refuters written in advance.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 11 and 2

- **Statement:** a seat can preserve self-organization if and only if it acts by introduction and instruments while the ground remains the anchor; the three forbidden moves (anchor update, outcome-deciding convention, habitat substitution) destroy the correction.
- **Sources, verbatim:**
  - Fig 11 :37 *"Where a seat exists, something is doing work, and it is exactly what the seat does: enumerate the habitat, rank by cascade, introduce actors, build instruments, read the ground."*
  - Fig 11 :22 *"the ground still decides"*, and Fig 11 :61 *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."*
  - Fig 2 :33 *"The anchor must not update."*
  - Fig 10 :47 *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*
  - Fig 11 :70 *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."*
- **Refuter:** exhibit a seat that changes outcomes without enumerating or changing contact; or an anchor that updates with the frames and still settles; or a convention that decides an outcome and remains a convention; or a substituted habitat that proves capability rather than wiring.

### C2 - governance-facing, checkable against institutional design

- **Statement:** polycentric selection between semi-independent rooms is the figure-licensed governance structure; aggregation into one long room is motion without progress, and agreement among frames sharing one evidence pool is not a verdict.
- **Sources, verbatim:**
  - Fig 7 :33 *"→ variation inside, selection between"*, and Fig 7 :32 *"→ one long room: motion, no progress"*
  - Fig 7 :35 *"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*
  - Fig 2 :40 *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*, and Fig 2 :42 *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*
- **What to check:** do the design principles of long-enduring common-pool institutions (minimal recognition of rights to organize, nested enterprises) actually match this structure, or is the match only verbal? Does a single central decision room track the ground as the question changes?
- **Refuter:** agreement among frames drawing on one evidence pool shown to carry verdict weight for a claim about the world; or a single fixed room whose agreement continues to track the ground across changing questions.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation about the steering vocabulary

- **Statement:** a steering language without comparison (⋛), sequence (→), subtraction (−), and negation (¬) cannot state a desirable outcome, a route, progress, or a refusal; "desirable" must be bound as an ordinary term, and residuals must stay per slot.
- **Sources, verbatim:**
  - Fig 12 :112 *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."*
  - Operators table :51 *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*
  - Fig 5 :65-66 *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."*
  - Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
- **Refuter:** exhibit a steering vocabulary containing none of ⋛, →, −, ¬ that can still state a desirable outcome, a route, progress, and a refusal; or a governance score aggregated over all slots that still tells an operator which slot to act on without disaggregation.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that any particular institution, policy, or KPI set is the answer. The framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*).
- Not that self-organization means autopoiesis. Maturana refused the term *"self-organization"* as *"Operationally it is impossible."*
- Not that agreement is worthless. It is *"efficient for a convention"* (Fig 2 :41); only its promotion to a verdict is forbidden.
- Not that a central seat is always illegitimate. The maintained mode has one. What is forbidden is capture, outcome-deciding conventions, and substitution.
- Not that more rules increase reach. Fig 6 :42 *"What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall."*
- Not grounded. Nothing here is asserted as settled by an experiment or an institutional record.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not more agreement:

1. **Contact test:** does a seat change outcomes without enumerating or changing contact?
2. **Anchor test:** does the success metric update in response to the steerer? Fig 2 :25 *"pinned: it does not update on A or B"*.
3. **Convention and substitution tests:** can a convention decide an outcome and remain a convention? Can a synthetic pilot prove capability rather than wiring?
4. **Isomer test:** can the maintained and unmaintained modes be written with the same bond to the seat?
5. **Per-slot test:** does an aggregated score still direct intervention without disaggregation?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :36-37, :61, :63 and Fig 2 :33, :42. Six short passages.
2. Check C2 against their own knowledge of polycentric and monocentric governance: is the match structural or only verbal?
3. Name one instrument that would detect whether a governance anchor is updating in response to the steerer, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
