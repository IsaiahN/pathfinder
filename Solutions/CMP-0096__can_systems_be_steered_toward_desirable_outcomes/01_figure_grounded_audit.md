## 0. The claim under audit, stated as a residual in effect terms

The question asks whether two things can hold at once: (A) a seat can steer a system toward a desirable outcome, and (B) the system keeps its capacity for self-organization. Stated as a residual R: the steering seat wants a state the system would not reliably reach alone, and every available way of steering appears to draw contact, variation, or selection away from the system and into the seat.

- **Option A (assert both can hold unconditionally):** steering is just setting goals and feedback; self-organization is what is left over. This leads to treating the anchor as something the steerer may move, which collapses correction into influence.
- **Option B (the figure-derived reading):** steering is one of two modes of a habitat, the maintained mode, and it preserves self-organization only if the seat acts on contact (enumerate, introduce, build instruments, read the ground) while the ground still decides. This leads to a checkable shape, a named set of forbidden moves, and an open slot until a ground settles each case.

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.** Arity: two conditions, so this is a **union compound**, not one hard question. Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*. Symmetry: steering is asymmetric and self-organization is not a symmetric partner to it, Fig 11 :61: *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* Scale: contact cascades, so a local steer has second and third order effects, Fig 11 :52: *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three"* (continues :53 *"steps out."*).

## 2. Findings

### F1 - Steering and self-organization are not contradictory (decisive for D1)

Fig 11 :36 *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."*

Fig 11 :37 *"Where a seat exists, something is doing work, and it is exactly what the seat does: enumerate the habitat, rank by cascade, introduce actors, build instruments, read the ground."*

Fig 11 :22 *"the ground still decides"*, and Fig 11 :63 *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."*

The figure gives two modes, not a contradiction. In the unmaintained mode nothing selects and what survives is what the ground kept (Fig 11 :56 *"Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept."*). In the maintained mode a seat exists and works on introduction and instruments, and the ground still decides. Self-organization is not removed by the seat: the seat adds to a habitat it did not author (Fig 11 :63, continuing *"maintained the seat adds to a habitat it still did not author"*).

The steering that preserves self-organization is therefore the maintained mode with the ground left in place. The question's apparent paradox comes from reading "steering" as authorship of the outcome, which is one of the forbidden moves in F2.

**Verdict D1: YES, conditionally. `triangulated`** (Fig 11 internal reading, plus the field's guided self-organization literature at low overlap, see `09_watson_answer.md`).

**Refuter:** exhibit a seat that acts on a habitat without enumerating contact, or a case where the ground decides an outcome the seat authored, or a managed habitat that reaches a solved state after which the work stops. Any one fires the refuter and the verdict is withdrawn.

### F2 - Three steering modes destroy self-organization, and all three move or replace the ground

1. **Capture of the anchor.** Fig 2 :33 *"The anchor must not update."* Fig 2 :34 *"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."* A steering system whose success metric changes in response to the steerer's influence has become another frame, and the coherence makes the loss invisible.
2. **A convention that decides the outcome.** Fig 10 :47 *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*; Fig 2 :43 *"Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* The seat may author conventions (Fig 10 :45 *"It does author conventions, because nothing else can see across a seam."*), but the moment a convention settles what the ground should return it becomes a constant carrying the seat's authority (Fig 10 :48).
3. **Isolation or substitution of the habitat.** Fig 11 :66 *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Fig 11 :70 *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."* A pilot, sandbox, or model that steers a substitute habitat proves the wiring, not the capability.

Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* A steering process that negotiates with its own metric rather than ranging against a fixed ground is panel 1.

**Verdict D2: NO for these three modes, YES for the ground-maintainer mode. `triangulated`** (Fig 2 and Fig 10, two figures with different objects).

**Refuter:** produce an anchor that updates with the frames and still settles a question; or a convention that decides an outcome and remains a convention; or a substituted habitat that demonstrates capability rather than wiring. Fig 2 :36 *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it"* sets the legitimacy test that such a demonstration would have to pass.

### F3 - Capability is agent-and-habitat, so improving the steerer in isolation is inert

Fig 11 :39 *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*

Fig 11 :40 *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

For governance this means that better trained central planners, better dashboards, or stronger mandates do not by themselves steer anything. Only a change in contact changes the outcome. Fig 11 :72 names the standing refuter directly: *"can still come back wrong: an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim."*

**Verdict D1 (sub): `general`, structural, `triangulated`** with the field's requisite-variety literature at low overlap (see `09_watson_answer.md`).

**Refuter:** an agent-only improvement that moves the ground. This is the figure's own stated falsifier, not one invented here.

### F4 - The residual is per slot, so a single steering score loses the signal

Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*

Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

Fig 1 :23 *"The ground enters as the only metric."*

A desirable outcome stated as one global index (GDP, a single wellbeing score, one compliance rate) aggregates across slots and throws away the only quantity that says where to intervene. Fig 1 :13 *"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"*: a system can look well steered while one slot is on fire. Governance must keep residuals per slot.

**Verdict D2 (sub): `general`, `single-source` for the figure statement (Fig 1), corroborated qualitatively by the field's emphasis on local, nested governance.**

**Refuter:** show a governance metric aggregated over all slots that still tells an operator which slot to act on, without disaggregation, under a fixed ground.

### F5 - A steering language needs bonds; "desirable outcome" is an unbound preference until written down

Fig 12 :111 *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."*

Fig 12 :112 *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."*

Operators table :51 *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*, and :46 *"progress is a subtraction, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot say how far along it is"*.

Fig 5 :65-66: *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."* `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction" repeats the discipline: a frame cannot check the direction of its own question because the sought-for shape is a preference, not a term.

So "desirable" is not a permission to steer. Until it is bound as an ordinary term with a comparison (⋛), a route (→), a progress measure (−), and a way to refuse (¬), a steering system can enumerate its levers and still not know what it is aiming at. Fig 12 :115: *"What can be said is that having terms for what a situation affords was taken for a dictionary and it is a grammar."*

**Verdict D2 (sub): `specified`, `triangulated`** (Fig 12 plus the Operators table plus Fig 5, three low-overlap figure objects).

**Refuter:** exhibit a steering vocabulary containing none of ⋛, →, −, ¬ that can nevertheless state a desirable outcome, a route to it, progress toward it, and a way to refuse a held belief about it.

### F6 - Polycentric selection beats one long room, and agreement is not a verdict

Fig 7 :35 *"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*

Fig 7 :33 *"→ variation inside, selection between"*, against Fig 7 :32 *"→ one long room: motion, no progress"* and Fig 7 :36 *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."*

Fig 2 :42 *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Fig 2 :40 *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; Fig 2 :41 *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."*

The governance reading: many semi-independent decision rooms selected against the residual each surfaces, with low correlation between them, is the figure-licensed structure. Accumulating all authority into one room is "motion, no progress", and selecting institutions for likely agreement manufactures the disqualifying correlation. Agreement still has a legitimate role, but it is efficient for a convention, not for a verdict.

**Verdict D3: YES for polycentric selection, NO for monocentric accumulation. `triangulated`** (Fig 7 plus Fig 2, plus the field's polycentric-governance literature at low overlap).

**Refuter:** show agreement among frames drawing on one evidence pool carrying verdict weight; or a single fixed room whose agreement continues to track the ground as the question changes. Fig 7 :31 *"no finite number of them ever arrives"* sets the limit that any such demonstration would have to overcome.

### F7 - Policy cuts are eliminations: keep them ranked and reversible

Fig 9 :41 *"so keep every cut ranked and reversible"*, and Fig 9 :42 *"→ narrowing gets you close, the ground decides."*

Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

A steering intervention is a cut in the space of candidate states. The figures require the cut to be reversible and ranked, because a wrong cut removes the answer and speeds up (Fig 9 :40 *"a wrong cut removes the answer and speeds up"*). Instruments and KPIs are filters for where to spend attention; they are not the verdict. A governance system that lets its metric decide the outcome has made a filter a witness.

**Verdict D3 (sub): `general`, `single-source` for the figure's own rule, corroborated by the reversibility language in adaptive governance.**

**Refuter:** a policy cut that cannot be reversed, or an instrument whose reading is taken as the settling verdict without a separate ground.

### F8 - A steering frame cannot expand its own reach by searching harder

Fig 6 :23 *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*

Fig 8 :45 *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."*

Fig 6 amendment :42 *"What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall."*

This cuts both ways for governance. Reaching a genuinely new steering option requires contact with a frame whose closure differs, not more analysis inside the same ministry. But adding a rule (a refusal) grows the record while shrinking the reach, so more governance can mean fewer achievable outcomes. That is a structural result, not a polemical one.

**Verdict D3 (sub): `specified`, `triangulated`** (Fig 6 and Fig 8, which share a source family but govern different objects: wall movement versus surplus location).

**Refuter:** a frame that expands its own reach by search inside a closed set; or a new rule that leaves the reachable set unchanged.

### F9 - Strains, re-census, and what survives

Three strains were hit and re-censused rather than smoothed over.

1. **"Introduce, never subtract" versus the real need to exclude harmful actors.** Fig 11 :61 *"The seat’s power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* Re-census: the figure forbids subtracting the *habitat*, that is, the contact itself (Fig 11 :61 continues *"You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about"* and :62 *"the world and does not consult you."*). A governance move that adds a competitor, predator, regulator, or counter-institution is an introduction and is legal. A move that pretends to delete contact is not. The strain is practical, not a figure conflict: real governance frequently wants exclusion, and the figure says that want is aimed at the wrong object.
2. **"More control means more capability" versus the fall of reach.** Fig 6 :42 says the reach can fall as the record grows. Re-census: Fig 6 governs closure and reach; the everyday claim governs perceived administrative capacity. Different objects, so no contradiction. The figure's warning stands: a corpus of rules is not a growing reach.
3. **"Desirable" as a preference rather than a term.** Re-census against Fig 5 and the Formula "Direction": this is a limit on the loop, not a defect in it. Bind the shape as an ordinary term; then it can accrue residual and be refused.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Required before any outreach: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A seat is exhibited that changes a habitat's outcomes without enumerating contact or changing contact at all (breaks Fig 11 :37, :40).
2. An anchor is exhibited that updates in response to the frames ranging against it and still settles the question (breaks Fig 2 :33, :34).
3. A convention that decides an outcome is exhibited that remains a convention with no truth claim (breaks Fig 10 :47).
4. A substituted habitat or synthetic test is exhibited that demonstrates capability rather than wiring (breaks Fig 11 :70).
5. Agreement among frames sharing one evidence pool is shown to carry verdict weight for a claim about the world (breaks Fig 2 :40).

If any fires, the corresponding verdict is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Lead with F1 and F2: steering is possible, but only as ground-maintenance, and the three forbidden moves are named before any theory is offered.
2. Bind "desirable outcome" as an ordinary term before steering (F5). An unbound preference cannot be checked.
3. Keep residuals per slot (F4); a single global score is the way a live signal disappears.
4. Design for polycentric selection and independence (F6); do not select checkers for agreement.
5. Keep every intervention cut ranked and reversible (F7), and never let a metric become the verdict.
6. Fig 4 :22 *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Send the method and the refuter, not a recording of a policy success.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives, conditionally: steering preserves self-organization exactly when it is ground-maintenance (introduce, enumerate, build instruments, let the ground decide) and fails when it moves the anchor, decides outcomes by convention, or substitutes the habitat. The specific content of "desirable" does not survive as a framework output. It is a bound term, provenanced by whoever binds it, and still ungrounded. Strains after re-census: three named, none hidden.
