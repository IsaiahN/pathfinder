# Outreach draft: check the claim before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are stated cleanly. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about steering and self-organization

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the framework's habitat figure has two modes. In the unmaintained mode nothing selects and what survives is what the ground kept. In the maintained mode a seat exists and does four things: enumerate the habitat, rank by cascade, introduce actors, build instruments, and read the ground. The ground still decides, and the seat's power is asymmetric: introduction is available, exclusion is not. So steering does not destroy self-organization when it is ground-maintenance. It preserves self-organization only when the ground is left in place.
>
> The claim is that steering fails in exactly three ways: when the anchor updates in response to the steerer, when a convention decides the outcome, and when the habitat is substituted by a model, sandbox, or test harness. A fourth failure is treating one global score as the signal, when the residual is indexed per slot rather than averaged.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** I am not claiming a particular desirable outcome follows from the framework. "Desirable" has to be written down as an ordinary term with a comparison, a route, a progress measure, and a way to refuse a held belief, before it can steer anything. I am not claiming polycentric governance is always better in every case, only that selection between semi-independent rooms is the figure-licensed structure and that agreement among frames sharing one evidence pool is not a verdict.
>
> **What would refute the claim:**
> 1. a seat that changes a habitat's outcomes without enumerating or changing contact;
> 2. an anchor that updates in response to the frames and still settles the question;
> 3. a convention that decides an outcome and remains a convention;
> 4. a substituted habitat or synthetic test that proves capability rather than wiring;
> 5. agreement among frames sharing one evidence pool shown to carry verdict weight for a claim about the world.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the corresponding part. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`, cite as `Fig N :line`
> - Figures README, canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md

> - Guided self-organization, overview: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organization
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
