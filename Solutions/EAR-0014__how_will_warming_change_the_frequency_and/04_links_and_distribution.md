## Wider literature that bears on the claim

1. **Frequency and intensity are two objects.** IPCC Chapter 11 explicitly separates the two perspectives and reports that frequency changes are nonlinear and larger for rarer events. This is the single strongest world corroboration of F3 and F4.
2. **Dependence, not just marginals, creates the compound.** Ridder et al. 2020 define the event as a joint threshold exceedance and show that the likelihood multiplication factor exceeds one for positively correlated hazard pairs. This is the statistical form of Fig 9's union compound.
3. **The typology has soft boundaries.** Zscheischler et al. 2020 say some cases inevitably fit more than one class. This matches Fig 13 :115, *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."*
4. **Cascades reach three steps out.** Niggli et al. 2022 trace heat and drought into health, energy, transport, grain markets and public services. This is the world analogue of Fig 11's DDT-to-eagles example.
5. **Observation networks are the decaying channel.** An *npj Natural Hazards* comment on the journal home, "Building on quicksand: the paradox of AI advances and declining in situ networks in hydrometeorology", states that high-quality training data depend on in situ measurements. That is Fig 10 :24 in contemporary language: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*.

The climate sources (IPCC, Zscheischler, Ridder, Niggli, Alcántara-Ayala) share a research pool and count as one witness. The statistics and network-science sources are genuinely lower overlap.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 10 :52**: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Sending the entire framework before a recipient has described what they would expect to see sends the source, not the residual. Lead with the union claim and its refuter.
- **Fig 10 :42**: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A reader who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28**: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would count as a failure of the shape claim before offering the links.
- **Fig 2 :40**: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* A mass send to people selected for receptiveness produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 7 :12-13**: *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"*; Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."* More agreeable readers add vocabulary, not evidence.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its refuter; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) record their verdict; (4) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (5) offer the formula as a method for their own problem, with their own ground and refuter.

## The two-stage offer

- **Stage 1 before stage 2.** Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* The recipient checks the per-slot claim without holding the framework's interior.
- **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Seat 4 must not hold the interiors. The verdict on stage 1 is recorded before any theory is shown, and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
- **Stage 3 sends a method.** Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* The formula is a generator; giving it away does not give away an answer.
- **Stage 3 still needs a ground per problem.** The formula's "Direction" section says a frame *"cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it holds."* Each user writes down what would refute their result before running it.

**Hard precondition.** Stages 1-3 work only if stage 1 survives. The refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 is written before the run and must stay in front of the links in any version that leaves the machine.
