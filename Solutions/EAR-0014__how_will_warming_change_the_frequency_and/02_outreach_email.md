**Subject:** Check one claim about compound extremes before you decide whether the framing is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified` on the per-domain parts, `general` on the structure, not `grounded`):** a compound extreme is a union of overlapping conditions, not one hazard with a bigger number. Because two conditions bind at once, a lever that clears a single marginal hazard leaves the intersection standing. Fig 9 of the figures I am using puts it as *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*
>
> Three consequences follow, and these are the parts I want checked:
>
> 1. **Frequency and intensity are different quantities.** An extreme is declared by crossing a threshold, which the figures define as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*. Once there is a threshold there is a count of crossings and a magnitude beyond it. The figures say *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* A single risk index that mixes the two is not a neutral summary. IPCC AR6 also examines them as two perspectives.
> 2. **The result is per slot, not a global multiplier.** The residual is indexed by place, season, hazard pair and threshold definition. *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A global warming level is the scenario that budgets the search, not the witness for a per-slot verdict.
> 3. **A small shift of the distribution moves the tail more than the mean.** The figures call a distribution an ordering and note that a remainder compounding through an amplifying chain produces chaos. IPCC reports that percentage changes in frequency are higher for rarer events, and that the frequency of hot extremes rises nonlinearly with warming.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the figures produce a percentage, a return-period multiplier or a named region as the answer. They do not, and I am not asking you to accept that they do. The framework returns *"a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* The number is owed to observations and instruments. I am also not claiming that warming raises every compound extreme everywhere: the sign is relative to the residual, and the assessment itself reports regional decreases.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a single scalar in global mean warming that is a sufficient statistic for the frequency and intensity of every compound type, with no per-slot residual left;
> 2. a lossless index that combines crossing count and exceedance magnitude across all slots;
> 3. a distribution shift under warming that leaves joint tail-crossing frequency unchanged;
> 4. a lever that clears one marginal hazard and also clears the compound intersection;
> 5. an impact accounting in which first-order hazard contact explains the whole loss, with no residual three steps out.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will withdraw the corresponding part. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical figures README (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - IPCC AR6 WG1 Chapter 11, Weather and Climate Extreme Events in a Changing Climate: https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/chapter/chapter-11/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
