## Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual line alone, before reading the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The gap between predicted and observed joint-crossing behaviour for a named place, season, hazard pair and threshold. Arity two or more, so a union compound (Fig 9 :57), not a scalar hazard. Symmetry: operands are role-ordered, so a symmetric conjunction loses the mechanism; the measurement may still be written as P(X and Y). Scale: event scale against global multi-decadal scale, a cross-scale question governed by Fig 4.
- **Domain 1, physical climate.** Actors: forcing, gradients, distributions, teleconnections, soil-moisture feedback. Currency: flux and probability. Anchor: instrument plus physical law. Union: thermodynamic and dynamic contributions overlap.
- **Domain 2, hazard statistics and detection.** Actors: thresholds, joint exceedances, dependence, reanalyses. Currency: count and magnitude. Anchor: observed joint exceedances. Union: marginal shift and dependence bind at once.
- **Domain 3, impacts and habitat.** Actors: soil moisture, fire, water, energy, transport, health, governance. Currency: damage and cascade reach. Anchor: impact records. Union of sectors.
- **Domain 4, verification and outreach.** Actors: ensembles, reanalyses, attribution studies, expert readers. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: ground, never agreement. Independence requires differing closures and non-shared evidence.

Order by dependency: perceive, name the compound vocabulary, state the objective (frequency or intensity), plan the lever, carry what changed contact (Fig 3 :20). The break is at the vocabulary/objective link, because a two-hazard vector has no inherited ordering (Extreme value theory: *"there is no natural way to order a set of vectors"*).

My census matches the primary's four domains and its union reading. Where the primary derived from Fig 9 and Fig 1, I derived from Fig 3's dependency chain and Fig 12's two-currency rule. Different starting figures, same boundaries: low overlap between routes within one framework pool, so the agreement is weak but not circular in the sense of one figure quoting another. It is still one evidence pool.

## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, the gap between what was sent up and what came back down. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07` and `09`: compound is a union; residual per slot; frequency and intensity separate; tail moves fastest; cascade reaches three steps out; agreement from one pool is one witness; no number is returned.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete answer for a working scientist: "warming increases the frequency and intensity of compound extremes, with the largest increases in hot-dry events, at roughly the rates reported in the assessment."

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **per-slot indexing**: the concrete sentence aggregates into a global direction, exactly the loss Fig 1 :22 names;
- the **two-currency separation**: "frequency and intensity" collapses into one intensifier, losing Fig 12 :125;
- the **dependence object**: the concrete sentence mentions only marginals, dropping the joint-dependence term that Ridder et al. 2020 measure;
- the **gap itself**: the concrete sentence smuggles in a rate, while Fig 9 :32 and Fig 13 :134 withhold it.

One thing survives cleanly: compound events are a union and need their own loop. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the figures condense into a formula" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
