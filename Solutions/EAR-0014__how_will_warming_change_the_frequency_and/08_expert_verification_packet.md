# Expert verification packet: the Tether on warming and compound extremes

**What this is:** a claim in a form an extreme-events researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain sub-claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the numerical magnitude. Not `grounded`.** No measurement was taken in this run.

**Standing:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A compound extreme is a union of overlapping conditions rather than one hazard with a larger number, so a lever that clears a single marginal hazard leaves the intersection standing. The residual must be indexed per slot (region, season, hazard pair, threshold definition); global mean warming is a scenario filter, not a witness. Frequency and intensity are two currencies read at a threshold and do not add: the count of joint crossings and the magnitude beyond the threshold need separate verdicts. Because warming shifts an ordering, rare joint crossings respond more strongly than the mean. Impacts reach two and three steps out, and the sign of the change is relative to the residual, not universal. The figures return this shape and no number; the number is owed to an independent joint observing system.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: Framework-internal plus statistics-facing; checkable by reading Fig 9, Fig 1 and Fig 12

- **Statement:** the compound is a union, its residual is per slot, and crossing count and exceedance magnitude are two currencies.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."* Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Operators :48 defines the threshold comparison as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*; Fig 12 :87 gives the test *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."*
- **What to check:** is a two-hazard joint exceedance correctly modelled as two binding conditions with a shared fraction, rather than as a single scalar hazard? Do the standard peaks-over-threshold methods in fact fit one distribution for event count and another for exceedance size? (Extreme value theory, Wikipedia: *"For POT data, the analysis may involve fitting two distributions: One for the number of events in a time period considered and a second for the size of the exceedances."*)
- **Refuter:** a single scalar sufficient statistic for all compound types, or a lossless index combining count and magnitude across all slots.

### C2: Climate-facing; checkable against IPCC AR6 and joint-probability studies

- **Statement:** a small shift of the underlying distribution changes joint tail-crossing frequency more than the mean, and dependence between hazards changes the joint probability independently of the marginals.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :31-32: *"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise."* Fig 13 :20 names the distribution *"ordering, so more and less can be said"*; Fig 13 :76: *"A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."*
- **What to check:** IPCC AR6 WG1 Chapter 11 states that *"Projected percentage changes in frequency are higher for the rarer extreme events (high confidence)"* and that *"The frequency of hot temperature extreme events will very likely increase nonlinearly with increasing global warming, with larger percentage increases for rarer events."* Ridder et al. 2020, *Nature Communications* 11:5956, find that a likelihood multiplication factor above one indicates positively correlated hazards and that univariate methods *"widely underestimate"* compound probability. Does the per-slot record support these statements for the compound pair you know best?
- **Refuter:** a demonstrated distribution shift under warming that leaves joint tail-crossing frequency unchanged across the same slots, or a result in which dependence between hazards has no effect on joint probability.

### C3: Habitat and verification; checkable against cascade and attribution studies

- **Statement:** impacts reach three steps out, sign is per residual, and the anchor is an independent observing system rather than ensemble agreement.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :52-53: *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does."*
- **What to check:** Niggli et al. 2022, *PLOS Climate* 1(8):e0000057, trace heat and drought into health, energy, transport and markets and state that *"cascading impacts can propagate through numerous sectors with far reaching consequences."* Alcántara-Ayala 2025, *npj Natural Hazards* 2:54, describe *"Hazards increasingly interact in ways that are non-linear, cross-scalar, and temporally dynamic."* Ridder et al. 2020 explicitly decline to swap reanalyses because doing so would create physical inconsistencies, which reduces independence. Do those studies establish an anchor, or only agreement within one pool?
- **Refuter:** an impact accounting fully explained by first-order hazard contact, or two studies on the same reanalysis counted as independent confirmations.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a percentage, return-period multiplier or named dominant region. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- Not that warming increases every compound extreme everywhere. Fig 11 :49 governs; the IPCC assessment reports two regions with decreases in agricultural and ecological drought at 4 °C.
- Not a unified risk index. Fig 12 :125-130 forbids adding the two currencies.
- Not that a model ensemble is independent evidence. Fig 2 :40.
- Not a new physical mechanism, and not an import that moves the wall (Fig 6 :21, *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom"*). Hazard names are bindings from the climate frame, provenanced as such.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a measurement taken in this run.

## 5. What would settle it

A joint observing system returning, per slot, both the count of joint threshold crossings and the exceedance magnitude, independent of the model frame that predicted them. Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* Marginal networks are the worse instrument already returning something; the improvement is joint, not a sensor specified from nothing.

A figure re-census would also move the verdict if a line were found that returns a numerical multiplier (it would close the declared gap) or if the live type strain on ⋛ were resolved by an instrument.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :55-57, Fig 1 :19, Fig 12 :125 and Fig 11 :49. Four short passages.
2. Check C2 against the compound pair you know best: is the dependence between hazards a separate object from the marginal shifts?
3. Name one observation that would discriminate a frequency change from an intensity change for that pair, or say why none exists.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
