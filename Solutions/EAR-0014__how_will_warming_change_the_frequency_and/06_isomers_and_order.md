# Isomers, functional groups and dependence order in compound extremes

**Mode: `specified` on the structural derivation, `general` on the analogue. Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).**

## RESIDUAL

`01` fixes the shape: a compound extreme is a union of overlapping conditions, indexed per slot, with frequency and intensity as two currencies. Two further questions are owed:

1. **Order.** In what dependency order do the links of the detection chain sit, and where does it actually break?
2. **Structure.** Are two compound pairs (heat with drought, storm surge with rainfall) different things, or two spellings of one thing? The bond vocabulary in Fig 12 decides this, and the answer determines what a refuter must target.

## DOMAIN CENSUS (supporting)

Atoms held in the hazard frame: heat, dry precondition, extreme rainfall, high streamflow, storm surge, high wind, fire weather, plus the atmospheric and surface states that couple them. Bonds held: the seven typed relations and negation (Fig 12 :46, *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands."*). The set of climate atoms is not the six-term physical substrate of Fig 13; applying Fig 13 literally to hazards would be a category error. The analogue used here is **structural only**, and that limitation is recorded in the retraction log of `01`.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: What is the dependency order, and where is the break?

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* Applied to this question:

1. **Perception:** does the observing system resolve a joint exceedance at all? Many networks measure the marginal variables but have no joint product at the same time and place.
2. **Vocabulary:** are there terms for the four compound types (preconditioned, multivariate, temporally compounding, spatially compounding) and for the bond between them? Zscheischler et al. 2020 supply the vocabulary and say the understanding is *"still in its infancy."*
3. **Objective:** what counts as a change? Frequency or intensity, and at which threshold? Extreme value theory says *"there is no natural way to order a set of vectors,"* so the objective cannot be inherited from the univariate case.
4. **Planning:** which lever, given that one lever leaves intersections standing (Fig 9 :55)?
5. **Learn and carry:** name what changed contact and verify it.

Fig 3 :25-26 locates the usual break: *"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."* So the break is between links 2 and 3, the vocabulary/objective link.

Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Higher-resolution global models improve perception, but they cannot rescue a missing objective for a bivariate event. Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

**Falsifier Q1:** an instrument-backed, unique ordering of multivariate compound severity that works across hazard pairs with no goal declaration. If one exists, the break moves and this derivation is wrong.

### Q2: Are compound pairs isomers, or members of a functional group?

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

- **Weak reading (as usually reported).** Heat-drought uses `{heat, dry precondition}`; surge-rainfall uses `{surge, extreme rainfall}`. They share few atoms. Under Fig 12 they are not isomers; they are two members of a **functional group** whose substructure is *two hazards jointly exceeding thresholds*. The group says what kind (a multivariate compound); the qualifier says which member (which pair).
- **Strong reading (the testable claim).** Write both pairs over the shared atom set `{heat, dryness, rainfall, streamflow, surge, wind, fire weather, atmospheric circulation}`. They then share all atoms and differ only in which atoms are bonded and by which bond. Under Fig 12 they are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance. That is why a lever that helps one can harm the other, and why "compound extremes" is not one risk category.

Fig 12 :92: *"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."* Joint exceedance of two hazards is a symmetric conjunction at the measurement level (P(X > x and Y > y) does not depend on the order of writing). But the *mechanism* is often ordered: a dry precondition before heat, a heatwave before fire, a cyclone before storm surge. Under Operators :34, *"the only bond a flat chain can express, so every term today is a sequence whether or not it should be"*: a flat bivariate probability records the conjunction and loses the order, which is exactly the information the impact cascade needs. Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."*

**Falsifier Q2:** if both pairs can be written without an atom the other requires, the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim stands. If the order of operands turns out to make no behavioural difference for impacts, the sequence reading fails and the conjunction reading stands.

### Q3: What role do catalysts and cycles play in the compound?

Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."* Soil moisture is the closest analogue: it is altered by heat and drought and in turn amplifies heat, surviving across the sequence and changing the next crossing. Treating it as a fixed precondition instead of a spent-and-regenerating part is a notation error with a real cost.

Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input."* Dry soil increases sensible heat flux, which increases evaporative demand, which further dries the soil. Fig 13 :31-32 describes the compounding remainder: *"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise."* A chain written as a sequence cannot close by construction; the cycle needs a different bond, and that is why feedbacks are a separate object from a list of hazards.

**Falsifier Q3:** a compound event in which every coupling part is spent on first use and no output re-enters as input. Then the catalyst and cycle readings are unnecessary and should be dropped.

### Q4: Why the union must be unbundled before it is searched

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* A bundled problem of heat, drought, fire and surge is a union of overlapping conditions with an unassignable shared fraction. Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*

The unbundling is not cosmetic. Once split, each compound has its own observing requirement and its own refuter. A single lever (for example lower mean temperature) clears regions where only heat binds and leaves the surge-rainfall intersection standing, because sea level rise is a different atom.

**Falsifier Q4:** a single intervention that reduces the frequency and intensity of every compound pair by the same factor across all slots. Then the union reading collapses to a partition.

### Q5: The live type strain on ⋛

Fig 12 :104 permits a settled molecule to become an atom: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."* Operators :48 defines ⋛ as *"a threshold relation between two quantities."* A compound extreme is a joint exceedance of two hazards, which at the decision level may be settled into one quantity (a joint return period). Whether that demotion is legal at the level of the decision is a type question, not a claim the figures settle. It is kept live here, in `01` and in `08_expert_verification_packet.md`, rather than smoothed over.

**Falsifier Q5:** an instrument that shows the joint pair cannot be treated as one quantity at the decision level. The comparison term must then be refused rather than redefined.

## CONCLUSION

The detection chain breaks at the vocabulary/objective link, not at model resolution. As usually reported, compound pairs are members of a functional group sharing the joint-exceedance substructure; over a fixed atom set they are a real isomer claim with a stated falsifier. Sequence is the bond that flat bivariate probability most often loses, and catalysts and cycles are the parts that make a cascade more than a list. The union must be unbundled before search. One type strain on the comparison operator remains live and named.
