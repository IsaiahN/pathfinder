# Are disclosure change and behaviour change isomers? Structure, order, and competing explanations

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question yields two effects of one act: a change in description (`Δo_desc`) and a change in real outcomes (`Δo_real`). Two supporting questions are owed: (1) what is the structural relation between them under Fig 12, and (2) in what dependency order must a study test them so that a failure at one link is not mistaken for a failure at another?

## DOMAIN CENSUS (brief, full version in `01` §1)

Atoms available for the analysis: a disclosure act, a firm library Γ, a verified outcome, a rating, an audience, an assurer. Bonds available: the seven plus negation. No new primitive is introduced. Anchor: verified physical and social outcomes (Fig 2 :29). Question type: union of two conditions, not a partition (Fig 9 :54-57).

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the two channels isomers? NO. CORRECTION TO AN EARLY READING

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- The two channels do not share an atom set. `Δo_real` is composed from emissions, injuries, incidents, and the physical operations that produce them. `Δo_desc` is composed from report text, metrics, and ratings. A rating is not an emission under another name.
- ⇒ Calling them isomers **strains** the figure's definition. **Retracted.** Isomerism would require writing both effects over a full shared atom set, differing only in arrangement. That has not been done and cannot be done here without an import that supplies the missing atom (Fig 6 :23).
- What the relation actually is, in the framework's own terms, is **projection and source**, the metamerism case in *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source."* A disclosure score is a reading whose source may be abatement, a boundary change, a vendor's weighting, or verbosity. A better instrument, not a better argument, is owed.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if both channels can be written over one shared atom set and differ only in the typed bond between the same atoms, the isomer claim would hold and the metamerism reading would be unnecessary. No such writing is available here. Triangulated (Fig 12 :117 plus the source essay) against the figure definition; the shared-atom premise is unmet.

### Q2 - What is the bond that does connect the disclosure act to an outcome?

Fig 12 defines seven bonds and one unary operator. Testing them:

| bond | Fig 12 test | fit here |
|---|---|---|
| `+` conjunction, symmetric | *"swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No."* | A report and an emission can co-occur, but co-occurrence is not the claim; too weak |
| `∥` disjunction, symmetric | *"remove one. Does it still work? Yes."* | Would say either channel alone suffices, which is precisely the union error |
| `≡` identity, symmetric | *"substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No."* | **Explicitly refused.** Substituting a rating for an emission loses the ground. This is the metamerism error |
| `→` sequence, ordered | *"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."* | Fits: disclosure precedes the measured change; reversing them makes the claim different |
| `⇒` production, ordered | *"is there a B before A fires? No."* | Tempting for "disclosure produces behaviour", but it commits to the empirical claim under test. Not licensed |
| `−` subtraction, ordered | *"is the second operand's absence the point? Yes."* | Fits a different question: what changed relative to a no-disclosure counterfactual. This is the causal estimand |
| `⋛` comparison, ordered | *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."* | Fits "mainly": a threshold relation between Δo_real and Δo_desc, in different units, which needs a declared common scale |
| `¬` negation, unary | *"is the point that it is false, rather than absent? Yes."* | Fits the refuter: a held term (rating as proxy for behaviour) is false, and what is owed is a refusal |

**Result.** The defensible bonds are `→` for temporal order, `−` for the counterfactual, `⋛` for "mainly" after a scale is declared, and `¬` for the refusal of the proxy. `≡` and `⇒` are refused. This is a `specified` structural result, not an empirical finding.

**Falsifier:** if a design can show that `Δo_desc` and `Δo_real` are related by identity at ground precision, the refusal of `≡` is false. If a controlled study shows a behaviour change that occurs before any disclosure, the `→` ordering is false for that case.

### Q3 - What order must the study run in?

Dependency order from Fig 3 and Formula step 2 (refuse before mint):

1. **Perceive.** Can the firm and the researcher read real outcomes at all? If emissions are estimated by self-reported factors, the perception link is broken. Fig 3 :5: *"the system can tell what is in front of it, and tell it apart from what was there before."*
2. **Vocabulary.** Do the terms exist for what the disclosure affords? "Material", "assurance", "scope 3" are mints from the reporting frame; they are frame-internal until checked (Fig 1 :27).
3. **Objective.** Can the study say what would count as progress **in physical units**? This is the middle link with no instrument (Fig 3 :24-27). Fig 12 :112: *"A goal is a comparison"*, so "mainly" needs a declared scale before it can be tested.
4. **Plan.** Choose the counterfactual and the blinded description measure. `−` and `→` are fixed here.
5. **Learn and carry.** Register the refuter, run the study, and carry the method up, not the recording (Fig 4 :22-25).

> **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** (Fig 3 :19)
> **"Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."** (Fig 3 :29)

Most disclosure research effort lands at step 3 and above (more standards, more metrics, bigger samples), where measurements already exist. The middle link, whether the objective is stated in physical units or in score units, is the one nobody has looked at (Fig 3 :27).

**Falsifier:** a documented study whose perception, vocabulary, and objective links all measure as working and whose failure is located at planning. That would falsify the "usually at the middle" claim, though not the dependency ordering.

### Q4 - Competing explanations, and which figure governs each

| explanation | figure that governs | what would discriminate it |
|---|---|---|
| Disclosure changes attention, which changes action, which changes contact | Fig 11 :39-40; Fig 6 :23 | measured contact change with a counterfactual and unchanged rating measurement procedure |
| Disclosure changes only the report and the rating | Fig 1 :27; Fig 10 :26 | description change with verified outcomes flat and an unpooled per-firm reading |
| Disclosure changes the measurement boundary, so the reported number moves while physical reality does not | Fig 10 :24; MIT assurance result | independent verification that restates the number; self-selection recorded |
| Disclosure changes capital flows, which later change operations | Fig 2 :29 (market as anchor, conditionally) | liquidity or flow change preceding a verified outcome change, with the flow measured, not inferred |
| Observers agree more after disclosure, so the claim looks settled | Fig 7 :12-13, :35; Fig 2 :40 | an independent ground test run by someone outside the agreeing room |

These are **not** five answers to one question. Under Fig 9 :54-57 they are a union of overlapping conditions. A single lever clears the region where one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. Verdicts are kept separate and never averaged.

**Falsifier for the union claim:** one intervention that produces all five effects with no categorically different residue, so that one outcome measure suffices. That would collapse the union into a partition.

## CONCLUSION

The two channels are **not isomers**; that reading strained Fig 12 :117 and is retracted. Their structural relation is projection to source (metamerism), with `→`, `−`, `⋛` and `¬` as the licensed bonds and `≡` plus `⇒` refused. The dependency order puts the objective link (what counts as progress, in which units) at the break, matching Fig 3's middle-link warning. Five competing explanations are a union and must be tested with separate instruments.

This rescues what is defensible without the part that failed: no new primitive, no claim that the causal chain runs, and no identity between a score and the behaviour it names. "Real abatement after disclosure" remains a ground question; "rating changed while outcomes did not" is a checkable shape.
