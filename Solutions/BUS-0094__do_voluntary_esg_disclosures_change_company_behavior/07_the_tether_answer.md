# The Tether's answer on voluntary ESG disclosure and company behaviour

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural claim. NOT `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route to a test, not an empirical result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading. The framework cannot choose between the two options in the question without a measurement, and it says exactly why.

## 1. The residual it answers

A voluntary ESG disclosure is an act that changes what a firm and its observers can describe. The question asks whether the effect of that act lands on behaviour in the world or mainly on description. The contact failure is not one puzzle with two opinions; it is a **union compound** (Fig 9 :54-57): a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It has its own loop and its own ground.

## 2. The shape

1. **A disclosure adds a term, not an atom to the world.** Fig 6 :25: *"An import adds no atom to the world."* So a change in description is the structural default of the act considered alone. A behavioural effect is a further causal chain through changed action and changed contact. The framework does not say that chain never runs. It says it is not contained in the act and must be supplied.
2. **The two effects are separate channels and must be measured in separate units.** Verified physical and social outcomes anchor one; report text, scores and ratings form the other. Fig 1 :23, :27: *"The ground enters as the only metric"* and *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Using a rating as the dependent variable for behaviour answers the description half by construction.
3. **A score is a projection whose source is not determined by the reading.** The green/metamerism case from *The Hard Problem is About Source*: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* A high ESG score can come from abatement, a boundary change, a vendor's weighting, or verbosity. What is owed is a discriminator, not a name.
4. **Ratings are instruments, not anchors.** Fig 2 :30: *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* The MIT Sloan Aggregate Confusion Project measured average correlation among prominent ESG raters of 0.54, against 0.92 for credit ratings. Under Fig 2 :40, agreement among raters drawing on the same reports is the pool talking, not two witnesses.
5. **The channel, not the ground, is what decays.** Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*, with *"saturated metrics, pooled readings"* named at :26. Assurance can repair the channel; the MIT Sloan assurance study reports that verified emissions were initially *"at least 13.5% higher than previously anticipated"* for firms that sought assurance. That is a channel correction, not a physical change caused by the report.
6. **The same disclosure changes valence when the residual changes.** Fig 11 :49: *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* A disclosure can be a regenerating catalyst under scrutiny or a spent part that decays into boilerplate. Christensen, Hail and Leuz report that about half of SEC registrants give *"generic or boilerplate"* sustainability information; Yu, Luu, Chen and Silvia report extensive disclosure paired with weak performance.
7. **Both branches occur in the world, from one evidence pool.** Bochkay, Choi and Hales find *"fewer negative ESG incidents, lower pollution levels, fewer work-related injuries, and higher ESG ratings"* after voluntary SASB adoption, and conclude *"what gets voluntarily disclosed gets managed."* Krueger et al find a liquidity effect. These do not conflict with the greenwashing findings under the figures; they are different conditions of a union (Fig 9 :57). They also do not constitute independent corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
8. **The framework stops before naming a winner.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* The shape it returns is: two channels, an anchor on verified outcomes, a union that must be unbundled, and an instrument that discriminates them per firm with a counterfactual.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that voluntary disclosure never changes behaviour. That is an empirical claim about whether the causal chain runs, and no figure line settles it.
- Not that voluntary disclosure always changes behaviour. The "what gets disclosed gets managed" finding is a world result from one pool, not a figure verdict.
- Not that a particular standard, metric, or reporting boundary is the one that works. That would be a name where the framework returns a shape.
- Not that ESG ratings are useless. They are instruments for the question "is this a good report", not anchors for "did the firm change".
- Not that greenwashing is the default moral condition of firms. It is a world-pool name for a source-discrimination failure, governed by Fig 11 :49 and Fig 12 :119.
- Not grounded. No measurement in this package pays for the shape.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

An instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*, the spectrometer resolved the green case where argument could not. Here the corresponding questions are:

- **Perception test:** are the real outcomes independently verified, or are they self-reported factors that move when the firm pushes on them? (Fig 3 :5, Fig 2 :30)
- **Objective test:** is progress stated in physical units before ratings are read? Fig 12 :112: *"A goal is a comparison"*, so "mainly" needs a declared scale (Fig 3 :24-27).
- **Two-channel difference-in-differences:** prospectively registered, per firm, with (a) verified emissions, injuries or incidents and (b) a blinded text or rating measure, both measured before and after a voluntary adoption event, with a counterfactual and no pooled average.
- **Boundary test:** an independent verification step that detects whether a reported change is a physical change or a measurement-boundary revision (MIT assurance result; Fig 10 :24).
- **Independence test:** the ground reading is taken by someone outside the room of observers who already agree (Fig 7 :35, Fig 10 :42).

**Standing falsifiers** from `01` section 4: either channel moving alone with adequate power; one instrument collapsing the union; a rating tracking verified outcomes at credit-rating-level correlation; valence invariance across a residual change; or a figure-level failure. Until one is run, the verdict is `candidate`.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The question is constitutively about the ground, so the figures can specify the check but cannot run it. The instrument that would name the source of a score is not held in this package. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and *"Indiscernibility is not identity."* A report that is indiscernible from good behaviour to a rating remains a report. The lineage of this stop is recorded in `01` §6 and `11_audit.md` so a later reader can locate the error rather than only feel it.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not pick a side in the argument about voluntary ESG disclosure. It says that a disclosure adds a term to the firm's and its observers' libraries, not an atom to the world, so a change in description is the structural default of the act alone. A change in behaviour is a separate causal chain that has to change the firm's contact with a paying ground, and it must be measured at verified outcomes, not at ratings, which correlate with one another at about 0.54 against 0.92 for credit ratings. The two effects form a union, not a partition: a firm that changes only its score and a firm that changes only its operations are different cases needing different instruments. The world literature supplies both, from one evidence pool, which is why neither is a verdict. What the framework offers is the shape any check must have, the refuters written in advance, and the instrument requirement that would close it. The question stays open until the ground settles it.
