## 0. The residual in effect terms

The loop defines R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| per slot s (Formula step 1; Symbols table, `R`). Here:

- **b** is the firm's belief about what investors, regulators, and stakeholders will reward.
- **a** is what the firm does: publish a report, adopt a standard, abate, retrain, change a supplier.
- **Γ(b,a)** is what the firm's library of routines and its public self-model predict will follow.
- **o′** arrives in two incommensurable units. `o_real` is measured physical and social outcome: tonnes of CO2e, injury rates, incident counts, labour practice audits. `o_desc` is the report text, the score, the rating, and the firm's public account of itself.
- **s** is the firm, indexed by time t. R is indexed by firm and by time, never averaged into a sector ESG average (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22).

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** (Fig 1 :19)
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** (Fig 1 :22)

The phenomenon to explain is the *differential response of the two channels to a voluntary disclosure event*. Call it `Δ = (Δo_real, Δo_desc)`. Observed in the world: both components can move, and they can move separately. A frame that predicts "disclosure always changes behaviour" or "disclosure never changes behaviour" has a residual here, because it has averaged over a union (section 1.3).

State the residual as: *after a firm voluntarily publishes an ESG disclosure, why does the gap between what its routines predict and what its operations return sometimes shrink in physical units, and sometimes only shrink in the units of its own description?* This is an effect description. It names no theory, no "stakeholder pressure", no "legitimacy", no "greenwashing". Those are candidate names and are withheld until the census.

**Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term** (Formula, "Direction"), before the run: the answer would be a pair of conditions whose conjunction separates contact change from description change, plus an instrument that would discriminate a failure of one from a failure of the other. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no loop check can see it. It is now bound, so the refuter in section 4 can fire on it.

**Anchor test.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* The question is constitutively about measured outcomes, because the word "behaviour" in the question refers to what the firm does in the world. Fig 2 :30: *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* ESG ratings, report scores, and analyst sentiment move when pushed by disclosure. Independently verified emissions, injuries, and incident counts are the candidate anchor. Note the asymmetry: a rating is an instrument for "is this a good report", not an anchor for "did the firm change" (Fig 2 :37-38, human judgment and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure).

**Immediate structural result.** A voluntary ESG disclosure is a change in the firm's library Γ, that is, a new term describing what the firm says it does. Fig 6 :25: *"An import adds no atom to the world."* So the disclosure event cannot itself be a physical change. Any behavioural effect must be mediated by a change in action that then changes contact with the ground. That is a `general` structural claim, and it is the first finding (F2). It does **not** settle whether the mediation happens; that is empirical.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### 1.1 Arity, symmetry, scale of R

- **Arity.** The disclosure event is a three-place relation: firm Γ, disclosure act a_D, audience reading. The consequents are two-place: (firm, real outcome) and (firm, description). The question therefore carries an **ordered** bond (`→`, sequence: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*, Fig 12 :72) because disclosure precedes any measured change, and a **comparison** bond (`⋛`, *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, Fig 12 :87) because "mainly" is a relative magnitude. It is not symmetric: the firm and the ground are not interchangeable, and tonnes of CO2 and rating points are not.
- **Symmetry.** "Mainly changes behaviour" is a threshold claim about which channel carries more change, not an absolute quantity. Persistence of a description without contact is asymmetric in time: a corrected report does not undo an emission already emitted.
- **Scale.** Three scales are in view: the single firm's step loop (Fig 4 one-agent panel), the population of firms with a shared library and *"something that ranks what they produce"* (Fig 4 :5), and the time channel across reporting years. The membrane between them is *"Not a wall: a filter with a direction"* (Fig 4 :21), and *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* (Fig 4 :22). The transform is lossy, so the round-trip gap R_T must be measured (Fig 4 :30).

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

A domain is admitted only if an anchor can be named. Where none can be named, it is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor, nameable? | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 The disclosure act** (frame-internal) | managers, standard setters (SASB, GRI, ISSB), report text | description: predicates minted, credibility accrued | yes for the *act*: a dated, public, registered filing. Not for its truth, which is Fig 1 :27's forbidden self-score | admitted |
| **D2 The physical and social ground** | emissions, injuries, incidents, workers, communities | physical and social outcomes | yes: verified measurement, *"A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome"* (Fig 2 :29) | admitted |
| **D3 The reading frame** (investors, analysts, raters) | ESG agencies, investors, analysts, the business press | ratings, rankings, capital flows | yes for capital actually committed (market, Fig 2 :29); no for rating agreement, which is a frame-internal quantity | admitted, ratings demoted to instrument |
| **D4 The assurance channel** | independent assurers, audit committees, regulators, verifiers | verification, correction of measurement | yes: independently verified restatement. The channel can be corrected, the ground does not decay (Fig 10 :24) | admitted |
| **X1 Sincerity of managerial intent** | managers | intentions | **no** anchor independent of the report itself and the manager's own account | not a domain yet |
| **X2 ESG investor preference as sole cause** | investors | preferences | measurable in flows, but this is already the habitat of D3, and Fig 11 :46 names conditions as habitat elements | absorbed into D3 |

X1 fails the anchor test and is excluded before any figure line is applied to it. This is a structural exclusion, not a claim that intentions never matter. It is the same exclusion the field makes when it refuses to score greenwashing by asking managers what they meant.

### 1.3 Partition or union

D1 and D2 overlap: the disclosure act is performed inside the firm and changes what the firm attends to and measures, so the overlap is a compound with its own loop. D2 and D3 overlap: ratings read outcomes, and capital flows back into operations. D3 and D4 overlap: assurance alters what the rating sees. The whole question is a **union**, not a partition.

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** (Fig 9 :54)
> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** (Fig 9 :55)
> **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."** (Fig 9 :57)

The unbundled conditions are:

- **C1, contact change:** did the firm's actions change in contact with a ground that still pays? Instrument: independently verified physical or social outcomes, per firm, with a counterfactual.
- **C2, description change:** did the firm's self-description change? Instrument: blinded text coding of reports and rating histories, kept in separate units from C1.

A firm failing only C1 and a firm failing only C2 are not different degrees of one failure. They are different compounds and need different instruments. A single lever, for example "require more disclosure", clears the region where only C2 holds and leaves the intersection standing (Fig 9 :55). This is the reason the verdicts in `09_watson_answer.md` are kept separate per domain and never averaged.

### 1.4 Dependency order (Fig 3)

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."** (Fig 3 :20)
> **"Why the middle link is where chains usually break."** (Fig 3 :24)
> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** (Fig 3 :27)

Order for this question: 1 perception (can the firm read its own emissions at all?) → 2 vocabulary (does Γ have terms for what disclosure affords: does it know which metric will be scrutinised?) → 3 objective (can the firm say what progress would count as: a better score, or lower tonnes?) → 4 planning → 5 learn and carry.

The middle link is the seam between C1 and C2. Fig 12 :113-115 says a vocabulary holding only ingredients can say what a situation contains and cannot say what would count as getting somewhere from it, and that *"having terms for what a situation affords was taken for a dictionary and it is a grammar."* Most ESG effort and most ESG research effort land above the break, on more metrics, more standards, more scores, because that is where the measurements already exist (Fig 3 :29-30). The objective link, what would count as progress in physical units, is the one with no instrument in many disclosure settings.

## 3. Findings

Mode tag on each: `general` if structural, `specified` if a falsifiable prediction about firms or a research design, `grounded` only if settled by a measurement. None is `grounded`.

### F1: A disclosure is frame-internal and cannot score itself

**Mode: `general`. Domains D1, D2, D3. Status: triangulated (Fig 1 and Fig 2, low overlap: slot-indexed residual vs anchor rule).**

> **"The ground enters as the only metric."** (Fig 1 :23)
> **"These do not count:"** (Fig 1 :24)
> **"predicates minted, and credibility accrued"** (Fig 1 :26)
> **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."** (Fig 1 :27)

A sustainability report is a quantity the firm produces, along with its credibility. Using report length, disclosure volume, rating improvement, or "number of ESG predicates minted" as the dependent variable for "did behaviour change" is exactly the frame-internal scoring the figure disqualifies. The same line forbids the mirror error: a fall in a firm's own disclosure score after an incident is not a ground reading either.

**Refuter:** a measure of behavioural change that is produced entirely inside the reporting frame and is shown, by an independent instrument, to track verified physical outcomes at ground precision without being adjusted after the fact. If such a measure exists, F1's disqualification is false for it. Note that the MIT assurance study discussed under F10 is the nearest world case, and it reports the opposite: assurance initially *raised* reported emissions by 13.5 percent, that is, the published reading was corrected by an external instrument.

### F2: A disclosure adds a term to the library, not an atom to the world

**Mode: `general`. Domain D1 to D2. Status: single-source (Fig 6, with Fig 5 :48 as a corroborating line from the same framework, so one witness).**

> **"An import adds no atom to the world."** (Fig 6 :25)
> **"The world does not gain primitives; a frame gains access."** (Fig 6 :26)
> **"Its own output is never a measure of success. A system can be enthusiastically curious and produce nothing."** (Fig 5 :48)

This is the structural reason the two options in the question are not the same option. A published report changes what the firm and its observers can say. It cannot, by itself, change a tonne of CO2 or an injury. Any behavioural effect must pass through a change in action that then changes contact. So Option B, "mainly changes description", is the **default structural reading of the disclosure act alone**, while Option A is a claim about a causal chain that must be supplied and measured, not assumed. The figures do not say the chain never runs. They say it is not contained in the act.

**Refuter:** a controlled case in which a disclosure changes independently verified physical outcomes while the firm's actions and contacts are demonstrated to be unchanged, with the change explained neither by measurement-boundary revision nor by someone acting on the report. Under Fig 6 :25 that should be impossible; if observed, F2 is false.

### F3: Real behavioural change requires changed contact

**Mode: `specified`. Domains D2, D1. Status: triangulated (Fig 11 and Fig 4, low overlap: habitat vs membrane).**

> **"And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."** (Fig 11 :39)
> **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."** (Fig 11 :40)
> **"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."** (Fig 4 :20)

A firm that improves its disclosure without changing what it does has improved the agent's description, not the firm-and-habitat's contact. The world finding usually quoted for the opposite view, Bochkay, Choi and Hales's *"what gets voluntarily disclosed gets managed"*, is consistent with F3 only if "managed" names an action that changed contact. Their own outcome list makes this checkable: negative ESG incidents, pollution levels, work-related injuries, and ESG ratings. Three are physical or social outcomes; the fourth is a frame output. They are reported together, but F3 requires them to be read apart.

**Refuter:** a documented firm whose verified emissions or injury rate falls through a change that is demonstrably not a change in contact with emissions or injuries, for example a pure emissions-factor reclassification that survives independent verification as a real physical reduction. If no such case can be produced, F3 stands as `specified`.

### F4: The channel decays, and metric saturation is the named failure

**Mode: `specified`. Domain D4. Status: triangulated (Fig 10 and the Cambridge Goodhart page, low overlap: framework seat vs measurement/social science).**

> **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"** (Fig 10 :24)
> **"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"** (Fig 10 :26)
> **"you have lost touch"** (Fig 10 :28)
> **"→ keep the anchor reachable"** (Fig 10 :29)

A firm can report ever more fluently while its channel to what pays degrades. The named decay modes translate directly: a saturated metric is an ESG score that has stopped discriminating among firms; a pooled reading is an industry average that hides the live slot; a stale percept is a baselineyear that no longer matches current operations. Goodhart's law, in the Cambridge page's wording, *"When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure,"* and *"Measuring a system usually disturbs it,"* corroborates the saturation branch from outside the framework's evidence pool. The essay *Arrival of the Fittest* itself lists Goodhart, Campbell and Strathern as an "instance" for the framework's proxy warning, which is recorded as high overlap on that one point.

**Refuter:** a documented setting in which disclosure fails to change physical outcomes even though the metric is unsaturated, the reading is unpooled, and the percepts are current. That would move the failure from channel decay to something else.

### F5: Measured outcomes anchor; rating agreement does not

**Mode: `general` on the rule, `specified` on the rating prediction. Domains D2, D3. Status: triangulated (Fig 2 and the Berg/Kölbel/Rigobon result as reported by MIT Sloan, low overlap: figure law vs finance measurement).**

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."** (Fig 2 :29)
> **"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."** (Fig 2 :30)
> **"Correlation has valence."** (Fig 2 :39)
> **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"** (Fig 2 :40)
> **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."** (Fig 2 :42)

MIT Sloan's Aggregate Confusion Project reports: *"We found the correlation among prominent agencies' ESG ratings was on average 0.54; by comparison, credit ratings from Moody's and Standard & Poor's are correlated at 0.92."* The ratings are frames that read the same corporate reports with different scope, measurement and weights. Their disagreement is not a measurement of corporate behaviour, and their agreement would not be evidence even if it were high, because they share an evidence pool. Fig 7 :13, *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*, applies to an ESG consensus exactly as it applies to a chain of rooms.

**Refuter:** a rating whose correlation with independently verified physical outcomes reaches the level credit ratings reach with default, and which does not move when the rated firm pushes on it. That would promote the rating from instrument to anchor and break F5.

### F6: The same disclosure changes valence when the residual changes

**Mode: `specified`. Domains D2, D3. Status: triangulated (Fig 11 and Fig 12, low overlap: habitat relations vs composition structures).**

> **"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** (Fig 11 :49)
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."** (Fig 11 :50)
> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."** (Fig 11 :51)
> **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."** (Fig 12 :119)

A disclosure can be a catalyst, a part that survives its own use and gets regenerated through scrutiny, or a spent part that gives one good report and then decays into boilerplate. Nothing in the report's description records which. Yu, Luu and Chen (2020) identify *"‘greenwashers’ as firms which seem very transparent and reveal large quantities of ESG data but perform poorly in ESG aspects"*; Silvia (2026) reports that *"firms with weaker environmental performance often produce more extensive sustainability reports"*. Both are the valence claim observed in the disclosure pool. Christensen, Hail and Leuz report that roughly *"50% of SEC-registered companies provide generic or boilerplate sustainability information in their regulatory filings"*, the spent-part case at population scale.

**Refuter:** a disclosure practice whose measured valence is invariant across a documented change of the firm's residual, including a change of what the ground pays for. If no such invariance can be found, F6 weakens to `general`.

### F7: The question is a union of two conditions, and the lever must be chosen after unbundling

**Mode: `general` with a `specified` methodological consequence. Domains all. Status: triangulated (Fig 9 and the Library of Babel essay, but the essay is framework-internal, so the external witness count is one).**

Fig 9 :54, :55 and :57 are quoted in section 1.3. The two conditions have different instruments:

- A **C1 failure** shows up as saturated metrics, pooled readings, a bracket that no longer matches the residual (Fig 10 :26, Fig 7 :38), or either factor of Fig 7 :37-39 going to zero.
- A **C2 failure** shows up as a closure that keeps handing itself the same room, no import, and a reach that stops moving while the library keeps growing (Fig 6 :21, Fig 7 :12).

A single lever, for example "mandate more disclosure", clears the region where only C2 holds and leaves the intersection standing. The Library of Babel essay names the same stop: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"*

**Refuter:** a single intervention that restores both conditions in both failure types with no categorically different residue left over. That would collapse the union into a partition.

### F8: Both branches occur in the world, which is why the question is empirical

**Mode: `specified`. Domains D1 to D4. Status: world-sourced, figure-governed.**

The figures say the channels are different quantities (F1, F2, F7). They do not say which one dominates in a given sample. The world literature supplies both:

- **Behaviour branch:** Bochkay, Choi and Hales find adopters of voluntary SASB standards have *"fewer negative ESG incidents, lower pollution levels, fewer work-related injuries, and higher ESG ratings in years following the standards adoption"*, and conclude *"what gets voluntarily disclosed gets managed."* The last of the four outcomes is a frame output, so the result is a mixed reading until separated.
- **Description branch:** Yu, Luu and Chen find extensive disclosure paired with poor ESG performance; Silvia (2026) finds greenwashing associated with more extensive disclosure and weaker reporting quality; Christensen, Hail and Leuz say *"evidence on the causal effects of regulation is still scarce"* and call parts of their analysis *"speculative"*.
- **Market branch:** Krueger, Sautner, Tang and Zhong find a *"positive effect of ESG disclosure mandates on firm-level stock liquidity"*, strongest for government requirements with enforcement. A liquidity effect is a capital-market consequence, not a physical outcome. It can coexist with either branch.

Agreement among these papers is not two or three witnesses. They share the sustainability-disclosure evidence pool, much of it drawn from the same corporate reports and the same rating vendors (Fig 2 :31, :40). They are filters for where to look and one pool of corroboration, not a verdict. Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

**Refuter:** a study that shows description and behaviour moving as one latent variable across settings, with no residual channel. That would collapse the union and make the question empirically answerable with a single outcome measure.

### F9: Which disclosure changes what, in which firm, is a gap

**Mode: `general`. Domains all. Status: figure-derived honest stop.**

> **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"** (Fig 9 :32)
> **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** (Fig 8 :39)
> **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"** (Fig 13 :134)

The framework returns the shape: two channels, an anchor on measured outcomes, an instrument that discriminates them, and a union that must be unbundled. It does not return the name of the disclosure that will work in a particular firm. Naming one without a ground reading is the metamerism error described in *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*, and it was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved the case. Here the missing instrument is a design that measures verified physical outcomes and blinded description separately, prospectively, per firm.

**Refuter:** a prospectively registered study that names and validates such an instrument and settles the branch at the ground. Until then the row is `gap`, not silence and not a verdict either way.

### F10: Assurance works on the channel, and that is evidence about the channel, not the ground

**Mode: `specified`. Domain D4. Status: triangulated (Fig 10 and the MIT assurance result, low overlap: figure seat vs measurement practice).**

> **"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"** (Fig 10 :18)
> **"→ install what can be violated"** (Fig 10 :19)
> **"→ keep the anchor reachable"** (Fig 10 :29)

The MIT Sloan report on third-party assurance says companies that verify emissions *"initially have higher carbon emissions and intensities than their peers"*, that *"even the good players discover that their absolute emissions are initially at least 13.5% higher than previously anticipated"*, and that such companies ultimately reduce emissions more. This is Fig 10 :24 in practice: the reading changed, the ground did not. Assurance is a channel instrument that makes the anchor reachable. It is not itself proof that disclosure caused abatement, because the study also reports self-selection: *"companies that verify and obtain assurance also seem to, on average, reduce CO2 emissions more than anyone else."* The self-selection must be recorded as a filter on the claim, not converted into a verdict (Fig 9 :60).

**Refuter:** a documented case where assurance changes a reported number and the verified physical measurement never changes, with no selection effect. That would show the channel can be repaired without ever touching the ground, which would support the description-only branch for that case.

### F11: Agreement and prestige are not witnesses here

**Mode: `general`. Domain D3. Status: figure-derived.**

> **"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."** (Fig 7 :35)
> **"same closure, handed along;"** (Fig 7 :12)
> **"agreement compounds, evidence does not"** (Fig 7 :13)
> **"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."** (Fig 10 :59)

An outreach list selected for people already inclined to agree with either branch, or ranked by how receptive they are to the framework, is one room wearing many names. The assurance study's self-selection point is the empirical twin of this rule. *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* states it in the strongest form: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."*

**Refuter:** a verdict produced by a room whose members were selected for agreement and that survives an independent ground test run by someone outside the room. If that happens, the selection rule was unnecessarily strict, though not thereby wrong about the general case.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the design-instrument rule: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* The shape claim and the separation of C1 and C2 are refuted if any of the following is observed:

1. A prospective, pre-registered difference-in-differences on a voluntary adoption event in which independently verified physical outcomes (emissions, injuries, incidents) change materially while a blinded description measure does not, with no selection effect and no boundary revision (breaks the description-only default for that setting, F2 and F8).
2. The mirror: a study in which description measures change materially and independently verified physical outcomes do not, with adequate power and an unpooled per-firm reading (breaks the behaviour-change branch for that setting, F3 and F8).
3. A single instrument that accounts for both branches with no categorically different residue, collapsing the union into a partition (breaks F7, Fig 9 :57).
4. An ESG rating whose correlation with verified physical outcomes reaches roughly the 0.92 level that credit ratings reach with default, and which does not move when the rated firm pushes on it (breaks F5).
5. A disclosure practice whose measured valence is invariant across a documented change of the firm's residual (breaks F6, Fig 11 :49-51).
6. Figure-level: one of the six substrate terms derived from the other five, or a frame that escapes its closure without import, or a frame that certifies its own limit (breaks the figures themselves, not just this reading).

No part of this solution may be upgraded to `grounded` until at least one of these has been run and has failed to fire, and until a verified-outcome measurement has paid for the shape.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the residual and the refuter, not with a theory of sustainability governance (Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"*).
2. Keep C1 and C2 separate in every design and every report. A blended "ESG performance score" is a pooled reading (Fig 10 :26) and destroys the diagnostic.

## 7. Recursive derivation summary

1. **Is this one question?** No. Fig 9 :54, :55, :57 plus the Library of Babel bundle passage: it is a union of contact change and description change, each with its own loop and instrument.
2. **What is the anchor?** Independently verified physical and social outcomes, named as the kind of thing that settles a question in Fig 2 :29. Not a report, not a rating, not analyst consensus.
3. **What does the disclosure act add?** A term to the library, not an atom to the world (Fig 6 :25). So behavioural change is a causal chain that must be supplied, not a property of the act.
4. **How could it change behaviour?** By changing what the firm attends to and does, so that contact changes (Fig 11 :40); the world reports one version as *"what gets voluntarily disclosed gets managed."*
5. **How could it change only description?** Through frame-internal scoring (Fig 1 :27), saturated metrics and pooled readings (Fig 10 :26), and valence drift toward boilerplate (Fig 12 :119). The world reports greenwashing and decoupling.
6. **Why do observers disagree?** Because ESG ratings are frames with differing scope, measurement and weights, correlated at 0.54 rather than 0.92 (MIT ACP), and because shared evidence pools make agreement worthless as a verdict (Fig 2 :40).
7. **What can be said at the stop?** A shape, two conditions, a per-firm indexing rule, a refuter, and an instrument requirement. Not which disclosure works, not a `grounded` answer. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

## 8. Updated verdict

The shape survives and the empirical question stays open. **A voluntary ESG disclosure adds a term to the firm's and its observers' libraries, not an atom to the world, so a change in description is the structural default of the act alone; a change in behaviour is a separate causal chain that requires changed contact with a paying ground and must be measured there. The two channels form a union, not a partition, and the world literature supplies both branches from one evidence pool, which is why no side can be declared the verdict from the figures.** Verdicts are kept separate per domain and never averaged. World sources did not flip any verdict; the apparent conflict between the "managed" finding and the greenwashing findings was returned to the census and resolved as a union, not softened into a middle position. Which specific disclosure changes what in which firm is a `gap`: the discriminator is an instrument that does not exist in this package. Mode `specified`, ungrounded. No unresolved strain remains after re-census.
