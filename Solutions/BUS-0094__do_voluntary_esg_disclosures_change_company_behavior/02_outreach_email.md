# Outreach draft: check the shape before you decide which side of the ESG question you take

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** the stage-1 output must survive the refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 before this is sent. That refuter is written down first, so there is something to fail.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about voluntary ESG disclosure and its refuter

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to agree with it, and I am not asking you to read the theory first. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a voluntary ESG disclosure adds a term to the firm's library and to its observers' libraries. It does not, by itself, add an atom to the world. Fig 6 :25 puts it plainly: *"An import adds no atom to the world."* So the question "does disclosure change behaviour, or mainly change how firms describe themselves" is a **union of two channels**, not one question with two answers:
>
> 1. **Contact change.** Did verified physical or social outcomes move after disclosure, per firm, with a counterfactual?
> 2. **Description change.** Did the report text, score, or rating move, measured separately and in its own units?
>
> The framework's structural claim is that a change in description is the default of the disclosure act alone, because a report cannot score itself: Fig 1 :27, *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* A behavioural effect is possible, but it is a causal chain through changed action and changed contact, and it has to be measured at the ground, which here means verified outcomes, not ratings. I am **not** claiming the chain never runs. Whether it runs in a given setting is an empirical question I am not settling here.
>
> **Why I am not using a blended ESG score as the answer:** Fig 9 :57, *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* A firm that changes only its description and a firm that changes only its operations are different compounds and need different instruments. Averaging them is how the live signal disappears (Fig 1 :22). Ratings are especially weak anchors here: the MIT Sloan Aggregate Confusion Project reports correlation among prominent ESG raters of about 0.54, against 0.92 for credit ratings.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. A pre-registered study in which verified emissions, injuries, or incidents change materially after voluntary adoption while a blinded description measure does not, with no selection effect and no measurement-boundary revision.
> 2. The mirror: description moves materially and verified physical outcomes do not, with adequate power and an unpooled per-firm reading.
> 3. One instrument that accounts for both channels with no categorically different residue left over.
> 4. An ESG rating that tracks verified outcomes at credit-rating-level correlation and does not move when the rated firm pushes on it.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract or narrow the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step formula (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Output first, theory second | Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* The recipient checks the two-channel shape without holding the framework's interior. |

| Verified outcomes as anchor, ratings as instrument | Fig 2 :29, :30; MIT Sloan ACP rating correlation 0.54 vs 0.92 |

| No "bombshell", no "true answer", no "trust" | *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* |
