## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not whether ESG disclosure is good or bad, nor whether a report is accurate in this or that case. It is whether the *effect* of a voluntary disclosure act lands on the firm's contact with the physical and social ground, or only on the firm's self-description. In effect terms:

- **b** is the firm's belief about what investors, regulators, and stakeholders will reward.
- **a** is what the firm does: abate emissions, change a supply chain, improve safety, or publish a report.
- **Γ(b,a)** is what the firm's library of routines and self-model predicts will follow.
- **o′** returns in two different units. The first is real outcomes: emissions, injuries, incidents, labour practices. The second is description: report text, ratings, scores, and the firm's public account of itself.
- **s** is the firm, indexed by time. R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| is measured per firm-slot, never averaged into an industry ESG score (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22).

The residual is: *after a voluntary disclosure event, does the slope of real outcomes change, or only the slope of the self-description?* This is an effect description. It names no theory, no "stakeholder governance", no "greenwashing", no "what gets measured gets managed". Those are candidate names withheld until the census.

- Option A, "disclosure mainly changes behaviour": leads to the prediction that an independently verified physical outcome changes after disclosure, with a proper counterfactual.
- Option B, "disclosure mainly changes description": leads to the prediction that report text, scores, and ratings change while independently verified physical outcomes do not.

**Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term** before the run (Formula, "Direction"): the answer would be a pair of measurable conditions that discriminate a change in contact with the ground from a change confined to the frame, plus an instrument that measures each separately. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no check in the loop can see it. It is now bound, so the refuter in `01` section 4 can fire on it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is that the framework **cannot choose between A and B without a ground measurement**, and that it can state the shape any check must have. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged. The world literature supplies both outcomes, which is itself the reason the question is empirical rather than figure-decidable.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles whether voluntary ESG disclosure changes behaviour, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of corporate accountability.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). A list of ESG advocates selected for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). Sustainability-disclosure researchers and raters frequently draw on the same corporate reports, so their agreement is one pool talking.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one. There is also a publishing constraint: the Journal of Business Ethics states that LLMs *"do not currently satisfy our authorship criteria"*, so this simulated package must not be submitted as authored work. See `04_links_and_distribution.md`.
