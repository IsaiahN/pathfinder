# Expert verification packet - the Tether on voluntary ESG disclosure

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ESG disclosure, accounting, or corporate accountability researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural claim. NOT `grounded`.** No measurement in this packet is claimed to settle whether voluntary disclosure changes company behaviour.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A voluntary ESG disclosure adds a term to the firm's and its observers' libraries, not an atom to the world. A change in description is therefore the structural default of the act considered alone; a change in behaviour is a separate causal chain that must change the firm's contact with a paying ground and be measured at independently verified outcomes. The two effects are a union of conditions, not a list of competing answers, and the firm that changes only its score and the firm that changes only its operations need different instruments. The framework stops at that shape. Which branch dominates in a given setting is an empirical question this packet does not answer.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1 and 6

- **Statement:** a self-description cannot witness its own effect, and a disclosure act cannot add a physical atom.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :27, *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Fig 6 :25, *"An import adds no atom to the world."* Supporting: Fig 1 :23, *"The ground enters as the only metric."*
- **Refuter:** a measure of behavioural change produced entirely inside the reporting frame and shown by an independent instrument to track verified physical outcomes at ground precision without post hoc adjustment; or a controlled case where a disclosure changes verified physical outcomes while actions and contacts are demonstrated unchanged, with no boundary revision.

### C2 - methods-facing; checkable by a researcher against their own design

- **Statement:** the two effects are a union of conditions requiring separate instruments, and "mainly" is a threshold comparison that needs a declared common scale before it can be tested.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :57, *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Fig 9 :54, *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 12 :112, *"A goal is a comparison"*, in the same line that reads *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."*
- **What to check:** does the design measure verified outcomes and blinded description separately, per firm, with a counterfactual, and does it declare the scale on which the two changes are compared? A design whose dependent variable is a rating has already answered the description half by construction.
- **Refuter:** a single instrument that accounts for both branches with no categorically different residue; or a demonstration that score units and verified physical units are related by identity at ground precision, so that one dependent variable suffices.

### C3 - measurement-facing; checkable against the ESG rating literature

- **Statement:** ratings are instruments for the quality of a report, not anchors for real behaviour, because they move when pushed and draw on the same evidence pool.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :30, *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* Fig 2 :40, *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* World measurement: MIT Sloan Aggregate Confusion Project, *"the correlation among prominent agencies' ESG ratings was on average 0.54; by comparison, credit ratings from Moody's and Standard & Poor's are correlated at 0.92."*
- **Refuter:** a rating whose correlation with independently verified physical outcomes reaches roughly the 0.92 level credit ratings reach with default, and which does not move when the rated firm pushes on it.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that voluntary ESG disclosure never changes behaviour, and not that it always does.
- Not that a particular standard, metric, or reporting boundary is the one that works. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that ESG ratings are useless. They measure report quality, not physical outcomes.
- Not that greenwashing is the universal condition of firms. It is a world-pool name for a source-discrimination failure.
- Not a derivation of "what gets voluntarily disclosed gets managed". That is Bochkay, Choi and Hales's empirical finding from one evidence pool, quoted as world evidence, never as a figure line.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating design, not another argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*, an instrument reading green *"cannot comment on the source"*, and the spectrometer resolved the case. Here:

1. **Perception test:** are real outcomes independently verified, or self-reported factors that move when pushed (Fig 2 :30, Fig 3 :5)?
2. **Objective test:** is progress stated in physical units before ratings are read (Fig 3 :24-27, Fig 12 :112)?
3. **Two-channel difference-in-differences:** prospectively registered, per firm, with verified physical outcomes and a blinded description measure taken before and after a voluntary adoption event, with a counterfactual and no pooled average.
4. **Boundary test:** an independent verification step that detects whether a reported change is physical or a measurement-boundary revision (MIT assurance study: assurance adopters discover emissions *"at least 13.5% higher than previously anticipated"*).
5. **Independence test:** the ground reading is taken by someone outside the room of observers who already agree (Fig 7 :35, Fig 10 :42).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :23, :27, Fig 6 :25, Fig 9 :54, :57, Fig 2 :30, :40, and Fig 12 :112. Eight short passages, all verifiable against the local figure text file.
2. Check C2 against a study you know: did it measure verified outcomes and blinded description separately, per firm, with a counterfactual, and did it declare the comparison scale? If not, which condition failed?
3. Name one instrument that would discriminate a real physical change from a boundary revision in a published report, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
