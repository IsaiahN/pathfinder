## What the wider literature bears on the question

1. **It supplies both branches, not a verdict.** Bochkay, Choi and Hales find real-outcome changes after voluntary SASB adoption; Yu, Luu, Chen, Silvia, and the boilerplate finding in Christensen, Hail and Leuz find decoupling. Krueger et al find a capital-market effect. Under Fig 9 :54-57 these are not conflicting answers to one question but a union that must be unbundled. A conflict sends the derivation back to the census, never to a softer average.
2. **It warns against using the dependent variable the question is about.** The MIT rating correlation of 0.54 against 0.92 for credit ratings is a direct measurement of the frame-instrument problem at Fig 2 :39-40. A study that uses an ESG rating as its outcome has already answered the "description" half by construction.
3. **It shows the channel can be repaired without the ground changing.** The MIT assurance study reports that verified numbers move on contact with an external instrument, while self-selection remains. That is Fig 10 :24 (*"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*) observed in practice.
4. **It is one evidence pool.** These papers cite one another, use the same rating vendors and the same corporate reports, and often share authors or institutions. Under Fig 2 :40 their agreement is the pool talking. Record overlap; do not multiply witnesses.
5. **A low-overlap witness agrees with the shape.** The Cambridge Goodhart page is not a sustainability-disclosure source. Its *"When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure"* corroborates the metric-saturation branch of Fig 10 :26 without sharing the disclosure evidence pool. This is corroboration, not a verdict.

## Publishing and AI-authorship caveat

The Journal of Business Ethics submission guidelines state: *"Large Language Models (LLMs), such as ChatGPT, do not currently satisfy our authorship criteria. Notably an attribution of authorship carries with it accountability for the work, which cannot be effectively applied to LLMs. Use of an LLM should be properly documented in the Methods section."*

This package is a **simulated derivation** produced by an LLM and checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is therefore **not a submittable authored article as it stands**. It can be used as:

- a private stage-1 shape claim sent to one person for refutation;
- a methods note that any human author rewrites, re-derives, and takes responsibility for, with the LLM use disclosed;
- a set of refuters that a human researcher adopts as their own pre-registration, after checking them.

Do not present it to a journal, a standard setter, or a funder as human-verified empirical work. That would be the exact confusion between a description of a derivation and a settled result that Fig 1 :27 and Fig 10 :18 forbid.
