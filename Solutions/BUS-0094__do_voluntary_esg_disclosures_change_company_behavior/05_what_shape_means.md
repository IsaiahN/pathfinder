## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (**a shape, not a name**)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134-135: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question the shape is:

> two separately measurable channels, one anchored on verified physical and social outcomes and one on the firm's description, with a union that must be unbundled before any lever is chosen, and an instrument that discriminates them per firm.

That is a shape. "Voluntary disclosure mainly causes emissions reductions" and "voluntary disclosure is only greenwashing" are names, and both are outside what the framework can return without a ground reading.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."* Here the starting vocabulary is "disclosure", "behaviour", "rating", "emissions", and the recurring new terms are "decoupling", "assurance", "materiality", "channel", "anchor".
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."*
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* This is the outreach rule in `02_outreach_email.md`: send the effect description, not the framework.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: the answer is reached when *"the finish line will also be the starting place"* - and the paradox is that by then you have *"composed the answer through the process of locating it."*

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* For this question, that means a prospective, registered measurement of verified outcomes and blinded description, with a counterfactual.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test. The MIT assurance result is the closest world case: it is an instrument from measurement practice, not a term invented inside the disclosure frame.

*They're Scanning Books When They Should* sharpens this: *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space."* Also: *"A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six."* And: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."*

The practical consequence: a choice of outcome variable (emissions versus rating) is a visible choice, not a neutral measurement. Declaring it makes it auditable. Treating it as the ground makes it a constant the researcher authored, which Fig 10 :48 forbids: *"a convention nothing can check is a constant the seat authored, carrying the seat’s authority."*

## 4. The source/provenance error, and why it governs this question

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. Metamerism: an instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source"* - one green light, or yellow+blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* And: *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."* It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved it.

Applied here: a high ESG score is a reading with several possible sources. It can come from real abatement, from a reporting boundary change, from a rating vendor's scope choice, or from a firm that simply writes more words. The reading does not determine the source. This is why the answer cannot be obtained by reading more reports or more ratings. What is owed is a discriminator that separates the sources, not a name for one of them.

*How Intelligence Actually Works* states the same at the level of composition: *"Projection is the expression of an inner state through to its furthest connectable medium... anything that reaches another medium in a form its source did not have is a projection, and the crossing is lossy."* A sustainability report is exactly such a projection. *"Indiscernibility is not identity"* - a report that is indiscernible from good behaviour by a rating is still a report.

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* states thevalidator limit: *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."* An ESG disclosure scored by the firm that produced it is the named failure.

## 5. Q2: what would license "disclosure changes behaviour" as a name?

**Nothing in the framework.** The framework returns the shape: two channels, an anchor, a union, a discriminator. A particular causal claim about a particular disclosure is licensed only by the ground settling a minted term, or by a provenanced import with a shadow test. Until one of those, the name is the searcher's own contribution and must be recorded as such. The same holds for "greenwashing". It is a world-pool name for a source-discrimination failure, not a figure term.

## 5b. Correction to my own §3 and §4

I initially wrote the "shape, not a name" rule as if it forbade naming any of the channels. That overstates it. The README's phrase is *"what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* The subject is a primitive not held in Γ. Here the channels are not missing primitives; they are two measurable consequences of an act, and naming them ("verified outcome", "description") is part of describing the residual in effect terms, which the method requires. What remains forbidden is naming *which* causal chain runs in a given firm, and treating a rating as if it were the anchor. Both claims are now recorded so neither is quietly lost.

I also initially considered calling the two channels **isomers** under Fig 12 :117, *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance."* That is retracted in `06_isomers_and_order.md`: they do not share an atom set, so the figure strains. The applicable Fig 12 lines are :5, *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording,"* and :111, *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* A report names a state; it does not name the route by which the state was reached.

## 6. Two consequences for this question

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."* An outreach campaign that gathers famous ESG names to agree with either branch is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask gives the claim a budget to be tried and lets the ground settle it. Fig 10 :59: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."*

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* *Arrival of the Fittest* adds that outputs from an LLM prompted with the formula "must be seen as simulacra" and that a reported fit *"could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula."* So `01` is a simulated derivation, checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of the claim about ESG disclosure and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on.

## 7. One more consequence from the society-of-agents essay

*How a Society of Agents Would Work* states: *"A group is only worth having while its members stay different."* It quotes Fig 2: independence is *"how little they draw on the same evidence"*, and warns that ten copies watching the same thing are one opinion said ten times. ESG raters and disclosure researchers who all read the same corporate reports are precisely such a group. The essay also notes that *"the ground sits under all three"* stores, and that *"Votes come from outcomes, not popularity."* That is the operational reason the two channels in this solution must be scored by verified outcomes, never by how many observers agree with a score.
