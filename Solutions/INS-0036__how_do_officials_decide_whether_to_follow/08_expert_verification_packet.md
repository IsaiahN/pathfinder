# Expert verification packet: official discretion under rules and networks

**What this is:** a claim in a form a public administration scholar, political scientist, auditor or oversight practitioner can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the institutional application, not `grounded`.** No enforcement record is claimed to have settled the per-slot residual. This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question is a union of two conditions, not one decision with two answers. For an official-case slot, the official compares two predictions at a threshold: the cost of deviating from the codified rule and the cost of defying the unwritten network expectation. The comparison is a goal in the figure's sense, not a merged field, and the weighting between private history and inherited prior is learnable from past outcomes. An unwritten expectation is efficient as a convention that coordinates shared meaning; it becomes a defect only when it decides an appointment, a protection or an exoneration, at which point it has stopped being a convention and is not an anchor. A network whose answer moves with its members' influence is a mirror. Standing is a stake that survives outcomes, so it is declared rather than denied, and a checking pool must be unauthored before it is examined. The framework returns this shape and its refuters. Which action a particular official took needs a source-discriminating enforcement record and is declared as a gap.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

- **Refuter:** one decision variable predicts per-case choices across both conditions with per-case residual trending down and no third term bound; or an official follows the higher-predicted-cost action with no new term bound and predicting.

### C2 — institution-facing; checkable against an enforcement record and an oversight design

- **Statement:** a network expectation is a convention until it decides an outcome; a network whose answer changes when members push on it fails the anchor test; an oversight room drawn from the same pool as the room it oversees produces agreement, not correction.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :41, *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."* Fig 2 :43, *"Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* Fig 2 :30, *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* Fig 7 :35, *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*
- **What to check:** is the room that audits a unit drawn from the same appointment channel and the same information as that unit? Does the unwritten expectation change when a senior member pushes on it? Is there an enforcement record that neither side can move?
- **Refuter:** a network expectation shown to be a genuine anchor that does not change when members push on it; or an oversight room with an unauthored, differing pool and different information that still fails to range against the enforcement record while a mirrored room succeeds.

### C3 — framework-internal derivation with an institutional consequence

- **Statement:** standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on, so declare it; the checking pool must be unauthored first and examined second; a tie's valence is relative to the residual, so reform adds oversight and cannot subtract contact.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :61, *"And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."* Fig 10 :60, *"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. - **Refuter:** a decision procedure where declared standing has no bearing on the reading; an actor whose valence toward the residual is invariant across a documented change of residual; or a reform that removes a contact rather than substituting a habitat.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that informal norms are always corrupt. A convention that coordinates meaning is efficient (Fig 2 :41).
- Not that a code of ethics or a written rule alone changes behaviour. Formal and informal rules are two frames, and neither alone settles the per-slot residual.
- Not that character explains the decision. The person-only domain has no nameable anchor and was withdrawn (`01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §1).
- Not that a corruption perception index, an audit pass rate or any threshold settles a case. Scores are filters for the budget (Fig 9 :60).
- Not which official followed which action. That is a declared gap (F9), not a hidden answer.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by an enforcement record.
- Not that the second seat in `11_audit.md` corroborates the first. It shares the evidence pool, so its agreement is internal consistency only (Fig 2 :40).

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."*

1. Does an enforcement record, for the same compliance reading, distinguish a rule-ranged decision from a network-ranged decision? If yes, the slot can be named and F9 closes.
2. Does an oversight room with a pool assembled before the question existed and a genuinely different information channel range against the enforcement record while a mirrored room does not? If yes, C2 and R5 survive.
3. Does a network expectation hold its answer when members push on it? If yes, it is an anchor and C2 is refuted.
4. Do two officials with matched histories and matched `w` diverge only when a new term is bound? If they diverge with nothing new, C1 and R3 are refuted.
