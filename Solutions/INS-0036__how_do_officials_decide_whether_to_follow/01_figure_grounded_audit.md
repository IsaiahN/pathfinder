## 0. The claim under audit and the residual

**The question as received:** How do officials decide whether to follow formal rules or the unwritten expectations of political and social networks?

**Residual in effect terms.** For official-slot `s`:

```
R_s = | Γ(b_s, a_s) - o'_s |
```

`b_s` is the belief formed before acting, from private history and inherited prior, mixed by `w`. `a_s` is one of two candidate actions: `a_R` (follow the codified rule) or `a_N` (follow the unwritten network expectation). `o'_s` is what the ground actually returns for that slot: sanction, protection, promotion, dismissal, survival. `Γ(b,a)` is the official's prediction of what each action returns. The residual is per slot, never aggregated:

> *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* (Fig 1 :19)
> *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* (Fig 1 :22)

The weighting `w` is the private-history / inherited-prior mixture, and it is learnable:

> *"How far the belief leans on private history versus inherited prior. w_A + w_B = 1, and w is learnable from the outcomes of past weightings. Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge."* (Symbols table :22-26)

So the network expectation is not a noise term on top of a rule-following baseline. It is a distinct input to `b`, with its own history and its own learnable weight. The two options are two frames with different closures and different anchors, not two answers to one question.

**Direction, bound as an ordinary term before the run.** An answer would look like this: a shape stating which closure may range against which non-updating anchor, how the two predicted residuals are compared at a threshold, and how `w` is updated from outcomes. It is a rule for a class of slots. It is not the name of a particular official and not a prediction of a particular case. Binding it now lets it accrue a residual and be refused by the same test as everything else (THE_FORMULA.md, "Direction"; Fig 5 :62-66).

## 1. Domain census (before any figure line was chosen)

Describe `R` by arity, symmetry and scale. **Arity:** a three-place relation (official belief, action, ground outcome), indexed by slot and ordered by time; it carries an ordered comparison bond and a sequence bond, not symmetric ones. **Symmetry:** "follow the rule" versus "follow the network" is a threshold relative to the predicted cost of each action (Fig 12 :87), not a symmetric join. **Scale:** single decision loop, oversight room, institutional habitat, and the time channel across a career; the membrane between scales is lossy (Fig 4 :27-32).

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Official per-slot loop | official, belief `b`, weighting `w`, action `a`, prediction `Γ(b,a)`, observation `o'` | residual reduction per slot | the actual outcome for that slot: sanction, protection, promotion, dismissal | admitted |
| D2 Formal rule frame | rules, auditors, courts, inspectors, merit procedures | compliance, legality, sanction | an enforcement record that does not move when the official pushes on it (Fig 2 :29-30) | admitted only when enforcement genuinely ranges; otherwise a frame pretending to be an anchor |
| D3 Network expectation frame | patrons, brokers, peers, political and social ties | standing, reciprocal favour, reputation | network continuation: who is promoted, protected, included | admitted as a convention, not as a verdict anchor; its "anchor" moves with members' influence (Fig 2 :13-15) |
| D4 Institutional habitat | oversight bodies, transparency rules, enforcement capacity, party and electoral context, merit systems | contact and valence | the ground that settles outcomes for the residual | admitted; enumerated, never composed (Fig 11 :43) |
| X1 Official character as a free-floating property | the person alone | talent or virtue | none nameable that settles the per-slot residual | **not a domain yet** (Fig 11 :39-40) |
| X2 "The rule alone determines the decision" | the rule | compliance | already inside D2 | absorbed, not a separate domain |

**Union, not partition.** D1/D3 and D2/D4 form two compounds. The whole is a union of condition C1 (formal anchor reachable and actually ranging) and condition C2 (network convention has taken the verdict seat). A case in two conditions is categorically different, not a harder case of either (Fig 9 :57). Split before searching (Fig 9 :4-5, :55).

**Order by dependency (Fig 3 :4-15):** perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry. The chain is ordered by dependency, not importance:

> *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* (Fig 3 :19)

**No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.** X1 fails that test, so "some officials are simply corrupt" and "some are simply virtuous by character" are not admitted as explanations. An improvement that changes only the person in isolation changes nothing:

> *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* (Fig 11 :40)

## 3. Findings

### F1 — Split the union; never average across officials (triangulated: Fig 1 and Fig 9, low overlap between per-slot indexing and union/partition logic)

> *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* (Fig 1 :19)
> *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* (Fig 1 :22)
> *"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* (Fig 9 :4-5)
> *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* (Fig 9 :57)

**Reading.** "Officials decide by X most of the time" is an average that throws away the only signal that says where to look. The two conditions (formal anchor reachable, network convention capturing the verdict seat) have separate loops and separate grounds. Verdicts stay separate and are never averaged. **Mode: `general`.** **Refuter:** a single decision variable that predicts per-slot choices across both conditions with per-slot residual trending down and no third term bound.

### F2 — The decision is a comparison of two predicted residuals at a threshold (triangulated: Fig 12 and Operators table, different roles)

> *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* (Fig 12 :112)
> *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* (Operators table :48; also Fig 12 :87)
> *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* (Fig 12 :111)

**Reading.** The official does not select a rule abstractly. The official compares the predicted cost of rule deviation against the predicted cost of network defection and acts on the smaller. That is `⋛`, a comparison, not a merger of the two into a third field. It is a goal in the figure's precise sense: a threshold relation between two quantities. **Mode: `specified` for officials.** **Refuter:** a case where an action is followed despite its predicted cost exceeding the other, with no new term (duty, identity, oath, threat) bound and predicting.

### F3 — The weighting `w` is learnable, so history biases the comparison (single-source: Symbols table)

> *"Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge."* (Symbols table :25-26)

**Reading.** The unwritten expectation is a private-history channel; the formal rule is an inherited prior. Two officials with identical training diverge because their histories and `w` diverge. This is why "same rules, different behaviour" is not a paradox and why a rule change alone cannot settle the residual. **Mode: `specified`.** **Refuter:** two officials with identical histories and identical `w` who diverge in their per-slot decisions with no new term bound.

### F4 — Network agreement is efficient for a convention and disqualifying for a verdict (triangulated: Fig 2 and Fig 10, different quantities)

> *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40)
> *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"* (Fig 2 :41)
> *"Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* (Fig 2 :43)
> *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."* (Fig 10 :49)

**Reading.** An unwritten expectation does useful work: it coordinates shared meaning where no written rule can. It becomes a defect at the moment it decides appointments, protection or exoneration. Then the convention has taken the verdict seat and is not an anchor. This is not a claim that all informal norms are corrupt; it is a claim about which office the norm is occupying. **Mode: `general` for the valence rule, `specified` for the bureaucracy.** **Refuter:** a network expectation shown to be a genuine anchor, that is, one that does not change its answer when members push on it, so it never took the verdict seat.

### F5 — The anchor must not update; a network that moves with influence is a mirror (triangulated: Fig 2 and Fig 7, anchor rule vs room chain)

> *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* (Fig 2 :29)
> *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* (Fig 2 :30)
> *"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."* (Fig 2 :34)
> *"same closure, handed along;"* (Fig 7 :12) *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"* (Fig 7 :13)
> *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35)

**Reading.** When the oversight room is drawn from the same pool as the room it oversees (same appointment channel, same information), their agreement is the pool talking. Capture is a mirror chain, not a single bad actor. The remedy is selection between rooms against the residual, not more agreement inside the chain. **Mode: `specified`.** **Refuter:** an oversight body whose pool was assembled before the question existed and whose closure genuinely differs, and which still fails to range against the enforcement record while a mirrored room succeeds.

### F6 — Stake, standing and the unauthored pool (triangulated: Fig 10 and Fig 11, seat rules vs habitat valence)

> *"The condition is that nothing about which value the ground returns today serves the occupant: an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* (Fig 10 :59)
> *"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."* (Fig 10 :60)
> *"And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."* (Fig 10 :61)
> *"A candidate set chosen by a participant is an authored room however well the selection is reasoned, so the pool must be unauthored first and examined second."* (Fig 10 :73-74)
> *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* (Fig 11 :49)

**Reading.** An official's interest in career outcome is unavoidable. Bending the reading of the rule is disqualifying. Standing (patronage, tenure earned under a prior coalition) is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on, which is why past protection predicts present deviation. Competitive examinations, lotteries and "everyone who qualifies" are attempts at an unauthored pool; patronage is an authored room however carefully the selection is justified. **Mode: `general` for the stake rule, `specified` for the application.** **Refuter:** a decision procedure where declared standing has no bearing on the reading, or a network tie whose valence is invariant across a documented change of residual.

### F7 — Habitat enumeration and asymmetric reform (triangulated: Fig 11, three distinct clauses)

> *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* (Fig 11 :43)
> *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* (Fig 11 :50)
> *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* (Fig 11 :61)
> *"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."* (Fig 11 :73)

**Reading.** List everything in contact with the decision before naming a cause: rules, auditors, peers, patrons, family ties, electoral context, enforcement capacity, then what contacts those, outward until the cascade stops mattering (Fig 11 :44). A patron is not intrinsically corrupt; valence is relative to the residual. Reform can add oversight actors and cannot subtract the habitat. Calling a reform "solved" ignores that managed contact is permanent work. **Mode: `specified`.** **Refuter:** an actor whose valence toward the residual is invariant across a documented change of residual, or a reform that removes a contact rather than substituting a habitat.

### F8 — Thresholds are filters, never witnesses (triangular with Fig 9 and the seat map)

> *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* (Fig 9 :60)
> *"same closure, handed along;"* (Fig 7 :12)

**Reading.** Corruption perception indices, audit pass rates and compliance scores rank where to spend attention. They do not settle whether a particular official followed the ground. Selecting officials for likely agreement, or selecting a checking room for likely receptiveness, manufactures the disqualifying correlation. **Mode: `general`.** **Refuter:** a threshold or score that settles the per-slot residual without a ground reading, or a room selected for agreement whose agreement still predicts the enforcement record at maximum independence.

### F9 — The honest gap: no figure line names the individual choice (`gap`)

> *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (Fig 9 :32)
> *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* (Fig 13 :134)
> *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* (Fig 8 :39)
> *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* (Fig 6 :23)

**Reading.** No figure line predicts which official will follow which action in a given case. That needs a ground reading per slot: an enforcement record that discriminates the two closures and a source-discriminating instrument (THE_FORMULA.md step 7; *The Hard Problem is About Source*: *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."*). What the figures return is a shape: which closure must be reachable, which anchor must not update, which weighting is learnable. **Mode: `general` for the limit. Status: `gap` after a genuine re-census.** **Refuter for the gap:** a figure line that directly predicts individual choice after re-census, or an instrument that returns the per-slot residual and names the source of the reading.

## 4. Strain test and re-census

1. **Fig 2 :40 versus Fig 2 :41.** One says agreement is disqualifying for a verdict, the other says it is efficient for a convention. Re-census: two quantities, agreement about the world versus agreement about a shared meaning. Not strained. Recorded as F4.
2. **Fig 10 :61 (standing is a stake) versus Fig 11 :50 (valence is relative).** Re-census: one governs a seat's stake across outcomes, the other governs an actor's valence toward a given residual. Different objects. Not strained.
3. **Fig 2 :37-38 versus Fig 10 :57-60.** The figure says *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."* The seat map says an anchor with a reputation to protect is disqualified. Re-census: the normative question (is this a good answer for a person) has human judgment as its anchor; the descriptive question (which closure did the official range against) has the enforcement record as its anchor. The two are different questions. Not strained, but the earlier draft of this audit conflated them. Corrected in §6.
4. **Fig 6 :23 versus Fig 2 :35.** Only contact with a differing closure moves the wall; alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. Same law read at two scales, low overlap between the reachability figure and the alignment figure. Within the framework this is one evidence pool, so it is not independent corroboration.

**After re-census: no live strain. One gap, declared (F9). One earlier domain withdrawn (X1).**

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the recommendation and conclusion)

Per the Seat Map :287: *"And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

- **R1.** A single decision variable predicts per-slot choices across both conditions with residual trending down and no third term bound (breaks F1).
- **R2.** An official follows the higher-predicted-cost action with no new term bound and predicting (breaks F2).
- **R3.** Two officials with identical histories and identical `w` diverge with no new term bound (breaks F3).
- **R4.** A network expectation is shown to be a genuine anchor, that is, it does not change its answer when members push on it (breaks F4 and F5).
- **R5.** An oversight room with an unauthored, differing pool and different information still fails to range against the enforcement record while a mirrored room succeeds (breaks F5 and F6).
- **R6.** An actor's valence toward the residual is invariant across a documented change of residual, or a reform removes a contact instead of substituting a habitat (breaks F7).
- **R7.** A figure line directly predicts individual choice after a genuine re-census, or an instrument returns the per-slot residual and names its source (breaks F9).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, nothing here may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Split the question into its two conditions before any search. Keep verdicts per domain, never averaged (F1).
2. Describe the per-slot residual before naming a frame or a field. Otherwise any discipline will seem to fit (Fig 9 :27-28).
3. Present the decision as a threshold comparison of two predictions, not as a merged field or a character trait (F2, F3).
4. Treat the unwritten expectation as a convention until it takes the verdict seat. Then name the failure as a seat failure, not as "corrupt culture" (F4, F5).
5. Declare standing. Make checking pools unauthored (lottery, everyone who qualifies, a set assembled before the question existed, or the enumerated habitat) before examining them (F6).
6. Enumerate the habitat. Add oversight; do not claim to subtract contact (F7).
7. Use scores as filters for the budget. Ask for a refutation, not agreement (F8).
8. State the gap. The framework returns a shape, not a name of the deciding person (F9).
