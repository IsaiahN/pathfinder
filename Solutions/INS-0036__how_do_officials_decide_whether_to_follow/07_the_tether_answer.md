# The Tether's answer: how officials decide whether to follow formal rules or unwritten network expectations

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the institutional application, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## 1. The residual it answers

The question presumes one decision with two answers. The figures say it is a union of two conditions with separate loops and separate grounds. For official-slot `s`, `R_s = |Γ(b_s,a_s) - o'_s|`, with `a_s` either the codified rule or the unwritten network expectation. `b_s` mixes private history and inherited prior by a learnable `w`. The residual is per slot, never averaged:

> *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* (Fig 1 :19)
> *"not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* (Fig 9 :5)

## 2. The shape

1. **It is a threshold comparison, not a merged field.** The official compares the predicted cost of deviating from the rule with the predicted cost of defying the network, and acts on the smaller. Fig 12 :87 and the Operators table :48 define that comparison as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*. Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* The comparison is a goal, not a third theory.
2. **History enters through a learnable weighting.** The unwritten expectation is the private-history channel; the formal rule is the inherited prior. Symbols table :25-26: *"Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge."* Same training, different histories, different decisions, and no paradox.
3. **The network expectation is a convention until it decides an outcome.** Fig 2 :41: *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."* Fig 2 :43: *"Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* When it decides appointments, protection or exoneration, a convention has taken the verdict seat. It is not thereby an anchor.
4. **A network whose answer moves with influence is a mirror, not an anchor.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Fig 2 :30: *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* Fig 7 :13: *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*. Capture is a chain of rooms sharing one pool, not one bad actor.
5. **Standing is a declared stake, and the checking pool must be unauthored.** Fig 10 :61: *"And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."* Fig 10 :60: *"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."* Fig 10 :73-74: *"A candidate set chosen by a participant is an authored room however well the selection is reasoned, so the pool must be unauthored first and examined second."*
6. **The habitat is enumerated; valence is relative to the residual.** Fig 11 :50: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* Fig 11 :61: *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* Reform adds oversight; it does not subtract contact.
7. **Scores are filters, never witnesses.** Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* A corruption index decides where to look, not what happened in a slot.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that all informal norms are corrupt. A convention that coordinates shared meaning is efficient (Fig 2 :41); only a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one (Fig 2 :43).
- Not that character alone explains the choice. X1 has no nameable anchor and was withdrawn in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §1. Improvement in isolation changes nothing (Fig 11 :40).
- Not that a written rule alone determines the outcome. That reading was absorbed into the formal-rule frame (D2), not admitted as a separate domain.
- Not which official follows which action in a given case. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- Not `grounded`. No enforcement record was read that discriminates the two closures per slot.

## 4. What would settle it

A ground reading, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* The instrument needed here must return, for the same compliance reading, whether the official ranged against the rule or the network. Concretely:

- an enforcement record with an unauthored, differing pool, compared against a mirrored room, for the same set of cases (tests F5 and R5);
- two officials with matched histories and matched `w` whose per-slot residuals diverge only when a new term is bound (tests F3 and R3);
- a network expectation that holds its answer when members push on it (tests F4 and R4);
- an actor whose valence flips when the residual changes (tests F7 and R6);
- the seven pre-registered refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §5, written before the run.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The six-term substrate is a visible guess, not nature's own list; declaring the primitives makes the choice visible, not neutral. The answer stops where the available instruments can no longer discriminate sources, and the lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* F9 stays a declared gap. Prestige and agreement are not instruments: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*)

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say whether officials are corrupt or virtuous, and it does not name who followed which rule. It says the question is two conditions, not one: before acting, an official compares the predicted cost of breaking the codified rule with the predicted cost of defying the network, at a threshold, under a weighting learnable from past outcomes, and the residual is measured per official and per case. An unwritten expectation is useful as a convention that coordinates shared meaning, but once it decides appointments, protection or exoneration it has stopped being a convention and is not an anchor. A network whose answer moves when its members push on it is a mirror, and a room that agrees with the room it oversees has produced variation, not a verdict. Standing is a stake that survives outcomes, so declare it and draw checking pools before the question exists. What the framework returns is that shape and the refuters written in advance. Which action a particular official took needs a source-discriminating enforcement record, and that is the gap the answer leaves open.
