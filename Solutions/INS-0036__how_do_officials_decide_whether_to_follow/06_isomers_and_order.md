# Isomers and order: the structure of the two decision frames

## DOMAIN CENSUS

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Isomer or functional group?

> *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* (Fig 12 :117)
> *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* (Fig 12 :124)

As usually described, the two frames do not share an atom set: the formal frame is written over `{rule, auditor, sanction}`, the network frame over `{tie, patron, standing}`. **They are not isomers.** They are two members of the functional group "threshold comparison over a predicted cost", with different atoms in the slots. The group says what kind (a decision); the qualifier says which member (rule-ranged or network-ranged).

The stronger form is available. If both are written over the full shared habitat `{rule, tie, audience, sanction, standing, outcome}`, differing only in which bond connects which atom to the outcome, then they **are** isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. That is the claim worth testing, because it predicts that swapping the bond while holding the atoms fixed changes the behaviour. **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either frame can be written without an atom the other requires (network-following with no rule at all, or rule-following with no network), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands.

### Q2: What order does the loop require?

> *"Where a given chain stops is a reading, never a property of the chain."* (Fig 3 :18)
> *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* (Fig 3 :19)
> *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"* (Fig 9 :27-28)
> *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* (Fig 12 :112)

The required order is: perceive the per-slot residual, acquire terms for what the action affords, state what would count as progress in those terms, choose the comparison, act, observe, then update `w` and carry what paid. A link with no instrument is not a link that works:

> *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* (Fig 3 :27)

If the frame is named first (public administration, clientelism, organizational sociology) and the residual is described afterwards, any of them will seem to fit. That is the inversion Fig 9 warns against. **Falsifier:** if the reversed order (frame first, residual second) still yields per-slot residuals trending down against an enforcement record at maximum independence, the describe-first rule is strained and must go back to census.

### Q3: The comparison is a goal, not a merged field

> *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* (Fig 12 :87; Operators table :48)

The official's "goal" in this figure's precise sense is the comparison itself, not a unified theory of rule and network. The comparison decides which predicted cost is smaller at the threshold. A vocabulary holding only nouns (rule, norm, culture) can name what a situation contains and cannot name a route through it:

> *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* (Fig 12 :111)

Negation matters here. A refusal is a negation and is not a bond (Fig 12 :112-113; Operators table :53-58). "The official did not follow the rule" is not the absence of a rule-following molecule; it is the refusal of a claim. A record that can only accumulate "instances of compliance" cannot carry the correction. This is why an audit that never records refusals cannot settle F9.

## CONCLUSION

The two decision frames are a **functional group** as usually described and a real **isomer claim** under the full shared atom set. Their union is ordered: perceive, name, compare, act, observe, update. The comparison is a goal in Fig 12's sense and a refusal is a negation, not a missing molecule. The wider fields corroborate the mechanism in effect terms, with low overlap only where their evidence pools genuinely differ. Two falsifiers were written before the run: one for the isomer claim, one for the order claim.
