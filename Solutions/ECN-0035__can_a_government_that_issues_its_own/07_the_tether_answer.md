# The Tether's answer on fiscal capacity

**Mode: `specified` on each domain verdict, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

"Can a government that issues its own currency run out of fiscal capacity?" asks one question using three different quantities. They do not share an anchor, so they cannot share a verdict. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* The residual is the contradiction between a true answer for the nominal condition and evidently true answers for the real and external conditions. The answer is a separator, not a number.

## 2. The shape and the per-domain verdicts

**D1, own-currency payment: cannot run out, as a closure fact.** Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* A government that authors the currency can always author another unit. That says what can be composed inside its own accounting, not what the units can buy. Mode `general`, structural. **Triangulated** (Fig 6 :21 and Fig 5 :44, two low-overlap figure lines). It becomes a defect only when promoted to a verdict about real capacity, which is the named failure *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"* (Fig 10 :47).

**D2, real command: can run out, at the slot.** Fig 13 :68: *"finiteness in a finite region is what makes the counting terminate."* Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* The anchor is real labour, materials and energy, read through prices and physical availability. New units add no atom (Fig 6 :25). Inflation is the signal that a specific slot is full, and the signal is per slot: Fig 1 :19, *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Mode `specified`. **Triangulated** (Fig 13, Fig 2, Fig 10, three low-overlap figure lines).

**D3, external obligation: can run out, if the obligation is in a currency the issuer cannot author.** Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Foreign exchange, export capacity and reserve holdings are imports from a differing closure. Mode `specified`. **Triangulated** (Fig 6 :23 and Fig 8 :45).

**D4, verification: agreement cannot settle any of the above.** Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Mode `general`. **Triangulated** (Fig 2 and Fig 7).

These four verdicts are kept separate and are never averaged. "A currency issuer cannot run out" and "a currency issuer can run out" are both correct about a different quantity. Flattening them is the error.

## 3. What constraints matter most, in dependency order

1. **Real command at the specific slot**, because it is the broken link (Fig 3 :19, *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*). This is the anchor that actually pays.
2. **External obligation**, downstream of real command, and binding only where the promise is in a currency the issuer does not author. A reserve-currency issuer has more room here, but the room is a privilege that can be lost, not a law of nature.
3. **Nominal financing**, which already works. Effort spent here arrives at a link that cannot use it.

**Debt ratios are filters for the budget, not witnesses for the verdict.** Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that deficits never matter. They matter at the real-resource link and at the external link.
- Not that inflation is impossible, or that any inflation is always caused by spending. The claim is only that inflation is the reading of a full slot.
- Not that foreign-currency debt is harmless. It is the clearest case of a constraint outside the issuer's closure.
- Not that any debt/GDP number, inflation target or reserve ratio is a threshold. No figure line returns one.
- Not that a consensus of economists settles the question. A survey is evidence about a room.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken here, and no country's fiscal space is assessed.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument at the slot, not another argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened 2026-11-23): *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"*; the spectrometer, not a debate, resolved the ambiguity. The proto-instruments already exist (Fig 6 :34): sectoral wage and price data, capacity utilisation, import cover, foreign-currency maturity profiles. They must be ranged against the ground separately (Fig 2 :45, *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*).

**Standing falsifiers, written before the run** (full set in `01` section 5):

1. involuntary own-currency default by a floating-rate issuer with no foreign-currency debt and no legal bar to issuance;
2. an economy where spending rises without limit and without real-resource or price effect at any slot;
3. a single aggregate statistic sufficient to locate the binding constraint with no per-slot residual;
4. a foreign-currency obligation discharged at will by the domestic issuer without import or foreign consent.

If any fires, the separator is revised in a visible retraction.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*. The figures return the shape of the answer, a union with per-slot thresholds and a dependency order. They do not return the number, the country, or the date at which the constraint binds. That needs a ground reading by someone with the data and an instrument. The two live strains are named in `01` section 7: the operand type of ⋛ across nominal and real levels, and whether reserve-currency privilege is a genuine imported atom or a convention. Neither is smoothed over.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that a currency issuer can never run out, and it does not say that crowding out is always around the corner. It says the question bundles three different conditions with different anchors. In its own currency the government cannot be forced into involuntary default, because it can always compose another unit inside its own closure. That fact says nothing about what the units can buy: the domestic constraint is real labour, materials and energy at the specific slot, with inflation as the reading of a full slot, and the aggregate debt ratio hides which slot is full. The external constraint is separate again: a promise in a currency the government cannot author is an atom it does not hold. These form a union, so one lever clears the first condition and leaves the others standing, and effort spent on financing cannot rescue a break at real command. What is offered is the separator, four refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. No country's fiscal space is assessed, because no measurement was taken.
