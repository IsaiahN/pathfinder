## 0. The claim under audit, restated as an effect

> "Can a government that issues its own currency run out of fiscal capacity, and what constraints matter most?"

The ordinary term doing the damage is **fiscal capacity**. It is used as though it named one stock that can be high or low. In effect terms it names at least three different relations:

1. **Nominal payment.** Can the government make payment in the units it alone authors?
2. **Real command.** Can those units command labour, materials, energy and productive capacity without moving prices beyond what the political community will bear?
3. **External obligation.** Can the government meet promises denominated in a currency it does not author, or whose acceptance by others it cannot decree?

The residual is the contradiction between the answer to (1), which is no for a floating-rate issuer, and the answers to (2) and (3), which are evidently yes under some conditions. A fourth relation, whether economists agree about the answer, is often smuggled in as a witness. It is not one.

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term (Direction).** A separator that says which of the three relations is the binding one at a given slot, what anchor settles it, and what would reverse the verdict. It does not look like a universal debt-to-GDP threshold.

## 1. Residual described by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** at least three conditions, plus a verification condition that is not a condition of the world. The question is a **union compound**, not a single quantity with a maximum (Fig 9 :55, *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*).
- **Symmetry:** "run out" is a **threshold relation**, not an additive stock. The relevant bond is comparison, ⋛, *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* (Fig 12 :87; Operators table :48-51). Capacity and obligation are compared; they are not simply summed. A symmetric conjunction would hide which operand is binding.
- **Scale:** first order inside the domestic monetary closure; second order at the interface with the real economy and the foreign sector. What counts as a constraint at one level is not what counts at another (Fig 12 :104, *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."*).

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | verdict |

**Partition or union: union.** The conditions overlap. A sovereign issuer with heavy foreign-currency debt sits in D1 intersect D3. An issuer with idle nominal room but a specific labour or energy bottleneck sits in D1 intersect D2. An issuer with spare domestic capacity but a pegged exchange rate sits in D2 intersect D3. Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* One lever, creating more own-currency units, clears the D1-only region and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55-57). Unbundle before searching.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Link 1 is nominal financing, and it works. Link 2 is real command, and it is where the chain typically breaks. Link 3 is external obligation, downstream of link 2. Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Effort spent on "how will we finance it" arrives at a link that cannot use it. Fig 3 :22: *"A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can."* Refuse the single-stock reading before minting an answer (`THE_FORMULA.md` step 2, refuse before mint).

**Strain test.** My first pass tried to read *"a government cannot run out of fiscal capacity"* directly from Fig 6 :21, *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* That line fits D1 exactly. Applied to D2 or D3 it strains: the government plainly can run out of real resources and foreign exchange. A line that only fits with a strain governs another quantity, so re-census. Fig 6 :21 governs **nominal composition inside a closure**, not command of atoms outside it. The apparent paradox dissolves when "fiscal capacity" is split. After re-census, no strain on that point. This is the mirror of the exemplar's finding that "spacetime is not a primitive": here **fiscal capacity is not a single stock**.

## 4. Findings, with verbatim quotes and refuters

### F1: "Fiscal capacity" is a union, not a stock (decisive for the framing)

Fig 9 :4: *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones."* Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*

The MMT literature answers the nominal question correctly and then lets that answer stand for the whole bundle. The World Bank / original-sin literature answers the external question correctly and lets that stand for the whole bundle. Both are right on their own subset. Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

**Refuter:** show that one measure (for example debt/GDP or the government's financing cost) is sufficient to locate the binding constraint in all three domains with no overlap left over. Until then the union verdict stands. Mode `general`, triangulated across Fig 9 and Fig 1.

### F2: The "cannot run out" claim is true only inside D1's closure

Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* Fig 6 :22: *"NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world."* Fig 6 :25: *"An import adds no atom to the world."* Fig 6 :26: *"The world does not gain primitives; a frame gains access."*

A government that authors the currency can always author another unit of it. That is a statement about what can be composed inside its own closure. It is not a statement about what the units can buy. Fig 5 :44: *"A term that explains everything by saying nothing fails this."* "The government can always pay, therefore there is no constraint" explains every nominal obligation by saying nothing about the real or external ones.

World corroboration (secondary only): the Modern Money Network states that fiat-and-floating governments *"face price stability constraints, but not solvency constraints per se"*, which already concedes the split. Wray (Levy Policy Note 2026/8) writes that *"a sovereign government can always make any payment on debt denominated in its own currency"* and immediately identifies inflation, not default, as the problem.

**Refuter:** an involuntary default on own-currency obligations by a floating-rate issuer with no foreign-currency debt and no legal or institutional bar to issuance would break the closure claim. Mode `specified` for the D1 reading, `general` for the closure rule. Triangulated: Fig 6 :21 and Fig 5 :44 are low-overlap figure lines.

### F3: The domestic anchor is real resources and inflation, not the government's account

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :30: *"you push on it, it is not an anchor."* The government's own balance sheet changes when the government writes numbers in it, so it fails the anchor test for its own capacity claim.

Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."* Fig 2 :34: *"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."*

Fig 13 :68: *"finiteness in a finite region is what makes the counting terminate."* Fig 13 :14 describes energy as *"conserved, finite, directionless alone."* Real resources are finite in a finite region, which is exactly what makes a capacity question answerable at all. Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* More nominal units do not change contact with labour or materials.

Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* Declaring that capacity exists because the treasury can write a cheque is a convention deciding an outcome.

World corroboration: Wikipedia's MMT article states the tenet that an issuer *"Is limited politically in its money creation only by demand-pull inflation"*, and that money-financed spending is sustainable *"only while the economy has spare capacity"*. Wray: *"The problem with too much of the wrong kind of spending is not the risk of default, but, rather, the risk of inflation."*

**Refuter:** demonstrate an economy in which spending can rise without limit and without any real-resource or price effect, at every slot. That would break the Fig 2 anchor and the Fig 13 finiteness claim. Mode `specified`. Triangulated: Fig 2, Fig 13, Fig 10 are three low-overlap figure lines.

### F4: The constraint registers per slot, and aggregate fiscal ratios hide it

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 1 :23: *"The ground enters as the only metric."*

A headline debt ratio is a pooled reading. Fig 10 :26 names the failure directly: *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*. The live signal is not "the debt ratio is high"; it is "this particular skilled labour category, this particular energy input, this particular region's productive capacity is already fully spoken for."

Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* A fiscal metric produced by the government's own accounting cannot score the government's own capacity.

World corroboration: the MMT article reports that Japan's *"labour shortages, population ageing, and persistently low inflation make MMT-style fiscal stimulus difficult to implement"*, and that Japan *"has little unused economic capacity remaining"*. Sawyer's objection to a job guarantee is that it *"would require productive capacity in the right quantities and places"*. Both are per-slot statements, not aggregate ones.

**Refuter:** show a single aggregate statistic that locates the binding constraint with no per-slot residual left over. Mode `specified`. Single-source on the figure side (Fig 1), corroborated by two world sources in a different field, so not upgraded to triangulated on the figure side alone.

### F5: Foreign-currency obligations are atoms outside the issuer's closure

Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* An obligation in a currency the government cannot author is an atom it does not hold. It can only be acquired by export, reserve drawdown, or the consent of a foreign holder, all of which are imports from a differing closure.

Fig 8 :45: *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."* Fig 8 :53: *"Residual first, frame second."* The union of domestic and external frames has a surplus only when a residual is described and aimed; an undirected union is mute (Fig 8 :19-21).

World corroboration: the original-sin literature defines the condition as one in which *"most countries are not able to borrow abroad in their domestic currency"* and notes that domestic-investment *"currency mismatch"* makes *"macroeconomic and financial instability"* likely. Wray concedes that loss of the dollar's *"exorbitant privilege"* *"could be costly"* and could set off devaluation and inflation pressure.

**Refuter:** show a government discharging a genuinely foreign-currency obligation at will, without import, exports, reserves or a foreign counterparty's consent. Mode `specified`. Triangulated: Fig 6 :23 and Fig 8 :45 are distinct figure lines.

### F6: A declaration of capacity is a convention that has become a verdict

Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* Fig 10 :49: *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."*

The statement "a currency issuer cannot run out" is harmless as a description of the nominal closure. It becomes a defect when it is promoted to a verdict about real capacity. `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: *"SETTLE — the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* Fig 5 :70: *"A term is settled when the ground pays for it."*

Fig 10 :59: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* Proponents of a particular fiscal programme have an unavoidable interest in the outcome. That interest must be declared and must not enter the reading of the constraint.

**Refuter:** show a case where a declared capacity, with no ground contact, reliably settles a real-resource dispute. Mode `general` on the seat rule. Single-source on the figure side (Fig 10), corroborated by the prestige-is-not-action essay.

### F7: Economists' agreement cannot settle the question

Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* Fig 7 :13: *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"* Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*

The Wikipedia article reports that in a 2019 survey of top US economists *"not a single respondent agreed with the basic aspects of MMT."* That is a fact about a room. By Fig 9 :60, *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* The survey can filter which venues get tried first. It cannot decide whether the constraint is real.

**Refuter:** show a case where consensus alone moved a non-updating anchor. Mode `general`. Triangulated across Fig 2 and Fig 7.

### F8: What would settle it is a discriminator, not another argument

*The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened 2026-11-23): *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"* and an instrument that cannot distinguish sources *"demonstrates its own inadequacy"*. The essay's spectrometer resolved the case, not an argument.

Here, a low debt ratio and a high debt ratio can each accompany an inflation breakout, depending on which resource slot is full. The aggregate reading cannot comment on the source. What is owed is a per-slot instrument, not a better slogan. Fig 6 :34: *"the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."* The proto-instruments already exist: wage and price data by sector, capacity utilisation, import cover, foreign-currency maturity profiles. They need to be ranged against the ground separately (Fig 2 :45, *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*).

**Refuter:** show that no possible discriminator could separate real-resource from nominal-finance constraints at a slot. That would make the question underivable in principle, a different fact from unreachable at this budget (Fig 8 :39). Mode `specified`, single-source on the figure side, world-supported.

### F9: The chain breaks between financing and real command

Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

Most public argument about fiscal capacity is spent on link 1, how to finance, because that is where the measurements already exist. Fig 3 :29 says improvement effort lands above the break. The missing link is the vocabulary for what a specific spending proposal affords in real terms at a specific slot. Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."*

**Refuter:** show that the financing link is actually broken for a floating-rate issuer (a true involuntary nominal default) and that real command is not. Mode `specified`. Triangulated: Fig 3 dependency order and Fig 12 bond vocabulary.

### F10: The figures return a shape, not a number

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*. No figure line returns a debt/GDP threshold, an inflation target, or a reserve ratio. What survives is the shape: a union of three conditions, one non-updating anchor per condition, a threshold comparison at each slot, and a dependency order that puts real command before external obligation.

**Refuter:** derive a universal numerical capacity bound from the figure set. Mode `general`. This is a declared `gap` of the shape-not-a-name kind, not a silence and not an invitation to invent a number.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the exemplar's discipline and the seat-map rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A floating-rate issuer with no foreign-currency debt and no legal bar to issuance is shown to have suffered an involuntary default on its own-currency obligations (breaks F2, Fig 6 :21).
2. Real resources are shown not to constrain spending at any level, with no inflation and no bottleneck at any slot (breaks F3, Fig 2 :29, Fig 13 :68).
3. A single aggregate fiscal statistic is shown sufficient to locate the binding constraint with no per-slot residual (breaks F4, Fig 1 :19, :22).
4. Foreign-currency obligations are shown dischargeable at will by the domestic issuer without import or foreign consent (breaks F5, Fig 6 :23).
5. Agreement among economists, with no new ground contact, is shown to settle the question (breaks F7, Fig 2 :35, :40).
6. A universal numerical capacity bound is derived from the figure set (this would overturn F10 and the declared gap).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or revised in a visible retraction, not smoothed over.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Answer the three domains separately and never average them: yes for nominal own-currency payment, no for real command under full capacity, no for foreign-currency obligations.
2. State the mode on every claim. "Cannot run out" is `general` structural for D1, not a `grounded` verdict about capacity.
3. Put dependency before importance (Fig 3 :20). Work the real-resource link before the financing link, and the external link after both.
4. Treat debt ratios as filters for attention, never as witnesses (Fig 9 :60).
5. Index the residual per slot (Fig 1 :19). Ask which specific resource or obligation is full.
6. Send the generator, the separator and the refuter, not a conclusion (Fig 4 :23, *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*).

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is "fiscal capacity" one quantity?** No. Union of nominal, real and external conditions (Fig 9 :55).
2. **Can the issuer run out of own-currency units?** No, inside D1's closure (Fig 6 :21). That is a shape, not a capacity verdict.
3. **Can it run out of real command?** Yes, at the slot where finiteness binds (Fig 13 :68, Fig 2 :29).
4. **Can it run out of foreign exchange?** Yes, if obligations are in atoms it does not hold (Fig 6 :23).
5. **Which link breaks first?** The real-command link; financing already works (Fig 3 :19).
6. **Does agreement settle it?** No. Rooms do not vote the ground (Fig 7 :35, Fig 2 :35).
7. **What is owed?** A per-slot discriminator with a non-updating anchor and a pre-registered refuter, not another argument.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives and is sharpened. The single-stock reading is refused. Nominal impossibility of default is a closure fact, real-resource and external constraints are ground facts, and they are not averaged. Strains after re-census: two live, named above; none hidden. No numerical bound is returned; that is a declared gap, which is a verdict and not a silence.
