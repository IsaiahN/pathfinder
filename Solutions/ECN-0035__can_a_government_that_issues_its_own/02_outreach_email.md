# Outreach draft: check the separator before you decide about the claim

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the union framing in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F1 to F3 is intact. If the recipient is handed a single "yes or no" verdict, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** One output to check about fiscal capacity, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The ordinary question "can a currency issuer run out of fiscal capacity?" bundles three different conditions that have different anchors. Split them before answering:
>
> 1. **Own-currency payment.** For a government that issues its own currency with a floating exchange rate, there is no involuntary nominal default. This is true as a statement about what the government can compose inside its own accounting closure. It is not a statement about what the units can buy. My mode on this is `general`, structural.
> 2. **Real command.** The binding domestic constraint is real labour, materials and energy at the specific slot. New units add no real atom. Inflation is the signal that a slot is full, and the signal is per slot, not an average.
> 3. **External obligation.** A promise in a currency the government cannot author is an atom it does not hold. It can only be met by export, reserves or a foreign counterparty's consent.
>
> The claim is that these form a **union of overlapping conditions, not one stock**. One lever, creating more of the government's own currency, clears the first condition and leaves the other two standing. So "cannot run out" and "can run out" are each correct about a different quantity, and averaging them produces no usable answer.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that deficits never matter, that inflation is impossible, that foreign-currency debt is harmless, or that any debt-to-GDP number is a threshold. I am also not claiming that a majority of economists settles the question. Agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool, so a survey result is a filter for attention, not a witness.
>
> **What would refute the separator.** I wrote these down before sending this:
> 1. an involuntary default on own-currency debt by a floating-rate issuer with no foreign-currency obligations and no legal bar to issuance;
> 2. an economy where spending can rise without limit and without any real-resource or price effect at any slot;
> 3. a single aggregate statistic that locates the binding constraint with no per-slot residual left over;
> 4. a foreign-currency obligation discharged at will by the domestic issuer without import or foreign consent.
>
> If any one holds, the separator is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
