## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Fiscal capacity" is a union of three conditions with different anchors, not one stock. For a floating-rate issuer, payment in its own currency cannot fail by scarcity of its own units; that is a closure fact and nothing more. Real command can fail at the specific slot, where finiteness makes the counting terminate, and inflation is the reading of that failure. External obligation can fail where the promise is in a currency the issuer cannot author. The conditions overlap, so one lever clears the first and leaves the others standing; the dependency order puts real command before external obligation and nominal financing last. Debt ratios are filters for attention, never witnesses for the verdict. The framework returns this shape, its refuters and the instruments that would close it, not a number.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 6, 9 and 13

- **Statement:** the bundle is a union of three conditions, and no figure line returns a universal capacity number.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55, *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57, *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Fig 9 :32, *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- **Refuter:** show that one measure suffices to locate the binding constraint in all three conditions with no overlap left over, or derive a universal numerical bound from the figure set.

### C2: the nominal claim is a closure fact, not a ground verdict

- **Statement:** a government that authors its currency can always author another unit, so it cannot be forced into involuntary default on own-currency obligations for want of its own units. This says nothing about real command or foreign obligations.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :21, *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* Fig 6 :25, *"An import adds no atom to the world."* Fig 10 :47, *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."*
- **What to check:** is the claim being used only about the nominal closure, or has it been promoted into a verdict about real capacity? World check: Wray writes that *"a sovereign government can always make any payment on debt denominated in its own currency"*, and the Modern Money Network concedes that such governments *"face price stability constraints, but not solvency constraints per se."*
- **Refuter:** an involuntary own-currency default by a floating-rate issuer with no foreign-currency debt and no legal bar to issuance.

### C3: the binding domestic constraint is real and per slot

- **Statement:** the domestic anchor is real labour, materials and energy; the residual is indexed per slot; inflation is the reading of a full slot; the external constraint is a separate closure question.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :68, *"finiteness in a finite region is what makes the counting terminate."* Fig 1 :19, *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 2 :29, *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 6 :23, *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*
- **What to check:** name the specific slot at which the constraint would bind, and the instrument that would read it. An aggregate ratio is not an instrument. World checks: the original-sin literature on borrowing abroad in domestic currency; carrying-capacity measurement failing where resources vary by region; reports of Japan's labour shortage and Sawyer's "right quantities and places" objection.
- **Refuter:** show an economy with unlimited spending and no real or price effect at any slot, or a single aggregate statistic that locates the constraint with no per-slot residual.
