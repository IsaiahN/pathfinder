## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, whether a government that issues its own currency can run out of fiscal capacity and which constraints matter most. Option A: treat "fiscal capacity" as one stock and answer yes or no. Option B: treat it as a union of three conditions with different anchors and give a per-domain verdict. What each leads to: A produces a slogan that is right on one subset and wrong on the others, and it invites a debate about agreement rather than a check against a ground. B produces a separator, a dependency order, refuters written in advance, and a declared gap where no figure line returns a number. The figures decide B.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The apparent contradiction between the impossibility of involuntary nominal default for a floating-rate issuer and the evident reality of real-resource and external constraints. Arity: three conditions plus a non-world verification condition, so a **union compound** (Fig 9 :55, *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*). Symmetry: "run out" is a threshold comparison, ⋛, not an additive stock (Fig 12 :87). Scale: first order inside the domestic closure, second order at the real and external interfaces.
- **D1, domestic nominal finance.** Actors: treasury, central bank, bond holders, currency users. Currency: the government's own nominal units, a convention the issuer authors. Anchor: none inside the frame that can settle the capacity claim, because its own balance sheet updates on what it does (Fig 2 :30, *"you push on it, it is not an anchor."*). Question type: a closure fact, `general`.
- **D2, real resources.** Actors: workers, firms, materials, energy, productive capacity. Currency: real labour and materials. Anchor: prices, physical availability and output, a market that does not move because the government declares success. Question type: threshold at a slot, `specified`.
- **D3, external obligation.** Actors: foreign holders, reserve-currency issuers, clearing systems, exchange rate. Currency: foreign exchange the issuer cannot author. Anchor: the foreign sector's holding decision and the exchange rate. Question type: closure contact, `specified`.
- **D4, verification / discourse.** Actors: economists, journals, institutes. Currency: agreement and prestige. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Question type: seat rule, `general`.
- **Partition or union: union.** The conditions overlap. A sovereign issuer can carry foreign-currency debt; an issuer with nominal room can be at full real capacity. Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* One lever clears the D1-only region and leaves the intersections standing.
- **Order by dependency.** Nominal financing works; real command is the break; external obligation is downstream (Fig 3 :19, *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*). Refuse the single-stock reading before minting an answer.

Strain test applied: my first pass read "cannot run out" directly from Fig 6 :21, which fits D1 and strains against D2 and D3. Re-census assigned that line to nominal composition only. After re-census the apparent contradiction dissolves. Two live strains remain and are named in section VERDICT.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The single-stock reading is refused. The shape survives: fiscal capacity is a union of a nominal closure fact, a real-resource threshold at a slot, and an external closure contact, with a dependency order that puts real command first and nominal financing last. Nominal own-currency default cannot occur for want of the issuer's own units; real command and external obligation can fail. Debt ratios and expert consensus are filters, not witnesses. No universal number is returned; that is a declared `gap`, of the shape-not-a-name kind, reported as unreached rather than as silence. Everything here is `specified` on the domains and `general` on the structure, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode, four refuters and the union framing before any conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending a slogan instead of a separator. Fig 2 :42 prices this: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The single-stock reading is removable without touching the figure derivation; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove it. No primitive has to be withdrawn, and a sent claim can be superseded by the corrected packet.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A per-slot instrument that discriminates real-resource from nominal-finance constraints would settle the binding condition; an involuntary own-currency default by a floating-rate issuer with no foreign-currency debt would break D1; an economy with unlimited non-inflationary spending at every slot would break D2; a foreign-currency obligation discharged at will without import would break D3; a derivation of a universal numerical capacity bound would close the gap; and an instrument showing ⋛'s operands to be the same quantity type at one level would clear S1. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
