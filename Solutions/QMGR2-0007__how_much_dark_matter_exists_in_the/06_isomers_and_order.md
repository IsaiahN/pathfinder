# Supporting derivation: structure, order and analogues

**Mode: `specified`.** A simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6). No mass value is produced.

## RESIDUAL

The main audit leaves three structural questions open. (1) What kind of structure is a "how much" answer over a population of faint systems? (2) What does "smallest" mean under the framework's level rule? (3) Are two clumps with the same mass but different internal arrangement interchangeable? If they are, a single mass number would be enough; if they are not, mass alone cannot answer the question.

## DERIVATION

### Q1. A mass function is a spectrum, not a list

- Fig 13 :19-20: "spectrum" / "ordering, so more and less can be said".
- A list of dwarf masses does not say more or less; a mass function does. The world counterpart is Bullock et al. 2017: "The comoving number density of dark matter halos rises steeply towards small masses" with a power-law slope near -1.9 (their section 1.3.2).
- ⇒ The structure that governs "how much across the class" is a spectrum. It is already among the six, so this is a binding onto a held atom, not an import.
- **Falsifier:** if the faint systems cannot be ordered by mass in a way that predicts anything (that is, the ordering carries no information), the spectrum reading fails and the answer degenerates to an unstructured list.

### Q2. Halos form a network, and contact cascades

- Fig 13 :23-24: "network" / "that entities relate, and influence travels".
- Fig 11 :52 "And contact cascades." and Fig 11 :52-53 "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."
- ⇒ A clump is not an isolated slot. It sits in a network of accretion, stripping and tidal influence. A mass measured inside one aperture is a local reading of a networked object. The relevant residual can be several links out, so a one-step census of "nearby" factors is not a census.
- **Falsifier:** if a clump's measured mass and profile are invariant under changes in its tidal environment and accretion history, the network reading adds nothing and should be dropped.

### Q3. "Smallest" is level-relative

- Fig 12 :104 "irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"; Fig 4 :33 "Which is why there is no top and no infinite regress."
- ⇒ "Smallest galaxy" names a level at which something composes, not a privileged atom of the world. The physical cutoff is a different question, imported from particle physics (main audit F2 and F8).
- **Falsifier:** derive a physical minimum halo mass from the six-term set alone. Then the level rule does not constrain this question and this subsection is withdrawn.

### Q4. Are two clumps with the same mass isomers?

- Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :97-99 "This is not an analogy. Chemistry hit it first: silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different properties, which is what forced structure into the notation."
- Fig 12 :124 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Two systems with identical total dark matter mass but different internal arrangement (a cuspy profile versus a cored one) are not interchangeable under Fig 12. The world report supports the distinction: AAS Nova (Lexi Gault, 2024-11-01) states that "a cored dark matter distribution agrees with the observations" for six ultra-faint dwarfs and that "the expected cusp profile fit is significantly different from the observed properties of these small galaxies." Same nominal mass class, different behaviour.
- ⇒ The isomer claim here is `specified`, not `grounded`: same mass budget, different bond (profile and tidal history), different substance. The weaker and safer claim is that they are members of one functional group (dark-matter-dominated faint system) with different qualifiers.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if measured behaviour (velocity dispersion, survival, profile) is fully determined by total mass alone, with no residual left for arrangement, the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim stands.

### Q5. What order is the "how much" question?

- Operators table :48-51: ⋛ "a threshold relation between two quantities", identified by "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."
- Fig 12 :111-113 "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." A goal is a comparison; a cutoff question is a comparison of a mass scale against a threshold, not a conjunction of two masses.
- Fig 12 :103 "Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible." The cutoff, once imported and settled, becomes an atom for the next question about smaller systems, so the loop can repeat.
- ⇒ The order is: measure per-slot masses (spectrum), arrange them (network), compare the low end against an imported threshold (⋛), record the lineage, repeat. A flat formula that lists ingredients without the threshold cannot state the cutoff question at all.
- **Falsifier:** if the low-mass end is shown to be a symmetric conjunction of independent conditions rather than a threshold on one mass scale, the ⋛ mapping is mistyped and must be withdrawn.

## CONCLUSION

The answer's structure is a spectrum (mass function) embedded in a network (hierarchical halos), read at a level that is not a physical bottom, and compared against an imported cutoff through a threshold relation. Two faint systems with the same mass but different arrangement are isomers under Fig 12, not interchangeable instances of one number. This rescues the structural content of "how much" without importing a mass value from the figures. The falsifiers are stated above and were written before this conclusion.
