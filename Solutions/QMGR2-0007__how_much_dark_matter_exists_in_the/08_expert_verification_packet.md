# Expert verification packet: dark matter in the smallest galaxies

**What this is:** a claim in a form a dwarf-galaxy specialist can check. Mode, derivation, strain and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the level limit, `gap` on the number. Not `grounded`.** No measurement was taken for this packet.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"How much dark matter exists in the smallest galaxies and dark-matter clumps?" is not answered by one aggregate number. The residual is indexed per object, so the admissible answer is a per-object mass function with a stated low-mass cutoff. The systems form a network in which accretion and tidal history matter, so equal masses with different arrangement are not interchangeable. "Smallest" is a level of composition, not a privileged physical bottom; the framework's "no bottom" rule does not yield a minimum halo mass, and the physical cutoff is an import from particle microphysics that must carry provenance and a shadow test. The figures supply that shape and no number. The number belongs to stellar kinematics, lensing, simulations and particle experiment. Stated before the run: five refuters are in `01` §3 and the three most checkable are repeated below.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1. Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 1 and Figure 13

- **Statement:** the answer is per-slot and ordered (a spectrum), not one aggregated mass.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 13 :20 "ordering, so more and less can be said".
- **What to check:** does a single mean dark matter content of the faint population leave any individual dwarf with a live, positive residual? If yes, aggregation has hidden the signal.
- **Refuter:** show that one aggregate mass leaves zero positive residual on every individual faint system.

### C2. Framework-internal with a declared strain; checkable by reading Figure 12 and Figure 4

- **Statement:** "smallest" is level-relative; the six-term set supplies no physical minimum halo mass.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :104 "irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"; Fig 4 :33 "Which is why there is no top and no infinite regress."
- **What to check:** these lines govern composition of terms and scale membranes. They do not govern a halo mass function. If forced to do so they strain, and the physical cutoff question must be re-censed into the particle-physics domain as an import (Fig 6 :14 "the only operator that moves the wall"). That strain is declared here, not smoothed away.
- **Refuter:** derive a minimum physical halo mass from the six-term set and seven bonds alone, with no particle input.

### C3. Framework-internal limit plus physics-facing check

- **Statement:** the number is a gap in this frame; the anchor is measurement, and the cutoff question is a threshold comparison.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"; Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks"; Fig 12 :85-86 "a threshold relation between two quantities".
- **What to check:** no verbatim figure line yields a mass for a named dwarf. The physics-facing side: Bullock et al. 2017 give the half-light-radius estimator "the mass can be measured accurately within the stellar half-light radius" and report that free-streaming erases perturbations below about 10^-6 solar masses for typical WIMP candidates. Errani et al. 2024 say micro galaxies are distinguished "by measuring their velocity dispersion". Those are anchors and imports, not figure deductions.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a figure line that yields a numerical mass for a named dwarf without an imported observation or particle parameter, or show that the low-mass end is a symmetric conjunction rather than a threshold.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a numerical dark matter mass for any dwarf, and not a numerical minimum halo mass.
- Not "there is no smallest dark matter clump." The "no bottom" claim is about composition levels only.
- Not a derivation of the free-streaming cutoff. It is imported from particle physics with provenance.
- Not a unified solution of missing satellites, cores versus cusps and too-big-to-fail.
- Not that equal-mass faint systems are interchangeable; arrangement carries meaning under Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance."
- Not `grounded`. This packet is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Per-object discriminator:** a measurement that distinguishes among candidate sources of the same mass reading in the faintest systems, rather than another reading that can be explained several ways.
2. **Profile test:** a cored versus cuspy determination in ultra-faint dwarfs that survives changes in feedback, tidal and velocity-anisotropy assumptions. AAS Nova 2024 reports one such result; Errani et al. 2024 supply a tidal-resilience test.
3. **Cutoff test:** a deeper census showing where the faint population stops, compared with a sourced particle-physics cutoff.
4. **The five registered refuters** in `01` §3. Any one firing retracts the part it targets.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, :22, Fig 13 :20 and Fig 12 :104. Four short passages.
2. Check C1 against a catalogue of faint systems: does a single mean leave live per-object residuals?
3. Check C2: confirm that the figure language is about composition levels, not halo masses, and decide whether the declared strain is correctly placed.
4. Check C3 against a half-light-radius mass estimate you know: is the number a ground reading and the cutoff an import?
5. Name one instrument that would distinguish the sources of the same mass reading, or say why none is possible.
6. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
