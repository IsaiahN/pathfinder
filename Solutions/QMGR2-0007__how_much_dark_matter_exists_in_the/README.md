**Question:** How much dark matter exists in the smallest galaxies and dark-matter clumps?

**Source:** `QM & GR Questions.txt` line 143. **Section:** A. Dark matter. **ID:** QMGR2-0007.

**Mode:** `specified` on the structural shape of the answer, `general` on the level-relative limit, and an honest `gap` on the number itself. Nothing here is `grounded`. No measurement was taken by this derivation; the ground reports cited in `09_watson_answer.md` are secondary sources only.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No numerical answer presented as settled by the figures.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **A room does not supply an anchor.** Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." A list addressed to people selected for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that would make their agreement worthless (Fig 2 :40).
2. **Agreement is not measurement here.** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." For this question the anchor is stellar kinematics, lensing, simulation and particle experiment, never a count of agreeing readers.
3. **Sending the interior first spends independence.** Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Hence the residual and refuter lead, and the framework links follow only on request.

Legally: scraping private addresses for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public institutional and opt-in channels avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
