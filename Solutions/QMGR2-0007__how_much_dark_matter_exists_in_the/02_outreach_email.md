# Outreach draft: check the output first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the stage-1 claim below has been checked against at least one real kinematic mass estimate in the literature. The two-stage offer collapses if the first stage does not survive.

---

**Subject:** Check one claim about dark matter in the smallest galaxies before you decide whether the theory is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output about dark matter in dwarf galaxies that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output fails, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** "how much dark matter" is not one number for all faint systems. The residual is indexed per object, so the answer has the shape of a per-slot mass function with a stated low-mass cutoff. Each dwarf spheroidal or ultra-faint dwarf carries its own mass within its half-light radius, and the class of such objects stops somewhere set by microphysics. This is a shape and a route, not a measurement. I am not claiming this framework settles the amount.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the framework predicts a numerical cutoff, that it names the particle, that it merges the missing-satellites, core-cusp and too-big-to-fail problems into one, or that a count of agreeing readers would corroborate any of this. The cutoff is an import from particle physics and must carry its provenance. The number comes from your instruments, not from the framework.
>
> **A strain I am declaring:** the framework says there is no bottom to levels of composition. That does not license a claim about a minimum physical halo mass. The physical cutoff is imported, not derived here. I would rather state that than have you find it.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before this message:**
> 1. one aggregate dark matter mass leaves no positive residual on any individual faint system;
> 2. a minimum physical halo mass follows from the six-term set alone, with no particle input;
> 3. no instrument, at any resolution, is already returning a failing mass reading for the faintest systems;
> 4. a figure line yields a numerical mass for a named dwarf without imported data;
> 5. two groups reading the same catalogue and kinematics carry independent evidence for the number.
>
> If any holds, I retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. What would you count as a failure here? If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract used for every quote above): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the loop is in the last link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
