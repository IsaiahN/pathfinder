# The Tether's answer: How much dark matter exists in the smallest galaxies and dark-matter clumps?

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the level-relative limit, and `gap` on the number. Not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading. No mass was measured for this answer.

## 1. The residual it answers

For each faint system `s`, a model predicts a mass within an aperture and an observation returns one; `R_s = |Γ(b,a) − o′|` is the per-object residual (Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."). The question asks for the value of that quantity over the smallest galaxies and clumps. It bundles three overlapping conditions: what sets the low-mass end of the halo population, how much mass the faintest observed systems actually contain, and whether the two agree. Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Each condition keeps its own verdict; none is averaged with another.

## 2. The shape

1. **The answer is a distribution, not a number.** Fig 13 :20 "ordering, so more and less can be said" gives the structure: a spectrum of masses over named slots. Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A single mean for "the smallest galaxies" throws away the signal that says where to look.
2. **The systems sit in a network.** Fig 13 :23-24 "that entities relate, and influence travels." Accretion, stripping and tidal history matter; Fig 11 :52 "And contact cascades." A mass inside one aperture is a local reading of a networked object.
3. **"Smallest" is level-relative, not a physical bottom.** Fig 12 :104 "irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"; Fig 4 :33 "Which is why there is no top and no infinite regress." The framework does not supply a minimum halo mass. The physical cutoff is imported from particle microphysics with provenance and a shadow test (Fig 6 :12-14). This is the F2 strain, declared rather than hidden.
4. **"How much" is a threshold comparison.** Fig 12 :85-86 "a threshold relation between two quantities"; Operators table :48-51 identifies ⋛ by "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes." The cutoff question compares a mass scale against a threshold; it is not a conjunction.
5. **The number is not reachable from the figures.** Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"; Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." After a genuine re-census, no figure line yields a mass for a named dwarf. The verdict is `gap` in this frame. Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" says so plainly.
6. **The anchor is measurement.** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Stellar kinematics, lensing, simulation and particle experiment settle it. Agreement between readers does not (Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool").

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a numerical mass for any dwarf, and not a numerical minimum halo mass. The framework returns a shape.
- Not "there is no smallest dark matter clump." It says there is no bottom to composition levels; the physical cutoff is an empirical question.
- Not a derivation of the free-streaming scale. Bullock et al. 2017 supply that from particle physics; it is an import, recorded as such.
- Not one theory of the missing-satellites, core-cusp and too-big-to-fail problems. They are a union of conditions with separate loops.
- Not that two faint systems of equal mass are interchangeable. Under Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance", arrangement matters.
- Not `grounded`. No instrument was operated for this derivation.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

The ground is a source-discriminating set of instruments, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (read 2026-11-23): a reading "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer, not an argument, settled that case. Here:

1. **Per-object masses:** do measured masses within the half-light radius (Bullock et al. 2017 give `M(<r1/2) = 3 σ² r1/2 / G`) discriminate among dwarf candidates, or do several sources produce the same reading? A discriminator is owed.
2. **Profile test:** do the ultra-faint systems have cored or cuspy profiles? AAS Nova 2024 reports a cored fit fitting six systems better than the cusp; Errani et al. 2024 give a tidal-resilience test. Either would move the arrangement question toward settlement.
3. **Cutoff test:** does the faint-galaxy population stop at a mass consistent with an imported free-streaming scale, or continue below it? This requires a wider and deeper census, not a new argument.
4. **The five registered refuters** in `01` §3: any one firing retracts the corresponding part of the shape claim.

## 6. One-paragraph version

> The Tether does not produce a number for the dark matter in the smallest galaxies, and it does not claim one. It says that "how much" must be answered as a per-object mass distribution rather than a single average, that those objects sit in a network where tidal history matters, that "smallest" is a level of composition rather than a privileged physical bottom, and that the low-mass cutoff is a threshold comparison whose value must be imported from particle physics and then checked against stellar kinematics, lensing and simulation. The figures return that shape, not the mass. The framework's "no bottom" claim governs levels of composition, not halo masses; saying otherwise is a strain this answer records rather than hides. What is offered is the shape any answer must have, five refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close the gap. The number stays open until the ground settles it.
