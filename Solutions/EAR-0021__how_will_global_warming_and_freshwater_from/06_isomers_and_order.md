# Structure, order and analogues: why the overturning is a loop and the forcings are a union

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

We need to decide three structural questions that the main audit left open: (1) is the overturning correctly modelled as a cycle or a chain, (2) what is the order of the two forcing channels relative to the circulation, and (3) are the thermal and freshwater channels isomers, a functional group, or a union compound? These are questions about the bond, not about the measured trend.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Cycle or chain?

- Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
- The meridional overturning returns its output as input: northward surface flow produces deep water, which returns southward and upwells, feeding the surface flow again. A chain cannot represent that.
- RAPID's own account confirms the structural point at the ground: the conveyor picture was overturned because "wind-forcing plays a dominant role in MOC variability" and "meridional coherence of the MOC appears much more complicated than previously expected". A conveyor is a chain; the observed system is a loop with at least two forcing entries.
- Fig 12 :119 "catalyst. A part that survives its own use." The northward salt transport is a candidate catalyst: it is part of what maintains the density that lets the flow keep sinking. Whether it is regenerating or spent across a use is not recorded by a transport number and needs an instrument.
- **Conclusion:** cycle, `general` (structural), triangulated across Fig 12 and the RAPID account. The sign of the loop is the open quantity.

### Q2 - What is the order of the forcing channels?

- Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"
- Warming acts on temperature; freshwater acts on salinity; both act on density, and density acts on the gradient that drives the flow. The channels are not sequential (A then B) and they are not production (A produces B). They are two operands joined into one compound condition on the same water.
- Fig 12 :82 "A with B removed or absent" defines subtraction: progress is a subtraction. But here removing the freshwater channel does not leave the thermal case standing alone in the observed system, because warm fresh water is buoyant for both reasons. The compound is not a clean subtraction; it is a conjunction of two conditions on one operand.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3):** perception (transport measured) → vocabulary (density and buoyancy terms) → objective (what counts as a change) → planning (which array) → learn and carry. The historic break was at the objective/planning seam because there was no continuous transport instrument. RAPID and OSNAP mend it.
- **Conclusion:** union compound, not sequence and not partition. `specified`, triangulated.

### Q3 - Are the two forcing channels isomers?

- Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- As forcing channels, warming and freshwater share no atoms in the strict sense: one is a temperature change, the other a salinity change. Under Fig 12 :117 they are **not isomers**; they are two members of the same **functional group**, namely "buoyancy-lowering surface forcing", with different qualifiers. This is the weaker and more honest claim.
- The stronger isomer reading is available only if both are written over the full shared atom set {Space, Time, Energy, Gradient, Network, Spectrum}, differing only in which structure mediates which state pair. That is a `specified` hypothesis with no ground yet, and it should be labelled as such rather than asserted.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either channel can be shown to act without the atom the other requires (for example, a density change in which salinity is demonstrably irrelevant across the observed range, or vice versa), the functional-group claim survives but the shared-substrate isomer claim fails. If both channels are shown to act through the same measured buoyancy flux with no distinguishable qualifier, then even the functional-group distinction collapses and the compound becomes a single condition.

### Q4 - What order is the union of the observations?

- Fig 4 :19 "The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand." The buoyancy-to-flow rule is the same at the convection site and at the basin scale; only the currency changes (mixed-layer depth versus Sverdrups).
- Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." Proxy reconstruction carried up to "weak overturning" and brought down to an array expectation is a lossy transform. R_T must be measured.
- Fig 2 :31 "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." RAPID and OSNAP are two instruments pointed at related but different parts of the circulation; a paleo proxy and a reanalysis drawn from the same observations are not.
- **Conclusion:** the observation union is ordered by independence, not by count. More reanalyses built on the same observations add spelling, not evidence.

### Q5 - What is the analogue that does *not* apply?

The Library of Babel essay warns that a problem may be a bundle masquerading as one. The tempting analogue here is *the failing machine*: treat the circulation as a single engine whose output declines. That analogue fails because it is a chain, and chains cannot close (Fig 12 :121). The correct analogue is *a loop whose sign can change*, which is why the falsifiers in `01` and `08` are about the sign of the feedback response, not about the slope of a trend.

## CONCLUSION

The overturning is a cycle, not a chain; its feedback sign is the quantity that decides whether a perturbation damps or reinforces. The two forcing channels are a union compound and stand as members of one functional group (buoyancy-lowering surface forcing), not as isomers on the strict Fig 12 definition. The observation union is ordered by independence, not by the number of products. The "failing machine" analogue is refused as a chain. No new primitive, no import that moves the wall, and no collapse date is required for any of these conclusions.
