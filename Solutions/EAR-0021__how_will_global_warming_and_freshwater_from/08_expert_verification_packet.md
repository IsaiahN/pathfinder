# Expert verification packet - the Tether on warming, meltwater and ocean circulation

**What this is:** a claim in a form an oceanographer or climate physicist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No new measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The circulation is a flow composed from an energy state and a density gradient (Fig 13 :22 "a difference that can be spent"; Fig 13 :76 "Energy and a gradient compose a flow."). Surface warming and freshwater from melting ice both reduce the surface-to-deep density contrast, so both erode the gradient that drives deep convection. The overturning is a loop rather than a chain, and the sign of its feedback decides whether a perturbation damps or reinforces (Fig 12 :121). The response must be indexed per circulation system, never as one global scalar, because the live signal sits in particular array legs and slots. The two forcing channels form a union compound, not a partition and not two independent questions. The framework does not name a collapse year and does not produce a single global strength number; it returns the shape any answer must have, with refuters written in advance.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal plus physics-facing; checkable by reading Figure 13 and comparing to the stratification literature

- **Statement:** a circulation is a flow composed from an energy state and a density gradient; eroding the gradient erodes the flow. Both warming and freshening erode it.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :22 "a difference that can be spent"; Fig 13 :76 "Energy and a gradient compose a flow." Also Fig 13 :45 "What makes a gradient a gradient is that it can be spent".
- **What to check:** is the characterisation faithful? The IPCC assessment reports "The upper ocean has become more stably stratified since at least 1970 over the vast majority of the globe (virtually certain), primarily due to surface-intensified warming and high-latitude surface freshening (very high confidence)." Willeit et al. (2024) report that the buoyancy flux "depends on the surface freshwater and heat fluxes and on sea surface temperature through the temperature dependence of the thermal expansion coefficient of seawater." Do those two mechanisms act on the same density gradient the figure names, or on different ones?
- **Refuter:** a sustained direct measurement in which the density gradient rises or holds in a convection region while both warming and local freshwater flux increase there, with no compensating wind or heat-flux change.

### C2 - physics-facing; checkable against the overturning observations and feedback estimates

- **Statement:** the overturning is a cycle, not a chain, and the quantity that matters is the sign of its feedback. A scalar trend cannot report the sign.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Also Fig 12 :119 "catalyst. A part that survives its own use."
- **What to check:** RAPID reports a downturn in 2010-11 "that marked the beginning of a sustained decline since that time" and also that "wind-forcing plays a dominant role in MOC variability, where previously it was believed to primarily respond to buoyancy forcing at high latitudes." Is the observed variability better described as a weakening chain or as a loop with competing feedback signs? Is the northward salt transport a regenerating or a spent part across a use?
- **Refuter:** a well-observed basin in which a freshwater anomaly produces the opposite sign of response to its feedback structure, or repeated same-sign anomalies produce opposite responses with unchanged background state.

### C3 - framework-internal and observation-facing; checkable against the array data

- **Statement:** the residual is indexed per system; a global scalar hides the live slot; the two forcing channels are a union compound.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- **What to check:** OSNAP reports monthly MOC 2014-2020 of 16.51 ± 3.3 Sv for the full array, 2.72 ± 1.34 Sv for West and 16.35 ± 2.91 Sv for East. Does a single basin number preserve the information needed to decide the question, or is the East/West split the operative fact? Do warming and freshwater forcing combine additively in the models you use, or is there an interaction term?
- **Refuter:** independent subtropical and subpolar systems showing high correlation after noise removal so one scalar predicts both, or controlled results showing the two channels combining additively with no interaction across the observed range.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a collapse date, not a probability, not a schedule. Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself".
- Not a replacement for the IPCC AR6 assessment. The shape is compatible with "very likely decline" and "medium confidence that the decline will not involve an abrupt collapse before 2100".
- Not "the global conveyor belt is weakening" as one sentence. That scalar has no anchor and averages away the slots.
- Not that the thermal and freshwater channels are independent problems. They are a compound (Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either").
- Not that the two channels are strict isomers. They are members of one functional group under Fig 12 :124; the stronger reading is a labelled hypothesis with a falsifier.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved the case. Here:

1. **Gradient test:** sustained direct measurement of the density contrast in a convection region against warming and freshwater flux, with wind and heat flux separated.
2. **Sign-of-loop test:** the response of a well-observed basin to freshwater anomalies of known sign, repeated under unchanged background conditions.
3. **Slot test:** the correlation structure between independent subtropical and subpolar indices after noise removal.
4. **Compound test:** the presence or absence of an interaction term between the thermal and freshwater channels.
5. **Channel test:** whether reanalysis disagreement survives contact with independent instruments and the paleo archive.
