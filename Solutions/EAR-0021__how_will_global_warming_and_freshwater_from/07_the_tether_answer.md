# The Tether's answer on warming, meltwater and ocean circulation

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

For each major circulation system, the predicted circulation state under a warming and freshening forcing differs from what the observing channels return. That gap is indexed per system and ordered in time. It is a union compound: the thermal and freshwater channels act on the same water mass, and their overlap (warm fresh buoyant surface water) means one lever cannot clear both conditions. Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."

## 2. The shape

1. **The circulation is a flow composed from an energy state and a density gradient.** Fig 13 :22 "a difference that can be spent"; Fig 13 :76 "Energy and a gradient compose a flow." Warming and meltwater both reduce the surface-to-deep density contrast, so both erode the gradient that drives deep convection and the overturning. The world assessment says the same thing in its own terms: stratification has increased since at least 1970, "primarily due to surface-intensified warming and high-latitude surface freshening".
2. **The overturning is a loop, not a chain.** Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." The question is therefore not only how far the circulation declines but what the sign of its feedback is: whether a freshwater perturbation is damped or reinforced. A scalar trend cannot tell you the sign.
3. **The response must be indexed per system.** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." The subpolar array reports East 16.35 Sv and West 2.72 Sv for 2014 to 2020; RAPID reports wind dominance and imperfect meridional coherence. A single "conveyor strength" averages the live slot into a quiet one.
4. **A gradient over space and a gradient over time are different terms.** Fig 13 :76 "A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is." Seasonal and latitudinal density structure are not interchangeable slots; collapsing them loses the distinction the question asks about.
5. **The channels are a functional group, not strict isomers.** Under Fig 12 :117 they share no atoms as forcings; under Fig 12 :124 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." they are two members of one buoyancy-lowering group. The stronger shared-substrate isomer reading is a labelled hypothesis, not a result (see `06_isomers_and_order.md` Q3).
6. **The framework returns a shape, not a date.** Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." No year of collapse and no single global number are derivable. The public assessment itself combines a very likely decline with medium confidence in no abrupt collapse before 2100 and low confidence in the quantitative rate. Our shape is compatible with that and adds an instruction about where to look.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a collapse forecast, not a date, not a probability. The framework has no instrument that produces a schedule.
- Not "the global conveyor belt is weakening" as one sentence. That scalar has no anchor and averages away the per-slot signal (Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average").
- Not a claim that the two channels are independent problems. They are a union compound.
- Not a replacement for the IPCC assessment. It is a structural constraint on what any answer must look like, not a competing estimate.
- Not grounded. No measurement was taken in this derivation.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it, not a debate. Here the discriminating questions are:

- **Gradient test:** does a sustained direct record show the surface-to-deep density contrast falling in a convection region while both warming and freshwater flux rise there, with no compensating wind or heat-flux change? If not, F1 fires.
- **Sign-of-loop test:** does a freshwater anomaly produce the response its feedback structure predicts, and do repeated same-sign anomalies produce the same sign of response with unchanged background state? If not, F2 fires.
- **Slot test:** after noise removal, do independent subtropical and subpolar systems move together so one global scalar predicts both? If yes, F3 fires.
- **Compound test:** do the thermal and freshwater channels combine with a measurable interaction term across the observed range? If not, F6 fires.
- **Channel test:** is a disagreement between reanalyses a channel problem or a circulation change? Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Independent instruments and the paleo archive decide.
- **Standing census refuter:** any admitted domain loses its nameable anchor on re-census.

## 5. The honest stop

**Reached with instrument limits, not unreachable.** Fig 6 :40 "What is unavailable is a sensor for something that has never registered anywhere, at any resolution." Here sensors exist: RAPID has sustained a record for more than 20 years, OSNAP reports a full-water-column subpolar record, Argo profiles the upper ocean, and paleo archives supply millennial regimes. So the circulation registers. What does not yet register is the sign of the feedback loop and the exact partition of the observed change among wind, buoyancy and channel effects. The answer stops at that limit and records the lineage. It does not convert a shape into a name. Fig 7 :13 "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not": a hundred agreeing models do not close the gap that one maintained array can.

The six-term substrate is a visible choice, not nature's own list (*They're Scanning Books*: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."*). Declaring it makes the derivation auditable. It does not make the answer final, and there is no last room.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not predict when the Atlantic overturning will collapse and does not replace the IPCC assessment. It says that the circulation is a flow made from an energy state and a density gradient, so warming and meltwater both erode the gradient that drives deep convection; that the overturning is a loop whose feedback sign decides whether a perturbation damps or reinforces, which a single strength number cannot show; and that the response must be tracked per circulation system, because the subpolar signal sits mostly in one leg of the array and wind forcing dominates parts of the variability. The two forcing channels are a compound, not two separate problems, and they stand as members of one buoyancy-lowering group rather than strict isomers. What the framework offers is the shape any answer must have, four refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The sign of the loop stays open until the arrays settle it.
