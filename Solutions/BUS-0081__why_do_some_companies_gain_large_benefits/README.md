## The residual in one line

`R_s = |Gamma(b,a) - o'|` indexed by firm-and-workflow slot `s`, never by industry average (Fig 1 :19, :22). The puzzle is not the purchase. It is why the same tool atom leaves one slot's residual small and another's large.

## Key verdict (from the figures, not from the world literature)

Buying similar tools is buying similar **ingredients**. The benefit lives in the **bonds and the arrangement** the firm builds around them (Fig 12 :5, :46, :94, :117) and in whether the firm keeps a channel to a non-updating ground (Fig 2 :29, Fig 10 :24) while moving its reach through contact with closures that differ (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :32). The question is a **union** of conditions, not one lever, so per-domain verdicts are kept separate and never averaged (Fig 9 :54-57, :60). Which particular tool or configuration wins is a declared `gap`: the framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134).

Three reasons, using the figures' own words: a room does not add an anchor (Seat Map :283); high correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention (Seat Map :163; Fig 2 :39-41); and a verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it (Fig 10 :42).
