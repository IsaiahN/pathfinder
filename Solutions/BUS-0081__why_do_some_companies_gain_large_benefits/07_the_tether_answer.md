# The Tether's answer on unequal benefit from similar technology

**Mode: `specified` on the firm-level claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the named winner. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading. No firm was measured.

## 1. The residual it answers

Two firms buy the same tool atom. In one workflow slot the gap between what the firm's routines predict and what the market returns stays small. In another it grows. Restated as effect: why does `R_s = |Gamma(b,a) - o'|` stay small in some slots and grow in others after an identical atom enters both? R is indexed per firm-and-workflow slot and per time, never averaged (Fig 1 :19, :22). The anchor is the market, explicitly named as an anchor in Fig 2 :29, not adoption counts or internal sentiment.

The purchase is not the explanation. Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*

## 2. The shape, one loop per domain, kept separate

**D1, the adoption loop.** Capability is a property of firm-and-habitat, never of the tool alone (Fig 11 :39-40). The highest-value diagnostic is which link the chain stops at and whether that was measured (Fig 3 :29). Buying and deploying is step-4 work; the break is usually at vocabulary and objective, steps 2 and 3 (Fig 3 :24-27). Verdict `specified`, triangulated (Fig 11 and Fig 4).

**D2, the market habitat.** The market settles; internal agreement does not (Fig 2 :29, :35). A tool's valence is relative to the residual: the same tool is a benefactor to one firm and a malfactor to another (Fig 11 :49-51), so no standing list of success factors survives (Fig 11 :51). Verdict `specified`, figure-governed for the refusal of the standing list.

**D3, the composition library.** Same atoms under different bonds are isomers: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour"* (Fig 12 :117). Over a declared shared atom set, the two configurations are isomers; where the sets differ, the weaker functional-group reading holds (Fig 12 :124). Reach moves only on contact with a closure that differs (Fig 6 :23), and a bought tool is a head start or a sourced import, never an automatic wall-moving atom (Fig 4 :22-24, Fig 8 :50-52). Verdict `specified`, triangulated (Fig 6, Fig 8, Fig 12).

**D4, the measurement channel.** Quick loss is channel failure, not ground decay: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"* (Fig 10 :24). The named decay modes are stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics and pooled readings (Fig 10 :26). Sustained benefit is a chain of handoffs with two factors, bracket fidelity and ground pressure (Fig 7 :37-39); if either goes to zero the firm runs in place. Verdict `specified`, triangulated (Fig 10 and Fig 7).

**Across domains, the union.** The five conditions (arrangement, vocabulary/objective, channel, reach, habitat reproduction) are a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate causes (Fig 9 :54-57). One lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. Verdicts are kept separate and never averaged (Fig 9 :60).

**The `gap`.** Which particular tool, configuration or percentage wins is not governed by any figure line after a genuine re-census. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The missing thing is a per-slot source-discriminating instrument, characterized in effect terms but not held here.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a list of winning tools, vendors, operating models or maturity levels. No figure returns a name.
- Not a percentage. A variance share or an average productivity effect is a filter for the budget, not a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).
- Not "technology does not matter." A tool that changes contact, for example by making an unreadable customer response readable, can matter greatly (Fig 6 :26 read with Fig 11 :40). What is refused is benefit as a property of the tool alone.
- Not a theory of digital transformation. No final room; the question stays a union with its own ground.
- Not `grounded`. No per-slot market measurement settles `left(R,phi)` here.
- Not a claim that complementarity, absorptive capacity or dynamic capabilities are framework primitives. They are business-pool bindings onto held bonds and reach, provenanced as such and carrying zero verdict weight until a shadow test is supplied (Fig 8 :50-52).

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A per-slot discriminator, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

1. **Arrangement test.** Do two slots with a declared shared atom set and identical typed bonds show different realized market benefits? If yes, F1's arrangement claim fails.
2. **Habitat test.** Does a firm's benefit survive a habitat substitution that removes the market's payment for what the tool does? If yes, the capability claim fails (Fig 11 :39-40).
3. **Channel test.** Is benefit lost while bracket fidelity and ground pressure both measure high and non-declining? If yes, the channel mechanism is not the cause (Fig 7 :37-39).
4. **Import test.** Does a tool that adds no atom and makes no search cheaper keep paying across a habitat change? If yes, idempotence fails (Fig 8 :32).
5. **Pilot test.** Does a pilot's success transfer with no reproduced habitat condition and no inherited unwanted actor? Under Fig 11 :66-70 that should be impossible.
6. **Union test.** Does one intervention restore all five conditions with no categorically different residue? If yes, the union collapses into a partition (Fig 9 :57).

All six were written before the conclusion and are repeated in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` and `09_watson_answer.md`.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The tool vocabulary a firm declares is *"a human's careful guess"* at what matters; declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral. Another closure's minimum set need not be these six. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."* The low-overlap witnesses (robotics reality gap, Rogers compatibility, Bainbridge's automation irony) corroborate the **mechanism shape** from different fields; they do not measure any firm's benefit and are not a second witness for the firm-level verdict.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name a winning technology and does not claim that tools are irrelevant. It says that buying similar tools is buying similar ingredients, and the benefit lives in the bonds and the arrangement around them: two firms with the same tool atom are isomers over a shared set, same atoms and different substance, where the difference is invisible in the purchase order. It says capability is a property of firm and habitat, so a tool changes nothing unless it changes contact with what the market pays for. It says quick loss is a failure of the channel (stale metrics, pooled readings, lost touch), not a decay of the ground, and that reach moves only through contact with a closure that differs, with a provenance and a shadow test for anything imported. It says a pilot substitutes a habitat, so its success proves wiring and not capability, and that the whole question is a union of five conditions that must be kept separate, never averaged into one benefit score. What it offers is that shape, the failure conditions written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. Which tool wins stays open until the ground settles it.
