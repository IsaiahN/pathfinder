## Two things in the literature that bear directly on this question

1. **The complementarity finding is about bonds, not about tool quality.** Brynjolfsson and Hitt call organizational investments a large influence on IT value, and the J-curve paper says GPTs *"enable and require significant complementary investments."* In figure terms these are the typed bonds around the tool atom (Fig 12 :46) and the imports that pass a source gate (Fig 8 :50-52). Do not present them as a framework result; they are a business-pool corroboration with one witness.
2. **Averaged productivity statistics cannot settle a per-slot question.** Solow's aphorism and the J-curve both live at the level of the economy. Fig 1 :22 says aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average, and Fig 9 :60 says filters are for the budget, never the verdict. Use the statistics to decide where to look, not to declare which firm gained.
