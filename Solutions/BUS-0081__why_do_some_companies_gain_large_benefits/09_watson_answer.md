## QUESTION

Why do some companies gain large benefits from new technology while others gain little despite buying similar tools?

Restated as an effect: why does the residual `R_s = |Gamma(b,a) - o'|` stay small in some firm-and-workflow slots and grow in others when the same tool atom enters both? R is indexed per slot and per time, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19, :22). Anchor: the market, named as an anchor in Fig 2 :29. Option A says the benefit is a property of the tool or of the buying firm's attributes. Option B says the benefit is a property of the arrangement and of contact, expressed as a union of conditions with per-slot instruments. Option A sends an ingredient list as an explanation and spends the independence of the people checking it. Option B sends a shape, written refuters, and an open slot until a ground settles it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Arity: a three-place relation (firm `Gamma`, action `a`, market return `o'`), indexed by slot and ordered by time; it carries an ordered bond and a comparison bond, not symmetric ones (Fig 12 :72, :87). On symmetry, the benefit threshold is relative to a baseline and to competitors. Scale: the single firm loop, the population ranking, and the time channel across adoption and diffusion; the membrane between scales is lossy (Fig 4 :21, :30).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The figure-derived verdict: a newly bought technology pays off when the firm composes it into its library with the right bonds over a declared atom set, keeps a channel to a paying ground that does not update on the firm's own report, renews its reach through contact with closures that differ under a source gate and shadow test, and reproduces the habitat that made any pilot succeed; benefit is lost quickly when any of these fails, and a tool's valence can flip when the residual changes. The five conditions are kept separate per domain and never averaged. Which particular tool or configuration wins is a declared `gap`, recorded as unreached rather than silence. Mode `specified` on the firm claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`. The world literature corroborates the mechanism from several fields but does not settle any per-slot verdict.

- **cost if wrong:** misdirected diagnosis. A firm failing reach renewal would be sold channel instruments, a firm failing channel would be told to import more, a firm failing arrangement would be told to buy a better tool, and one lever would be applied to a union that needs several. In outreach terms, an expert's twenty minutes would be spent refuting a shape that was never the right shape. The cost is real, bounded and largely reversible. The deeper cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending a recording of our conclusion instead of the generator, which Fig 2 :42 prices: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*
- **undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails; a refutation is added, and the reach falls because *"something was added rather than because anything was deleted"* (Fig 6 :42-43). The shape and its nine falsifiers are recorded with provenance so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-64). No firm has to withdraw a tool on the strength of this packet.
- **reversed by:** any of the six pre-registered falsifiers, in particular (1) identical atoms and bonds with different benefits, (2) benefit unchanged after the market stops paying, (3) benefit lost while bracket fidelity and ground pressure both measure high, (4) a tool adding no atom and no cheaper search that keeps paying across a habitat change, (5) a pilot transferring with no reproduced habitat condition, or (6) one intervention restoring all conditions with no categorically different residue. A source with a genuinely different evidence pool showing that per-slot benefit is fully predicted by averaged industry effects would also reverse it. None has fired.
