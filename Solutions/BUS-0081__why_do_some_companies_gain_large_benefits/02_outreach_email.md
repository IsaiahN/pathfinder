**Subject:** One checkable separator for the "same tools, different benefit" problem

> Hi [name],
>
> I work on information systems and productivity questions. I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (`specified`, not `grounded`).** Take `R_s = |Gamma(b,a) - o'|` per firm-and-workflow slot, never as an industry average. The puzzle in "why do some companies gain large benefits from new technology while others gain little despite buying similar tools?" is not the purchase. Two firms buy the same atom. The question is why one slot's residual stays small and the other's grows. The figures I am using say the answer is not in the tool. Buying similar tools is buying similar ingredients; the benefit lives in the bonds and the arrangement around them, in whether the firm keeps a channel to a ground that does not update on its own reports, and in whether it renews its reach through contact with a closure that differs.
>
> **The verdict I recorded, before any theory:** this is a union of at least five conditions, not one lever, and which particular tool wins is a declared `gap`. The conditions are: the arrangement of tool, data, skills and routines; the firm's vocabulary and objective for the tool; the channel to a non-updating ground; renewal of reach from a differing closure; and reproduction of the habitat that made any pilot succeed. Kept separate. Never averaged into a single benefit score.
>
> **What would refute it.** I wrote these down before writing the verdict:
> 1. two slots with identical atom sets and identical typed bonds whose realized market benefits differ;
> 2. a firm's benefit unchanged after a habitat substitution removes the market's payment for what the tool does;
> 3. benefit lost while bracket fidelity and ground pressure both measure high and non-declining;
> 4. a tool that adds no atom and makes no search cheaper yet keeps paying across a habitat change;
> 5. a pilot whose success transfers with no reproduced habitat condition and no inherited unwanted actor;
> 6. one intervention that restores all five conditions with no categorically different residue.
>
> If any one holds, I retract the shape. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
