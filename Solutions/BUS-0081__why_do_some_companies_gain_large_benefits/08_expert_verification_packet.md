# Expert verification packet: the Tether on unequal technology benefit

**What this is:** a claim in a form an information systems or productivity researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the named winner. Not `grounded`.** No per-slot market measurement is claimed. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Buying similar tools is buying similar ingredients. The realized benefit lives in the typed bonds and the arrangement the firm builds around the tool, in whether the firm keeps a channel to a ground that does not update on its own reports, in whether it renews its reach through contact with a closure that differs, and in whether it reproduces the habitat that made any pilot succeed. Over a declared shared atom set two configurations are isomers: same atoms, different bond, different substance, invisible in the purchase order and decisive in the outcome. The question is a union of five conditions with different instruments. Verdicts are kept separate per domain and never averaged. Which particular tool or configuration wins is a declared `gap`.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal plus arrangement-facing; checkable from Fig 12 and from adopter data

- **Statement:** same tool atom does not determine benefit; the bond and arrangement do. Over a declared shared atom set the two configurations are isomers, with a functional-group reading when the sets differ.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Fig 12 :46: *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands. A recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself."* Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- **What to check:** enumerate the atom set actually shared by two adopter slots, then the typed bonds between the tool and data, skills, routines, decision rights and the market reading. Is the bond recoverable from the ingredient list? In the data, can two slots with the same atom list be told apart only by their arrangement?
- **Refuter:** two slots with identical atom sets and identical typed bonds, measured per slot, whose realized market benefits differ; or two slots with different bonds whose benefits are indistinguishable after the ground test. Either breaks C1.
- **Live strain, named:** no real pair of adopters holds literally identical atom sets, so the isomer claim is a controlled comparison over a declared set. Do not upgrade it to `grounded` without one.

### C2: channel and habitat; checkable from Fig 10, Fig 7 and Fig 11

- **Statement:** quick loss of benefit is channel failure rather than ground decay, and a pilot substitutes a habitat, so synthetic success proves wiring and never capability.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; Fig 10 :26: *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*; Fig 7 :37: *"Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:"*, Fig 7 :38: *"the bracket’s fidelity across the handoff"*, Fig 7 :39: *"the ground’s pressure on the room"*; Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."*, Fig 11 :70: *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."*
- **What to check:** in a firm that lost benefit, were bracket fidelity and ground pressure measured, or only asserted? In a pilot that succeeded, were the conditions the goal required reproduced in production, and were the actors unintentionally introduced enumerated?
- **Refuter:** benefit lost while both factors of Fig 7 :37-39 measure high and non-declining; or a pilot whose success transfers with no reproduced habitat condition and no inherited unwanted actor.

### C3: reach and provenance; checkable from Fig 6 and Fig 8

- **Statement:** a bought tool moves the wall only as a sourced import that adds an atom, or as a head start that makes the next search cheaper. Composition inside a closed set adds nothing.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :14: *"the only operator that moves the wall"*; Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*; Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."*; Fig 8 :50-52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."*; Fig 4 :24: *"downward: something that makes the next search cheaper. Never a replay of what worked once."*
- **What to check:** for a tool claimed to have changed what the firm can do, show the source gate (where the capability came from) and the shadow test (which gap it explains). If neither can be produced, the purchase is a transaction record, not a sourced atom.
- **Refuter:** a tool that adds no new atom and makes no search cheaper, yet whose benefit survives a change of habitat and passes the ground test. That breaks idempotence and C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a list of winning tools, vendors, maturity models or operating models. The framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32).
- Not a percentage, variance share or industry average as the verdict. Averages are filters for the budget (Fig 9 :60); averaging across slots throws away the signal (Fig 1 :22).
- Not that technology is irrelevant. A tool that changes contact can matter (Fig 6 :26, Fig 11 :40). What is refused is benefit as a property of the tool alone.
- Not a derivation of the economics or IS literature from the figures. Brynjolfsson and Hitt, the J-curve paper, Cohen and Levinthal, Rogers, Bainbridge and the robotics survey are secondary corroboration from several fields, and the business-pool sources are one witness, not several.
- Not `grounded`. No per-slot market measurement is reported here.
