## 0. The question, stated as an effect

Source line: `Questions/complexity science.txt` line 11, section 1 Foundations.

> Can two systems with very different components and mechanisms belong to the same universality class?

Restated in effect terms. We are asked whether **macroscopic behavioural equivalence** (two systems showing the same large-scale scaling, the same exponents, hence one class) can hold when the **microscopic components and mechanisms** differ. Two options:

- **Option A (yes).** The class is fixed by coarse structure (symmetry, dimension, interaction range), so the fine components may differ without moving the system to a different class.
- **Option B (no).** The components and mechanisms fix the behaviour, so different components necessarily give different classes.

What each leads to. A licenses cross-domain transfer of methods and predictions. B makes every system its own frame and forbids cross-domain prediction. The residual is not the truth of either option. It is the **gap between the two scales**: the fine components we can name and the coarse class we want to predict. Fig 4 names that boundary the membrane; Fig 10 names the transform across it lossy.

**Binding the sought-for shape as an ordinary term** (`THE_FORMULA.md`, "Direction"). Before searching, write down what an answer would look like: *a rule that says which properties of a system decide its class and which do not*. This makes the preference refusable by the same test as everything else. The pre-registered refuter in section 5 is that binding made checkable.

## 1. Domain census (done before choosing any figure line)

### R by arity, symmetry and scale (Fig 9 :22: "arity · symmetry · scale")

- **Arity.** The residual is a relation between **two systems** compared for class membership, plus a **third object**, the class itself (φ*), that both might instantiate. Two operands and a candidate: not one system inspected alone.
- **Symmetry.** Class membership is an equivalence relation (≡, two names one referent) over the coarse descriptors. The act of deciding membership is a threshold comparison (⋛) of the two systems' measured exponents. The relevant symmetry is the symmetry of the arrangement (order parameter symmetry, interaction rule symmetry), which lives in the bond, not the atom.
- **Scale.** The class lives at the coarse limit (scaling limit, thermodynamic limit, continuum field). The components live at the fine scale. The question is therefore a **scale-crossing**, which is exactly the membrane of Fig 4 and the lossy transform of Fig 10. A reading that stays at one scale cannot answer it.

### One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

A domain needs a nameable anchor. Where none exists, it is not a domain yet.

- **Domain 1, statistical physics / critical phenomena (the field the question comes from).** Actors: microscopic degrees of freedom (spins, molecules, lattice sites, agents). Currency: energy, free energy, fluctuations. Anchor: **measured critical exponents**, an instrument reading that does not move because a frame thinks differently. Anchor present, so this is a domain.
- **Domain 2, the framework kernel / membrane (Fig 4, Fig 10).** Actors: agents, one or a thousand, and whatever composes over them. Currency: the method that survives the crossing. Anchor: the ground, which does not decay. Anchor present. This domain governs **what may change while the class stays the same**.
- **Domain 3, composition and structure (Fig 12, Fig 13).** Actors: atoms and the bonds between them. Currency: the arrangement. Anchor: the behaviour of the resulting substance, which is checked against the world. Anchor present. This domain governs **components versus arrangement**, which is the exact distinction the question blurs.
- **Domain 4, verification and triangulation (Fig 2, Fig 8).** Actors: two frames with differing closures. Currency: independence, the degree to which they draw on separate evidence. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Anchor present. This domain governs whether the agreement "same class" means anything.

### Partition or union

Domains 1 to 3 are a **union**, not competing answers. Domain 1 is the object level; Domains 2 and 3 are the structural rules that govern it; Domain 4 is how we could know. A single lever that clears one condition leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again.").

There is a real **partition inside Domain 3** that must be unbundled before searching. The question fuses two conditions:

- C1: "very different **components**" (different atoms, different routes, different microscopic stuff);
- C2: "very different **mechanisms**" (different bonds, different arrangement, different rule of composition).

Fig 13 permits C1 to land in one class and forbids C2 from doing so when it changes the arrangement. A bundled answer ("yes, different stuff still shares a class") would be wrong for C2. The residue after unbundling is categorically different from the mixture and is finally describable (Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either.").

### Order by dependency (Fig 3)

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it." Dependency order here:

1. **Perception / vocabulary:** describe the class by its coarse descriptors, arity, symmetry, scale (Fig 9 :22). Without this, "same class" has no predicate.
2. **Objective:** say what would count as progress in our own terms, namely a class assignment that predicts the two systems' exponents.
3. **Planning:** compare the two systems against those descriptors, not against their component lists.
4. **Learn and carry:** only then carry the method across scales (Fig 4 :22). Order matters: naming the class before describing the residual is apophenia (Fig 9 :27, "describe it before you go looking").

## 3. Findings

### F1: The kernel survives a change of actors (affirmative half, `general`, triangulated)

Fig 4 :19-20:

> **"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."**

Fig 10 :68:

> **"Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between."**

Read in effect. "Actors" is the figure's word for the things that are swapped. In this question the actors are the components. "Kernel" is the figure's word for what does not change when the actors are swapped. In this question the kernel is the invariant large-scale behaviour, the universality class. Two systems with very different components can therefore instantiate one kernel. This is a structural claim (`general`), not an empirical measurement of any specific class.

Two figures with low overlap (the membrane between scales and the ground maintainer's crossing) give the same verdict, so this is **triangulated**. They are still one framework, so see the overlap note in section 7.

**Refuter.** Show a case where the kernel changes while only the actors change, with the coarse descriptors held fixed. Equivalently, a controlled renormalization-group result in which matched symmetry, dimensionality and interaction range produce different critical exponents.

### F2: Different routes arrive at the same substance (affirmative half, `general`, triangulated)

Fig 13 :121:

> **"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."**

Fig 13 :123:

> **"Two chains built from different links may compose functionally identical terms, and the converse"**

Fig 12 :117:

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

"Different routes" and "different links" are the figure's own language for different microscopic construction. "Functionally identical terms" is the class. Fig 12 adds the decisive converse: the same atoms under a different bond give a different substance, so the **arrangement**, not the ingredient list, carries class identity. Read together, the two figures say: different construction can converge on one class, and identical construction under a different rule can diverge into two classes.

Triangulated across Fig 12 and Fig 13 (two figures, different objects: the priced bond versus the minimum set). Mode `general` for the structural claim; applying it to a named physics class is `specified` and is done in F3 and `07`.

**Refuter.** Produce a case where two chains built from genuinely different links do not compose functionally identical terms while the coarse descriptors match, and show why Fig 13 :121 fails there.

### F3: The class is named by arity, symmetry and scale (criterion, `specified`, single-source from the figures)

Fig 9 :22:

> **"arity · symmetry · scale"**

Fig 9 :26:

> **"R's own predicates name the habitat that holds φ*"**

The residual's own predicates decide which frame holds the answer. For this question those predicates are symmetry, dimensionality and interaction range. The class is therefore identified by the coarse structure, not by a component inventory. This is the figure-side criterion; it is **single-source** (Fig 9 only) and is corroborated from a low-overlap field by the world source "Universality class" (Wikipedia, 2026), which states: "The critical exponents are independent of microscopic details of the model, but depend on dimensionality, symmetry, and range of interactions (i.e., only depend on the universality class)." The wording maps one to one onto "arity · symmetry · scale", which is why the corroboration is strong even though the figure line stands alone inside the framework.

Mode `specified` because it names the criterion for this question. It becomes `grounded` only when a measured exponent set settles a particular assignment, which this derivation does not do.

**Refuter.** Identify a microscopic detail that controls the exponents and is not dimensionality, symmetry or interaction range. That would break the criterion at the ground and send the census back to step 1.

### F4: The mechanism condition is load bearing (negative half, `general`, triangulated)

Fig 13 :124:

> **"holds too: the same ingredients under a different bond are a different substance."**

Fig 12 :116-117 (quoted in F2) gives the definition of the isomer and the functional group. The unbundled conditions now separate cleanly:

- **C1, different components:** permitted to share a class (F1, F2).
- **C2, different mechanism / bond / arrangement:** **not** permitted to share a class when it changes the coarse symmetry or the range of interaction. Same atoms, different bond, different substance.

This is the honest constraint on any "yes" answer. A blanket "yes, different systems can share a class" is false if the difference is in the mechanism that sets the symmetry. The correct answer is conditional on the difference being in the actors (the fine detail) and not in the bond (the coarse structure). Triangulated across Fig 13 and Fig 12.

**Refuter.** Show a system pair whose mechanisms differ in the symmetry-relevant respect yet whose measured exponents coincide, with no additional compensating mechanism. That would collapse the bond/actor distinction and require re-census.

### F5: Agreement only means something at low overlap (verification, `general`, triangulated)

Fig 8 :47:

> **"Independence is one quantity read twice."**

Fig 2 :31:

> **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."**

Fig 2 :35:

> **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

Two systems that "agree" on a class are two frames. If they draw on the same evidence pool, their agreement is the pool talking (Fig 2 :40) and carries no verdict weight. This is why the world search in `09` declares overlap: the Wikipedia "Universality" and "Universality class" pages are one pool, while the Barzel and Barabási primary research paper is a different pool. It is also why the two systems being compared must be low-overlap for the comparison to be informative. A room of agreeing systems does not add an anchor.

Triangulated across Fig 8 and Fig 2. Mode `general`.

**Refuter.** Demonstrate that two frames sharing all their evidence still produced a genuine ground correction. By Fig 2 :34 that would make the anchor another frame and the drift coherent, so the framework would need re-census.

### F6: Keep the two verdicts separate, never average them (per-slot rule, `general`, single-source)

Fig 1 :19:

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**

Fig 1 :22:

> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

Each system is a slot. A averaged "how similar are they" score would throw away the only signal that says where the class boundary lies. Verdicts are recorded per domain and per system and are never averaged. Single-source (Fig 1) but structural.

**Refuter.** Show that the class boundary is recovered only after aggregating the two systems into one scalar, and that no per-system reading carries it.

### F7: The condensation is lossy, and the gap must be measured (round trip, `general`, single-source)

The shorthand "different components can share a class" drops two constraints that the full figure set holds: the coarse descriptors must match (F3) and the bond must not change the arrangement (F4). The round-trip gap R_T is therefore nonzero and is enumerated qualitatively in `11_audit.md`. Treating the formula as identical to the figure set would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error this line names. Single-source (Fig 4), carried forward.

**Refuter.** Show a condensation of the figure set into the one-line claim that preserves both F3 and F4 unchanged. Then R_T would be zero for that condensation.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach. A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is:

> **Two systems can belong to one universality class when their coarse descriptors (symmetry, dimensionality, interaction range) match, even if their microscopic components differ. A difference in the bond that changes the coarse arrangement puts them in different classes.**

It is refuted if any of the following fires:

1. A controlled scaling or renormalization-group measurement shows matched symmetry, dimensionality and interaction range but different critical exponents. This breaks F1 and F2 at the ground.
2. A microscopic detail outside the three coarse descriptors is shown to control the exponents. This breaks F3's criterion.
3. A re-census shows the kernel depends on the actors rather than on the loop, so Fig 4 :19-20 and Fig 10 :68 no longer govern the fine/coarse distinction. This breaks F1 structurally.
4. Two systems whose mechanisms differ in the symmetry-relevant respect are measured to share exponents with no compensating mechanism. This breaks F4.

If any fires, the shape claim is withdrawn or re-censused, and the retraction is recorded here rather than silently erased. Until one is run, no part of this folder may be labelled `grounded`. A conflict from a world source sends the census back to section 1; it never flips the verdict by itself.
