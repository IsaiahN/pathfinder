**Subject:** one question about universality classes, and what would refute my answer

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question:** can two systems with very different components and mechanisms belong to the same universality class?
>
> **The output (`general` shape, `specified` application, not `grounded`):** the class is fixed by the coarse descriptors, symmetry, dimensionality and interaction range, not by the identity of the components. So very different components can share a class. The condition is load bearing: if the difference is in the mechanism that changes the coarse arrangement, the systems are in different classes. The two conditions in the question, "different components" and "different mechanisms", have to be unbundled before the comparison is made, because the figures treat them differently.
>
> I am not claiming which physical systems belong to which class, and I am not claiming this settles the physics. I am claiming a criterion and a conditional answer.
>
> **What would refute it:**
> 1. matched symmetry, dimensionality and interaction range producing different critical exponents in a controlled scaling study;
> 2. a microscopic detail outside those three descriptors controlling the exponents;
> 3. a measurement where two systems with symmetry-relevant mechanism differences share exponents with no compensating mechanism.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one fires, I retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **Before you answer, what would you count as a failure?** I am asking for your verdict on the output first, recorded before any theory is shown.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula (local canonical text): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The field literature that corroborates the criterion: "Universality class", Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universality_class
> - Network-dynamics classes from primary research: Barzel and Barabasi, "Universality in network dynamics", Nature Physics 9, 673 (2013), https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3852675/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and its author warns that outputs can be contrived to fit the formula. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
