# Isomers, identity and order: the supporting derivation

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

"Same universality class" is an equivalence claim. To decide it we need (1) what the equivalence is over, (2) whether it is the figure's identity bond or something weaker, (3) what order the comparison must be made in, and (4) which differences are category differences rather than degree differences. The question's two conditions, different components and different mechanisms, turn out to be different bond questions.

## DOMAIN CENSUS (carried from `01`, not repeated in full)

Atoms held: the six of Fig 13 (space, time, energy states; spectrum, gradient, network structures). Bonds held: the seven of Fig 12 plus negation. No new primitive is available without an import. The class is not a seventh member; it is a composition over what is held, and whether it is a settled molecule at the next level is left open in `01` section 8.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: What bond is "same class"?

Fig 12 :66-69 defines identity:

> **"identity"**
> **"two names, one referent"**
> **"substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No."**

Fig 12 :92:

> "Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing. Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term"

Two systems in one class are not "the same system under two names" in the literal sense, because their components differ. What is identical is the **coarse descriptor set** (symmetry, dimensionality, interaction range) and the resulting exponent set. So "same class" is an identity claim over the descriptors, decided by the symmetric bond ≡, while the systems themselves remain distinct operands. This is a `specified` reading: the figure gives the bond, the question supplies the operands.

**Refuter.** Show that class membership requires an ordered relation rather than an equivalence (for example, that A in a class with B and B in a class with C does not put A in a class with C). That would break the symmetric-bond reading and require re-census.

### Q2: Are two different-component systems isomers?

Fig 12 :116-117:

> "Four structures a compositional system exhibits, and each names something the flat form cannot write."
> "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

Under Fig 12's strict definition, two systems with **different components** are **not isomers**, because isomerism requires the same atom set. They are, in the figure's next structure, members of the same **functional group**: Fig 12 :123, "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." The functional group here is the shared coarse structure (same symmetry class, same dimension, same interaction range); the qualifier is the particular component set.

This matters because it keeps the affirmative answer honest. Two systems in one class share a **form**, not a substance. The world source "Universality class" says the Ising class "contains a variety of phase transitions as different as ferromagnetism close to the Curie point and critical opalescence of liquid near its critical point". A magnet and a fluid are not isomers; they are different members of one functional group, exactly as the figure predicts.

The stronger isomer claim is available in a different reading: if both systems could be written over the **full shared six-term atom set**, differing only in which structure mediates which state pair, then they would be isomers. That is the claim worth testing when the systems are formal models over one vocabulary, not physical substances. Mode `specified`, unmeasured.

**Falsifier, written before the run.** If a pair of systems can be shown to share a class while having no common coarse descriptor at all (no shared symmetry, no shared dimension, no shared interaction range), the functional-group reading fails and the equivalence must be re-censused.

### Q3: Which differences are category differences and which are degree differences?

Fig 13 :35-39:

> "Space, time and energy are not three readings of one quantity. Neither are spectrum, gradient and network. Each answers a question the other two cannot"
> "Six members that overlapped would be fewer members wearing six names."

Applied to the question:

- A change in the **component identity** (spin versus molecule, site versus agent) is a change of actor. It can be a degree difference with respect to the class, because Fig 4 :20 says "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change" while the kernel stays.
- A change in the **symmetry-relevant bond** is a category difference, because Fig 13 :124 says "the same ingredients under a different bond are a different substance" and Fig 12 :117 says the difference is "decisive in the behaviour".

So the question's word "very" is doing no work by itself. "Very different components" is not a category boundary; "different mechanism in the bond that sets the symmetry" is. The honest answer depends on which the questioner meant, which is why the census unbundles them.

**Refuter.** Produce a case where a change of component identity alone moves the exponents while the coarse descriptors are held fixed. That would make component identity load-bearing and contradict Fig 4 :20.

### Q4: What is the order of the comparison?

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."

The required order:

1. Describe the residual by arity, symmetry and scale (Fig 9 :22). Without this, "same class" is an unbound preference.
2. Fix the class definition from the coarse descriptors, before looking at either system (Fig 9 :27, "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit").
3. Compare each system to that definition **separately**, keeping the verdicts per slot (Fig 1 :19, "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated").
4. Check the overlap of the two frames before treating their agreement as meaningful (Fig 8 :47, "Independence is one quantity read twice.").
5. Only then assert class membership, and only as `specified` until the ground settles it.

Reversing steps 1 and 2 is apophenia: pick the systems first and any class will seem to fit. This is the same failure the exemplar names for GR/QM mappings, and the same reason the outreach order in `02` puts the output before the theory.

**Refuter.** Show a reliable class assignment made by inspecting the components first and the coarse descriptors afterwards, with no prior description of the residual. If that worked generally, Fig 9 :27 would not govern this domain.

### Q5: Does the round trip change the answer?

Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."

Carry the shape up (coarse descriptors, conditional yes), bring it back down (a claim about two concrete systems), and compare. The gap R_T is nonzero: the up-trip throws away the component inventory and the down-trip must not pretend to recover it. The class is the projection; the mechanism is the source that was discarded. This is the metamerism point of `05` section 3 in round-trip form.

**Refuter.** Show a down-trip that recovers the mechanism from the class alone. By the source essay that would require an instrument that discriminates sources from the reading, which is precisely what a class reading does not do.

## CONCLUSION

"Same universality class" is a symmetric identity relation over the **coarse descriptors**, not over the systems' components, so two systems with very different components can share one; they stand to each other as members of a functional group, not strictly as isomers. The difference that breaks the sharing is a change in the bond that sets the coarse structure. The comparison must be ordered: describe the descriptors, then compare each system separately, then check overlap, then assign, and the assignment stays `specified` until a ground reading settles it. The round-trip gap records what the abstraction threw away, namely the mechanism.
