# The Tether's answer: can very different systems share a universality class?

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the application. Not `grounded`.** This is a criterion and a conditional answer, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Two scales are in tension. At the fine scale we can name the components and mechanisms and they differ. At the coarse scale we observe the class and it can coincide. The question asks whether the coarse equivalence can survive the fine difference. Arity: two systems plus the candidate class. Symmetry: equivalence over descriptors, decided by a threshold comparison of exponents. Scale: the class lives at the scaling limit, the components at the microscopic scale, so this is a membrane crossing (Fig 4, Fig 10).

The question also bundles two conditions that behave differently. "Different components" and "different mechanisms" must be unbundled before comparison (Fig 9 :54). That unbundling is half the answer.

## 2. The shape

1. **Yes for different components.** Fig 4 :19-20: "The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." Fig 10 :68: "Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between." Fig 13 :121: "Different routes can arrive at the same substance." The kernel (the class) survives a change of actors (the components). `general`, triangulated.
2. **The class is fixed by coarse descriptors, not by components.** Fig 9 :22: "arity · symmetry · scale". Fig 9 :26: "R's own predicates name the habitat that holds φ*". Symmetry, dimensionality and interaction range decide class membership. This is the criterion that makes the yes checkable. `specified`, single-source from the figures, corroborated by the world literature.
3. **No for a mechanism change that alters the coarse arrangement.** Fig 13 :124: "the same ingredients under a different bond are a different substance." Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance." If the "different mechanism" changes the symmetry or the interaction range, the systems are in different classes even if their components are identical. `general`, triangulated.
4. **The relation is an equivalence over descriptors, not an identity of systems.** Fig 12 :65-69 defines ≡ as "two names, one referent"; applied here the referent is the descriptor set, not the system. In figure terms the two systems are members of a **functional group** (Fig 12 :123), not strict isomers, unless they are written over one shared atom set. `specified`.
5. **Class membership is a reading, not a source.** From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information" and "the instrument is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source." Being in one class says nothing about the mechanisms being the same. This is the lossy transform of Fig 10 :68. `general` with a `specified` application.
6. **Keep the verdicts separate and declare overlap.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 8 :47: "Independence is one quantity read twice." Compare each system separately and check whether the two frames draw on the same evidence before treating their agreement as meaningful. `general`.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not an unqualified yes. The yes holds only for differences in the fine detail.
- Not a claim that two systems in one class have the same mechanism. The reading cannot comment on the source.
- Not a list of which physical systems belong to which class. That is a name, and the framework returns a shape.
- Not a derivation of the critical exponents. Those are measured; `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5 says "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays."
- Not `grounded`. No scaling measurement was taken in this derivation.
- Not a claim that universality is proved by agreement. Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

## 4. What would settle it, and the standing falsifiers

The ground is a controlled scaling or renormalization-group measurement, not a further argument. Specifically:

1. Matched symmetry, dimensionality and interaction range but different critical exponents: breaks the affirmative half.
2. A microscopic detail outside those three descriptors controlling the exponents: breaks the criterion.
3. Two systems with symmetry-relevant mechanism differences sharing exponents with no compensating mechanism: breaks the constraint.
4. A source-discriminating instrument that recovers the mechanism from the class reading: would falsify the projection claim in section 2.5 and would be the spectrometer analogue from the source essay.

The pre-registered form of these is in `01` section 5, written before this conclusion. A conflict from a world source sends the census back to `01` section 1; it never flips the verdict by itself.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The descriptors are a visible choice of compression axes, not nature's own list, and declaring them makes the choice visible rather than neutral. The class is the projection; the mechanism is the source that the projection discards. What remains open: whether a universality class is a primitive or a settled molecule that becomes an atom at the next level (Fig 12 :104). No instrument in this derivation decides that. The stop is recorded with its lineage so it is checkable. Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence".

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> Yes, with a condition. Under the figures, what decides a system's broad behaviour is its coarse structure, its symmetry, dimensionality and interaction range, not the components it is built from. The kernel stays the same while the actors change, and different routes can arrive at the same substance, so two systems with very different components can share one universality class. The condition is load bearing: if the difference is in the mechanism that changes the coarse arrangement, the systems are in different classes, because the same ingredients under a different bond give a different substance. Class membership is a reading, not a source: it tells you the exponents match, not that the mechanisms match. We state the criterion, the two unbundled conditions and the falsifiers written in advance, and we stop where the instruments stop. A controlled scaling measurement, not agreement, would settle any particular case.
