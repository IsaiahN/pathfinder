# Expert verification packet: the Tether on universality classes

**What this is:** a claim in a form a complex-systems researcher or statistical physicist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the application. Not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed. This is a criterion and a conditional answer, not a result.

**Status: simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.** It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Two systems can belong to one universality class when their coarse descriptors (symmetry, dimensionality, interaction range) match, even if their microscopic components differ. This is because the kernel is invariant while the actors change (Fig 4 :19-20) and different routes can arrive at the same substance (Fig 13 :121). The condition is load bearing: a difference in the mechanism that changes the coarse arrangement puts the systems in different classes, because "the same ingredients under a different bond are a different substance" (Fig 13 :124). Class membership is an equivalence over the descriptors, and the class is a projection with lossy information: it says the macroscopic readings match, not that the mechanisms match. The relation stays `specified` until a controlled scaling measurement settles it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 4 and 13

- **Statement:** the kernel survives a change of components; different microscopic construction can converge on one functional class.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :19-20 - "The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." Fig 13 :121 - "Different routes can arrive at the same substance." Fig 13 :123 - "Two chains built from different links may compose functionally identical terms".
- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Refuter:** a controlled scaling result with matched symmetry, dimensionality and interaction range but different critical exponents; or a demonstration that the kernel changes while only the actors change.

### C2: the criterion; checkable by reading Figure 9 and the public literature

- **Statement:** class membership is decided by arity, symmetry and scale, that is by symmetry, dimensionality and interaction range, not by component identity.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :22 - "arity · symmetry · scale". Fig 9 :26 - "R's own predicates name the habitat that holds φ*". World corroboration: "Universality class" (Wikipedia, 2026) - "The critical exponents are independent of microscopic details of the model, but depend on dimensionality, symmetry, and range of interactions (i.e., only depend on the universality class)."
- **Mode:** `specified` for this question.
- **Refuter:** identify a microscopic detail outside those three descriptors that controls the exponents. That breaks the criterion at the ground.

### C3: the constraint; checkable by reading Figures 12 and 13

- **Statement:** a difference in the bond or arrangement that changes the coarse structure makes a different class, even with identical components.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :124 - "holds too: the same ingredients under a different bond are a different substance." Fig 12 :117 - "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Refuter:** two systems whose mechanisms differ in the symmetry-relevant respect are measured to share exponents with no compensating mechanism; or the bond/actor distinction collapses under re-census.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not an unqualified yes. The yes holds only for differences in the fine detail.
- Not that two systems in one class have the same mechanism. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: "the instrument is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source."
- Not a list of which systems belong to which class. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not a derivation of any exponent. Those are measured.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not that agreement among many systems settles the class. Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

## 5. What would settle it

A controlled scaling or renormalization-group measurement, not a further argument.

1. Do matched symmetry, dimensionality and interaction range yield the same critical exponents? If not, C1 fails.
2. Is any microscopic detail outside those three descriptors load bearing for the exponents? If yes, C2 fails.
3. Do mechanism changes that alter symmetry produce different classes, as C3 predicts?
4. Is there an instrument that recovers the mechanism from the class reading? That would be the spectrometer analogue and would settle the projection claim.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 4 :19-20, Fig 9 :22, :26, Fig 12 :117, Fig 13 :121, :124. Six short passages.
2. Check C2 against your own field: is the class criterion really the coarse descriptors and not the components?
3. Name one controlled case where C1 and C3 would come apart, or say why none exists.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
