## 3. Wider literature that bears on the question

These are grouped by the slot they bear on, so the union is not pooled.

- **Main belt.** Walsh et al. (2011, Nature) show the Grand Tack first empties and then repopulates the belt from two source regions, explaining the S-type inner / C-type outer gradient. Walsh et al. (2012, Meteoritics & Planetary Science) state the belt has been depleted in total mass by at least a factor of 1000 and was repopulated from two distinct source regions. The National Academies chapter adds that asteroids above roughly 100 km are mostly primordial while most smaller asteroids are fragmentation products, and that giant collisions produce dynamical families.
- **Trojans.** The National Academies chapter reports that in the Nice model Jupiter's primordial Trojan population is lost and the Lagrange regions are repopulated with scattered Kuiper belt material, making the Trojans "KBOs currently orbiting the Sun at 5.2 AU" in that model.
- **Kuiper belt.** Levison et al. (2008, Icarus / arxiv 0712.0553) show that a truncated primordial disk leaves the Kuiper belt initially empty and that Neptune's eccentric phase can deliver objects into it, reproducing the main observed properties. Morbidelli et al. (2010) identify the 1:2 resonance outer edge as the feature this model uniquely explains.
- **Oort cloud.** Portegies Zwart et al. (2021, A&A) present a causal chronology and report that roughly 70 percent of the cloud comes from 15 to 35 au, with a possible non-native captured fraction. They also note that no global model explains the Solar System in unison, which is an honest statement of the open item.
- **Cross-cutting.** The National Academies chapter states that no local asteroid can be assumed identical to another of the same taxonomic type, and that most information comes from remote sensing with a small number of spacecraft visits. This is the ground-contact limit on the whole question.

Overlap warning: Walsh, Morbidelli, Raymond, Levison and colleagues appear across several of these papers. Their agreement is partly a shared evidence pool. The cosmochemical and survey evidence in the National Academies chapter is the lower-overlap witness.

## 4. Distribution rules: offer, do not push

- Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." A recipient who has absorbed the full architecture before being asked for a verdict has been handed a recording, not a generator.
- Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Seat 4 must not hold the interiors.
- Fig 9 :27: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what distribution pattern the reader would expect under each history before offering any framework material.
- Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." A mass send selected for people who already agree produces a convention, not corroboration.
- Fig 8 :51: convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents; only the record of where each came from separates them. Every mechanism named in the outreach material carries its provenance.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the union shape claim and the five refuters from `02_outreach_email.md`; (2) ask what the reader would count as a failure and what observation would discriminate two histories; (3) record that verdict; (4) only if curious, offer the figure extract and the source ledger, with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority.

## 5. The two-stage offer

**Stage 1.** The shape claim and its refuter go out first. No theory, no links to the framework, no request for agreement or trust. This is Fig 10 :52, "Bring a characterised residual, never the source."

**Stage 2, opt-in.** Only after a stage-1 verdict is recorded, offer the three kinds of source: the figure extract with line numbers, the world sources actually read, and the public article on the architecture for context. The reader's later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.

**Stage 3, opt-in.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse. It is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer. Each user must state their own ground and write their own refuter before running it, because a loop cannot check the direction of its own question (THE_FORMULA.md, "Direction").

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 work only if the stage-1 output survives. Its survival is a question for a ground-visible reader, not for this simulated derivation.
