# The Tether's answer: how the Solar System's small bodies acquired their distributions

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on figure-derived constraints, `candidate` and ungrounded on every named mechanism. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question.**

## 1. The residual it answers

We observe current distributions (orbital elements, taxonomic gradients, size-frequency curves, reservoir populations) and owe an account of the histories that produced them. The residual is the gap between what each candidate history predicts and what the ground returns. It is a **union compound**, not one hard question: five populations with non-shrinking, population-specific residuals, and causes that overlap across them.

So the answer is five histories, not one: main belt, Jupiter Trojans, Kuiper belt objects, Oort cloud comets, near-Earth objects. **D2, dynamical forcing.** Fig 11 :56: "Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept." No seat arranged these distributions. Differential survival ran without an agent (Fig 11 :36: "Nothing was intelligent; selection ran."). ## 3. What the answer does not say

- Not that any mechanism is derived from the figures. Grand Tack, the Nice instability, resonance sweeping, collisional families and Oort cloud injection are provenanced imports from planetary science. Their agreement with the shape is corroboration only where overlap is low.
- Not that the five populations are one population, and not that one mechanism explains them all.
- Not that a maintainer chose these distributions. There is no seat in the Fig 11 sense.
- Not that an actor has a fixed valence. Jupiter is neither a universal destabiliser nor a universal benefactor.
- Not a final model. Fig 7 :6 names "the limit: no last room"; Portegies Zwart et al. (2021) state that "there is no global model that explains the Solar System in unison".
- Not `grounded`. No observation or measurement was taken in this derivation, and no named history is asserted as settled here.

## 4. What would settle it

The ground, not agreement. Fig 9 :42: "narrowing gets you close, the ground decides." Discriminating readings include:

1. Isotope and taxonomic measurements that separate two source reservoirs for a given slot, rather than merely fitting one after the fact.
2. A Kuiper belt observation (edge structure, inclination distribution, cold classical population) that distinguishes implantation from in-place survival.
3. A direct observation of Oort cloud objects with enough orbital information to test formation chronologies. Until that instrument exists, the slot is unreached, not empty (Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence.").
4. A survey result that returns a distribution no imported mechanism predicted.

The pre-registered refuter in `01` section 4 lists the six conditions under which the **shape claim itself** falls. A mechanism being revised is not one of them; the shape is at a different level.

## 5. The honest stop

The figures license the shape and refuse the name (Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"). The lineage of each mechanism import is part of the term: "Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available." The current S/C gradient does not display its own history. Two routes can arrive at the same substance (Fig 13 :121). So the answer stops with the shape, the enumerated habitat, the per-slot ground requirements, and the recorded imports, and it hands the naming back to the people who can range it against the ground.

**Live strain, named:** Fig 8 :56 says the triangulation claim "fits four cases already known and has not been tested forward". This solution uses triangulation as a rule, not as a validated result on this question.

## 6. One-paragraph version

> The Tether does not say that the Grand Tack or the Nice instability produced the small-body distributions, and it does not offer a final model. It says that "small bodies" is a union of at least five separate slots whose residuals do not shrink together, so a pooled story loses the signal; that these distributions were acquired in unmaintained mode, with no maintainer choosing them, by differential survival over a habitat that must be enumerated outward through giant planets, resonances, collisions, the birth cluster and the galactic tide; that actor valence is relative to the slot, so the same planet can empty one reservoir and populate another; that dynamics and cosmochemistry are low-overlap frames whose agreement counts, while agreement within one modelling tradition does not; and that the framework returns this shape, not a mechanism name. What the solution offers is the shape any acceptable history must have, five refuters written in advance, and the recorded route by which each import entered.
