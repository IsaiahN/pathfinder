# Slots, conditions, isomers and order: a supporting derivation

**Mode: `specified` for the structural claims, `candidate` for the analogues. Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading.**

## RESIDUAL

Two questions are owed by `01`: (1) along which axis does "small bodies" have to be unbundled, populations or causes; (2) can the resulting pieces be treated as isomers, a functional group, or something weaker. A third question follows: in what dependency order must the work be done, and which link has no instrument.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Which axis: populations or conditions?

- Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
- Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

The populations are **slots** (main belt, Trojans, KBOs, Oort comets, NEOs). The causes are **overlapping conditions** (a migrating giant, a resonance, a collision, a tide). Both axes are real and they are different. Unbundling only by population leaves the condition-overlaps standing; unbundling only by condition leaves the slot residuals pooled. Fig 9 :54 requires "unbundle before you search again" when the split lands on a union.

**Verdict: two loops per pass, one over slots, one over condition-overlaps.** Refuter: a single unbundling axis that leaves no residual on the other axis.

### Q2 - What is the dependency order, and which link is missing its instrument?

- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
- Fig 3 :25-26: the middle link, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, "has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments".
- Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

Ordered chain for this question:

1. **Perceive** the distributions (catalogues, samples, size-frequency curves). Instrument exists.
2. **Vocabulary** for what a body affords (resonance, volatile content, orbital class). Instrument partly exists.
3. **Objective**: which distribution is owed for which slot? This is the weak middle link. A model can always be fitted to an existing distribution if no discriminating prediction is stated.
4. **Plan** a history and its refuter.
5. **Verify and carry** against the ground.

Optimising step 4 (more elaborate N-body histories) cannot rescue a missing step 3 (no stated discriminating observation). This is the dependency break Fig 3 warns about, and it is a methodological claim about this field's practice, not a claim that the science has failed.

### Q3 - Are the mechanisms isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance."

As the world record writes them, Grand Tack (Jupiter migration plus gas drag plus two source reservoirs, Walsh et al. 2011), the Nice instability (giant planet instability plus resonance sweeping, Morbidelli et al. 2010) and Oort cloud injection (scattering plus galactic tide plus cluster environment, Portegies Zwart et al. 2021) do **not** share an atom set. They invoke different actors, different conditions and different scales. Under Fig 12 :117 they are **not isomers**.

The stronger, falsifiable form: write all three over the **full shared substrate set** {space, time, energy; spectrum, gradient, network}, differing only in which structure mediates which state pair.

- Grand Tack reads as a radial gradient (snow line) over space, bonded through a giant-planet network to the asteroid belt distribution.
- Nice reads as a temporal instability gradient bonded through resonances to the Kuiper belt, Trojan and NEO slots.
- Oort injection reads as a network (birth cluster, galactic tide) bonded to energy and space at a different scale.

Under that shared atom set they **are** isomers in the figure's sense: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. This is a `specified`, unmeasured claim about how to describe them, not a result of the simulations.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if one history can be written without an atom another requires (for example, Oort injection with no network, or Grand Tack with no gradient over space), the isomer claim fails and the weaker functional-group reading stands.

### Q4 - The functional group reading

- Fig 12 :123: "A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

If the isomer claim fails, what survives is that all three histories share a **functional group**: an unmaintained habitat that is enumerated, with a contact cascade and a per-slot ground check. The group says what kind of history it is; the qualifier (radial migration, instability, galactic tide) says which member. This is the honest weaker claim and it is the one carried in `07` and `08`.

### Q5 - Two weaker analogues, named but not leaned on

- Fig 12 :119: "A part that survives its own use." A mean-motion resonance behaves catalyst-like: it pumps eccentricity, clears a gap, and remains available to act again. This is an analogy from the figure's definition, not a mechanism claim. A resonance is a structure, not a seat.
- Fig 12 :121: "The output re-enters as input." Collisional families behave cycle-like: fragmentation feeds the smaller-body population that then fragments again. The sign of the loop decides whether the population closes or decays; the National Academies chapter reports that most asteroids below about 40 km are fragmentation byproducts, so the loop is running.

Both analogues are `candidate` and neither is used as evidence for a mechanism.

### Q6 - Two currencies, held apart

- Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."
- Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."

A migration history can be cheap to state (a few parameters) and expensive to settle (a long N-body run plus a survey that discriminates it). Conversely a complicated-looking history can be cheap to refute with one isotope ratio. Summing "elegance" and "cost of test" into one score is the error Fig 12 :125 names. The budget decides how much of the space is reached; it never decides which history is true (Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict.").

## CONCLUSION

1. Unbundle along **two** axes: populations are slots, causes are overlapping conditions. Neither axis alone closes the residual.
2. The dependency chain breaks at the objective link (step 3): without a stated discriminating prediction, later modelling is effort above the break.
3. The three named histories are **not** isomers as the world writes them. Over a shared six-term atom set they are a real isomer claim with a stated falsifier; if that fails, the **functional group** reading survives.
4. Resonance and collisional cycling are weaker analogues, recorded but not used as evidence.
5. Description cost and settlement cost are two currencies and are never added.

This framing rescues what is defensible in the world literature without pretending the figures produced it: the order, the unbundling and the isomer test need no new primitive and no claim that the loop named a mechanism.
