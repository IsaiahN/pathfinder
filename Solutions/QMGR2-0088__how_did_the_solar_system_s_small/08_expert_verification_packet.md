# Expert verification packet: the Tether on small-body origins

**What this is:** a claim in a form a small-body researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the figure-derived constraints, `candidate` and ungrounded on every named mechanism. No measurement is claimed. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.**

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Small bodies" is a union of at least five separate populations (main belt, Jupiter Trojans, Kuiper belt objects, Oort cloud comets, near-Earth objects), not one distribution, so each slot has its own residual and its own history. Under the framework's habitat rule these distributions are acquired in unmaintained mode: no maintainer chose them, differential survival ran, and what survives is what the ground kept. The habitat must be enumerated rather than composed, including third-order actors such as the galactic tide and passing stars, because contact cascades. Actor valence is relative to the slot, so Jupiter can deplete one reservoir and populate another. The framework returns this shape, and refuses to name the mechanism; Grand Tack, the Nice instability, resonance sweeping and Oort cloud injection are provenanced imports from planetary science, not figure outputs.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that the Grand Tack, Nice instability, resonance sweeping, collisional families or any Oort cloud chronology is derived from the figures.
- Not that those mechanisms are false. They are `candidate` imports here, and several are well supported in their own field.
- Not that one population's history transfers to another.
- Not that any actor has a fixed valence across slots.
- Not a final model; there is no last room (Fig 7 :6).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a measurement in this run.
