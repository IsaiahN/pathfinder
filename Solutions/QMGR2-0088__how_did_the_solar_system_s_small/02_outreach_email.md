# Outreach draft: check one output before deciding whether the framing is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. No personal addresses are included here.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter have survived your own check. The offer collapses on contact if the output does not survive stage 1.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about small-body origins, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one output about how the Solar System's small bodies acquired their current distributions that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on structure, `specified` on the constraints, not `grounded`):** the question is not one question. The main belt, Jupiter Trojans, Kuiper belt objects, Oort cloud comets and near-Earth objects are separate slots with separate residuals, and a pooled "distribution of small bodies" throws away the signal that says where to look. Under the framework's habitat rule, these distributions are acquired in unmaintained mode: there is no maintainer in the seat, nothing selects in the sense of an agent choosing, differential survival still runs, and what survives is what the ground kept. The habitat must be enumerated rather than composed, including third-order actors such as the galactic tide and passing stars, because contact cascades. The framework returns this shape and refuses to name the mechanism.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** I am not claiming that the Grand Tack, the Nice instability, resonance sweeping or any particular Oort cloud chronology is derived from the framework. Those are names from planetary science, brought in as provenanced imports. I am not claiming the simulations settle the history, and I am not claiming a theory of everything. Agreement among readers is not evidence.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written down before sending this:**
> 1. one mechanism reproduces all five population distributions with no population-specific residual left over;
> 2. a maintainer is demonstrated to have acted on these habitats in the framework's specific sense of instrumenting, distilling and introducing;
> 3. a standing valence list for one actor (for example, "Jupiter always destabilises") survives across all five slots;
> 4. sample returns or surveys return a distribution that no imported mechanism predicts;
> 5. the figures themselves are shown to return a specific mechanism name for this residual.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract the shape claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send the sources. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Figures and formula, with line numbers: local framework extract and the public GitHub figures folder offered on request.
> - Public article on the architecture, for context only.
> - The world sources I actually read, listed with dates and quotes in the source ledger.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is public. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks for reading,
> [Pathfinder solution author]

## Record the verdict before stage 2

Stage 1 is the shape claim and the five refuters above. Record the reader's answer, including a refusal, **before** any framework material is shown. Stage 2 is opt-in only: once a reader has absorbed the theory, their later agreement is not pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"). Stage 3 offers the formula as a generator, not as an answer.
