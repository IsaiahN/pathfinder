## 0. The residual in effect terms

The loop defines `R = |Γ(b,a) − o′|` per slot `s` (Formula step 1; Symbols table, `R`). Here:

- **b** is what a would-be questioner takes to be the case: the inherited picture of which topics are legitimate, who may speak, and what a career can survive.
- **a** is the act of asking: putting a question into a grant, a seminar, a paper, a court, a classroom, a lab notebook.
- **Γ(b,a)** is what the questioner's library predicts will happen next: publication, a hearing, a rebuke, a funding line, a dismissal.
- **o′** is what actually happened: the question was asked and answered, or it was not asked, or it was asked and punished.
- **s** is the question-slot. `R` is indexed per question, per asker and per time, never averaged into a single "climate of freedom" score.

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** (Fig 1 :19)
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** (Fig 1 :22)
> **"The gap between prediction and observation. The central quantity: everything the system does is driven by it. It is the aim, not only the error signal, and it is always a slice: what cannot be perceived yet is residual and simply cannot be read."** (Symbols table, `R`)

The phenomenon to explain is not the number of questions asked. It is the **shape of the boundary between askable and unaskable** and how that boundary moved. A frame that predicts all questions are equally askable, or that a question's safety is a property of the question alone, has a residual here.

Stated as an effect: *why does the set of questions that could be asked safely have the shape it has, rather than being the set of questions that could in principle be asked?* No theory is named yet. "Patronage", "ideology", "market", "gatekeeping" are candidate names and are withheld until the census.

**Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term** (Formula, "Direction"), before the run: the answer would be (i) the classes of actor that act on the boundary, (ii) the currency they trade in, (iii) the anchor that settles whether a question was safe, (iv) the asymmetry of the seat's power, and (v) an instrument that would reveal a question that was never asked. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no loop check can see it. It is now bound, so the refuter below can fire on it.

**Anchor test.** Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."** Fig 2 :30: **"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."** Consensus among historians, a prize, a canon, a reputation, all move when pushed; they are instruments at best. The candidate anchor is the ground record: grant and censorship decisions, institutional minutes, employment and prison records, publication and withdrawal records, textbook revisions. The archive of what was actually asked and what was actually punished settles a per-slot claim and does not change because a later frame admires or condemns it. Fig 10 :93: **"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."** A committee whose standing depends on the answer it gives is not that anchor.

## 0b. Pre-registered refuter (written before the run)

Per the design-instrument rule: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* The shape claim is refuted if any of the following is observed:

1. A question-slot becomes safely askable by **subtracting** an actor from the habitat, with the causal chain measured, where no actor was introduced (breaks F2, Fig 11 :61).
2. A gatekeeping rule whose authors demonstrably have no stake in which value the ground returns, and which does not change which questions get asked (breaks F3, Fig 10 :47, :59).
3. A complete, sealed archive for a domain in which every question that could have been asked is shown to have been asked, with no silent slots (breaks F4, Fig 8 :39, Fig 9 :44).
4. Two genuinely independent instruments (low overlap, separate evidence pools) converging per slot on the same suppression with no shared source (breaks F5, Fig 2 :40). Their agreement would be real corroboration and would overturn the "one pool" finding.
5. A single lever that restores all three unbundled conditions with no categorically different residue left over (breaks F7, Fig 9 :57).
6. A figure line that names the specific question and computes the causal weight of each interest (breaks F8, which declares that a `gap`).
7. Fig 6 idempotence fails inside a closed set, or a seventh member is derived from the six (breaks the framework itself, not just this reading).

No part of this solution may be upgraded to `grounded` until at least one of these has been run without firing, and until a ground record has paid for the per-slot shape.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### 1.1 Arity, symmetry and scale of R

- **Arity.** The residual is a four-place relation: power-holder, interest, question-slot, observer, ordered in time (before, during and after the boundary moves). It carries an **ordered** bond, `→` sequence, *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"* (Fig 12 :72), because patronage precedes the question and punishment follows it. It carries a **comparison** bond, `⋛`, *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* (Fig 12 :87), because "safely" is a threshold relative to a penalty, not an absolute property. It also requires **negation**: the object of the question includes questions that were NOT asked, which is a refusal of a claim rather than the absence of an operand. Operators table :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."*
- **Symmetry.** Not symmetric. Power-holder and question are not interchangeable. "Safely" is relative to a censor or patron exactly as "superior" is relative to competitors (Fig 12 :85-89). The same actor changes valence when the residual changes (Fig 11 :49-51).
- **Scale.** Three scales are in view: the single questioner's step-by-step loop, the habitat (patron, state, journal, market, university), and the historical cascade across generations. The membrane is *"Not a wall: a filter with a direction"* (Fig 4 :21), and the transform is lossy, so the round-trip gap `R_T` must be measured, not assumed (Fig 4 :30).

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

A domain is admitted only if an anchor can be named. Where none can be named, it is not a domain yet.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 The questioner's loop** | researchers, teachers, their Γ, beliefs and acts | asking, residual reduction | yes: the archive of what was actually asked | admitted |
| **D2 The habitat of power and interest** | patrons, censors, funders, journals, regulators, markets, universities, party organs | money, permission, career, punishment, reputation | yes: recorded decisions, grants, bans, dismissals, publication | admitted |

X1 fails the anchor test and is excluded before any figure line is applied to it. This is a structural exclusion, not a claim that individuals never matter. Fig 11 :39: **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**

### 1.3 Partition or union

D1 and D3 overlap: a questioner can also be the checker, so their overlap is a compound with its own loop and its own ground. D2 and D4 overlap: the habitat acts inside the transmission channel, so that is a second compound. The whole question is a **union**, not a partition.

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** (Fig 9 :54)
> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** (Fig 9 :55)
> **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."** (Fig 9 :57)

The unbundled conditions are:

- **C1 habitat authorship:** the boundary is written by actors who can introduce but not subtract (Fig 11 :61).
- **C2 channel decay:** the unasked question is a dropped transient or pooled reading, not a measured absence (Fig 10 :26).
- **C3 interested seat:** the body that decides safety has a stake in the reading (Fig 10 :59, :93).

A habitat-authorship failure, a channel failure and an interested-seat failure are not degrees of one failure. They need different instruments. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55).

### 1.4 Dependency order (Fig 3)

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."** (Fig 3 :20)
> **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** (Fig 3 :19)
> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** (Fig 3 :27)

Order for this question: 1 perception, *"the system can tell what is in front of it, and tell it apart from what was there before"* (Fig 3 :5), meaning can we perceive the suppressed question at all; 2 vocabulary, *"it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked"* (Fig 3 :7), meaning do we have terms for what a censor or patron affords; 3 objective, *"it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms"* (Fig 3 :10), meaning what would count as a change in safety; then 4 planning and 5 learn and carry. The middle link is the one with no instrument: "safely askable" is still a vague phrase. Most effort lands above the break, where the measurements already exist (Fig 3 :29-30). This audit does not manufacture a measurement for that link; it names the instrument that would supply one.

### F1: The answer is indexed per question, never averaged into an "intellectual climate"

**Mode: `general` with a `specified` consequence for method. Domains all. Status: triangulated (Fig 1 per-slot indexing and Fig 9 split rule; low topical overlap).**

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** (Fig 1 :19)
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** (Fig 1 :22)
> **"The ground enters as the only metric."** (Fig 1 :23)
> **"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."** (Fig 9 :4-5)

A claim such as "the period was hostile to inquiry" scores the frame with an average. The specified consequence: any answer to HIS-0031 that is a single index of openness has already thrown away the signal saying *which* question, *for whom*, *in which venue*, *in which year*. Fig 1 :26: *"predicates minted, and credibility accrued"*; Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*

**Refuter:** an averaged, period-level statistic that predicts prospectively and per question which named slot will become unaskable next. If one exists, F1's methodological consequence is false.

### F2: Power and interest write the habitat; the seat can introduce but not subtract

**Mode: `specified`. Domains D1, D2. Status: triangulated (Fig 11 habitat asymmetry and Fig 10 authored-room rule; low overlap between habitat enumeration and selection rules).**

> **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."** (Fig 11 :43)
> **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."** (Fig 11 :44)
> **"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."** (Fig 11 :48)
> **"The seat’s power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."** (Fig 11 :61)
> **"The residual selects the members, but only from what is available. A candidate set chosen by a participant is an authored room however well the selection is reasoned, so the pool must be unauthored first and examined second. Reverse the order and the stacking is the selection."** (Fig 10 :73-74)

The mechanism is not a list of ideas that won or lost. It is the introduction of actors into the habitat of a question: a patron, a censor, a funding stream, a journal, a chair, a textbook committee. Once introduced, their contact with the question is a fact the questioner cannot switch off. A question that would have been askable in one habitat is unaskable in another without the question itself changing. Fig 11 :49-51: **"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** The same patron is a benefactor to one question and a malfactor to another.

The world corroborates at a different stratum. Borinskaya, Ermolaev and Kolchinsky, *Genetics* 2019, on the 1948 Soviet biology session: *"State authorities directly interfered with the scientific arguments of biologists and prohibited research, in this case in genetics, a rapidly developing scientific discipline at that time."* That is a first-order introduction of an actor (the party organ) into the habitat of a question, with the ground record of dismissals and destroyed textbooks. It is one witness from the history-of-science pool, not a general law.

**Refuter:** a documented case where a question became safely askable by removing an actor from the habitat with no actor introduced, the causal chain measured per slot. Under Fig 11 :61 that should be impossible; if observed, F2 is false.

### F3: The safety rule is a convention, and a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one

**Mode: `specified`. Domain D2, D3. Status: triangulated (Fig 10 convention/prohibition rule and Fig 2 anchor rule; low overlap).**

> **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"** (Fig 10 :47)
> **"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."** (Fig 10 :49)
> **"A seat with no stake at all does not exist."** (Fig 10 :58)
> **"The condition is that nothing about which value the ground returns today serves the occupant: an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."** (Fig 10 :59)
> **"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."** (Fig 10 :60)
> **"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."** (Fig 10 :93)

"Which questions may safely be asked" is presented as a convention: a norm of propriety, a reviewer's standard, a funder's remit, a journal's scope. The figures' rule says that when such a convention decides which questions get asked and which careers survive, it has become a verdict. Its keeper is disqualified as an anchor because the ground's answer serves them. This is the framework's structural reading of conflict of interest, not an accusation against any named person.

The world corroborates with a mechanism at the question-formulation point. The Catalogue of Bias, *Industry Sponsorship bias* (2019), lists among its mechanisms *"posing a research question such that the answer is true but misleading"*. Millum, *Wellcome Open Research* 2024, reports that *"several writers call attention to the fact that decisions about the allocation of research resources are often political, at least in part."* Both describe an interested seat shaping the question before any result exists. They share the research-integrity and science-policy pool with each other, so they are one witness, not two (Fig 2 :31-32).

**Refuter:** a gate whose authors have no stake in which value the ground returns, measured by their incentives, and whose rulings do not change which questions get asked. If one is documented, F3's scope narrows to staked gates only.

### F4: Unasked questions are a silent, pooled reading, not a measured absence

**Mode: `general` for the signal claim, `specified` for the historical application. Domains D3, D4. Status: triangulated (Fig 10 channel decay versus Fig 8 and Fig 9 underivability; distinct roles).**

> **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"** (Fig 10 :24)
> **"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"** (Fig 10 :26)
> **"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"** (Fig 10 :27-28)
> **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** (Fig 8 :39)
> **"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."** (Fig 9 :44)

An unasked question is not an empty slot that was checked and found empty. It is a dropped transient in a channel. The archive records what was published, not the full set of what could have been asked, so the absence of a question in the archive is not evidence that it was never suppressed. Fig 11 :52-53: **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."** A question three steps out, in a textbook or a curriculum, is where the effect lands.

The world corroborates from agnotology. Oreskes, *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society A* 2015, read via JSTOR Daily 2026-11-23: *"The complexity of science and the inescapable uncertainties surrounding scientific claims offer a rich landscape of opportunities to challenge science."* The article reports an industry campaign that *"purchased academic figures as mouthpieces to sow doubt about the facts"* and *"organized a campaign to alter the entire American education system, from grade school to universities."* That is a three-step cascade, not a single veto. Oreskes and the history-of-science pool overlap with Borinskaya's pool only weakly (different cases, different archives), so this is a second, low-overlap witness for the cascade shape.

**Refuter:** a complete, sealed archive for a domain in which every question that could have been asked is shown to have been asked, with no silent slots. Fig 9 :44 says no frame is a sealed room; a real sealed archive would break that.

### F5: Agreement among historians is one evidence pool, not corroboration

**Mode: `general`. Domains D3, all. Status: triangulated (Fig 2 anchor rule and Fig 7 room-chain rule; low overlap).**

> **"one evidence pool"** (Fig 2 :18)
> **"they never talk, and still agree"** (Fig 2 :19)
> **"so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other"** (Fig 2 :20-21)
> **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"** (Fig 2 :40)
> **"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"** (Fig 7 :12-13)
> **"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."** (Fig 7 :35)

A survey showing that most scholars now attribute a gap to politics is a room's agreement. It is efficient as a convention about meaning and worthless as a verdict about the world. Fig 2 :42: **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."** The cost here is high, so independence must be bought: grant records read against censorship edicts, employment records read against publication records, with the overlap recorded rather than hidden.

**Refuter:** two genuinely independent instruments with low overlap converging per slot on the same suppression. Their agreement would be real corroboration and would overturn this finding.

### F6: Prestige can fund the asking; it cannot settle the safety

**Mode: `specified`. Domain D2, D3. Status: single-source (Fig 1 frame-internal rule plus the framework essay; same author and evidence pool, so one witness).**

> **"coverage, and compression achieved"** (Fig 1 :25)
> **"predicates minted, and credibility accrued"** (Fig 1 :26)
> **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."** (Fig 1 :27)
> **"And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."** (Fig 10 :61)

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, read 2026-11-23: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."* A famous name can get a question a hearing, which is a budget. It cannot make the question safe, which is a verdict about the ground. Polanyi, *The Republic of Science*, 1962, read 2026-11-23: *"You can kill or mutilate the advance of science, you cannot shape it."* The same separation, from a different century and a different pool: power changes what gets tried, not what the ground pays.

**Refuter:** a documented case where the reputation of an asker, with no change in ground contact, changed which questions were safe and whose gate later paid out. If prestige settles safety directly, F6 is false.

### F7: The question is a union of three conditions; one lever will not clear it

**Mode: `specified`. Domains all. Status: triangulated (Fig 9 union rule and Fig 3 dependency ordering).**

The three conditions C1 habitat authorship, C2 channel decay and C3 interested seat have different instruments:

- A C1 failure shows up as a new actor in the habitat with asymmetric introduction (Fig 11 :61), and a per-slot change in what can be asked.
- A C2 failure shows up as dropped transients and pooled readings (Fig 10 :26), a question lost between the asker and the archive.
- A C3 failure shows up as a gate whose keeper benefits from the answer (Fig 10 :59, :93), with provenance that is clean and a ruling that is still wrong.

F7 is the reason the verdicts in `09_watson_answer.md` are kept separate per domain and never averaged. It is also why the outreach packet asks for a refutation of one sub-claim at a time.

**Refuter:** a single intervention that restores all three conditions and leaves no categorically different residue. That would collapse the union into a partition (Fig 9 :57).

> **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"** (Fig 9 :32)
> **"A system with a closure can answer that before it searches, and what it returns is a shape rather than a name."** (Fig 13 :133)

No figure line names a specific suppressed question, assigns a numerical causal weight to power versus interest, or ranks historical cases. A genuine re-census across all 13 figures, both tables and the formula finds no such line. ## 4. Recommendation

1. Lead with the per-slot residual and the pre-registered refuter, never with a theory of power (Fig 10 :52, **"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."**).
2. Keep the three conditions separate in every report. A blended "repression score" is a pooled reading (Fig 10 :26) and destroys the diagnostic.
3. Enumerate the habitat before naming actors: everything in contact with the question-slot, then what contacts those, outward until the cascade stops (Fig 11 :43-44). The pool must be unauthored first and examined second (Fig 10 :73-74).
4. Declare the stake of every seat that decides safety (Fig 10 :60). An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged.
5. Treat every list of "influences" as Γ, a candidate library whose reach must be tested against the ground, never as the answer (Fig 1 :27).
6. Use the history and STS literature as budget filters and as one witness, not as the verdict. Polanyi, Borinskaya and Oreskes overlap in the politics-of-science pool (Fig 2 :31-32).
7. Offer the formula as a method only after a verdict is recorded (Fig 4 :22). Each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.
8. Stop at the shape. Do not name the specific question or compute the causal weight. The framework returns **"a kind of thing, never the answer itself"** (Fig 9 :32).
