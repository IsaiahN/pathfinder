## DERIVATION

### Q1: Are the cases isomers?

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** (Fig 12 :117)
> **"A + B + C" ... "one substance" ... "this bond is symmetric, so the four are one term"** (Fig 12 :18-23)

Take two cases with the same atom set `{researcher, patron, censor, journal, archive}`:

- **Case A, direct patronage.** The patron precedes the question and the journal follows it. The bond between patron and question is `→` sequence: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"* (Fig 12 :72). The substance is a question that is askable because it is funded, and disappears when the patron does.
- **Case B, market gate.** The funder compares the question against a threshold of expected return: `⋛` comparison, *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* (Fig 12 :87). The substance is a question that is askable because it clears a metric, not because a person chose it.

Same atoms, different bond, different behaviour when the habitat changes. Under Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**. This is a structural claim, not a historical one; mode `specified`, unmeasured.

**Important correction to an early reading.** I first wrote the silencing act as `−` subtraction, "the censor removed the question". Fig 12 :80-84 defines `−` as *"A with B removed or absent"*, which is the absence of an operand. But a silenced question is not absent from the record of what could have been asked; it is **refused**. Operators table :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."* The correct operator is `¬`, negation, which acts on one operand and is not a bond (Fig 12 :134-136). So:

> **"¬" ... "this does not hold"** (Operators table :54-55)

The safety rule does not subtract a question from the world; it refuses the claim that the question may be asked. This matters for F4: a refused claim leaves no empty slot to count, which is exactly why silence is not a measured absence. **Retraction recorded:** the `−` reading is withdrawn, not silently erased.

### Q2: Does order matter?

> **"→" ... "A then B; B needs A to have happened"** (Fig 12 :70-72)
> **"the only bond a flat chain can express, so every term today is a sequence whether or not it should be"** (Operators table :34)

Order is not optional here. The refuter in `01` section 0b item 1 asks for a case where a question becomes askable by subtracting an actor with no introduction. The causal order that would have to be shown is: introduction of actor, then change in the affordance of the question, then asking or refusal. If the order reverses (the question changes first, the actor arrives later as a rationalisation), the habitat-authorship claim weakens to a correlation.

But Fig 12 warns that a vocabulary with only `→` writes every term as a sequence *"whether or not it should be"*. So I do **not** claim the three conditions C1, C2, C3 are a strict chain. They are a union (Fig 9 :55); their order is a reading to be measured per case, not a property of the framework. Mode `specified`; this is the standing warning against turning the union into a story.

### Q3: What kind of thing are the three conditions?

> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** (Fig 12 :123)
> **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."** (Fig 12 :119)

The three conditions are **functional groups**, not separate substances. The presence of an interested gate fixes the kind of outcome; the particular gate (a journal, a funder, a party organ) says which member. A gatekeeping institution is a **catalyst** in Fig 12 :119's sense: it survives its own use, so the same institution that silenced one question is still there to silence the next. Nothing in the institution's description records whether it is spent or regenerating. That is why a per-slot archive is needed rather than a list of institutions.

> **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."** (Fig 12 :121)

The catalyst plus the archive creates a cycle: a ruling becomes provenance (Fig 10 :62), the provenance becomes the next gate's input, and the loop closes if the gate can cite its own past ruling as a reason. Whether it closes in a given case is a measurement, not a reading (Fig 12 :121).

## FALSIFIER (written before the run)

The structure claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. Two cases with the same actor classes, different bonds, show **no** difference in behaviour when the habitat changes. That breaks the isomer reading (Fig 12 :117).
2. A gate that silences a question is spent by that use and does not reappear in the next case. That breaks the catalyst reading (Fig 12 :119).
3. A documented case in which the question changed first and the interested actor arrived later, with the actor's own record conceding the sequence. That breaks the ordering claim in Q2.
4. The three conditions behave as a strict chain with a measured, invariant order across cases. That breaks the union reading (Fig 9 :55) and would let one lever clear all three, firing refuter item 5 in `01` section 0b.

If any fires, the corresponding structural claim is withdrawn and the finding moves to the retraction table in `01` section 5.
