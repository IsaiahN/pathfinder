# The Tether's answer on political power, economic interest and the askable question

**Mode: `specified` on the shape claims, `general` on the structural rules, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 4), not a ground reading of the archive.

## 1. The residual it answers

Which questions could safely be asked is not a property of the questions. It is a per-slot residual: the gap between what a questioner's library predicted would happen if they asked and what the habitat actually did. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* The question is a **union** of three overlapping conditions, not one hard problem: habitat authorship, channel decay and an interested seat (Fig 9 :54-57). Each has its own loop and its own ground.

## 2. The shape

1. **Askability lives in the question-and-habitat, not in the question.** Fig 11 :39: *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* A question that is safe in one habitat is unsafe in another without the question changing.

2. **The habitat is written by introduction, not subtraction.** Fig 11 :61: *"The seat’s power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."* Power and economic interest work by introducing actors (a patron, a censor, a funding stream, a journal, a chair), whose contact with the question the asker cannot switch off. Valence is relative to the residual: the same actor is a benefactor to one question and a malfactor to another (Fig 11 :49-51).

3. **The safety rule becomes a verdict when it decides outcomes.** Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* Fig 10 :59: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* A scope rule or norm of propriety is a convention until it decides which questions get asked and which careers survive. Its keeper is then disqualified as an anchor (Fig 10 :93). This is a structural reading of conflict of interest, not an accusation against a named person.

4. **Unasked questions are channel losses, not measured absences.** Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; Fig 10 :26: *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*. The archive records what was published, not the full set of what could have been asked. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The effect often lands three steps out, in a curriculum or textbook (Fig 11 :52-53).

5. **Silence is a refusal, not a subtraction.** Operators table :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."* The gate does not remove the question from the world; it refuses the claim that the question may be asked. That is why no empty slot appears in the archive to be counted.

6. **Prestige funds the asking; it cannot settle the safety.** *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* A famous name gets a hearing, which is a budget. It does not make the question safe, which is a verdict about the ground.

7. **Agreement is one pool, not corroboration.** Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; Fig 7 :12-13: *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"*. A consensus of historians is a room's agreement, efficient as a convention about meaning and worthless as a verdict about the world.

8. **The framework returns a shape, not a name.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* It does not say which question was suppressed, which actor did it, or with what causal weight. That is a declared `gap`, not a silence (Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a theory of everything, and not a final verdict on an era. The boundary keeps moving; there is no last room (Fig 7 :6, *"the limit: no last room"*).
- Not a claim that politics and money are the only causes, or that they act identically in every domain. Ideology and economics were absorbed into D2, not promoted to separate domains.
- Not a claim that any named person was a censor or a beneficiary. The mapping of actor classes onto names is provenance from the world pool, recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv`, not a figure derivation.
- Not "science is nothing but power". Polanyi's *"You can kill or mutilate the advance of science, you cannot shape it"* is a world-source binding; the figures' own claim is narrower and about the habitat of a question, not about the truth of its answer.
- Not a causal weight, not a ranked list of villains, not a periodisation.
- Not `grounded`. No archive reading paid for any per-slot claim here.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

An archive reading, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads a result *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved the case. Here the corresponding questions are:

- **Per-slot enumeration.** For one named question, list everything in contact with it before naming any actor (Fig 11 :43-44). Does a new actor enter immediately before the question disappears, or after?
- **Gate stake test.** Did the keeper of the safety rule benefit from which value the ground returned (Fig 10 :59, :93)? Measured by incentives and recorded decisions, not by reputation.
- **Channel test.** Can the question be traced from asker to archive, or does it vanish at a textbook or curriculum boundary (Fig 11 :52-53)? A finding of dropped transient rather than deliberate veto changes which condition is doing the work.
- **The six standing falsifiers** in `01` section 0b, written before the run. Any one fires against the part of the shape it names.
- **The structural falsifiers** in `06_isomers_and_order.md`: no behavioural difference between isomers, a spent gate, reversed order, or an invariant strict chain.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The actor classes are bindings from a particular frame, not nature's own list. Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* The answer stops where the instruments stop: at a shape, three separated conditions and a set of refuters. The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*; Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name a villain or assign a percentage to power versus money. It says that which questions could safely be asked is a property of the question and its habitat, not of the question alone: power and interest work by introducing actors into that habitat, and once introduced their contact cannot be switched off. A rule about what may safely be asked starts as a convention and becomes a verdict once it decides outcomes, at which point its keeper has a stake in the answer and agreement with the gate is not evidence. Unasked questions are channel losses, not measured absences, so a silent archive is not proof that nothing was suppressed. Prestige can buy a question a hearing but cannot make it safe, and consensus among historians is one evidence pool, not corroboration. What the framework offers is the shape any answer must have, three conditions kept separate, refuters written in advance, and the per-slot archive reading that would close it. The specific question, actor and weight stay open until that ground pays.
