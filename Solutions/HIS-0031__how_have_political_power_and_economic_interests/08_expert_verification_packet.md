**What this is:** a claim in a form a historian of science or STS scholar can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural rules, not `grounded`.** No archive reading is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. **Status: simulated derivation, not a ground reading.**

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Which questions could safely be asked is a per-question residual, not a climate of an era. Three conditions do the work and must be kept separate: (C1) power and economic interest write the habitat by introducing actors whose contact the asker cannot subtract; (C2) unasked questions are channel losses rather than measured absences, so silence is a refusal and not an empty slot; (C3) a safety rule becomes a verdict when it decides outcomes, and its keeper's stake disqualifies agreement with the gate as evidence. Prestige can fund the asking but cannot settle the safety, and consensus among historians is one evidence pool, not corroboration. The framework returns this shape only: it does not name a specific question, actor or causal weight. Those are a declared gap that a per-slot archive reading would close.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: habitat authorship by introduction (checkable by a historian against one named case)

- **Statement:** a question's askability changed when an actor was introduced into its habitat; the actor could not be subtracted afterwards by the asker. Actors that help one question harm another; there is no standing list of good or bad actors.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :61, *"The seat’s power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."* Also Fig 11 :49-51: *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* and *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* Fig 10 :73-74: *"A candidate set chosen by a participant is an authored room however well the selection is reasoned, so the pool must be unauthored first and examined second."*
- **Refuter:** a documented case where a question became safely askable by subtracting an actor from the habitat with no actor introduced, causal chain measured per slot. If observed, C1 is false.

### C2: silence is a channel loss, not a measured absence (checkable against archive practice)

- **Statement:** the archive records what was published, not the set of what could have been asked. A missing question is not evidence that nothing was suppressed. The effect can land three steps out, in a textbook or curriculum.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :24, *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; Fig 10 :26, *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*; Fig 8 :39, *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 11 :52-53: *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* Operators table :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."*
- **Refuter:** a complete, sealed archive for a domain in which every question that could have been asked is shown to have been asked, with no silent slots. Fig 9 :44 says no frame is a sealed room; a real sealed archive would break C2.

### C3: the safety convention becomes a verdict (checkable against recorded decisions and incentives)

- **Statement:** a rule about what may safely be asked is a convention until it decides outcomes. Once it does, its keeper has a stake in which value the ground returns, so agreement with the gate is not evidence about the world.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :47, *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*; Fig 10 :59, *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"*; Fig 10 :93, *"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."* Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*
- **Refuter:** a gate whose authors demonstrably have no stake in which value the ground returns, measured by their incentives, and whose rulings do not change which questions get asked. If one is documented, C3 narrows to staked gates only.

## 5. What would settle it

A per-slot archive reading, not a further argument.

1. For one named question, enumerate everything in contact with it before naming any actor (Fig 11 :43-44). Did a new actor enter immediately before the question disappeared?
2. Did the keeper of the safety rule benefit from which value the ground returned (Fig 10 :59, :93), measured by incentives and recorded decisions?
3. Can the question be traced from asker to archive, or does it vanish at a textbook or curriculum boundary (Fig 11 :52-53)?
4. Do two cases with the same actor classes but different bonds behave differently when the habitat changes (Fig 12 :117, *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance"*)?
5. Does the gate that silenced one question still be there to silence the next (Fig 12 :119, *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use"*)?
