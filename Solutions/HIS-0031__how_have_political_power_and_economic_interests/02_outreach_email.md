# Outreach draft: check one output before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. Nothing is sent by this agent.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until a historian of science has checked the shape claim against one named per-slot case. The output must survive contact or the two-stage offer collapses.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about which questions could safely be asked

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a figure-grounded derivation of a history-of-science question and have one specific output I would like you to check against a case you know well. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** I treat the question as a residual indexed per question-slot, not as a climate of an era. From that, three things follow, and they are kept separate:
>
> 1. **The habitat is written by introduction, not subtraction.** Political power and economic interest act by introducing actors into the habitat of a question: a patron, a censor, a funding stream, a journal, a chair, a textbook committee. Once introduced, their contact with the question is a fact the asker cannot switch off. The same actor changes sign when the question changes; there is no standing list of good or bad actors.
> 2. **A safety rule becomes a verdict when it decides outcomes.** What may be asked is presented as a convention of scope or propriety. When that convention decides which questions get asked and which careers survive, it has stopped being a convention. Its keeper has a stake in the answer, so agreement with the gate is not evidence about the world.
> 3. **An unasked question is a channel loss, not a measured absence.** The archive records what was published. It does not record the full set of what could have been asked. Absence from the archive is not evidence that nothing was suppressed. The effect often lands three steps out, in a curriculum or a textbook.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not naming a specific question, a specific actor or a numerical causal weight. The framework returns a shape, not a name. I am not claiming that power and money are the only causes, that all historians who agree are wrong, or that this is a theory of everything. I am not claiming the historical record is settled by this derivation. It is a `specified` shape checked for internal consistency, not a ground reading.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim.** I wrote these down before sending this:
>
> 1. a question becoming safely askable by subtracting an actor from the habitat, with no actor introduced, and the causal chain measured;
> 2. a gate whose authors demonstrably have no stake in which value the ground returns, and which does not change which questions get asked;
> 3. a complete, sealed archive for a domain in which every question that could have been asked is shown to have been asked;
> 4. two genuinely independent instruments, with separate evidence pools, converging per slot on the same suppression.
>
> If any one holds, the corresponding part of the shape is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would count as a check:** pick one question-slot you know, enumerate everything in contact with it before naming any actor, and say which of the three conditions is doing the work. If none is, the shape is wrong.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Figures and formula for direct download (branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a question you know and can check against an archive. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thank you for your time,
> [Your name]
