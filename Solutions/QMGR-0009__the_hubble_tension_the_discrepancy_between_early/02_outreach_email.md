# Outreach draft: validate the output first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the stage-1 shape claim and its refuter survive your own check. If the shape does not survive, there is nothing else to show.

**Mode of the claim offered: `specified`, not `grounded`.** It is a shape and a route. It reports no value of H0.

**Subject:** Check one structural claim about the Hubble tension before you decide whether the method is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. I am asking what you would count as a failure of the claim below. If it fails, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the Hubble tension should not be treated as one number with two disagreeing measurements. It has two slots that share a name. One route infers H0 from a high-redshift angular scale through ΛCDM; the other measures a local, parallax-anchored ladder. They are bonded to different states and reach the label H0 by different routes. A framework I work with has a per-slot residual rule: the residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated, and aggregating across slots throws away the only signal that says where to look. So a consensus H0 formed by pooling the two values is the wrong move, and the right first move is to unbundle the question into a calibration part, an extrapolation part and a possible-new-atom part.
>
> The same framework orders the routes. Before minting new physics, check whether a term already held is false and its residual persists without trending down. If so, what is owed is a refusal of that term, not an additional atom. Early dark energy may still be right, but it is an import, and an import owes provenance, a shadow test and a ground that was not used in its own fit. A better χ² is a gate, not a ground.
>
> **What would refute this, written down before I sent this:**
> 1. Two genuinely low-overlap measurements in the same operationally defined slot converge within systematics as data accumulate. That kills the "does not shrink with effort" premise and the two-slot reading.
> 2. A specific held term is experimentally refuted and removing it closes the gap. That does not kill the shape, but it shows the refusal branch rather than the mint branch was correct, and I will record the correction.
> 3. A source-discriminating instrument returns the same value in two frames whose calibration chains share nothing. That closes the channel-failure branch.
> 4. The framework actually derives a numerical H0 or names the missing atom. That breaks its own shape-not-a-name limit and I will withdraw the claim.
>
> I wrote these before contacting you. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed. If you do reply, please give me your verdict on stage 1 before I show you any theory, because once you have the framework in front of you your agreement stops being an outside check.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The two primary measurements and the review: arXiv:1807.06209, arXiv:2112.04510, arXiv:2103.01183.
>
> **If you want to use the method:** the eight-step loop is public. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
