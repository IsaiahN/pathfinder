## What in the wider literature bears on this question

1. **The two headline frames are genuinely low-overlap in data, which is why their disagreement is informative.** Planck uses CMB temperature, polarization and lensing; SH0ES uses HST Cepheids, Gaia parallaxes, the NGC 4258 maser and supernovae. The review by Di Valentino et al. draws on both, so its agreement with each is one evidence pool, not two confirmations (Fig 2 :40). This is an independence finding, not a verdict on either value.
2. **Independent methods do not cluster at one value.** 67.4 (Planck + ΛCDM), 68.52 (DESI BAO + BBN + acoustic scale), 69.8 (Population II candles), 71.5 (independent TRGB), 73.04 (SH0ES) and 73.3 (SBF). That spread is the residue worth describing before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28). It is `specified`: these are published values, not ground settlements of this derivation.
3. **New physics is a candidate import with a stated outstanding ground.** Poulin et al. propose early dark energy and concede that future surveys will further probe it. That is exactly the provenance and shadow-test picture in Fig 6 :12-14, with settlement still owed (Formula step 5: a gate is not the ground).
4. **A widening error bar is not a closing gap.** The review notes that reduced tension can come from increased uncertainty under degeneracy with new physics. By Fig 1 :27 a frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces; by Fig 9 :60 sigma is a filter, not a witness.
5. **Look-elsewhere is a genuinely low-overlap warning.** Gross and Vitells come from high-energy statistics and share no cosmology data. Their result predicts that a single good fit among many proposed atoms is less decisive than its local significance suggests, which corroborates the review's "no specific proposal makes a strong case" without sharing its evidence pool.
