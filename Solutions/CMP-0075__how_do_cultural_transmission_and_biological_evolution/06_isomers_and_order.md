## DERIVATION

### Q1 : Are genetic and cultural evolution the same substance?

The CES explainer states that cultural change "constitutes an evolutionary process that shares fundamental similarities with – but also differs in key ways from – genetic evolution." Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance." Shared atoms alone do not settle identity; the bond carries the information the operands do not.

Fig 8 :48-49: "Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names." If the bonds were identical, the two processes would collapse to one frame with two vocabularies. They are not identical. The CES page lists the differences directly: "in humans, genetic information flows from parents to children. Cultural inheritance has many more pathways"; "genes are inherited with very high fidelity, whereas cultural traits are often transformed and reconstructed as they are passed from person to person"; "genetic mutation is undirected, whereas cultural innovation is often directed towards specific goals"; and "cultural evolution features transmission biases such as conformity (following the majority) and prestige bias (copying high status individuals) which are absent in genetic evolution."

- **Verdict:** not the same substance. `specified`, world-defined atoms plus figure-defined bond test.

### Q2 : Are they isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
-Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- If we quantify over the shared atom set `{variation, inheritance, selection}`, genetic evolution and cultural evolution have the same atoms. Their bonds differ: high-fidelity vertical inheritance with undirected mutation, against multi-pathway inheritance with transformation, directed innovation, conformity and prestige bias. By Fig 12's definition they are **isomers**: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance.
- The weaker reading is the **functional group**: both are "a process with variation, inheritance and selection", with different qualifiers. This is what the CES page asserts when it says cultural evolution shares fundamental similarities but differs in key ways.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a transmission bond is found that is identical in both channels at the same level, including fidelity, route structure and directionality of innovation, the isomer claim fails and the two collapse to one frame wearing two names (Fig 8 :48-49). Equivalently, if a single formal recursion with one set of parameter meanings predicts both channels without relabelling any bond, there is no surplus.

- **Verdict:** `specified`, triangulated between Fig 12's isomer definition and the CES field definition (low overlap between a metatheory figure and a society explainer). World modelling corroborates the behavioural difference but shares one evidence pool.

### Q3 : Cycle, catalyst and functional group in the composition

- Fig 12 :121: "The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
- Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."
- The cultural practice is the catalyst-like part in the niche-construction cases: a practice survives its own use and keeps modifying the selective environment, while a genetic variant that is spent in reproduction does not persist in the same way. Fig 12 :119 explicitly warns that "nothing records which a term is", so this is a `specified` reading, not a settlement. It needs an instrument that tracks practice persistence separately from allele persistence.
- The composed gene-culture object is a **cycle**, because the output (a modified selective environment) re-enters as input (selection on genes, which alters learning biases). A chain representation cannot close by construction. This matches the world result that quasiperiodic cycles appear in domestication models (Van Amburg et al., 2026) and stable oscillations in cultural niche-construction models (Creanza and Feldman, 2014), but those are corroborations from one modelling pool.
- **Falsifier:** a case where the modified environment does not re-enter, because the practice vanishes before selection responds. Then the object is a chain, and the cycle claim fails for that case.

### Q4 : What is the order of composition?

Order is dependency, not importance (Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."). The chain relevant here is Fig 3's five links:

1. **Perception:** resolve which behavior slot carries residual. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
2. **Vocabulary:** name what each channel affords. Genes afford differential reproduction; culture affords biased transmission.
3. **Objective:** state what counts as progress. Here: a composed model predicts slot-level spread and gene-frequency redirection that neither single-channel model predicts.
4. **Planning:** choose a measurement. Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
5. **Learn and carry:** Fig 3 :15: "name what worked, verify it, and carry it to the next problem." What crosses is a method, never a recording (Fig 4 :23).

Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Spending more effort on gene sequencing cannot rescue a missing instrument for behavior-slot transmission rates.

### Q5 : What crosses the scale membrane, and what is the round-trip gap?

- Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
- Fig 4 :23: "upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."
- Fig 4 :24: "downward: something that makes the next search cheaper. Never a replay of what worked once."
- **Up:** a learned practice that can be reapplied in a new environment. A recording of one successful hunt is not a method. The CES explainer's "cultural traits are often transformed and reconstructed as they are passed from person to person" is the world-side statement of why the upward transform is lossy.
- **Down:** an evolved learning bias, a head start that makes the learner's search cheaper. It is not a replay of ancestral behavior. Katz's evo-devo result is the lower-overlap corroboration: neural architecture biases which behaviors can evolve repeatedly.
- **Round trip `R_T`:** Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." `R_T = gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`. Going up throws away detail, so a practice carried up and brought back down will not match what was sent. The gap is not an error to eliminate; it is "the only honest report of how much was given up" (Fig 4 :31-32).
- **Falsifier:** a practice that crosses up and returns down bit-identical, with no loss of context and no re-embedding in a new habitat. Then the transform is invertible and the membrane is not a filter (contradicts Fig 4 :21, "the membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction.").

## CONCLUSION

Genetic and cultural evolution are **isomers** over the shared atoms `{variation, inheritance, selection}`: same atoms, different bonds, different substance. The weaker functional-group reading is always available if the atom set is widened. Their composition is a **cycle with a sign** plus a **catalyst-like** practice that survives its use, and the dependency order runs from slot-resolved perception to a verifiable method, not from a global narrative. The membrane carries methods up and head starts down, never recordings, and the round-trip gap is a measurement, not a rounding error. No new primitive is required for any of this, which is why the claim is `specified` and not an import that moves the wall (Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom.").
