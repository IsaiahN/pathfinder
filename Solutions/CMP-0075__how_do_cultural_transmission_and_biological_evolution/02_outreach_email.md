**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** the shape claim and its refuter must stay in front of the links. If the reply only engages the theory, the two-stage offer has collapsed on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one output about gene-culture interaction before you decide whether the frame is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I am writing with one specific output to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** Cultural transmission and biological evolution are not one inheritance channel running at two speeds. They have different currency and different anchor. Genetic change trades in allelic variation and differential reproduction; cultural transmission trades in information copied by social learning, with fidelity, conformity and teaching as its currency. Because the currency differs, neither reduces to the other, and the union reaches what neither reaches alone. The relation is ordered, not symmetric: a transmitted practice can produce a selective environment that was not there before, and genes send down biases that make the next search cheaper. That makes the composition a cycle with a sign, not a chain. Collective behavior is what that cycle leaves at the population scale, and the capability belongs to the agent-and-habitat system, not to genes or to individual minds in isolation.
>
> This is a **shape**, not a named mechanism. The frame does not say which gene or which norm occupies a slot. Particular cases, such as dairying and lactase persistence, are bindings from the world literature, recorded as such, not derivations from the frame.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that culture is just extended genetics, that genes determine which norms spread, that the loop always stabilizes, or that any of this is settled by experiment here. No measurement in this note decides which channel dominates in a given population.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim.** I wrote these down before sending this:
> 1. A single-channel model, genes only or culture only, predicts the observed rate, direction and behavior-slot distribution of cumulative collective behavior as well as the composed model.
> 2. A cultural practice leaves the selective environment statistically unchanged across generations while gene frequencies move in the predicted direction, or a genetic change alters no transmission bias.
> 3. An agent-only or gene-only improvement moves the ground.
> 4. A single-channel laboratory habitat reproduces the full cumulative phenotype, including the redirection of selection.
> 5. Global averages of cultural and genetic change predict behavior-slot spread as well as slot-resolved measurements.
>
> If any one holds, the corresponding part of the claim fails and I will withdraw it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Cultural Evolution Society, "What is cultural evolution?" (public field definition, opened 2026-11-22): https://culturalevolutionsociety.org/about-cultural-evolution/what-is-cultural-evolution/
> - Kasser, Lala, Fortunato and Feldman, "Not by Selection Alone: Expanding the Scope of Gene-Culture Coevolution", Evolutionary Anthropology, 2025: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12275967/
> - Creanza and Feldman, "Complexity in models of cultural niche construction with selection and homophily", PNAS, 2014: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4113930/

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
