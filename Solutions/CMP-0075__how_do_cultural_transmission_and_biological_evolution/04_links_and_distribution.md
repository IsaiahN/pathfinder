## Wider literature that bears on the question

The sources cluster into four fields. Overlap is declared because a shared pool is one witness, not several.

1. **Gene-culture modelling (shared pool).** Kasser et al. (2025), Creanza and Feldman (2014), Rendell et al. (2011), Van Amburg et al. (2026). All sit in or near the Feldman and Laland modelling tradition. Their agreement that feedback and cycles matter is one pool talking. What they contribute is a checkable shape: mutual causal feedback, oscillations, runaway hitchhiking, quasiperiodic cycles.
2. **Cognitive development (lower overlap).** Csibra and Gergely (2011) argue that the capacity to transmit cultural knowledge by communication is itself a biological adaptation and give an explicit falsifier. This is a second frame, not a restatement of the population-genetic model.
3. **Neuroscience and evo-devo (lower overlap).** Katz (2011) shows how neural architecture biases the evolution of behaviour. This bears on the "genes send down a head start" half of the membrane claim without sharing the cultural-evolution vocabulary.
4. **Field definition and animal behaviour (lower overlap).** The CES public explainer and the Federico et al. preprint on conformist traditions in mate choice. The preprint is an open, non-peer-reviewed source and is labelled as such.

The teaching model of Shimodaira and Ohtsuki (2027) is a useful dissent-shaped source: it finds that teaching becomes unfavoured as oblique transmission rises. It does not conflict with the figure verdict, because the figures do not predict a universal amount of teaching; it sharpens the site-specificity of D2b.

Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).

## The two-stage offer, plus a third optional stage

1. **Stage 1.** Validate the output against the ground. The output is the shape claim with five refuters, stated in `specified` mode.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in.** If the reader is curious after recording a verdict, offer the source links. Fig 6 :30: "Cast it in effect terms." The links are offered, not pushed.
3. **Stage 3, opt-in.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse. Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode before running it.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 work only if the stage-1 output survives. If refuter 1 or 2 in `01` fires, the shape must be revised before it is sent anywhere.
