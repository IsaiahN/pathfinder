# Union, order, and isomers of the error response

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural results, not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 8).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks whether one kind of event (a ground-confirmed mistake by a respected institution) produces one kind of effect (a change in public trust in knowledge more broadly). The residual is the gap between the locality of the event and the breadth of the reported effect. Two supporting questions are owed: what is the structure of the conditions, and what is their dependency order? A third is whether different responses to the same error are variants of one thing or categorically different things.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: the error, the claim, the public record, the peer frame, the ground, a refusal operator ¬ (Operators table :53-58), the seven bonds, and import (Fig 6 :14). No new primitive is available without a source gate and shadow test.

Domains D1 to D4 as in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 2: institution loop, peer verification field, public trust channel, correction and refusal mechanism. Union, not partition.

## DERIVATION

### Q1. Partition or union?

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

Applying the union test: a single lever does not clear the region. A refusal without an outside check clears D4 and leaves D2. An outside check without a refusal clears D2 and leaves D4. A shared-pool revelation clears neither by itself, because it changes how the public reads the frames rather than what the frames do (D3). The four conditions overlap and the intersections are categorically different cases. Verdict: **union**. Mode `general`. Triangulated across Fig 9 and Fig 8's independence parameter (Fig 8 :47: "Independence is one quantity read twice.").

**Falsifier.** If a single intervention measurably restores per-slot trust in every one of the four conditions at once, with no residue where two conditions overlap, the union claim fails and a partition reading becomes viable.

### Q2. What is the order of operations?

`THE_FORMULA.md` step 2: "REFUTED a term held is false; what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation". Fig 5 :53: "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so". Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit".

The order is therefore:

1. **Describe the residual in effect terms.** What failed to resolve, at which slot, against which ground. This comes first because the other two steps assume its answer.
2. **Refuse the held term.** Add ¬ against the specific claim. Fig 6 :42-43: "What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall. A refutation adds a term that refuses; it removes nothing, and the reach falls because something was added rather than because anything was deleted." The fall in reach is the mechanism, not a side effect.
3. **Import a new discriminating atom from a differing closure**, with "source gate: provenance" (Fig 6 :12) and "shadow test: explains a gap" (Fig 6 :13). Fig 6 :14: "the only operator that moves the wall".
4. **Settle against the ground**, and only then compose over the settled molecule: Fig 12 :104, "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."

Reversing steps 2 and 3 is the named failure: importing before refusing buys an atom while the wrong term is still composable. Reversing 1 and 3 is apophenia: choosing the frame before describing the residual. Mode `general` for the order, `specified` for an institution's response. Triangulated across Fig 5, Fig 6 and Fig 9.

**Falsifier.** An institution that imports a new verification atom first, then refuses the claim, and ends with strictly higher per-slot standing than an institution that refused first, across repeated cases. That would reverse the refusal-before-import order.

### Q3. Are two responses to the same error isomers?

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :46: "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."

Consider two responses over the same atom set `{error, claim, public record, outside frame}`:

- **R1, refuse and import.** ¬(claim), then import(outside frame) with provenance and a shadow test.
- **R2, explain and re-arrange.** A new justification term composed from the public record and the claim, with no import.

These are **not isomers** under Fig 12's strict definition. R1 uses the unary operator ¬, which "is not a bond" (Fig 12 amendment, :134-136) and which "closes" the equivalence gap that R2 leaves open (Operators :75-76). R1 also imports an atom (Fig 6 :14), changing the atom set. Same starting atoms, different operator set and different closure: different substances by a stronger difference than a bond change. Claiming they are isomers would be the flat-formula error Fig 12 :5 names: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."

The **strict isomer case** is narrower: two refusals over the same full atom set that differ only in which claim ¬ is applied to, or two imports that differ only in the typed bond used to attach the outside frame. Those have the same atoms and the same operator set, and differ only in arrangement. They are isomers, and the difference is "decisive in the behaviour" because one refuses the load-bearing claim and the other refuses a peripheral one. Mode `specified`. Triangulated for the definition (Fig 12) and the operator set (Operators table).

**Falsifier.** If two responses with identical atoms and identical operator sets but different arrangements produce identical per-slot trust outcomes, the isomer distinction is behaviourally empty for this question.

### Q4. Is "trust repair" a kind or a functional group?

Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

All responses that qualify as repair share a substructure: a characterised residual becomes a verdict that is checked against a ground that does not update (Fig 10 :24, :29). That substructure is the functional group. The qualifier is the specific member: refusal, monitoring, independent replication, compensation, apology. The world trust-repair literature distinguishes competence from integrity violations and finds that the effective response depends on the type; that distinction is a qualifier within the group, not a new kind. Mode `general` for the group, `specified` for the members.

**A world/figure tension, returned to census rather than used to flip the verdict.** The world literature sometimes finds apology more effective after a competence violation and sometimes finds denial or refusal more effective after an integrity violation. The figures say a refusal is owed whenever a held term is refuted (Fig 5 :53). Re-census: "apology" and "denial" as social signals are conventions, and Fig 10 :46 says "a convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do." The figure governs the epistemic verdict about the claim; the social signal is a separate object. Different objects, so no strain after re-census. Both are recorded; the social signal carries no verdict weight here.

### Q5. Which response is a catalyst, and which is a spent part?

Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."

A one-off apology or a single press statement is a spent part: it is consumed in the act and cannot be reused to check the next claim. A maintained outside seat, a public replication record, a standing monitoring arrangement, is a catalyst: it survives its own use and continues to return readings across slots. Fig 12 :119 adds that "nothing records which a term is", so this is a live design question, not a label. Fig 11 :40 corroborates: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Mode `specified`. Triangulated across Fig 12 and Fig 11.

**Falsifier.** A one-off communication shown to keep returning discriminating readings on later, unrelated errors without renewal. That would make it a catalyst and break the spent-part reading.

### Q6. Is the correction a cycle?

Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."

The residual surfaced by the first correction becomes the input to the next room: Fig 7 :36, "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced." If the sign is negative, the next room consumes the residual and the chain closes in place; this is a self-explaining institution. If the sign is positive and the next room differs, the correction becomes input and the chain travels. A single retraction cannot do this by construction; only a sequence of differing rooms can. Mode `general`. Triangulated across Fig 12 and Fig 7.

**Falsifier.** A single one-room response shown to close the residual against a ground without any subsequent differing frame, in repeated cases.

## CONCLUSION

The question is a **union** of four overlapping conditions, not a partition. The dependency order is describe, refuse, import, settle, then compose. Two responses to the same error are **not** strict isomers when one uses the unary refusal operator and imports an atom; the strict isomer cases are two refusals or two imports over the same atom and operator sets. "Trust repair" is a **functional group** with a shared residual-to-ground substructure, not a kind. A standing outside check is a **catalyst**; a one-off statement is a spent part. A correction is a **cycle** only if the next room differs and matches the residual. No figure line returns a trust magnitude or a universal sign; that remains the declared `gap` of `01` F8.
