## 3. What the wider literature adds, and what it cannot do

The world sources are secondary. They corroborate mechanism shapes; they do not decide the verdict.

- **The "more broadly" step is a framing and overlap effect, not a property of the error.** Ophir and Jamieson show that a "science is broken" narrative lowers trust more than a retraction narrative, and Lupia et al. show that the decline in confidence in science is similar to or less than the decline in many other institutions. Together they support the figure-derived claim that the spread follows the pool and the frame, not the content of the mistake. They are two different evidence pools (communication experiment versus survey synthesis), so they count as two witnesses for that shape.
- **A single trust number is a poor instrument.** Besley and Tiffany, Wolff et al. and the Wellcome/RAND review all distinguish ability, integrity and benevolence, and warn against single-item trust scores. This is world corroboration for the per-slot and per-dimension reading (Fig 1 :19, :22), not a substitute for it.
- **Prestige as proof is a named failure in both pools.** Greenhalgh's account of the Lancet kitemark and the Open Science Collaboration's statement that claims should not gain credence from the status of their originator independently corroborate Fig 1 :26-27 and Fig 10 :93. The Gilbert et al. comment is a genuine conflict inside the meta-science pool about the size of the reproducibility problem; it is recorded as a conflict, returned to the census, and does not flip the verdict. It strengthens the point that a single meta-analysis is a filter for the budget, not a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).
- **Correction requires monitoring, not just messaging.** Gordon's monitoring-based repair, the trust-repair literature's competence versus integrity distinction, and Motta and Stecula's finding that negative media attention carried the Wakefield effect all corroborate the figure-derived shape: keep an outside check reachable (Fig 10 :29), refuse the held term (Fig 5 :53), and import a new discriminating atom (Fig 6 :23).
- **Conflict rule not triggered at the figure level.** No world source conflicts with the figure verdict. The one world/world conflict (Open Science Collaboration versus Gilbert et al.) was handled as an evidence-pool disagreement, not as a reason to change the derivation.

## 4. Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 10 :52:** "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Lead with the error, the per-slot claim and the refuter. Offer the figures only after a verdict is recorded.
- **Fig 10 :42:** "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28:** "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what they would expect to see if the per-slot claim were true before giving them the theory.
- **Fig 2 :40:** "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." No list selected for likely agreement, no bulk send, no receptiveness rank.
- **Fig 2 :42:** "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Spend effort on venues whose readers are positioned to refute the claim, not on venues whose readers are likely to like it.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the per-slot shape claim and its five refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and which slot they would test first; (3) record that answer; (4) only then offer the three local framework links, with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority.

## 5. The two-stage offer, with a third stage

1. **Stage 1.** Residual and refuter only. Institution trust, field trust, peer trust, method trust and ground are named as separate slots. No theory. Record the verdict.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the figures text, the canonical usage rule and the formula. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the loop as a method to reuse. Each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode before running it. The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap's own warning applies: "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 shape survives. It has not been ground tested here; this is a simulated derivation. That status is stated in the first line of every substantive file, and it must be stated in any actual outreach.

## 6. Practical constraint on submission

`Public Understanding of Science` states that AI chatbots should not be listed as authors. The Journal of Trust Research and SAGE both use double-anonymized or anonymous review. In any submission, disclose that this package is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency, state the mode on each claim, and do not present it as an empirical finding. A failed check is more useful than an endorsement, and an editor who declines is not a refutation of the shape.
