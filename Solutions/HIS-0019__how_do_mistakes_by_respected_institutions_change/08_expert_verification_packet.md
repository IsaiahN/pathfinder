# Expert verification packet: HIS-0019

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher on institutional trust can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on magnitude and sign. Not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed. This packet is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A ground-confirmed mistake by a respected institution changes trust per slot, not as one global stock. The institution was a frame granted anchor standing, and the error reclassifies it. The spread to other domains follows the overlap of evidence pools that had been treated as independent: shared pools fall together, independent frames keep the error local and can strengthen trust in the method. What falls is contact with the ground, not knowledge itself, because the ground does not decay and the channel does. The owed response is a refusal of the refuted claim, not an explanation of it, and a repair that adds no new discriminating atom from a differing closure is re-arrangement rather than repair. No figure returns the size or the universal sign of the change; that is a declared gap.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1. The per-slot indexing and the union (framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 1 and 9)

- **Statement:** the residual must be indexed per slot; "public trust in knowledge" is a union of overlapping conditions, not one quantity with one lever.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- **Refuter:** a single intervention measurably restoring per-slot trust in all four conditions at once, with no residue in the overlaps; or repeated cases in which trust moves proportionally across institution, field, peers, method and ground with no differential by standing or pool overlap.

### C2. Overlap governs the spread, and the ground does not decay (partly world-facing)

- **Statement:** the breadth of the trust change is the radius of the shared evidence pool, not a function of the error's content; trust is a channel reading, and the ground is unchanged.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :47: "Independence is one quantity read twice." Fig 8 :48-49: "Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names." Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does". Fig 10 :27-28: "the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch".
- **What to check against the world:** does the observed spread track documented shared sources and narratives, rather than the technical size of the error? The Ophir and Jamieson narrative experiment and the Lupia et al. PNAS trend synthesis bear on this. They are secondary and do not decide the verdict.
- **Refuter:** a broad uniform decline while all other trusted frames demonstrably draw on independent evidence and no shared-pool revelation occurs; or a decline in trust while independently measured contact with the ground rises and the public can see it.

### C3. Refusal before explanation, and import before settlement (framework-internal derivation)

- **Statement:** an error is a REFUTED gap; the owed term is a refusal, which lowers reach without deleting anything. A genuine repair imports a new discriminating atom with provenance and a shadow test; re-arrangement inside the same closure adds no atom.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :53: "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so". Fig 5 :55: "What it removes is not a term but a term's standing to be composed over. Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach." Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 6 :12: "source gate: provenance". Fig 6 :13: "shadow test: explains a gap". Operators table :58: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate".
- **Refuter:** a clean specific refusal followed by no loss of standing to be composed over in any slot; or an internal reform with no differing-closure import that adds a new discriminating atom, changes contact and restores trust across slots.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a magnitude, a coefficient, or a universal direction for the trust change.
- Not that knowledge changed, that science is broken, or that the ground moved.
- Not that skepticism is good in itself, or that a particular institution is corrupt.
- Not that an apology, a retraction or a mission statement repairs anything by itself. A retraction is a refusal only if it actually refuses the held term.
- Not that the error always spreads. Spread follows overlap and can be local.
- Not `grounded`. No survey or experiment was run for this packet.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. The owed instruments are:

1. A per-slot trust reading that separates institution, field, peers, method and ground, and separates ability, integrity and benevolence.
2. An overlap map of the evidence pools trusted frames actually draw on, independent of their stated independence.
3. A contact reading of the ground that does not update on what any frame thinks: replication, prediction, outcome.
4. A refusal audit: was ¬ added against the load-bearing claim, or only a justification term?
5. A catalyst test: does the correction keep returning readings on later errors, or is it spent?

The full set of six pre-registered refuters is in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 5. Any one firing sends the derivation back to the domain census.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19 and :22, Fig 9 :55 and :57 (four short passages). Do trust readings in your field behave as per-slot or as one stock?
2. Read Fig 8 :47-49 and Fig 10 :24. Name one documented case where a single error spread across institutions. Did the spread track shared sources?
3. Read Fig 5 :53 and :55 and Fig 6 :12-14, :23. Look at one real institutional response. Did it refuse the load-bearing claim, and did it import an outside check with provenance?
4. Say which of the three refuters you would test first, and what would count as a failure.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
