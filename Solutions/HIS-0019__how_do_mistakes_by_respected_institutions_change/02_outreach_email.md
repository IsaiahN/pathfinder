**Subject:** A per-slot claim about institutional error and public trust, with its refuter attached

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know about institutional trust. I am not asking you to read the framework first. I am not asking you to agree with it. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`).** Public trust in knowledge is not one stock that rises and falls as a whole. It is a set of per-slot readings: trust in the institution that erred; in the field it covers; in peer institutions; in the method as a generator; and in the ground, which does not move. What a ground-confirmed mistake changes, in the first instance, is the standing of the frame that had been treated as an anchor. How far that change spreads is governed by how much of the same evidence the other trusted frames draw on. If they share one evidence pool, trust in all of them moves together and the error reads as a failure of knowledge more broadly. If they range separately against the same ground, the error stays local and can even strengthen trust in the method, because a differing frame was visibly selected against the residual.
>
> Two consequences follow, both checkable. First, what drops when trust drops is contact with the ground, not knowledge itself. Second, the response that is owed to a ground-refuted claim is a refusal of that claim, not an explanation of it. A refusal lowers the standing of the claim without erasing the institution's other settled contributions. An explanation without a refusal only accumulates justifications and turns a competence error into an integrity problem.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming that trust always falls, that it always falls by a certain amount, or that there is a universal coefficient. No figure line returns a number. I am not claiming that a particular institution is corrupt, that science is broken, or that skepticism is good in itself. I am not claiming that an apology or a retraction repairs anything by itself. I am not claiming to have measured anything. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.
>
> **What would refute the claim.** I wrote these down before doing the world search:
> 1. Trust in all five slots moves as one quantity with no differential by prior standing or evidence-pool overlap, in repeated cases.
> 2. An error is corrected by an unmoved ground with visibly independent peer frames, and institution, field, peers and method trust all move together with no separation.
> 3. A clean, specific refusal by a ground-refuted institution costs it no standing to be composed over in any slot.
> 4. A reform with no import from a differing closure adds a new discriminating atom and restores trust across slots.
> 5. A figure line is found that returns the magnitude or the universal sign of the change.
>
> If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the public sources. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Figures text with line numbers (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`

>

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
