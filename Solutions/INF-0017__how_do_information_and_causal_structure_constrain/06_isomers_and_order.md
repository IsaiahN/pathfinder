# Order, kinds and analogues in the information / causal question

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The audit established that information and causal structure are two conditions of a union, with a compound at their contact. Two questions are owed here: (1) what *order* is licensed between them, if any, and (2) are they **isomers** in the sense of Fig 12, or do they stand in some other structural relation?

## DOMAIN CENSUS (independent, before choosing a figure line)

- Atoms held for this question: "measurement" and "arrangement" as the two sides named by Fig 13 :16 and :26, plus the six substrate terms of Fig 13 when a physical theory is at issue.
- Relations available: the seven bonds plus negation. The relevant ones here are sequence `→` and comparison `⋛`.
- The order question is a **structure** question, not a state question: it asks how the two sides are joined, not what they contain.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Does `→` (sequence) apply to the information / causal relation? REFUSED as written

Fig 12 :70-74 defines sequence, and its test is verbatim:

> **"A then B; B needs A to have happened"**
> **"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."**

Operators table :29-33 gives the same. The information / causal question asks which constrains which; it does not assert that one *happens* before the other in time. Writing `information → causal structure` would commit to an ordering the derivation does not license. This is the same defect the exemplar found when a `→` was used where the figure used commas.

The figure's own substrate passage uses commas, not arrows: Fig 13 :74-76:

> **"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."**
> **"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state. Energy and a gradient compose a flow."**

So the correct notation for the two-sided constraint is a comparison or a mutual condition, not a temporal sequence. **Order is refused, mode `general`.**

- **Falsifier:** a proof that one side must be established before the other in every physical theory, with the before/after relation measured, not assumed. Then sequence would be licensed.

### Q2: Is `⋛` (comparison) the right bond? STRAINED, named, not smoothed

Fig 12 :85-89 defines comparison with a verbatim test:

> **"a threshold relation between two quantities"**
> **"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."**

Operators table :47-51 adds: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one."*

Comparison is between **two quantities**. At the contact of D1 and D2, the operands are a measurement-side reading and an arrangement-side relation. Whether those count as quantities at the same level, so that "which is larger" could even be asked, is not established. The honest status is a strain: comparison is the closest available bond, but its operand type is strained. This is recorded, not hidden, and it is the one open item carried into the packet.

- **Falsifier:** exhibit a derived common quantity in which both sides are measured and a threshold comparison is well defined. That would clear the strain.

### Q3: Are the information side and the causal side isomers? NO, under the figure's definition

Fig 12 :117:

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fig 12 :124:

> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

An isomer requires **same atoms, different bond**. The information side and the causal side are not built from the same atoms: one is a reading, the other an arrangement of relations. They fail the first condition. They are better read as two members of a **functional group** in the broad sense: each is a "measured / arranged" pair with different content. The stronger isomer claim would require rewriting both over a shared atom set, which in turn needs an import with a source gate and shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), and that import has not been supplied.

This is a meaningful negative result: it blocks the tempting analogy "information and causation are two isomers of one underlying stuff." Under Fig 12 that is not what they are.

- **Falsifier:** exhibit a shared atom set over which both sides are written, differing only in the bond. Then the isomer claim holds and the functional-group reading is demoted.

### Q4: What is the order of the union?

Fig 9 :54-58:

> **"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**

Fig 9 :20-21 states the chain rule:

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces"**

So the only defensible order is the **dependency order of the investigation**, not an order in nature: perceive each side (link 1), acquire terms for what each affords (link 2), state what would count as progress on the joint (link 3), then plan a joint account (link 4). Fig 3 :25-27 warns that the middle link attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments:

> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

This is why the first checkable artifact in the packet is an exclusion experiment, not a definition.

- **Falsifier:** show that a joint account can be planned and settled while the "what would count as progress" link is still unmeasured. Then the dependency order is wrong.

### Q5: Which analogues travel, and which do not

Fig 8 :50-52:

> **"Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."**
> **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"**

The 2009 information-causality result and the 2015 causal-structure result are two low-overlap witnesses that reach the same shape by different routes. Converted to Fig 8 terms, this is convergent derivation at high independence, **provided the records are kept**. If either paper had merely adopted the other's result, the agreement would be one witness wearing two names.

An analogue that does **not** travel: the six-term substrate of Fig 13. *They're Scanning Books* says it is *"a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes"*, and the causal-set program takes causal order as primitive. Re-census (recorded in `01` §6) resolves the apparent conflict: Fig 13 governs the primitives a composing system needs; a causal set is a world structure being described. Different domains, so no strain after re-census.

- **Falsifier:** show that the 2015 result cites and adopts the 2009 principle rather than deriving it from causal-graph structure. Then the independence claim fails and the two collapse to one witness.

## CONCLUSION

Refused as written: a temporal `→` between the two sides (Q1). Named as a strain: the operand type of `⋛` at the contact (Q2). Refused under the figure's definition: the isomer analogy (Q3). Kept: the dependency order of the investigation, not of nature (Q4), and the convergent-derivation reading of the two world witnesses, conditional on their provenance (Q5). No new primitive is minted and no wall is moved; this is a structural clarification with its own falsifiers.
