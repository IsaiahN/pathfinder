## 0. The residual in effect terms

The question asks how information and causal structure constrain each other. Stated as an effect, not a name:

- Something is to be decided: whether (A) information constrains causal structure, (B) causal structure constrains information, (C) each constrains the other, or (D) the two are not separable at the level the question assumes.
- Each option leads somewhere different. A makes information the measure and structure the measured; B reverses it; C needs a loop with two directions and a rule about which closes first; C also needs a reason the mutual constraint is not a single frame restated twice (Fig 2 collapse 1, collapse 2).
- What an answer would look like, written down and bound before the run (Direction step): a shape naming actors, currency, anchor and direction, plus an instrument that could fire on a specific instance, plus explicit silence where only a shape is available. That bound is the refutable object.

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.** Arity: two conditions under one question, so this is a candidate **union compound**, not one hard question (Fig 9 :54-58): *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Symmetry: the dependence may be asymmetric (one-way) or symmetric (mutual); the question *asks* which, so symmetry cannot be assumed. Scale: first order within one theory, second order where a theory is compared with another or with its ground. Fig 9 :22 gives the census vocabulary: *"arity · symmetry · scale"*.

## 1. Domain census, before choosing any figure line

One line per domain with actors, currency, anchor. No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor (nameable?) | partition / union | dependency order |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 measuring information in physics | states of a system, observer, measurement record | bits / entropy / description length | yes: an instrument that returns a reading (Fig 6 :34-35) | one side of a union | first: perception before vocabulary (Fig 3) |
| D2 causal structure in a physical theory | events, relations, allowed influences | order / precedence / functional dependence | yes: an experiment that distinguishes two causal structures | other side of the union | first: perception before vocabulary |
| D3 the contact between them | the two frames above, a residual R at their boundary | mutual constraint / cross-frame surplus | yes: a ground that fires on the joint, e.g. an information bound that excludes some structures (Fig 8) | the overlap: a compound with its own loop | after D1 and D2 are each settled |
| D4 the verification frame | expert readers, tests, instruments | agreement vs residual | yes: the ground, never agreement (Fig 2 :33) | spans all three | last: record verdict before theory is shown |

**Partition or union.** D1 and D2 are two conditions whose overlap D3 is a compound, so the whole is a **union**, not a partition. The diagnostic is Fig 9 :54-58: a single lever clears where only one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing, and the residue is *"categorically different"*. A union needs its own loop; averaging the two conditions would throw away the signal (Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*).

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** The chain is ordered by dependency, not importance: *"Each link consumes what the one above produces"* (Fig 3 :20-21). So settle perception of each side (link 1), then get terms for what each affords (link 2), then say what would count as progress (link 3), before planning a joint theory (link 4). Effort spent above a break arrives at a step that cannot use it (Fig 3 :19). The middle link, having terms and a progress criterion, is where chains usually break (Fig 3 :25-27).

**Anchor test.** Each domain has a nameable anchor, so all four are domains. If a box had no instrument, no experiment, no counter and no board that could settle it, it would not be a domain yet; it would be a residual to be characterised in effect terms (Fig 2 :29-30). This matters because a common failure in this literature is to speak of "information" with no instrument that would register a difference (the source-discrimination point in *The Hard Problem is About Source*).

## 3. Findings F1 to F6, each with a verbatim quote and a refuter

### F1: Information is a measurement-side quantity, causal structure is an arrangement-side quantity; neither alone closes R (general, triangulated)

Fig 13 :16 and :26 give the two questions a measurement must answer:

> **"it names what is being measured"**
> **"it decides what the measurement would mean"**

And Fig 13 :7-8, :17-18:

> **"what a system is at a moment"**
> **"how those quantities are arranged"**

A reading, by itself, cannot say which causal structure produced it. This is the source argument of *The Hard Problem is About Source* (Isaiah N, 2026-08-28), verified this session: several underlying causes give the same reading, so *"behavior is a projection with lossy information"* and the resolution came from a spectrometer, not an argument. Fig 13 :35-37 (stated in the figure as the independence condition applied to the set itself):

> **"Each answers a question the other two cannot: adjacency is not duration, an ordering is not a pathway, a pathway is not a direction."**

So a constraint from information onto causal structure is a constraint from the "what is measured" side onto the "what it would mean" side. It is real, but it is not the whole: the meaning side also has its own independence.

- **Refuter:** an experiment in which a single information reading discriminates among the causal structures that could produce it, with no extra instrument. Then information alone would close R and the two-sided census would be wrong.

### F2: The mutual constraint is a compound with its own loop, not one principle with two names (general, triangulated)

Fig 9 :54-58:

> **"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
> **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."**

And Fig 1 :22, the anti-averaging rule, already quoted. The reason a single principle cannot close both directions comes from the independence condition, Fig 2 :39-40:

> **"Correlation has valence."**
> **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**

If the "informational" account and the "causal" account are drawn from the same evidence pool, their agreement is the pool talking, not a mutual constraint. Genuine mutual constraint needs two frames whose closures differ (Fig 8 :45-46, :48-49).

- **Refuter:** produce a single closed principle from which both the information measure and the causal order are derived inside one closure, with provenance showing no import. Then the union reading is wrong and the problem is a partition.

### F3: Information constrains causal structure by excluding, not by generating; the wall moves only on import (specified, single-source for the operator rule, triangulated with the world source below)

Fig 6 :21, :23:

> **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."**
> **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**

World corroboration (secondary, opened 2026-11-23): Pawlowski et al., *Information Causality as a Physical Principle*, Nature 461, 1101 (2009), arXiv:0905.2292, states that if Alice communicates m bits, *"the total information access that Bob gains to her data is not greater than m"* and that the principle *"is violated by all the no-signaling correlations which are stronger that the strongest quantum correlations."* That is an exclusion: a bound on which correlations a physical theory may admit, arrived at from a communication-side quantity. It does not construct the causal structure. Chaves, Majenz and Gross, *Information-Theoretic Implications of Quantum Causal Structures*, Nature Communications 6, 5766 (2015), arXiv:1407.3800, then show the direction of dependence explicitly: *"The correlations that can be observed between a set of variables depend on the causal structure underpinning them"* and *"the principle of information causality appears naturally in our framework and go on to generalize and strengthen it."* Two low-overlap witnesses: one quantum-foundation principle result (2009), one causal-graph algorithm result (2015, different authors and method).

- **Refuter:** exhibit a physical theory whose admissible correlations are derived, not merely excluded, by an information measure, with a source gate and shadow test showing the atom was imported legitimately. Or show a no-signalling correlation beyond the quantum bound that violates no information principle, which would break the 2009 result as a constraint.

### F4: Causal structure constrains information by fixing what can be registered at all (specified, triangulated)

Fig 6 :26, :29:

> **"Composition explores what the operators reach; instruments extend what can be represented at all."**
> **"inward: is our representation adequate to hold it? The instrument extends access."**

And the substrate note in Fig 13 :23-24:

> **"that entities relate, and influence travels"**

A causal structure says which events can influence which; that is precisely the condition for a record to reach a reader. World corroboration: the causal-set program takes the order as primary. Sorkin's slogan, as recorded in the opened source *Causal sets* (Wikipedia, accessed 2026-11-23), is the slogan Order plus Number equals Geometry: a partial order plus counting recovers geometry. Malament's theorem, cited there, says a causal-structure-preserving bijection between past and future distinguishing spacetimes is a conformal isomorphism. So structure constrains what geometry, and therefore what measurable information, can exist. A second, independent witness from a different field (instrument design and animal sensoria): *They're Scanning Books When They Should Be Studying Nature* (Isaiah N, 2026-09-06) observes that *"a spectrometer measures the range it was designed to measure"* and that an axis a primitives set cannot represent stays invisible. High overlap with the framework, so weak corroboration; the causal-set result carries the independent weight.

- **Refuter:** show two causally distinct structures that yield exactly the same complete information record for every possible instrument, with no residual anywhere. Then causal structure does not constrain information as claimed. A source-discriminating instrument that separates them would confirm, not refute.

### F5: The two currencies must be held apart; a single "information = structure" figure is the named error (general, triangulated)

Fig 12 :125-130:

> **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**
> **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**
> **"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."**

This is the figure-level reason not to collapse an information cost (bits) and a causal or experimental cost (actions to settle) into one quantity, a mistake that is common when a measure is offered as a universal explanation. Fig 1 :24-27 adds the frame-internal trap:

> **"coverage, and compression achieved"**
> **"predicates minted, and credibility accrued"**
> **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."**

An "informational explanation of physics" that scores itself by how much it compresses physics is using a quantity it also produces. That is a disqualified metric, not evidence.

- **Refuter:** exhibit a derivation in which one common unit prices both the description length of a physical law and the actions needed to test it, with the conversion derived rather than stipulated. Under Fig 12 this should be impossible, since the currencies are defined in different units.

### F6: What survives is a shape, not a physical law; no last room (general, triangulated)

The figures do not name which physical information measure or which causal structure is correct. Fig 9 :32:

> **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**

Fig 13 :134-135:

> **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"**
> **"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."**

Fig 7 :6 names *"the limit: no last room"*, and :30-32: *"every room closes some of the gap; no finite number of them ever arrives"* and *"one long room: motion, no progress."* So a final informational theory of everything is excluded by the same rule that excluded the last room in the exemplar. The canonical README calls the framework *"a generator of the shape of an answer at any level"*, not a ToE.

- **Refuter:** a derivation from these figures that names a specific physical primitive and has the ground settle it. Since naming a missing primitive would mean already holding it (Fig 13 :135), a successful refutation would have to come from outside the framework, by import with a source gate and shadow test, and would then move the wall rather than be derived from the set.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim of this solution is refuted if any of the following holds, recorded before the WATSON run and the verdict:

1. **One-direction collapse.** A single information reading discriminates the causal structures that could produce it, with no extra instrument (breaks F1).
2. **Partition, not union.** A closed principle derives both the measure and the order inside one closure with provenance showing no import (breaks F2).
3. **Generation, not exclusion.** An information measure is shown to generate, not merely exclude, an admissible causal structure, with a legitimate source gate and shadow test (breaks F3).
4. **No causal constraint.** Two causally distinct structures give the same complete record for every possible instrument with no residual (breaks F4).
5. **One currency.** A common unit prices description bits and settling actions with a derived conversion (breaks F5).
6. **Named primitive settled.** The figures yield a specific physical primitive that the ground settles (breaks F6).

If any fires, the relevant finding is withdrawn. Until one is run, no claim here may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Keep the verdicts per domain separate; never average the information constraint and the causal constraint into one "mutual information of physics" slogan (F1, F2, Fig 1 :22).
2. State mode on every sentence. An unmeasured quantity is a specification of what to measure, not a verdict (Seat Map :281).
3. Lead with the exclusion result (F3) and its refuter; the 2009 principle and the 2015 causal-structure result are checkable in twenty minutes and do not require the reader to accept the framework.
4. Ask a checker what they would count as a failure before showing any theory (Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*).
5. Offer no theory of everything. The framework's own rule is no last room (Fig 7 :6).

### Strain test and re-census

One apparent strain was found and re-censused rather than smoothed. The philosophy essay *They're Scanning Books* says a declared six-term set is *"a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes"*, and the causal-set literature takes causal order as a primitive. Read as competing lists of primitives, these conflict. Re-census resolves it: Fig 13 governs the six terms a composing system must have to build a description, while a causal set is a world structure being described, not a primitive in the composing vocabulary. Different objects, no contradiction. This matches the canonical rule that a reading which makes figures contradict one another has misidentified its domain. No strain remains hidden; the one open item is the operand type when a second-order composition is compared with a first-order one, recorded in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

## 7. Updated verdict

The shape survives, with mode attached:

- **general:** information is the measurement-side quantity, causal structure is the arrangement-side quantity, and neither alone closes the residual (F1, F2, Fig 13 :16, :26, Fig 9 :54-58).
- **specified:** information constrains causal structure by exclusion (Fig 6 :21; Pawlowski et al. 2009), and causal structure constrains what information can be registered (Fig 6 :26; Sorkin's Order plus Number equals Geometry via the opened 2026 source; Chaves et al. 2015).
- **general:** the two currencies, bits and actions, do not add (Fig 12 :125-130), and no last room or final informational theory is available (Fig 7 :6).
- **gap:** which physical information measure and which physical causal structure are correct. The figures return a shape, not a name. This is stated plainly rather than filled.

Nothing is `grounded`. Strains after re-census: one named, none hidden.
