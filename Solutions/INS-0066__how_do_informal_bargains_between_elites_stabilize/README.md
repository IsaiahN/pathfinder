## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is why a small set of actors who each hold the power to take more instead repeatedly restrain themselves, and why the same restraint sometimes collapses. The two options and what each leads to:

- **Option A:** treat an informal elite bargain as a self-enforcing agreement that directly produces order. This leads to a verdict stated without a ground, and it mistakes an internal room agreement for a verdict from the ground (Fig 7 :35).
- **Option B:** treat the bargain as a **checkable convention ranged against an anchor the bargainers cannot move**, and treat breakdown as the failure of that convention's standing. This leads to a shape with named failure conditions and refuters written in advance.

An answer would look like: a set of conditions any durable informal elite bargain must satisfy, the failure modes that remove its standing, and an instrument that would tell the two apart. That sought-for shape is bound as an ordinary term in `01` and allowed to accrue a residual, so it can be refused like any other term rather than smuggled in as a preference.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with this derivation.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that any particular country, pact, or institution is "the" answer. The framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32).

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **Agreement is not a verdict.** *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that makes their agreement worthless.
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). This simulated second seat shares the primary's evidence pool, so it cannot corroborate.
3. **Trust before checking spends independence.** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42) The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, never the framework's interiors.

Legally: unsolicited bulk email to scraped personal addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. The venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv` are institutional and opt-in.
