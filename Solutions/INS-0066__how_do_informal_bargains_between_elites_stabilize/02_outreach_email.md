**Subject:** One checkable claim about informal elite bargains, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output about informal elite bargains that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** An informal bargain is a convention, not a verdict. It stabilizes order while it is checkable and while it does not itself decide who gets what. The framework's own words for the pivot are: a convention makes no claim about the world, and *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*. So the shape claim is this: a durable informal elite bargain needs a breach that somebody can see, and an anchor that the bargainers cannot move by agreeing among themselves. Where there is no such anchor, the group converges on whoever had the most influence, and the convergence looks like consensus because the drift is coherent. Where there is no visible breach, what looks like stability is an uncheckable assertion of whoever's authority.
>
> The same move explains breakdown. Defection is not something removed from the record. It adds a refusal, and what falls is the standing of every arrangement built on the belief that the others would restrain themselves. That is why a long calm can end abruptly without anything visible having been taken away.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the framework names any particular bargain, country, or institution. It returns **a shape, not a name**. I am not claiming that courts, parties, markets, or monitoring bodies are *the* answer; those are bindings from another frame and carry zero evidentiary weight until their provenance is shown. I am not claiming this is settled. Nothing here is `grounded`.
>
> **What would refute it, written down before this message:**
> 1. A durable bargain in which the agreement itself decides outcomes and still stays checkable and stable with no grounding.
> 2. A durable bargain with no anchor independent of the bargainers that does not drift toward the strongest party, measured rather than assumed.
> 3. A stable bargain across repeated defection opportunities with no monitoring and no violation signal.
> 4. A demonstration that the elite-scale description reproduces the population-scale order losslessly, so the round-trip gap is zero.
>
> If any one holds, the corresponding part of the claim is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the material is below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
