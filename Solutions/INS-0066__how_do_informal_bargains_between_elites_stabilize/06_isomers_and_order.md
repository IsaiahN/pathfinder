# Arrangement, order, and analogues in an elite bargain

**Mode: `specified` on the derivation, `general` on the bond definitions, not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The census in `01` left two unpaid questions. (1) If two elite coalitions have the same members and the same payoffs, can they produce different orders? (2) In what order must a diagnosis run, so that a reading is not taken below a broken link? The first is a structure question, the second an order question. Neither is settled by naming a country.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Is the bargain a bond, and does the arrangement carry information?

Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."*

A list of elite names and asset holdings is an ingredient list. It cannot tell you whether the relation among them is mutual restraint plus monitoring (a symmetric conjunction), a patron-client chain (an ordered production), or a subtraction where one actor's absence is the point. Operators table :15: *"swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No."* for conjunction; :33: *"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."* for sequence.

So **the bond carries information the actors do not**, and counting elites and resources records half the arrangement. This is `general` (structural) and follows from Fig 12, not from a political-science source.

### Q2 - Are two elite orders isomers?

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

Take two coalitions with the same elites and the same payoff matrix. Coalition A binds them with mutual restraint and a shared breach monitor. Coalition B binds them with a hierarchical exchange in which a broker reports breaches upward. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. On Fig 12's definition they are **isomers**, and the difference is invisible in an ingredient list.

Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."* An institution that is spent and regenerating behaves differently across uses from a personal deal that works once. Nothing in an ingredient list records which one a term is. This is why "they have a bargain" is an under-description.

**Status: single-source for the figure definition.** The political illustration is an analogue, not a derivation. The clientelism source in `10_source_ledger.csv` (Hicken's dyadic, contingent, hierarchical, iterative elements) is secondary corroboration that hierarchy and iteration are distinguishable properties.

### Q3 - What order does the diagnosis run in?

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*

Applied to a bargain:

1. **Perception.** Can each elite tell what the others did, and tell a breach from routine variation?
2. **Vocabulary.** Are there terms for what a move affords, that is, what can be blocked, punished, or repaired?
3. **Objective.** Can the group say what would count as a breach or progress in its own terms?
4. **Planning.** Is there a route to that objective under incomplete information?
5. **Learn and carry.** Is what worked named, verified, and carried to the next round?

Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* So adding more sanctions to a group that cannot perceive a breach is effort above the break.

The refusal branch runs before the mint. `THE_FORMULA.md` step 2 names the fifth bin: *"a term held is false; what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation"*. When an elite's belief that the others will restrain themselves is refuted, the first owed move is a refusal, not a redesigned bargain. Operators table :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."*

### Q4 - What is the sign of the loop?

Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*

A bargain whose output (compliance) re-enters as input (evidence of reliability) is a cycle. With a positive sign it closes and stabilizes. With a negative sign, compliance invites further demands and the loop unwinds. A one-off pact is a chain: it cannot close by construction, whatever its size. So "how many bargains have we signed" is the wrong question; the sign of the returning edge is the question.

Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Mutual restraint plus a visible breach is a functional group. The particular elites are the qualifiers. This is why the shape generalizes across cases while no single case is named.

### Q5 - Low-overlap analogues, secondary only

These corroborate the shape; they do not decide it. Overlap is recorded in the ledger.

- **Mathematics, repeated games.** [Folk theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_theorem_(game_theory)): *"if the players are patient enough and far-sighted... repeated interaction can result in virtually any average payoff"*, and a threat is useless if *"the threat of punishment is not credible."* This is Q4's sign condition in another vocabulary. Fisher's objection that the theorem is *"not a positive theory"* corroborates shape-not-a-name.
- **Evolutionary biology and game theory.** [The Evolution of Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Evolution_of_Cooperation): *"they must be able to track their previous history with any given player"* and *"clusters of nice strategies can get established."* This is Q1's bond carrying history and Q3's perception link.
- **Primatology.** [Frans de Waal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frans_de_Waal): *"reconciliation"* after fights, and *"what binds primate societies together rather than how competition structures them."* Post-conflict repair is a cycle with a positive sign, arrived at in a low-overlap field.
- **Institutional economics.** [Elinor Ostrom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elinor_Ostrom): the design principles include *"Monitoring: Monitors, who actively audit CPR conditions and appropriator behavior"* and *"Graduated sanctions"*, a checked convention rather than a declared one.

Agreement between these sources and the figures is one evidence pool at most where they share the repeated-game literature. It is not four witnesses.

## CONCLUSION

The bargain is a typed bond, not an ingredient list. Two coalitions with identical members and payoffs can be isomers under Fig 12 :117, and a surviving institution is a catalyst under Fig 12 :119. The diagnosis runs in dependency order (Fig 3 :20), refuses before it mints, and asks for the sign of the returning edge rather than the number of pacts. All of this is `specified` or `general`, none `grounded`.

## FALSIFIER, written before the conclusion

If either of the following holds, the supporting derivation fails:

1. The bond between elites is fully recoverable from the actors and payoffs alone, so the arrangement carries no extra information. That breaks Fig 12 :5 for this domain.
2. A one-off (chain) bargain is observed to close and sustain itself across generations without a returning edge, or a repeated bargain with a negative-sign loop is observed to close anyway. That breaks Fig 12 :121.
