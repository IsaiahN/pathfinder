## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22** - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and its refuter; offer the links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** - *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the article.
- **Fig 2 :40** - *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* A list selected for people likely to agree produces a convention, not corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its four refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) record their verdict; (4) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1, opt-in by construction.** Send the residual, the shape claim, and the pre-registered refuter. This is Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* The recipient checks the output without holding the framework's interior.
2. **Stage 2, only if curious.** Offer the source links. Their verdict on stage 1 must be recorded *before* any theory is shown, and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
3. **Stage 3, method reuse.** The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer (Fig 4 :22). Each user must write their own ground and refuter before running it (`THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction").

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. It is `specified`, not `grounded`, and it carries the four refuters in `08_expert_verification_packet.md`. Ask for a failure, not an endorsement.
