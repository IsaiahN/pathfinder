# The Tether's answer: reasoning versus pattern extension

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` per domain, `gap` on empirical attribution. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading. No measurement of any AI system was taken.

## 1. The residual it answers

A system succeeds on a problem absent from its examples. The question asks which of two sources produced the success: reasoning through the unfamiliar, or extension of learned patterns. The residual is the per-slot gap between what the system's library predicted the problem required and what the problem demanded (Fig 1 :11). The source of a closed residual is not the same thing as the closing of it. R is indexed by system and problem-slot (Fig 1 :19): *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*

## 2. The shape

1. **The binary is a union, not a partition.** Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* A success can be search inside a closure (S1), a new molecule from held atoms (S2), or an import from a differing closure (S3). S2 is a new composition and a reuse of the same atoms at once. The offered options overlap.
2. **Novelty is relative to the system, per slot.** Fig 6 :22: *"NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world."* A problem unfamiliar to one system is a near-neighbour for another with a different library. Chollet's framing agrees from a low-overlap angle: priors can *"buy"* skill while masking generalization power.
3. **New combinations need no new atom.** Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* Fig 13 :115: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."* A system that only reuses learned atoms can still produce a response never present in its examples. A novel successful output is therefore not evidence of a mechanism beyond prior examples.
4. **A missing atom needs a different frame.** Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 8 :45: *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."* When no composition in the current closure closes R, the owed thing is an import with a source gate, not more inward search.
5. **A score cannot name a source.** Fig 8 :50: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*; Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* A fluent trace is another projection. What is owed is a discriminator that varies novelty while holding structure fixed, plus provenance.
6. **Capability belongs to a system and its habitat.** Fig 11 :39: *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :70: *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."*
7. **No last room.** Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* No panel, benchmark or publication settles the attribution once and for all.

Verdicts per domain are kept separate and never averaged. D1 (composition) says new molecules need no new atom. D2 (habitat) says capability needs contact and imports. D3 (evaluation) says scores underdetermine source. D4 (human/animal comparison) says the two conditions overlap and have operational discriminators in other substrates. D5 (verification) says this package is one evidence pool and cannot corroborate itself. These are four different answers to four different questions, not four votes to be combined.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that current AI systems do reason. No test was run, no system was named.
- Not that current AI systems only extend patterns. That is a source inference from a score, which F5 refuses.
- Not that reasoning and pattern extension are the same thing. They are overlapping conditions in a union, with a real difference at S3.
- Not a theory of consciousness, understanding, or the Chinese room. Fig 11 :36 calls the "what is intelligence" form *"the wrong question"*; this answer stays with the contact question.
- Not a verdict on any benchmark. A benchmark is an instrument for the residual, not a witness for a source (Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a measurement. Status: `candidate`.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: the instrument reading green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

- **Discriminator Test:** on a fixed system and a fixed held-out problem, can a probe vary novelty while holding structure fixed and show whether the success was S1, S2 or S3, with the result replicated by a frame that does not share the training corpus?
- **Provenance Test:** can the record show where each atom in a successful composition came from, so convergent derivation and adopted import are distinguishable by route rather than by contents?
- **Import Test:** does a problem remain unsolved after exhaustive S1 and S2 search, with a specified missing atom characterised in effect terms and a named differing frame that supplies it under a source gate?
- **Habitat Test:** does an agent-only improvement that changes no contact transfer to a genuinely novel habitat? If yes, Fig 11 :40 falls (refuter R4).
- **Standing refuters R1 to R5** in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 fire against specific findings, each with a named figure line.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** *How Intelligence Actually Works* calls the six-term set *"a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes"*; declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral. *They're Scanning Books* says *"every reasoner, biological, artificial, or composed, can only be surprised along axes its primitives can already represent."* The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."* The empirical attribution is a `gap` reported plainly: here is what an instrument would have to detect, and no instrument opened this session detects it. That is a verdict (Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*), not an omission.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say whether AI systems reason or only pattern-match, and it refuses the binary. It says the two options overlap: a system can produce a genuinely new combination from the atoms it already holds, which is novel as an arrangement and ordinary as a reuse of prior material, so a surprising correct answer is not evidence of a mechanism beyond the examples. Only a success that requires an atom the system never held needs something from a different frame, and that is an import with provenance, not a more impressive score. A benchmark reading cannot tell you which of these happened, because a reading does not comment on its own source. What the framework returns is the shape of the discriminator that could: novelty indexed per problem, a record of where each atom came from, a ground that does not move when the model's score changes, and a habitat rather than a lone model. Which named system passes remains open until such an instrument pays.
