**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` survive the reader's own check. If the shape claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about what regulates black hole and galaxy growth

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read any framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output fails, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The question "what regulates the growth of galaxies and their central black holes?" is usually answered by naming an agent. I am not doing that. I am stating the shape any answer must have and the refuter that would break it.
>
> The shape has four parts:
> 1. Regulation requires a returning loop, not a one-way chain. The output of accretion has to re-enter as an input to the gas that feeds both the black hole and the stars. A chain can describe growth; by construction it cannot describe regulation.
> 2. The black hole persists across episodes, so it can act repeatedly. Its valence is relative to the residual: the same outflow that suppresses star formation in the galaxy can also self-limit the black hole's own growth. "Negative feedback" is too flat a label for that.
> 3. The coupling cascades. The effect on a galaxy-scale measurement is several steps out from the accretion event, through molecular gas. Scoping the habitat to the central parsecs misses the mediator.
> 4. The parsec description and the kiloparsec description are related by a lossy transform. Their round trip gap has to be measured, not assumed zero.
>
> The question is also a union, not one question: feeding, stellar coupling and halo supply are overlapping conditions. No single lever clears all of them, so I am not asking which one regulator wins.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the framework names the regulator. It returns a shape, not a name. Naming AGN feedback, jets, winds, supernovae or mergers is a binding from the astronomy frame, recorded with its source, not a derivation. I am also not claiming that agreement among simulations settles anything: several use related subgrid physics and are calibrated to some of the same local relations, so their agreement is one evidence pool talking.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written down before this message:**
> 1. A one-way chain with no returning edge that still produces the observed small-scatter coupling between black hole mass and host properties.
> 2. Measured evidence that some actor has invariant valence for black hole growth and star formation in the same objects at the same epoch.
> 3. A measured parsec-to-kiloparsec transform whose round trip gap is zero for the quantities carrying the scaling relation.
> 4. A single intervention that clears all three conditions at once and leaves no compound.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the shape claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.

> - The eight-step loop and mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`

## Two-stage offer, stated plainly

1. **Stage 1:** send the shape claim and the four refuters. Ask what the reader would count as a failure. Record their verdict **before** showing any framework material.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, send the local figure text, the formula and two arXiv links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user states their own ground and writes their own refuter first.
