# Expert verification packet: the Tether on instability rates in planetary systems

**What this is:** a claim in a form an exoplanet scientist or dynamical astronomer can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on two predictions, `gap` on the number. Not `grounded`.** No frequency value is claimed and no measurement was taken in this derivation.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"How often do planets form in systems that later become unstable?" is not one quantity but a union of three conditions: formation, later dynamical instability, and survival into the detected sample. The figures govern the shape of any answer: report a per-system distribution rather than a global average; correct for survivorship, because the detected population is what survived; declare the instability threshold before the cut; require detection frames with low overlap. The mechanism is chaos in a gravitational network, so identical ingredients in a different arrangement can produce different outcomes. The numerical frequency is a `gap`: no figure line governs it, and it is owed by instruments, not arguments. What is offered is the shape, the refuters written in advance, and the instrument that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1 and 11

- **Statement:** a frequency report must be indexed per system and corrected for survivorship. A bare average discards the live per-slot signal, and the observed population is post-selection.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 11 :15 "nothing selects; what survives is what the ground kept." Fig 11 :36 "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."
- **World check:** Yang et al. (2023), PAST IV, LAMOST-Gaia-Kepler: host fraction near 50 percent at all ages, multiplicity down from about 3 to about 1.8, inclination dispersion up. Budd and Mann (2018), Evolution: survivorship biases generate apparent rate heterogeneity in homogeneous models.
- **Refuter:** a survey showing the detected sample is statistically indistinguishable from the born sample with respect to instability history, using an instrument that distinguishes ejected from retained architectures; or a result whose aggregate is invariant under arbitrary relabelling of per-system times with no distributional change.

### C2: physics-facing; checkable by an astronomer against the literature

- **Statement:** the observed age-dependent decline in multiplicity is survivor-shaped and chaos in a network is a real contributor, but its size is not given by the figures. Same masses in different arrangements give different outcomes.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :32 "a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise." Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :96 "Two entries can share every ingredient and be inverses of one another."
- **What to check:** is the mass-ordering result of Marzari (2024) faithful? He reports that the least massive planet is ejected in about 70 percent of scattering events, that a light outermost planet gives much longer stability, and that "many transiting multiple planetary systems appear to be on the verge of instability with lifetimes of the same order of their age". Does the Yang et al. forward model genuinely unbundle formation from detection, or does it assume the answer?
- **Refuter:** a source-discriminating instrument showing the entire age decline is a formation or detection effect with no dynamical-instability contribution; or two systems with identical masses, separations and inclinations and no perturber giving different instability outcomes under deterministic integration.

### C3: convention and anchor; checkable by anyone who reads the methods

- **Statement:** "unstable" is a threshold relation between a timescale and a clock, and the threshold must be declared before the cut. Detection frames must be independently anchored.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :87 "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes." Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool".
- **World check:** Zhu and Dong (2021) show that the same term carries different meanings across studies and that deriving the fraction of stars with planets relies on assumptions about architecture and mutual inclination.
- **Refuter:** the instability fraction flips sign when the declared threshold moves within survey uncertainty and the authors chose it after looking; or two surveys agree while sharing a completeness model and target list with the overlap declared after the fact.
