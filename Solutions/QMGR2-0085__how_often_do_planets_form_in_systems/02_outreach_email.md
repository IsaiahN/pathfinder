# Outreach draft: check the shape before you decide about the number

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the convention question in F4 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is answered in the text: what exactly counts as "unstable", and who set that threshold. The offer collapses on contact if the recipient has to supply it for us.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about instability rates in planetary systems

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about planetary system dynamics. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on two predictions; not `grounded`):** "How often do planets form in systems that later become unstable?" is a union of conditions, not one quantity. Formation, later dynamical instability, and the survivorship of the detected sample are three conditions with their own anchors. From the figures it follows that:
>
> 1. the answer cannot be a single averaged percentage; it must be a distribution indexed per system, because a global average hides the per-system signal;
> 2. the observed population is a survivor population, so any measured rate must be corrected for the fact that unstable systems are underrepresented in compact multiplanet samples;
> 3. "unstable" is a comparison between a timescale and a threshold, and the threshold must be declared before the cut, or it is a convention that decides the outcome;
> 4. independent detection frames are required, because transit and radial velocity agreement is worth something only if they do not share a completeness model.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** I am not giving you the number. The figures return a shape, not a name, and certainly not a survey frequency. The numerical frequency is a `gap` in this derivation: no figure line governs it, and it is owed by instruments, not by arguments. I am also not claiming that every system becomes unstable. Chaos is a property of the amplifying chain, not a universal timescale.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a source-discriminating instrument showing that the age-dependent decline in multiplicity is entirely a formation effect, with no dynamical-instability contribution;
> 2. a survey showing the detected sample is statistically indistinguishable from the born sample with respect to instability history;
> 3. a result whose instability fraction flips when the declared threshold is moved within survey uncertainty, where the threshold was chosen after looking;
> 4. a figure line from which the numerical frequency follows by composition.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical figures repository: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Exoplanet statistics and occurrence-rate definitions, Zhu and Dong 2021: https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.02127
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
