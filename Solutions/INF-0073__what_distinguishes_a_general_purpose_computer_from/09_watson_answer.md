## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the separator between a general-purpose computer and a system that can imitate a computation only under a chosen interpretation. Option A: computation is a state-to-formal-state mapping and any system under some mapping computes. Option B: general-purpose computation requires a generator kernel with unbounded program arrangements plus outputs settled against a ground that does not move with the description. What each leads to: A makes the predicate unable to fail and therefore unable to select, design or check a machine; B keeps a narrow, checkable class and a declared gap on which physical systems qualify.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The unassigned difference between the two consequences above. Arity: two conditions, so a **union compound**, not one property with opposite answers (Fig 9 :57 "A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"). Symmetry: universality is ordered (a program is a route), interpretation is symmetric (two names, one referent). Scale: the computation runs at the system's own scale, the interpretation is imported from the observer's frame across a scale membrane.
- **Domain 1, the system's internal loop.** Actors: states, transition rules, instruction set, program stored as data. Currency: generated outputs, measured as residual against the claimed function. Anchor: the output under counterfactual input, checkable. Verdict: this domain can bear a ground.
- **Domain 2, the observer's frame.** Actors: interpreter plus state mapping. Currency: an interpretation, an identity claim. Anchor: none nameable. The mapping updates on what the observer chooses, so it fails Fig 2 :29. Verdict: a frame, not a ground-bearing domain.
- **Domain 3, the verification seat.** Actors: tasks, counterfactual inputs, instruments. Currency: checkable outputs across many programs. Anchor: the ground, which does not decay (Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does"). Verdict: this is where the question is settled, per slot.
- **Partition or union.** Union. A machine can be programmable and ungrounded, or ground-settled and fixed-function; one lever leaves the intersections standing. Verdicts are kept separate per domain and never averaged.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance").** Perception, then vocabulary (route terms), then objective, then planning and settlement. The middle link has no instrument (Fig 3 :27), so an interpreted system breaks there.

Strain test: Fig 13's six-term set is a physical substrate, not an instruction set. Re-census assigns Fig 13 only the substrate-agnostic closed-set/unbounded-arrangements claim. No hidden strain remains after re-census (strain S1 in `01` section 7).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The separator is a union of two conditions with different anchors. A general-purpose computer is a generator: a closed instruction set with unbounded program arrangements, a program stored as data, a machine table that fixes its states, and a method that can be reapplied across scales. An interpretation-only imitation has a fixed arrangement, a trace rather than a structure, a symmetric identity mapping where an ordered route is needed, a flat lookup table where a program is needed, a clock and dial that make the weak mapping trivial, and a habitat supplied by the interpreter. It proves wiring, not capability. Over a fixed alphabet, program and lookup table are isomers, not spellings. "Everything computes" is a refuted term, and what is owed is a refusal. No figure line returns a decision procedure naming which physical systems compute; that is a declared shape-not-a-name gap, unreached, with a source-discriminating instrument for the implementation relation owed. The verdict is `general` on the shape, `specified` on the domains, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, five failure conditions and per-domain verdicts before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending a recording instead of a generator, which Fig 2 :42 prices ("Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."). That cost is avoidable by leading with the separator and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The two conditions are a binding onto held loop and ground properties, not a new primitive. They can be restated without withdrawing any figure line, and a superseding packet can replace a sent one.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An observer-independent instrument making the compute verdict invariant across interpretations would close the gap; a fixed-arrangement system reaching the universal closure without imported mapping, or a single trace streaming new correct outputs, would break the generator claim; a derivation of one of the six terms from the other five, or a demonstration that universality and ground settlement are one condition, would break the union; a figure re-census admitting identity as a route-bearing bond would break the bond-type argument. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
