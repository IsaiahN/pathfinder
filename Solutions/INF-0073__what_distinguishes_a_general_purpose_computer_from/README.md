## Restating the question as an effect

Two candidate answers lead to different consequences and the question asks which one holds.

- **Option A (interpretation suffices).** A system computes f whenever an observer can map its states onto the states of an automaton whose transitions mirror the system's transitions. Under this reading every open noncyclical physical system implements every finite-state automaton (Putnam's result, reconstructed and answered in Chalmers 1996), so "general-purpose computer" collapses into "any physical thing under some chosen mapping." The term loses all discriminating power and no instrument could ever fail to find computation.
- **Option B (generated, reprogrammable, ground-settled).** A general-purpose computer is a generator whose kernel is fixed while its programs vary, whose reach is the closure of a universal instruction set, and whose outputs settle against a ground that does not move when an observer changes the interpretation. An interpretation-only imitation has a fixed arrangement, a single trace rather than a structure, an imported mapping with no provenance or shadow test, and a substituted habitat. The term keeps a narrow, checkable class.

What an answer would look like: an ordered list of separating conditions, each figure-grounded, with a pre-registered refuter, stated mode, and an honest mark on the one thing the figures do not return (a universal classifier naming which physical systems compute). That sought-for shape is bound as an ordinary term in `01` section 3 so it accrues a residual like anything else (Formula "Direction").

## What is deliberately not here

- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the claim.
