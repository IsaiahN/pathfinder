## Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual line alone, before aligning my wording with the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the unassigned difference between "interpretation suffices" and "generation plus ground is required." Arity: two conditions, hence a union compound rather than a single spectrum. Symmetry: the question mixes an ordered item (a program is a route) with a symmetric item (a mapping is identity). Scale: the machine's loop is inside the system, the interpretation is imposed from an observer frame across the membrane.
- **Domain 1, the system's loop.** Actors: states, transitions, instruction set, stored program. Currency: outputs and their residual against the claimed function. Anchor: output under counterfactual input. Can bear a ground.
- **Domain 2, the observer's frame.** Actors: interpreter and mapping. Currency: an identity claim. Anchor: none. A mapping that is changed to fit has no internal resistance. This is a frame, not a ground-bearing domain.
- **Domain 3, the verification seat.** Actors: tasks, counterfactual inputs, instruments. Currency: checkable outputs. Anchor: the ground, which does not update. Where the question is settled.
- **Partition or union.** Union. Programmable-but-ungrounded and ground-settled-but-fixed are two nonempty regions, so one lever leaves the intersection standing.
- **Order by dependency.** Read states, then check for route terms, then objective, then settlement. A verdict below the vocabulary break is a reading of nothing.

My census matches the primary's three domains and its union reading. Where the primary derived the union from Fig 9 and the anchor test from Fig 2, I derived the union from the two answer-consequences in section 0 (one predicate cannot fail, the other can) and the anchor requirement from the symmetry mismatch (identity versus sequence). ## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E composed with T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. **R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **counterfactual-input test** (Chalmers's strong conditional), which the shorthand compresses into "ground settled" but which needs an instrument that can present an input never in the mapping;
- the **provenance of the mapping** (Fig 8 :51), which the shorthand compresses into "import with no source gate" but which requires an actual record from the system's side;
- the **two-currency accounting** (Fig 12 :125), which the shorthand drops when it turns the separator into a single yes or no;
- the **gap itself**, which the shorthand can restate as "no classifier" but which in the concrete case is a claim about the current union horizon of instruments, not a permanent property.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a program is a route and a table is a flat recording, so over a fixed alphabet the two are isomers. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints.
