## Two findings in the wider literature that bear directly on this question

1. **Programmability is the live word, not mere computation.** The SEP Computational Theory of Mind entry notes, following Chalmers 2011, that calling a system a "computer" strongly suggests it is programmable, and that most Turing machines are not programmable. Critics object that the mind is not a programmable general purpose computer; the entry says that objection is misdirected because computationalists need not make that claim. That is a direct world-side confirmation that the question's phrase "general-purpose computer" adds a programmability condition on top of "computes." Cite it when explaining why the separator has two conditions.

2. **Trace is not structure.** Chalmers 1996 shows that once a clock and a dial are stipulated, every physical system implements every inputless finite-state automaton on the weak mapping account, but the construction only mirrors a particular run. The requirement that fails is the strong counterfactual conditional: if the system were in state p it would transition to q, not merely that it happened to do so on this occasion. This is the world-side version of Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." It is a retrodiction of the figure's distinction, not a result the framework produced. Say so.

3. **A live conflict, kept visible.** The Oxford 2022 chapter "Computation as Implementation" argues that Chalmersian implementation is necessary but not sufficient for computation (abstract retrieved through the public search index; the publisher page returned 403 on direct fetch on 2026-11-23). That is a genuine world-side tension with a purely structural reading. It was returned to the census, not used to flip the verdict, and it corroborates the second condition of the union: something beyond formal causal mirroring is required. Recorded as strain S3 in `01`.
