## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, whether a single institutional arrangement can limit elite capture while keeping public decision-making workable. Option A: treat capture as a moral defect of bad actors, replace them with impartial occupants, and add rules until influence is eliminated. Option B: treat capture as an anchor failure with three separable conditions and a workability constraint, arrange checks that cannot be authored by the body they grade, and leave the winning package open. What each leads to: A issues a prohibition no real occupant can satisfy, builds a ground with a reputation to protect, and leaves union intersections standing; B yields a checkable shape, refuters written in advance, and a declared gap where no instrument discriminates.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain giving actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Per-decision-slot failure of the reference that should settle an outcome, where decisions converge on influence instead. Arity: many slots, never one national score (Fig 1 :19). Symmetry: asymmetric, because the defect is an interest in the reading, not a symmetrical disagreement (Fig 10 :59). Scale: decision, institution, polity, with a lossy transform between (Fig 10 :68). Union of three conditions C1 pinned reference, C2 non-authored checker and pool, C3 refusal, plus a workability constraint on every lever (Fig 9 :55).
- **D1, the decision frame.** Actors: decision-makers, agenda-setters, citizens, applicants. Currency: votes, influence, agreements, assignments. Anchor: the outcome the public interest would settle on, observable in principle as a count, a record, a price, a court finding. Question type: partition, which compositions cross from opinion to ground.
- **D2, the checking seat.** Actors: auditors, courts, inspectors, opposition, journalists, peer reviewers. Currency: verdicts and residuals. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Question type: partition, who may range against the ground and who may not.
- **D3, the habitat and selection frame.** Actors: the pool of decision-makers and checkers, interest groups, media, information channels. Currency: contact and introduction. Anchor: the unauthored habitat, enumerated and not composed (Fig 11 :43). Question type: union, capture is a compound of conditions, not a list of bad actors.
- **Order by dependency.** Pin the reference, then bind terms for what a decision affords, then state an objective in those terms, then arrange independent checking, then carry verdicts with provenance (Fig 3 :20). Refusal is available at every step as a unary operator, not a bond.

Strain test applied: Fig 2 :34 (a moving anchor produces coherent drift) and Fig 10 :24 (the channel decays, the ground does not) govern different objects, standing of the anchor versus reachability of the channel, so no strain after re-census. Fig 10 :53 (no impossible prohibition) and Fig 2 :33 (anchor must not update) likewise govern occupant and reference. The live strain is that Fig 12 :121 says the sign of a maintenance loop decides whether it closes but supplies no instrument for reading that sign; this is named, not hidden.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

Option B. The shape survives and the specific package does not. Capture is a union of a movable reference, an authored checker or pool, and a vocabulary without refusal, with workability as a constraint on every lever. The reference must stay pinned; checkers must be unable to author what they check and must draw on separate evidence; the pool must be unauthored; a captured standing must be refusable; interests in outcomes are declared and interests in readings are disqualifying; and maintenance is permanent because contact cannot be subtracted from a habitat. No named institution, country or reform wins. This is a `specified`, ungrounded result with a declared `gap` on the discriminating instrument.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. Each claim carries a refuter and the residual is per slot, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a recording instead of a generator, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Under-constraining has the opposite cost: the anchor moves and the drift is coherent, so nobody notices (Fig 2 :34).
- **undoable:** yes. The bindings are conventions and packages, not primitives. A defective binding can be withdrawn without touching the derivation, and `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already do. Rulings carry provenance (Fig 10 :62), so a later reader can locate the error. Nothing requires a constitutional overhaul to test the shape.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A decision-record instrument that distinguishes a pinned reference from a moving one would close C1; a demonstration that a checker authored its own rule, data and certification and remained sound would break C2; a refusal-free vocabulary shown to remove a captured standing would break C3; an office with no interest and no standing that can actually be filled would break the declared-interest reading; and a maintenance-free design with no substituted habitat would break D3. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
