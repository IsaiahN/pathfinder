## DERIVATION

### Q1: Is this a union or a partition?

- Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- Fig 9 :56: *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*
- In `01`, C1 is a pinned reference, C2 is a non-authored checker and pool, C3 is a vocabulary with refusal. These are not three points on an "institutional strength" axis. A country can have excellent audit courts and no refusal, or strong refusal and a reference the agency can rewrite.
- ⇒ The residual is a **union**. It must be unbundled before a lever is chosen (Fig 9 :54). The residue after one lever is categorically different, not merely smaller (Fig 9 :57).
- **Falsifier:** if a dataset shows one lever clearing all three conditions across cases where they initially failed separately, the union claim fails and the three conditions collapse into one quantity.

### Q2: What order do the levers come in?

- Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*
- Fig 3 :20: dependency order, not importance.
- Fig 3 :22: *"A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can."*
- Applied: if the reference can be rewritten by the body being checked (link 1 broken), adding independent auditors (link 4) spends effort above the break. Their verdicts will be ranged against a moving target. If the institution has no term for "refuse a finding" (link 2 broken), adding an appeals court (link 4) gives it a route with no destination.
- ⇒ Order: pin the reference, then bind terms for what a decision affords, then state an objective in those terms, then arrange independent checking, then carry verdicts with provenance.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a case where adding a checker to a process with a movable reference measurably reduces drift. If it does, dependency order is wrong for this domain.

### Q3: When are two arrangements isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- Two reform packages can share all seven atoms: pinned reference, independent auditor, lottery pool, refusal, disclosed interest, public record, maintenance duty. If they bind them differently, for example a lottery pool feeding a refusal body versus an audit court feeding a lottery replacement pool, they are **isomers** under Fig 12 :117. Same atoms, different bonds, different behaviour.
- A package that has only some of the atoms is not a weaker isomer. It is a different substance with a different functional group (Fig 12 :124).
- **Falsifier:** if two packages share every atom and every bond and still behave differently, the bond is not carrying the information the figure says it carries. Re-census the bond, not the cases.

### Q4: What is the cycle, and what is the catalyst?

- Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*
- Maintenance is a cycle: each ruling enters the record, the record changes the pool and the declared stakes, and the next ruling is ranged against that changed habitat. A negative feedback loop is a check; a positive one is a capture spiral. The sign decides, not the presence of the loop.
- Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."* A record-keeping office that survives the ruling it carries is a catalyst. Fig 12 :119 continues that *"Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."* So an audit body whose members are consumed by each inquiry and an audit body whose members are regenerated are different substances even if their written mandate is identical. Nothing in a mandate records which.
- **Falsifier:** show a maintenance loop whose sign cannot be read from any observable output. Then the cycle claim is structurally true but operationally empty, and the verdict must be narrowed.

## CONCLUSION

The three conditions are a union, so the answer is a bundle of levers ordered by dependency, not a single reform. Two packages over the same atoms are isomers when the bonds differ, so a mandate list is not a sufficient description of an arrangement. Maintenance is a signed cycle, and record-keeping parts are catalysts whose spent or regenerated status is not recorded in their mandates. The analogues corroborate from genuinely different fields but never decide.

What survives is the shape. Which package, in which polity, is a declared `gap` (Fig 9 :32).
