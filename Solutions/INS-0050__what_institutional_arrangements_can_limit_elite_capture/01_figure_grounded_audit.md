## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

A public decision is supposed to range against a ground: what actually happens when the rule is applied, a published count, a court finding, a market price, an outcome. Capture is the name we give to a family of cases in which that ground stops settling the decision and the decision instead converges on whoever had the most influence.

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :14-15 describe the collapse directly: the parties *"converge, and what they converge on"* is *"whoever had the most influence."* Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."* Fig 2 :34: *"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."*

That last clause is the residual. Capture is hard to see from inside because the drift is coherent. Everyone in the room agrees, and the agreement feels like truth, while the reference point has quietly moved.

Two things must be held apart, or the question becomes malformed:

1. **The anti-capture requirement:** keep a ground that the process cannot move; keep checkers that cannot author what they check; keep a pool that an interested party did not compose; keep the power to refuse a captured term.
2. **The workability requirement:** the arrangement must be executable by real occupants. Fig 10 :53: *"And a prohibition nothing can satisfy is a prohibition stated wrong."* Fig 10 :54: *"A rule no real occupant could meet describes an idealisation, not a position."* Fig 10 :55: *"An idealisation licenses inferences no real position would support"* and those inferences get drawn by whoever finds them useful.

An answer that satisfies (1) by violating (2) is not a solution. It is a specification for a committee that never meets.

**Bound as an ordinary term (formula step "Direction").** What an answer would look like: a finite set of constraints on an arrangement, each stated in effect terms, each with a refuter, such that an auditor could look at a decision record and say whether the ground was reachable and whether decisions still resolved. Written down and bound now, so it accrues a residual like any other term instead of remaining an invisible preference (Fig 5 :65-66).

## 1. Domain census, before any figure line is chosen

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** many. Capture is indexed per decision slot, not per institution and not per country. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A single national "capture score" throws away the only signal that says where to look.
- **Symmetry:** asymmetric. The defect is not that two sides disagree symmetrically. It is that one party has *"an interest in the reading"* (Fig 10 :59) and therefore bends the instrument. Fig 10 :59: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."*
- **Scale:** three nested scales, individual decision, institution, polity, with a lossy transform between. Fig 10 :68: *"Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between."* What counts as a working check at one scale is a recording at the next.

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

A domain needs a nameable anchor. Where none can be named, it is not yet a domain.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1, the decision frame | decision-makers, agenda-setters, citizens, applicants | votes, influence, agreements, assignments | the outcome the public interest would settle on, observable in principle as a count, record, price, audit finding or court finding | partition: which compositions cross from opinion to ground |
| D2, the checking seat | auditors, courts, inspectors, opposition, journalists, peer reviewers | verdicts and residuals | the ground, never agreement | partition: who may range against the ground and who may not |
| D3, the habitat and selection frame | the pool of decision-makers and checkers, interest groups, media, information channels | contact and introduction | the unauthored habitat, enumerated not composed | union: capture is a compound of conditions, not a list of bad actors |

All three have nameable anchors, so all three are domains. D1 without an anchor would collapse into pure negotiation, which is Fig 2 panel 1.

### 1.3 Partition or union

Capture is a **union compound**, not a single hard question and not a partition. Three conditions bind:

- **C1, the ground is allowed to update.** The thing that should settle the decision moves when the frame pushes on it. Fig 2 :25: *"pinned: it does not update on A or B"* is the requirement; Fig 2 :34 is the failure.
- **C2, the checker or the pool is authored by the interested party.** Fig 10 :74: *"A candidate set chosen by a participant is an authored room however well the selection is reasoned, so the pool must be unauthored first and examined second."* An interest in the reading is structural, so the check must be arranged, not wished away.
- **C3, the vocabulary has no refusal.** A captured term can be accumulated over but not refused. Operators table :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."* Fig 10 :82: *"Three panels, one move: arrange for refusal."*

Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :56: *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."* A transparency rule clears part of C1; an independent audit clears part of C2; neither touches C3. The intersections need more than one lever, and the residue is categorically different, not merely smaller (Fig 9 :57).

The workability requirement is **not** a fourth condition. It is a constraint on every lever: a lever that no real occupant can operate is not a lever.

### 1.4 Order by dependency (Fig 3)

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*

1. **Anchor reachable (link 1).** Can anything at all range against a ground that the process cannot move? Fig 10 :29: *"→ keep the anchor reachable"* and Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does."* If the channel is severed, the output is not a verdict but *"you have lost touch"* (Fig 10 :28).
2. **Terms for what a decision affords (link 2).** Does the institution have vocabulary for what can be moved, blocked, compared, refused?
3. **Objective (link 3).** Can it say what would count as progress in its own terms? Fig 3 :25-27 warns this is where chains usually break because it has neither an obvious failure nor an obvious test.
4. **Checking and carrying (links 4 and 5).** Independence, rotation, provenance.

Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Adding more auditors above a broken anchor is effort spent above the break. Fig 3 :22: *"A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can."*

## 3. Findings, F1 to F10

### F1: Capture is an anchor failure before it is a moral failure (governs C1)

Fig 2 :29-30: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."* Fig 2 :34: *"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."*

The practical shape: an arrangement limits capture to the extent that its ground is pinned against the very frame it grades. A published dataset that the agency being audited can revise at will is not a ground. A court whose budget and tenure are set by the party it judges is moving toward panel 1. Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*

World corroboration, secondary: Federalist No. 51 (Hamilton or Madison, 1788, Yale Avalon Project), *"Ambition must be made to counteract ambition."* This predicts that counter-power is arranged rather than discovered in virtuous occupants. It corroborates Fig 10 :59, not Fig 2 :29, so it is a different witness for a neighbouring point.

**Refuter:** exhibit a decision process whose outcome settles a question without any pinned reference, and show that its drift trends downward against an independent record. If that is impossible, C1 stands.

### F2: The checker must not be able to author what it checks

Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Fig 10 :43: *"Soundness requires the checker to be unable to produce what it is checking. Never a player, never a proxy, never the author of a verdict or an atom."* Fig 10 :86: *"Outside the slice being graded. Whoever is measured by a reading cannot own it."*

This is the structural reason for separation of functions, independent audit, an opposition with its own information, and a free press with separate sources. It is **not** a claim that a named country has such separation, and it is **not** a claim that formal letterhead suffices. Fig 8 :48: *"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."* Two regulators who read the same industry brief are one frame wearing two names (Fig 8 :49).

World corroboration, secondary: principal-agent theory (Jensen and Meckling 1976, via the Wikipedia article opened this session) names the divergence of interest and information asymmetry directly: *"The principal-agent problem typically arises where the two parties have different interests and asymmetric information."* It predicts monitoring and incentive alignment, and it states that *"the multiple principal problem is particularly serious in the public sector."* Corroborates the structural reading. Overlap with the institutional-economics pool is declared.

**Refuter:** show a check that is sound while the checker can write the rule, supply the data, and certify the result. Under Fig 10 :42 that is not a check. If a real case shows otherwise, the figure is wrong for this domain and the domain must be re-censused.

### F3: Independence is bought, not declared, and it has a price (governs C2)

Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Fig 10 :80: *"Frames whose independence is renewed by separate experience can be maintained; frames whose correlation is fixed at origin can only be rotated, because no amount of use makes them less alike and replacement is the only refresh."*

Two consequences. First, rotating the same four people through five committees buys spelling, not independence. Second, an anti-capture design must spend independence where the cost of being wrong is high, and need not spend it where the cost is low. This is a budget rule, not a verdict rule.

World corroboration, secondary and low overlap: Byzantine fault tolerance (Lamport, Shostak and Pease 1982, via Wikipedia's Byzantine fault article opened this session). *"Without message signing, Byzantine fault tolerance can only be achieved if the total number of generals is greater than three times the number of disloyal (faulty) generals."* Engineering buys tolerance with a numerical margin of independent nodes, not with trust. Corroborates Fig 2 :42 from a genuinely different field.

**Refuter:** exhibit two frames with identical evidence whose agreement carries verdict weight under these figures. Fig 2 :40 refuses it. If a case requires it, the verdict on independence must be re-derived.

### F4: The pool must be unauthored, so sortition and open qualification enter as mechanisms, not as fashions

Fig 10 :73: *"The residual selects the members, but only from what is available."* Fig 10 :74: *"A candidate set chosen by a participant is an authored room however well the selection is reasoned, so the pool must be unauthored first and examined second."* Fig 10 :75: *"A conscientious selection is still a selection, so unauthored needs a mechanism:"* and the figure lists, at :76 to :79, *"a lottery", "everyone who qualifies", "a set assembled before the question existed"*, and *"or the habitat itself, enumerated rather than composed."*

World corroboration, secondary: sortition (Wikipedia article opened this session, citing Manin 1997, Dahl 1989, Fishkin 2009, OECD 2020). *"In governance, sortition is the selection of public officials or jurors at random, i.e., by lottery, in order to obtain a representative sample."* The Venetian case is on point: *"The use of lot to select nominators made it more difficult for political sects to exert power, and discouraged campaigning."* Citizens' assemblies are reported to have *"added advantages in issues where politicians have a conflict of interest."* Corroborates the unauthored-pool requirement and the declared-stake requirement.

A correction kept visible: this is **not** an endorsement of lottocracy as the best regime. It is the narrow claim that an interested party must not compose the pool it is graded by. Which of the four unauthored mechanisms fits a given place is a `gap`.

**Refuter:** show a pool authored by an interested party whose selection does not bias the residual, after the evidence overlap is measured. Or show that all four unauthored mechanisms are unavailable in a real case without habitat substitution; that would move the verdict to `gap`, not flip it.

### F5: Capture cannot be refused by a rule set that has no negation

Fig 10 :82: *"Three panels, one move: arrange for refusal."* Operators table :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."* Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."*

An institution whose only move is to add rules, add disclosures and add oversight bodies, with no power to disallow a captured term, remove a standing, or refuse a finding, can be corrected and cannot carry the correction. Refusal is unary, not a bond (Operators table :70-74), so it must not be smuggled into the bond count as a seventh relation.

World corroboration, secondary: Arrow's impossibility theorem (Wikipedia, Arrow 1950 and 1963, opened this session). *"no group decision-making procedure based on ordinal utilities ... can satisfy the requirements of rational choice."* Arrow's own remark is the workability half: *"Most systems are not going to work badly all of the time. All I proved is that all can work badly at times."* Corroborates that a perfect rule set is not on offer and that refusal must be arranged rather than aggregated away. Low overlap with political science.

**Refuter:** show a decision vocabulary with no unary refusal that nonetheless removes a captured standing. Under the operators table this is structurally impossible. If a working example is produced, the operator claim needs re-census.

### F6: Workability is a figure constraint, not a compromise with the figures

Fig 10 :53: *"And a prohibition nothing can satisfy is a prohibition stated wrong."* Fig 10 :54: *"A rule no real occupant could meet describes an idealisation, not a position."* Fig 10 :55: *"An idealisation licenses inferences no real position would support"* and those inferences *"will be drawn by whoever"* finds them useful (Fig 10 :55-56). Fig 10 :58: *"A seat with no stake at all does not exist."* Fig 10 :59: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* Fig 10 :60: *"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."*

This is where the second half of the question is answered. The workable anti-capture arrangement does **not** require disinterested occupants. It requires that the occupant's interest in the outcome not be an interest in which reading the instrument returns. Interests are declared, not abolished. Fig 10 :61: *"And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."* A reputation earned by a past clean ruling is itself a stake that must be declared.

World corroboration, secondary: dominance hierarchy (Wikipedia, opened this session). The ethology record notes that in some species *"dominant animals must maintain alliances with subordinates and grant them favours to receive their support in order to retain their dominant rank."* The point is not that parliaments are primate troops. It is that even dominance is checked by arranged counter-power, not by removing dominance. Low overlap with institutional theory. Corroborates the "limit, not eliminate" reading.

**Refuter:** exhibit a real office whose occupant has no interest in any outcome and no standing, and show it can be filled and held. If none can, any design premised on pure impartiality is an idealisation and must be withdrawn.

### F7: Prevention is permanent work, and isolation substitutes a habitat

Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* This refuses the answer "elect better people" as a complete answer. Fig 11 :61: *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* Fig 11 :63: *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."*

Isolation looks like the clean solution: move the decision into a sealed commission, a test harness, a special tribunal. Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Two costs are invisible until they act: Fig 11 :68 *"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"* and Fig 11 :69 *"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts."* Fig 11 :70: *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."*

World corroboration, secondary: Ostrom (Nobel Prize facts, 2009, Nobel Foundation). *"She showed that when natural resources are jointly used by their users, in time, rules are established for how these are to be cared for and used in a way that is both economically and ecologically sustainable."* Users build workable rules in place; they do not exit the habitat. Corroborates F7. Shares an institutional-economics pool with F1 and F10.

**Refuter:** exhibit an anti-capture arrangement that works once and needs no maintenance, with no unchosen actors introduced and no contact lost. Fig 11 :61-69 predicts this cannot be shown.

### F8: A room of agreeable experts cannot corroborate the design

Fig 7 :13: *"agreement compounds, evidence does not."* Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 7 :36: *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."* Fig 7 :41: *"the pool it is drawn from must be unauthored."*

This governs outreach, not just theory. A panel selected because its members are likely to like the shape claim is an authored room, and its agreement is a convention. Seat Map :163: *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."*

**Refuter:** show a selected-for-agreement panel whose agreement carries verdict weight under these figures. Fig 2 :40 refuses it. The practical refuter is procedural: ask each checker for a failure condition before showing them the claim, and see whether the recorded residuals differ.

### F9: Actor valence is relative to the residual, so no standing blacklist survives

Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* Fig 11 :52: *"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles."* A businesses, unions, foundations, ministries and media outlets all change sign when the residual changes. Second-order effects sit three steps out.

This retracts any reading of the earlier sections that treats "the elite" as a fixed kind. The elite is whoever currently has an interest in the reading plus the power to move the frame. That is a relation, not a category.

World corroboration, secondary: elite capture literature (Wikipedia article opened this session) notes that *"Elite capture is a form of corruption whereby public resources are biased for the benefit of a small group of people with superior social status (the elite), often damaging the welfare of the larger population"* and that *"Elite capture of public resources by local elites can be relatively lessened by the presence of media to balance the asymmetry of information."* The second sentence is the F2/F3 point in a different field. Wikipedia is a map, not an authority; it is recorded as such in the ledger.

**Refuter:** name an actor that is a malfactor for every residual in the habitat. By Fig 11 :49-51 the name will turn out to be a relation in disguise.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written here before the recommendation, per Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A decision process settles questions with no pinned ground and its drift against an independent record does not trend down.
2. A check is demonstrably sound while the checker authored the rule, the data and the certification.
3. Two frames drawing on one evidence pool produce a verdict that survives the effective-sample-size correction.
4. A pool composed by an interested party shows no residual bias after overlap is measured.
5. A working office is exhibited whose occupant has no interest in any outcome and no standing.
6. An anti-capture regime is shown to work once with no maintenance and no substituted habitat.
7. A decision vocabulary with no unary refusal is shown to remove a captured standing.
8. A single lever is shown to clear the intersections of C1, C2 and C3.

If any fires, the relevant finding is withdrawn and the domain is re-censused. Until one is run, no part of this is `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. State the residual per decision slot, never as a national average (F1).
2. Spend independence where the cost of being wrong is high, and measure it as evidence overlap (F3).
3. Make the pool unauthored by an interested party using one of the four named mechanisms, and record which one and why (F4).
4. Declare interests and standings rather than pretending to abolish them (F6).
5. Keep a unary refusal available, and keep provenance on every ruling so a later reader can locate the error (F5, Fig 10 :62, :65).
6. Treat maintenance as the job, not as a temporary campaign (F7).
7. Offer the shape and its refuters first; ask what would fail; show the framework only after a verdict is recorded (F8).
8. Name no winning institution. Report the stop as a `gap` with a discriminator in effect terms (F10).
