## What is being decided, in effect terms

The question is not "are elites bad" and not "which constitution is best." It is this: a public decision is supposed to be settled by something the decision process cannot move, an outcome, a count, a published record, a court finding, a price. Elite capture is what happens when that something starts updating on the elite's own reading, so decisions converge on whoever had the most influence rather than on what the ground returns (Fig 2 :14-15). The second half of the question is a hard constraint, not a polite caveat: an arrangement that stops all decisions, or that requires a decision-maker with no interest at all, is a prohibition no real occupant can satisfy (Fig 10 :53-54). The two halves must be answered together.

- Option A: treat capture as a moral defect of bad actors and try to remove elite influence or install perfectly impartial occupants. What it leads to: an idealisation that describes nobody, a ground with a reputation to protect, a checker that authors what it checks, and a single lever that clears one condition while leaving the intersections standing.
- Option B: arrange the checking structure so the ground cannot be moved by the frame, buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong, keep checkers unable to author what they check, draw the pool from an unauthored mechanism, declare unavoidable stakes, keep a record, preserve the power to refuse, and accept that maintenance is permanent work. What it leads to: capture is bounded and locatable per decision slot, decisions keep resolving because the rules are satisfiable by real occupants, and the exact arrangement for a given polity stays an honest `gap`.

An answer would look like a shape: a set of constraints any working arrangement must satisfy, plus refuters written in advance. It would not look like a named institution declared the winner, because the figures return a kind of thing, never the answer itself (Fig 9 :32). That ordinary-term binding is done explicitly in section 2 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with this derivation.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **A room does not upgrade the label.** *"A room reaching a conclusion without the ground is agreement, and agreement among frames is a weighted average of influence. Adding people raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor."* (Seat Map :283). A list of people selected for agreement manufactures a convention, not a check.
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* (Seat Map :163). Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."*
3. **Asking for trust before a check spends the only independence the reader contributes.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Seat 4 must not hold the interiors, the records, the anchor, any stake, or any authority (Seat Map :139).

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. The public opt-in venues in `03` avoid that practical failure as well as the structural one.
