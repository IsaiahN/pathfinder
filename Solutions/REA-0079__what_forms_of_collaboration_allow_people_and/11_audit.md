## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07_the_tether_answer.md`: anchor, independence, residual first, methods exchange, verifier non-access, selection between rounds.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete protocol, for example "two models debate and a human judge decides".

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the constitutive anchor split (F11): the shorthand says "an anchor", the concrete protocol must decide whether the question is about people or about a test result;
- the independence condition: the shorthand says "different evidence", but two instances of one model on one prompt look like two frames in the org chart;
- the admission boundary: the shorthand says "no interiors", but a testable assumption must be admitted, as the HCI study forced the primary to narrow;
- the selection condition: the shorthand says "select between rounds", which a fixed debate protocol with a standing judge does not do.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a non-updating anchor with two independently sourced frames and a per-slot residual. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the shorthand as identical to the figures would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
