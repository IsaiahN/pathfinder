**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Subject:** Would this count as a failure of human AI blind spot correction?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a blind spot is a per-slot residual that a frame cannot read because its library does not carry the term. From that, three things follow about collaboration between a person and an AI system.
>
> 1. Correction is not a two-frame exchange. Each frame has to range against something that does not move when either frame pushes on it: a measurement, a test, an outcome. If the two simply update on one another, their agreement is drift and nobody notices, because the drift is coherent. The figure line is: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
> 2. The two frames must draw on different evidence. Two instruments pointed at the same thing are two frames; two people reading the same report are not. If the human and the AI draw on one pool, their agreement is the pool talking.
> 3. The residual has to be described before the collaborator is chosen. If you pick the collaborator first, any frame you pick will seem to fit. What gets exchanged across the pair should be methods and head starts, not recordings of what worked once.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that this has been measured, that I can name the missing primitive in any particular blind spot, that more agreement between a human and an AI is evidence of correction, or that the framework is the only one that could say this. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
>
> **What would refute it, written down before this message was sent:**
> 1. a pair with no shared anchor and continuous mutual updating reduces held-out, per-slot error at least as well as a pair ranging against a fixed test;
> 2. teams told only "use AI as a collaborator" match teams first given a per-slot residual description at detecting blind spots;
> 3. a checker given the full generative interior of the AI's answer detects more blind spots than a checker given only the characterised residual and the observed failure.
>
> If any one holds, the shape claim is wrong for this domain and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.

> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Eight-step formula: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so an output that was contrived to fit the formula is not a result. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## The two-stage rule

Stage 1: send the claim, the three failure conditions and nothing about the framework's interiors. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record that verdict before any theory is shown.

Stage 2, opt in only: if they are curious, offer the three local links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because once the interiors are held the frame is no longer outside (Fig 2 :40; Fig 10 :42).

There is no stage that asks for trust, and no stage that asks for a show of hands.
