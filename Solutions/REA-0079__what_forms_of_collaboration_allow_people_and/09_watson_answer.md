# WATSON answer: what collaboration forms let people and AI correct blind spots?

**Mode: `specified` on the forms, `general` on the shape.** Not `grounded`.

**Status: SIMULATED DERIVATION, not a ground reading.** This WATSON run synthesises material already in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, `05_what_shape_means.md`, `06_isomers_and_order.md`, `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md`. It is checked for internal consistency against the figures. By its own rule (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, LLM output is "simulacra" and "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself") it cannot settle the question it reports on. No human-AI pair was measured.

## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, which collaboration arrangements allow a person and an AI system to reduce each other's blind spots, where a blind spot is a per-slot residual a frame cannot read. Option A: any arrangement in which the pair communicates and converges. Option B: only arrangements in which each frame ranges separately against a non-updating anchor, with low evidence overlap, a residual described before a collaborator is chosen, methods rather than recordings exchanged, verifier non-access, and selection between successive rounds against the residual. What each leads to: A produces coherent drift or the pool talking, both of which look like correction from inside; B produces a checkable surplus with an admissible channel and a written refuter.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** A per-slot error a frame cannot perceive because its library lacks the term (Fig 1 :11, :19; Symbols table :39). Arity: ostensibly two frames, but correction requires a third non-updating term, so R is three-place with a two-frame surface. Symmetry: symmetric in office only; the operation is ordered (describe, book, import, settle). Scale: within session, across sessions, across a chain of collaborations. Fig 9 makes it a union of conditions, not one hard question: a case in several conditions is not a harder case of either.
- **Domain 1, frame and ground contact.** Actors: human frame, AI frame, ground. Currency: per-slot residual, settlement. Anchor: measurement, test or outcome that does not update. Partition boundary: what may compose across frame and ground.
- **Domain 2, coupling between frames.** Actors: two libraries, methods, head starts. Currency: reapplicable methods and provenance. Anchor: the described residual. Union: what may cross is a method, not a recording.
- **Domain 3, verification seat.** Actors: maker, outside frame, ground maintainer, ground. Currency: verdicts, non-access, provenance. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Partition by who may see what.
- **Domain 4, habitat and team composition.** Actors: people, AI instances, instruments, data, conditions. Currency: contact, enumeration, introduction. Anchor: the ground through the habitat. Union of actors in contact with the residual.
- **Domain 5, chain over rounds.** Actors: room t, room t+1, ground. Currency: surfaced residual, bracket fidelity, ground pressure. Anchor: the ground. Ordered selection, not composition.
- **Order by dependency.** Anchor first, then per-slot perception, then terms for what a slot affords and what would count as correction, then collaborator, then carry and round-trip measurement (Fig 3). Optimising a later link cannot rescue an earlier break.

No nameable anchor means not yet a correction domain. The proposed domain "general trust between the pair" is declined because trust has no anchor that fails to move when the pair pushes on it, making it a frame-internal quantity (Fig 1 :24-27).

Strain test applied: "one another" strained against Fig 2 :46 and was re-censused as symmetry of office, not of operation (`06_isomers_and_order.md` Q3). The strong verification-access claim strained against one HCI study and was narrowed to "withhold the reconstructive interior, admit the testable assumption" (`01` section 7).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

Reading B is correct. Correction between a person and an AI system is triangulation against a non-updating anchor, not negotiation. The forms that permit it share a bond: a non-updating anchor, low evidence overlap, a residual described before the collaborator is chosen and kept per slot, methods and head starts rather than recordings, verifier non-access narrowed to the reconstructive interior, and selection between successive rounds against the residual the last round surfaced. The same participants under a mutual-update or shared-evidence bond are a different substance with the same org chart: coherent drift or the pool talking. Capability lives in the agent-and-habitat pair, not in a model upgrade. Two layers of the question need two differently legitimated anchors. This is a `specified`, ungrounded, candidate result; no named protocol is validated by it.

- **cost if wrong:** a wrong design belief about human-AI teams, bought by people who trusted the shape, plus the opportunity cost of a ground experiment not run. The avoidable part is spending the independence of outside readers by sending them the framework before their verdict, which Fig 2 :42 prices.
- **undoable:** yes. The named-practice bindings are removable without touching the shape; `07_the_tether_answer.md` already marks them as instances. A published claim can be superseded by a corrected packet. No primitive has to be withdrawn.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, never agreement. Specifically: a held-out per-slot measurement in which a no-anchor pair matches or beats the anchored pair; low-overlap pairs failing to beat high-overlap pairs; residual-first teams failing to beat frame-first teams; checkers with the full generative interior detecting more blind spots; or pooled scores beating per-slot readings. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
