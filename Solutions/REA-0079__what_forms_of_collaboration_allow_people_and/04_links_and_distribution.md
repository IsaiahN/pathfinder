## Distribution: offer, do not push

- Fig 4 :22 *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator.
- Fig 10 :42 *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- Fig 9 :27 *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the source.
- Fig 2 :40 *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (full line begins "disqualifying for a verdict:"). A campaign selected for people likely to agree produces a convention, not corroboration.
- Fig 2 :42 *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* The cost here is a wrong belief about how to design human-AI teams, so independence is worth paying for.

Recommended order in a reply: (1) the shape claim and the three refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and record it; (3) only then the three local links, with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

Stage 1 before stage 2 is Fig 10 :52 *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* The recipient checks the claim without holding the framework's interior.

Stage 2 is opt in by construction. Once the interior is held, the recipient's agreement stops being evidence, so the stage-1 verdict must be recorded first and is never pooled as corroboration.

Stage 3, if it happens, sends a method. Fig 4 :22 permits this: a generator can be given away without giving away an answer. Each user still needs a ground of their own and must write a refuter before running it, because `THE_FORMULA.md` says a frame "cannot check the direction of its own question".
