## 0. The residual in effect terms

A blind spot is not a lack of effort. It is a residual on a slot that a frame cannot perceive because its library does not carry the term for it. Fig 1 :11 establishes the indexed quantity, Fig 1 :19 states the rule, and the Symbols table :39 says what cannot be perceived yet is residual and simply cannot be read.

The question asks for forms of collaboration H such that, when a person and an AI system work together, the per-slot residual of each falls because of contact with the other and with a ground. Two readings are available:

- **Reading A:** H is a two-place relation. They correct each other by exchanging information and reaching agreement.
- **Reading B:** H is a three-term arrangement. Each frame is corrected separately by something that does not move when either frame pushes on it, and the two frames stay independent.

What each leads to: A leads to coherent drift or to a shared pool talking, both of which look like correction from inside (Fig 2 :34). B leads to a checkable surplus, because the union of two differing closures can reach what neither reaches alone (Fig 8 :45). The figures decide between them before any world literature is consulted.

The sought-for shape is bound as an ordinary term before the run, per `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": *a correcting collaboration is an arrangement in which both frames range against a non-updating anchor, with low evidence overlap, a residual described before a frame is chosen, methods rather than recordings exchanged, verifier non-access, and selection between successive rounds against the surfaced residual.* That binding is what the pre-registered refuter in section 4 points at.

## 1. Domain census

### R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** ostensibly two (person, AI), but correction requires a third term that does not update. So R is a three-place relation with a two-frame surface. A two-place reading is a mis-census, not a simpler theory.
- **Symmetry:** the phrase "one another" is symmetric in office only. Fig 2 :46 has each frame corrected separately; Fig 13 :4-5 has the two kinds in lockstep with crossing as a change of role. The operation is ordered: describe, book, import, settle. See `06_isomers_and_order.md` Q3.
- **Scale:** within a session (the per-step loop), across sessions (Fig 4 : methods up, head starts down), and across a chain of collaborations (Fig 7 : rooms selected against the surfaced residual).

### One line per domain

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Frame / ground contact | human frame, AI frame, the ground | per-slot residual R, settlement | a measurement, test or outcome that does not update | triangulation; a partition boundary (what may compose across frames and ground) |
| D2 Coupling between frames | two libraries, methods, head starts | reapplicable methods, provenance | the described residual | union; what may cross is a method, not a recording |
| D3 Verification seat | maker, outside frame, ground maintainer, ground | verdicts, non-access, provenance | the ground, never agreement | partition by who may see what |
| D4 Habitat and team composition | people, AI instances, instruments, data, conditions | contact, enumeration, introduction | the ground through the habitat | union of actors in contact with the residual |
| D5 Chain over rounds | room t, room t+1, the ground | surfaced residual, bracket fidelity, ground pressure | the ground | ordered selection, not composition |

No nameable anchor means it is not yet a correction domain. D1 through D5 all have one. A proposed sixth domain, "general trust between the pair", is declined: trust has no anchor that fails to move when the pair pushes on it, so it is a frame-internal quantity (Fig 1 :24-27), not a domain.

### Partition or union

The residual is a **union** of conditions, not a list. Fig 9 :55 notes that a bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions and that a single lever clears only the region where one condition holds. So "add a second reviewer" clears the region where independence was missing and leaves the anchor, description and selection intersections standing. Each overlap is a compound with its own loop.

### Dependency order (Fig 3)

1. Anchor exists and does not update (else nothing to range against).
2. Residual per slot is perceived and kept per slot (else no signal about where to look).
3. Terms exist for what a slot affords and what would count as correction (the middle link, usually uninstrumented).
4. A collaborator with a differing closure is booked against that residual.
5. A method, not a recording, is carried to the next round and the round trip gap is measured.

Optimising a later link cannot rescue an earlier break (Fig 3 :19).

## 3. Findings

Each finding is `general` (structural) or `specified` (falsifiable here), and carries its own refuter. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.

### F1 : Correction is triangulation, not negotiation (decisive, `general`)

Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**
Fig 2 :45: **"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."**
Fig 2 :46: **"Each frame is corrected by it separately, and what makes the correction sound is that neither frame can move it."**

The anchor is not a third party to the disagreement and is not a tie-breaker in a vote; it is what both range against. Applying this to D1: a person and an AI that reconcile their answers by discussion have not thereby corrected anything. A correction has occurred only if an independent reading changed each of them separately.

**Refuter:** a pair with no anchor whose members continuously update on each other reduces held-out per-slot error at least as fast as a pair ranging against a fixed test. If that is shown, the mirror law is wrong for this domain and F1 is withdrawn.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 2 and Fig 5's fifth kind (a refused term pays the same bargain), with low overlap between the ground figure and the mint pipeline.

### F2 : The two collapses that imitation of correction produces (`general`)

Fig 2 :13: **"no anchor; each updates on the others"**
Fig 2 :14: **"they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence"**
Fig 2 :18: **"one evidence pool"**
Fig 2 :19: **"they never talk, and still agree"**
Fig 2 :20: **"so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other"**
Fig 2 :33: **"The anchor must not update."**

Two different failures are named. In the first, the pair talks and drifts coherently toward whoever had more influence. In the second, the pair never talks and agrees anyway because both read the same source. A third variant follows from Fig 2 :34: **"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."** A shared rubric that the pair is allowed to revise is an anchor that updates, so it is panel 1 arrived at by a different route.

World check: Lyell and Coiera's systematic review finds overreliance even in single-task settings when verification complexity is high; the Wikipedia automation bias entry reports that a second crew member does not necessarily remove the bias. Both are the same collapse at the human-machine and human-human levels, but they share a topic pool, so they count as one witness.

**Refuter:** demonstrate that a rubric which both parties may revise, with no external test, still reduces held-out error more than a rubric neither may revise.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 2 panels 1 and 2 and the world literature, with the world overlap declared.

### F3 : Independence is the scarce quantity, not the number of participants (`specified`)

Fig 2 :31: **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."**
Fig 2 :31: **"Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."**
Fig 8 :49: **"Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."**
Fig 6 :53: **"searchers drawing on the same evidence are one searcher wearing many names"**

For D2 this means the relevant question about a person and an AI is not "do they collaborate?" but "what evidence does each draw on that the other does not?". Two AI instances from the same base model given the same prompt are one frame wearing two names. Adding a third such instance adds spelling, not search. The engineering analogue is bagging and random forests, where de-correlating the trees, not adding correlated ones, buys the accuracy gain.

**Refuter:** a random-pairs experiment in which measured low evidence overlap does not outperform high overlap on errors that neither party catches alone.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 2, Fig 8 and Fig 6, plus an independent field (machine learning). The figure verdict does not rest on the world source.

### F4 : Residual first, frame second (`specified`)

Fig 9 :27: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**
Fig 8 :53: **"Residual first, frame second."**
Fig 9 :31: **"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"**
Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

A collaboration form cannot be chosen until the blind spot is described in effect terms. "The AI is better at X, the human at Y" is a frame-first description and will fit any collaborator. "On slot s the system's prediction missed observation o, and the human's prediction missed it too, in different directions" is a residual description. It names a habitat to search, not a product.

**Refuter:** teams told only to collaborate with AI match teams first given a per-slot residual description on blind-spot detection.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 9, Fig 8 and Fig 1.

### F5 : The union is mute unless a residual is described and a surplus crosses back (`general`)

Fig 8 :19: **"closures differ, so a surplus exists"**
Fig 8 :20: **"but no residual is described"**
Fig 8 :21: **"nothing was aimed; the union is mute"**
Fig 8 :32: **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."**
Fig 8 :46: **"Composition crosses the boundary between them, and that surplus is where imports live."**
Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**

Two frames with different closures can reach what neither reaches alone, but the surplus does not announce itself. An undirected interdisciplinary conversation has a surplus and no aim. What makes it a correction is a described residual plus a return path: the atom crosses back into the pair's library (Fig 8 :26). Any practice borrowed this way carries a provenance and a shadow test, and is debited against the lending frame's independence (Fig 6 :24 **"Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."**).

**Refuter:** two frames with provably differing closures, working on a described residual, produce no correction that neither produced alone and no bearable provenance for what they did exchange.

**Status:** single-source for the figure mechanism (Fig 8), corroborated at low overlap by the ensemble-methods literature.

### F6 : What may cross is a method, and the checker must not hold the interior (`specified`)

Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**
Fig 4 :25: **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."**
Fig 10 :42: **"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."**
Fig 10 :52: **"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."**

A correction arrangement that ships the full transcript of how the AI reached its answer to the human checker has given the checker the frame's interior. The checker then grades reports against its own picture rather than against the ground. Fig 9 :51 states the admissible channel: **"what the verifier may see: evidence bearing on the residual is admissible; evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel, and the verifier is denied it"**

Important scope point: a reliability statistic about the system is evidence bearing on the residual, so it is admissible. The generative trace that lets the checker reconstruct the answer is not. That distinction is what resolves the conflict with the world literature in section 7.

**Refuter:** a checker given the full generative interior detects more blind spots than a checker given only the characterised residual and the observed failure, with the same ground available. If so, F6 is false for this domain.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 4, Fig 10 and Fig 9; the strong form was strained by one world source and re-censused (section 7).

### F7 : The ground does not decay; the channel does (`general`)

Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**
Fig 10 :25: **"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"**
Fig 10 :27: **"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"**
Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

A collaboration can keep producing output after it has lost contact with the ground, and the output looks exactly as it did before. Pooled readings are named as a channel failure: averaging the pair's confidence into one score is a saturated metric. The LLM-writing study from MIT Media Lab reports that neural connectivity scaled down with external support and that participants using an LLM could not quote their own essay minutes later. That is a channel observation from a different field, declared preliminary and not peer reviewed.

**Refuter:** blind-spot detection improves when per-slot readings are pooled into one aggregate score rather than kept separate.

**Status:** single-source in the figures (Fig 10), corroborated by a preprint, so not independent enough to raise the mode.

### F8 : Select between collaborations; do not hand the same room along (`specified`)

Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**
Fig 7 :36: **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."**
Fig 7 :13: **"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"**
Fig 11 :39: **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**
Fig 11 :40: **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

A sequence of agreements is motion, not progress. The next collaborator has to differ from the previous one and match the residual the previous one surfaced. Fig 11 extends this from frames to habitat: changing the model without changing what the pair is in contact with changes nothing, however much it improves. The labour-economics finding that the same AI tool helps novices substantially and experts minimally is the world version of valence relative to residual, not a property of the tool.

**Refuter:** a team that never changes composition or contact with the ground improves blind-spot correction indefinitely by adding more agreement rounds.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 7 and Fig 11; the world finding is corroboration at low overlap.

### F9 : Enumerate the habitat; valence is relative to the residual (`general`)

Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**
Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
Fig 11 :50: **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**
Fig 11 :73: **"The residual selects the members, but only from what is available."**

The list of who may correct whom is read off the world, not composed from a role chart. An AI instance that is a benefactor for a factual-recall residual may be a malfactor for a calibration residual. There is no standing list of good collaborators that survives a change of residual. Selection is from what is available, and a pool chosen by a participant is an authored room.

**Refuter:** a fixed, role-defined team retains the same correction performance across residuals of different kinds.

**Status:** single-source in the figures (Fig 11), corroborated at low overlap by the labour-economics heterogeneity result.

### F10 : Declare the stake and keep the lineage (`general`)

Fig 10 :59: **"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."**
Fig 10 :60: **"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it."**
Fig 10 :62: **"A ruling carries provenance like an atom."**
Fig 2 :42: **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."**

A checker who benefits from a particular answer has an interest in the reading and is disqualified, whether or not they are consciously biased. If the interest cannot be removed, it is declared. Every verdict about a collaboration form carries the residual it answered, the reasoning and the date, so a later reader can locate the error. Independence is purchased in proportion to the cost of being wrong; the cost here is a wrong design belief about human-AI teams, so the purchase is worth making.

**Refuter:** a correction arrangement in which the checker benefits from one reading nevertheless has its agreement counted as evidence.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 10 and Fig 2.

### F11 : The anchor is legitimate only for what the question is constitutively about (`general`)

Fig 2 :36: **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."**
Fig 2 :37: **"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."**

This question has two layers and therefore two anchors. For "was this correction good for the people involved?", human judgment is the right anchor; it is constitutively about people. For "did the per-slot residual fall against the test?", human judgment is an instrument only, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure. Mixing the two is how a team ends up with a pleasant collaboration that corrected nothing, or a technically correct correction that is bad for the people in it.

**Refuter:** show a single anchor that legitimately settles both layers without changing its answer when either frame pushes it.

**Status:** single-source (Fig 2 :36-38), stated as a scope condition rather than a verdict.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the recommendation and before `07_the_tether_answer.md`. A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device.

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A pair with a shared non-updating anchor and measured low evidence overlap fails to reduce held-out per-slot residuals more than a high-overlap pair or a single frame.
2. A pair with no anchor, whose members continuously update on each other, reduces held-out per-slot error at the same rate or better than the anchored pair.
3. Teams told only to collaborate with AI match teams first given a per-slot residual description at detecting blind spots.
4. A checker given the full generative interior detects more blind spots than a checker given only the characterised residual and the observed failure, with the same ground available.

If (2) or (4) fires, the mirror and verifier claims are wrong for this domain and the shape claim is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this audit may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the shape, not with a product list: anchor, independence, residual first, methods exchange, verifier non-access, selection between rounds (F1 to F8).
2. Keep residuals per slot. A single satisfaction score destroys the only signal that says where to look (F4, Fig 1 :22).
3. Describe the blind spot in effect terms before choosing the collaborator (F4).
4. Treat any borrowed protocol as an import with a source gate and a shadow test, debited against the lender's independence (F5).
5. Give the checker the residual and the observed failure, not the generative interior (F6).
6. Change the pair when the residual changes; do not hand the same room along (F8).
7. Declare the stake on both layers named in F11 (F10).
8. Send the generator and its refuter, not a recording of a successful collaboration. Fig 4 :25: **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."**

## 7. World conflict, re-census and confirmation

Ghosh, Sarkar, Lindley and Poelitz (arXiv 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18033) report that an assumption-focused cognitive forcing function reduced overreliance without increasing cognitive load. A flat reading says showing the checker more of the AI's plan helps, which conflicts with the strong form of F6.

Per the WATSON rule, a conflict sends the derivation back to the census; it never flips the figure verdict. Re-census: there are two different things called "showing the checker something".

- A stated assumption of the plan, presented so the checker can test it, is **evidence bearing on the residual**. Fig 9 :51 admits it. It is a convention that makes the bug statable (Fig 10 :16-17).
- The model's generative interior, which lets the checker reconstruct the answer, is **evidence bearing only on its maker**. Fig 10 :42 bars it.

So the world source corroborates the need for an explicit bracket and does not refute verifier non-access. F6's strong form is narrowed, not silently erased: what is withheld is the reconstructive interior; what is admitted is the checkable assumption. The narrowed claim is recorded here and used in `08_expert_verification_packet.md`.

The other world sources do not conflict: the labour, machine-learning, metascience, collective-cognition and human-factors sources corroborate separate sub-claims from low-overlap fields. Overlap is declared in `10_source_ledger.csv`. The automation-bias Wikipedia entry and the JAMIA systematic review are one witness, not two.

## 8. Verdict summary

The figures decide Reading B. Correction between a person and an AI system is triangulation against a non-updating anchor, not negotiation. The forms that permit it are distinguished by their bond, not by their participants: a non-updating anchor, low evidence overlap, a residual described before the collaborator is chosen, methods and head starts rather than recordings, verifier non-access, and selection between successive rounds against the residual. The same participants under a mutual-update or shared-pool bond produce coherent drift or the pool talking, not correction.

Mode `specified`, not `grounded`, candidate. Cost if wrong: a wrong design belief about human-AI teams, spent on people who trusted the shape. Undoable: yes, by withdrawing the protocol bindings while keeping the shape. Reversed by: a held-out per-slot measurement, not agreement, and specifically by any of the four pre-registered failures in section 4.
