## The residual in effect terms

A blind spot is a residual on a slot that a frame cannot read because its library does not carry the term (Fig 1 :11, Symbols table :39). The question asks which collaboration forms make two frames, one human and one AI, reduce each other's per-slot residuals. The figures decide that the two-frame picture is incomplete: correction is a three-term relation, frame / frame / ground. A mirror cannot correct you (Fig 2 :35). So the answer is a set of forms that install an anchor, keep the two frames independent, describe the residual before choosing a collaborator, exchange methods rather than recordings, and select between successive collaborations against the residual. Each option leads somewhere different: negotiation leads to coherent drift, a shared evidence pool leads to agreement that is the pool talking, an undirected union is mute, and triangulation buys correction.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation has been tested against a real human-AI team.

Three figure-grounded reasons:

1. Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Selecting people for likely agreement manufactures the correlation that makes their agreement worthless.
2. Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (full line begins "disqualifying for a verdict:"). A recipient who has absorbed the whole framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
3. Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* So the first contact carries the residual and its refuter, not the source.

Legally, scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in venues avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
