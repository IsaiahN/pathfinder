# Expert verification packet: the Tether on human AI blind spot correction

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in human factors, HCI, CSCW or human-AI teaming can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** No human-AI pair was measured for this packet. It states a shape and four failure conditions.

**Status:** simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A blind spot is a per-slot residual a frame cannot read because its library lacks the term. A person and an AI system correct each other's blind spots only in arrangements where each ranges separately against a non-updating anchor, the two draw on different evidence, the residual is described before the collaborator is chosen, what crosses between them is methods and head starts rather than recordings, the checker receives the characterised residual and testable assumptions rather than the generative interior, and successive collaborations are selected against the residual the last one surfaced. Under a mutual-update or shared-evidence bond the same participants produce coherent drift or the pool talking, not correction. The framework returns this shape, not a named protocol.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 : Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 2

- **Statement:** correction is triangulation against an anchor that does not update. Agreement between the two frames is not evidence of correction; each must be corrected separately by the ground.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 2 :46 *"Each frame is corrected by it separately, and what makes the correction sound is that neither frame can move it."* Fig 2 :33 *"The anchor must not update."*
- **Refuter:** a pair with no anchor, whose members continuously update on each other, reduces held-out per-slot error at least as fast as a pair ranging against a fixed test.

### C2 : Team-composition and method; checkable against experiment or engineering practice

- **Statement:** independence of evidence, not headcount, decides whether the pair has any surplus; the collaborator must be chosen after the residual is described, kept per slot; successive collaborations must differ and match the surfaced residual.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :31 *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Fig 8 :49 *"Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."* Fig 9 :27 *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"* Fig 7 :36 *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."*
- **What to check:** in a real pair, can you name evidence the human has that the model does not, and vice versa? Is the residual recorded per slot? Did the next collaborator change when the residual changed?
- **Refuter:** measured low evidence overlap does not outperform high overlap on errors neither party catches alone; or residual-first teams do no better than frame-first teams.

### C3 : Verification seat; checkable against protocol design

- **Statement:** the checker receives the characterised residual, the observed failure and testable assumptions, but not the generative interior that would let it reconstruct the AI's answer. Evidence bearing on the residual is admissible; evidence bearing only on its maker is not.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :42 *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Fig 10 :52 *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Fig 9 :51 *"what the verifier may see: evidence bearing on the residual is admissible; evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel, and the verifier is denied it"*
- **Scope, narrowed after a world conflict:** a stated assumption that can be tested is admissible; the model's reconstructive trace is withheld. See `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 7.
- **Refuter:** with the same ground available, a checker given the full generative interior detects more blind spots than a checker given only the residual and the failure.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that agreement between the pair is evidence of correction.
- Not that mutual updating, shared rubrics with no external test, or one evidence pool correct anything.
- Not a named protocol. "Blind review", "debate" and "red teaming" are bindings, not derivations.
- Not that human judgment is always the anchor. For "was this good for the people?" it is (Fig 2 :37); for "did the residual fall against the test?" it is an instrument only.
- Not that this packet is `grounded`, and not that the framework can validate itself.
- Not a claim about any specific vendor, model or product.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A held-out per-slot test of the anchored, low-overlap pair against a high-overlap pair and against each frame alone.
2. A drift test: a no-anchor pair updating on each other must not beat the anchored pair.
3. A residual-order test: describe-first must beat "just collaborate".
4. An access test: withholding the interior must not lose blind spots.
5. A channel test: per-slot readings must beat one pooled score.

4. Name one instrument that would distinguish a shared pool from separate anchoring, or say why none is possible. (Three minutes.)
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. (Two minutes.)
