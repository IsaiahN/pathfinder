# Order and isomers in human AI correction

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading.

## RESIDUAL

The question uses the phrase "correct one another's blind spots". That is a two-place, apparently symmetric relation. The figures describe correction as a three-term relation in which each frame is corrected separately by an anchor. This file derives the order and the symmetry question that the main audit left as a strain.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Actors: a human frame, an AI frame, an anchor, and in the maintained case a ground-maintainer seat. Currency: per-slot residual, methods, head starts, provenance. Anchor: whatever settles the question and does not move when either frame pushes on it. No nameable anchor means there is no correction domain yet, only negotiation. Partition or union: the question is a union of five conditions (anchor, independence, residual description, exchange rule, selection between rounds), not one lever (Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*). A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.

## Q1: What is the dependency order of a correcting collaboration?

Fig 3 gives five links in dependency order: perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry. Mapped here:

1. **Perception.** Can each frame tell what is in front of it and distinguish it from what was there before? Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* If residuals are averaged into a single satisfaction score, there is no signal about where to look.
2. **Vocabulary.** Does each frame have terms for what the situation affords: what can be checked, moved, measured? This is the link that has no instrument and attracts the least attention (Fig 3 :25-26).
3. **Objective.** Can the pair say what would count as correction in its own terms? In this folder that is the pre-registered refuter. Fig 12 :112 *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."*
4. **Planning.** Which collaborator with which differing closure is booked next? Fig 9 :27 *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*.
5. **Learn and carry.** Fig 4 :22 *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* A protocol that records answers instead of methods produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one (Fig 4 :25).

Fig 3 :19 *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* So a better model at step 4 cannot rescue a pair that has no anchor at step 3. Most effort in practice lands above the break because that is where measurements exist.

**Falsifier:** if a team with no stated objective and no per-slot residual nevertheless reduces blind-spot error by the same amount as a team that wrote both down, the dependency order is wrong for this domain.

## Q2: Are different collaboration forms isomers?

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Take three arrangements with identical ingredients (a person, a model, and a set of tests):

- **Anchored triangulation:** both range against the test, which does not update.
- **Mutual update:** each changes its answer in response to the other, with no independent test.
- **One shared pool:** both read the same report and never talk.

Same org chart, same participants, same number of meetings. The bond differs. Under Fig 2 :13 *"no anchor; each updates on the others"* the second is the coherent-drift collapse; under Fig 2 :18 *"one evidence pool"* the third is the common-cause collapse. Under Fig 2 :46 *"Each frame is corrected by it separately"* the first is the only one that is correction at all.

Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."** An audit that counts "did the human and the AI talk?" has recorded ingredients and not arrangement.

**Falsifier:** if an observer blind to the bond cannot tell these three arrangements apart by their error trajectories on held-out slots, the isomer claim has no behavioural consequence and should be dropped.

## Q3: Is "one another" symmetric?

This is the live strain. Fig 2 :46 says each frame is corrected separately by the anchor, and Fig 2 :33 says *"The anchor must not update."* So the mechanism is not symmetric. If both parties update on each other, Fig 2 :34 says the anchor has become another frame and everything drifts together coherently.

Re-census resolves the strain without changing the verdict: the symmetry in "one another" is a symmetry of **office**, not of **operation**. Fig 4 :19-20 says the kernel is unchanged with size and *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* Fig 13 :4-5 says the two kinds are in lockstep, *"a crossing is a change of role rather than a step upward, and no member of the pair is senior to the other."* At one turn the human is maker and the AI is the outside frame; at the next the roles cross. Neither is senior. What neither may do is treat the other as the anchor.

Operators table: `+` is symmetric, `→` is ordered. A correction protocol is a sequence, not a conjunction: describe residual, then book a differing frame, then import with a source gate, then settle against the ground. Writing it as a flat `+` is the flat-formula collapse Fig 12 :6-16 names.

**Falsifier:** a case where a protocol that lets both parties update on each other, with no separate anchor, outperforms the anchored protocol on held-out slots would reverse this re-census and make "one another" symmetric in operation as well as office.

## Q4: What order does the exchange have?

Fig 4 :22 *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* From the pair to the organisation goes a method that can be reapplied; from the organisation to the next pair goes a head start, never a recording. Fig 4 :30 requires the round trip: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

The round trip gap R_T here is the difference between "we say the pair corrected a blind spot" and "the pair's per-slot residual fell against an independent test". It is not zero by assumption. It is measured or it is unknown.

## CONCLUSION

A correcting collaboration has a dependency order (anchor, vocabulary, objective, collaborator, carry), not a checklist. Three arrangements with the same participants are isomers whose difference is carried entirely by the bond, and the phrase "one another" is symmetric only in office: roles may cross, the anchor may not. Both claims carry written falsifiers.
