## 1. A shape is a constraint you can state before you can name the case

The canonical rule: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

For HIS-0091 the shape is **the bond slot**, written `IDEA --(bond ?)--> CHANGE`. It constrains: the slot must be filled by route-discriminating evidence, and co-occurrence cannot fill it. It does not fill: it names no thinker, no century, and no verdict. A completed attribution, "Darwin caused the Victorian shift in X", is a name, not a shape.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already hold that is closest to the thing, then look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it and recurse. *"With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."*
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."*
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: *"You will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place, as there is nowhere else to search and nothing else that can describe the answer better."*

Applied here: begin with "idea and change occurred together", which is the effect vocabulary any historian already has. Add "the change was absent before the idea fired" as a narrowing term. Add "the route is in the dated record, not the text" as a second. The space collapses from "influence" to four checkable bond types.

## 3. What licenses a name, and what does not

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* The canonical README: the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."*
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :51-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*They're Scanning Books* sharpens the difference: *"A written sentence is a lossy compression of the reasoning that produced it."* And: *"Proof of provenance does not remove the priors. It makes them auditable, which is the most that honesty allows when the priors are unavoidable."* A dated archive entry is provenance for a route, not proof that the route was causal. That distinction is the whole of F5 in `01`.

## 4. The central move: the reading does not determine the source

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. An instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source"*. One green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered, all produce the same reading. Then: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* And: *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it."* It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved it.

The historian's analogue is exact. A text that caused a practice and a text that summarized it read the same in the archive. What is owed is a discriminator, dated arrival against dated practice, matched sites, traces, not a closer reading of the text. *How Intelligence Actually Works*: *"often, very little about a composed term tells you, from looking at it alone, which route produced it."* It also records the two honest cases: *"There are provable cases, where there is no trace left: it was lost in transformation (like the color example). The only thing that can provide their distinction is proof of provenance provided by other means than the projection itself."*

## 5. Outputs are not generators

*They're Scanning Books*: *"A corpus is the compressed output of human minds; the capacity that produced it is a generator, and outputs do not contain their generator."* An idea that reflected a change is an output of the process that produced the change. An idea that changed a practice became a generator in a new closure. The two are different kinds of thing, and no quantity of the first sums to the second. Citation volume measures outputs.

*How a Society of Agents Would Work* makes the operational version: *"Independent discoveries count most"*, because *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."* So every entry keeps its history. That is a direct method rule for a historian: keep the arrival record, not just the text.

## 6. The retrospective label

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."* A famous thinker's name buys the case a hearing, not a verdict.

The same essay: *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."* And *Arrival of the Fittest* warns that LLM assisted outputs are *"simulacra"* and *"could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula."*

## 7. My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, `06_isomers_and_order.md` and `09_watson_answer.md` are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are not tests of any historical claim and must be labelled as such. They cannot settle anything they report on.

## 8. Correction to my own §3 and §4

I first wrote "the archive can never decide the route". That is too strong. *How Intelligence Actually Works* gives a counter-case: *"In the gemstone case, provenance is recoverable: isotope ratios genuinely differ, so if you have a tool that can perceive the trace, you regain distinction. The tool only works because a trace existed, and not because a better tool was created."* And: *"Some processes leave traces and depth recovers them; some produce identical output and no depth helps. That is the whole provenance argument in one contrast, and it is why the lineage has to be recorded rather than reconstructed."*

So the correct claim is: **content alone does not decide; a trace in the record can.** Both readings are kept here so neither is quietly lost. The F1 refuter in `01` is the test that would break the stronger version.

## 9. Round trip and stop

Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* The shorthand "the idea changed society" loses the bond type, the common cause, the arrival date, and the change in contact. Carrying the shorthand down to a case without re-measuring is dead reckoning. The honest report is a recorded gap `R_T`, computed in `11_audit.md`.
