# Expert verification packet: cause or reflection in intellectual history

**What this is:** a claim in a form an historian can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the per-case tests, not `grounded`.** No historical case is asserted to be settled. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The bond between an idea and a social change is not readable from the artefact. A production claim and a reflection claim can end in the same text, so only the record of where each came from separates them. Production has a dated signature, the change or its necessary mechanism was absent before the idea fired, but that test is necessary rather than sufficient, because a second cause can be ready to produce the same effect. What moves a case from `gap` to `candidate` is a route-discriminating record plus a witness frame whose evidence does not come from the same texts. A verdict requires changed contact, not citation volume, influence or scholarly agreement, and retrospective labels are not verdicts. The output is a test sequence and an honesty rule, not a name attached to a thinker.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 8 and 13

- **Statement:** the artefact cannot decide the route; route lives in the lineage.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :51 *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents."* Fig 8 :52 *"Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."* Fig 13 :128 *"Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available."* Fig 13 :129 *"the order is not recoverable from the artefact. It is recoverable from the lineage."*
- **Refuter:** exhibit a route-invariant marker in the content itself that can only be written before the change and never after, and show that it classifies cases correctly against a ground. If that works, this sub-claim fails.

### C2 - historiography-facing; checkable against a case the reader knows

- **Statement:** production is parsed as `A produces B; B did not exist before`, tested by asking whether B was present before A fired. It is a necessary condition only. Sequence, by contrast, permits B to have been waiting.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :77 *"A produces B; B did not exist before"*; Fig 12 :79 *"is there a B before A fires? No."*; Fig 12 :35 *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*; Fig 9 :42 *"narrowing gets you close, the ground decides"*.
- **What to check:** take one case you know well. Was the change, or the mechanism that had to produce it, already present when the idea arrived? If yes, does an account that still calls the idea the producer survive a second look? Could a different process have produced the same change? The philosophy source in `10_source_ledger.csv` gives preemption cases where the answer to the last question is yes.
- **Refuter:** a case where the change demonstrably predates arrival in that closure and the correct account still calls the idea its producer, with no later redirection. Also accept the converse strain: a case that passes the before-test but where a backup cause was already sufficient. Report both.

### C3 - verification-facing; checkable against the reader's own standards of evidence

- **Statement:** agreement, citation and consensus are filters for the budget, never witnesses for the verdict. What counts is separate ranging against a ground by frames with low evidence overlap, and a change in contact.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :39 *"Correlation has valence."*; Fig 2 :40 *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; Fig 7 :35 *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*; Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*; Fig 11 :40 *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*
- **Refuter:** a case where scholarly consensus or citation volume later proves causally diagnostic without any ground reading; or a case where a change in practice is correctly attributed to an idea while no change in contact with any ground can be located.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- No verdict on any actual thinker, book, movement or century.
- Not that an idea which reflected a change was worthless, and not that influence is the same as causation.
- Not that a clean provenance record makes a claim true. Fig 10 :65: *"Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right: the ground does that, and a wrong ruling stays wrong however clean its record."*
- Not that absence of a record proves the idea merely reflected the change. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
- Not `grounded`. Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."*

## 5. What would settle a case

A source-discriminating record, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. For an historical case:

1. A dated record showing the change or its necessary mechanism absent before the idea reached that closure.
2. A matched pair of closures, one reached by the idea and one not, with the practice appearing only in the first.
3. A second frame with non-overlapping evidence that ranges against the same record separately and reaches the same bond.
4. The standing falsifiers from `01` §4, any one of which withdraws the corresponding claim.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 8 :51-52, Fig 12 :77-79, Fig 11 :40 and Fig 9 :60, four short passages.
2. Check C2 against one case you know: was the change present before the idea arrived, and does that settle anything on its own?
3. Name one dated record or trace that would discriminate production from common cause in that case, or say why none exists.
4. Say what you would count as a failure of the shape claim. Send that, not an endorsement. A failure is more useful than agreement.
