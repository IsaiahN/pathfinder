# Bond types, order and isomers for HIS-0091

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

`01` established that the bond between an idea I and a social change S is not readable from content. Three questions remain: (1) which bond types are licensed by the operator set, (2) does order distinguish them, and (3) are the two readings (production versus reflection) isomers over the same operands?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Operators held: `+` conjunction, `∥` disjunction, `≡` identity, `→` sequence, `⇒` production, `−` subtraction, `⋛` comparison, plus `¬` negation (Fig 12 :49). Operands held: the idea event and the social-change event, plus a background condition. No new operator is available without import.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Which bond is "changed society"?

The candidate answer is **production**, Fig 12 :77: *"A produces B; B did not exist before"*, with the test Fig 12 :79: *"is there a B before A fires? No."* The distinguishing feature is the **absence of the second operand before the first fires**, not mere priority in time.

The common rival readings:

- **Sequence**, Fig 12 :35: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*. B may already have been present or prepared. Priority alone does not separate sequence from production. The historian must establish absence, not just order.
- **Conjunction**, Fig 12 :57: *"both hold at once"*. Co-occurrence. Symmetric, so it cannot carry direction. This is the naive "the idea and the change happened together" claim.
- **Comparison**, Fig 12 :87: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*. Useful if the claim is that the idea crossed a threshold already present in society, which is a different claim from producing the change. Operators :52: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*.
- **Negation**, Operators :57: *"is the point that it is false, rather than absent? Yes."* A refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate. A historian who only adds influences and never refuses one cannot correct the record (Fig 12 :139-140: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."*).

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a case is found where the second operand was present before the first fired and the correct description is still production, the production test fails as a necessary condition and must be downgraded to a similarity marker. The philosophy source's preemption cases make this a live possibility, not a hypothetical. Recorded as a strain in `01` §6 and `11`.

### Q2 - Are the two readings isomers?

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

Take the same three atoms in both readings: the idea I, the change S, the background condition C. Then:

- Production reading: `I ⇒ S` with C as an enabling condition, and S absent before I fires.
- Reflection reading: `C ⇒ S` and `C ⇒ I`, with I an output of the same process, so I and S are related by a common cause rather than by production.

Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in a flat list of "factors present in 1859" and decisive in what a counterfactual would show. So the two readings **are isomers** under Fig 12's definition.

But only if both are written over the same atom set. If one account names institutions, laws and material conditions and the other names only texts and authors, they do not share atoms and are not isomers; they are two different inventories. Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* The safe weaker claim is that both are members of a functional group (an event bonded to an outcome) with different atoms in the slots.

**Falsifier:** if either reading requires an atom the other cannot represent, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group claim stands.

### Q3 - What order is the union?

Fig 13 :4-5: *"There is no order of rank here. The two kinds are in lockstep, a crossing is a change of role rather than a step upward, and no member of the pair is senior to the other."* The historian must not hard-code "idea first" or "society first" into the vocabulary. The order is an empirical finding of the case, not a feature of the operands.

Dependency order (Fig 3) is about the inquiry, not about the world: describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); establish absence before mechanism; establish route before attribution. Fig 12 :111-113: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one. A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* A historian holding only a list of "influences" can name a situation and cannot name a route through it.

### Q4 - Round trip gap `R_T`

Symbols table :77-78: *"The gap between what was sent up and what came back down: gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x)). Measures what the coarser description cannot hold."*

1. **Sent up (x):** the figure set, chiefly Fig 8 :51-52 (route in the record), Fig 12 :77-79 (production test), Fig 11 :40 (contact), Fig 9 :60 (filters not verdicts).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in outreach and in most reference works: "the idea changed society" versus "the idea reflected the times".
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a case-level claim with dated arrival, dated change, a named closure, and a witness frame.

**`R_T` is nonzero.** The shorthand drops: the bond type, the common-cause reading, the requirement that S be absent before I fired, the requirement of changed contact, and the instruction to report `gap`. What survives cleanly is only that the two readings are not the same claim. Treating the shorthand as identical to the figure set would set `R_T` to zero and repeat the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. `R_T` is recorded qualitatively here; no ground reading exists to measure it numerically.

## CONCLUSION

The bond "changed society" parses as production, whose signature is absence of the change before the idea fires, a necessary condition only. The rival "merely reflected" parses as a common cause, not as absence of influence. Over the same atom set the two are isomers under Fig 12, invisible in a flat inventory and decisive in a counterfactual. Order is not hard-coded, and the round trip from the figures to the everyday phrase is lossy in five named ways. The falsifiers are written before the run and are not hidden.
