# Solution HIS-0091

**Question:** How can historians determine whether an idea changed society or merely reflected changes already underway?

**Source:** history of science and knowledge.txt line 112

**Section:** 10. Historical explanation and contemporary relevance

**Mode:** `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the per-case tests, `grounded` on nothing in this folder. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading and not a ruling on any actual historical case.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

## What would answer it

Restated as an effect: for an idea I and a social change S in a domain D, what kind of bond holds between them? Option A, the idea produced the change (the change was absent before the idea fired). Option B, the idea expressed a change already under way (a common cause, a convergent derivation, or an adopted import). Reading the idea's content cannot by itself decide A or B, because the two routes can end in the same artefact. The answer is therefore a test sequence and an honesty rule, not a name attached to a favourite thinker.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run: `IDEA --(bond type ?)--> CHANGE`, with the bond slot filled only by a route-discriminating record and a low-overlap witness, or left open as `gap`.

## What is deliberately not here

- No verdict on any actual historical case. No ground reading was performed.
- No personal emails of named historians, no receptiveness ranking, no bulk-send plan.
- No claim that a widely cited idea was therefore causally effective, and no claim that an idea that merely reflected a change was therefore worthless.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **Content does not carry route.** *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"* (Fig 8 :51).
2. **Agreement is not an anchor.** *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35).
3. **Asking a reader to trust the framework before they check the output spends their independence.** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42).

Legally: unsolicited bulk email to scraped personal addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. The venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv` are public institutional submission or opt-in channels.
