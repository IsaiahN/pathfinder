## 0. The residual, in effect terms

For an idea I and a social change S observed together in a domain D, the historian must decide the **bond** between them. Two candidate bonds:

- **Production:** I produced S. S was absent before I fired. This is what "the idea changed society" asserts.
- **Reflection:** S, or its causes, were already present. I is an expression of S, a convergent derivation of it, or an adopted import from it. This is what "the idea merely reflected changes already underway" asserts.

A third possibility is a **common cause** behind both, which Figure 2 names as a collapse: *"collapse 2: common cause (hidden shared source)"* (Fig 2 :6-7). The residual is not "was the idea influential?" and not "was the idea cited?". Those are frame-internal quantities. Figure 1 :27 says: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*

Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term before the run, per the formula's "Direction": `IDEA --(bond ?)--> CHANGE`, where the slot is filled only by a route-discriminating record and a low-overlap witness, or is reported as `gap`.

### R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** at least three. An idea, a change, and a background condition that might produce both. A two-place reading ("idea and change occurred together") cannot express the common-cause case. This matches the contrastive shape in the metaphysics source: the claim is of the form cause rather than rival cause, effect rather than rival effect.
- **Symmetry:** production is ordered. Swapping the operands gives a different claim. Reflection by co-occurrence is symmetric and therefore carries no direction. Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."*
- **Scale:** from a text, a reading or a decision, up to an institution, a law, a practice. Fig 11 :52-53: *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* A "society-wide" effect may be several steps out from the idea's first reception.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor. No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | verdict from this domain alone |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1, textual / idea frame** | authors, texts, concepts, readers, citation chains | readings, conceptual content, publication dates | the dated corpus and archive | `single-source`. Can establish that I existed and what it said. Cannot establish route. |
| **D2, social-practice frame** | institutions, laws, behaviours, material conditions, adoption events | practices, outcomes, dated indicators | records of practice, material evidence, administrative data | `single-source`. Can establish timing of S. Cannot establish that I produced it. |
| **D3, contact / verification frame (union)** | the historian, natural experiments, differentially reached closures | provenance, lineage, discriminating tests | a ground that neither frame can move: arrival dates against change dates, matched sites where I arrived and did not | `triangulated` only when two low-overlap frames range against the ground separately; otherwise `gap`. |

**Partition or union:** union, not partition. Fig 9 :57-58: *"A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."* The two conditions must be unbundled before searching (Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*). Studying I harder and studying S harder are different searches, and neither reaches the bond.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3):** (1) describe the residual before choosing a frame, Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*; (2) establish sequence, was S present before I fired; (3) seek a route-discriminating record; (4) bring in a low-overlap witness frame; (5) only then fill the bond slot. Fig 9 :42: *"narrowing gets you close, the ground decides"*.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The artefact cannot decide the route (decisive for the shape)

Fig 8 :51-52:

> **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."**

Fig 13 :128:

> **"Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available."**

Fig 13 :129:

> **"The space has no last route and every turn begins from a remainder the last one left, so the order is not recoverable from the artefact. It is recoverable from the lineage."**

Fig 12 :14-16:

> **"the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not, nothing downstream can tell them apart, and nothing signals that anything was lost"**

An idea that caused a change and an idea that summarized it can say the same sentences to the same audience. Textual similarity, even exact quotation, is not a discriminator. The essay *How Intelligence Actually Works* puts it in one line: *"often, very little about a composed term tells you, from looking at it alone, which route produced it."*

**Refuter:** exhibit a route-invariant marker in the content itself, a feature that can only be written before the change and never after it, and show that it classifies cases correctly against a ground. Until such a marker is shown, content alone is not a witness. This is a `single-source` finding in D1, strengthened by the figure line in D3.

### F2 - Production has a signature, but it is necessary, not sufficient

Fig 12 :77:

> **"A produces B; B did not exist before"**

Fig 12 :79:

> **"is there a B before A fires? No."**

Contrast sequence, Fig 12 :35: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*. Sequence permits B to have been waiting; production does not. The historian's first discriminating question is therefore a dated one: was the social change, or the mechanism that necessarily produced it, already present when the idea arrived in that closure?

The epidemiology source gives the same test from a different field: *"Temporality: The effect has to occur after the cause"*. The social-science method source agrees: *"Process-tracing emphasizes the temporal sequence of events, and requires fine-grained case knowledge."*

**This is a hoop test, not a smoking gun.** The philosophy source warns: *"Cases of preemption show us that causes need not be necessary for their effects."* An idea can arrive after a change began and still redirect it; the before-test alone would then wrongly acquit it. Fig 9 :42 decides the remedy: *"narrowing gets you close, the ground decides"*. So the before-test removes a candidate when it fails, but passing it does not confer a verdict. Triangulated across Fig 12, Hill temporality and process tracing; the sufficiency claim is explicitly retracted (see §6).

### F3 - This is a union compound, so the two sides must be unbundled

Fig 9 :57-58, Fig 9 :54, Fig 9 :27-28 (quoted in §1). Fig 8 :53: *"Residual first, frame second."*

The intellectual history source frames the same two-context requirement from inside the field: *"the investigative premise of intellectual history is that ideas do not develop in isolation from the thinkers who conceptualize and apply those ideas; thus the intellectual historian studies ideas in two contexts: (i) as abstract propositions for critical application; and (ii) in concrete terms of culture, life, and history."* A single-context study answers one condition of the compound and leaves the bond untested.

**Refuter:** a case where the bond can be recovered by adding the two single-frame results, with no new witness frame and no lineage record. If that works generally, F3 fails.

### F4 - Correlation, consensus and prestige are not witnesses for the verdict

Fig 2 :39-40:

> **"Correlation has valence. disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**

Fig 2 :31-32:

> **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."**

Fig 7 :35:

> **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**

Fig 9 :60:

> **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

Seat Map :163: *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* Citation counts, the number of historians who agree, and the reputation of the thinker are **filters for the budget**, useful for deciding which case to open first. They are not evidence about the bond. The statistics source states the same exclusion: *"Causal analysis is the field of experimental design and statistics pertaining to establishing cause and effect. Typically it involves establishing four elements: correlation, sequence in time (that is, causes must occur before their proposed effect), a plausible physical or information-theoretical mechanism for an observed effect to follow from a possible cause, and eliminating the possibility of common and alternative ("special") causes."*

**Refuter:** a case where consensus or citation volume later proves causally diagnostic without any ground reading. Until then, a list of agreeable readers is a list selected to agree.

### F5 - A retrospective label is not a verdict, and "changed society" must mean changed contact

Fig 11 :36:

> **"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."**

Fig 11 :40:

> **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

Fig 10 :65-66:

> **"Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right: the ground does that, and a wrong ruling stays wrong however clean its record."**

Calling an idea "the cause" after the change is complete is exactly the retrospective move Fig 11 warns about. The historian's fallacy source names the error in the field's own terms: *"Only in retrospect do the warning signs seem obvious; signs that pointed in other directions tend to be forgotten."* The cognition source adds that *"Examples of hindsight bias can be seen in the writings of historians describing the outcomes of battles"*. What survives the warning is not a ban on causal claims. It is a demand that the label be paid for by a dated record of changed contact: a practice that appeared where the idea arrived and did not appear where it did not.

**Refuter:** a case where the idea demonstrably changed no contact with any ground and is still correctly said to have changed society, with the change located and dated.

### F6 - The honest stop is a verdict, not a silence

Fig 8 :39:

> **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

Fig 13 :134:

> **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"**

Fig 9 :53: *"Nobody chooses. The gap does, and the gap is only as good as its description."* Fig 13 :140-141: *"not only what was concluded, but what could have been concluded, and what would have had to be true for the answer to differ."*

From the source essay: *"being below the union horizon is never a settled verdict, only a report that nobody has reached it yet."* When the archive cannot discriminate production from reflection, the correct entry is `gap`, not "probably merely reflected" and not "probably influential". Absence of a discriminating record is evidence about the archive, not about the idea.

**Refuter:** a case where the absence of a route-discriminating record is itself sufficient to conclude that the idea only reflected the change. That would turn the archive into an anchor, which Fig 2 :29-30 forbids: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."*

### F7 - Idea and society are in lockstep, not senior and junior

Fig 13 :4-5:

> **"There is no order of rank here. The two kinds are in lockstep, a crossing is a change of role rather than a step upward, and no member of the pair is senior to the other."**

The question's own phrasing ("changed society" versus "reflected changes already underway") smuggles in a rank: either the idea is the driver or society is. The figure declines that rank. The bond can run in either direction and can change role with the level, so the historian must specify the level and the direction as part of the claim, not assume them. Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*

**Refuter:** a case where one side is demonstrably senior at every relevant scale and the relation is one-way. If that is shown, the lockstep reading fails at that scale.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach, per Seat Map :287: *"And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A route-invariant content marker is exhibited and validated against a ground, so that the bond is readable from the artefact alone (breaks F1 and Fig 8 :51-52).
2. A case is found where S demonstrably existed before I arrived in that closure and a still-correct account calls I the producer of S, with no redirection or new production after arrival (breaks the Fig 12 :77-79 before-test as a necessary condition).
3. Two genuinely low-overlap frames range separately against the same ground, agree on production, and the ground later shows no change in contact attributable to I (breaks F4 and the triangulation claim).
4. The retrospective label is the only evidence and the verdict still survives a blind re-check (breaks F5 and Fig 11 :36).
5. A case is decided without any route-discriminating record, and the decision is later confirmed by a newly found dated record (breaks F6; the earlier verdict was luck, not method).

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the audit is re-censused. Until one is run, nothing here is `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the bond question, not with a thinker. Write `IDEA --(bond ?)--> CHANGE` before opening any archive (Fig 9 :27-28).
2. Run the before-test first, as a necessary condition, not as a verdict (Fig 12 :77-79; §6 R1).
3. Then look for a route-discriminating record: dated arrival against dated change, matched sites where the idea arrived and did not, or a source that left traces. An instrument that cannot discriminate sources demonstrates its own inadequacy, per *The Hard Problem is About Source*: *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it."*
4. Bring in one low-overlap witness frame whose evidence does not draw on the same texts (Fig 8 :29: *"low overlap: B does not draw on A’s evidence"*).
5. Use consensus and citation as filters for which case to open, never as witnesses (Fig 9 :60).
6. Record `gap` where the archive cannot discriminate. Report what would have had to be true for the verdict to differ (Fig 13 :140-141).
7. Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Send the test sequence and the refuter, not a finished attribution.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives: the bond is not readable from content; a dated before-test removes candidates but does not confer a verdict; route-discriminating lineage and a low-overlap witness are what move a case from `gap` to `candidate`; consensus and correlation never do. The per-case verdicts remain separate and are never averaged. This is `general` on the shape and `specified` on the tests, with no case `grounded`.
