# Union, order and the two currencies: supporting derivation

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The question joins two things a firm's books omit: environmental harms and social harms. Two orders of question are owed. (1) Is the pair a partition (two separate questions) or a union (overlapping conditions needing a compound loop)? (2) Given that any monetary summary is a lossy condensation of the slot vector, what is the round-trip gap `R_T` and what would have to be held apart for the condensation to be honest?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held in the firm's Γ: prices, costs, revenues, provisions, budgets. Atoms not held and imported when present: physical measures (emissions, exposure, cases, hectares), social outcomes (injury, health, displacement), and the arrangements that carry them (supply chains, habitats, institutions). Bonds available: the seven typed bonds plus negation. No new primitive of the six-term substrate is available without import, and none is needed.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Partition or union?

- Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either". Fig 9 :54: when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again.
- A pollutant that damages a river and the health of the people who drink from it satisfies two conditions at once. A lever that cleans the river may leave the health condition standing, and vice versa. By Fig 9 :55-56, a single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.
- ⇒ The pair is a **union**, not a partition and not one scalar. Each condition gets its own loop; the overlap is a compound with its own ground. Verdict: `triangulated` across Fig 9 and Fig 13 :74 (cross-kind composition).
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** a case where a lever that clears each condition alone also clears the joint condition with no residue in the intersection. Then the compound reading is unnecessary for that case.

### Q2 — What is the order of the union?

- Fig 3 :19-23: the chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. A reading taken below the break is a reading of nothing.
- Dependency order here: **perceive** the harm (instrument exists?) → **vocabulary** (terms for what the harm affords?) → **objective** (what counts as progress on that slot?) → **plan** (abatement, assurance) → **learn and carry**.
- Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." A firm with a mature disclosure process but no measurement instrument for a specific harm is strong above the break and empty below it. Effort spent on reporting cannot rescue the missing vocabulary link.
- ⇒ The order is perception first, valuation last. A monetary number is a step-4 or step-5 artifact, not a step-1 input. Verdict: `triangulated` across Fig 3 and Fig 6 :32.

### Q3 — Are environmental and social harms isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- As a firm usually writes them, an environmental harm and a social harm share few atoms: tonnes emitted versus people affected. By Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**; they are two members of a functional group ("unpriced harm") with different atoms in the slots.
- The stronger form: if both are written over a shared atom set (the same physical and social measurements, differing only in which structure mediates which state), then they **are** isomers, same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. That is the checkable structural claim.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either harm can be written without an atom the other requires, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. This mirrors the exemplar's Q3 in `06_isomers_and_order.md` but applies it to harms rather than physical theories.

### Q4 — What may be compared, and what may not be added?

- Fig 12 :87: "a threshold relation between two quantities" defines ⋛, the comparison bond. Operators :52: "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one." Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."
- A firm can therefore **compare** two harms at a level: which is larger on a given measure, which crossed a threshold. Comparison is licensed and is how a goal is stated.
- Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds." Fig 12 :130: "Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it. A price in bits and a price in actions are two readings, held apart."
- ⇒ Comparison is allowed; summation is not. The strain first read as "⋛ permits a single aggregate" was re-censed: the bond compares two quantities at a level, while the two-currency rule governs what may be done with description and settlement. Different objects. The strain is dissolved, not erased (see `01` F9 and §7).
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** a derivation showing that the comparison bond authorises adding descriptions to settlement actions in one currency. If shown, the re-census fails.

### Q5 — The round-trip gap `R_T`

- Symbols table `R_T`: "The gap between what was sent up and what came back down: gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x)). Measures what the coarser description cannot hold."
- Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."
- **Sent up (x):** the per-slot vector of physical and social measurements, with provenance.
- **Abstraction (T_A):** a single monetary summary, a rating, or an SROI ratio. Detail is thrown away, which is what makes the result reusable as a budget filter.
- **Concretisation (T_E):** using that number to decide an actual abatement action, investment, or disclosure.
- **`R_T` is nonzero and must be reported.** What the shorthand cannot hold: (1) which slot got worse while the total improved; (2) the source of each imported measure; (3) the two-currency distinction between describing a harm and paying to settle whether the valuation holds; (4) the second-order cascade that a first-order enumeration missed. `R_T` was not measured numerically here, because no ground reading was taken; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the number is the same thing in fewer digits" would set `R_T` to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.

### Q6 — Analogues from low-overlap fields

- **Metamerism (*The Hard Problem is About Source*):** an instrument reads green and cannot comment on the source. A single total reads harm and cannot comment on which harm. The remedy is a discriminator, not a better argument.
- **DDT cascade (Fig 11 :52-54):** a first-order enumeration missed a second-order effect three steps out. A firm that counts only direct operational emissions misses the supply-chain and use-phase slots.
- **Goodhart / Campbell (cited in the architecture article):** when a measure becomes a target, it stops being a good measure. Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." A shadow price adopted as a target ceases to be an estimate of harm.
- **Zero-knowledge verification (Fig 10 :84):** the verifier's non-access to the witness is what makes verification sound. An assurance provider who can reconstruct the valuation from the firm's own model cannot verify it.

## CONCLUSION

Environmental and social harms are a union, not a partition; the order is perception before valuation; the two are a functional group and become a real isomer claim only over a shared atom set; comparison at a level is licensed but summation across the two currencies is the named error; and any single-number condensation carries a nonzero round-trip gap that must be reported rather than assumed away. The falsifiers above are written before the run and are repeated in `01`, `07`, `08` and `09`.
