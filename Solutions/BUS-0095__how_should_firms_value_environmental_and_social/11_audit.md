## 4. Round trip gap `R_T`

The Symbols table defines `R_T` as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the per-slot vector of physical and social measurements with provenance, e.g. emissions by source, exposure by cohort, outcome by place.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** a single monetary summary, a rating or an SROI ratio.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** using that number to choose an abatement action, an investment or a disclosure.

**`R_T` is nonzero.** Five things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- which specific slot worsened while the total improved (Fig 1 :22);
- the provenance of each imported physical measure (Fig 8 :51);
- the distinction between describing a harm and paying to settle whether the valuation holds (Fig 12 :125-130);
- the second-order cascade that a first-order enumeration missed (Fig 11 :52-54);
- the stake declaration and the seat arrangement that produced the number (Fig 10 :59-60).

One thing survives cleanly: the comparison of two quantities at a level, which is what ⋛ licenses (Fig 12 :87). So a total may rank options as a filter, but it may not carry the lineage that makes the ranking a witness. `R_T` was not measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the number is the vector in fewer digits" as identity would set `R_T` to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
