# Outreach draft — check the shape first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` survive the recipient's own check. The two-stage offer collapses on contact if stage 1 does not hold.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about how ownership rules change access to knowledge

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about knowledge ownership. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at. I will record your verdict on this before showing you anything else.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "how have laws, patents and ownership rules shaped access to knowledge and control over its use" is two questions, not one. Access and control have different anchors and must be measured separately; averaging them into one "impact of IP" number destroys the signal. Against that, the invariant structural claim is that a legal rule adds an exclusion to the record but adds no primitive to what a community can compose. So a rule can gate reach without creating knowledge, and the effect it has can go either way: a disclosure mandate can lower the cost of composing, while a thicket of overlapping licences can raise it. The framework does not return a signed verdict on property. It returns the shape any answer must have and the instruments that would close it.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that all enclosure is harmful, that patents never help, that the historical magnitude is known, or that any named statute is good or bad. I am not claiming the public domain is a final room. I am not claiming that a convention is a measurement of access. And I am not claiming this derivation settles the history. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency, not a ground reading.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. show a legal rule that adds a new primitive to what a community can compose, rather than only gating reach;
> 2. show that access and control are one quantity with one anchor, so averaging them loses no signal;
> 3. show an ownership convention that decides an access or use outcome while remaining checkable against a ground it cannot move;
> 4. show a case where excluding composition increases the reachable set with no compensating import and no change of habitat.
>
> I wrote these down before running the derivation. If any one holds, I will withdraw or narrow the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text and line numbers (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Eight essays and the formula are public at the links in `04_links_and_distribution.md`; I will send them on request.
>
> **If you want to use the method:** the formula is in the figures repository. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [name]
