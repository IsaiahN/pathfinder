# Are open access, the commons, the public domain and copyleft isomers? And what order is a rights chain?

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

Two supporting questions are owed by the main audit:

1. Under Fig 12's definition, are "open access", "governed commons", "public domain" and "copyleft" distinct substances, or different spellings of one?
2. A rights chain runs legislature → grant → licence → use → derived work. What is the dependency order, and where does the chain usually break?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held in the legal frame: a work, a claim, a term, a permission, an attribution, a fee, a copy. Bonds held from the framework: the seven compositional bonds plus negation. The legal frame also has its own vocabulary, which is not the framework's bond set (strain recorded in `01` F1). No new primitive is available without an import.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — The spelling test

Fig 12 :23: *"this bond is symmetric, so the four are one term and nothing consults the bond to find that out."* Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."** Fig 12 :95 to :96: *"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."*

All four regimes can be written with the same ingredients: a work, a permission to use, an attribution requirement and a term. Under the spelling test they are **not** one term, because the bonds differ:

| regime | bond between work and user | bond between copy and licence | consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| public domain | none, or disjunction of all uses | no obligation carried forward | any composition is reachable with zero permission cost |
| open access (read only) | permission to read, not to reuse | no obligation carried forward | access rises while control is retained; the two slots diverge |
| governed commons | permission conditional on membership and rules | permission carried by the community's convention | cost is in actions (participate, negotiate), not only in bits |
| copyleft | permission to use and modify | the licence condition is carried into derivatives (a production bond, `⇒`) | reuse is reachable only if the derivative re-enters the same condition |

Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** These four pass the isomer test at the level of the legal vocabulary: same ingredients, different arrangement, different behaviour. They are **isomers in the legal frame's own sense**, and the framework's bond vocabulary can name the difference (none, disjunction, condition, production).

**Strain, named.** A licence condition is not literally one of the seven compositional bonds, so calling copyleft a `⇒` production bond is an analogy. The benzoin/fulminate case that forced structure into chemical notation (Fig 12 :97 to :98) is about compositional bonds; here the relation is a legal convention. The analogy is licensed only because both are *arrangements that the ingredient list does not record*. It is not an identity claim. Fig 12 :90: *"A relation that fails all six is not a bond you are missing. It is a claim that has not been made yet."*

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two of the four regimes produce identical behaviour on all four measured outputs (access, reuse, derivative reach, cost of checking) despite differing ingredient arrangements, the isomer claim fails and they are one term with four spellings.

### Q2 — The chain and its break

Fig 3 :4 to :16 names five links: perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry. Mapped to a rights chain:

1. **perception** (can the system tell what is in front of it): can a user tell which works are protected, which are licensed, and which are in the public domain?
2. **vocabulary** (terms for what things afford): does the user have a term for "reuse", "derivative", "fair use", "research exemption"?
3. **objective** (what would count as progress here): can the user say what a successful composition would be, for example a working derivative that does not infringe?
4. **planning** (a route under incomplete information): can the user assemble the licences, materials and prior art?
5. **learn and carry** (name what worked and carry it): is the route recorded so the next user's search is cheaper?

Fig 3 :25 to :27: *"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments. A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

The predicted break is at link 3, the objective. Patent and copyright systems are rich in perception (registers, classifications) and in planning (licensing procedures). They are comparatively poor at stating, in the user's own terms, what would count as a successful composition that does not infringe. Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* A faster examination process (link 1) cannot rescue a missing objective (link 3).

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a regime produces a public, checkable instrument that lets a user state "this derivative would count as successful and non-infringing" before assembling the licences, the predicted break moves from link 3 and this derivation is corrected.

### Q3 — Is the union a partition or a compound?

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* A single "open versus closed" axis is a blend (Fig 8 :4 to :5, *"collapse 4: the blend"*). The four regimes above cross two independent conditions: permission to read and permission to carry forward. Open access sits where only the first holds. Copyleft sits where both hold but the second binds the derivative. This is a **specific compound**, not a harder point on a line (Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*).

**Falsifier:** show that the two conditions always co-vary across all observed regimes, so the four cells are not all populated. If they co-vary, the compound collapses to a single axis and this Q3 is withdrawn.

## CONCLUSION

The four regimes are isomers in the legal frame: the ingredient list is the same and the bonds are different, so the difference is carried entirely by the arrangement. The rights chain is ordered by dependency and is predicted to break at the objective link (link 3), the one with the fewest instruments. The open/closed split is a union of two conditions, not a single axis. All three claims are `specified`, unmeasured, and each carries its falsifier above.
