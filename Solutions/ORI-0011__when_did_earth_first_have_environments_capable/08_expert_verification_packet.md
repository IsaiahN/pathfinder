## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "when did Earth first have environments capable of supporting prebiotic chemistry?" is a question about the earliest local setting, not a planetary average. A residual is indexed per object slot, so if one pond or vent was capable while the planet mostly was not, the correct "first" is that slot. "Where and when" cannot be answered by pairing a place with a date, because space and time are both states and a structure must sit between them; the environment, with water, a spendable energy gradient, feedstock and duration, is that structure. The scarcity of rock older than 4.0 Ga cannot be converted into a scarcity of environments, and the number, when it comes, is a mineral anchor reading rather than an agreement among researchers. The framework returns this shape and nothing more: it does not return a date and does not name a winning setting.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not another argument.

1. Do the Hadean zircon isotope signatures discriminate among a stable surface environment, a transient hydrothermal setting and an abiotic carbon source?
2. Do Hadean zircon localities beyond Jack Hills carry the same signatures, and what does that say about local versus planetary extent?
3. Is there direct mineral evidence of prebiotic products in grains older than 4.0 Ga?

A positive result on any of these would move the lower bound. A result showing the pre-4.0 Ga record complete in the relevant sense, or a positive measurement setting a later bound, would fire C3's refuter.
