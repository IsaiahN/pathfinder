## How these sources bear on the claim

Two points survive the figure audit, and the fetched literature supports them as secondary corroboration:

1. **A lower bound exists but is not a first date.** Zircon oxygen isotopes put surface water around 4.3 to 4.4 Ga, yet the rock record older than 4.0 Ga is fragmentary. That is a lower bound plus a limit, exactly the shape F3 predicts.
2. **The record's silence is not the world's silence.** Korenaga (2021) states plainly that the lack of a geological record does not mean there was no exposed land, and that exposed land may have occupied a substantial fraction of the surface in the mid-Hadean. Bell et al. (2015) show that the only access to pre-4.0 Ga history is through mineral grains in younger sediments. Both are world instances of Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

The carbon-isotope result in Bell et al. is a possible biosphere signal, not a measurement of when prebiotic chemistry first became possible. It is used only to show that the instrument can already return something at 4.1 Ga, not to name a date. The paper's own caution is recorded above.
