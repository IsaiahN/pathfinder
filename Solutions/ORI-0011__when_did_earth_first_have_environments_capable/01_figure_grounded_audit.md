## 0. The question under audit

> "When did Earth first have environments capable of supporting prebiotic chemistry?"
>
> Source: origins of life.txt line 18. Section: 2. Early Earth: where and when?

The phrase "where and when" is itself the first item to audit. In the six-term substrate, space and time are both states, and Fig 13 :75 says "Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state." So a "where and when" question cannot be answered by naming a place and a date. A structure (an environment with conditions) must sit between them. The question already asks for that structure when it asks for "environments capable of supporting prebiotic chemistry." The audit therefore treats the environment as the mediator, not as an optional detail attached to a date.

## 1. The residual in effect terms

**R, stated as an effect, not a summary.** We want the earliest time t at which at least one local setting simultaneously carried: (i) liquid water, (ii) a spendable energy gradient, (iii) feedstock molecules or the conditions that make them, and (iv) duration long enough for chemistry to accumulate. An answer is a time bound attached to a slot, with an instrument that would move it.

- **Arity.** Four conjunctive conditions plus an ordering ("first"). The ordering is not a fifth condition of the same kind; it is a comparison across times, so the answer has the form of a threshold (Fig 12's ⋛ comparison, "a threshold relation between two quantities" in the Operators table), not a list.
- **Symmetry.** The question is asymmetric. "First" distinguishes earlier from later, and one capable slot is sufficient while a global average is not (Fig 1 :19). Swapping a capable slot with an inert planet does not leave the same answer.
- **Scale.** Two scales are in play: the local environment (the slot) and the planetary state. Fig 4 :19-20: "The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." The same question at planetary scale is a different question, and averaging the two is how the signal is lost.

**What an answer would look like (binding the sought-for shape as an ordinary term, per THE_FORMULA "Direction"):** "The earliest slot for which a mineral or isotopic reading returns positive mass on all four conditions, with the oldest such reading giving a lower bound and the sparsity of older material giving the honest limit." This is written down before the run so it can accrue a residual and be refused by the same test as anything else.

## 4. Findings, with verbatim figure lines and refuters

### F1: The question is a slot question, not a global one (decisive for the object)

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."

Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

The source text asks about "environments" (plural) and section 17 of the same source asks which settings "lasted long enough for complicated chemistry to build up." Both are slot questions. If Earth as a whole was mostly inhospitable while one pond or vent was capable, the correct answer to "first environments" is the pond or vent. A planetary average would report a later date or no date at all and would be wrong for the question asked.

Fig 11 :39: "And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."

So "capable" is not a property of the chemistry alone or of the planet alone. It is a property of a setting and what happens in it.

**Refuter (falsifiable):** show that the intended object of this question is a single global habitability state, so that aggregating across slots is legitimate, or show a measurement in which one capable local setting existed while the global state was incapable and that the local setting is excluded by the question's own terms. Either result breaks this finding.

### F2: "Where and when" needs a structure between the two states (decisive for the shape)

Fig 13 :74: "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."

Fig 13 :75: "Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."

Space and time are states. A direct space-time bond is not composable. The environment is the structure that must sit between them: Fig 13 :76 "A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is." A vent gradient over rock pores and a wet-dry gradient over a pond surface are different terms because the state bonded to the gradient differs.

This is not a strain against the section heading "where and when". It is the reason the heading needs the word "environments". The answer cannot be a pair (place, date); it must be a structure that composes them.

Fig 11 :46: "conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"

Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."

Fig 11 :48: "You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."

So the settings are read off the world, not generated by recombining a vocabulary. The list of candidate environments in the source (vents, ponds, volcanic land, ice, crust interior) is an enumeration from observation and experiment, not an isomer set manufactured from the six terms.

**Refuter:** show a legitimate composition of space and time with no intervening structure under the six-term set, or show that the conditions for prebiotic chemistry are properties of a single state and do not require a structure. Either breaks F2.

### F3: Absence of old rock is not absence of old environments (central limit on "first")

Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

Fig 6 :32: "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."

Fig 6 :34-35: "Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."

The Hadean rock record is extremely sparse. Korenaga (2021) reports that there is no accepted exposure horizon before 3.0 Ga and that "Virtually no crust is of the Hadean age (before 4.0 Ga)"; the Bell et al. (2015) paper states that "Because Earth's known rock record extends only to ∼4 Ga, earlier periods of history are accessible only through mineral grains deposited in later sediments." Under Fig 8 :39, that absence cannot be converted into "no capable environments before 4.0 Ga." Under Fig 6 :34-35, the search is possible only because zircons are already returning ages and isotope ratios; the proto-instrument exists and can be sharpened. The correct output is a lower bound plus an honest statement of the limit, not a first date.

Fig 9 :44: "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."

**Refuter:** demonstrate that the pre-4.0 Ga record is complete in the relevant sense, so that absence of mineral evidence is evidence of absence of the conditions, or produce a positive measurement showing no capable slot existed before a later bound. Either breaks F3.

### F4: The number is settled by an anchor, not by agreement (governs the verdict's mode)

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."

Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source."

A date is an anchor reading: a U-Pb age, a δ18O value, a δ13C value. No number of readers agreeing on a narrative about the Hadean moves it. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The cost of a wrong date is high for every downstream origin-of-life question, so independent instruments, not pooled opinions, are owed.

**Refuter:** show that the date can be settled by consensus among frames without a mineral or experimental reading, or show a case where an anchor moved because of what a frame thought. Either breaks F4.

### F5: Describe the residual before picking a setting (guards against apophenia)

Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"

The settings in the source are candidate frames. If we start with "hydrothermal vents" or "warm little ponds" and then read the residual off them, any setting will seem to fit. The derived residual is instead: earliest local slot with water, spendable gradient, feedstock, and duration, anchored to a measurement. Only after that is fixed do the candidate settings get compared. The framework returns a kind of thing (a capable slot with a structure in the middle), not the date and not the name of the winning setting.

**Refuter:** show a derivation that selects a specific setting from the six-term set alone, with no world import and no source gate, contradicting the shape-not-a-name limit. That breaks F5.

### F6: The "first" operator is a comparison across slots, not a component

Operators table: ⋛ is "a threshold relation between two quantities", with test "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes." Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

"First" orders candidate times by the date at which a slot becomes capable. It is not an atom that can be added to the environment. Confusing the two would produce a seventh condition ("earliness") rather than a threshold over existing conditions. The supporting derivation in `06_isomers_and_order.md` applies this to the settings.

**Refuter:** show "earliness" functioning as a composable condition inside the environment rather than as a comparison between environments. That breaks F6.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the standing rule, "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument." The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. The object of the question is shown to be a single global habitability state rather than an earliest local slot, so aggregation across slots is legitimate (breaks F1).
2. A composition of space and time with no intervening structure is admitted under the six-term set, or the conditions are shown to be properties of one state (breaks F2).
3. The pre-4.0 Ga rock record is shown complete in the relevant sense, or a positive measurement shows no capable slot existed before a later bound (breaks F3).
4. A date is settled by agreement without any instrument reading, or an anchor is shown to move when a frame pushes on it (breaks F4).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn and the answer reverts to an open gap. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Lead with F1 and F3. They are the two moves that change the answer: the object is a slot, and an old-rock absence is not an old-environment absence.
2. State the mode on every claim. The world-sourced lower bound is corroboration, not a figure verdict (F4).
3. Describe the residual before enumerating settings (F5). Do not let a preferred setting hand you the answer.
4. Use `03_public_contact_points.csv` for opt-in, institutional venues and ask for a refutation, never belief. Send the packet in `08`, which carries the refuter before the conclusion.
