# The Tether's answer: how restoration goals should change

**Mode: `specified` , not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a site prescription. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A restoration goal compares a target with an observed site. The historical target was assembled under a past climate. When climate no longer resembles that past, the plan's predictions fail per slot: planted assemblages die back, recruitment shifts, interactions rearrange, conditions leave the range the target assumed. The residual is the vector of per-slot misses, not one score (Fig 1 :19, :22). The decision is whether the historical reference still settles success, or whether it is a held term that climate has refuted.

## 2. The shape, per domain, kept separate

1. **The historical reference is not an anchor.** Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :30: "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." Push climate and the reference moves. It is a term in `Γ`, not the ground. When a convention decides an outcome it "has stopped being one" (Fig 10 :47). Verdict: REFUTED as settlement criterion, `general`, triangulated.

2. **The owed term is a refusal, not a new community name.** Fig 5 :53: "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so." The refusal is `¬(fidelity settles success)`. Nothing is deleted; the reach falls because something was added. What replaces it is the *form* of a goal, not a named target. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Verdict: `general`, triangulated.

3. **The goal's operands must change when the question changes.** Fig 10 :71-72: "When the question changes, the room should change. A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom." Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." A species that served the old goal need not serve the new one. Verdict: `general`, triangulated.

4. **Re-enumerate the habitat from contact.** Fig 11 :44: "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." Conditions and relations are read off the world (Fig 11 :46-48), including cascades two or three steps out. Verdict: `general`, single-source for the enumeration rule.

5. **The work is permanent and asymmetric.** Fig 11 :61-62: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." Fig 11 :63: "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state." Moving material to track climate substitutes a habitat: "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose" (Fig 11 :67). Verdict: `specified`, triangulated with Fig 10 :71-72.

6. **Success becomes a per-slot, falsifiable comparison with a measured round trip.** Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 4 :30 requires the gap between the management shorthand and the site to be measured, not assumed. Verdict: `specified`, triangulated.

7. **The old and new goal forms are isomers, at minimum one functional group.** Same atoms, different bonds, different behaviour (`06_isomers_and_order.md`). The difference is the arrangement, not a different species list.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a named replacement community. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Which species to plant needs local measurement and a provenanced import.
- Not that historical information is worthless. It is evidence about processes and legacies; it is not an anchor that decides an outcome.
- Not that any one of resist, accept or direct is correct. The RAD framework (USGS CASC, 2026) offers all three; the figures constrain their form, not the choice among them without a ground.
- Not that historic fidelity is always wrong. It can be right when the climate and the constraints have not moved enough to change the residual. The claim is conditional on the question's premise.
- Not that we can remove the habitat. We can remove organisms; we cannot subtract the conditions.
- Not `grounded`. No field result settled the target choice.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A long-term, multi-site comparison under observed climate change, with per-slot survival, recruitment, interaction persistence and function measured against pre-registered criteria. An instrument that discriminates a persisting assemblage from a planted list held alive by maintenance would name what a single fidelity index cannot (*The Hard Problem is About Source*: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"). The pre-registered refuters in `01` §3 are the standing tests. A result in which historical targets match or beat trajectory targets, with no interaction with climate novelty, sends the derivation back to the census.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The shape stops where the instruments stop distinguishing. Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence." We can say the goal must be rebound, per-slot and falsifiable; we cannot say which band to steer toward. Declaring that band is a management choice with a declared stake, not a figure-derived name.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The figures do not tell you which community to plant. They tell you what form the goal has to take. A historical reference is not an anchor, because an anchor must not update and this one moves when climate moves, so the thing actually owed is a refusal of "fidelity settles success," not a new name for a target. The habitat has to be re-enumerated from current contact, because the same actors constitute a different habitat when the question changes; actor valence is relative to the residual. The manager can add actors and cannot subtract the habitat, so this is permanent work, not a return followed by an endpoint. Success has to be a per-slot, falsifiable comparison remeasured as conditions move, with the loss between the management shorthand and the site measured rather than assumed. The old and the new goal forms are isomers over the same atoms, different bonds, different behaviour. What would settle the remaining choice is a field result, not agreement: a long-term comparison under the actual climate with pre-registered, per-slot criteria. Until then the answer is a shape and a refuter, not a prescription.
