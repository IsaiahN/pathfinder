# Expert verification packet : restoration goals under climate change

**What this is:** a claim in a form a restoration ecologist or climate-adaptation manager can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` , not `grounded`.** No field result is claimed to have settled which target a site should be steered toward. This is a shape and a route, not a site prescription.

**Field:** climate-adaptive restoration research.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A historical reference state is not the thing that settles whether a restoration succeeded once climate moves. An anchor must not update, and the reference does update when climate is pushed, so what is owed is a refusal of "fidelity settles success," not a new community name. The habitat must be re-enumerated from present contact, because the same actors constitute a different habitat when the question changes. Success becomes a per-slot, falsifiable comparison over a trajectory, with the target operand rebound as conditions move, maintained by a seat that can add actors and cannot subtract the habitat. The old and new goal forms are isomers over the same atoms with different bonds, so the difference is in the arrangement, not in a different species list. Which band to steer toward is left open for the ground to settle.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 : Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 2, 5 and 10

- **Statement:** the historical reference fails the anchor test, so the owed term is a refusal, not a new target.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :30 "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one". Fig 5 :53 "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so".
- **Refuter:** a target that keeps settling success under a climate push while remaining a convention that does not decide the outcome, or a demonstration that the residual is a "missing" kind (a missing propagule) rather than a "wrong" kind. If either holds, C1 fails.

### C2 : World-facing; checkable against restoration theory and practice

- **Statement:** reference conditions under a moved climate are unmeasurable, unfalsifiable or unattainable as targets; contemporary, falsifiable benchmarks and explicit goal choices are the available alternative.
- **What to check:** McNellie et al. 2020 (*Global Change Biology* 26(12), 6702 to 6714, open access) write that historical conditions are "challenging to quantify, may be difficult to falsify, and may no longer be an attainable target in a contemporary ecosystem," and that contemporary references states are "measurable and falsifiable." The USGS CASC RAD framework defines **Resist**, **Accept** and **Direct** as three distinct goal options and says managers "may need to switch RAD strategies to balance current and future value as systems, feasibility of strategies, and desirability of objectives change." The BES Lehmann lovegrass case shows a practitioner accepting a novel state where "eradication is impractical" and "restoration of native grasses is similarly impractical."
- **Refuter:** a long-term multi-biome result in which historical-composition targets equal or exceed function and trajectory targets on persistence, with no interaction with climate novelty. Also: a fixed species list whose valence is invariant across two genuinely different residuals and still pays against the ground. Either sends the derivation back to the census.

### C3 : Framework-internal plus measurement; checkable by reading Figures 1, 4, 11 and 12

- **Statement:** goals must be re-enumerated from contact, stated per slot as falsifiable comparisons, and the work is permanent because introduction is available and habitat subtraction is not.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :44 "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." Fig 11 :61-62 "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." Fig 11 :63 "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state." Fig 12 :112 "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."
- **Refuter:** one global fidelity score that predicts per-slot persistence as well as per-slot measurement across two climates, or an intervention that subtracts a habitat constraint and leaves the residual closed across seasons. Either breaks C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a named replacement community, assemblage or planting list.
- Not that historical information is worthless. It is evidence about processes, not a settlement criterion.
- Not that resist, accept or direct is the right choice in any particular place.
- Not that historical fidelity is always wrong. The claim is conditional on the question's premise that climate no longer resembles the historical conditions.
- Not that habitats can be subtracted. Organisms can be removed; conditions and contact cannot.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here has been settled by a field measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

The ground, not agreement. Specifically:

1. A source-discriminating measurement that separates a persisting assemblage from a planted list maintained by intervention, per slot, with pre-registered criteria.
2. A long-term multi-site comparison under observed climate change, testing the four refuter items in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §3.
3. A demonstrated habitat subtraction that closes the residual across seasons.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29-30, Fig 5 :53 and Fig 10 :47. Three short passages. Decide whether the anchor test applies to a reference condition in your system.
2. Check C2 against your own literature. Does your field already have measurable, falsifiable contemporary benchmarks, and does it treat resist, accept and direct as distinct goal choices?
3. Read Fig 11 :44 and :61-63, Fig 12 :112 and Fig 1 :22. Ask whether your current success metric is one global score or a per-slot set.
4. Name one instrument that would distinguish persistence from maintained appearance in your system, or say why none is available.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
