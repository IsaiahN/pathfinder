## Verified sources, grouped by what they bear on

### Reference states and historical baselines

| what | URL | verified content |
|---|---|---|
| McNellie et al. 2020, *Reference state and benchmark concepts for better biodiversity conservation in contemporary ecosystems* | https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7756865 | *Global Change Biology* 26(12), 6702 to 6714. Open access. States that historical conditions are "challenging to quantify, may be difficult to falsify, and may no longer be an attainable target in a contemporary ecosystem"; that historical reference states are "often unmeasurable", "almost always unfalsifiable" and may be unattainable; and that contemporary references states are "measurable and falsifiable." This is the strongest world-side corroboration of F1 and F6. |
| Novel ecosystem synthesis | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novel_ecosystem | Defines novel ecosystems as assemblages pushed "outside of historical configurations, creating systems with no precedent in the ecological record." Notes that "accelerating climate change often renders those baselines unattainable" and that "many researches agree that the emphasis should be placed on the trajectories of ongoing change, rather than on identifying the specific past moment of change." Secondary review, shares the Hobbs evidence pool with the primary papers. |

### Climate novelty and the scale of the change

| what | URL | verified content |
|---|---|---|
| Ordonez et al. 2024, *Towards a novel biosphere in 2300* | https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10999272 | *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B* 379(1902), 20230022. Open access. "unless rapid mitigation measures are taken, the coverage of novel ecosystems could be over 50% of the land surface by 2100 under all change scenarios. | Assisted migration synthesis | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assisted_migration | Defines assisted migration as "the intentional establishment of populations or meta-populations beyond the boundary of a species' historic range for the purpose of tracking suitable habitats through a period of changing climate." Documents the invasive-species risk, the unreliability of niche models under multi-directional climate shifts, and the 2023 US Fish and Wildlife Service change removing "historical range" from the experimental-population rule. Secondary review. |

### Practice and management options

| what | URL | verified content |
|---|---|---|
