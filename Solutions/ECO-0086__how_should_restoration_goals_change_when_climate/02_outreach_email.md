# Outreach draft : check the claim about restoration targets before deciding about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. Field: climate-adaptive restoration research.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the four refuter items in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §3 have been checked once against your own case material. The claim must survive a ground check before the two-stage offer has anything to offer.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about restoration targets when climate has moved

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question in restoration: what should a restoration goal be when the site no longer resembles the historical conditions the goal was written for? I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a historical reference state is not the thing that settles whether a restoration succeeded, once climate moves. It is a target whose answer changes when climate is pushed. What is owed is not a new named community but a change in the form of the goal: re-enumerate what is actually in contact with the site now, then state success as measurable, falsifiable comparisons per population and per interaction, over a trajectory that is rebound as conditions change. The manager can add actors and cannot subtract the habitat, so the work is permanent rather than a return followed by an endpoint.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** I am not naming which species or assemblage should be planted. The framework I am using returns a shape for a goal, not a name for a community. That choice needs local measurement and a ground, and I have neither for your system. I am also not claiming that historical information is useless: it is evidence about processes, just not an anchor that decides an outcome.
>
> **What would refute the claim (written before this message):**
> 1. a restoration target that keeps settling success when climate is pushed, while remaining a convention that does not decide the outcome;
> 2. a fixed species list whose valence is invariant across two genuinely different restoration residuals and still pays against the ground;
> 3. one global fidelity score that predicts per-slot persistence as well as per-slot measurement across two climates;
> 4. a long-term multi-biome result in which historical-composition targets match or beat function and trajectory targets on persistence, with no interaction with climate novelty.
>
> If any of these holds, the claim is wrong and I will retract the relevant part. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the two sources that bear most directly, plus the full figure text if you want the authority rather than my summary.
> - McNellie et al. 2020, *Global Change Biology*, on why historical reference states are often unmeasurable, unfalsifiable and unattainable, and why contemporary states are measurable and falsifiable: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7756865
> - USGS Climate Adaptation Science Centers, Resist-Accept-Direct framework, on the three available goal options under ecosystem transformation: https://www.usgs.gov/programs/climate-adaptation-science-centers/ncasc-webinar-series-incorporating-rad-resist-accept
> - Verbatim figures with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the figure set and the eight-step formula are public. Run them on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state whether what you got is general, specified or grounded. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
>  [your name]

## Distribution rule

Stage 1: send the claim and the four refuter items. Record the verdict.
Stage 2, opt-in only: send the three links, noting that the figures and the papers, not this summary, are the authority.
Stage 3, opt-in only: offer the formula as a method, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

Later agreement from someone who has read the theory is never pooled as corroboration. Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool".
