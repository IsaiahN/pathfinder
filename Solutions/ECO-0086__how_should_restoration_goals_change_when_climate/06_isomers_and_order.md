# Goal forms as isomers, and the order in which they must be derived

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The restoration community currently writes two goal forms over what is largely the same material: a historical-fidelity form and a climate-adaptive form. Two questions are owed: (1) are they different terms under Fig 12's definition, or only different parameter values of one term, and (2) in what order must the parts of the answer be derived so that a figure line is not forced to govern the wrong quantity?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held in the goal vocabulary: species populations, site conditions (light, water, temperature, season, terrain, Fig 11 :46), relations (pollination, predation, competition, dependency, Fig 11 :47), the reference state, the target state, and the observed state. Bonds held: the seven, plus negation as the eighth unary operator. No new primitive is available without an import with a source gate.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 : Is the goal a comparison, and what follows from that?

Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."
Fig 12 :78: "is there a B before A fires? No." (the test for production, `⇒`)

A restoration goal has the form `target ⋛ observed`, an ordered threshold relation. The order matters: swapping operands changes the sign of the gap. Under a moving climate, the target operand changes value while the written goal usually does not, so a goal that once closed the residual stops closing it. Two repairs are available and they are not the same:

1. Rebind the target operand each loop, keeping the comparison bond. This is the shape derived in `01`.
2. Replace the comparison with identity, `target ≡ observed`, and drive the observed toward the target. This is the historical-fidelity form.

Operators table :51: "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one." A vocabulary that contains only `≡` and `−` can say what a site should match and what should be removed from it, and cannot state a trajectory toward a moving band. The fix is not to discard the comparison operator but to feed it a target that is restated as ordinary, bound, residual-accruing terms (Formula "Direction": make the sought-for shape an ordinary term).

### Q2 : Are the two goal forms isomers?

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
Fig 12 :96: "Two entries can share every ingredient and be inverses of one another."

Write the two forms over the same atoms:

- Historical form: `{species list, conditions, relations, reference} ` with bonds `reference ≡ target` and `target − non-native`, then a single terminal comparison at project close.
- Adaptive form: `{species list, conditions, relations, reference}` with bonds `reference → target` (the reference becomes input, not identity), `target ⋛ observed` evaluated per slot each loop, and `¬(fidelity settles success)` as the unary refusal.

They share the atom set and differ in the bonds. Under Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**: same ingredients, different substance, and the difference is decisive in behaviour. The historical form drives removal and a terminal audit; the adaptive form drives repeated rebinding and continuous per-slot comparison.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if the two forms can be written with the identical bond structure and differ only in a numeric parameter, such as the date of the reference or the value of a threshold, the isomer claim fails. They would then be one goal form with two settings, and the figure-derived requirement would reduce to "pick a different date," which is exactly the answer the audit refuses.

### Q3 : Functional group: what kind of thing are both forms?

Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Both forms contain the substructure `{target, comparison, observed}`. That substructure fixes the kind: a goal. The qualifier, `≡` versus `⋛ plus ¬`, fixes which member. This is the weaker and safer claim. It survives even if Q2's stronger isomer claim is refuted. Triangulated for the definition (Fig 12 :117 and :124 are two different structures in the same figure), single-source for the attribution.

### Q4 : What is the dependency order?

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

Map Fig 3's five links onto this question:

| Fig 3 link | restoration form | failure if skipped |
|---|---|---|
| 1 perception | re-enumerate habitat under the current climate (Fig 11 :43-48) | goal written against actors and conditions no longer in contact |
| 2 vocabulary | refuse "fidelity settles success" (Fig 5 :53); give terms to persistence and function | the middle link, having terms for what a situation affords, is a dictionary where a grammar is needed (Fig 12 :115) |
| 3 objective | state a per-slot, falsifiable comparison (Fig 12 :112, Fig 1 :19) | no declared progress, so nothing can be diagnosed |
| 4 planning | choose introductions under the asymmetry (Fig 11 :61-62) | effort lands on a goal the prior links cannot support |
| 5 learn and carry | measure the round trip (Fig 4 :30) and re-enumerate | dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked |

The order is **perception before vocabulary before objective before planning**. In particular, naming a replacement community before re-enumerating the habitat is a planning move made above the break: it arrives at a step that cannot use it. Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."

### Q5 : What about scale and cascades?

Fig 11 :52: "And contact cascades." Fig 11 :53-54 records that the residual that mattered was three steps out from direct contact (DDT to eagles, via biomagnification). A per-slot comparison that only tracks the planted species is first-order enumeration. The goal must include slots two and three out: pollinators, predators, soil biota, hydrology, and the conditions that carry them. Fig 4 :30 requires the round trip at each scale change to be measured, since the coarser description cannot hold the detail.

**Falsifier for Q5:** a goal that tracks only first-order slots and still predicts persistence and function across two climates would break the cascade requirement. The BES Lehmann lovegrass case is a standing counterexample to the naive version: accepting the novel grass was based on its soil stabilisation function, a first-order service, while its forage quality and invasion dynamics remained unresolved.

## CONCLUSION

The two goal forms are **isomers** over a shared atom set under Fig 12 :117, or at minimum members of one **functional group** under Fig 12 :124. They are not two settings of one term unless the bond difference collapses, which the falsifier in Q2 tests. The derivation order is fixed by Fig 3: re-enumerate, refuse, restate, plan, carry. This rescues what is defensible in both management positions without importing a new primitive or naming a community the framework cannot name.
