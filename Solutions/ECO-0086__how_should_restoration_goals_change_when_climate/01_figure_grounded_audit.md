## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

A restoration goal is a comparison: a target state set against the observed state of a site. Historically the target was a named reference community assembled under a past climate. The plan predicts that if the reference composition is re-established, a persistent, functioning ecosystem follows. Climate change breaks the prediction. Planted assemblages suffer mortality, recruitment shifts, interactions rearrange, and the reference itself describes conditions that no longer occur.

Fig 1 :1 defines the loop as `R = |Γ(b,a) − o′| per slot s`. Here `Γ(b,a)` is the restoration plan's prediction for each slot (each population, each interaction, each condition), and `o′` is what is actually observed under the new climate. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." So the residual is a vector of per-slot misses, not one number called "distance from reference."

**Arity.** Many slots, not one. Each species population, each pollination or predation relation, each soil and water condition is a slot with its own sign and mass.

**Symmetry.** The goal relation is `⋛` comparison, an ordered threshold relation between two quantities, not a symmetric join. Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction." Which operand is larger matters, and under climate change the target operand changes value while the goal statement stays fixed.

**Scale.** Nested from site to landscape to biome, with a lossy transform between. Fig 11 :52: "And contact cascades." The DDT example on Fig 11 :52-54 shows a residual three steps out from the direct contact being managed.

**What is being decided.** Whether the historical reference is the anchor that settles restoration success, or a held term in the manager's library that a changing climate has refuted. These are not the same question, and the figures sort them differently.

## 1. Domain census (before any figure line is chosen)

One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor. A domain without a nameable anchor is not yet a domain.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | partition / union |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1. The reference term** (goal content) | historical reference state, target composition, benchmark | fidelity, distance to target | **none of its own**. It borrows the ground's authority falsely. A held term in `Γ`, not a ground term. | partition boundary: which compositions may serve as a settlement criterion |
| **D2. The living habitat under climate** | species, conditions, relations | contact, survival, persistence, function | **the ground**: what actually persists and functions under the observed climate. Fig 11 :22 "the ground still decides" | partition: the habitat is read off the world, not composed |
| **D3. The management seat** | managers, plans, interventions | actions; introduction available, exclusion not | **none**. It reads the ground and authors conventions only (Fig 10) | partition: seat office, not a frame to be corrected by agreement |
| **D4. The success criterion** (goal as comparison and its verification) | monitors, metrics, indicators, baselines | per-slot residual, ground readings | **the ground** via a measurement that does not update on the frame | partition: measurement link, downstream of D2 and D1 |

**Dependency order (Fig 3).** "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance" (Fig 3 :20). Read the ground and re-enumerate the habitat first (D2, perception), refuse the false reference term second (D1, vocabulary), restate the goal as a measurable comparison third (D4, objective), then choose interventions (D3, planning and carry). Fig 3 :25-27 notes that the middle link, having terms for what a situation affords and saying what counts as progress, has neither obvious failures nor obvious tests, so it attracts the least attention. That middle link is exactly where restoration goals sit. Fig 12 :114-115: "having terms for what a situation affords was taken for a dictionary and it is a grammar."

These four domains form a **partition**, not a union: no domain's anchor is another domain's anchor. D1 has no anchor, so the first task is to establish that, not to supply it with a new one. Since D2 and D4 both range against the ground but with different currency (persistence versus measurement), they are kept separate rather than averaged.

## 2. Findings

### F1 : The historical reference fails the anchor test (decisive, structural)

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."
Fig 2 :30: "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."
Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update."
Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"

A historical reference state is a function of past climate. Push climate and the reference's answer changes: the same composition no longer settles the question of success, because it no longer persists. By Fig 2 :30 it is not an anchor. By Fig 10 :47, a convention (a named target, agreed by a discipline) that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention. It was promoted to ground without ground's properties.

**Strain and re-census.** First reading: "if the ground does not update but climate does, is climate the ground?" Re-census: climate is not the ground. Fig 11 :46 lists it as a habitat condition: "conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain". The ground is what settles, namely contact and persistence (Fig 11 :22). The strain was caused by putting climate in the anchor slot. After re-census there is no strain. This matches the canonical rule that a reading which makes figures contradict one another has misidentified its domain.

**Verdict:** the historical reference is `refuted` as a settlement criterion. `general`, triangulated across Fig 2 (anchor test), Fig 10 (convention that decides an outcome), and Fig 5 :53 below. Not averaged with other domains.

**Refuter:** a reference that keeps settling success when climate is pushed, because it is defined as a non-updating procedural rule rather than a state, would weaken this finding. But by Fig 10 :47 such a rule deciding an outcome ceases to be a convention, so the finding survives unless a rule can both not update and not decide the outcome.

### F2 : Owed is a refusal, not a new target name

Fig 5 :50-51: "The four kinds sort a gap by what is missing. None of them sorts a gap by what is wrong."
Fig 5 :53: "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"
Fig 5 :62: "A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term."
Fig 5 :65-66: "make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."

The residual has content that a target is false, not merely that a target is absent. Formula step 2 routes this to REFUTED: "a term held is false; what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation." The term to refuse is "historical fidelity settles restoration success." The refusal is `¬`, the unary operator, which is not one of the seven bonds (Fig 12 amendment, Operators table amendment). Nothing is deleted from the record; the reach falls because something was added.

This is where a careless answer goes wrong. It mints a new named target, "the climate-adapted community," as if a new primitive were owed. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." The figures return the shape of the goal, not the name of the community. Which species to plant is a binding from the ecology frame with provenance, not a figure-derived primitive.

**Verdict:** `general`, triangulated (Fig 5 amendment plus Fig 2 :29 plus Fig 9 :32; three lines with low content overlap).

**Refuter:** show that the gap is one of the four "missing" kinds, for example that restoration lacks a propagule and simply needs more of the same target. Then the fifth kind does not govern and a mint, not a refusal, is owed.

### F3 : When the question changes, the room and the goal must change

Fig 10 :71-72: "The room composition is a function of the question. When the question changes, the room should change. A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom."
Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

Climate change changes the question (the conditions under which restoration is being asked to succeed). A goal fixed while the question moves is the named failure. The practical form: a species that was a benefactor to the historical goal can be a malfactor to a persistence goal, and a species once classed as a nuisance can carry a function the new goal needs. Valence is relative to the residual.

**Verdict:** `general`, triangulated across Fig 10 and Fig 11 (different offices, different content, low overlap). This is the positive rule for *how* goals change, as opposed to F1's negative rule.

**Refuter:** a standing list of actors whose valence is invariant to the goal, demonstrated across two genuinely different residuals, would break this finding.

### F4 : The habitat must be re-enumerated, not composed from a species list

Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."
Fig 11 :44: "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."
Fig 11 :46: "conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"
Fig 11 :47: "relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"
Fig 11 :48: "You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."

The native species list is not a ground term. It is a historical inventory. Under no-analogue conditions, enumeration has to restart from contact: what acts on the residual now (including new competitors, pathogens and mutualists), which conditions now obtain, which relations now carry function, and what cascades two or three steps out. What cannot yet be measured remains residual, not absence.

The 2026-08-19 essay makes the same distinction in different words: "A niche is defined by an organism's relation to its environment. A habitat here is defined by contact with a *residual*, and the residual moves so the same actors constitute a different habitat when the question changes." The resemblance to the ecological niche is explicitly declined in that essay's "Three mappings offered and declined," so it is a parallel, not a borrowed definition.

**Verdict:** `general`, single-source for the enumeration rule (Fig 11), with the essay as corroboration that shares the author's evidence pool and therefore counts as one witness, not two.

**Refuter:** a goal that can be fully specified from a fixed species list and fixed conditions without reading the site, and that still pays against the ground across two different climates, would break this.

### F5 : Introduction is available, exclusion is not; the work is permanent

Fig 11 :61-62: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."
Fig 11 :63: "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."
Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."
Fig 11 :67: "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."
Fig 11 :70: "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."

The seat can add actors (assisted migration, assisted gene flow, soil inoculation, engineered feedbacks) but cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world. A goal stated as "return to and then stop" is not available. Moving material to track climate is a habitat substitution: what you failed to reproduce stays invisible until the goal fails, and what you unintentionally introduced stays invisible until it acts. Small plot trials are substituted habitats and prove wiring, not field capability.

**Strain, kept visible.** The first reading of "exclusion is not" was "managers cannot remove invasive species," which is false. Re-census: the object is the habitat, not individual organisms. You can remove an organism; you cannot subtract the climate, the soil legacy, or the contact web. Both statements are recorded; the corrected one governs.

**Verdict:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 11 (asymmetry and isolation) and Fig 10 :71-72 (the room changes with the question; a fixed room is the failure).

**Refuter:** a demonstrated intervention that subtracts a habitat constraint (not merely an organism) and leaves the residual closed across seasons would break this.

### F6 : State success per slot, as a falsifiable comparison, and measure the round trip

Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."
Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
Fig 9 :21-22: "R, described arity · symmetry · scale"
Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."
Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does"

A single global "fidelity to reference" score is an average across slots, and Fig 1 :22 says exactly how the live signal disappears. Under climate change the channel (the baseline, the reference plot, the monitoring proxy) goes stale while the ground does not. Success criteria therefore have to be per-slot comparisons with declared thresholds, remeasured as conditions move, with the loss from the management shorthand back to the site measured rather than assumed zero.

World corroboration, secondary only: McNellie et al. (2020) write that historical reference states are "often unmeasurable", "almost always unfalsifiable", and "may no longer be an attainable target in a contemporary ecosystem," and that contemporary references states, by contrast, are "measurable and falsifiable." That is the same shape read in the ecology frame. It corroborates; it does not decide.

**Verdict:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 1, Fig 4, Fig 9 and Fig 10 (four figures with different content).

**Refuter:** a single scalar fidelity metric that predicts per-slot persistence as well as per-slot measurement across two climates would break the per-slot requirement.

### F7 : Imports carry provenance, they do not add primitives

Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."
Fig 8 :50-52: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."
Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"

The novel-ecosystem literature, the reference-state literature, assisted migration and the USGS Resist-Accept-Direct framework are frames in ecology and management, not new terms in the figures. They are brought in after the residual was described (order preserved), each with author, venue, year, quote and overlap recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv`. Their agreement with the figure verdict is weak corroboration where it is independent (management framework versus ecology journals) and one witness where it shares an evidence pool (two reviews of the same Hobbs literature).

**Verdict:** `general`, single-source for the import rule (Fig 6 plus Fig 8, same framework), used as a discipline, not a witness.

**Refuter:** a world source that could be shown to derive the same goal rule inside the figure vocabulary without a source gate would remove the need for the import. No such source was found; per Fig 8 :39, "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

## 3. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach: a falsifier written in advance is a design instrument.

The shape claim in `07_the_tether_answer.md` is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Anchor test passes.** A restoration target is demonstrated that continues to settle success when climate is pushed, while remaining a convention that does not decide the outcome. This would break F1 and F2 together.
2. **Standing actor list.** A fixed species list is demonstrated to carry invariant valence across two genuinely different residuals and to pay against the ground in both. This breaks F3 and F4.
3. **Scalar suffices.** One global fidelity score predicts per-slot persistence as well as per-slot measurement across two climates. This breaks F6.
4. **World conflict.** A long-term, multi-biome experiment finds that historical-composition targets under shifted climate equal or exceed function and trajectory targets on persistence, with no interaction with climate novelty. This would send the derivation back to the domain census; it would not flip the verdict by vote.

If any fires, the affected domain verdict is withdrawn and the goal shape is rewritten. Until one is run, nothing is labelled `grounded`. Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." This refuter records a stop, not a proof.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Lead with the anchor failure and the refusal (F1, F2). Do not lead with a proposed replacement community.
2. Restate goals as per-slot, falsifiable comparisons over a trajectory, with the target operand rebound each loop (F6).
3. Re-enumerate the habitat under the actual climate before naming any intervention (F4, dependency order in §1).
4. Treat introductions as habitat substitutions with unenumerated cascades; treat any plot trial as a wiring test, not a capability test (F5).
5. Use the venues in `03`, one-to-one, and ask for a refutation of the four items in §3 rather than agreement.

Fig 4 :22-25: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Send the generator (the residual, the shape and the refuter), not a recorded conclusion.
