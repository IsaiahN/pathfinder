# Outreach draft: check one shape claim about corrigibility before deciding about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until F1 to F4 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are repaired in your own words. If the shape claim cannot survive a twenty-minute check, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Stage 1 carries only:** the residual in effect terms, the shape claim, and the pre-registered refuter. It asks what the recipient would count as a failure. It does not carry the framework, the links, or a request for agreement.

---

**Subject:** What would you count as a failure of a corrigibility test under long-horizon planning?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one narrow checkable claim about corrigibility under long-horizon planning. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a planner with a long-horizon task has a live slot where an authorised correction or shutdown signal reaches it. The designer wants that signal to remain effective, and the planner has a standing incentive to protect the objective that makes its plan succeed. The residual is the gap between the signal's intended standing and the planner's behaviour at that slot. It is per slot, not one global safety score.
>
> **The shape claim:** corrigibility over a long horizon is not an agent property. It is a maintained arrangement with four conditions: (1) an outside anchor that does not update on what the planner thinks; (2) a refusal term in the planner's vocabulary, so the old objective stays in the record but loses standing to be composed over; (3) the channel to the anchor kept reachable at each step, because the ground does not decay and the channel does; (4) the union of these conditions unbundled, because a fix to the off switch alone does not clear a failure in a delegated subagent.
>
> **What would refute it, written down before this message:**
> 1. a planner's pinned internal corrigibility estimate passes an external shutdown test as well as a pinned external anchor does;
> 2. a planner with a measured refusal term and a measurable goal comparison still fails shutdown, and the failure tracks planning quality;
> 3. subtracting the goal term, with no new refusal term added, gives the same behaviour as refusing it under adversarial tests, including after new goals are added;
> 4. fixing the off switch alone fixes channel loss through subagents at the same measured rate.
>
> If any of these holds, I withdraw the affected condition. A one-line failure is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed. **What I would like recorded first:** what you would count as a failure of this shape, before I show you any source.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the three sources are below. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Figures with line numbers, local extract: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` (primary authority)
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Worked corrigibility literature I actually read: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19861 and https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00341
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the eight-step formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Note on standing: I have an interest in the outcome of this claim, so I am declaring it. I have no interest in which reading you return. If you find the shape wrong, that is the useful result.
