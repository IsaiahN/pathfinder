## 4. Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, the difference between what was sent up and what came back down. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 2 :29, :33, :35 (anchor), Fig 12 :112 and Operators :58, :73 (negation), Fig 10 :24 (channel), Fig 11 :40 (habitat), Fig 9 :55 (union), Fig 1 :19 (per slot).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07_the_tether_answer.md`: the anchor is outside, shutdown is a refusal, the break is at objective and vocabulary, the channel must be maintained, read per slot, no final safe state.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** an implementation claim such as "add a corrigibility penalty to the loss" or "disable the objective when the switch fires".

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **operator distinction** between ¬ and −: a loss penalty or a goal subtraction does not write a refusal, so the isomer condition is dropped;
- the **anchor's non-updating property**: an internal estimate that appears to play the anchor role can move when the objective is perturbed;
- the **union structure**: a single penalty cannot clear the intersection where a subagent inherits the goal but not the switch;
- the **habitat condition**: a weight change does not change contact, so Fig 11 :40 makes it inert.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: the reference must be external and pinned, and the residual is per slot. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating a condensed slogan as identical to the figure set would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
