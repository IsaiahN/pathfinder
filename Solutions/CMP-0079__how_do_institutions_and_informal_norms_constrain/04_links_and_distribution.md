## 2. What the wider literature bears on the question

The world sources corroborate shapes only. They do not settle the question and are not witnesses for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).

1. **Norm as grammar and as an introduction problem.** SEP gives the grammar-of-interaction shape and the rigidity failure: a norm can be *"so rigid as to prevent the fine-tuning that would be necessary to accommodate new cases."* This predicts the Fig 10 convention failure (F3) and the Fig 11 introduction-only rule (F2). Muldoon's point that there is *"no simple way to intentionally modify a social norm, as one can with a law or institutional rule"* corroborates *"exclusion is not"* at the informal-norm stratum. Corroborates, no conflict.
2. **Constraining and enabling, not good and bad.** New institutionalism frames institutions by *"the constraining and enabling effects of formal and informal rules"* and reports heterogeneous responses rather than simple convergence. This corroborates F1 and F4 (valence relative to the residual) and refuses a single score for "norm impact". One evidence pool with the Institution page; counted once.
3. **Isomorphism, path dependence, local maxima.** The Institution page reports DiMaggio and Powell's coercive, mimetic and normative isomorphism, North's incremental change through formal and informal institutions, path dependence, and Lustick's local-maxima trap. These predict the shapes in F5 and F9. **Conflict handled:** rational choice institutionalism's *"exogenously given constraints"* conflicts with F4; it was returned to the census and re-mapped to a fixed residual, so the verdict was not flipped. Lustick's own caveat that diagnosing a local maximum *"does nothing to solve the problem"* corroborates the declared gap F11: a shape is not a named fix.
4. **Complex adaptive systems.** The CAS page gives *"may be influenced but not precisely controlled"* and path-dependent sensitivity to initial conditions. This predicts the introduction-only, permanently-managed shape of F2 and the valence-of-F4. Low overlap with the institutional pages; a distinct witness for the mechanism shape, not for a specific case.
5. **Polycentric governance (record only).** Ostrom's lecture title names the governance shape that the framework essay reports as a convergent derivation from a different field. It is recorded with provenance and a shadow test still owed. Because the full text was not opened here, it carries no substantive quotation and no independent verdict weight.
6. **Framework-internal essays.** Both are by the framework's author and share its evidence pool. Their agreement with the figures is expected and carries no independent corroboration weight. They are used for the canonical reading of "shape" in `05_what_shape_means.md`, not as outside witnesses.
