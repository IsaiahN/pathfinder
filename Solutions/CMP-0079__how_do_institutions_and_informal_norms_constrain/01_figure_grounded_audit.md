## 0. The residual in effect terms

Networked agents run a per-slot loop: perceive, plan, act, predict, with `R = |Γ(b,a) - o'|` indexed by object slot. Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :13: **"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"**. The residual R is the change the adaptation is trying to close.

The question asks what institutions and informal norms do to that loop. The residual is not "what is an institution" (a definition) and not "are norms good" (a verdict). It is: by what structure does a norm change the trajectory of R across a population of slots? Arity: a three-place relation (norm, agent-slot, ground return), indexed per slot and ordered in time. Symmetry: a norm is an ordered, threshold-like constraint, not a symmetric join. It carries the comparison bond and the sequence bond (Fig 12 :112: **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."**). Scale: single-agent loop (Fig 1), population with a shared library and a ranking (Fig 4 :5), and habitat cascade outward several steps (Fig 11 :52-54).

Two readings were tested and only one survives. Reading A treats institutions and norms as an internal add-on to the agent. Reading B treats them as habitat in contact with R. The census and the strain test below decide for B.

**Partition or union.** D1/D3/D5 form one compound (loop, population, closure). D2/D4/D6 form another (habitat, convention, verification). The two compounds overlap at the ground, so the whole is a **union, not a partition**. Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** In particular "constrain" and "redirect" are not one lever (F7).

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Perception of what is in contact (D2) before vocabulary for what a norm affords; objective (what counts as adaptation) before planning; refuse before mint (Formula step 2); describe R before naming a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); install a violable convention before it decides an outcome (Fig 10 :18-19); selection between rooms after variation (Fig 7 :35).

**Strain test applied before concluding.** Reading A strains against Fig 11 :40 and :61-62: a norm that only changes an agent's internal belief without changing contact is not adaptation, and "constraint" read as subtraction contradicts *"exclusion is not"*. Per the usage rule, a strained line governs another quantity, so the census was redone: the norm was re-censed as habitat/contact (D2) and "constraint" was re-bound as change of contact, not removal of contact. ## 2. Findings F1-F12

### F1 Institutions and norms are habitat, not agent internals (decisive, triangulated)

Fig 11 :39: **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**
Fig 11 :40: **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**
Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**
Fig 11 :73: **"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."**

Triangulated with Fig 4 :19-20: **"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."** The loop is invariant; what changes across a population is the cast, that is, the habitat. Low overlap between the habitat figure and the membrane figure. Mode `general` for the structure, `specified` for institutions and norms.

**Refuter:** a controlled case where the same norm produces the same change in R after the habitat (all actors, conditions, and relations in contact with R) has been substituted so that the norm is no longer in contact. If adaptation still changes, the norm is an agent-internal property and F1 fails.

### F2 Constraint works by introduction, not subtraction (triangulated)

Fig 11 :61: **"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."**
Fig 11 :62: **"You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."**
Fig 11 :63: **"a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state"**

A norm constrains by adding an instrument, a convention that makes deviance statable, or a new actor whose cascade changes what pays. It cannot subtract contact. This resolves the strain named in §1: "constrain" in the question must be read as change of contact, not removal of contact. A norm that appears to remove a behaviour is actually introducing a cost or an alternative into the habitat; the contact remains and must be managed permanently.

**Refuter:** a documented case where a norm changes the trajectory of R by removing an actor or condition from contact with R, with the removal shown to be the operative cause and no introduction involved. That would break the asymmetry and F2 fails.

### F3 An informal norm is a convention and must be violable (triangulated)

Fig 10 :18: **"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"**
Fig 10 :19: **"install what can be violated"**
Fig 10 :47: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**
Fig 10 :49: **"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."**
Fig 2 :43: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**

A healthy norm makes deviance statable without deciding the outcome. It is a convention: a constraint with no truth value about the world. Fig 10 :46: **"A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do."** When a norm hardens so that it settles which adaptation is allowed, or when it cannot be checked, it becomes **"a constant the seat authored, carrying the seat's authority"** (Fig 10 :48). That is the named failure mode: norm capture. It is why informal norms can redirect adaptation while formal rules can freeze it, and why the distinction is structural rather than about written versus unwritten.

**Refuter:** show a norm that decides outcomes, cannot be violated, and still counts as a convention under the figures, or show that a checkable, violable norm has no effect on deviance statability. Either breaks F3.

### F4 Valence is relative to the residual (triangulated)

Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
Fig 11 :50: **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**
Fig 11 :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

An institution that enables adaptation to one residual is a malfactor for another. There is no figure-licensed list of good or bad norms. This is why the world literature's *"constraining and enabling effects of formal and informal rules"* (New institutionalism, Wikipedia, accessed 2026-11-22) is a corroborating shape, not a classification: the same rule does both depending on R. It also refuses the rational-choice framing of institutions as *"exogenously given constraints"* as a verdict; when that conflict was met it was returned to the census (see §6).

**Refuter:** an invariant valence for one norm across two documented residuals with different grounds, where the norm's sign for R does not change. That would refute the relative-valence claim.

### F5 Mutual-update norms produce coherent drift, not adaptation (triangulated)

Fig 2 :13: **"no anchor; each updates on the others"**
Fig 2 :15: **"is whoever had the most influence"**
Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**
Fig 7 :12-13: **"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"**

When a norm makes agents update on one another with no anchor, they converge on whoever had the most influence. The drift is coherent, so nobody notices. Agreement among networked agents is not evidence that adaptation occurred. This bears directly on institutional isomorphism (DiMaggio and Powell 1983, as reported in the Institution and New institutionalism pages): convergence on legitimacy is a real shape, but it is a drift shape, not a ground shape. F5 does **not** claim isomorphism and coherent drift are the same thing; see the strain note in §6.

**Refuter:** a population whose members update only on one another, with no anchor in contact, whose residuals nevertheless track the ground better over time. That would break the drift verdict.

### F6 Contact cascades; direct-scope enumeration is a failure (single-source)

Fig 11 :52-54: **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**
Fig 11 :44: **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**

A norm introduced at one node has second and third order effects. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is a named failure, not a simplification. Single-source (Fig 11 only), with the DDT case as the figure's own example, not an independently checked measurement here.

**Refuter:** a norm whose entire effect on R is captured at first-order contact with no measurable second-order effect after the cascade is enumerated. Then the cascade warning does not govern this case.

### F7 Constraint and redirection form a union, not one lever (triangulated)

Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**
Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

"Constrain or redirect" is a bundled question. A single lever (say, change the reward rule) clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing, because the second condition still binds. Unbundle before searching: (C1) the norm changes contact, (C2) the convention remains violable and checkable, (C3) the ground still selects. The three are conditions of a union, not stages of one mechanism.

**Refuter:** one intervention that restores all three conditions at once and leaves no categorically different residue, across two cases where initially only one condition held. That would collapse the union to a partition.

### F8 The ground decides, not agreement (triangulated)

Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**
Fig 7 :37: **"Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:"**
Fig 7 :38: **"the bracket's fidelity across the handoff"**
Fig 7 :39: **"the ground's pressure on the room"**

A norm-governed group can agree perfectly and still not adapt. Progress per handoff needs both bracket fidelity and ground pressure; zero either and the chain runs in place. This is the operational form of Fig 2 :29, where an anchor is **"a measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome"** and does not move because of what a frame thinks.

**Refuter:** a group where both bracket fidelity and ground pressure measure high and non-declining, yet R does not close over repeated handoffs. That would break the two-factor claim.

### F9 Norms can hold a closure or open it (triangulated)

Fig 6 :14: **"the only operator that moves the wall"**
Fig 6 :21: **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."**
Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**
Fig 8 :32: **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."**

A norm that keeps agents updating within one closure cannot manufacture the atom needed to close R; more search inside it is "more search, not another atom" only if the atom is already in the closure (Fig 8 :40-43). A norm that opens contact to a differing closure can move the wall by import. This is the structural difference between a norm that constrains adaptation to the current vocabulary and one that redirects it to a new one.

**Refuter:** a population that closes R by internal composition alone with no new atom and no contact with a differing closure, where R genuinely required a new atom. That would break idempotence here.

### F10 Standing is a stake; an institution with a reputation to protect cannot be the ground (triangulated)

Fig 10 :61: **"standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on"**
Fig 10 :60: **"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it."**
Fig 10 :93: **"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."**
Fig 2 :42: **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."**

Institutions accumulate standing. Standing is a stake that outlives the outcome that earned it, so an institution cannot serve as the anchor for the adaptation it governs without declaring the stake. This is not a moral objection; it is the seat rule. It also explains why "trust the institution" is a collapse and not corroboration.

**Refuter:** an institution whose standing changes the ground's answer, shown to leave the ground unmoved and the verdict unchanged. Then the stake rule does not bind this case.

### F11 GAP: which specific norm, in which case, and with what sign and magnitude (genuine gap)

No figure line governs this after re-census. The figures return a kind of thing, not a name. Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**. Fig 9 :44: **"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room."** Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"** and Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

The three routes were kept distinct, in the required order:

1. **SEARCHED (asked first, cheapest).** The world literature was searched and read: institutional theory, new institutionalism, social norms, complex adaptive systems. It supplies shapes (grammar of interaction, isomorphism, path dependence, influence but not control), not the specific norm for a specific case. Being first does not let it hand a verdict (Fig 9 :60).
2. **INWARD.** Extend the instrument from one already returning something: measure bracket fidelity and ground pressure on a running group; per-slot R, not the average (Fig 1 :28: **"The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."**). This refines in our own terms and cannot, by itself, supply a missing atom.
3. **OUTWARD.** Import: characterize R in effect terms, then find the frame whose closure predicts its shape. The Ostrom/Polycentric governance frame is a candidate and its provenance is recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv`, with the shadow test still owed. INWARD and OUTWARD were not read against each other (Fig 6 :30-31).

**Verdict: gap.** Unreachable at this budget, not proven absent. "Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence."

## 3. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. The same norm changes R identically after the habitat in contact with R is substituted so the norm is absent from contact (breaks F1).
2. A norm changes the trajectory of R by subtracting an actor or condition from contact, with no introduction operative (breaks F2).
3. A norm that decides outcomes and cannot be violated still satisfies the figure's convention test (breaks F3).
4. One norm keeps invariant valence across two residuals with different grounds (breaks F4).
5. A population updating only on itself, with no anchor in contact, tracks the ground better over time (breaks F5).
6. All three union conditions are restored by one lever with no categorically different residue across two cases (breaks F7).
7. Progress continues with bracket fidelity or ground pressure measured at zero (breaks F8).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, no claim is labelled `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Lead with F1, F2, and F3: norms are habitat; they work by introduction; a convention must be violable.
2. Keep the union unbundled (F7). Do not offer a single lever for "constrain or redirect".
3. Name no specific norm as the answer. Name the conditions a specific norm would have to satisfy and leave the slot open (F11).
4. Treat agreement inside a norm-governed group as variation, never as a verdict (F5, F8).
5. Declare the standing of any institution acting as ground (F10).
6. Send the generator (the shape, the refuter, and the lineage), not the recording. Fig 4 :25: **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."**
