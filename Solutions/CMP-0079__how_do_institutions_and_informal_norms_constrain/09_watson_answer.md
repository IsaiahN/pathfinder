## QUESTION

How do institutions and informal norms constrain or redirect the adaptation of networked agents? Restated as an effect: by what structure does a norm change the trajectory of the per-slot residual `R = |Γ(b,a) - o'|` of networked agents? Arity: a three-place relation (norm, agent-slot, ground return), indexed per slot and ordered in time. Symmetry: a norm is an ordered threshold-like constraint, not a symmetric join; it carries sequence, comparison, subtraction and (for refusal) negation (Fig 12 :112). Scale: single-agent loop, population with shared library and ranking, and habitat cascade outward several steps. Option A: norms act on the agents as an internal add-on, so constraint is subtraction and the norm can be understood from the agent alone. Option B: norms are habitat in contact with `R`, so constraint is introduction and the norm can only be understood by enumerating the habitat. What each leads to: A licenses a single lever and a good/bad classification; B yields a union of conditions, relative valence, and a declared gap. The figures decide for B.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The figure-derived verdict, kept separate per domain and never averaged: institutions and norms are habitat, not agent internals; they change per-slot `R` by introducing contacts and violable conventions, never by subtracting contact; a norm that decides outcomes or cannot be checked stops being a convention; a norm's sign is relative to the residual, so no good/bad list survives; agreement inside a governed group is variation and the ground selects between rooms; an institution with standing cannot serve as its own anchor; which norm, where, and with what sign is a declared `gap`. Mode `specified` for the application, `general` for the structure, not `grounded`. The world literature corroborates the mechanism shape after re-mapping; the one conflict (exogenous constraints) returned to the census and did not flip the verdict. The strongest checkable point is the introduction-only asymmetry, because it is refused by the intuitive subtraction reading and is therefore falsifiable in ordinary cases.

- **Cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. If the habitat claim fails, a real norm would be redesigned as an agent-internal fix and the operative contact would never be touched; if the violability claim fails, a convention would be allowed to decide an outcome and an interested institution would settle the question. The cost to outreach is that an expert's twenty minutes is spent refuting a shape that was never the right shape. Fig 2 :42 prices the remedy: independence must be bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong. That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter and never pooling later agreement.
- **Undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails; a refusal is added and the reach falls because *"something was added rather than because anything was deleted"* (Fig 6 :42-43). The shape, its refuters and its re-census trail are recorded with provenance so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62: *"A ruling carries provenance like an atom."*). A superseding packet can replace a sent one without withdrawing a primitive.
- **Reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. Any of the pre-registered falsifiers: (1) identical `R` change after substitution removes the norm from contact; (2) an operative subtraction of contact; (3) a non-violable outcome-deciding norm that passes the convention test; (4) invariant norm sign across two different residuals; (5) progress with bracket fidelity or ground pressure at zero; (6) closing a genuinely new-atom residual by internal composition alone. Also a source with a genuinely different evidence pool showing that per-agent adaptation is fully predicted by the norm treated as an exogenous fixed constraint with invariant valence. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
