# Bond structure, order, and analogues for institutional constraint

**Mode: `general` on the bond structure, `specified` on the application. Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).**

## RESIDUAL

"Constraint" and "redirection" are being used as if one mechanism explained both. Two questions are owed: (1) what is the bond structure of a norm's action on per-slot `R`, and (2) are constraint and redirection isomers, a functional group, or a union of conditions? The answer decides whether one lever can clear the question.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: an actor, a condition, a relation, a threshold (comparison), an ordered succession, a subtraction, and a refusal (negation). Bonds held: the seven, plus the unary negation operator. No new primitive is available without import (Fig 13 :74: **"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."**). The ground is the anchor; agreement inside the norm is not.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 What bonds does a norm's action actually use?

Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**
Fig 12 :112: **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."**

A norm that merely names a required state (be honest, be punctual) uses only an ingredient vocabulary. It cannot write a route. The route requires four different operators:

| operator | figure definition | use in a norm's action |
|---|---|---|
| `→` sequence | A then B; B needs A to have happened | the norm applies after a triggering condition, not before |
| `⋛` comparison | a threshold relation between two quantities | the norm is a threshold on `R` or on a cost, not an absolute rule |
| `−` subtraction | A with B removed or absent | progress is a subtraction of `R`; the norm changes what can be subtracted |
| `¬` negation | this does not hold | a refusal of a held term; a violable convention needs it, which is why a vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate |

The fourth row is decisive for F3 of the audit. A norm that cannot be refused because the group has no negation operator is not a strong convention; it is an accumulation. Fig 10 :47: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."** The grammar-of-interaction shape from the SEP article is the world-side corroboration of this operator requirement, not its source.

Mode `general` for the operator requirements; `specified` for the mapping onto norms.

### Q2 Are constraint and redirection isomers?

Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

- **As commonly written**, "constraint" and "redirection" share almost nothing: constraint names a cost or prohibition, redirection names a new affordance. By Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**; they are two members of a broader **functional group** (norms that change `R`), with different atoms in the slots.
- **The stronger version**: if both are written over the full shared habitat atom set (actor, condition, relation, threshold, cost, refusal) and differ only in which bond mediates which contact, then they **are isomers**: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. That is the claim worth testing, because it predicts that the same institutional atoms can produce either effect with a change of bond alone, no new primitive and no import.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if redirection requires an atom that constraint does not (for example a new instrument or a frame with a differing closure, Fig 6 :23), then the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. Import then moves the wall (Fig 6 :14: **"the only operator that moves the wall"**), so the two are categorically different, not rearrangements of one set.

The isomer reading is single-source for the figure definition, with the habitat enumeration still unmeasured.

### Q3 What is the order of the union?

Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

The conditions are (C1) the norm changes contact by introduction, (C2) the convention is violable and checkable, (C3) the ground still selects between rooms. Dependency order, using Fig 3:

1. **Perception of contact** (D2): enumerate what is in contact with `R` before calling anything a norm. Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
2. **Vocabulary**: do we have `→`, `⋛`, `−` and `¬` to write the route, or only ingredients? A missing `¬` is a missing refusal, and it is the middle link most likely to be unmeasured (Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**).
3. **Objective**: state what would count as adaptation on this residual, per slot, not as an average (Fig 1 :28).
4. **Planning and carry**: choose the room against the residual it surfaced; carry methods up, head starts down (Fig 4 :22).
5. **Settlement**: the ground votes between rooms (Fig 7 :35).

A chain that is strong at the top and broken at the vocabulary link produces nothing. Most institutional analysis lands at step 1 or 3, where instruments already exist; step 2 is the unlooked-at link. Mode `specified`.

### Q4 Two analogues, and why they are not the answer

- **Catalyst.** Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."** Some norms survive their own enforcement (a convention internalised into a routine); others are spent each time (a sanction that depletes compliance). Whether a norm is a catalyst is a measurable question about repeated use, not a property readable from its wording. Refuter: identical behaviour across uses after a norm is shown to be spent rather than regenerated.
- **Cycle.** Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."** Norms that feed their own outcomes back (success breeds stricter enforcement, failure breeds relaxation) are cycles, not chains. A chain vocabulary cannot close them, which is why a linear "norm causes adaptation" model misses the sign. Refuter: a norm whose output never re-enters as input, with the loop sign demonstrably zero.
- **Isomorphism and coherent drift, kept separate.** Institutional isomorphism (DiMaggio and Powell 1983, as reported on the Institution and New institutionalism pages) is convergence on legitimacy; coherent drift (Fig 2 :13-15) is convergence with no anchor. They share a shape, not a measure. I retracted the earlier identity claim in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §6. Their agreement is one evidence pool, not two witnesses.

### Q5 Level relativity of a settled norm

Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"**

A norm that is settled against the ground in one habitat becomes an atom for the next habitat's composition. It is not irreducible in the world, only at that level. This is why an "institution" can be treated as a primitive by one analysis (a firm's rulebook) and as a composition by another (a cascade of earlier settlements). Re-census, not contradiction. Mode `general`.

## CONCLUSION

A norm's action needs four operators, and the missing one is usually negation, so a norm that cannot accumulate a refusal can only accumulate. Constraint and redirection are a functional group as written, and an isomer claim only survives if both are written over the full shared habitat atom set; the falsifier is a required atom on one side that the other lacks. Their order is a union of three conditions, and the unmeasured link is the vocabulary link (can the group write a route and a refusal?), not the obvious perception or objective links. Catalyst and cycle are real but measurable properties, not answers. No new primitive is needed for any of this; if one is needed, the wall moves by import and the two effects are categorically different.
