# Expert verification packet: the Tether on institutions and norms

**What this is:** a claim in a form an institutional or complex-systems researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so the reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` structurally, `specified` in application, not `grounded`.** No measurement of a specific norm is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a case result.

**Status of this packet:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Institutions and informal norms are not a module inside the adapting agent. They are part of the habitat in contact with the per-slot residual `R`. They change adaptation by introducing contacts and violable conventions, never by subtracting contact. A healthy norm makes deviance statable without deciding the outcome; when it decides the outcome or cannot be checked, it stops being a convention and becomes an authored constant. The sign of a norm is relative to the residual, so the same rule can enable one adaptation and block another. Constraint and redirection are a union of conditions, not one lever. Agreement inside a norm-governed group is variation, never a verdict; the ground selects between rooms. Which norm, where, and with what sign is a declared gap.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 Framework-internal and habitat-facing: norms act on contact, by introduction only

- **Statement:** a norm changes adaptation only by changing what is in contact with `R`; the seat can introduce but not subtract. If the norm is removed from contact by habitat substitution and `R` changes identically, the claim fails.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39 *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"*; Fig 11 :40 *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*; Fig 11 :61 *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."*
- **What to check:** is the norm's effect actually a contact change, or does the evidence support a direct change to the agents' internal rules with contact held fixed? Is there an operative subtraction?
- **Refuter:** identical change in `R` after substitution removes the norm from contact; or a norm whose operative cause is subtracting an actor or condition from contact with no introduction involved.

### C2 Convention-facing: a norm must be violable and its sign is residual-relative

- **Statement:** a norm that decides outcomes and cannot be violated is no longer a convention; and no norm has an invariant good or bad sign across residuals.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :47 *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*; Fig 10 :19 *"install what can be violated"*; Fig 11 :51 *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*
- **What to check:** can a case be shown where a norm decides which adaptation is allowed yet still functions as a convention? Does one norm keep the same sign across two residuals with different grounds?
- **Refuter:** a non-violable, outcome-deciding norm that passes the convention test; or invariant norm valence across two different residuals. Either breaks C2.
- **Corroboration only:** the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry *Social Norms* (Bicchieri, Muldoon, Sontuoso, 2011/2023) gives the grammar-of-interaction shape and notes norms *"so rigid as to prevent the fine-tuning that would be necessary to accommodate new cases."* This is a world source, secondary, and not used to decide C2.

### C3 Verification-facing: agreement is not adaptation; the ground selects

- **Statement:** a norm-governed group can agree perfectly and still not adapt. Progress requires bracket fidelity and ground pressure; zero either and the chain runs in place. An institution with standing cannot be its own ground.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 7 :35 *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*; Fig 7 :37 *"Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:"*; Fig 10 :61 *"standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on"*
- **What to check:** is the reported success an outcome of a ground measurement or of agreement among governed agents? Is the institution whose standing is cited also the instrument that settles the question?
- **Refuter:** progress continuing with bracket fidelity or ground pressure at zero; or a population updating only on itself, with no anchor, tracking the ground better over time.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a rank or classification of institutions, and not a named winning norm.
- Not that norms are agent-internal, and not that constraint is subtraction.
- Not that institutional isomorphism equals coherent drift. They share a shape, not a measure; the identity claim was retracted in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §6.
- Not that a specific norm is good, and not that the framework can read a norm off a reputation.
- Not a single lever that clears the union of constraint and redirection.
- Not grounded. No measurement at any stratum was taken by this agent.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not another argument. Fig 1 :28: *"The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."*

1. **Contact substitution:** does `R` change identically when the norm is no longer in contact?
2. **Inversion test:** can a norm change `R` by subtracting contact, with no introduction operative?
3. **Violability and cross-residual sign tests:** does a non-violable outcome-deciding norm pass the convention test, and does one norm keep an invariant sign across two residuals?
4. **Two-factor measurement:** bracket fidelity and ground pressure, per slot. Does progress survive either at zero?
5. **Narrowing:** enumerate the habitat outward until the cascade stops mattering (Fig 11 :44), then run INWARD and OUTWARD as separate tracks. Result: a kind of thing and a route, never a name.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :39-40 and :61, Fig 10 :47, and Fig 7 :35. Five short passages.
2. Check C1 against one case you know: was the norm's effect a contact change by introduction, or an internal change to agents with contact fixed?
3. Check C2: does the norm stay violable, and does its sign change when the residual changes?
4. Name one instrument that would distinguish contact change from agent-internal change in that case, or say why none is available.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
