**Restated as an effect.** Networked agents each run a per-slot adaptation loop (Fig 1 :19, `R` indexed by object slot). Institutions and informal norms are candidate modifiers of that loop. Two readings are possible. Reading A: institutions and norms act on the agents as an internal add-on, so the effect is a change in the agents' own rules. Reading B: institutions and norms are part of the habitat in contact with the residual, so the effect is a change in contact. What each leads to: A predicts that a norm can be understood by inspecting the agent alone and that removing the norm is a subtraction; B predicts that a norm can only be understood by enumerating the habitat and that the seat can introduce but not subtract (Fig 11 :43, :61-62). The framework decides for B. The bound term is "constraint": it is bound as a change of contact, not as a removal of contact.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons in the figures' own words:

1. **A room does not upgrade the label.** *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35)
2. **Selecting for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation.** *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A list ranked by likely agreement is a list selected to agree.
3. **Asking for trust before checking spends the reader's independence.** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42)

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR/CAN-SPAM-style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in institutional channels avoid that practical failure as well as the structural one.
