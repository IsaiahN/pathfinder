# The Tether's answer on institutions, norms, and adaptation

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the application to institutions and informal norms, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## 1. The residual it answers

Networked agents run a per-slot adaptation loop with residual `R` indexed by object slot. Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** The question asks how institutions and informal norms change that loop. The answer must name the structure of the change, not a good or bad norm. The candidate forms a union of conditions (contact change, violable convention, ground selection), not one hard problem (Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**).

## 2. The shape, one loop per domain

1. **Institutions and norms are habitat, not agent internals.** Fig 11 :39: **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."** Fig 11 :40: **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."** They change what is in contact with `R`; they are enumerated, not composed (Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**). Mode `general`, triangulated with Fig 4 :19-20.
2. **They work by introduction, not subtraction.** Fig 11 :61: **"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."** A norm constrains by adding a cost, a convention, or an actor whose cascade changes what pays. Contact is a fact about the world and does not consult the seat. A managed habitat is permanent work (Fig 11 :63). Mode `general`, triangulated with the SEP rigidity and modification findings as corroboration only.
3. **A healthy informal norm is a violable convention.** Fig 10 :47: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."** It makes deviance statable without deciding the outcome (Fig 10 :19: **"install what can be violated"**). When it settles which adaptation is allowed, or cannot be checked, it becomes an authored constant carrying the seat's authority (Fig 10 :48). Mode `specified` for informal norms; `general` for the convention rule.
4. **The sign of a norm is relative to the residual.** Fig 11 :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."** The same institution enables one adaptation and blocks another. Mode `general` for the rule; the specific sign is a `gap` (below).
5. **Agreement is not adaptation.** Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."** Progress needs both bracket fidelity and ground pressure; zero either and the chain runs in place (Fig 7 :37-39). Norms that make agents update only on one another produce coherent drift (Fig 2 :13-15), which is invisible from inside. Mode `general`, triangulated with Fig 2 and Fig 7.
6. **Norms bear on closure.** More search inside one closure cannot add the atom needed to close `R` (Fig 8 :32: **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."**). A norm that opens contact to a differing closure can move the wall by import; a norm that closes it freezes the reach. Mode `general`, triangulated with Fig 6 and Fig 8.
7. **Standing is a disqualifying interest at the ground.** Fig 10 :61: **"standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on"**; Fig 10 :93: **"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."** An institution cannot both govern an adaptation and serve as its unmovable anchor without declaring the stake. Mode `specified`.
8. **Which norm, where, and with what sign: `gap`.** No figure line governs this after a genuine re-census. Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**; Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"**. The figure returns the conditions a norm must satisfy, not the norm.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any particular norm is good, efficient, or legitimate. Valence is relative to `R`.
- Not that norms are a module inside the agent or that constraint is subtraction. Both readings were refused in the census.
- Not that a norm can be read off an institution's reputation or that agreement among governed agents is evidence.
- Not that institutional isomorphism and coherent drift are the same thing. They share a shape; they are different variables, and the identity claim was retracted.
- Not that a single lever clears the union. Constraint and redirection are categorically different in the intersection.
- Not grounded. No measurement of a specific norm's effect on a specific `R` was taken.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (carried mapping): an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the questions are:

- **Contact substitution.** Does `R` change identically after the habitat is substituted so the norm is no longer in contact? If yes, the habitat claim fails.
- **Inversion test.** Can a norm be shown to change `R` by subtracting contact, with no introduction operative? If yes, the asymmetry claim fails.
- **Violability test.** Can a norm that decides outcomes and cannot be violated still satisfy the convention test? If yes, F3 fails.
- **Cross-residual sign test.** Does one norm keep an invariant sign across two residuals with different grounds? If yes, relative valence fails.
- **Two-factor test.** Measure per slot: bracket fidelity and ground pressure. Does progress continue with either at zero? If yes, F8 fails.
- **Narrowing, not naming.** Enumerate the habitat outward until the cascade stops mattering (Fig 11 :44) and run INWARD and OUTWARD as two separate tracks (Fig 6 :30-31). The result is a kind of thing and a route, never a name.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The figures' enumeration and asymmetry rules are *"rules this framework imposes on itself"* and *"fit cases already known and have not been tested forward, so they can still come back wrong"* (Fig 11 :71). The world sources corroborate the mechanism shape after re-mapping; they do not ground it, and the two framework essays share the figures' evidence pool. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not rank institutions or name the norm that wins. It says that institutions and informal norms are habitat, not a module inside the agent: they change the trajectory of a networked group's residual by introducing contacts and conventions, never by subtracting contact, and a convention must stay violable or it stops being a convention and becomes an authored constant. Their sign is relative to the residual, so the same rule that enables one adaptation blocks another, and there is no standing list of good norms. Constraint and redirection are a union of conditions rather than one lever, agreement inside the governed group is variation rather than a verdict, and an institution with a reputation to protect cannot serve as the ground that settles the adaptation. What is offered is that shape, five falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close the open slot. Which norm, in which case, and with what sign stays open until the ground settles it.
