**Subject:** A shape claim about norms and adaptation, with its refuter, for a two-minute check

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual.** Networked agents each run a per-slot adaptation loop, with a residual `R` measured per slot and never as a global average. The question is what institutions and informal norms do to that loop: do they change the agents themselves, or do they change what the agents are in contact with? Fig 1 :19 says *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 11 :40 says *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*
>
> **The shape claim (`general` structurally, `specified` in application, not `grounded`).** Institutions and norms are not a module inside the agent. They are part of the habitat in contact with the residual. Three structural consequences follow:
> 1. They work by **introduction, not subtraction**. Fig 11 :61: *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* A norm constrains by adding a cost, a convention, or an actor whose cascade changes what pays. Contact itself cannot be removed.
> 2. A healthy informal norm is a **convention that can be violated**. Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* A norm that settles which adaptation is allowed, or that cannot be checked, stops being a convention and becomes an authored constant.
> 3. The sign of a norm is **relative to the residual**, not carried by the norm. Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* The same rule enables one adaptation and blocks another.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming that any particular norm is good, that a norm can be read off an institution's reputation, or that agreement among agents shows adaptation happened. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Which norm, in which case, with what sign, is a declared gap I am not filling. I am also not claiming the framework is the only one or a final theory.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written before this message):**
> 1. The same norm changes the residual identically after the habitat in contact with it is substituted so that the norm is no longer in contact.
> 2. A norm changes the trajectory of the residual by subtracting an actor or condition from contact, with no introduction operative.
> 3. A norm that decides outcomes and cannot be violated still passes the convention test.
> 4. One norm keeps an invariant sign across two residuals with different grounds.
> 5. A population updating only on itself, with no anchor, tracks the ground better over time.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. Your verdict on this stage is recorded before I show you anything else. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>

> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
