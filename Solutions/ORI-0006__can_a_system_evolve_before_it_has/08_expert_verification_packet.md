# Expert verification packet: the Tether on evolution before genes

**What this is:** a claim in a form an origins-of-life researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, not `grounded`.** No measurement or historical dataset is claimed. This is a shape and a dependency order, not a result.

**Status of this packet:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Selection is unmaintained ground contact. It runs where nothing maintains anything, and its two factors are variation inside and selection between; a modern gene is not among them. A modern gene is a later hereditary record, the compressed record of a lineage that already met its ground, a generator that crosses to the next level and adds access for a frame rather than a new atom to the world. The dependency order therefore runs selection first and gene downstream. The question also bundles a definition dispute about the word "evolution", which is a gap, not a verdict. Repetition without variation and differential persistence is not evolution. What is offered is the shape, the dependency order, the operational discriminator and refuters written in advance. The first carrier is not named.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — process; checkable by reading Figures 11 and 7

- **Statement:** selection does not require a gene, a maintainer, or a selector. Its two factors are variation inside and differential persistence at the ground.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :36 — "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran." Fig 11 :56 — "Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept." Fig 7 :33 — "variation inside, selection between". Fig 7 :35 — "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
- **Refuter:** a demonstration that differential accumulation of adaptations requires template-dependent replication; or a repetition-only system that nonetheless accumulates adaptations; or a figure line listing a modern gene as a third factor of selection. Any one fires the C1 verdict.

### C2 — carrier and bond; checkable by reading Figures 10, 4, 13 and 12, plus the Operators table

- **Statement:** a modern gene is a later hereditary record composed over an already-running process. The bond to the earlier process is production (`⇒`), not identity (`≡`). It adds access for a frame, not a world primitive.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :68 — "a generator crosses, an instance does not, and the level it lands on remakes it." Fig 4 :34-35 — "A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes." Fig 13 :142 — "The seed was always the seedline." Fig 12 :104 — "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it." Fig 6 :25 — "An import adds no atom to the world." The Operators table defines `⇒` as "A produces B; B did not exist before" and `≡` as "two names, one referent".
- **Check:** does substituting a modern gene for an autocatalytic core lose the compartment-level selectability and the genotype-to-phenotype mapping? If it does, identity fails and production stands. If it does not, the bond claim is wrong.
- **Refuter:** a derivation making gene-level heredity a primitive on which selection depends; or a demonstration that a hereditary template adds a primitive to the world rather than access for a frame; or a settled record shown unable to become an atom for the next level.

### C3 — operational discriminator; checkable against a real chemical system

- **Statement:** a system evolves only if it has both variation inside and differential persistence at the ground. Catalyst survival and cycle closure are continuity and closure, not selection.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 7 :37 — "Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:". Fig 12 :119 — "catalyst. A part that survives its own use." Fig 12 :121 — "cycle. The output re-enters as input."
- **What to check:** is there a system in the literature with one factor only that nonetheless accumulates adaptations? Vasas et al. 2012 is the hardest available case: they show that autocatalytic sets *per se* are not evolvable, and that many viable cores plus compartmentalization plus core loss at division are required. That is a discriminator in favour of C3, not a demonstration that C3 is complete.
- **Refuter:** a one-factor system accumulating adaptations, or an assay showing the two factors are not the right cut.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a date, a setting, or a named first carrier. No RNA-first, metabolism-first, hydrothermal vent, or 3.8-billion-year claim.
- Not that all chemistry is alive, or that repetition counts as evolution.
- Not that genes are irrelevant. They are a major downstream arrival; only their status as a precondition is refused.
- Not that the Major Evolutionary Transitions account is wrong. Its objection that prebiotic chemistry lacks "evolution proper" is a word-level dispute, recorded as the Domain D gap, and left open.
- Not `grounded`. No experiment, measurement or decisive historical dataset was used.
- Not an isomer claim. The early carrier and the modern gene share a functional group, not (on the weak reading) an identical atom set. See `06` Q3 and its falsifier.

## 5. What would settle it

An experiment or a decisive historical dataset, not a definition and not agreement. Concretely:

1. a system with variation and differential persistence whose carrier lacks template replication, shown to accumulate adaptations in a way that survives an independent assay (closes the carrier slot);
2. a repetition-only system shown to accumulate adaptations (fires C1 and C3);
3. a historical dataset showing that every lineage that accumulated adaptations already had a modern gene (fires the whole claim).

Agreement among researchers is not such a dataset. The origins-of-life sources read here share one evidence pool, and the conflict inside it (the MET objection) is more informative than the agreement.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :36 and :56 and Fig 7 :33, :35, :37. Five short passages. Ask whether the two-factor account of selection anywhere mentions a gene or a maintainer.
2. Check C2 against the genotype-to-phenotype mapping in Vasas et al. 2012: does substituting a modern gene for a viable core lose anything? If yes, identity fails.
3. Name one assay that would distinguish variation-plus-differential-persistence from mere repetition in a pre-template system, or say why none is possible.
4. Say which side of the Domain D definition dispute you would adopt, and whether that changes anything about the process verdict. It should not.
5. Send the refutation, not an endorsement. A failure is more useful than an agreement.
