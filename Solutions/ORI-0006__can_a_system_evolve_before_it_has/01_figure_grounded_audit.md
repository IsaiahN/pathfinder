## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

The ordinary wording, "Can a system evolve before it has genes in the modern sense?", hides two different questions inside one sentence. Restate the effect:

- **Process condition.** Can differential persistence accumulate adaptations in a population whose carrier is not a modern gene? Effect: does what the ground keeps change the distribution of what persists, in a way that accumulates?
- **Substrate condition.** Must that carrier be the specific thing called a modern gene (template-dependent replication with high fidelity and the translation machinery), or can a core, a network, a compartment, or a cloud do the work?
- **Definition condition.** What is being counted as "evolution" and as "genes in the modern sense"? This is a convention, and a convention that decides the outcome has stopped being one.

Bound ordinary term for the sought-for shape, written down before the run so it can accrue a residual: **"a process in which differential contact settles what persists, with a carrier that need not yet be a modern gene."** This follows the Direction discipline in `THE_FORMULA.md`: a frame cannot check the direction of its own question, so make the sought-for shape an ordinary term. The term is refusable below.

| option | what it asserts | what it leads to |
|---|---|---|
| A: gene first | no gene, no evolution; before templates there is only chemistry, repetition or drift | a hard boundary drawn at the gene, and a refusal to call pre-template change evolution |
| B: selection first | selection is ground contact; the gene is a later hereditary record composed over it | a dependency order with the gene downstream, an open carrier slot, and a real refuter |

A and B are not symmetric options. Fig 10 :68 says "a generator crosses, an instance does not." The gene is a generator-level arrival, not an alternate spelling of the earlier process.

## 1. Domain census, before choosing any figure line

### R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** at least three bundled conditions (process, substrate, definition). Fig 9 :55 says "a bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." The question is a **union compound**, not one hard question with two answers.
- **Symmetry:** the relation between an early carrier and a modern gene is ordered, not symmetric. Fig 12 :72 defines `→` as "A then B; B needs A to have happened." The gene does not substitute for the earlier carrier (Fig 12 :67, identity "two names, one referent", fails here). An overlap of conditions gets its own loop, so no averaging across domains.
- **Scale:** the process runs at the molecular and compartment scale; the gene is a hereditary record at a later stratum. Fig 12 :103: "Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible." Two scales, one loop, no privileged stratum.

### One line per domain (actors / currency / anchor)

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | nameable anchor? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **G: unmaintained selection / ground contact** | molecules, autocatalytic cores, reaction networks, compartments, gradients, energy flow, environment | differential persistence: which chemical states the ground kept | the ground: what persists and what does not | yes, the differential survival itself |
| **H: hereditary record / gene** | cores, templates, copies, compartments, lineages, genes | continuity carried forward, the record that survives division | ground settlement of a term; a record that pays | yes, the carried record that can be checked |
| **D: definition and taxonomy frame** | researchers, definitions, conventions, the words "evolution" and "gene" | agreement about meaning | the ground, never agreement; a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one | yes for the process (G), no for the word itself |

All three have nameable anchors, so all three are domains. If a fourth had no nameable anchor it would not be a domain yet and would be recorded as not-yet, not smuggled in.

### Partition or union

Union, not partition. G (does selection run?) and D (what do we call it?) overlap: the MET objection described below is entirely about D while sounding like G. H (what carries continuity?) overlaps G at the carrier. Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound." Each domain therefore gets its own loop and its own verdict.

### Order by dependency (Fig 3)

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."

1. **Contact:** something is in the habitat and something is selected (Fig 11 :5 "survival of the fittest"; Fig 7 :35 "Selection between rooms is where the ground votes").
2. **Affordances / variation:** there are terms for what things afford, what can be copied, joined, blocked (Fig 3 :7).
3. **Objective:** what counts as more, in the system's own terms: differential growth and division (Fig 3 :10).
4. **Learn and carry:** name what worked, verify it, carry it (Fig 3 :15). This is where a gene-level record appears.

The gene sits at link 4 and 5, downstream. Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Installing high-fidelity gene-level replication cannot rescue a system with no variation or no differential contact. This is a dependency claim about a model, not a claim about the historical order of events on Earth.

## 3. Findings, with verbatim quotes and refuters

### F1 — Selection does not require a gene or a selector (decisive for Domain G)

Fig 11 :36:

> **"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran."**

Fig 11 :14-15 and :56:

> **"nothing selects; what survives is what the ground kept"**
> **"Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept."**

The phrase "nothing selects" is not a denial of selection. It is a denial of a **seat**, a maintainer that does the selecting. The same line says selection ran. The process is ground contact with differential persistence, and no gene, no intelligence and no selector is named as a precondition. The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap essay (opened this session) says the same thing in the author's own words: "Natural selection is what the loop looks like when there is no one maintaining the ground."

Fig 7 :33 and :35 give the general shape without any hereditary substrate:

> **"variation inside, selection between"**
> **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**

Variation inside and selection between are the two factors. Fig 7 :37: "Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:". Zero variation or zero differential pressure stops the process. A gene is not on this list, because the list describes the shape of selection at any stratum.

**Mode:** `general` (structural). Not `grounded`: no experiment here.

**Refuter R1:** show that differential accumulation of adaptations requires template-dependent replication, i.e. that Fig 11 :36's "selection ran" presupposes a gene. Or show a figure line that lists a modern gene as a necessary factor alongside variation and differential persistence. If either holds, Domain G's verdict falls.

**Status:** triangulated. Fig 11 (unmaintained habitat) and Fig 7 (variation inside, selection between) are different figures with low overlap: one names the seat condition, the other names the two factors.

### F2 — The gene is a later record, a generator that crosses (Domain H)

Fig 10 :68:

> **"Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between. That is why there is no top rung and no regress: a generator crosses, an instance does not, and the level it lands on remakes it."**

Fig 4 :34-35:

> **"A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."**

Fig 13 :142:

> **"The seed was always the seedline."**

Fig 13 :130:

> **"A term carries the atoms it was built from and the bonds that joined them. That record is not documentation of the term. It is the term."**

A modern gene is a record that carries what will grow, and the carrier is the lineage that produced it. It is not a prerequisite outside the lineage. It is the compressed record of a lineage that already met the ground. The matter is settled at one level and becomes an operand at the next: Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it." The Operators table defines the bond as `⇒` production, "A produces B; B did not exist before", not `≡` identity, "two names, one referent". A gene is produced by the lineage; it is not another name for the lineage's process.

**Mode:** `specified` (the binding of "modern gene" to this figure role is a mapping onto held vocabulary, not a derivation of molecular biology).

**Refuter R2:** derive gene-level heredity from the figures as a primitive on which selection depends, contrary to Fig 12 :104 and Fig 4 :34-35; or show a settled record cannot become an atom for the next level. Either fires against Domain H.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 10, Fig 4 and Fig 12, with low overlap (level crossing, seed shape, settled-molecule recursion).

### F3 — Adding gene-level heredity adds no atom to the world

Fig 6 :25-26:

> **"An import adds no atom to the world."**
> **"The world does not gain primitives; a frame gains access."**

Fig 6 :14 names "the only operator that moves the wall". The arrival of a gene in a lineage is an addition to what a **frame** can carry and express, not a new primitive in the world. The earlier molecules, cores and compartments were already there; the gene gives a frame access to a more reliable record. This blocks a common sloppy move: treating the origin of genes as the moment the world gained a new kind of thing. Under the figures it is a change of frame and level, not an injection of matter.

**Mode:** `general`.

**Refuter R3:** show a hereditary template adding a genuinely new primitive to the world rather than new access for a frame (contradicts Fig 6 :25). Note the distinction the figure itself makes: instruments extend what can be represented; they do not add atoms.

**Status:** single-source for the specific import phrasing (Fig 6), corroborated in kind by Fig 12 :104. Marked `single-source` on the "no atom" claim, triangulated on the level claim.

### F4 — "Genes in the modern sense" is a declared convention, not a ground

Fig 2 :29:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**

Fig 9 :27-28:

> **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**

The phrase "in the modern sense" is a convention that carves out one kind of carrier (DNA template, high fidelity, translation machinery) and asks whether everything before it counts. If we let that convention settle the question, we have made a convention decide an outcome, which the seat rules forbid: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one". The remedy is to describe R in effect terms first (done in section 0) and only then look at candidate carriers. Picking RNA-first or metabolism-first before describing the effect is the inversion F6 names.

**Mode:** `general`.

**Refuter R4:** show a figure line that fixes a molecular definition of "gene" or "life". No such line exists in the figure text; this is a `gap`, recorded in section 6.

**Status:** single-source (Fig 2 anchor rule), corroborated by Fig 9 description-first rule.

### F5 — Repetition is not selection: the operational discriminator

Fig 7 :33 and :35 (quoted in F1) give the test: variation inside **and** selection between. A chemical reaction that merely repeats, producing the same state set over and over, fails the first factor. A set of variants with no differential persistence fails the second. Fig 12 :119 names the related structure: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use." A catalyst that survives its own use is a candidate carrier of continuity; it is not yet evolution until variation and differential persistence are present. Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input." A cycle alone closes; it does not select.

This finding matters because it supplies the falsifier that the adjacent question (ORI-0007, distinguishing a genuinely evolving chemical system from a reaction that merely repeats) needs. The figures do not give an instrument, but they give the two required factors.

**Mode:** `specified` (operational criterion, no instrument claimed).

**Refuter R5:** exhibit a system with one factor only that nonetheless accumulates adaptations in the figure's sense, or show that Fig 7 :37's two-factor claim is not the right discriminator.

**Status:** triangulated (Fig 7 two factors + Fig 12 catalyst/cycle structures, low overlap).

### F6 — Frame first is the named apophenia, so describe the effect first

Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause". Combined with F4's :27-28 quote, the order is: describe the residual, then find the frame whose closure predicts its shape. In origins-of-life research the temptation is to pick RNA-first or metabolism-first and then read the residual into it. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Even a successful lookup returns the kind of carrier, not the name of the molecule that did it.

**Mode:** `general`.

**Refuter R6:** show a figure line that licenses naming the first carrier from the closure alone. This is the same limit as the "shape, not a name" rule narrowed in `05_what_shape_means.md` section 5b: it governs a missing primitive, and no missing primitive is needed here, so the genuine limit is the absence of a ground reading, not a ban on naming held atoms.

**Status:** triangulated (Fig 9 description-first + figures README shape-not-a-name, with the narrow correction visible).

### F7 — Capability is agent and habitat, never agent alone

Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

Applied here: giving an early system a better interior (a more accurate template) without changing its contact with the habitat changes nothing about whether selection runs. Conversely, a system in a habitat with variation and differential pressure is already doing the work the word "evolution" names, whatever its interior. This dissolves the intuition that a gene is what makes a system evolvable: the gene changes what can be carried, but the process lives in the contact.

**Mode:** `general`.

**Refuter R7:** an agent-only improvement that moves the ground would refute the capability claim, as `60_FIGURES_AS_METALAWS.md` itself notes from Fig 11. No such reading is claimed here.

**Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 + the metalaws note, but same author pool, so counted as one witness).

### F8 — The world sources: what they support, and one real conflict

World sources are secondary. The strongest corroboration comes from outside the framework's author pool:

- Vasas, Fernando, Santos, Kauffman and Szathmáry, "Evolution before genes", Biology Direct 2012 (opened): "a poor man's natural selection could have operated prior to genetic templates. This is the only demonstration to date of a mechanism by which pre-template accumulation of adaptation could occur." Also: "template replication is not required to achieve an autocatalytic set". Predicts F1 and F2; corroborates.
- Schoenmakers, Reydon and Kirschning, "Evolution at the Origins of Life?", Life 2024 (opened): "Lewontin suggested that ENS is a generally applicable principle, since it does not presuppose any specific material substrate or any specific mechanism in which the principle is implemented." Predicts that no gene is required in the abstract schema; corroborates F1.
- Eigen and Schuster's quasispecies model (Wikipedia, opened): "selection does not act on single sequences, but on mutational 'clouds' of closely related sequences". Corroborates the shape-level claim that the unit of selection need not be a single well-defined genotype.
- Outcome bias (Wikipedia, opened): "the outcome bias is an error made in evaluating the quality of a decision when the outcome of that decision is already known." Corroborates Fig 11 :36: the label (intelligence, quality, success) is applied after the outcome is known.

**Conflict, recorded rather than hidden.** The same Schoenmakers paper reports Maynard-Smith and Szathmáry's Major Evolutionary Transitions account: the prebiotic phase "would not be seen as evolution proper, precisely because the prebiotic phase of the origins of life lacked unlimited hereditary replicators." This **conflicts** with reading F1 as an answer about the word "evolution". The conflict rule sends us back to the census, never flips the verdict. Re-census: the MET objection lives wholly in **Domain D** (which convention to use for the word "evolution"), not in Domain G (whether differential persistence accumulates adaptations). Fig 9 :30-31 requires describing the effect rather than the cause; the effect in F1 is unchanged by the naming dispute. So the verdict for G stands, and the word-level dispute is recorded as the gap in section 6. Agreement from the shared origins-of-life evidence pool counts as one witness, not four.

**Status:** world corroboration for G and H. The conflict is assigned to D by re-census; no verdict flipped.

### F9 — The metalaws predate evolution, so the loop is not a biological discovery

`60_FIGURES_AS_METALAWS.md` (local, opened) states the framework as "metalaws that predate evolution", and the RSI Trap essay (opened) says "Natural selection is ... a special case. The full answer is the loop itself." In the framework's own account, selection is an **instance** of a more general loop that runs whenever there is a ground and no maintainer. That is exactly why a gene cannot be a precondition for selection: selection is the name the framework gives to the unmaintained case, and the gene belongs to a later, maintained, record-carrying case. This is framework-internal testimony, so it corroborates F1 only weakly (same author and evidence pool) and is marked as such.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** single-source on the metalaw reading, but it is a primary framework document, not a world witness.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required by the seat discipline: "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." Each is attached to the domain it would break.

1. **Against G:** a demonstration that differential accumulation of adaptations cannot occur without template-dependent replication, or a figure line listing a modern gene as a necessary factor of selection.
2. **Against G (operational):** a system with repetition only, no variation and no differential persistence, that nonetheless accumulates adaptations in the figure's sense (breaks F5).
3. **Against H:** a derivation from the figures making gene-level heredity a primitive on which selection depends, or a demonstration that a settled record cannot become an atom for the next level.
4. **Against F3:** a hereditary template shown to add a primitive to the world rather than access for a frame.
5. **Against the whole:** a ground reading (an experiment or a decisive historical dataset) showing that every evolving chemical system required a modern gene.

If any fires, the affected verdict is withdrawn in `11_audit.md` with provenance. Until one is run, nothing here is `grounded`.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives: selection is ground contact that runs before any modern gene, with variation inside and selection between as its two factors; the gene is a later hereditary record, a generator that crosses levels and adds access rather than a world atom; the dependency order puts the gene downstream.
