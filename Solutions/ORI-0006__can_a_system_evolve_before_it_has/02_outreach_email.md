**Subject:** One checkable claim about evolution before genes, and what would refute it

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** Does differential persistence accumulate adaptations in a population whose carrier is not a modern gene? This bundles three things: whether selection runs without a gene, what carries continuity, and what we choose to call evolution. I keep those verdicts separate.
>
> **The output (`general` on structure, `specified` per domain, not `grounded`).** Selection is unmaintained ground contact. It runs where nothing maintains anything, with two factors: variation inside, and selection between. A gene is not on that list. A modern gene is a later hereditary record, a generator that crosses levels and adds access to a frame, not a new primitive in the world. So the dependency order is selection first, gene downstream. The carrier that actually did the work first stays open. I am not claiming to know whether it was RNA, a network, a core, or a compartment.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** Not that chemistry is already life by convention. Not that repetition counts as evolution: a reaction that merely repeats has neither factor. Not a date, not a named first molecule, and no claim that any measurement was taken. The word "in the modern sense" is a convention, and I do not let it settle the question.
>
> **What would refute it, written down before I sent this:**
> 1. a demonstration that differential accumulation of adaptations requires template-dependent replication;
> 2. a system with repetition only, no variation and no differential persistence, that nonetheless accumulates adaptations;
> 3. a derivation making gene-level heredity a primitive on which selection depends;
> 4. a hereditary template shown to add a primitive to the world rather than access for a frame;
> 5. a ground reading showing that every evolving chemical system required a modern gene.
>
> If any one holds, the affected verdict falls and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would like from you:** what would you count as a failure of the shape claim? Please answer that before reading any of the material below, and record your verdict first.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Canonical figure text with line numbers (local extract used for every citation): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures and formula for direct download: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - "Evolution before genes", Vasas et al., Biology Direct 2012: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3284417
> - "Evolution at the Origins of Life?", Schoenmakers et al., Life 2024: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10890241
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so outputs can be contrived to fit. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [name]
