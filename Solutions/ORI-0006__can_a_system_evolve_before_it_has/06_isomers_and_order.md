# Dependency order, the bond type of a gene, and the repetition/selection split

**Mode: `specified`.** Supporting derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The main audit leaves three structural questions open: (1) in what order do selection, variation and a hereditary record depend on one another; (2) what **bond** connects an early carrier to a modern gene, identity or production; (3) are the two carriers isomers under Fig 12, or something else? A fourth question is operational: what distinguishes a system that evolves from a reaction that merely repeats? This file answers those four, with a falsifier written before the run.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Dependency order: is the gene the floor or a later link?

- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
- The chain in this question reads: contact (something is in the habitat and something is selected) → affordances (what can be copied, joined, blocked) → objective (what counts as more, in the system's own terms) → learn and carry (name what worked, verify it, carry it). Fig 3 :5/:7/:10/:15 supply those four links.
- A modern gene is the **carry** link. It is not link 1. Installing high-fidelity replication at link 4 cannot rescue a system with zero variation at link 2 or zero differential pressure at link 3.
- Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it." The record is composed over already-settled units.
- **Conclusion:** gene downstream. `general` on the dependency claim; no historical date asserted.

### Q2 — What bond connects an early carrier and a modern gene?

- Fig 12 :67 defines identity `≡` as "two names, one referent", with the test "substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No." Substituting a modern gene for an autocatalytic core loses the mapping to a phenotype and the compartment-level selectability described by Vasas et al. 2012. So identity fails.
- The Operators table defines `⇒` production: "A produces B; B did not exist before", and distinguishes it from sequence: "the second operand is not waiting, it is absent until the first fires."
- A hereditary record is produced by a lineage that already ran; it was absent before that lineage settled. Fig 13 :142: "The seed was always the seedline."
- **Conclusion:** the bond is `⇒` production, not `≡` identity. It is also not `+` conjunction: a gene and the process are not two independently present ingredients that merely co-occur. This matters because the question's "before" is a production relation in time, not a symmetric comparison of two alternative designs.

### Q3 — Are the early carrier and the modern gene isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Under the **weak** reading (early core versus modern gene as two members of "hereditary carrier"), the two do not share the same atom set: a modern gene brings template fidelity and translation machinery that the core lacks. By Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**. They are two members of a **functional group** whose presence fixes the outcome, "the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Under the **strong** reading (both written over the full shared atom set, differing only in which structure mediates which state pair), an isomer claim becomes stateable. This is the analogue of the exemplar's GR/QM move. But it requires an instrument to decide whether the gene-level record is genuinely the same atoms differently bonded, not merely a later stratum with more atoms. No such instrument is supplied here.
- **Falsifier F, written before the run:** if either carrier can be written without an atom the other requires (for example, a gene-level heritable record with no compartment-level selectability, or a core that already has template fidelity), the strong isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. This is the same test the exemplar used for its own isomer claim, adapted to this residual.
- **Conclusion:** the defensible structural claim is the functional-group one. The isomer claim is a checkable stronger form, not a result.

### Q4 — What separates evolution from mere repetition?

- Fig 7 :33: "variation inside, selection between". Fig 7 :37: "Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:".
- Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use." Survival of a catalyst across uses is continuity, not selection.
- Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input." A cycle closes; a chain cannot close by construction (Fig 12 :121). Closure is not evolution.
- Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." The vote is the discriminator. Where there is no between-unit differential, there is repetition, however much the interior churns.
- **Conclusion:** the operational criterion is two-factor: variation inside **and** differential persistence at the ground. Neither alone is enough. This is `specified`, because no instrument is named; the figures give the factors, not the assay.

### Q5 — Does "before" introduce a strand?

Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." If the question is read as "which came first, gene or evolution", the two are not competing frames to be negotiated. They are different links in a dependency chain (Q1). The strand is dissolved by re-census: "before" is production order, not a contest between two explanations. Fig 9 :44: "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room." A search for a single first cause in the historical record is not sealed, so a failure to find one would not prove absence (Fig 8 :39: "a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence.").

## CONCLUSION

The gene is downstream, connected to the running process by production (`⇒`), not identity (`≡`). The early carrier and the modern gene are not isomers under Fig 12's definition on the weak reading; they share a functional group, and the stronger isomer claim needs an instrument. Evolution is separated from repetition by two factors, variation inside and selection between; catalyst survival and cycle closure are continuity, not selection. The historical "before" is a production order in a model, not a dated event.

This preserves what is defensible without naming a first carrier or a date. A falsifier (F above) is written before the run and is attached to the only structural claim that could be strengthened.
