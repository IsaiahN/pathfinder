# The Tether's answer: can biological information be defined without evolution, function, or survival?

**Mode: `specified` on the three slot verdicts, `general` on the shape. Not `grounded`.** This is a structural result about reachability and composition, not a measurement and not a decision among rival philosophical theories. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Two frames use "biological information" for different things. The statistical frame wants a measurable quantity over symbols and probabilities. The semantic frame wants aboutness, asymmetry, and accuracy conditions. The question "can it be defined without evolution, function, or survival?" bundles those two wants with three different candidate references. Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." So the answer is given per slot, never averaged.

## 2. The three verdicts, kept separate

### Slot 1, correlational or syntactic information: YES, definable without any of the three

A measure over a sequence or signal (Shannon entropy, mutual information, program length) is composed entirely inside the statistical frame's closure. Its atoms are symbols, probabilities, channels, and code lengths. Function, selection history, and survival are not needed to state it and do not appear in its definition.

Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." The statistical quantity is a legal molecule of that closure. **Mode: `specified`, triangulated** (Fig 6 composition rule plus Fig 13's closed-set rule; world confirmation from Adriaans 2023 and Artiga 2024, different author pool).

Caveat, not a retraction: this is the correlational sense. Artiga (2024) notes that the string `0000011111` and `1001011100` carry the same Shannon information, so the quantity is "a rather weak concept" for biological purposes. The verdict is about definability, not about explanatory adequacy.

### Slot 2, semantic information: NO, not from the statistical closure alone

The properties that make a signal count as information *for* a biological system are asymmetry, normativity, and non-ubiquity: an alarm call is about a snake, the snake is not about the call; a signal can be accurate or inaccurate; not every physical correlation is a message. Those properties are not atoms of the statistical frame. Since mint composes and never adds an atom, they cannot be derived from entropy or mutual information by composition.

Fig 6 :14: "the only operator that moves the wall." Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." The function atom must be imported from the biological frame, and the import needs a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 8 :51). **Mode: `specified`, triangulated** (Fig 6 plus Fig 12 :5; world confirmation from Artiga 2024 §1.1 and §1.2).

**What the framework does not do here:** it does not name a winning theory of semantic information. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." It reports the shape of what would close the slot, namely an atom supplying asymmetry, normativity, and non-ubiquity over a correlational base.

### Slot 3, evolution, function, and survival: NOT ONE CONDITION, split by dependency

The three references in the question are not interchangeable.

- A definition can appeal to a **function** without appealing to evolutionary history. Allen (2026, SEP) reports organizational and causal-role accounts that "make no appeal to the selection history of the trait."
- An **etiological** function does appeal to selection history. Schulte and Neander (2022, SEP): teleological theories "usually understand these functions to be what the items with the functions were selected for, either by phylogenetic natural selection or by some other similar process." On that reading, evolution enters the definition.
- **Survival** or fitness is one currency inside the etiological frame, not a synonym for function and not a synonym for evolution. It is a separate slot because forcing it into either of the others is the strain the re-census refused (`01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §5).

Dependency order (Fig 3 :19): "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Settle S1, then S2, then S3. Reversing the order lets a preferred theory of function substitute for the residual.

**Mode: `specified` on the split, `general` on the claim that the three are distinct; single-source for the figure dependency rule, with world corroboration from Allen (2026) and Schulte and Neander (2022).**

### What the answer is not

It is not "no, biological information requires evolution." It is not "yes, Shannon entropy is all there is." It is the per-slot result: yes for correlation, no for semantics without import, and a split for the three references. Averaging these into one word is the failure Fig 1 :22 names: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that correlational information is useless in biology. It is measurable and often explanatory.
- Not that semantic information is unreal or that function is dispensable. The claim is frame-relative reachability, and Fig 6 :27 warns that depth and genuine absence look the same from inside a frame.
- Not a decision between etiological, organizational, causal-role, or hybrid accounts of function. The figures return a shape, not a name.
- Not `grounded`. No discriminating case has fired in this simulated run.
- Not a claim that any particular philosopher's definition is wrong because it is unpopular or expensive. Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A discriminator, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." Candidates:

1. A signal with the right statistical profile and no biological function, where a definition must say whether information is present.
2. A trait with a function but no informational role, for example a heart pumping blood (Artiga 2024).
3. The Swampman case: a molecule-by-molecule duplicate with no selection history, which teleological theories say carries no information (Artiga 2024).
4. A functional-indeterminacy case (Dretske's magnetosomes, from Schulte and Neander 2022), where the same physical state can be described as indicating the distal or the proximal feature.

Standing falsifiers from `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §3: derive normativity from correlation with no added atom; collapse the two senses into one concept; collapse the etiological and organizational function accounts; admit normativity as a substrate primitive; or show that no discriminating case could ever separate the definitions.

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The answer stops at the limit of the discriminating cases available here. ## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The question bundles at least three questions, so the Tether answers it slot by slot. A correlational measure over sequences and signals is definable with no appeal to evolution, function, or survival; that definition lives entirely inside a statistical closure. A semantic notion, one that is asymmetric, normative, and about something, is not reachable from correlation alone, because mint composes inside a closure and never adds an atom; it needs a function atom imported from the biological frame, with its provenance recorded. Once a function is required, "evolution", "function", and "survival" still are not one thing: organizational accounts of function make no appeal to selection history, etiological accounts do, and survival is a currency inside one of them. The framework returns this shape, not a name for the winning theory. It is a `specified` structural result, not a measurement; a discriminating case, not a vote among definitions, would settle it.
