## 0. The question as a residual in effect terms

The researcher holds a simple model M that already predicts many observations, and a complex candidate theory T is offered. The residual R is not "which theory do we like". It is the per-slot gap between what the current library predicts and what the ground returns: R_s = |Gamma(b,a) - o'| for each observation slot s. The decision owed is one of five routes: TRANSFERRED (nothing owed), NOVEL (extend perception), BROKEN with rebinding (refit M), BROKEN with mechanism failure (a new term is owed), or REFUTED (a held term of M is false, so a refusal is owed). These are from `THE_FORMULA.md` step 2 and Fig 5 :10-14, :50-56.

Stating the sought-for shape as an ordinary term before the run: an answer should (i) keep the verdicts for description economy, live-slot gap and provenance separate, (ii) name what would refute each, and (iii) point to a discriminating ground rather than to agreement. This is the "Direction" discipline of `THE_FORMULA.md`, and Fig 5 :65-66: *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."*

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** the comparison has three roles, not two: M, T, and the ground that settles between them. Treating it as a two-item ranking is already a category error. The bond between M and T is comparison (⋛), not symmetric conjunction.
- **Symmetry:** ordered, not symmetric. Operators table :48-51 defines ⋛ as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* with the test *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."* Swapping M and T changes the standing of the operands: M is held and settled, T is a candidate. The quantity compared is also directional: cost(T) plus leftover versus cost(M).
- **Scale:** first order inside each theory (a model predicts observations against a ground), second order at the comparison. Fig 12 :103-104: *"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."* and *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."* Complexity is therefore level-relative; fix the level before comparing.

### One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

**D1, description economy (statistics, information theory, model selection).**
Actors: a hypothesis set, the two candidates, a code, the data. Currency: bits, that is cost(H) plus cost(data given H). Anchor: the data under a fixed declared code, with settlement by lossless compression. This is a partition question, does admitting T pay under the same code. Fig 5 :45 names this exact domain: minimum description length.

**D2, ground and settlement (experiment, instrument, observation).**
Actors: instruments, observation slots, the ground. Currency: per-slot residual, not aggregate fit. Anchor: *"whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks"* (Fig 2 :29-30). This is where a discriminating measurement must live. Without a nameable anchor here, a claim is not yet in this domain.

**D3, frame-internal bookkeeping (the community's own scores).**
Actors: coverage counts, fit statistics, credibility, prestige, elegance, number of predicates. Currency: self-produced quantities. Anchor: none that does not move with the frames, so this is not yet a verdict-bearing domain. It is a filter for the budget only. Fig 1 :24-27 disqualifies it.

**D4, provenance and lineage (where T came from).**
Actors: the candidate term, its derivation record, the frame it was imported from. Currency: provenance and shadow test. Anchor: the record of where each came from, which does not move with the contents. Fig 8 :50-52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*.

**D5, verification and outreach (outside checkers).**
Actors: expert readers in different closures. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all seats draw on one evidence pool, so independence here is zero and any agreement is internal consistency, not corroboration.

### Partition or union

This is a **union compound**, not one hard question. Fig 9 :4-5: *"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* Description economy, live-slot gap and provenance can come apart. T can be more economical and still fail on a live slot; T can fit perfectly and be post-hoc on the very data it fits. Fig 9 :54-59 requires unbundling a union before searching again: a single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. Verdicts stay separate and are never averaged.

### Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. Ask whether there is a fact here and split the union (Fig 9 :4-5, :54-59).
2. Describe R in effect terms before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).
3. Route the gap: refuse before mint (formula step 2; Fig 5 :50-56).
4. Apply the three guards as a product: support, reachability, novelty (Fig 5 :19-41).
5. Apply the MDL bargain in D1 (Fig 5 :42-45).
6. Seek a discriminating ground in D2 (Fig 6 :32-35).
7. Record provenance in D4, then offer the result to D5 without pooling agreement (Fig 8 :50-52; Fig 2 :40).

Effort spent above a broken link cannot rescue it (Fig 3 :19-21). Applying Occam's razor before routing the residual is effort spent above the break.

## 3. Findings (verbatim quotes before conclusions)

### F1 "Fits many observations" is a frame-internal aggregate and is disqualified as a verdict

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
Fig 1 :23: *"The ground enters as the only metric."*
Fig 1 :24-27: *"coverage, and compression achieved"*, *"predicates minted, and credibility accrued"*, *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*

**Prediction (`general`):** a theory comparison that uses "number of observations fitted" or "compression achieved" as its verdict is using a quantity the frame produces to score the frame. The correct move is per-slot residual against an external ground. This also means a global near-zero residual with one live slot is a legal state (Fig 1 :13), which is exactly the situation a complex theory may address.

**Refuter:** show a figure-sanctioned rule in which a coverage count, with no anchor outside the two frames, decides the comparison.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 1 and Fig 2 (low overlap: per-slot bookkeeping versus anchor definition).

### F2 The only figure-licensed selection rule is the MDL bargain, and it is directional

Fig 5 :42: *"The acceptance test is a bargain, not a threshold."*
Fig 5 :43-44: *"A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before. A term that explains everything by saying nothing fails this, and so does a term that explains one occasion perfectly."*
Fig 5 :45: *"This is minimum description length, Rissanen 1978, which is itself a composition of Kolmogorov complexity and Shannon coding put to work on model selection."*
`THE_FORMULA.md` step 3: accept iff cost(phi) + left(R,phi) < cost(R).

**Prediction (`specified`):** admit T only if cost(T) + left(R,T) < cost(M) under one declared code. The phrase "explains one occasion perfectly" is the figure's own name for a complex theory that fits the already-known data and buys nothing.

**Refuter:** a case admitted by the framework in which cost(T) + left(R,T) exceeds cost(M) under the same code.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 5, the formula, and the world MDL source (different fields, same inequality).

### F3 Complexity must be priced in two currencies that do not add

Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*
Fig 12 :126: *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*
Fig 12 :129: *"Neither figure predicts the other, so both are read or the choice is made blind."*
Fig 12 :130: *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."*

**Prediction (`specified`):** report both description length and the actions needed to test T. A long chain of cheap parts can be expensive to describe and cheap to refute; a short arrangement over a costly operand is cheap to state and may take a whole budget to settle (Fig 12 :128-129). Do not collapse them into one score.

**Refuter:** a figure-derived rule that returns a single scalar verdict by summing bits and actions.

**Status:** single-source for the figure rule (Fig 12), corroborated weakly by world statistics sources that keep fit and parameter penalty as separate terms then combine them by a stated criterion, which is not the same as a framework verdict.

### F4 The comparison is a union compound; split it and keep verdicts separate

Fig 9 :4-5: *"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*
Fig 9 :54-55: *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*
Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

**Prediction (`general`):** the answer is a short procedure over separate domains, not an averaged score. Simplicity, fit and provenance are filters for the budget when they are frame-internal, and witnesses only when tied to an anchor.

**Refuter:** a figure-derived procedure that averages description economy, live-slot gap and provenance into one verdict.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 9 and Fig 1 (different mechanisms: union splitting versus slot indexing).

### F5 Route the gap before comparing: REFUTED owes a refusal, not a bigger theory

Fig 5 :50-51: *"The four kinds sort a gap by what is missing. None of them sorts a gap by what is wrong."*
Fig 5 :53: *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"*
`THE_FORMULA.md` step 2: *"REFUTED a term held is false; what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation"*; and the fifth sort is reached by no arrangement of the other four.

**Prediction (`specified`):** before asking whether T is worth its cost, ask what kind of gap is open. If M's held regularity is false, a complex replacement that merely re-describes the gap without refusing that term is out of order. A vocabulary without negation can only accumulate (Operators table :57-58; Fig 12 amendment :139-140).

**Refuter:** a case in the figures where a held term known to be false is answered by minting a larger explanation with no refusal, and the result is admitted.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 5 and the formula (the fifth kind is named in both), with the negation operator from the Operators table as corroboration.

### F6 The round-trip gap is what the complex theory must buy

Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*
Fig 4 :31-32: *"The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold, and it is the only honest report of how much was given up."*

**Prediction (`specified`):** treat the simple model as a coarse description of the ground. Abstraction throws away detail; concretisation puts it back. The gap R_T is precisely what M cannot hold. If R_T is zero on every live slot, T buys nothing. If R_T is large and concentrated on one live slot, and T pays for it under F2, T is owed. R_T is not a number computed here, because no measurement was taken; it is the qualitative set of dropped constraints.

**Refuter:** a complex theory admitted on these figures with an empty or zero round-trip gap on all live slots.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 4 and Fig 1 (round trip versus per-slot residual).

### F7 The ground, not agreement or elegance, settles the comparison

Fig 2 :29-30: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."*
Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*
Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*
Fig 9 :51: *"what the verifier may see: evidence bearing on the residual is admissible; evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel, and the verifier is denied it"*

**Prediction (`general`):** the discriminator must be an instrument or measurement that does not move when researchers change their minds. Elegance, author reputation and panel agreement are excluded from verdict weight; independence between checkers is bought, not assumed.

**Refuter:** a comparison settled by consensus with no anchor outside the agreeing frames.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 2 and Fig 9 (anchor/triangulation versus admissible evidence).

### F8 A discriminating instrument must already be returning something

Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."*
Fig 6 :33: *"Nobody specified a telescope from an account of Mars. A wandering point in the sky was already a reading, and every sharper instrument was built on the residual the last one produced."*
Fig 6 :34-35: *"The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve."*

**Prediction (`specified`):** do not answer "a crucial experiment could in principle distinguish them" and stop. Name a current reading that fails to resolve. If nothing at any available resolution is returning a failing reading, the two are indiscernible at this budget; record `unreachable-at-this-budget`, not a verdict. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

**Refuter:** a figure-licensed procedure that designs a discriminator from a pure description of the difference with no prior failing reading.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 6 and Fig 8 (instrument construction versus search that finds nothing).

### F9 Provenance separates convergent derivation from post-hoc fit

Fig 8 :50-52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."*
Fig 8 :53: *"Residual first, frame second."*
Fig 10 :65: *"Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right: the ground does that, and a wrong ruling stays wrong however clean its record."*

**Prediction (`specified`):** a theory constructed after seeing the data cannot count those same data as independent corroboration; only the derivation record separates that from a theory that predicted the reading first. Conversely, a clean provenance does not make a theory right. Both halves must be stated.

**Refuter:** a figure-derived rule in which content alone, with no record, settles whether a fit was predicted or adopted.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 8 and Fig 10 (surplus/provenance versus the seat's record rule).

### F10 Pre-register the sought shape; a frame cannot check its own preference for simplicity

Fig 5 :62-63: *"A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term. It is not bound, it predicts nothing, and it accrues no residual, so nothing here can see it."*
Fig 5 :65-66: *"The remedy is a discipline rather than a branch: make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."*
Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*

**Prediction (`general`):** write down, before examining the fit, what would count as "T is better" and what would refute that claim. Otherwise the preference for either simplicity or novelty is invisible to every check the pipeline can run, and any complex theory will seem to fit because the yardstick was chosen after the fact.

**Refuter:** a frame that certifies its own simplicity preference using only the checks in Fig 5 and the formula.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 5 and Fig 9 (direction limit versus describe-before-looking).

### F11 Complexity is level-relative

Fig 12 :103: *"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."*
Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."*
Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

**Prediction (`specified`):** fix the level at which the comparison is made. A theory that is complex at the level of observation slots may be a single settled atom at the next level, where it composes with other settled theories. Two candidates with the same ingredients under different bonds are isomers (Fig 12 :117), so equal atom counts do not mean equal theories.

**Refuter:** a figure-derived verdict on complexity that does not state the level at which irreducibility is being measured.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 12 and the formula (recursive composition and settled-molecule rule).

### F12 Search versus import, and the honest stop

Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."*
Fig 8 :40-43: *"Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions."*
Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*
Fig 13 :115: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."*

**Prediction (`general`):** if T's extra primitive is not in closure(Gamma), the remedy is an import with a source gate and a shadow test; importing adds an atom and moves the wall (Fig 6 :14, :21-23). If it is already in the closure and merely unfound, the remedy is more independent search, not a new atom. If no figure line governs after a genuine re-census, say `gap` plainly.

**Refuter:** a case where importing against a budget limit is the figure-sanctioned remedy for an unfound but reachable term.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 8, Fig 13 and Fig 6 (barrier, closure, import price).

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required by the framework's own discipline: Fig 5 :65-66 binds the sought shape as an ordinary term, and the canonical phrase is *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* The shape claim in this folder is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A figure-derived rule uses a frame-produced count (coverage, compression achieved, credibility) as the verdict without an anchor outside the frames (contradicts F1, Fig 1 :24-27).
2. A candidate with cost(T) plus leftover greater than cost(M), under the same declared code, is admitted by the rule (contradicts F2, Fig 5 :43).
3. Bits and actions are summed into one scalar verdict (contradicts F3, Fig 12 :130).
4. The three sub-verdicts are averaged into one score (contradicts F4, Fig 9 :60).
5. A held term known to be false is answered by a larger explanation without a refusal (contradicts F5, formula step 2).
6. A complex theory is admitted with an empty round-trip gap on all live slots (contradicts F6, Fig 4 :30).
7. Agreement among checkers selected for receptiveness is pooled as corroboration (contradicts F7, Fig 2 :40).
8. A discriminator is specified purely from a description of the difference, with no already-failing reading (contradicts F8, Fig 6 :32).

A run-level refuter, written before any real data are examined: if a discriminating measurement at the available resolution returns identical per-slot residuals for M and T on every live slot, the claim that T buys a round-trip gap is withdrawn for that budget. A search that finds nothing is not proof of absence (Fig 8 :39), so this withdraws the claim for that budget, not for all budgets.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Split the question into three domains before scoring anything: description economy (D1), live-slot ground gap (D2), provenance (D4). Keep verdicts separate (F4).
2. Route the residual. If a held term of M is false, owe a refusal first (F5).
3. In D1, apply the MDL bargain under one declared code: admit T only when cost(T) + left(R,T) < cost(M) (F2).
4. In the same report, price the actions needed to test T in a separate column. Do not add the two prices (F3).
5. In D2, measure the round-trip gap per slot, not aggregate fit (F6, F1). Name a current reading that fails to resolve; if none exists, report `unreachable-at-this-budget` (F8, F12).
6. In D4, record whether T was derived before or adopted after the data. Do not let a clean record stand in for a ground verdict (F9).
7. Write the refuter before the run and state the mode on every claim (F10). Fix the level at which complexity is being judged (F11).
8. Carry methods, not recordings, to the next comparison (Fig 4 :22-25: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*).

### Strain test and round trip

- **Apparent strain, Fig 5 'refuted' versus Fig 5 'mechanism broken'.** Re-census: the four kinds sort by what is missing, the fifth by what is wrong (Fig 5 :50-53). Different objects, no strain after re-census.
- **Apparent strain, simplicity as heuristic versus simplicity as verdict.** Re-census: Fig 1 disqualifies frame-internal scores as verdicts; the world Occam page calls the razor a heuristic for developing models, not an arbiter. Same distinction, no strain.
- **Apparent strain, MDL (bits) versus test cost (actions).** Re-census: Fig 12 :125-130 explicitly says they are two readings held apart. No strain, but the folder reports both rather than choosing one.
- **Live strain, named not hidden:** the world statistics criteria (AIC, BIC) do combine fit and parameter count into one scalar. That is a useful budget filter in their own field, but it is not the figure verdict here, which keeps bits and actions apart. This folder does not import their scalar as a witness (Fig 9 :60).
- **Round trip R_T:** sent up, the verbatim figure set chiefly Fig 1 :19/24-27, Fig 5 :42-45, Fig 9 :4-5/60, Fig 12 :125-130. Abstracted, the shorthand is route, bargain, discriminate, record. ### Updated verdict

The shape survives: compare by routing the residual, then the MDL bargain, then a ground discriminator, with description economy, live-slot gap and provenance kept as separate verdicts and bits and actions priced apart. No figure line gives a single scalar "which theory wins" rule. Mode `general` for the shape, `specified` for the six checks, nothing `grounded`. Strains after re-census: one live (the scalar world criteria are filters, not witnesses), named above.
