# Outreach draft: check three claims about theory comparison before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. Field: theory comparison research.

**Hard precondition:** the claim in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` must survive your own check before you send this. If the stage 1 output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.

---

**Subject:** Three checkable claims about comparing a complex theory with a simpler model

> Hi [name],
>
> I am writing about a narrow methods question: how researchers should compare a complex new theory with a simpler model that already fits many observations. I have three specific claims I would like you to check against your own work. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the claims do not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, stated as an effect:** a researcher holds a simpler model that predicts many observation slots, and a complex candidate is offered. The live question is not which one is more elegant or which one has more supporters. It is what the per-slot gap between prediction and observation owes next: nothing, a wider perception, a refit of the simple model, a new term, or a refusal of a held term. "Fits many observations" is an aggregate, and an aggregate can hide one live slot.
>
> **The three claims (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the checks; none is `grounded`):**
>
> 1. A frame-produced score such as coverage achieved or credibility accrued cannot decide the comparison. It is a quantity the frame produces, so it cannot score the frame. What decides is an anchor that does not move when researchers change their minds.
> 2. The only figure-licensed admission rule is a bargain: add the candidate and what it still leaves unexplained, and compare that with what was unexplained before. A candidate that explains everything by saying nothing, or that explains one occasion perfectly, fails this. Two prices must be reported separately, the bits to describe the candidate and the actions needed to test it. They do not add.
> 3. The comparison is a union of at least three sub-questions, description economy, live-slot ground gap, and provenance. Their verdicts stay separate. A single averaged score, or a vote among people chosen because they are likely to agree, is not a verdict.
>
> **What would refute these claims (written down before this message):**
> 1. A figure-derived rule that lets a frame-produced count decide the comparison with no external anchor.
> 2. A candidate admitted when its stated cost plus leftover exceeds the cost of the simpler model under the same code.
> 3. A figure-derived rule that sums description bits and testing actions into one verdict, or averages the three sub-verdicts.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that this derivation is a measurement. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against a figure set, and by its own rule it cannot settle the question it reports on. I am also not claiming that a crucial experiment can always be designed from a description of the difference. The figures require an instrument already returning a failing reading, and where none exists the honest result is a budget limit, not a verdict.
>
> **If the claims survive, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula for direct download (branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Worked theory-comparison literature in the public domain: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimum_description_length
>
> **If you want to use the method:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a theory comparison you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks for reading,
> [name]

## Stage order

1. **Stage 1:** send the residual, the three claims and the refuter. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict **before** showing any framework material.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the three links. The figures, not the summary, are the authority.
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

A Stage 2 agreement is never pooled as corroboration. A Stage 1 refutation is copied into `11_audit.md` with provenance and re-evaluated by the auditor seat as if it came from any other source.
