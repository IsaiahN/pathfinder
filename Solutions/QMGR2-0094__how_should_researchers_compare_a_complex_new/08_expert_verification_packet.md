# Expert verification packet: the Tether on theory comparison

**What this is:** a claim in a form a methodology researcher, philosopher of science or model-selection statistician can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the three sub-claims, not `grounded`.** No measurement of any real theory comparison is claimed. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 1. The claim in one paragraph

A complex new theory should be compared with a simpler well-fitting model by a six-step procedure, not by a single score. Split the question into description economy, live-slot ground gap and provenance and keep the verdicts separate. Route the residual first, so that a held false term gets a refusal rather than a larger explanation. Apply the minimum-description-length bargain under one declared code: admit the complex theory only if its cost plus what it leaves unexplained is less than the simpler model's unexplained residue. Price the actions needed to test it in a separate column, because bits and actions do not add. Require a discriminator already returning a failing reading, and record whether the theory was derived before or fitted after the data. Where no such reading exists, report a budget limit rather than a verdict.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 Framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 1, 5 and 9

- **Statement:** "fits many observations" cannot decide the comparison, and a single averaged score is not a verdict. The comparison is a union compound and the residual is per slot.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*; Fig 1 :27 *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*; Fig 9 :4-5 *"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*; Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
- **What to check:** do the cited lines say what is claimed, and does the union reading follow from them rather than from a preference for pluralism?
- **Refuter:** a figure-derived rule that uses a frame-produced count as the verdict with no external anchor, or that averages the three sub-verdicts.

### C2 Selection rule, checkable by reading Figure 5 and Figure 12

- **Statement:** the only figure-licensed admission test is the MDL bargain, and the two prices, description bits and testing actions, must be reported apart.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :43-44 *"A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before. A term that explains everything by saying nothing fails this, and so does a term that explains one occasion perfectly."*; Fig 5 :45 *"This is minimum description length, Rissanen 1978, which is itself a composition of Kolmogorov complexity and Shannon coding put to work on model selection."*; Fig 12 :125 *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*; Fig 12 :130 *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."*
- **What to check:** does a theory fitted after seeing the data fail the bargain when the same data are counted as explained, and is the two-currency rule consistent with standard model-selection practice in your field? A world check: the MDL and AIC pages give the fit-versus-parameter trade-off and also warn that a relative criterion says nothing about absolute quality.
- **Refuter:** a candidate admitted by the figures with cost plus leftover greater than the simpler model's cost under the same code, or a figure-derived scalar that adds bits and actions.

### C3 Ground and provenance, checkable by reading Figures 2, 6 and 8

- **Statement:** agreement cannot settle a theory comparison; a discriminator must already be returning a failing reading; and post-hoc fit is separated from predicted fit only by the record.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*; Fig 2 :29-30 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."*; Fig 6 :32 *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."*; Fig 8 :50-52 *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*
- **What to check:** do these lines forbid consensus and purely imagined crucial experiments, and is the provenance point consistent with the underdetermination literature? A world check: the experimentum crucis article records the Duhem-Quine objection to decisive experiments.
- **Refuter:** a figure-sanctioned comparison settled by panel agreement with no anchor, or a discriminator specified from description alone with no already-failing reading.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that the simpler theory is correct by default, and not that complexity is a virtue.
- Not a single ranking number, and not that AIC or BIC is the framework's verdict. Those are useful budget filters in their own fields; they are not witnesses here (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that a crucial experiment can always be designed in advance.
- Not that a clean provenance makes a theory right. Provenance shows legitimacy, not truth (Fig 10 :65).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. Specifically:

1. A per-slot measurement in which the simpler model's residual persists and does not trend down while the complex theory's pays for its cost, under a declared code and with a discriminator already returning a failing reading.
2. A demonstration that the complex theory's extra primitive is outside closure(Gamma), with a source gate and shadow test, rather than an unfound term already inside the closure.
3. A re-census that derives one of the figure terms used here from the others, or that licenses adding bits and actions into one verdict. Either would break the claim.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, :27; Fig 5 :43-45; Fig 9 :4-5, :60; Fig 12 :125, :130; Fig 2 :29-30, :35; Fig 6 :32; Fig 8 :50-52. Ten short passages.
2. Check C2 against your own field's model-selection practice. Does keeping fit and test cost apart change any conclusion you would otherwise draw?
3. Name one current instrument or reading in your field that fails to resolve between two competing theories. If you cannot, say so. That is the honest `unreachable-at-this-budget` answer.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
