## DERIVATION

### Q1 Are two rival theories isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :95-96: *"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."*
- If M and T are written over the same set of observational ingredients but arrange them differently (different stated relations, different mediated quantities), they are **isomers** under Fig 12. Same atom count does not make them duplicates, and equal fit on the existing data does not make them the same theory.
- Under the weaker form, they are a **functional group** (Fig 12 :124): *"A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Both are `state → structure → state` arrangements that predict the same slots, with different occupants.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if one of the two theories requires an atom the other does not, the isomer claim fails and only the weaker functional-group claim stands. This is checkable from the two written theories without a measurement.
- **Triangulation:** single-source for the figure definition (Fig 12), corroborated weakly by the world underdetermination article, which records that the same evidence may fail to distinguish rival theories. Corroboration from a shared broad pool, not a second witness.

### Q2 What order must the comparison follow?

- Fig 3 :19-20: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* and *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*
- Fig 9 :4-5: *"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*
- Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*
- `THE_FORMULA.md` step 2 routes REFUTED separately: *"a term held is false; what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation"*, and no arrangement of the other four routes reaches it.
- Fig 5 :33: *"The three guards are a product, not a checklist."* Support is before the search, reachability during, novelty after; any factor at zero stops the mint.
- Fig 5 :32: *"then check it against the ground, then carry it up"*
- **Required order:** split the union, describe R in effect terms, route the gap (refuse before mint), pass the three guards, pay the MDL bargain, check against the ground, carry up the method. Applying simplicity or elegance first is effort above the break.
- **Falsifier:** a figure-derived comparison in which a complexity score is applied before the residual is routed and the union is split. Such a rule would score a bundle as if it were one thing.

### Q3 How are the two prices carried?

- Fig 12 :125-126: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* and *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*
- Fig 12 :128-129: *"A long chain of cheap parts is expensive to describe and may cost one action to refute. A short arrangement over a costly operand is cheap to state and may take a whole budget to settle."* and *"Neither figure predicts the other, so both are read or the choice is made blind."*
- Fig 12 :130: *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."*
- The correct report is therefore a pair of columns: description length under a declared code, and the actions required to obtain a discriminating reading. A candidate with a lower description cost but an unaffordable test cost is not admitted by the figures; it is parked. Fig 5 :27-28: *"park it"*, *"keep the gap, look for an import"*.
- **Falsifier:** any figure-derived rule that returns one number by adding or weighted-summing the two prices. World information criteria (AIC, BIC, MDL tutorial) do combine fit and parameter count into a scalar in their own domains; that scalar is a budget filter here, not a witness (Fig 9 :60).

### Q4 Where does the simple model sit in the composition?

- Fig 4 :19-20: *"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."*
- Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*
- The simpler model is a coarse description of the ground, the result of an abstraction that threw away detail. Bringing it back down and measuring the gap (Fig 4 :30) tells us what the coarser description cannot hold. That gap is the only figure-licensed reason to admit extra complexity. If the gap is empty, the extra term is a recording, not a method.
- **Falsifier:** a complex theory admitted with no round-trip gap and no live slot residual.

## CONCLUSION

Two rival theories over the same ingredients are **isomers** under Fig 12, which is why equal fit does not make them duplicates; if their atom sets differ, the weaker functional-group reading applies. The comparison must follow dependency order: split, describe, route (refuse before mint), guard, bargain, settle, carry up. Its output is a pair of prices plus a per-slot gap, not a scalar. This rescues what is defensible in ordinary theory comparison language without inventing a new primitive or treating elegance as a verdict.
