# WATSON answer: how should researchers compare a complex new theory with a simpler model that already fits?

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the sub-claims, not `grounded`.**

**Status: SIMULATED DERIVATION, not a ground reading.** This run synthesizes `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, `05_what_shape_means.md`, `06_isomers_and_order.md`, `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md`. It is checked for internal consistency against the figures. By its own rule it cannot settle the question it reports on. No measurement was taken.

## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what a comparison procedure must look like when a complex new theory T meets a simpler model M that already fits many observations. Option A: treat comparison as a single ranking by fit and simplicity. Option B: split the residual into description economy, live-slot ground gap and provenance, route the gap before scoring, apply the description bargain with two prices held apart, and demand a ground discriminator. What each leads to: A gives an averaged, frame-internal score that can hide one live slot and can be manufactured by post-hoc fitting; B gives separate checkable verdicts, a pre-registered refuter, and an honest budget limit where no discriminator exists.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The per-slot gap between what the current library predicts and what the ground returns. Arity: three roles, M, T and the ground; the comparison bond ⋛ is ordered, not symmetric. Symmetry: broken, because M is held and settled while T is a candidate, and cost(T) plus leftover is compared against cost(M). Scale: first order inside each theory, second order at the comparison. Fig 9 :4-5 makes it a union compound, not one hard question.
- **Domain 1, description economy.** Actors: hypotheses, a code, the data. Currency: bits, that is cost(H) plus cost(data given H). Anchor: the data under a fixed declared code. Question type: partition, does admitting T pay.
- **Domain 2, ground and settlement.** Actors: instruments, observation slots, the ground. Currency: per-slot residual. Anchor: whatever settles the question and does not change with what any frame thinks. Without a nameable anchor, the claim is not yet in this domain.
- **Domain 3, frame-internal bookkeeping.** Actors: coverage counts, credibility, prestige, elegance. Currency: self-produced quantities. Anchor: none that does not move with the frames, so it is not verdict-bearing. Filter for the budget only.
- **Domain 4, provenance and lineage.** Actors: the candidate, its derivation record, the frame it came from. Currency: provenance and shadow test. Anchor: the record, which does not move with the contents.
- **Domain 5, verification and outreach.** Actors: expert readers in different closures. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all seats draw on one evidence pool, so this domain has zero independence.
- **Order by dependency.** Split the union, describe R in effect terms, refuse before mint, pass the three guards, pay the bargain, check against the ground, carry up the method (Fig 3, formula steps 2 to 5).

Strain test applied: the world information criteria combine fit and parameter count into one scalar, while Fig 12 :130 forbids adding the two prices. Re-census resolves this: the scalar world criteria are budget filters in their own fields, not the figure verdict here (Fig 9 :60). No strain remains after re-census; one is recorded as live-but-bounded in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §6.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives, and no winner is named. Compare by splitting the union, describing the residual in effect terms, routing the gap (refusing a false held term before minting), applying the MDL bargain under one declared code, pricing testing actions separately from description bits, demanding a discriminator already returning a failing reading, and recording provenance. Keep verdicts per domain separate and never averaged. Simplicity and elegance are budget filters, not witnesses. Where no reading fails to resolve, the honest output is `unreachable-at-this-budget`, not a verdict. Mode `general` on the shape, `specified` on the checks, nothing `grounded`.

- **cost if wrong:** the procedure could discard a genuinely valuable complex theory because its current description cost looks high, or retain a simpler model whose live slot is hidden by an aggregate. The cost is bounded and locatable because the packet states the code, the per-slot residuals and the two prices; an error is findable rather than invisible. The larger avoidable cost is spending outside readers' independence by sending a summary before a residual (Fig 2 :42, "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong.").
- **undoable:** yes. No primitive is withdrawn and no theory is deleted. A rejected admission is reversible by declaring a different code, by finding a live slot, or by obtaining a discriminator. A wrong provenance ruling can be superseded by the record (Fig 10 :62-66). The record only grows; the reach can fall and rise again with new growth.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A discriminator returning a per-slot difference in favor of T under a declared code would move T toward `grounded`; a figure re-census that licenses adding bits and actions would break the two-currency rule; a derivation of one held figure term from the others would break the audit; a measurement showing identical per-slot residuals on every live slot would withdraw the claim that T buys a gap, for that budget only (Fig 8 :39, "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."). Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
