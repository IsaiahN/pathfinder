## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** A fall at slot s and a live residual at s' or s'' reached through contact. Arity: at least two slots plus a connecting relation; cascades raise the arity. Symmetry: not symmetric in general, because the receiving slot is often a different class of actor; valence is relative to the residual. Scale: local lever versus habitat cascade, with the residual that mattered three steps out. Fig 9 :55 ("A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds.") names this a union compound, not one hard question.
- **Domain 1, intervention design frame.** Actors: designers, target variable, lever, success metric. Currency: fall in measured R at s. Anchor: the outcome at s, but it updates on the design frame's own report and is therefore not yet an anchor. Verdict: local control only; cannot settle displacement.
- **Domain 2, habitat and adaptive system frame.** Actors: actors, conditions, relations, the network, adaptive agents. Currency: contact, cascade and behaviour change. Anchor: an independent reading at receiving slots. Verdict: this domain carries displacement; its list is read off the world, not composed.
- **Domain 3, measurement and verification frame.** Actors: instruments, monitored slots, unmonitored slots. Currency: per-slot residual readings. Anchor: independent ground readings at several slots. Verdict: agreement inside one closure is one evidence pool talking, not corroboration.
- **Partition or union.** Union, not partition. Verdicts per domain stay separate and are never averaged. A net risk score is a filter for the budget, not a witness for the verdict.
- **Order by dependency.** Describe R per slot; enumerate the habitat; install a receiving-slot instrument; state the objective as a comparison; then choose a lever. The middle link, vocabulary and objective, is where the chain breaks in practice.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the names and the universal checklist do not. Keep the residual per slot, enumerate contacts outward until the cascade stops, treat valence as residual-relative, range receiving-slot readings against a non-updating anchor, and report unresolved receiving slots as unreached. Displacement re-arranges a closed set of contacts, so over the same actors two designs are isomers and a difference of bond is decisive where a list of ingredients is mute. Which slot, what magnitude and which named mechanism are a `gap` owed to an instrument, not a failure to search. Mode `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the method, not `grounded`, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet is mode-labelled, carries six refuters written before the run and records the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside checkers by sending a catalogue of cases instead of a generator. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The named mechanisms and the checklist temptation are removable without touching the derivation. `07` and `08` already remove them. A sent claim can be superseded by the corrected packet, and no primitive has to be withdrawn.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A per-slot instrument that separates the fall at s from the rise at s' and s'' over a delay window, an anchor that holds when the target report changes, a live residual at second or third contact when first contact is clean, a measured global subtraction with no contact change, or a standing safety classification that keeps its valence under a changed residual, would each break the corresponding claim. A figure re-census that shows a universal complete enumeration is derivable would clear the `gap`. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
