# Displacement as re-arrangement: structure, order and analogues

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the order claim, not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The effect to be explained: an intervention lowers a reading at target slot s and the residual is live at receiving slot s' or s''. Two questions are owed: (1) is the displacement a new substance or a re-arrangement of what was already in the habitat, and (2) in what order does the cascade run, and can that order be read off the intervention's own vocabulary?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: actors, conditions and relations in contact with the residual (Fig 11 :45-47), the network (Fig 13 :23-24). Bonds held: the seven typed relations plus negation. No new primitive is available without import. Currency: contact, cascade and per-slot residual. Anchor: a receiving-slot reading that does not update on the target report. Question type: union compound, not partition (Fig 9 :54-57).

## Q1 — Is displacement a new substance or a re-arrangement?

- Fig 13 :115 "The set is closed. The arrangements are not."
- Fig 13 :116 "a new member would have to be a new kind, and composition only ever rearranges the kinds already held"
- Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance."
- Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 11 :61 "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."
- Fig 11 :62 "You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about"

Reading: the actors and relations are already enumerated. A lever changes the bond arrangement, not the atom list. Two intervention designs over the same atoms that differ only in which relation is acted on are **isomers** under Fig 12 :117: same ingredients, different substance, and the difference is invisible in a list of ingredients. This is why an intervention report that lists what it changed (the ingredients) cannot tell you where the residual went (the arrangement): the bond was never recorded.

**Falsifier, written before the run.** If a measured intervention lowers total residual across every enumerated slot, with no compensating rise anywhere, no new actor introduced and no change in contact, then the displacement is a genuine subtraction and the re-arrangement claim fails. Status: `specified`, triangulated between Fig 13 and Fig 12 (two figures, different objects, low overlap) and Fig 11 (contact asymmetry).

**S1 strain, kept visible.** A first pass read "exclusion is not" as a ban on reducing risk. Re-census: the governed object is the habitat and its contacts, not a substance called risk. Risk relocates through contact. The claim is narrowed to contact and the strain is dissolved, not erased.

## Q2 — In what order does the cascade run?

- Fig 11 :52 "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."
- Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."
- Fig 11 :44 "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."
- Fig 11 :48 "You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."
- Fig 11 :54 "Carson, Silent Spring, 1962. The mechanism, biomagnification through the food chain to eggshell thinning, was established over the following decade."

The order is **not** a design choice. It is read outward from the residual: direct contact, then the contacts of those, until the cascade stops mattering. The canonical case has the residual three steps out. The mechanism was established over the following decade, which means the time scale of the instrument lagged the time scale of the intervention. Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does" explains why first-order monitoring looked successful: the channel to the third-order ground had not yet been built.

**Ordering caveat.** For this question the order is a cascade order in a habitat, not Fig 12's sequence bond `→` applied to the six-term substrate. A first pass tried to write `Risk → Actor → Risk` as a `state → structure → state` molecule. That strains: the actors and conditions of Fig 11 are not the states and structures of Fig 13. Re-census assigns the sequence to contact order, and the six-term substrate is withdrawn from this derivation. Triangulated for the outward rule (Fig 11 :44 and :52); single-source for the three-step case.

**Falsifier.** A design case where instrumented second and third-order contacts show no residual while first-order contact does, over a time window that includes the measured delay. Then the outward order is not the binding order in that domain.

## Q3 — Union or partition? Why one lever leaves the intersections standing

- Fig 9 :54 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- Fig 9 :55 "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."
- Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."
- Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

The three conditions are: local effect at s (D1), contact cascade and adaptive response (D2), and the measurement frame (D3). Reducing the target risk clears the D1-only region. It does not clear D2-and-D1, because the contacts still bind, and it does not clear D3-and-D1, because the pooled metric still hides the live slot. A net risk score is a filter for the budget; it is not a witness that the compound is gone.

**Falsifier.** A single lever measured to remove the target risk and the downstream residual at every intersection, with per-slot readings, would dissolve the union reading for that case.

## Q4 — What kind of analogue travels, and what does not

- Fig 6 :30 "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."
- Fig 9 :30 "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"
- Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"
- Fig 8 :50 "Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."
- Fig 8 :51 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"

The analogue that travels is the **effect shape**: a fall at one slot with a live residual at a slot reached through contact, with a delay. That shape is what the world sources in `09` share across road safety, energy economics, impact assessment and system dynamics. What does not travel is the mechanism name: "cobra effect", "Peltzman effect", "carbon leakage", "rebound" are bound to their own frames and imported only with provenance and a shadow test. Two sources describing the same shape by convergence are corroboration at maximum independence; two sources repeating the same reviewed case are one pool wearing two names. The record, not the contents, decides which.

**Falsifier.** If a mechanism name imported from one field predicts the receiving slot in another field without any source gate or shadow test and does so prospectively across repeated interventions, then the provenance requirement is unnecessary for that class of case.

## Q5 — The order of the checking loop

- Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."
- Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
- Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
- Fig 12 :111 "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."
- Operators table :52 "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"

The checking loop runs: describe R per slot; enumerate the habitat; install a receiving-slot instrument; state what counts as success as a comparison; then choose a lever. Most intervention effort lands after the lever, where the measurements already exist (Fig 3 :29-30). Optimising the target metric cannot rescue a missing objective link. A vocabulary that can name the target risk but has no term for the route to a receiving slot can name a state and cannot name a route to one.

**Falsifier.** A team without terms for cascade and route that still anticipates receiving slots reliably across repeated interventions, with the route written down only after the fact. Then the vocabulary link is not the break.

## CONCLUSION

Under a fixed set of actors, conditions and relations, displacement is a re-arrangement of a closed set: two intervention designs that differ only in the bond they act on are isomers, and the difference is invisible in an ingredient list. The cascade is ordered outward from the residual by contact, with the residual that matters potentially three steps out and an instrument that may lag by a decade. The question is a union compound, so one lever clears the D1-only region and leaves both intersections standing. What travels between fields is the effect shape, never the mechanism name without provenance. The check loop breaks at the vocabulary/objective link, where teams have a target metric but no term for the route.

This rescues what is defensible without inventing a universal checklist: the isomer claim, the outward order and the union reading need no new primitive and no wall-moving import. What remains open is which bond was changed in a given intervention and which slot carries the residual, both of which owe a ground reading.
