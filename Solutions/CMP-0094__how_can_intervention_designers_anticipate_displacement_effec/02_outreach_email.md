# Outreach draft: check the output before deciding about the method

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the stage-one output below survives a check. If it does not, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable output on displacement effects, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms:** an intervention reduces a measured risk at a target slot, and the residual turns up at another slot reached through the system's contacts. The figures index a residual per slot, not as a total: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." They also say a near-zero global reading with one live slot is a legal state, and that "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." So a single net risk score is structurally incapable of showing a displacement, whatever the intervention did.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the structure, `general` on the method, not `grounded`):**
> 1. Keep the residual per slot. Never reduce it to one net number.
> 2. Enumerate contacts outward, not just direct stakeholders. The figures' own example is literal: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."
> 3. Treat actor and lever valence as relative to the residual, so a prior "safe intervention" list cannot be reused across a changed residual.
> 4. Range the receiving-slot reading against an anchor that does not move when the design team changes its own success report. An anchor is "whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."
> 5. Report a receiving slot you could not resolve as unreached, not as no effect. "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
>
> This is a shape and a method, not a catalogue of side effects. The framework returns a kind of thing, never the answer itself. It does not name the slot or the magnitude without a measurement.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that risk compensation, the cobra effect, carbon leakage or the rebound effect are derived from the figures. They are not. They are bindings from other fields, offered as provenanced examples only after your verdict on stage one. I am also not claiming a checklist that guarantees complete enumeration; no figure line returns one.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. A complete per-slot enumeration out to third contact, with instruments that would have registered a residual, showing no live residual at any receiving slot.
> 2. A success metric that demonstrably updates on the designers' own report, yet still settles the receiving-slot question.
> 3. A within-closure search shown to be a sealed room, so finding nothing proves absence.
> 4. A standing classification of interventions as safe that keeps the same valence across two materially different residuals.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, the corresponding claim is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local framework copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Full verification packet with three checkable sub-claims: `08_expert_verification_packet.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the framework material. Run it on an intervention you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [name]

## Why this version is ordered this way

| move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Output and residual first, theory second | Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Fig 2 :45 "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." |
| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." |
| Pre-registered refuter before conclusion | A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device. |
| Mode stated on every claim | `THE_FORMULA.md`: "Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which." |
| Named mechanisms withheld until after stage one | Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." A name borrowed from a world frame is a binding, and a binding can manufacture the agreement it is meant to test. |
| No request for trust or prestige | "Prestige can fund an action... but it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it." |
| Links only after the verdict is recorded | Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." |
| Offer the formula as a method, not an answer | Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." |
| Ask each user to state their own ground and refuter | `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": a frame cannot check the direction of its own question, because the sought-for shape is not a term it holds. |

**Stage order.** (1) Residual and refuter, with a request for what the reader would count as a failure. (2) Record that verdict. (3) Only if curious, offer the three links and the packet. (4) Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
