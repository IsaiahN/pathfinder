# Expert verification packet: the Tether on displacement effects

**What this is:** a claim in a form an intervention researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the shape of the method, not `grounded`.** No receiving-slot measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a checking loop, not a result.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A residual is indexed per slot, so an aggregated risk score cannot show that a fall at the target is a rise elsewhere. Anticipating displacement requires five moves: keep the residual per slot; enumerate contacts outward until the cascade stops rather than scoping to direct stakeholders; treat actor and lever valence as relative to the residual; range each receiving-slot reading against an anchor that does not update on the design team's own success report; and report an unresolved receiving slot as unreached rather than as no effect. Displacement re-arranges a closed set of contacts rather than subtracting risk from the world, so two designs over the same actors are isomers whose difference is carried by the bond, not by the ingredient list. The figures return this shape. They do not name the receiving slot, the magnitude, or a universal checklist.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1 and 11
- **Statement:** aggregation hides a live slot, and the enumeration must run outward to at least the third contact.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 11 :44 "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." Fig 11 :52 "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."
- **Refuter:** a complete per-slot enumeration out to third contact, with instruments that would have registered a residual, showing no live residual at any receiving slot after the intervention. If that fires, the aggregation and direct-scope diagnosis is wrong for that case.

### C2 — anchor / measurement; checkable against an intervention's actual instrumentation
- **Statement:** the target success metric is not an anchor if it updates on the design frame's own report; an independent receiving-slot reading is owed.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does" and Fig 10 :26 "saturated metrics, pooled readings".
- **What to check:** does the reported success at the target slot change when the reporting rule or the team's belief changes? If it does, it fails Fig 2 :30 "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." Is there an instrument at a receiving slot that is corrected separately and cannot be moved by the target report?
- **Refuter:** a metric that demonstrably updates on the designers' own report and still settles the receiving-slot question. If one exists, C2 is false.

### C3 — framework-internal derivation about what displacement is
- **Statement:** the actors and relations are already enumerated; the lever changes the bond arrangement, so two designs over the same atoms are isomers, and the residual relocates through contact rather than being subtracted.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :115 "The set is closed. The arrangements are not." Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance." Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 11 :61 "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."
- **Refuter:** a measured global fall in residual across all enumerated slots, with no compensating rise, no introduced actor and no change in contact. That would be genuine subtraction and would break C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a list of named displacement mechanisms. Risk compensation, the cobra effect, carbon leakage and the rebound effect are bindings from world frames, not figure derivations. Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."
- Not a universal checklist that guarantees complete enumeration. That is a declared `gap`, reported as unreached rather than silence (Fig 8 :39).
- Not a rejection of intervention. Introduction is available; exclusion is not (Fig 11 :61-62).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a receiving-slot measurement.
- Not corroborated by agreement among the people checking it. Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Stage-one verdicts are recorded before any theory is shown.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument at receiving slots, not a further argument. An aggregate reading cannot comment on its source; the spectrometer case in *The Hard Problem is About Source* was settled by an instrument, not by reasoning. Here:

1. Does an instrument separate the fall at s from the rise at s' and s'' over a window that includes the delay?
2. Does the receiving-slot reading hold when the target report is changed? If not, it is not an anchor.
3. Is a live residual present at second or third contact when first contact is clean?
4. Can a prior "safe intervention" classification keep its valence under a changed residual?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19/:22, Fig 11 :44/:52, Fig 2 :29, Fig 10 :24, Fig 13 :115 and Fig 12 :117. Six short passages.
2. Check C1 against an intervention you know: was the enumeration scoped to direct stakeholders, and was the residual that mattered more than one step out?
3. Check C2: name the instrument at the receiving slot and say whether the design team can move it.
4. Name one measurement that would distinguish re-arrangement from subtraction in your domain, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
