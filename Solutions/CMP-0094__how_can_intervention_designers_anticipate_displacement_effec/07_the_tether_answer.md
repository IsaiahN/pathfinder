# The Tether's answer on displacement effects

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the shape of the method, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a checking loop, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading. No receiving-slot measurement was taken.

## 1. The residual it answers

A designer reduces a measured risk at a target slot and the residual is live elsewhere. Fig 1 :13 "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state" and Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." The question as posed is a **union compound**, not one problem with a displacement coefficient. Fig 9 :55 "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds." Local reduction, contact cascade, and the measurement frame are three overlapping conditions. Verdicts stay separate per domain and are never averaged.

## 2. The shape

1. **Keep the residual per slot.** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." A single net risk score is structurally unable to show a displacement. This is `general` and structural.
2. **Enumerate contacts outward, not just direct stakeholders.** Fig 11 :44 "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." Fig 11 :52 gives the canonical case: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." A team-authored stakeholder map is an authored room; the list is read off the world (Fig 11 :48). `specified`, falsifiable.
3. **Re-derive valence per residual.** Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." A lever that is a benefactor under one residual is a malfactor under another, so a prior safety classification cannot be reused. `general`.
4. **Range reading against a non-updating anchor.** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." A target metric that falls because the team decided the intervention worked is panel 1 or 2 of Fig 2, not panel 3. The receiving-slot instrument must not move when the design report changes. Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does" explains why first-order monitoring can look successful while the channel to the third-order ground is missing. `specified`.
5. **Displacement is a re-arrangement, not a subtraction.** Fig 13 :115 "The set is closed. The arrangements are not." Fig 11 :61 "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." You can introduce an actor; you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world. Over a fixed atom set, two designs that differ only in which bond they act on are isomers (Fig 12 :117). `general`.
6. **Import a differing closure with gates, in that order.** Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 9 :27 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Describe the residual in effect terms, then find the frame whose closure contains the receiving slots; apply a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :12-13) and debit independence (Fig 6 :24). `specified`.
7. **An unfound side effect is an unreached verdict, not silence.** Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 9 :44 "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room." `general`, proved limit.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a catalogue of named side effects. The figures return a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32). Risk compensation, the cobra effect, carbon leakage and the rebound effect are world bindings, not figure derivations.
- Not a checklist that guarantees complete enumeration. No figure line returns one. This is a declared `gap`.
- Not which slot or what magnitude in a real intervention. Owing to a ground reading at receiving slots.
- Not a rejection of intervention. Fig 11 :58 "Maintained. A seat exists and can act: choose the instruments, distil the impactful actors, introduce what was not there." The seat's power is asymmetric, so introduction is available and exclusion is not. The method is for working within that asymmetry honestly.
- Not `grounded`. No instrument was read for this derivation.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument at receiving slots, not a better argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*, opened this session: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; "the arrival of a better instrument" resolved the case. Here:

- **Per-slot discriminator:** an instrument that separates a fall at s from a rise at s' and s'' rather than reporting a net score, over a window that includes the measured delay.
- **Third-contact test:** instrument the second and third contacts of the residual, not just direct stakeholders. The canonical case says the residual that mattered was three steps out.
- **Anchor test:** show that the receiving-slot reading does not move when the design team changes its success report. If it moves, it is not an anchor.
- **Standing-list test:** re-run the same intervention classification under a different residual and see whether valence flips.
- **Subtraction test:** look for a global fall with no compensating rise, no introduced actor and no contact change. Absence of that is what the re-arrangement claim predicts.
- Standing falsifiers from `01` section 4: six conditions written before the run.

## 5. The honest stop

**Shape reached, ground unreached.** The universal anticipation method that guarantees a complete enumeration is a `gap` of the shape-not-a-name kind (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134): the framework can characterise what a missing instrument would have to do but cannot name it. And "we monitored and found no displacement" is unreachable-at-this-budget, not proof of absence (Fig 8 :39). Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." The stop is recorded with the lineage so a later reader can locate the error rather than only feel it.

**Cost if wrong:** bounded. The shape is mode-labelled and carries its refuter, so an error is findable. The real cost is spending the independence of outside checkers by sending a catalogue of cases instead of a generator (Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong.").

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a list of displacement effects, and it does not claim a method that guarantees you will find them. It says that a residual is indexed per slot, so a net risk score is structurally unable to show that a fall here is a rise there; that you must enumerate contacts outward until the cascade stops, because the residual that mattered in the canonical case was three steps out; that actor and lever valence are relative to the residual, so no prior safety list survives a change of question; that the receiving-slot reading must come from an anchor that does not move when the design team changes its own success report; and that finding nothing inside one frame is an unreached verdict, not proof of absence. Displacement re-arranges a closed set of contacts, so over the same actors two designs differ by bond, not by ingredients. What is offered is that shape, six refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The receiving slots stay open until the ground settles them.
