## Wider literature that bears on the separator

1. **Information cascades and independence.** The SEP entry's discussion of Bikhchandani et al. (1992) is the closest world analogue to Fig 7 :12-13 ("same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not") and Fig 2 :31 ("Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence"). Each agent updates on the observed action of the previous agent, obscuring private information. This corroborates the mechanism shape, not the figure verdict.
2. **Bubbles versus echo chambers.** Nguyen (2020) is a genuine low-overlap corroborator for the figure distinction between an undirected union (Fig 8 :19-21, a surplus that is mute because no residual is described) and a self-sealing chain of mirrors (Fig 7 :36). The world distinction is conceptual; the figure distinction is structural. Agreement between them is weak corroboration because they were derived in different domains.
3. **Homophily and biased diffusion.** Cinelli et al. (2021) measures the parameter that Fig 8 :47-49 calls "one quantity read twice." Their result that some platforms show strong clustering and others do not is evidence that the parameter is measurable, which the figures do not and need not assert.
4. **Informational versus normative influence.** The Conformity page distinguishes turning to the group because it is better informed from turning to it for acceptance. That maps onto the figure split between reputation as a ground-contact record and reputation as standing accrued inside the frame (Fig 1 :26-27). The map is an analogue, not a derivation.
5. **Private versus public answer.** Asch's finding that written private answers were more accurate than spoken ones is a corpus-level analogue for Fig 10 :42 ("A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it"): removing the audience restores a channel to the ground. It does not establish the seat rule; it corroborates its shape.

**Overlap declaration.** SEP, PhilPapers, APA, and the journal pages are all philosophy infrastructure and count as one broad evidence pool, not several witnesses. Wikipedia pages are secondary syntheses and share that pool where they cite the same papers. Cinelli et al. and Nguyen are the two genuinely lower-overlap voices (network science and analytic philosophy of social epistemology respectively), and even they partly overlap through the echo-chamber literature. No world source conflicts with the figure verdict; the conflict rule was not triggered.

- **Fig 10 :52** "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Send the separator and the refuter first. The framework's interiors come later, and only if asked for.
- **Fig 2 :45** "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." Ask what the recipient would count as a failure before giving them the derivation.
- **Fig 7 :35** "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Do not select recipients for likely agreement, and do not pool later agreement as corroboration.

## The two-stage offer

**Stage 1.** Validate the separator against the ground or against the reader's own field knowledge. No theory is shown. The verdict is recorded before stage 2.

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If the reader is curious, offer the four links. Their later agreement is never counted as corroboration, because once they hold the framework's interiors they have joined the frame being checked (Fig 10 :42).

**Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse. Each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode before running it. A recorded failure is more useful than a reported success.
