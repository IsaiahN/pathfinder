**Subject:** Check one separator about trust and belief before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`):** the question "how do trust, reputation and social identity affect whether information is believed or shared" is not one question. It is a union of at least four conditions with different anchors. The separator I get is this:
>
> 1. All three cues enter the belief through its inherited side, the weighting w, not through the observation. They tilt what is expected before the claim is checked. That is the mechanism by which confidence can rise without a change in ground contact.
> 2. Reputation is a metric only when it is a record of contact with an anchor that does not update on the community's own reports. When it is accrued from being repeated by those who already believe, it is a frame scoring itself, and the figures explicitly disqualify that quantity.
> 3. Social identity is the legitimate anchor for questions of belonging and shared meaning, and is structurally disqualified from settling truth. The same agreement is efficient for a convention and worthless for a verdict. Those are two columns, not an average.
> 4. Repetition inside a like-minded group compounds agreement and does not add evidence. The next source has to differ from the last and match the residual that surfaced, or the chain is motion without progress.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the framework returns a number for how much trust raises belief. It returns a shape, not a coefficient. I am not claiming a universal ranking of the three cues. Their valence is relative to the claim, and no standing list of trustworthy sources survives a change of claim. I am also not claiming that group agreement corroborates the separator. In this simulated run all the sources share one evidence pool, so their agreement is one pool talking, not independent witnesses.
>
> **What would refute the separator:** (1) a cue shown to change belief by changing the observation with ground contact held constant; (2) a reputation measure produced inside the believing frame that still predicts out-of-sample ground readings; (3) group identity shown to change the truth value of a ground-constitutive proposition; (4) a closed group whose repetition improves ground accuracy with no import from a differing closure; (5) a fixed list of trustworthy source properties invariant across claims.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the separator is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures text with line numbers (local extract in the framework): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Social Epistemology: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epistemology-social/
> - Nguyen 2020, Echo Chambers and Epistemic Bubbles, Episteme: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/episteme/article/echo-chambers-and-epistemic-bubbles/5D4AC3A808C538E17C50A7C09EC706F0
>
