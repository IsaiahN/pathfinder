## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is a per-claim, per-receiver acceptance or propagation: does receiver R take proposition P as true, and does R pass P on? The options lead to different outcomes. If trust, reputation, and identity are treated as evidence about P, then a claim is believed in proportion to source cues and the ground (whether P is actually true) is reached only accidentally. If they are treated as inherited priors that weight the belief while a separate channel ranges the claim against a non-updating anchor, then belief can be corrected and sharing can be routed. An answer would look like a separator: which of the three cues is which kind of quantity, in which domain, against which anchor, and what would falsify the separation.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the derivation.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons in the figures' own words:

1. **A room does not supply an anchor.** Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Agreement among a selected audience is the product of a convention, not evidence about a claim.
2. **Selecting for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation.** Fig 2 :41: "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it" while Fig 2 :40 says the same correlation is "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool".
3. **Asking for trust before checking spends the only thing the checker contributes.** Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR or CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and would get the sending domain blacklisted. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one.
