Two questions are owed: (1) are distinct microscopic theories in one universality class **isomers** under Fig 12's definition, or something weaker; and (2) what is the order of the description across levels, and where does an analogue-gravity system sit in that order?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are universality classes functional groups or isomers?

- Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 13 :121: **"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."**

A universality class is fixed by a substructure: symmetry, dimensionality and range of interactions. That is the *group says what kind* part of the functional-group definition; the specific microscopic theory is *the qualifier says which member*. So, as the physics is normally written (a lattice spin model versus a continuum field theory), two members of one universality class are **not isomers**: they do not even share the same UV atom set. They are Fig 13's *different routes to the same substance*. Triangulated for the reading: Fig 12's functional-group definition plus Fig 13's route claim, two distinct figures with low overlap.

- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two members of one universality class can be written over the identical atom set and differ only in the bond arrangement, the isomer reading wins and the functional-group reading is too weak. Record that as a live alternative, do not hide it.

### Q2 - When would the stronger isomer claim hold?

- If both descriptions are rewritten over the full shared atom set `{Space, Time, Energy, Spectrum, Gradient, Network}` and differ only in which structure mediates which state pair, then they satisfy Fig 12 :117: same atoms, different bond, different substance. That is a real isomer claim.
- Fig 13 :76: **"A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."** So arranging the same structures over different states yields genuinely different terms, which is the mechanism the isomer claim needs.
- The stronger form is checkable, not asserted. It requires both theories to be rewritten in the substrate vocabulary, which is a binding from the physics frame and must be provenanced. Status `specified`, ungrounded.

### Q3 - What is the order across levels?

- Fig 12 :103-104: **"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."** and **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."**
- A first-order crossing is `microscopic states → structure → effective states`, one membrane crossing. The EFT itself is first order relative to its microscopic system.
- A second-order composition appears when two settled effective theories are composed over: `(settled EFT_1) and (settled EFT_2)` become atoms for a joint description. This is legal only because both first-order molecules settled against the ground. It requires no new primitive; it is Fig 12's recursion, not an import.
- Fig 13 :75: **"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."** So the joint description of two effective theories must likewise run through structures and states, never state to state or structure to structure at one level.
- **Falsifier:** if a joint effective description can be formed by bonding two effective states directly, with no intervening structure, the order claim fails.

### Q4 - Where does an analogue-gravity system sit?

An analogue system is **not** an isomer of gravity, because it does not share the same atoms; it is a different frame whose closure differs. Fig 8 :50-51: **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."** So:

- If the same effective behaviour is derived independently in the analogue frame and in the gravitational frame, that is convergent derivation at maximum independence, corroboration under Fig 8.
- If a structure is borrowed from the analogue frame and used in the gravitational description, that is an adopted import. It needs a source gate and a shadow test and is debited against the borrowing frame's independence (Fig 6 import rule).
- The verdict depends on the record, not on the contents. The analogue gravity review's own warning, *"analogy is not identity"*, is the physics-frame statement of the same distinction. Triangulated for the distinction (figure plus world source); the specific experiments are `specified`.

- **Falsifier:** if two frames can be shown to draw on the same evidence pool yet their agreement is treated as a verdict, the independence reading fails and the analogue's corroboration must be withdrawn (Fig 2 :40).

## CONCLUSION

Members of one universality class are, in the ordinary writing, a **functional group** (symmetry, dimensionality and range fix the kind; the microscopic theory is the member), not isomers. They are Fig 13's *different routes to one substance*. The stronger isomer claim holds only when both are rewritten over the shared six-term atom set and differ solely in bond arrangement, and it is then checkable rather than asserted. Order across levels is first order for a single EFT and second order for a joint description of settled EFTs, licensed by Fig 12's settled-molecule rule and requiring no new primitive. An analogue-gravity system is a different closure: its value is triangulation by convergent derivation, not identity and not an import that escapes a wall.

Two falsifiers are standing (the identical-atom-set test for isomers, the direct state-state bond test for order), plus the shared-pool test for analogue corroboration. None was fired in this simulated run.
