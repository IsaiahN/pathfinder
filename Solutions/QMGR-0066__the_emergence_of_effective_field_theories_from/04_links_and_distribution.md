## Three things in the wider literature that bear directly on this question

1. **Universality is a many-to-one map, not a coincidence.** Wilczek reports that *"the same value of the critical exponent is observed in many different materials with vastly different Curie temperatures, a property we call 'universality'"*. Wilson's Nobel lecture says the fixed point becomes *"independent of details of the system at the atomic level"*. This is the physics counterpart of Fig 13 :121 (*"Different routes can arrive at the same substance"*) and of Fig 4 :28 (*"Going up throws away detail; that is what makes the result reapplicable."*). Use it to explain why the lossy transform is a feature, not a defect. Do not present it as a derivation the framework produced; it is a retrodiction from an independent literature.

2. **The RG flow loses information.** Nakayama writes that *"the renormalization group flow is accompanied with a kind of coarse graining. We lose information along the flow."* This is the independent, low-overlap corroboration of Fig 4 :27 (*"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."*) and of the nonzero round-trip gap R_T. It also supports the refusal to treat the EFT as a lossless compression of the microscopic theory.

3. **An analogy is a different closure, not the ground.** The analogue gravity review states *"analogy is not identity"* and describes the field as using condensed matter to probe curved-space quantum fields. Read with Fig 6 :23 (*"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*), this says exactly what an analogue system can and cannot do for the emergence claim: it can triangulate; it cannot replace the anchor. Belenchia, Liberati and Mohd give a concrete case where a geometric theory emerges from a flat-spacetime quantum condensate, which is a binding from a different frame, provenanced as such.
