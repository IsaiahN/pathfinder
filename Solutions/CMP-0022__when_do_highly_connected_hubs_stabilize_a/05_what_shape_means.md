## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (verified exact). Fig 13 :134: *"It can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands"* and *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it"* (both verified as substrings).

From *The Library of Babel*: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** here is the narrowed form of an answer that can be stated before the sign is measured. It constrains; it does not fill. The shape derived in `01` is: valence is relational, the question is a union, an anchor must be named, and same-degree arrangements can have different outcomes. A filled answer would be "hubs always stabilise" or "hubs are always the Achilles' heel". Both are names where the framework returns a shape, and both are refused.

## 2. How the shape was obtained, and why it matters here

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, then look for new terms that overlap or correlate. *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."* Applied here: the known vocabulary was "hub", "robust", "fragile"; the recurring new terms were "valence relative to residual", "anchor", "union", "percolation", "cascade". Each narrowed the question.
- *Library of Babel* on bundles: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually."* This is the essay-level version of fig 9 :4-5. The question about hubs is exactly such a bundle.
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"*.
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* This is why the outreach in `02` sends an effect (a flip in sign with perturbation) and a refuter, not a theory.

## 3. What licenses a name

Two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* For this question that means a percolation measurement, an attack simulation with a reported statistic, or a field measurement of a propagating quantity.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (fig 6 :23), with a source gate and a shadow test. The network-science result that hubs are fragile under targeted attack is an import from that frame into the figure vocabulary. Its record must show where it came from, because *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"* (fig 8 :51).

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the discrimination rule. An instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*. Several underlying causes can give identical readings: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"*. It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved the case: *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."*

Applied here: observing that a network fragments after removing high-degree nodes does not by itself identify which structural source produced the fragmentation. Degree rank, betweenness, core position and spatial embedding can all give the same reading. What is owed is the discriminating instrument, not the name "hub".

## 4. Why a single verdict about hubs is the specific forbidden error

The question as posed asks for a single answer to "when". A single answer of the form "hubs stabilise" is a name attached to a slot the framework leaves open. Three independent reasons:

1. fig 11 :50: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* The hub's sign depends on which residual is being served.
2. fig 9 :57: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. Random failure and targeted attack are two conditions.
3. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a reading of one quantity (connectivity loss) does not identify the source. The essays' metamerism case is the exact structural analogue of the Albert/Doyle disagreement: same degree sequence, different arrangement, different measured outcome.

## 5. Q: what licenses the specific shape answer here?

**Answer:** the figures license the relational shape, not the sign. The sign for a given network is licensed only by the ground, meaning a named anchor and a measurement. Until then the correct form is the conditional in `01` section 0: for anchor A and threat T, hubs have sign S by mechanism M, with S left open.

**Does this package commit the forbidden move?** No. It does not name which structure occupies a missing slot, because no new primitive is being claimed. It does name the conditions that must be fixed before a sign can be read. It also does not treat the world sources as deriving the verdict: they are imports with provenance, and the conflict between them is resolved by re-census (different arrangements), not by prestige.

## 6. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly"*. And: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* The famous 2000 result gives the hub question a budget to be tried; it does not settle the arrangement question. A campaign that gathers famous names to agree with either side is the named collapse.

**This audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post"* but *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* The essay *"I am not claiming that the Tether is a self-validating axiom system"*. So `01` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It cannot settle the hub question it reports on, and it says so on every substantive file.
