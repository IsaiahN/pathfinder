## What the world sources jointly support and where they conflict

All seven web sources agree on one conditional: the sign of the hub effect depends on the perturbation and on where the high-degree nodes sit relative to global connectivity. They do not all agree on how common that arrangement is.

- Albert et al 2000 and Callaway et al 2000 share the scale-free literature pool. Their agreement is one pool talking, not two witnesses (fig 2 :40).
- Doyle et al 2005 conflicts with the strong, universal reading of the 2000 result. The conflict is handled by returning to the census: Albert et al study a preferential-attachment ensemble with hubs at the core; Doyle et al study engineered topologies with high-degree nodes at the periphery. Same atom set (degree sequence), different arrangement, different measured outcome. Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Neither paper is discarded.
- Broido and Clauset 2019 is a different method (statistical model comparison over 928 networks) and a field-adjacent check on how often the premise even holds. It corroborates the need to measure the specific network rather than assume the degree law.
- May 1972 is the lowest-overlap source (ecology and random matrix mathematics). It corroborates the structural point that more connectance can be destabilising, which is the opposite direction from the naive "more links means more stable" reading.
- Pastor-Satorras and Vespignani 2001 supplies the dynamics loop: the same hub structure that improves tolerance to random removal removes the epidemic threshold.

No world source overrules a figure. A conflict sends the derivation back to the domain census, which is what happened with the Albert/Doyle pair.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full figure set before a recipient has named their own anchor sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and its refuter.
- fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Seat 4 must not hold the interiors. A reader who has absorbed the whole framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"* Ask what they would expect to see if the valence claim were true before giving them the articles.
- fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Do not ask for agreement.
- fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Do not select recipients for likely agreement.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its three refuters; (2) ask what measurement would count as a failure in their network; (3) record their verdict before any theory is shown; (4) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (5) only if asked, the formula as a method to reuse with their own refuter and mode.

## The two-stage offer

**Stage 1.** Send the residual in effect terms, the shape claim and the pre-registered refuter. Ask what they would count as a failure. Record the answer before showing theory. This is fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the three source links above. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because once they have seen the framework they are no longer an outside frame (fig 2 :40).

**Stage 3, on request.** Offer the formula as a generator. A frame cannot check the direction of its own question, so each user must write down what would refute their result before running it and label the mode of what they get (`THE_FORMULA.md`, "Direction"; Seat map :287).

**Hard precondition.** Stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 shape claim is stated with an anchor. As currently drafted, "stability" without a measurement is the defect that stalls the question. Name percolation, cascade die-out, synchrony or epidemic prevalence before sending.
