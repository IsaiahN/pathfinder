## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **A room is not an anchor.** Fig 7 :35, *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* A list of receptive readers raises the vocabulary, not the ground.
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** Seat Map :163, *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* Selecting recipients for likely agreement manufactures the very correlation that makes their agreement worthless. Note the deliberate contrast with this question: the norm itself is a convention, so agreement among community members is *"the product, not a corruption of it"* (Fig 2 :41). That is a claim about the object of study, not a licence to select agreeing reviewers.
3. **Asking for trust before checking spends independence.** Fig 10 :42, *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* The first contact carries the residual and the refuter, never the framework's interiors.

Legally: unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in institutional channels avoid that failure on top of the structural one.
