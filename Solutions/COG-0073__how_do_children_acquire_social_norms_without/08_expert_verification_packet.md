# Expert verification packet  -  the Tether on implicit norm acquisition

**What this is:** a shape claim in a form a researcher in social development and norm acquisition can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the low-overlap-frames prediction, `gap` on the mechanism name. Not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The absence of explicit instruction is the required shape of transmission across a generation, not a hole in the account. What crosses into the child is a head start, not a recording of a particular success: a seed carries no small tree, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes. The norm is grown in an enumerated habitat of caregivers, siblings, peers, artifacts and institutions; capability is a property of agent and habitat, never of the child alone. The child ranges its per-slot behaviour against a convention that is constitutively about shared meaning, so agreement is the product here, not a corrupted verdict. Caregiver, peer and institution must be low-overlap frames for the child to gain a surplus. The framework does not name the cognitive mechanism that carries the head start; it returns a shape, not a name. What it offers is that shape, refuters written in advance, and the tests that would close the open slot.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1  -  framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 4 and 11
- **Statement:** what crosses a generation into a child is a head start, and norm capability is agent-and-habitat, not child alone.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :22  -  **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** Fig 4 :34  -  **"A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."** Fig 11 :39  -  **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"**; Fig 11 :43  -  **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**
- **Refuter:** a child acquires a norm-guided behaviour in a novel case with no change in habitat contact, from an intact explicit rule alone; or identical contact yields different capability for reasons internal to the child and not altering contact. Either breaks the shape.

### C2  -  convention and per-slot structure; checkable by reading Figures 2 and 1
- **Statement:** a norm is settled by the community's practice, which does not move because of what the child thinks; agreement about shared meaning is the product; behaviour is tracked per slot, not averaged.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :41  -  **"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."** Fig 2 :36  -  **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."** Fig 2 :45  -  **"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."** Fig 1 :19  -  **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
- **What to check:** does the community's practice in your populations actually behave like an anchor for these norm slots, and do per-context measurements show residues that a global score would hide?
- **Refuter:** the practice changes because of the child's own judgement, so the anchor updates and the group drifts coherently; or a global norm index predicts every context with no residue. Either breaks this sub-claim.

### C3  -  the cross-frame prediction; checkable against your data
- **Statement:** caregiver, peer and institution must have different closures. A single frame cannot supply the full novel-context repertoire.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :31  -  **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."** Fig 8 :48  -  **"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."** Fig 6 :23  -  **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**
- **Refuter:** a child isolated from peers and institutions, receiving one consistent caregiver message, acquires the full novel-context repertoire. That would falsify the prediction. Note the provenance limit: if the frames merely repeat one lesson, they are one pool under several names and no surplus is expected (Fig 8 :49).

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that children are blank slates. The framework's author writes *"The child is a lineage."*
- Not that explicit teaching never occurs. A rule *"makes the bug statable"* (Fig 10 :17), but *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"* (Fig 10 :18).
- Not that any cognitive mechanism is the answer. "Imitation," "theory of mind," "natural pedagogy" and "norm psychology" are bindings from the science frame. Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."**
- Not that all cultures follow one route. The shape is structural; its content and the balance of observation, participation and teaching vary by population.
- Not a final theory and not `grounded`. No measurement in this packet settles it.

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Contact test:** hold child and head start fixed, change only habitat contact; capability should move (Fig 11 :40).
2. **Recording test:** explicit rule, no contact change, novel case; predicted failure (Fig 4 :22-26).
3. **Single-frame test:** isolate one frame; predicted failure of the full repertoire (Fig 2 :31, Fig 8 :48).
4. **Anchor test:** make community practice respond to the child's judgement; if it moves, it was not an anchor (Fig 2 :33-34).
5. **Mechanism test:** from *The Hard Problem is About Source*, an instrument that *"is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source"* is the problem; *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* A source-discriminating instrument would close the mechanism `gap`.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 4 :22 and :34, Fig 11 :39 and :43, Fig 2 :36 and :41, Fig 1 :19, Fig 2 :31, Fig 8 :48. Eight short passages.
2. Check C2 against your own data: do per-context residuals survive a global average?

4. Name one instrument that would separate the candidate mechanisms while holding contact fixed, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
