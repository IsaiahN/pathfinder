## 0. The residual in effect terms

A child begins without the ambient regularities of turn-taking, sharing, personal space, politeness, reciprocity and obligation. Later the child's behaviour matches them. No rule sentence was handed over as the cause of the change. The gap is between the child's current behaviour and the community's practice, indexed per slot (each norm, each context, each person), and the question asks what closes it.

What an answer would look like: a transmission mechanism, stated in effect terms, that (a) takes a regularity from the social environment into the child's behaviour, (b) works without an explicit teaching signal, (c) is observable or discriminable in principle, and (d) names what would have to be present for the child to acquire a norm it has never been told. Bound as an ordinary term per the Formula's "Direction": **"a norm-acquisition mechanism is a contact path that makes the next child's search for how to behave here cheaper without replaying a particular success."** That binding is what the loop can check.

Two traps are refused at the outset. First, "without explicit instruction" is not a missing datum to be filled with an invented module. Second, "how" at the level of a named cognitive mechanism (imitation, theory of mind, natural pedagogy, norm psychology, reinforcement) is not something the figures can deliver. They return a shape, not a name. The name is a binding from the developmental-science frame and is provenanced as such.

## 3. Findings

### F1  -  "Without explicit instruction" is the required transmission shape, not a defect (decisive, `general`)

Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** Fig 4 :23-24: **"upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."** and **"downward: something that makes the next search cheaper. Never a replay of what worked once."**

A norm handed over as a rule to be replayed is a recording of one occasion. Fig 4 :25-26: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion. Replayed downward, it produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one."* So a child who only replays an explicit rule would not acquire a norm in the sense the question asks: a norm that works in new situations. What crosses into the child is a head start. Fig 4 :34-35: **"A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."**

This is the first-order answer to the question. The absence of explicit instruction is what a head start looks like from the receiving side. Triangulated: Fig 4 (membrane rule) and Fig 11 (capability as agent-and-habitat, §F3) are low-overlap figures, one about scale crossing, one about field of contact. **Refuter:** a norm installed as an intact recording, with no change in the child's habitat contact, produces novel norm-guided behaviour in a situation the rule did not cover.

### F2  -  The norm is grown in a habitat, not installed in an isolated child (`general`)

Fig 11 :39: **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."** Fig 11 :40: **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."** Fig 11 :43-44: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."** and **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**

So the correct form of "how" is an enumeration of contact: who acts on the child, what conditions hold, what relations carry influence. Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* A caregiver, a peer or a school rule is a benefactor or malfactor relative to the particular norm slot, never by standing list. Fig 11 :61: **"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."** Caregivers can add actors, models and experiences. They cannot subtract the habitat, and *"the ground still decides"* (Fig 11 :22). This is the maintained mode of Fig 11, not the unmaintained one: a seat acts, but *"nothing chooses"* about what the ground keeps.

World corroboration (secondary, low overlap with the framework): Legare 2019, *"Children inhabit cultural ecologies that consist of group-specific knowledge, practices, and technologies that are inherited and modified over generations."* Legare 2017: *"Children experience enculturation from infancy through their interaction with caregivers, artifacts, and cultural institutions."* Triangulated for the shape (Fig 11 plus two independent reviews); the specific actor list is single-source per study. **Refuter:** identical contact producing different norm capability with no child-internal change attributable to contact would break the capability claim, exactly the forward test Fig 11 :72 names.

### F3  -  Agreement about a norm is the product, not a corrupted verdict (`general`)

Fig 2 :36: **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."** Fig 2 :41: **"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."** Fig 2 :45: **"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."**

This distinction decides the whole reading. For a claim about the world, shared evidence disqualifies agreement (Fig 2 :40). For a convention, shared meaning is the thing being acquired. Children acquire the norm by ranging their behaviour against the community's practice, which does not move because of what the child thinks. Fig 2 :37 makes the same move for persons: *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."*

World corroboration: Legare 2017: *"Cues to conventionality increase imitative fidelity. One is causal opacity (i.e., lack of a physical causal mechanism). A second is consensus (i.e., multiple actors performing the same actions). A third is synchrony (i.e., multiple actors performing the same actions at the same time)."* Aplin et al. 2015 (animal behaviour, very low overlap): *"In human societies, cultural norms arise when behaviours are transmitted through social networks via high-fidelity social learning."* and the study demonstrated *"a strong effect of social conformity, with individuals disproportionately adopting the most frequent local variant when first acquiring an innovation, and continuing to favour social information over personal information."* Triangulated across Fig 2, a developmental review and a wild-bird experiment. **Refuter:** a convention whose content changes when one child ranges against it would cease to be an anchor and become panel 1 of Fig 2 (coherent drift).

### F4  -  Variation inside the child, selection by the habitat (`general`, with a `specified` prediction)

Fig 5 :46: **"When nothing is scoring, the drive manufactures a gradient."** Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."** Fig 7 :33: *"→ variation inside, selection between"*

The child's exploratory behaviour supplies variation; the social ground selects which variants persist. This is why the question cannot be answered by pointing at a single imitation event. World corroboration: Legare 2019: *"Children are characteristically curious"* and *"The motivation to understand unexpected outcomes guides children's exploratory play"*; also *"Children often overimitate or copy actions that are not causally relevant to achieving a goal."* Both exploration and high-fidelity copying are in the same review, so they are one evidence pool, not two witnesses. Legare 2017: *"High-fidelity imitation also may function as a reinclusion behavior in reaction to the threat of social exclusion from an in-group in childhood."* **Refuter:** a norm acquired with no behavioural variation and no social selection, a single one-shot imprinting that needs no maintenance, would break this finding.

### F5  -  Per-slot, never averaged; the conditions form a union (`general`)

Fig 1 :19 and :22 (above). Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** Fig 9 :58: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*

Family norms, peer norms and institutional rules overlap. A child navigating them is not solving one bigger problem; it is in a compound with its own loop. World corroboration: Legare 2019: *"Children's engagement in observational learning differs dramatically across cultures"*; *"Children have early-developing norm psychology... that guides their attention to group expectations for beliefs and behavior."* So the child has biases (homophily, consensus), but their content is set by the local habitat. **Refuter:** a single global "norm acquisition" score that predicted behaviour in every context would contradict the per-slot rule and the observed cross-cultural variation.

### F6  -  Multiple low-overlap frames are required; one teacher is not enough (`general`)

Fig 2 :31: **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."** Fig 8 :48: **"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."** Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**

Caregiver, sibling, peer group and institution are frames with different closures. The child moves its wall by contact with them. If they all draw on one evidence pool (the same lesson, the same rule sentence), they are one frame wearing several names, and the child gains no surplus. This is a structural prediction, not a claim that any particular child has been studied with independent frames. **Refuter:** a single frame, perfectly isolated from peers and institutions, producing the full novel-context norm repertoire would break the triangulation requirement. The exemplar's own caution applies: convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents, so provenance, not content, decides (Fig 8 :50-52).

### F7  -  Explicit rules make the norm statable, not installed (`general`)

Fig 10 :16-18: **"the convention does not fix the bug / it makes the bug statable"** and **"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"**. An explicit rule is a convention that gives the child a term for a regularity. It does not by itself ground the regularity in behaviour. Fig 10 :46: *"A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do."*

World corroboration: Legare 2017: *"Direct active teaching is such a normative feature of cultural transmission in WEIRD populations that some are calling for limiting its use because of its potentially detrimental effects on self-directed discovery and exploration."* Paradise and Rogoff 2009: *"We try to correct the frequent misconception that this way of learning is essentially a nonverbal process by showing that speech is commonly used, but judiciously, in support of efficient communication rather than for 'lessons.'"* So the framework does not deny that speech occurs; it denies that a declared rule is the ground. **Refuter:** a rule sentence that, with no contact change, produced correct norm behaviour in an unstated novel case would collapse the convention/ground distinction.

### F8  -  The mechanism's name is a `gap`, not an unreachable silence

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**. Figures README: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*. Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."*

No figure line selects imitation over theory of mind over natural pedagogy over reinforcement. The world literature disagrees among itself: Csibra and Gergely 2009 propose *"natural pedagogy"* that *"enables fast and efficient social learning of cognitively opaque cultural knowledge that would be hard to acquire relying on purely observational learning mechanisms alone"*; Heyes 2015 replies that *"We engage, not in natural pedagogy, but in cultural pedagogy."* A conflict sends us back to the census, never to a verdict. Both are bindings from the developmental-science frame, to be provenanced (Fig 8 :50-52). The honest label for "which named mechanism" is `gap`. **Refuter:** a figure line that actually names a mechanism for norm transmission would close this gap; until one is quoted, the name stays out.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per Seat Map :287, *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A norm can be installed as an intact explicit recording, with no change in habitat contact, and the child then behaves correctly in a novel case the rule never covered. Breaks F1 (Fig 4 :22-26).
2. Capability is shown to be a property of the child alone: identical contact, different norm outcomes accounted for only by an internal change that did not alter contact. Breaks F2 (Fig 11 :39-40). This is the forward test Fig 11 :72 leaves open.
3. A single frame, isolated from peers and institutions, produces the full novel-context norm repertoire. Breaks F6 (Fig 2 :31, Fig 8 :48-49).
4. The community's practice changes because of what the child thinks, so the purported anchor updates and the group drifts coherently. Breaks F3 (Fig 2 :33-34).
5. A global norm score predicts behaviour across all contexts without residue. Breaks F5 (Fig 1 :19, :22).
6. A figure line is quoted that names a specific cognitive mechanism for norm transmission. Would close F8; until then the mechanism stays `gap`.

If any fires, the shape claim is withdrawn or reduced. Until one is run, no part may be labelled `grounded`.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives. A norm reaches a child as a head start across a membrane, is grown by contact with an enumerated habitat, is corrected against a convention that is constitutively about shared meaning, is tracked per slot, and is selected from variation inside the child. The framework does not name the cognitive mechanism that carries the head start, and the world literature's competing names are bindings, not figure lines. Status: `general` and `specified`, ungrounded, candidate, with `gap` on the mechanism name. Strains after re-census: none hidden; the one genuine conflict (innate pedagogy) is recorded, not resolved.
