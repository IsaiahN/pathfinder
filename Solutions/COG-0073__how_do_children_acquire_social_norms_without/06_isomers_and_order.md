# Bonds, cycles and order in norm acquisition

## RESIDUAL

The main audit establishes the shape: a head start crosses a membrane, is grown in an enumerated habitat, and is corrected against a convention. Three supporting questions remain. (1) Which bond joins the head start to the resulting capability? (2) What structure makes a norm persist through being used? (3) In what order do the overlapping conditions of family, peers and institutions compose, and what does the rule sentence actually buy?

## DERIVATION

### Q1  -  Production, not sequence

- Operators table :35-39: **"production. A produces B; B did not exist before"**, with the test **"is there a B before A fires? No."** Fig 12 :77: *"A produces B; B did not exist before."*
- The specific convention does not exist in the child before the head start meets the habitat. So `head start ⇒ norm-guided behaviour` is a production bond, not a mere `→` sequence.
- Operators table :29-33 defines sequence as **"A then B; B needs A to have happened"**, test *"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."* Sequence would leave open whether B was already present, which is exactly the blank-slate ambiguity.
- Fig 12 :111-113: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."** *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* A list of influences (parents, peers, schools) names ingredients; it does not name the route. The bond carries the information the list does not.
- **Strain, named:** children show early, broadly distributed norm psychology (Legare 2019: *"Children have early-developing norm psychology... that guides their attention to group expectations for beliefs and behavior"*). So the *generic* capacity precedes specific contact. The production claim applies to the **specific convention**, not to the generic bias. Different objects, re-census, no correction to the figures. Corroboration here is single-source (one review), `specified`.

### Q2  -  The norm is a catalyst and a cycle

- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**
- Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**
- A norm survives being enacted. Each enactment re-enters the child's loop as a condition on the next act, which is why the convention is maintained without a fresh rule each time. This is the structural reason "acquired" does not mean "stored once": a stored recording does not regenerate.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** a norm that is consumed by its own use, a single instruction that guides exactly one act and leaves no standing expectation, is not a catalyst and breaks this reading. Equally, a norm that does not re-enter as input to the next act is a chain, not a cycle.

### Q3  -  Union of conditions, per-slot order

- Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** Fig 9 :58: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."**
- Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**
- So the correct order is: enumerate slots first (which norm, which context, which person), then check which conditions overlap, then compose the compound. A child navigating family and peer expectations is not solving a larger single problem.
- **Falsifier:** a global "norm acquisition" index that predicted every context without residue would break both lines. The cross-cultural variation documented in Legare 2019 (*"Children's engagement in observational learning differs dramatically across cultures"*) is corroboration from a different field, but it is not a figure line and does not decide the verdict.

### Q4  -  Two currencies: the rule is cheap to state, the contact is expensive

- Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."** Fig 12 :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**
- Fig 12 :127-128: **"They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them"** and *"It proposes arrangements it cannot afford to test, and the budget is what decides how much of the space is reached at all."*
- An explicit rule is a short description: cheap in bits. Whether the norm holds in a new situation must be found out through contact: paid in actions. Conflating the two is the error of thinking a stated rule installed the norm. This is the structural counterpart of F7 in `01`.
- Fig 12 :129-130: **"The two readings come apart in both directions."** A short rule over a costly habitat is cheap to state and may take a whole budget to settle. So a rule sentence can be perfectly correct and still not be the mechanism.
- **Falsifier:** a rule whose statement alone paid for the ground test, with no contact actions, would collapse the two currencies and break this derivation.

## CONCLUSION

The head start produces the specific convention (⇒), it does not merely precede it (→). The convention persists as a catalyst and re-enters as a cycle. The conditions form a union of overlapping slots, never one averaged problem. The explicit rule is cheap in bits while the contact that settles it is expensive in actions, which is why the rule makes the norm statable without installing it.

This framing keeps the part of the common account that survives (contact, participation, parallel influences) without the part that fails (a named mechanism, a single global score, a replayed rule as the cause). "Imitation is the mechanism" remains a binding from the science frame, not a figure result. "The head start produces the convention in a habitat" is the checkable structural claim.
