# The Tether's answer: what observations could distinguish a bounce or cyclic model from standard cosmology

**Mode: `specified` on the channel list, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a measurement. It is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 8), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Standard inflationary cosmology and bounce / cyclic cosmology both reproduce the observed near scale-invariant, adiabatic scalar spectrum, successful nucleosynthesis, and an expanding universe today. The residual is not "which story is right". It is: at which observational slots do the two prediction functions separate, and at which of those does an instrument already return a reading that fails to resolve the difference? The answer is a finite, ranked, unbundled list with a losing condition on each entry. The list is a union of tests over a partition of models, not one smoking gun (Fig 9 :57 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*; Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*).

## 2. The shape

1. **One spectrum, several histories.** Fig 13 :121 *"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."* Both families produce the same phenomenological atom, so the scalar spectrum is a **functional group** (Fig 12 :123 *"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member"*), not a discriminator. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument.

2. **A candidate discriminant must already return something.** Fig 6 :32 *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* Fig 6 :35 *"Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."* A hypothetical sensor is not an observation. B-mode bandpowers, CMB bispectra, pulsar-timing correlations and interferometer strain limits are.

3. **The channels, as an import with provenance.** From the physics frame (world sources, secondary, each with its own falsifier in `01` F5):
   - **Primordial tensors / CMB B-modes.** Battefeld and Peter: *"If primordial gravitational waves are observed, certain bouncing cosmologies, such as the cyclic scenario, are in trouble, while others remain valid."* BK18: `r_0.05 < 0.036` (95 percent CL). A detection would damage some cyclic models, not all bounce models.
   - **Primordial non-Gaussianity.** Battefeld and Peter report large `f_NL` as a side effect of fine tuning in many bounces; Planck 2019 reports `f_NL^local = -0.9 ± 5.1` with no detection; Delgado et al. predict a low-`ℓ` bispectrum with signal-to-noise above 10 for LQC-motivated bounces. These are different models and different statistics and are kept separate.
   - **Tilt, running and features.** A blue tilt is common but not universal in bounce models; Ijjas and Steinhardt argue ekpyrotic models fit the observed red tilt.
   - **Stochastic gravitational-wave background across frequency.** NANOGrav reports Hellings-Downs correlations consistent with supermassive black-hole binaries, with cosmological sources not excluded. The spectral shape across PTA, LISA and ground bands is a live slot.
   - **Parity-odd correlations and features**, constrained but not detected by Planck 2019.
   - **Shear / anisotropy**, named as a bounce-model problem by Battefeld and Peter.
   - **BBN and expansion history**, a consistency constraint with low discriminating power.
   No channel is decisive on its own, and none is figure-derived. They are imported names, debited against independence (Fig 6 :24).

4. **The search order is fixed.** SEARCHED first (the scalar spectrum has already been searched; more of the same fit adds no atom, Fig 8 :32), then INWARD (extend instruments already returning something), then OUTWARD (import an interpretation with a source gate and a shadow test). INWARD and OUTWARD are never read against each other (Fig 6 :31).

5. **Null is not equivalence.** Fig 8 :39 *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* If every channel returns null at the current instrument set, the honest report is that the distinction is below the present **union horizon** (*How Intelligence Actually Works*), not that the histories are identical. The canonical README: *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not which cosmology is true. No figure line decides a cosmological history, and no measurement was taken here.
- Not that tensors, `f_NL`, or any other channel is a universal discriminant. Each is model-dependent inside the bounce family.
- Not a theory of everything, and not a new cosmological model.
- Not `grounded`. A source-discriminating measurement would be required.
- Not that the framework names the decisive observation. It returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32).
- Not that "cycle" in Fig 12 settles a cyclic universe. That is a composition bond, used here only as an analogy (`06` Q2).

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A trace that exists, read by an instrument that can see it. From *How Intelligence Actually Works*: the diamond case, where *"isotope ratios genuinely differ, so if you have a tool that can perceive the trace, you regain distinction."* The corresponding questions:

1. **Tensor channel:** does a primordial B-mode signal appear at a level cyclic models cannot produce? A detection at the forecast reach of LiteBIRD / CMB-S4 / Simons Observatory would fire this slot.
2. **Bispectrum channel:** does a local or low-`ℓ` shape with the predicted amplitude and scale dependence appear, and is it attributable to a bounce rather than to foregrounds or another mechanism?
3. **Background channel:** can component separation identify a primordial spectral component in the PTA to ground-band stochastic background, or show the signal is entirely astrophysical?
4. **Feature / parity channel:** do any of the constrained feature or parity-odd templates produce a detection?
5. **Standing falsifiers from `01` section 4:** one statistic that closes all slots; a channel with no instrument; a unique determination of the mechanism by the scalar spectrum; a null reported as equivalence.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not pick a winner between a cosmic bounce and standard inflation. It says that both families reproduce the same scalar spectrum, so that spectrum is a shared functional group and cannot discriminate. What could discriminate is a per-slot difference where an instrument already returns a reading that fails to resolve: primordial B-modes, the bispectrum, the tilt and features, a stochastic gravitational-wave background across frequencies, shear, and nucleosynthesis consistency. Those names come from the published literature, not from the framework; each is model-dependent and carries its own failure condition. The search order is to re-search what is already in hand, then extend instruments already returning something, then import an interpretation from another frame with its provenance recorded. A null result places the distinction below the current union horizon of our instruments; it is not proof the histories are identical. What the framework offers is the shape a valid answer must have and the falsifiers written in advance. The ground settles the rest.
