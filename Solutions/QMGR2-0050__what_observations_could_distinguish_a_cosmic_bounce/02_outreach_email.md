# Outreach draft: check the shape of the discriminant before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the channel list in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 3 (F5) has been checked by a working cosmologist. The email below records a shape and its refuter; it does not assert that any listed channel actually separates the two model families.

---

**Subject:** One structural claim about bounce versus inflation tests, with the failure condition first

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with a preferred cosmology. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** standard inflationary cosmology and bounce / cyclic cosmology both reproduce a red, nearly scale-invariant, adiabatic scalar spectrum. That shared success is a case of one reading with several possible sources: the instrument reads the spectrum and cannot say which history produced it. So the question "what observations could distinguish them" is not answered by another fit to the scalar spectrum. It is answered by finding the slots where the two prediction functions actually separate *and* where an instrument already returns a reading that fails to resolve the difference.
>
> The shape of a valid answer, written down before any search:
>
> 1. a finite, ranked list of observational channels, not one aggregate goodness-of-fit;
> 2. each channel must have an instrument that has already returned something at some resolution;
> 3. each channel carries a losing condition stated in advance;
> 4. a null result is reported as indiscernibility at a stated instrument set, never as proof the models are identical.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular channel on my current list is a decisive test. The names come from the published literature, not from the framework, and I record them as imports with provenance: primordial B-modes / the tensor-to-scalar ratio, primordial non-Gaussianity and bispectrum shape, the tilt and small-scale features, a stochastic gravitational-wave background across PTA and interferometer bands, shear, and BBN consistency. Each is model-dependent inside the bounce family. I am not claiming the framework names the winner, and I am not claiming a measurement settles anything here.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. one observable that closes the residual across all channels at once, so the per-slot structure disappears;
> 2. a listed channel for which no instrument has ever returned a reading at any resolution, or whose reading is already fully resolved for both models;
> 3. a proof that the scalar power spectrum uniquely determines the early-universe mechanism;
> 4. a null search reported as observational equivalence.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will narrow or retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the source links are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: `https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md`
> - Independent review of bounce models and their observational tests: `https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.2790`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
