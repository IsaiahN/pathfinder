# Outreach draft: check the boundary shape before you decide whether the framing is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the per-slot table and the pre-registered refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §5 are attached. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about when sustainability strategy pays, and where it does not

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** "does sustainability strategy improve business performance" is the wrong single question. The residual has to be indexed per firm and per impact slot, never averaged into one score (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22). On that reading, the boundary condition has two regimes:
>
> 1. **It improves performance** where the action changes contact with a ground that actually pays, a customer, an investor, a regulator, and the per-slot residuals fall against a market anchor that does not update on the firm's own report (Fig 2 :29).
> 2. **It involves a real trade-off** where a positive residual persists and does not trend down in a slot the market does not price, often a supplier, a region, or a later date. Formula step 2 calls that signature *"the residual that PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN."* A single composite rating aggregates that signal away.
>
> Two further checks follow from the same derivation. Disclosure moves the channel, not the ground: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"* (Fig 10 :24), so a better report is not evidence of a smaller impact. And a boundary dispute, Scope 1 versus 3, offsets versus reductions, this horizon versus that one, is a broken objective link, not a measurement link (Fig 3 :19, :27). More reporting cannot rescue it.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I can name which practice wins, or the numerical threshold at which an improvement counts. The framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32). I am also not claiming that any strategy is a quantity of virtue: two strategies with the same atoms and different bonds are isomers (Fig 12 :117), and the bit-cost of a report and the action-cost of a physical change cannot be added into one ROI (Fig 12 :125-130).
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a disclosure-only change followed by a ground-verified persistent fall in realized impact and return;
> 2. a firm whose paid residual falls while a supplier or regional residual rises, with no slot carrying a positive residual that persists;
> 3. a market anchor that moves when the report changes but cash flows and impact do not;
> 4. a figure re-census that names the winning practice or makes the two currencies commensurable.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. A failure is a result. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the materials are below. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Figures and formula, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Worked narrative of the six-term derivation: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-intelligence-actually-works
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** run the formula on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The source essay warns that outputs *"could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula"*, so each user needs their own ground and their own refuter. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - [name]
