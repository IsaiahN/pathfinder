# Isomers, catalysts, cycles and order: the supporting derivation for BUS-0092

**Mode: `specified` on the business applications, `general` on the structural rules.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks when sustainability strategy pays and when it involves a trade-off. A secondary residual remains after `01`: *what kind of thing is a strategy form, and in what order do its parts compose?* If two firms hold the same resources and differ only in arrangement, are they degrees of one thing or different things? And which link of the chain must be settled before the others?

## DOMAIN CENSUS (reused from `01` §2)

Atoms held: the six substrate terms (space, time, energy as states; spectrum, gradient, network as structures) plus the business-frame atoms read off the world: capital, labour, technology, suppliers, a paying market, a reporting channel. Bonds held: the seven, including `→` sequence, `⋛` comparison, and `−` subtraction; plus negation. No new primitive is available without import. Anchor: a market that does not update on the report (D2) and a physical/social ground that does not decay (D3).

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are two sustainability strategies isomers?

- Fig 12 :117, verbatim: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :94, verbatim: **"The arrangement is the substance."**
- Fig 12 :14-16: **"the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not / nothing downstream can tell them apart, / and nothing signals that anything was lost"**

Two configurations:

- **Integrated:** the sustainability action is bonded into the operating loop by production (`⇒`) and sequence (`→`): a process change alters the input, the cost and the output together.
- **Bolt-on:** the same atoms are bonded to the reporting channel by conjunction (`+`): a budget, a report and an offset purchase are added beside the unchanged operating loop.

Under Fig 12 they share atoms and differ in bond, so they are **isomers**, not quantities of virtue. A composite score records the ingredients and not the bond, which is exactly the flat-formula failure. **Falsifier, written before the run:** if the two configurations can be ranked by their ingredient lists alone, or if changing the bond produces no behavioural difference on any ground, the isomer claim fails.

### Q2 - Correction: naming the bond is not inventing a seventh atom

- The README's rule (opened 2026-11-22): *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* The subject is a **missing** primitive.
- "Integrated" and "bolt-on" are bindings from the business frame onto atoms already held. Neither adds a sixth or seventh term.
- Fig 13 :115, verbatim: **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."**
- ⇒ Naming the bond is legal and needed; naming a new primitive to hold it is not. **I overstated the "shape, not a name" rule in `05` §3 as applied to held atoms; corrected there and here.** Triangulated (README + Fig 13 + Fig 12 :94).

### Q3 - Catalyst: which parts survive their own use?

- Fig 12 :119, verbatim: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**
- A regenerative practice (a soil-restoration contract, a repair loop whose operator is paid from the savings) survives its own use and can be composed again. A one-off offset purchase is spent.
- The composite score records the spend, not whether the part is spent or regenerating, so *"nothing records which a term is."* The world source *Circular economy* reports that *"Less than 10% of economic activity worldwide in 2022 and 2023 is circular"*, which is consistent with most recorded practice being spent rather than regenerating.
- **Falsifier:** a documentation scheme that reliably records spent versus regenerating parts on the ground, and shows no performance difference between them.

### Q4 - Cycle: can a closed loop close?

- Fig 12 :121, verbatim: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**
- A circular strategy requires the loop bond. A linear chain of "make, use, dispose, then buy an offset" cannot close by construction, regardless of score.
- The world source *Circular economy* states the thermodynamic critique directly: a future where *"material loops are closed, and products are recycled indefinitely is, in any practical sense, impossible."* So "closed loop" is a limiting shape, not an achieved state. This is a **live strain**, recorded in `01` §7 and `11_audit.md`: the figure's cycle bond is structural, the world's limit is physical, and neither is smoothed into the other.
- **Falsifier:** a demonstrated material loop that closes indefinitely without external input. If found, the physical limit is wrong, not the figure.

### Q5 - Functional group: what do all the paying strategies share?

- Fig 12 :123, verbatim: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Fig 9 :32, verbatim: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**
- The functional group here is **contact with a paying ground plus a per-slot residual that falls**: the group says what kind (a ground-verified reduction); the qualifier says which member (energy, water, supplier, region). The framework fixes the group and withholds the member list.
- **Falsifier:** a strategy that lowers the paid residual without changing contact with a paying ground, sustained across a cascade horizon.

### Q6 - The two currencies, again

- Fig 12 :125, verbatim: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**
- Fig 12 :126, verbatim: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**
- Fig 12 :130, verbatim: **"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it. A price in bits and a price in actions are two readings, held apart."**
- A disclosure is cheap in actions and expensive in bits; a supply-chain redesign is the reverse. The world source *Circular economy* observes both readings in one data set: higher perceived production costs (actions) and a turnover premium (a different reading again, a market return). Three readings, not one ROI.
- **Falsifier:** a common unit that prices description and action without loss.

### Q7 - Order: which link comes first?

Dependency order from Fig 3 (perception -> vocabulary -> objective -> planning -> learn and carry), with two verbatim lines:

- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
- Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

The objective link (what counts as progress: boundary, scope, horizon) is the unmeasured middle link in this question. Reporting is a link above it. Therefore the order is:

1. settle the objective: name the slots, the boundary and the horizon, with an instrument that does not update on the firm's report;
2. only then measure the residual per slot (Fig 1 :19);
3. only then choose the bond (integrated versus bolt-on, Fig 12 :117);
4. only then compute the bargain `cost(φ) + left(R,φ)` against `cost(R)`;
5. only then settle against the ground (Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."*).

Skipping step 1 is how a firm reports beautifully against a boundary that excludes the trade-off. Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*

### Q8 - Analogue from the ecology frame (low overlap)

The world source *Ecological resilience* (opened 2026-11-22) reports that *"Disturbances of sufficient magnitude or duration can profoundly affect an ecosystem and may force an ecosystem to reach a threshold beyond which a different regime of processes and structures predominates"*, and that specialisation weakens resilience because systems become *"accustomed to and dependent upon their prevailing conditions."*

Mapping, with the borrowed words kept labeled per *They're Scanning Books*: a firm optimised to one reporting regime loses the capacity to register a residual outside it; the threshold is a comparison (Fig 12 :87, *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*); the regime shift is a change of bond, not more of the same atom. This corroborates Q1 and Q5 at the mechanism level only. It is **not** a firm-level measurement and carries no verdict weight for a particular company. The syntax transfers, the semantics do not, exactly as Fig 9 :30-31 warns: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."*

## CONCLUSION

Strategy forms are **isomers** over a shared atom set, not quantities of a single virtue. Their distinguishing feature is the bond, which a composite score does not record. Some parts survive their own use (catalysts) and some are spent, and the score does not record which. A circular strategy needs a cycle bond; a chain cannot close by construction, and the physical limit keeps the loop a shape rather than an achieved state. All paying strategies share a **functional group**, ground-verified contact with a falling per-slot residual, while the member list is withheld. The bit-cost and the action-cost are held apart. The objective link is settled before the measurement link, because a chain is ordered by dependency.

A falsifier was written for each of Q1, Q3, Q4, Q5 and Q6 before the conclusions were drawn. The live strain (cycle versus thermodynamic limit) is named, not hidden.
