**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** Figures are primary and decide. ## QUESTION

When does corporate sustainability strategy improve business performance, and when does it involve real trade-offs? Restated as an effect: under what conditions does a sustainability action lower R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| for a firm-slot against a ground that pays, and under what conditions does it leave a positive residual in some slot that persists and does not trend down? R is indexed per firm and per impact slot, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19). Option A treats "improve" and "trade-off" as opposite answers to one question and seeks the average. Option B treats them as a union of two regimes with different anchors. A averages away the live signal and hands back a single score; B keeps the verdicts separate and names the discriminator that would close each regime.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Three-place relation (firm Γ, action a, ground observation o′), indexed per firm and per impact slot and ordered in time. Symmetry: "improve" is a threshold comparison, not a symmetric conjunction; progress is a subtraction (Operators table :46, :52). Scale: firm loop, market ranking, supply-chain and temporal cascade, with a lossy membrane between scales (Fig 4).
- **D1 Firm strategy loop.** Actors: managers, routines, Γ, candidate action φ. Currency: cost(φ) + left(R,φ) against cost(R), in two currencies. Anchor: a return a ground pays. Admitted.
- **D2 Market and payment habitat.** Actors: customers, investors, lenders, regulators, competitors. Currency: willingness to pay, cost of capital, license to operate. Anchor: the market, which does not update on the firm's own report (Fig 2 :29). Admitted.
- **D3 Biophysical and social ground.** Actors: physical flows, emissions, water, suppliers, communities, future generations. Currency: physical and social impact. Anchor: the ground, which does not decay (Fig 10 :24). Admitted.
- **D4 Measurement and time channel.** Actors: metrics, ratings, reports, verifiers. Currency: contact, which decays. Anchor: the ground reached through a channel that carries stale reads and pooled readings. Admitted.
- **X1 "Sustainability strategy" as a monolithic good.** Anchor: none nameable before splitting. Not a domain yet.
- **X2 "Ethical duty" as free-floating property.** Anchor: not named. Not a domain yet.

Union, not partition: D1+D2 form the paid-contact compound, D3+D4 form the reach-and-cascade compound, and the priced-impact overlap is a compound with its own loop. Fig 9 :55 says *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Dependency order: perception -> vocabulary -> objective -> planning -> learn and carry (Fig 3). The objective link (scope, boundary, horizon) is the broken middle link; reporting is a later link (Fig 3 :19, :27).

Strain test applied: the market anchor and the physical ground appeared to compete for "the" anchor. Re-census resolved it: this is a union with one anchor per domain, and Fig 2 :45 says *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* without requiring a single ground for a union. No strain after re-census. Two other strains remain live and are named in §VERDICT and in `11_audit.md`.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives, the names and numbers do not. A sustainability strategy improves performance where it changes contact with a ground that pays and the per-slot residuals fall against an anchor that does not update on the report. It involves a real trade-off where a positive residual persists and does not trend down in a slot the market does not price, often reached by a cascade beyond direct contact. Disclosure is a decaying channel, not the ground; the boundary dispute is a broken objective link that more reporting cannot rescue; strategy forms are isomers over two incommensurable currencies; and where the harm is unpriced, only an imported atom from a differing closure moves the wall. Which practice pays and the universal threshold are a declared `gap`. Mode `specified` per domain, `general` for the shape, not `grounded`. This is a simulated derivation, not a ground reading, and it cannot settle the question it reports on.

- **cost if wrong:** a union treated as one question gets one lever. A firm failing the objective link would be sold better reporting; a firm carrying an unpriced supplier residual would be told to optimise inside a closure that cannot reach it. The cost is misdirected diagnosis, bounded but real, and it is priced by Fig 2 :42, "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The avoidable part is spending an outside reader's independence by sending a score instead of a per-slot claim.
- **undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if a finding fails; a refutation is added and the reach falls because something was added rather than struck out (Fig 6 :42-43). The boundary shape, the four refuters and the provenance trail are recorded so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-64).
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A source-discriminating instrument that separates reduction from boundary shift would name the slot; a disclosure-only ground improvement, a report-sensitive market anchor, an independent-frame convergence from disjoint data, a commensurable currency, or a figure re-census that names the practice would each reverse the corresponding finding. Live strains to watch: the cycle bond versus the thermodynamic limit on closed loops, and the social ground versus the anchor rule where human judgment is partly constitutive. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
