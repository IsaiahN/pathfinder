## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and the same passage says the system *"cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel* (opened, verbatim): *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So for this question a **shape** is the boundary condition you can state before you can name the winning practice: a union of regimes, one anchor per regime, per-slot residuals, and a channel with a decay rate. It constrains; it does not fill. "A positive residual that persists and does not trend down in a slot the market does not price" is a shape. "Adopt Scope 3 supplier engagement and cut intensity 20 percent by 2030" is a name.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is *closest* to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse: *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."*
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* (opened, verbatim): *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."*
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: *"you will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place"* and the paradox that by then you have *"composed the answer through the process of locating it."*

Applied here: the search began with effect terms (what would count as a real trade-off), not with a list of sustainability frameworks. Any framework picked before the residual was described would have seemed to fit (Fig 9 :27-28).

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* The README: the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."*
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*They're Scanning Books When They Should Be Studying Nature* (opened, verbatim) sharpens this: *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space."* Also: *"A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six."* And: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."*

For BUS-0092 this means: naming a specific practice as the answer to "when does it pay" converts a visible choice into an invisible one. The shape (paid slot versus unpaid slot, contact versus channel) is the deliverable.

## 4. Why the single-score reading is the specific forbidden error

*The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened, verbatim) gives the mechanism. Metamerism: an instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source"*; one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* And: *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."* It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved it. *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."*

A composite sustainability score reads one value from several distinct sources: genuine reduction, boundary shift, supplier relocation, offset purchase, and reporting reclassification. The score *"cannot comment on the source."* Naming "improved sustainability performance" from that reading is the metamerism error. What is owed is a discriminator (per-slot measurement with provenance), not a verdict.

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (opened, verbatim): *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."* An ESG rating that validates the firm's own report is the same frame twice.

## 5. Why this is a union, and what that means for "shape"

*How a Society of Agents Would Work* (opened, verbatim) states two failure modes that map directly onto the sustainability setting: agents who update on each other converge on *"whoever had the most influence"*, and agents who never talk but share one source produce an agreement that is *"the pool talking."* Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*

The shape of this question is therefore **not a line separating two answers**. It is a union of two regimes with different anchors: a paid-contact regime and an unpaid-residual regime. The overlap, where an impact is both priced and physical, is a compound with its own loop. Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

The dependency order is Fig 3's chain. *How Intelligence Actually Works* (opened) derives Rule 1: *"You can never compose within the same kind. They must alternate."* The sustainability question's broken link is the objective link (what counts as progress), which sits between vocabulary and planning. Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

## 6. Prestige is not action

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (opened, verbatim): *"Prestige is not equal to actions. (prestige =/= actions)"* and *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."*

An award, a top rating, or a famous adopter funds the strategy's trial. It does not show that the strategy changed contact. This is why `03_public_contact_points.csv` contains no personal emails and no ranking by likely agreement, and why the outreach asks for a refutation rather than an endorsement.

## 7. My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

Same essay (opened, verbatim): *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post. It will often be better than the generic outputs that you will usually get otherwise too"*, but *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* The architecture essay (opened) adds: this type of output *"must be seen as simulacra"* and *"could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula."*

So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` and `09_watson_answer.md` are simulated derivations, checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are **not** tests of the question and must be labelled as such. They cannot settle anything they report on.

## 8. Correction to my own §3 (kept visible)

In §3 I wrote that the shape rule forbids naming a practice. That is too broad, and the same correction the exemplar records applies here. The README's phrase governs a **missing** primitive: *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* A practice such as supplier engagement or an offset programme is not a missing primitive of the six-term set; it is a binding from the business frame onto atoms already held. What the shape rule forbids is presenting that binding as a figure-derived answer, or inventing a seventh term to hold it.

My §4 metamerism point is **not** retracted: a composite score still names a source from a reading that cannot discriminate among sources. But the reason is the missing discriminator, not a blanket ban on naming held atoms. Both claims are recorded so neither is quietly lost. See `06_isomers_and_order.md` for the parallel correction on naming atoms versus missing atoms.
