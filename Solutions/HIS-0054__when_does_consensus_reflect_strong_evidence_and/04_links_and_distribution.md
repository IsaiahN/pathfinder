## Relevant wider literature, by field

- **Social psychology:** Asch's majority-pressure studies and Janis's groupthink. Both show agreement produced by influence rather than by the ground, and Janis's own prevention list is a figure-consistent remedy (independent groups).
- **Behavioural economics and networks:** information cascades. A sequence of decision makers can converge on the wrong answer even when each is reasoning from a private signal, because later actors see earlier choices but not earlier information.
- **Survey statistics:** Kish's design effect. Correlated observations have a smaller effective sample size than their raw count suggests. This is the same arithmetic Fig 2 :44 reads as a warning about shared evidence pools.
- **Sociology and history of science:** the Matthew effect and the Matilda effect. Both describe credit concentrating where standing already exists, which is the mechanism behind an authored room whose members were selected before the question was asked.
- **Philosophy of science:** the scientific-consensus entry, including the identification of consensus with convergent independent evidence and the Kuhnian warning that consensus is never purely a matter of logic or facts.

All seven world sources are outside the framework's author pool. Their agreement with the figures is therefore not the author agreeing with himself. They still do not outrank the figures, and where they merely restate a figure they count as one corroborating witness, not two.
