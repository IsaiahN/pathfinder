# Outreach draft - validate the output first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the refuter in §5 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is stated in the recipient's own words. The offer collapses on contact if the stage-1 output does not survive.

---

**Subject:** Check one discriminator before you decide whether the theory is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a question you may already have an instrument for: when a field reaches consensus, what would let you tell strong evidence from conformity or exclusion? I am not asking you to agree with anything, and I am not sending you a theory first. I am asking you to check one claim.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** agreement alone cannot discriminate. The same high agreement is produced by at least three different processes: people updating on each other with no anchor; people who never talk because they share one evidence pool; and a chain of rooms that hand the same closure along. The framework's word for the discriminator is independence, defined as how little two frames draw on the same evidence. Two instruments pointed at the same thing are two frames. Two people reading the same report are not. An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation.
>
> So the claim predicts: consensus reflects strong evidence when independent frames, each drawing on different evidence, range separately against an anchor none of them can move. It reflects conformity or exclusion when the agreement comes from a shared pool, a mirror chain, or a room whose members were selected by someone with a stake in the outcome. It also predicts that adding agreeers from the same room increases agreement and not evidence.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that consensus is worthless, that any particular historical case was wrong, or that a dissenting minority is automatically right. Agreement about a shared meaning is a convention, and conventions are efficient. The claim is only about agreement used as a verdict about the world. A convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one.
>
> **What would refute it (written before this email):**
> 1. frames drawing on the same evidence pool carrying verdict weight when the ground is reachable and independent;
> 2. adding a room from the same closure increasing evidence rather than only agreement;
> 3. a set chosen by a participant turning out not to be an authored room;
> 4. correlation fixed at origin being refreshed by use alone, with no replacement;
> 5. a single global agreement score preserving a live dissenting slot that a per-slot reading loses.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Local verbatim figure text with line numbers: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` (the relevant passages are Fig 2 :25-45, Fig 7 :12-36, Fig 8 :47-52, Fig 10 :59-81, Fig 11 :61-69).
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`.
> - The eight-step loop and its mode rule: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** run it on a case you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so a result without a pre-registered refuter is only an output contrived to fit. Report failures; they are more useful to me than successes.
>
> - [your name]
