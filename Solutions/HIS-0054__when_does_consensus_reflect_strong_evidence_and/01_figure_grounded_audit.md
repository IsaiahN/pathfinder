## 0. The question under audit

> When does consensus reflect strong evidence, and when can it reflect conformity or exclusion?
>
> Source: `Questions/history of science and knowledge.txt` line 67, section "6. Disagreement, controversy, and consensus".

Stated as an effect: we observe a community converging on one conclusion C. The same observable, agreement, is produced by two different processes. In one, C is a report from a ground that the community did not author. In the other, C is a report about the community's own influence structure. We cannot read which process ran from the agreement level alone. The residual is the missing discriminator.

---

## 1. Residual in effect terms

R is not "is the consensus true". That is a question about C, not about the process that produced C. R is: **what in the observed agreement would let a checker tell whether the frames ranged against a ground or ranged against each other?** The framework's own Eq. 1 gives the form: *"R = |Γ(b,a) − o′| per slot s"*, with *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated"* (Fig 1 :19). Here the slots are the individual frames, not the community. Collapsing them into a single approval percentage is exactly the error Fig 1 :22 names: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

The answer therefore cannot be a threshold on the number of agreeers. It must be a property of the *relation* between the frames and the ground. The figure text states the discriminator in two sentences before the question is even asked (Fig 2 :31-32): *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are. Independence is what makes agreement mean something."*

## 2. Domain census

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor. No nameable anchor means it is not yet a domain.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Arity: two conditions, evidence-driven and social-driven agreement, both capable of producing the same C. Symmetry: agreement between frames is symmetric (Fig 12 lists `+` as symmetric: *"both hold at once"*), but the frame-to-ground relation is asymmetric: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* (Fig 2 :45). Scale: first order at one judgement, second order at the room, third order at the chain of rooms and institutions.
- **Domain 1, the epistemic frame.** Actors: individual inquirers, instruments, models. Currency: residuals against observations. Anchor: a measurement, experiment, counter, or patient outcome that does not update: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* (Fig 2 :29-30). Verdict: triangulation when frames are independent, one pool otherwise.
- **Domain 2, the social dynamics frame.** Actors: community members, majority, reviewers. Currency: influence and reputation rather than residuals. Anchor: none installed, unless a ground is deliberately kept reachable. Verdict: mutual update (Fig 2 :13-15), common cause (Fig 2 :18-21), or mirror chain (Fig 7 :12-13) according to which collapse is operating. This is a real domain: the anchor is *absent* rather than unnamed, and its absence is the finding.
- **Domain 3, the institutional gatekeeping frame.** Actors: editors, funders, committees, prize panels. Currency: credit and visibility. Anchor: the ground only if the seat keeps it reachable; otherwise the institution's own reputation. Verdict: an authored room when participants are selected by a stakeholder (Fig 10 :73-74), and an invisible failure when the substitution is not recorded (Fig 11 :68-69).
- **Domain 4, the verification frame.** Actors: outside checkers. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all four domains draw on one evidence pool (the same figures, essays and web pages), so independence here is approximately zero. **This audit is internal consistency, not corroboration.**
- **Partition or union.** A union, not a partition. Evidence and conformity overlap: a community can have genuine independent confirmation on one subset and a cascade on another. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* A single lever, for example adding more agreeers, clears only the evidence-only region and leaves the intersection standing (Fig 9 :55-58).
- **Dependency order (Fig 3).** Install and keep the anchor reachable before asking what agreement means; if "what would count as a failure here" is unmeasured, any verdict is taken below the break and is *"a reading of nothing"* (Fig 3 :23). Then describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 8 :53: *"Residual first, frame second."*).

Strain test applied during the census: the literal statement that *"exclusion is not"* available to a seat (Fig 11 :62) initially strained against the obvious fact that institutions exclude people. Re-census resolves it: Fig 11 governs a seat's power over a habitat (one may introduce an actor, not subtract the habitat), whereas institutional exclusion is a substitution of habitat (Fig 11 :66) and an authored room (Fig 10 :73-74). Different objects, no strain after re-census. The correction is kept in the log in §7.

## 4. Findings

### F1 - Agreement is not the discriminator; independence plus an immovable anchor is (triangulated)

Fig 2 :25-27 gives the grounded panel: *"pinned: it does not update on A or B; both range against it; it is not a third party to the disagreement."* Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."* Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*

Fig 8 :47-49 repeats the same quantity at a different scale: *"Independence is one quantity read twice. How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus. Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."* And Fig 13 :38-39 applies it to the substrate itself: *"independence is how little two frames draw on the same evidence, and it is what makes their agreement mean anything. Six members that overlapped would be fewer members wearing six names."*

Three passages, three different objects (a dyad, a pair of frames at a union boundary, the six primitives), low overlap. Verdict: **triangulated**. Consensus reflects strong evidence when the agreeing parties draw on different evidence and each ranges separately against an anchor that neither can move. Consensus is not a headcount; it is a property of the geometry.

### F2 - Three distinct mechanisms manufacture agreement without evidence (single-source per mechanism, triangulated across the three)

Fig 2 :13-15, mutual update: *"no anchor; each updates on the others"* then *"they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence"* This is coherent drift, and Fig 2 :34 explains why nobody notices: *"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."*

Fig 2 :18-21, common cause: *"one evidence pool"*, *"they never talk, and still agree"*, *"so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other"*

Fig 7 :12-13, the mirror chain: *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not."* Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*

These are not the same mechanism and must not be merged. Mutual update requires interaction; common cause requires no interaction at all; a mirror chain requires succession. All three produce the same observable, high agreement. Verdict: each line is **single-source** for its own mechanism; the claim that agreement alone cannot discriminate among them is **triangulated** across Figs 2 and 7.

### F3 - Adding more agreeers in the same closure is motion, not progress (triangulated)

Fig 7 :36: *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."* Fig 7 :37-39: *"Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:"*, *"the bracket's fidelity across the handoff"*, *"the ground's pressure on the room"* Fig 8 :14-15: *"their union adds nothing"*, *"two names for one frame"*

The practical consequence for a historian: a consensus that grows by citing the same review, the same dataset, or the same authoritative textbook gains vocabulary, not warrant. Fig 2 :44 names the arithmetic: *"The effective-sample-size correction for correlated observations is standard in survey statistics and meta-analysis; the collapse is the same arithmetic read as a warning."* Verdict: **triangulated** across Fig 7 and Fig 8, with the statistics world source as corroboration (one witness, not two).

### F4 - Correlation has valence: what disqualifies a verdict can be efficient for a convention (triangulated)

Fig 2 :39-41, verbatim: *"Correlation has valence."* *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"* Seat Map :163: *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* Fig 2 :43 and Fig 10 :47 both state the guard: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."*

This is the exact hinge the question turns on. A community can have a perfectly good consensus about what a term means, which is a convention and should be agreed. The same agreement about what the world is like carries no verdict weight if the frames share a pool. When a convention is used to decide an outcome, it stops being a convention and becomes an authored verdict. Verdict: **triangulated** (Fig 2 + Seat Map, with Fig 10 as a third line at the institutional scale).

### F5 - Exclusion is an authored room and an invisible failure (single-source in the figures; corroborated by two world sources)

Fig 10 :73-74: *"The residual selects the members, but only from what is available. A candidate set chosen by a participant is an authored room however well the selection is reasoned, so the pool must be unauthored first and examined second."* Fig 10 :75-79: *"A conscientious selection is still a selection, so unauthored needs a mechanism:"* with the mechanisms *"a lottery"*, *"everyone who qualifies"*, *"a set assembled before the question existed"*, *"or the habitat itself, enumerated rather than composed"*

Stakes make this worse rather than better. Fig 10 :59-61: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying. Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it."* And *"standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on"* (Fig 10 :61). Fig 10 :93: *"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."*

Fig 11 :68-69 names the failure mode: *"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails; what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts."* Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* So exclusion in a scientific community is not a vacuum, it is a substituted habitat with different actors, and the substitution is invisible from inside.

Verdict: **single-source** within the figures (Fig 10 and Fig 11 are one evidence pool). The Matthew effect and Matilda effect world sources corroborate the mechanism from outside the framework, but they are history/sociology of science, so they agree with a claim the framework already makes; they do not make it two independent confirmations of the *figure* verdict.

### F6 - A single consensus number hides the slot that carries the signal (single-source)

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*

A headline such as "98% of experts agree" is a frame-internal quantity. It is produced by the same community it is used to grade. The dissenting minority may be the one live slot with positive residual mass (Fig 1 :20-21: *"Support is satisfied when some slot carries positive mass, so a global residual near zero with one live slot is a legal state and not an inert one."*). Verdict: **single-source** (Fig 1), corroborated by the effective-sample-size statistics but not derived from it.

### F7 - Consensus can be stale contact with a ground that has not moved (single-source)

Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does."* Fig 10 :25-28: *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"* and *"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"* A community can keep agreeing long after the instrument stopped resolving, because the drift is in the channel, not the ground. The remedy named in the figure is *"keep the anchor reachable"* (Fig 10 :29), not gather more agreement. Verdict: **single-source** (Fig 10).

### F8 - This question is a union compound and must be unbundled (triangulated)

Fig 9 :5: *"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* Fig 9 :54-58: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again. A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing."*

The Library of Babel essay, opened 2026-11-23, gives the same instruction in different words: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"* Verdict: **triangulated** across Fig 9 and the essay, at low overlap (one is a law about search, the other a first-person account of search). This is a method finding, not a world verdict.

### F9 - The discriminator is a source question, not an agreement question (triangulated)

*The Hard Problem is About Source*, opened 2026-11-23: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"* and *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it."* Agreement is the projection; it cannot comment on the source. Fig 8 :51-52 says why the distinction is recoverable at all: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."* Verdict: **triangulated** (essay + Fig 8, different domains), and it is the reason provenance, not headcount, is the answer's centre.

### F10 - Fixed correlation cannot be refreshed by more use; it needs replacement (single-source)

Fig 10 :80-81: *"And divergence regenerates unevenly. Frames whose independence is renewed by separate experience can be maintained; frames whose correlation is fixed at origin can only be rotated, because no amount of use makes them less alike and replacement is the only refresh."* Fig 6 :23-24: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs. Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."*

So the remedy for a captured consensus is not "more voices" but a frame whose closure differs, brought in with its provenance recorded and its independence debit accounted. Verdict: **single-source** for the refresh claim (Fig 10), triangulated with Fig 6 for the import mechanism.

### F11 - What survives: the shape

The answer the figures license is a discriminator with three tests, not a threshold:

| test | corresponds to | figure |
|---|---|---|
| Do the agreeing parties draw on different evidence? | independence | Fig 2 :31-32, Fig 8 :49 |
| Is there an anchor none of them can move, and is it reachable? | triangulation | Fig 2 :25, :33, :45; Fig 10 :29 |
| Was the pool unauthored and the provenance recorded? | no authored room, no hidden pool | Fig 10 :73-79; Fig 8 :50-52 |

If all three hold, agreement is a report from the ground. If any fails, agreement is a report about the community, and its verdict weight is zero until the failure is repaired. This is `specified`, not `grounded`: the tests are checkable, but no measurement in this simulated run has been taken.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Required before any outreach, per Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* Written here before the conclusion in §11 and before `07_the_tether_answer.md` was drafted.

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. Frames demonstrably drawing on the same evidence pool are shown to carry verdict weight when the ground is reachable and independent (breaks Fig 2 :31-32 and :40 and the Fig 8 :49 overlap law).
2. Adding a room drawn from the same closure, selected for agreement, is shown to increase evidence rather than only agreement (breaks Fig 7 :13, :35-36).
3. A candidate set chosen by a participant is shown not to be an authored room, or a convention that decides an outcome is shown to remain a convention (breaks Fig 10 :73-74 and Fig 2 :43).
4. Correlation fixed at origin is shown to be refreshable by use alone, without replacement (breaks Fig 10 :80-81).
5. A single global agreement measure is shown to preserve a live slot residual that a per-slot reading loses (breaks Fig 1 :19, :22).

If any fires, the discriminator is wrong and the answer is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Do not treat a consensus percentage as evidence. Treat it as a claim about a room and ask who selected it (F5, F6).
2. Ask the two questions first: what evidence did each party draw on, and what anchor could any of them have moved? (F1, F9).
3. Separate the three conformity mechanisms before proposing a remedy: mutual update needs a ground, common cause needs independent instruments, a mirror chain needs a different next room (F2).
4. When the room is captured, replace a frame rather than add a fifth copy; record the import and debit its independence (F10).
5. Keep the dissent per slot. A minority with a live residual is a signal, not noise (F6).
6. Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict: *"Never let a filter hand you a verdict"* (Fig 9 :60).

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is agreement a verdict?** Only if the frames are independent and range against an immovable ground (Fig 2 :31-33). Headcount is never a verdict.
2. **What produces the observed agreement?** Three branches: mutual update, common cause, mirror chain (Fig 2 :13-21, Fig 7 :12-13). They are observationally identical.
3. **Does adding members help?** Only if they differ from the last room and match the residual it surfaced (Fig 7 :36). Otherwise motion, not progress.
4. **Is the question one question?** No, a union compound (Fig 9 :54-58); unbundle before searching.
5. **Where does exclusion enter?** Through an authored room and a substituted habitat (Fig 10 :73-74, Fig 11 :66-69), visible to an outside seat only (Fig 10 :91).
6. **What repairs a captured room?** Replacement by a frame whose closure differs, debited against independence (Fig 6 :23-24, Fig 10 :80-81).
7. **What settles the discriminator?** An instrument that distinguishes the source of the reading, not further argument (source essay; Fig 8 :50-52). Status remains `candidate`.

## 8. Updated verdict

The shape survives and the headcount reading does not. Consensus reflects strong evidence when independent frames, drawing on different evidence, each range against an anchor none of them can move, from a pool that was unauthored before the question was asked, with the provenance of each import recorded. Consensus reflects conformity or exclusion when the agreement comes from mutual update with no anchor, one shared evidence pool, a chain of mirrors, an authored room, or a substituted habitat whose losses are invisible from inside. The three mechanisms are kept separate and never averaged. Verdicts per domain: D1 triangulated, D2 triangulated for the failure of agreement as a discriminator and single-source per mechanism, D3 single-source in the figures with independent world corroboration, D4 simulated and non-corroborating. Strains after re-census: **none hidden; one resolved and logged** (Fig 11 :62 versus institutional exclusion).
