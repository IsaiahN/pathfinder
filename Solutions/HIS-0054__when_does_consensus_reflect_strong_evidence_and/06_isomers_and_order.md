# Structure, order and analogues of agreement

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The agreement reading is the same in four different processes. We need a structural account of why the same reading fails to discriminate, and an account of the order in which the checks must be made. Two questions are owed: (1) what kind of bond is agreement, and (2) are the four processes isomers under Fig 12's definition?

Atoms held for this derivation: two or more frames, one conclusion C, one ground, one pool of evidence, one channel. Bonds held: the seven, including symmetric `+` (conjunction), `≡` (identity), and ordered `→` (sequence), `−` (subtraction), `⋛` (comparison), plus the unary `¬` (negation). ## DERIVATION

### Q1 - What kind of bond is agreement?

Fig 12 :55-59 lists conjunction as *"both hold at once"* with the test *"swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No."* Agreement between two parties is that bond: A and B both hold C. Swap them, nothing changes. Fig 12 :92-93: *"Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term."* A consensus count is a spelling count, not a term count.

The frame-to-ground relation is not symmetric. Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* Fig 2 :46: *"The anchor is not a third party to the disagreement and it is not the tie-breaker in a vote."*

So a vocabulary that records only the agreement bond can say that k parties hold C, and cannot say whether they hold it because they ranged against a ground or against each other. Fig 12 :111-113: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one. A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* The same applies to a vocabulary with only a symmetric bond: it can name the state (agreement) and not the route (triangulation, drift, cascade, handoff).

**Conclusion Q1:** agreement is a symmetric join. It carries no directional information about its source. The missing bond is the ordered range to the ground, which the framework supplies as triangulation (Fig 2 :35). Verdict: **triangulated** (Fig 12 bond table + Fig 2 :45-46, low overlap).

### Q2 - Are the four processes isomers?

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

The four processes, written at the level of the agreement reading:

| process | written form | bond between frames | substance |
|---|---|---|---|
| triangulation | anchor pinned; A and B both range against it | ordered range to a shared, immovable ground | evidence |
| mutual update | A updates on B, B updates on A | ordered, circular, no anchor | coherent drift toward whoever had most influence |
| common cause | A and B both read pool P; never talk | symmetric `+` through a hidden third term | the pool talking |
| mirror chain | room t+1 inherits closure of room t | ordered handoff of the same closure | motion without progress |

All four return the same formula: "N parties agree on C". Under Fig 12 :94, *"The arrangement is the substance."* The difference is carried entirely by the bond, which the agreement count does not record.

Strictness caveat, recorded rather than smoothed over: in triangulation, common cause and the mirror chain the operands are not identical (there is an anchor, a pool or a succession of rooms in some cases and not others), so these are **isomers of the agreement reading**, not isomers of a single fixed atom set. Under Fig 12 :117's definition the claim is exact only at the level of the observable formula, where the bond is indeed the entire difference. The weaker, safer claim is that they are **functional analogues**: Fig 12 :124 defines a functional group as *"a substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* The shared functional group is "agreement among parties"; the qualifier is the bond to the ground.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a symmetric agreement count is shown to carry directional information that discriminates among these four processes, or if two of them are shown to be compositionally identical (same atoms and same bond) yet yield different verdicts, the structural claim fails and the functional-group reading alone stands. Verdict: **single-source** for the isomer reading (Fig 12), with the functional-group form retained as the fallback.

### Q3 - What order do the checks run in?

Fig 3 :20-21: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."* The order for this question is:

1. Is there a fact here? Fig 9 :5: *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*
2. Is the bundle a union or a partition? Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
3. Is an anchor reachable and does it fail to update? Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."* Fig 10 :29: *"keep the anchor reachable."*
4. Are the frames independent? Fig 2 :31-32.
5. Was the pool unauthored and the provenance recorded? Fig 10 :73-79, Fig 8 :50-52.

Two ordered operators do work here. Operators table :46: *"progress is a subtraction, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot say how far along it is."* The discriminator subtracts the shared-pool component from raw agreement. Operators table :52: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one."* The verdict "is there a fact here" is a threshold comparison, not a count.

Reversing the order is the named failure. Fig 8 :53: *"Residual first, frame second."* Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* A historian who picks the social explanation before describing the disagreement will find social explanations in every case.

### Q4 - Negation, accumulation and exclusion

Fig 12 :139-140: *"Without a unary operator a system can write what a situation contains, what follows what, and how far along it is - and it cannot write that any of those is false. A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate."* Operators table :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."*

This is the structural reason exclusion matters to consensus. A process that can accumulate claims but cannot refuse one has no internal way to remove a term that has lost contact with the ground. Its only way to change is to add, and the additions are made by whoever holds the seat. Fig 11 :61-62: *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* In the habitat vocabulary one cannot subtract contact, only substitute a habitat (Fig 11 :66), and *"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"* (Fig 11 :68).

So the record of a field's dissenting positions is not decoration. It is the only negation operator the history has, and removing it leaves an accumulating library with no reach reduction. Fig 6 :42-43: *"What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall."*

**Falsifier:** if a review process is shown to reduce the reach by deleting a term from the record, rather than by adding a refusal to it, this reading fails. Verdict: **single-source** (Fig 12 amendment + Fig 11, one evidence pool), corroborated by the Matilda effect source but not derived from it.

## CONCLUSION

Agreement is a symmetric bond and therefore silent about its own source. The four processes that produce it are best read as isomers of the agreement reading, with the functional-group claim as the strict fallback. The checks must run in dependency order: split the bundle, install the anchor, test independence, then test authorship and provenance. And negation is the missing operator that makes exclusion structurally consequential: a process that can only accumulate cannot correct itself, and the seat's introductions become the field's history.
