# Expert verification packet - the Tether on consensus and evidence

**What this is:** a claim in a form a historian or philosopher of science can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No claim is made about any named historical consensus. This is a discriminator and a route, not a verdict.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Agreement is a symmetric join and therefore carries no information about its own source. Consensus reflects strong evidence when independent frames, drawing on different evidence, each range against an anchor that none of them can move, from a pool assembled before the question was asked, with each import recorded as provenance. Consensus reflects conformity when the agreement comes from mutual update with no anchor, from one shared evidence pool, or from a chain of rooms handing the same closure along: agreement compounds, evidence does not. Consensus reflects exclusion when the room was authored by someone with a stake, when standing outlives the result it earned, or when a habitat was substituted and the losses are invisible from inside. The same agreement about a shared meaning is an efficient convention; the same agreement about the world with a shared pool carries no verdict weight. The framework offers the discriminator, the tests that would close it, and refuters written in advance. It names no culprit.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 2, 8 and 13
- **Statement:** agreement has verdict weight only when the agreeing frames are independent and range against an anchor that does not update. A shared pool disqualifies a verdict about the world while remaining efficient for a convention.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :31-32 - *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are. Independence is what makes agreement mean something."* Fig 2 :33 - *"The anchor must not update."* Fig 2 :40-41 - *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool; efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."* Fig 8 :49 - *"Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."*
- **Refuter:** show frames drawing on the same evidence pool carrying verdict weight when the ground is reachable and independent; or show a convention that decides an outcome still functioning as a convention (Fig 2 :43).

### C2 - social-dynamics; checkable against any case the reader knows
- **Statement:** three different processes produce the same observable, high agreement, and adding members from the same closure increases agreement without increasing evidence.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :15 - *"they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence."* Fig 2 :21 - *"so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other."* Fig 7 :13 - *"agreement compounds, evidence does not."* Fig 7 :35 - *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 7 :36 - *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."*
- **What to check:** in a case you know, can you tell from the agreement level alone which of the three processes ran? If not, the claim stands. Can you name a case where adding agreeers from the same literature changed the ground rather than only the vote?
- **Refuter:** show a room drawn from the same closure, selected for agreement, increasing evidence rather than only agreement; or show that the three mechanisms are compositionally identical and should be merged.

### C3 - institutional; checkable against a review or funding record
- **Statement:** an authored room and a substituted habitat make exclusion invisible from inside. Standing is a stake that survives the outcome, and no intent is required for a selection to be an authored one.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :74 - *"A candidate set chosen by a participant is an authored room however well the selection is reasoned, so the pool must be unauthored first and examined second."* Fig 10 :75 - *"A conscientious selection is still a selection."* Fig 10 :61 - *"And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."* Fig 10 :93 - *"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."* Fig 11 :66 - *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Fig 11 :68 - *"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails."*
- **Refuter:** show a set chosen by a participant that is not an authored room; show a correlation fixed at origin being refreshed by use alone without replacement (Fig 10 :80-81); or show a review process that reduces the reach by deleting a term from the record rather than by adding a refusal (Fig 6 :42-43, Fig 12 :140).

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that consensus is worthless; agreement about a shared meaning is an efficient convention (Fig 2 :41).
- Not that a dissenting minority is automatically right; it is a live slot, not a verdict (Fig 1 :20-22).
- Not that any named historical consensus was conformity or exclusion. Naming a case requires a source-discriminating instrument for that case.
- Not that exclusion was a conspiracy. A conscientious selection is still a selection (Fig 10 :75).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a measurement in this simulated run.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument reading green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved that case. Here:

1. Can we show which evidence each agreeing party actually drew on? Coincident pools mean no verdict weight.
2. Is the anchor reachable and does it fail to move? A stale or pooled channel returns *"you have lost touch"*, not a verdict (Fig 10 :24-28).
3. Was the pool assembled before the question (lottery, everyone who qualifies, a pre-existing set, or the habitat enumerated), and is each import recorded (Fig 10 :75-79, Fig 8 :50-52)?
4. Can independence be refreshed by replacement from a frame whose closure differs, with the import debited (Fig 6 :23-24)?
5. Does anyone gain from which value the ground returns today (Fig 10 :59-61)?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :31-41 and Fig 7 :12-13, :35-36 - six short passages.
2. Take one consensus you know well. Can you tell from the agreement level which of the three mechanisms ran? Write down what you would need to see to tell.
3. Look at how the relevant review panel or editorial board was assembled. Was it chosen before the question existed, or by a participant (Fig 10 :73-75)?
4. Name one instrument that would distinguish shared-pool agreement from independent convergence in that case, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
