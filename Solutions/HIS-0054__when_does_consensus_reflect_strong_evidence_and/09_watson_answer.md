## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what distinguishes a consensus that reports strong evidence from one that reports conformity or exclusion. Option A: treat the agreement level as the discriminator. Option B: treat the relation between the frames and the ground as the discriminator. What each leads to: A makes a headcount a verdict and spends the independence of the people checking it; B sends a testable shape, written refuters, and an open case until a source-discriminating instrument closes it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The observed agreement on one conclusion is produced by at least four different processes. Arity: a union of conditions, not one hard question (Fig 9 :54-58). Symmetry: agreement among frames is symmetric, the frame-to-ground relation is asymmetric (Fig 2 :45). Scale: first order at a judgement, second order at the room, third order at the chain and the institution.
- **Domain 1, the epistemic frame.** Actors: inquirers, instruments, models. Currency: residuals against observations. Anchor: a measurement or experiment that does not move when a frame pushes on it (Fig 2 :29-30). Question type: triangulation versus one pool. Verdict: triangulated.
- **Domain 2, the social dynamics frame.** Actors: majority, peers, reviewers. Currency: influence. Anchor: none installed unless deliberately kept reachable. Question type: mutual update, common cause, or mirror chain. Verdict: agreement fails as a discriminator (triangulated); each mechanism single-source.
- **Domain 3, the institutional gatekeeping frame.** Actors: editors, funders, panels. Currency: credit and visibility. Anchor: the ground only if the seat keeps it reachable; otherwise institutional reputation. Question type: authored room and substituted habitat. Verdict: single-source in the figures, with independent world corroboration.
- **Domain 4, the verification frame.** Actors: outside checkers. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all four domains share one evidence pool, so independence here is near zero and this audit is internal consistency only.
- **Partition or union.** A union (Fig 9 :54-58). Evidence and conformity can both operate in the same case, so a single lever clears only the region where one condition holds.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Split the bundle; install and keep the anchor reachable; test independence; test authorship and provenance; then grade the agreement.

Strain test applied: the literal *"exclusion is not"* available to a seat (Fig 11 :62) initially strained against institutional exclusion. Re-census resolved it: Fig 11 governs a seat's power over a habitat, while institutional exclusion is habitat substitution (Fig 11 :66) and an authored room (Fig 10 :74). Different objects, so no strain after re-census. The correction is logged in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §7.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

Consensus reflects strong evidence when independent frames, drawing on different evidence, each range against an anchor none of them can move, from a pool assembled before the question was asked, with each import recorded as provenance. It reflects conformity when the agreement comes from mutual update with no anchor, one shared evidence pool, or a chain of rooms handing the same closure along; adding agreeers from that closure compounds agreement and not evidence. It reflects exclusion when the room was authored by someone with a stake, when standing survives the outcome it earned, or when a habitat was substituted and the losses are invisible from inside. The same agreement about a shared meaning is an efficient convention; the same agreement about the world with a shared pool carries no verdict weight. A consensus score is frame-internal and cannot grade the frame that produced it. This is `specified`, ungrounded, candidate. The shape is a discriminator, not a name: no historical case is settled here.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, three checkable sub-claims, verbatim lines and refuters before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is treating a headcount as a verdict and thereby giving an authored room the standing of a ground. Fig 2 :42 prices the remedy: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the discriminator and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The headcount reading is removable without touching the derivation; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove it. A captured room can be repaired by replacing a frame from a frame whose closure differs, with the import recorded and debited (Fig 6 :23-24). No primitive must be withdrawn.
- **reversed by:** a source-discriminating instrument, not agreement. An instrument that shows which evidence each agreeing party drew on would settle whether the pools coincided; a demonstration that a room selected by a participant was not authored would break Fig 10 :74; a demonstration that correlation fixed at origin is refreshable by use alone would break Fig 10 :80-81; a demonstration that a global agreement score preserves a live slot residual would break Fig 1 :19-22. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
