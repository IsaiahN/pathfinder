## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."

From *The Library of Babel*: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

So for BUS-0017 a **shape** is the form the answer must have before we are allowed to name a management fashion. The shape here is: two distinct loops with different currencies against one unmoving ground; a directed membrane that carries methods up and head starts down; per-slot residuals; a measured round-trip gap; and a named compound where both conditions bind. "Ambidextrous organisation", "lean startup", "high reliability organisation" and "continuous improvement" are all **names**. None of them is the shape, and importing one as the answer would be the error the README forbids.

The same essay gives the closest thing to a direct statement of this question's structure: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way." That is Fig 9 :4-5 in other words, and it is why F1 in `01` says split first.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary already held that is closest to the thing, then look for new terms that "overlap, correlate, or are even used interchangeably" with it. "With each new term gained, you narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary." Stop when "the finish line will also be the starting place."
- Fig 6 :30: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."
- *How Intelligence Actually Works*: "If a practitioner traverses the framework and supplies measurements at each level, then the output will eventually converge toward a perceptibility that is indiscernible." But "indiscernibility is not identity", so the lineage has to be kept.

Applied here: describe the residual without saying "exploration versus exploitation" first, because that phrase already picks the frame. Describe it as two ways of failing to predict: a settled routine whose reading drifts, and a novel slot with no term to predict it. The vocabulary "reliability loop" and "mint loop" is earned by proximity to Fig 10 and Fig 5, not borrowed from management literature in advance.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." The README: the framework "stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*They're Scanning Books* sharpens this: "The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space." And: "A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six." Also: "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."

For BUS-0017 this means that saying "the organisation should be ambidextrous" names a world frame and smuggles in its whole vocabulary. The figure-derived claim is thinner and checkable: two seats, a directed membrane, per-slot residuals, a measured gap, and a compound at the intersection. Whether an organisation calls that ambidexterity, reliability engineering or something else is a naming decision whose provenance must be recorded.

## 4. The ground and the source problem

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. An instrument reads green and "it cannot comment on the source." One green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." The case "was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."

This bears directly on F2 and F5. An uptime figure, an incident count and an experiment velocity are all projections. Several different underlying states can produce the same reading: a genuinely reliable operation, a suppressed reporting channel, a saturated metric, or a pooled average that hides one live failing slot. Naming the source ("we are reliable") from the reading is the metamerism error. What is owed is a better instrument, that is, a per-slot residual against an anchor that does not move, not a more confident reading of the same number.

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself." This is why an organisation cannot certify its own reliability with a metric it also produces (Fig 1 :27: "A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces").

## 5. Answer to the shape question

**What does "shape" mean for this question?** It means the constraints any answer must satisfy, stated before an answer is chosen:

| constraint | source |
|---|---|
| two conditions, not one objective; split before searching | Fig 9 :4-5; *Library of Babel* on bundled problems |
| two currencies that do not add | Fig 12 :125-130 |
| reliability is maintained contact with an unmoving anchor, measured per slot | Fig 10 :24, Fig 1 :19, Fig 2 :29-30 |
| experimentation is a gated mint settled by the ground, not by its own output | Fig 5 :48, formula step 5 |
| only methods cross up, only head starts cross down, with a measured gap | Fig 4 :22-30 |
| a pilot substitutes a habitat and proves wiring, not capability | Fig 11 :66-70 |
| where both conditions bind, the intersection is a compound with its own loop | Fig 9 :54-58 |
| no final settled state | Fig 13 :115-118; Fig 11 :63 |

**What licenses a name here?** Only a ground reading or a provenanced import. Neither has happened for BUS-0017. So the answer in `07` keeps the slot open: the shape says what the combination must look like, not which branded programme occupies it.

## 5b. Correction to my own §3

I initially wrote that "shape, not a name" forbids naming any organisational form at all. That overstates it. The README's phrase governs a **missing** thing, a primitive not held in Γ. Business vocabulary such as "separate units" and "senior integration" is already in the world frame; O'Reilly and Tushman (2004) hold it. The prohibition is against claiming the framework *derived* that name, or against letting the name do the work of the derivation. Convergent derivation from a separate field is explicitly allowed: Fig 8 :50-52 says "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them." *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* gives the author's own instance: of his work on polycentric governance he wrote, "My paper trail was different, and my vocabulary was different. But the structures matched." So "ambidextrous organisation" may be cited as a converging world frame, with provenance, but not asserted as the figure's answer. Both readings are recorded here so neither is quietly lost.

## 6. Two consequences for this question

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige." A well-reviewed management framework can fund a trial. It cannot settle whether the trial reduced a per-slot residual. The public venues in `03` are asked for a refutation, not an endorsement.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." And *Arrival of the Fittest*: outputs "could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula." So `01` through `11` are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are **not** tests of any organisation and must be labelled as such. They cannot settle anything they report on.

## 7. A note that bears on F5

*How a Society of Agents Would Work* warns that a shared store must use "the success rate per try, not the total," because "whatever gets tried most piles up votes." *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* makes the same point about teaching: "watch the trend in correction size, not the count of lessons given." Both are separate-stratum corroborations of Fig 5 :48, "Its own output is never a measure of success." Counts are not verdicts at any level.
