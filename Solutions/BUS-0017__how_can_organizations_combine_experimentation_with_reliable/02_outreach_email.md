# Outreach draft: check the shape before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the audit in `01` has survived the refuter in section 3. The stage-1 output must stand on its own or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about organisations that run both experiments and reliable operations

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The question is how one organisation can run experiments and also run reliable, repeatable operations. In effect terms: for each operation there is a gap between what the organisation's routines predict and what the ground returns (a customer outcome, a patient outcome, an incident, a market). Reliability fails when a settled routine's prediction drifts from the ground, or when the reading stops arriving because the metrics are saturated or pooled. Experimentation fails when a new situation arrives whose shape is not in the organisation's repertoire and no term exists to predict it.
>
> The shape claim is that these are **two conditions, not one objective to balance**. Each needs its own seat and its own currency, connected by a directed filter: reapplicable methods travel from experiments to operations, and only head starts travel back, never a recording of a particular success. Where a single unit must be both reliable and changing, that intersection is a third thing with its own loop, not a weighted average of the other two.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming that any named organisational form is the answer, that an experiment count measures success, that a falling incident count proves safety, or that a successful pilot transfers to operations. I have measured no organisation. This is a `specified` shape with a route, not a `grounded` result.
>
> **What would refute it, written down before this message:**
> 1. One process, with no separate seats and no directed handoff, that demonstrably drives per-operation error to zero on settled routines *and* mints new ground-settled terms on novel ones, in two independent organisations, against an anchor that does not move when pushed.
> 2. A controlled pilot that transfers to production capability with no enumeration of what the test setting dropped, and no measured gap across the handoff.
> 3. A global uptime or safety KPI near zero that hides a persistently live failing operation, with no degradation at the ground over a full cycle.
> 4. An experiment count or incident count that predicts ground-settled improvement above chance once shared evidence pools are controlled.
>
> If any of these holds, I retract the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **Only if the shape survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical figures README: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Figures and formula for direct download: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks for reading,
> [name]

## Stage discipline

1. **Stage 1:** the shape claim and the four refuters. Ask what they would count as a failure. **Record their verdict before showing any theory.**
2. **Stage 2, opt in only:** if they are curious, the three links, with the note that the figures are the authority.
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration. By Fig 2 :40, "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."
