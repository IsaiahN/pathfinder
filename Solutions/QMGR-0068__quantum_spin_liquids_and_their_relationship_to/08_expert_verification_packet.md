# Expert verification packet - the Tether on spin liquids and lattice gauge theories

**What this is:** a claim in a form a condensed-matter physicist, quantum-simulator experimentalist or lattice-gauge theorist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so the reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the sub-claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No measurement in this session is claimed. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The spin-liquid / lattice-gauge relation is a union of three conditions, derivation, import and settlement, not one relation. The word "relationship" is symmetric in form while the substantive claim is ordered, so identity is refused and production retained as `specified`. A gauge structure derived inside the spin closure (Kitaev honeycomb, toric code, Rydberg blockade) adds no atom to nature; importing the non-Abelian lattice-QCD apparatus into a spin system is a different move that owes a source gate and a shadow test. The gauge description is irreducible at the effective level and its bond carries pinch points, string orders and anyon statistics that the spin list does not. Ordered magnet and spin liquid are isomers over the same spins; Z2 and U(1) are a further isomer claim over a shared atom set with a stated falsifier. The union is second order: the spin phase settles before the effective gauge theory composes over it. The framework does not name the gauge group of a material, and a simulator settles its own habitat only.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 8 and 6

- **Statement:** convergent derivation and adopted import are different operators even when their contents look identical. A derivation inside the closure adds no atom; an import from a differing closure needs provenance (a source gate) and a shadow test (the gap it explains).
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :51 - "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them". Fig 6 :21 - "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Fig 6 :23 - "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 6 :25 - "An import adds no atom to the world."
- **Physics-facing check:** is the Kitaev mapping a derivation from the spin Hamiltonian (yes, per the opened abstract: "solved exactly by a reduction to free fermions in a static $\mathbb{Z}_{2}$ gauge field"), and is the use of SU(3) lattice QCD in a spin system an import from a different frame (yes, per Wiese 2013, opened)? Does the import state the gap it explains?
- **Refuter:** exhibit a case where the gauge atom is genuinely absent from the spin frame's closure and the derivation still succeeds without an import (breaks Fig 6 :21); or show an import with a stated source and a stated shadow test where the framework still refuses it.
- **Status:** `specified`, triangulated (Fig 8 provenance plus Fig 6 wall, low overlap).

### C2 - Physics-facing; checkable against the two phases

- **Statement:** over a fixed atom set `{Space, Time, Energy, Spectrum, Gradient, Network}`, a Z2 spin liquid and a U(1) spin liquid are isomers, while the ordered magnet and the spin liquid over the same spins are the stronger isomer case. The lattice geometry is the functional group and the gauge group is the qualifier.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 - "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :95 - "Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond." Fig 12 :123 - "The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." Fig 12 :94 - "The arrangement is the substance." Fig 12 :97 notes this is not an analogy: "silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different properties".
- **What to check:** can Z2 and U(1) spin liquids each be written over the same atom set, differing only in which structure mediates which state pair? Wen 2001 (opened) reports "hundreds of symmetric spin liquids, which have SU(2), U(1) or Z2 gauge structures at low energies", which is the world-level existence claim for the group.
- **Refuter, written before the run:** if either phase requires an atom the other does not (a U(1) spin liquid with no network atom, or a Z2 spin liquid with no spectrum), the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim stands.
- **Status:** `specified`, single-source on the figure definition, physics check unmeasured. Not upgraded by citing two papers from one evidence pool.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that quantum spin liquids are lattice gauge theories in the QCD sense. The SU(3) apparatus is an import, not a derivation.
- Not that any named material (herbertsmithite, RuCl3, an organic triangular salt) is a confirmed spin liquid.
- Not that the lattice or the continuum is the real object. Discreteness and smoothness are both derivatives (Fig 13 :124).
- Not that the gauge field is fundamental, and not that it is a free rename.
- Not that a simulator result transfers to a solid-state material. Fig 11 :70 - "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment in this session.
- Not a claim that the framework derived a gauge-group name. Naming is a binding from the physics frame; the correction is visible in F9 of `01`.

## 5. What would settle it

3. Is there a measurable round-trip gap between the spin description and the gauge description? Nonzero keeps the production arrow; zero permits identity.
