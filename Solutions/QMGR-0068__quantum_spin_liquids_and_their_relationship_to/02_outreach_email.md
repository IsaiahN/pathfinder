**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Subject:** One checkable output on spin liquids and lattice gauge theories

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** Take the lattice geometry and spin Hamiltonian of a frustrated magnet as one closure, and the lattice gauge description (link variables, plaquettes, fluxes, spinons, string operators) as another. The figure-grounded derivation says:
>
> 1. The relation is not an identity. Spins are not *definitionally* a gauge theory. A correlation between "spin-liquid behaviour" and "a gauge description" is symmetric and cannot choose the arrow; the production reading has to be earned by a derivation.
> 2. Two routes that look the same on the page must be kept apart. The Kitaev honeycomb solution is a derivation of a Z2 gauge structure from inside the spin model; a toric-code or Rydberg-blockade mapping is likewise derived. Borrowing the full non-Abelian lattice-QCD apparatus into a spin system is an import, and an import needs a stated source and a stated gap it explains. Without that record the two are indistinguishable in their contents.
> 3. The gauge description is irreducible at the effective level and adds no primitive to nature. What it adds is a bond, and the bond carries information (pinch points, string order, anyon statistics) that the list of spin couplings does not.
> 4. The framework does not say which gauge group a given material realises. It returns a shape, not a name. Naming Z2 or U(1) for a material is a binding from the physics frame, and I record it as such, not as a derivation.
> 5. A programmable quantum simulator can settle its own gauge wiring. It cannot settle a solid-state material, because it has substituted a habitat and inherited actors the material did not have.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that spin liquids are literally lattice gauge theories in the QCD sense; that the lattice or the continuum is the real object; that any named material is a confirmed spin liquid; that a simulator result transfers to a material; or that this is a grounded result. No measurement was taken in producing this note.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim.** I wrote these down before sending this:
> 1. A derivation in which topological order arises from a direct spin-spin bond with no intervening structure, admitted by the framework and not refuseable.
> 2. A material whose gauge structure requires an atom absent from the spin closure, with no source and no gap explained by the import.
> 3. A source-discriminating instrument that produces the same topological signatures from a non-gauge mechanism, or names a different gauge group than the effective theory predicted.
> 4. A derivation of one of the six framework primitives from the other five.
> 5. A demonstration that Z2 and U(1) spin liquids cannot be written over a shared atom set.
>
> If any one holds, the corresponding claim is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>

## Contact discipline

- One-to-one only. No mail merge, no bulk send, no recipient selected for likely agreement.
- The recipient's verdict on stage 1 is recorded before the links are shown.
- Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
- A Track B style email, if the owner ever adds one, carries `verdict_weight = 0` by definition and must be published by the recipient on an institutional page. No such emails are in this folder.
