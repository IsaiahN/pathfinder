# The Tether's answer: quantum spin liquids and lattice gauge theories

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A frustrated spin system fails to order at any temperature yet is not disordered: it carries long-range entanglement, fractionalized excitations and topological structure. The lattice gauge frame already has terms for link variables, plaquettes, fluxes, Gauss law, deconfinement and topological order. The residual is the unstated operator between these two frames, indexed per material slot (Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."). It is a **union** of three overlapping conditions, derivation, import and settlement, not one relation (Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones.").

## 2. The shape

1. **It is not an identity.** Fig 2 :39: "Correlation has valence." The word "relationship" is symmetric, but the physics claim is ordered. Identity (spins *are* a gauge theory, the `≡` bond) is refused until a round trip with zero gap is measured (Operators :15; Fig 5 :65). Production is kept as `specified`, ungrounded.
2. **Two things that look alike must be kept apart.** Kitaev's honeycomb solution is a derivation of a Z2 gauge structure inside the spin closure; the toric-code and Rydberg-blockade mappings are likewise derived. Installing non-Abelian lattice-QCD machinery into a spin system is an import from a differing frame and owes a source gate and a shadow test. Fig 8 :51: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them". Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."
3. **Emergence is level-relative irreducibility.** Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world". The gauge description is irreducible at the effective level and adds no primitive to nature; what it adds is a bond. Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Pinch points, string orders and anyon statistics are carried by the bond, not by the list of spin couplings.
4. **The phases are isomers over a shared atom set.** Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Ordered magnet versus spin liquid, and Z2 versus U(1) over the full six-term set, are isomer claims with a falsifier written in advance. The lattice geometry is the functional group (Fig 12 :123: "The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member.") and the gauge group is the qualifier.
5. **The union is second order.** The spin phase settles first and becomes an atom for the effective gauge theory. Fig 13 :121: "Different routes can arrive at the same substance." Spin-first and gauge-first routes can converge on one phase, but the record must show where each came from (Fig 8 :50). The continuum is a further lossy abstraction: Fig 13 :43 "A continuum was the seventh and was removed for failing exactly this", and the round-trip gap must be measured (Fig 4 :30).
6. **The framework does not name the material's gauge group.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Naming Z2 or U(1) is a binding from the physics frame onto held atoms, not a derivation and not a wall-moving import. This narrows my first pass; the correction is visible in `01` F9, `05` section 5 and `06` Q1.
7. **A simulator settles its own habitat only.** Fig 11 :70: "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability." Semeghini's 219-atom string-order detection and Karch's U(1) pinch points are genuine grounds for the simulator, not for herbertsmithite or RuCl3.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that spin liquids *are* lattice gauge theories in the QCD sense. The SU(3) apparatus is an import, not a derivation, and carries no verdict without provenance.
- Not that any named material is a confirmed spin liquid. Failure to observe order is not proof (Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence.").
- Not that the lattice or the continuum is the real object. Discreteness and smoothness are both derivatives (Fig 13 :124).
- Not that the gauge field is fundamental, and not that it is a free rename. Both are level or notation errors.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement in this session settled a binding.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument per material, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument reads green and "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it by arrival of a better instrument. Here:

- **Material discriminator:** does a combined reading of topological string order, pinch points and thermal Hall response name one gauge group rather than several? Yang, Shannon and Lee's daughter-phase analysis and Linsel et al.'s percolation-inspired order parameters are candidate instruments, not settlements.
- **Source test:** can the same signatures arise from a non-gauge mechanism? If yes, the reading does not determine the source and the name is withheld.
- **Direction test:** is the arrow `⇒` (spins produce gauge structure) or `≡` (two names, one referent)? A measured round-trip gap `R_T` decides: zero permits identity, nonzero keeps production.
- **Standing falsifiers from `01` section 4:** direct-bond derivation, ungated import, metamerism failure, derivation of one of the six, failure of the shared-atom isomer claim.

## 5. The honest stop

**Reached on the shape, unreached on the name.** The six-term set is "a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes" (*They're Scanning Books*, carried); declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral. Which gauge group a given material realises is out of reach with the instruments actually brought to bear here. Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". The answer stops at the limit of what the available instruments distinguish, records the lineage, and does not invent a number or a name to fill the slot. "Indiscernibility is not identity."

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that quantum spin liquids are lattice gauge theories, and it does not claim a final answer. It says that the relation is a bond whose provenance decides its weight: the Kitaev and toric-code mappings are derivations inside the spin closure, while importing the full lattice-QCD apparatus is a different move that needs a source and a gap it explains. The gauge description is irreducible at the effective level and adds no primitive to nature; what it adds is a bond carrying pinch points, string order and anyon statistics that the list of spin couplings does not. Spin liquid and ordered magnet are isomers over the same spins, Z2 and U(1) are a further isomer claim over a shared atom set with a stated falsifier, and the union is second order because the spin phase settles before the effective gauge theory composes over it. The framework does not name the gauge group of a material, because a name is a binding, not a derivation, and a programmable simulator settles only its own substituted habitat. What it offers is the shape, the refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The material slot stays open until a source-discriminating ground reads it.
