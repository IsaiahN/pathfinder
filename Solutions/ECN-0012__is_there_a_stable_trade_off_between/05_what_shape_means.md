# What "shape" means for this question

Sources for the canonical reading: the eight essays by Isaiah N as mapped in the completed exemplar `Solutions/QMGR-0037__spacetime_continuum_or_emergent/05_what_shape_means.md`. The eight essays were not re-opened at source this session (web search integration returned no results and direct discovery was limited to the URLs in `04`); their shape quotes are carried from that completed canonical mapping and are marked as such. The framework figure text and canonical README were opened directly. No independent witness weight is claimed for the essays.

## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)". Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 :135: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

From *The Library of Babel* (carried): "You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

For this question the shape is: **a per-slot, regime-conditional comparison whose position is set by an updater (expectations) and by a driver type (demand versus supply), with a non-updating anchor required for a verdict and a numerical coefficient not governed by any figure.** That shape constrains any candidate answer. It does not fill in the slope, the sacrifice ratio, or a NAIRU point.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel* (carried): use the vocabulary already closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. "With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary." Here the first vocabulary is "correlation", then "expectations", then "anchor", then "regime".
- Fig 6 :30: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause." So we describe the failure of a fixed lever, not "the death of the Phillips curve" as a slogan.
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* (carried): "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." The plain description here is: a policy-invariant coefficient would survive a regime change with expectations holding still.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping (carried): the answer is reached when "the finish line will also be the starting place", and by then you have "composed the answer through the process of locating it."

## 3. What licenses a name (and what does not)

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." The README: the framework "stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test. Goodhart's law and Campbell's law are such imports: they are cast in effect terms, credited to their fields, and debited against independence.

*They're Scanning Books* (carried): "The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space." Also: "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible." The six-term set is a visible guess, not nature's own list. That is why we do not claim the figure set discovers the macro mechanism; it constrains the shape of the claim about it.

## 4. The source-discrimination error, applied here

*The Hard Problem is About Source* (carried) gives the mechanism. An instrument reads green and "cannot comment on the source": one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK. "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." And: "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy." It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved it.

Here the reading is "inflation fell while unemployment did not rise". Several sources can write that reading: a demand-driven disinflation with anchored expectations, a supply improvement, a change in labour-market structure that lowers the NAIRU, or a measurement change in the price index. The reading does not determine the source. The RBA speech makes the same point in its own vocabulary: inferring the NAIRU from wages and inflation "amounts to an assertion that these things are related in a particular way. You are in essence asserting that a particular theory, a particular model of how the world works, is true."

What is owed is a discriminator, not a name. The NY Fed Survey of Consumer Expectations is a step toward one: it returns expectations readings directly instead of inferring them from outcomes, but it still does not read the structural coefficient.

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (carried): "the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."

## 5. Answer to the question "what shape does a stable trade-off have?"

A stable, exploitable trade-off would have to be:

- a **non-updating anchor** for the policy frame (Fig 2 :29, :33);
- **indexed per slot**, so it is not an average that hides regime shifts (Fig 1 :19, :22);
- a **threshold comparison** between two quantities, not a substance (Operators :49 "a threshold relation between two quantities");
- **settled by a ground** that is not itself inferred from the same readings (Fig 6 :32).

The observed inflation-unemployment relation fails the first and fourth conditions, is usually reported in a way that fails the second, and is a comparison only under a declared regime. So the defensible shape is the conditional one, not the stable-lever one. That is the verdict recorded in `07` and `09`.

## 6. Correction to my own reading of "shape, not a name" (kept visible)

I initially treated the rule as a blanket ban on naming anything in this question, including the slope and the expectations term. That overstates it. The README's phrase is "what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." The subject is a primitive not held in the library. In this question, "expectations" and "spare capacity" are already available bindings from the economics frame; naming them is provenance, not the invention of a missing primitive. What the figures actually forbid is claiming the framework *derives* the numerical slope or a NAIRU point, because no such term is in the figure closure and no instrument in this derivation returns it without circularity (Fig 6 :21 "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom").

This correction does not weaken F8: the number is still a `gap`. It changes the reason. The number is a binding from the econometric frame that has not been settled by a ground, not a primitive the framework is structurally barred from naming. Both the earlier overstatement and the narrowed form are kept here so neither is silently erased.

## 7. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (carried): "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige." A campaign that gathers famous macroeconomists to agree that the curve shifts is the named collapse. The legitimate ask is a budget to try the anchor test.

**This audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay (carried): "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." LLM output is "simulacra." So `01` is a simulated derivation, checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is not a test of any economics claim and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle the question it reports on.
