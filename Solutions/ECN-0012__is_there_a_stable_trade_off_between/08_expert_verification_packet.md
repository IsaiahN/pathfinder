## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "stable trade-off or shifting relation" is a union of overlapping conditions, not a two-way choice. The observed inflation-unemployment relation is indexed per case, and a regime-pooled curve hides the slot where it breaks. A trade-off used as a policy lever is not an anchor, because expectations update on the lever and move the curve when you push on it; a supply shock is a hidden common cause that writes both series. The short-run inverse relation is real and useful as a convention, and the policy target is a coordinating convention that must not be promoted into a structural verdict. Over a fixed atom set the demand and supply episodes are isomers, same atoms arranged differently. No figure returns a slope, a sacrifice ratio, or a NAIRU value, so the number is a `gap` with an instrument owed.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 2 and 9
- **Statement:** the pooled curve hides per-slot residuals, and a relation whose position moves when pushed is not an anchor. The question is a union, not a partition.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 2 :30 "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." Fig 2 :33 "The anchor must not update." Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."
- **Refuter:** demonstrate a policy-invariant coefficient that survives a deliberate regime change without expectations updating on the change; or show a per-slot residual distribution that collapses to one stable curve with no regime shift.

### C2 - economics-facing; checkable against the empirical record
- **Statement:** the position of the short-run relation is set by expectations and by driver type (demand versus supply), and supply shocks can move inflation and unemployment together (stagflation), so a common cause can produce the co-movement without a trade-off.
- **What to check:** does the expectations-augmented curve fit the regime shifts better than a single curve? Are there episodes of simultaneous high inflation and high unemployment? Do the demand and supply episodes contain the same atoms (spare capacity, expectations, price setting, supply shock) with different bonds?
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :34 "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices." Fig 2 :6 "collapse 2: common cause" (from the figure label). Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- **Refuter:** independently measured expectations move while the short-run relation does not; or supply shocks are shown never to move inflation and unemployment together; or either episode can be written without an atom the other requires (which voids the isomer claim and leaves a parameter-difference reading).

### C3 - framework-internal derivation, with an open type question
- **Statement:** the policy target is a convention and the observed correlation has two valences; a convention that decides an outcome stops being one. No figure returns the numerical coefficient, which is a `gap`.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one". Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Fig 2 :41 "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it". Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself".
- **Refuter:** an instrument returns the slope or NAIRU point without inferring it from the same inflation and unemployment readings it is meant to explain; or a convention is shown to decide an outcome without any loss of ground contact (which would break the Fig 10 rule).
- **Live strain, named:** the isomer reading in C2 requires a fixed atom set. If a new expectation regime or labour-market institution changes the atom set, the relation is composition plus import, not isomerism. This is declared, not hidden.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a defence or attack of any school of macroeconomics.
- Not "there is no short-run relation." The within-regime inverse relation is real and useful as a convention.
- Not "expectations do not matter." They are the updater.
- Not a number. No slope, no sacrifice ratio, no NAIRU value, no policy rule.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by this agent's measurement.
- Not that the figure six-term set derives the macro mechanism. "Expectations" and "spare capacity" are bindings from the economics frame, provenanced as such (`05` section 6, `06` Q1).

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. Concretely:

1. **Invariant anchor:** does a measured coefficient survive a policy-regime change with expectations held still?
2. **Expectation discriminator:** do independently measured expectations move while the short-run relation does not?
3. **Common-cause discriminator:** are there episodes of inflation and unemployment rising together, with an identified supply shock? Stagflation supplies one.
4. **Instrument for the invisible:** does any method return the slope or NAIRU without circular inference from the same data?
5. **Standing refuters:** the six conditions in `01` section 4, written before the conclusion, any one of which retracts the union verdict for its domain.
