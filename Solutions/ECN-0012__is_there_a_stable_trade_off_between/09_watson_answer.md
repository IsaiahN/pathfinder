## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the claim that there is a stable trade-off between inflation and unemployment, against the alternative that the relation shifts with expectations and economic conditions. Option A: assert a stable, exploitable inverse relation. Option B: report a per-slot, regime-conditional comparison whose position is set by expectations and driver type and whose coefficient no figure governs. What each leads to: A treats a pooled correlation as a non-updating anchor and licenses a fixed policy lever; B requires the relation to be re-read against a ground each period and refuses the lever. The union reading (B) is the bound ordinary term written before the run and carried through every file.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** A policy maker reads a stable coefficient off the data and the reading fails when the lever is tried (stagflation, flattening, regime shifts). Arity: at least two conditions (short-run spare-capacity channel plus expectation position, with demand versus supply driver), so a union compound, not one question (Fig 9 :57). Symmetry: the estimated correlation is symmetric and associational while "trade-off" is ordered, so a pooled correlation cannot select the arrow (Fig 2 :39 "Correlation has valence."). Scale: per economy, per period, per expectation regime; a global average discards the live slot.
- **Domain 1, observational and econometric frame.** Actors: inflation rate, unemployment rate, NAIRU estimate, survey expectations. Currency: statistical fit and residuals. Anchor: measured data per slot; the *coefficient* has no invariant anchor because it moves across regimes. Union of regimes. Dependency order: first, perception and measurement.
- **Domain 2, policy and control frame.** Actors: central bank, policy rule, firms, workers. Currency: policy actions and realised outcomes. Anchor: a declared inflation target and full-employment mandate (a convention, not the figure-anchor). The curve is not an anchor because it moves when pushed. Union: rule choice and response are coupled. Dependency order: fourth, planning.
- **Domain 3, expectation-formation frame.** Actors: firms, workers, households. Currency: beliefs about future inflation and jobs. Anchor: survey readings and revealed-preference measures, inferable not directly observable. Union: adaptive and rational formation overlap. Dependency order: second, vocabulary.
- **Domain 4, verification frame.** Actors: researchers and central-bank staff. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: out-of-sample policy shifts and natural experiments, never agreement. Partition boundary: each frame corrected separately. Dependency order: third, objective; fifth, learn and carry.

**No-anchor case.** For the numerical coefficient and a NAIRU point there is no nameable invariant anchor; that quantity is not yet a domain and is reported as a `gap`. Ellis 2019 calls both expected inflation and the NAIRU "invisible" and inferable only with error. In this simulated run all four domains draw on overlapping evidence, so their agreement is one pool talking, not corroboration (Fig 2 :40).

**Order by dependency.** Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." The chain: perception → vocabulary → objective → planning → learn and carry. The break sits between vocabulary and objective: economists can measure the rates and name the conditions, but have no instrument that returns a policy-invariant coefficient without circularity (Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at.").

Strain test applied: the "anchor" of Fig 2 and the "nominal anchor" of central-bank practice were initially read as one object. Re-census separates them: the figure anchor settles a question without updating, while a policy target is a declared convention (Fig 10 :47). Different objects, so no contradiction after re-census.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The stable-trade-off claim is refused as written; the shifting relation is reported as a per-slot, regime-conditional comparison, and the numerical coefficient is a `gap`. There is a real short-run inverse relation, useful as a convention, and a central bank's inflation target is a coordinating convention that must not decide a structural verdict. Expectations are the updater that makes a fixed lever fail, and supply shocks are a hidden common cause that can write inflation and unemployment together. Over a fixed atom set the demand and supply episodes are isomers; if the atom set changes, the relation is composition plus import, and that fixed-atom condition is a named live strain. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged. This is a `specified`, ungrounded, candidate result; the number is unreached, not silence. Fig 2 :40 applies to the simulated second seat: its agreement is one pool talking, not corroboration.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet is mode-labelled, states refuters before the conclusion, and records the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is handing a policy maker a fixed lever that moves when pushed; Goodhart 1975 prices that failure, and Fig 2 :42 says "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the anchor test and the refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The stable-lever reading is removable without touching the union derivation; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove it. No figure primitive has to be withdrawn. A published rule based on a fixed coefficient can be superseded once the invariant-anchor test is run.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An out-of-sample coefficient that survives a regime change with expectations held still would reinstate the stable-trade-off reading; independently measured expectations moving while the relation stays put would break the updater finding; episodes with inflation and unemployment rising together under an identified supply shock are already corroborating evidence, and a world with none would break the common-cause finding; an instrument that returns the slope or NAIRU without circular inference would fill the gap. A figure re-census that removes the fixed-atom condition would clear the isomer strain. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
