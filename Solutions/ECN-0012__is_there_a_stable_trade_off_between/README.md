**Question:** Is there a stable trade-off between inflation and unemployment, or does that relationship shift with expectations and economic conditions?

## What is being decided, in effect terms

Option A: the observed inverse relation between inflation and unemployment is a stable, exploitable structure. Policy can buy lower unemployment with higher inflation along a fixed curve. Option B: that relation is a regime-conditional regularity whose position is set by expectations and by supply or demand conditions, so it moves when policy leans on it. What each leads to: A treats a pooled correlation as a non-updating anchor and licenses a control rule; B treats it as a per-slot signal that must be re-read against a ground each period, and refuses the fixed lever.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, Direction): a separator that tells us which readings of the relation are stable, which are updaters, and which quantity no figure can name. That bound term is stated in `01` section 1 and carried through every file.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals. The institution-level addresses visible on public pages (for example [institutional contact: see source URL]) are listed as institutional contact points only and are not used as a mailing list.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree that the relation shifts.
- No bulk-send plan. One-to-one contact through public opt-in venues only.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. A room is not an anchor. Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
2. Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation. Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool".
3. Showing the framework before asking for a verdict spends the reader's independence. Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."

Legally: unsolicited bulk email to harvested addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one.
