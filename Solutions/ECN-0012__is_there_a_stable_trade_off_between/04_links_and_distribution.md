## Wider literature relevant to this question

- Friedman 1968, "The Role of Monetary Policy", American Economic Review 58(1), pp 1 to 17: the natural-rate hypothesis and the temporary/permanent distinction. Cited through the Wikipedia and RBA records that quote it; the original was not opened this session, so it is marked as a bibliographic record, not a read source.
- Phelps 1967 and the expectations-augmented wage curve: the λ term that turns expected inflation into wage growth. Same caveat, cited through secondary records.
- Modigliani and Papademos 1975, "Targets for Monetary Policy in the Coming Year", Brookings Papers on Economic Activity: introduced NIRU, the precursor of NAIRU. Bibliographic record only.
- Ball and Mankiw 2002, "The NAIRU in Theory and Practice", Journal of Economic Perspectives 16(4): the canonical uncertainty discussion. Fetch blocked, recorded, not cited as read.
- Campbell 1979, "Assessing the impact of planned social change", Evaluation and Program Planning 2(1): the original of Campbell's law, obtained verbatim through the Wikipedia record.
- Goodhart 1975, "Problems of Monetary Management: The UK Experience": the original of Goodhart's law, obtained verbatim through the Wikipedia record.

Overlap declaration: the Phillips curve, Lucas critique, and NAIRU pages are one macroeconomics evidence pool, not three witnesses. Goodhart and Campbell are genuinely lower-overlap corroborators (control / measurement, and evaluation research / social psychology). The RBA speech is a practitioner record from inside the same macro pool but adds an instrument-level observation (invisibility and inferability) that the others do not. The essays share the framework author and carry no independent witness weight.
