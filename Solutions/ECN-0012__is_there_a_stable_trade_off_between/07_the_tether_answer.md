# The Tether's answer: stable trade-off or shifting relation?

**Mode: `specified` on each domain verdict, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the numerical coefficient. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A policy maker reads the observed inverse relation between inflation and unemployment and wants to use it as a fixed lever: accept this much inflation, buy that much unemployment. The reading fails when the lever is tried, because expectations and conditions move the curve. The residual is the status of that relation. It is a **union compound**, not one hard question (Fig 9 :55-57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"). It has its own loop and its own ground.

## 2. The shape and the per-domain verdicts

1. **Do not pool the cases.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A single curve across countries, decades, and expectation regimes hides the slot where the relation has broken. Verdict D1: `specified`, triangulated.

2. **A stable exploitable trade-off is refused by the anchor test.** Fig 2 :30: "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update." Push on the curve through policy, and expectations update, so the curve moves. Goodhart 1975 and Campbell 1979 corroborate from different fields. Verdict D2: `specified`, triangulated.

3. **Expectations are the updater inside the frame.** Fig 2 :34: "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices." The expectations-augmented curve is this loop in economics language. Verdict D3: `specified`; figure mechanics single-source, world corroborated.

4. **Stagflation is a hidden common cause, not a broken trade-off.** Fig 2 :6-7 "collapse 2: common cause (hidden shared source)": a supply shock writes both inflation and unemployment, so their co-movement is the pool talking, not a trade. Verdict within D1: `specified`, triangulated.

5. **The target is a convention, the correlation has two valences.** Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." The inflation target coordinates expectations; it must not decide the structural verdict. The observed correlation is "efficient for a convention" (Fig 2 :41) and "disqualifying for a verdict" (Fig 2 :40). Verdict D2/D4: `general` on the distinction, `specified` on the application.

6. **Over a fixed atom set, the episodes are isomers.** Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Demand-driven and supply-driven episodes share the atoms and differ in arrangement. Live strain: the fixed-atom condition. Verdict D1: `specified` with S1 named.

7. **The number is a gap.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". No figure returns a slope, sacrifice ratio, or NAIRU point, and no instrument in this derivation returns one without inferring it from the same readings. Verdict D4: `gap` (shape-not-a-name kind), unreached, not silence.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "there is no short-run relation." A within-regime inverse relation is real and useful as a convention.
- Not "expectations do not matter." They are the updater that makes the fixed lever fail.
- Not a defence or attack of any school of macroeconomics. Schools are frames; the test is the anchor test.
- Not a number. No slope, no sacrifice ratio, no NAIRU value.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken by this agent; the world sources are secondary records and are corroboration only.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not more argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (carried): an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

- **Invariant-anchor test:** does a measured coefficient survive a deliberate policy-regime change with expectations held still? An anchor that does not move when pushed would reinstate the stable-trade-off reading.
- **Expectation discriminator:** do independently measured expectations (for example the NY Fed SCE one, three, and five year medians) move while the short-run relation does not? If yes, F4 breaks.
- **Common-cause discriminator:** are there episodes where inflation and unemployment rise together? Stagflation already supplies one; a world without any would break F5.
- **Instrument for the invisible:** does any method return the slope or NAIRU without inferring it from the inflation and unemployment data it is meant to explain? That fills the F8 gap.
- **Standing falsifiers from `01`:** six conditions are written before the conclusion and repeated in `08` and `09`.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable in principle.** The expectations and spare-capacity vocabulary are bindings available from the economics frame; the figures constrain the shape of the claim about them and do not derive the macro mechanism. ## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not pick a side between "stable trade-off" and "shifting relation", because that is a union of conditions dressed as a two-way choice. It says the observed inflation-unemployment relation is indexed per case, that a regime-pooled curve hides the slot where it breaks, and that a trade-off used as a policy lever cannot be a stable anchor because expectations update on the lever and move it when you push. Stagflation is the signature of a hidden common cause writing both series, not of a curve that failed. The short-run inverse relation is real and useful as a convention, and the inflation target is a coordinating convention that must not be promoted into a structural verdict. Over a fixed atom set the demand and supply episodes are isomers: same atoms arranged differently. No figure returns a slope, a sacrifice ratio, or a NAIRU point, so the number is reported as a gap with an instrument owed, not as an estimate. What is offered is the shape any answer must have, six falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The number stays open until a ground settles it.
