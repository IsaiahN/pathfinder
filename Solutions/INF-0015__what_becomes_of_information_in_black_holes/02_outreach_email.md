# Outreach draft - check the shape first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F1 to F7 survives the reader's own check. The output must stand on its own or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable shape claim about black hole information, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output about black hole information that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the shape claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`):** the question is not a two-way choice between information preserved and information lost. It is a union of three overlapping conditions: a semiclassical calculation that is settled in its own regime and records only coarse ingredients (mass, charge, angular momentum, a thermal spectrum), a unitarity requirement settled in the laboratory, and the fact that no complete evaporation has been measured. Those have different anchors, so averaging them or voting between them decides nothing.
>
> What survives derivation is a shape. Any answer must (a) keep the fine-grained map invertible per radiation mode, not just reproduce a hole-wide entropy; (b) identify the typed relation that carries the distinction across the horizon, because a list of ingredients is not the arrangement; (c) come with an instrument that would discriminate among the candidate mechanisms; and (d) record where any new atom was imported from. The framework does not name which mechanism is right. I record that as a gap, unreached at this budget, not as silence.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the framework solves the information paradox; that it derived holography or the island rule; that the majority view that information is preserved is therefore correct; or that "information" is a new fundamental primitive. The framework's own text lists Bekenstein and holography among cases it fits from known work and says the claim has not been tested forward. That is a retrodiction, not a prediction it produced. Majority belief can fund an attempt to check a claim; it cannot settle one.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before this message:**
> 1. the fine-grained information is recovered from the semiclassical calculation with no new atom, so the per-mode entanglement is restored;
> 2. a measurement of the radiation's fine-grained entanglement from a complete evaporation, or an analogue that genuinely reproduces the gravitational entropy, shows a Page curve or its definite absence;
> 3. one of the six substrate terms of the framework is derived from the other five, so the composition rule the shape relies on fails;
> 4. the two anchors collapse, so laboratory unitarity moves when the gravitational reading changes;
> 5. a forward prediction from the figures about evaporation is confirmed at the ground.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Page, "Information in Black Hole Radiation", Phys Rev Lett 71 (1993): https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9306083
> - Almheiri et al., "Replica Wormholes and the Entropy of Hawking Radiation" (2019/2020): https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.12333
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
