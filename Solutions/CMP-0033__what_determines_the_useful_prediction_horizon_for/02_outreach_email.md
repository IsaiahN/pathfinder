# Outreach draft: check the output before you decide whether the frame is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the refuter below is stated in the recipient's own words and their verdict on stage 1 is recorded. The ask is a refutation, not belief.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about prediction horizons in feedback systems

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` shape, `specified` mechanisms, not `grounded`):** for a system with nonlinear feedback and changing structure, the useful prediction horizon is not a single time constant. It is a per-slot boundary where the residual (prediction minus observation) crosses the tolerance the decision actually uses. What moves that boundary falls into four classes:
> 1. the sign of each feedback loop (output re-entering as input, closing or amplifying),
> 2. the round-trip gap introduced by compressing the situation into a forecast and bringing it back down,
> 3. new actors or relations entering contact as the structure changes, which the frame's existing vocabulary cannot reach,
> 4. decay of the channel to the ground (stale observations, pooled readings), rather than any change in the ground itself.
>
> There is also a split that decides the remedy: if the needed term is already in the frame's closure and merely unfound, more search extends the horizon; if the term is absent from the closure, no amount of search helps and the frame must import. From inside, the two look the same, so the census has to be done explicitly.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** a number of days, steps or cycles for any named system. The framework returns a shape, not a name, and a number would need a measured residual from a real system. I am also not claiming that chaos makes all long prediction impossible: it is one limiter among four, and maintaining contact with the ground is a separate lever.
>
> **What would refute this:**
> 1. a single global tolerance on a mean residual that predicts every slot's failure turn across a regime change;
> 2. an active amplifying loop whose residuals stay bounded and trend down indefinitely without channel maintenance;
> 3. a term brought in by structural change that is derivable inside the unchanged frame's closure;
> 4. a lossy abstraction whose round-trip gap against the ground measures zero.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Formula and mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a system you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
