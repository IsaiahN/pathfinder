## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as `gap(x, (T_E o T_A)(x))`, the gap between what was sent up and what came back down. Fig 4 :30 requires measuring it rather than assuming it. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 (per-slot residual), Fig 12 (cycle sign), Fig 11 (contact cascade), Fig 4 (round trip) and Fig 8 (budget vs absence).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07_the_tether_answer.md`: a per-slot boundary with four limiter classes and a budget/absence split.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a decision maker's concrete need: the turn at which to stop trusting this forecast and switch to scenarios or resilience.

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **tolerance** is defined by the decision's scoring function, not by the figures. A concrete decision must supply it; the abstraction silently carried it as "the tolerance the decision uses";
- the **loop sign** must be read from the actual system, not inferred from the residual curve. A metamerism risk: the same score decline can come from amplification or from a new atom;
- the **budget/absence call** needs a controlled comparison (vary compute, then vary the frame), which the shorthand compresses into a single phrase.

One thing survives cleanly: the residual is per slot, and averaging across slots destroys the live signal. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists. It is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a measured gap, which `09_watson_answer.md` states.
