## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question the shape is: any answer must include a system with organization, a pose-dependent mapping, and a ground that does not update. "Intrinsic" and "observer-relative" are names for two of those three terms, each pretending the other two do not exist.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."* The effect here is: an ascription that survives intervention on the system and is invariant to irrelevant redescription.
- *The Hard Problem is About Source*: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* The reading (the observed input/output behaviour) does not determine the source (the causal organization that produced it). What is owed is a discriminator, not a name.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: *"you will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place."* The paradox is that by then you have *"composed the answer through the process of locating it."*

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays."* The figures README says the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."*
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :51-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*They're Scanning Books* sharpens this: *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space."* And: *"A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six."* Also: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."*

Applied here: choosing "intrinsic" or "observer-relative" as the name declares a primitive. What the shape requires is that the choice be visible and provenanced, not that it be smuggled in as the answer.

## 4. Why the source question matters for this question

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. A color instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source."* One green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or an undiscovered scheme can all give the same reading. *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."* It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that settled it: *"It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."*

The computation question has the same form. Observed input/output behaviour is the green reading. "The system is computing X" names a source of that behaviour. An observer mapping states to inputs and outputs is a reading of a projection. To name the source from the reading alone is the metamerism error. What is owed is an instrument that discriminates causal organization from imposed description. Until it exists, the answer is `unreached`, which the figure text calls a verdict: *"unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"* (Fig 13 :134).

## 5. Q: Does the shape decide between the two options?

**Answer: no.** Fig 9 :32 gives a kind of thing, never the answer. Fig 13 :135 forbids naming a missing primitive. The framework returns the three-term shape and the refuters, and stops. The following are refused as names:

- "Computation is a property of the system alone." Refused because the pose and the ground are omitted (Fig 11 :39, Fig 2 :29).
- "Computation is whatever an observer maps it to be." Refused because a declared meaning is not a grounded one (Fig 10 :49).
- "The framework settles the debate." Refused because *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself"* (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*), and Fig 8 :37: *"A frame cannot certify its own limit."*

## 5b. Correction to my own §3 and §4

I initially wrote that "shape, not a name" forbids calling anything in the shape by a name, and therefore that the framework must refuse both "intrinsic" and "observer-relative" outright. That overreads the phrase. The README's wording is *"what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* The subject is a primitive **not held** in the library. Here the three terms (system, mapping, ground) are all already held. Naming them is a binding onto held structure, provenanced as such, not the invention of a missing primitive.

What remains wrong in the two options is not that they name something, but that each drops a term and then claims the dropped term does not exist. My §4 metamerism point stands, but for the corrected reason: the error is naming the source from a projection without a discriminator, not naming held atoms. Both readings are retained so neither is quietly lost.

## 6. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* A list of famous philosophers selected for agreement with either camp is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask gives the claim a budget to be tried and lets the ground settle it.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* So `01` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is not a test of the claim and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on.
