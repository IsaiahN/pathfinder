# Isomers, order, and the rock: the supporting derivation

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

Why does the same physical state sequence count as a computation in one description and not in another, and what order must the three terms take? The world source (SEP, Computation in Physical Systems) gives the concrete case: a rock warming in the sun goes T, T+1, T+2, T+3; map T and T+2 to 0, T+1 and T+3 to 1 and the rock implements a NOT gate with feedback under the simple mapping account. Under the causal and dispositional accounts it does not, because *"T does not cause T+1, nor does the rock have a disposition to go into T+1 when it is in T. Rather, the rock changes its state due to the action of the sun."*

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Are the rock and the NOT gate isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- The rock and the NOT gate can share the same state pattern (the ingredients) while differing in the bond: the rock's transition is driven by the sun (an external cause), the gate's transition is produced by its own causal organization. Fig 12 :95-96: *"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."*
- ⇒ Under the figures they are **isomers**, not variants of one thing: same atom set, different typed bond, different substance. This is the figure-grounded version of the world literature's restriction on acceptable mappings.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a complete description of the rock's states and transitions plus the mapping yields the same bond type as the gate's, the isomer claim fails and the two are the same substance under two names.

### Q2: What order are the three terms?

- Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*
- Order: (1) track the system's states; (2) have a pose-dependent mapping of those states to inputs and outputs; (3) settle the mapping against the ground.
- Fig 3 :22-23: *"A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can. Which is why a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing."* A claim of computation made before the ground link exists is a reading below the break.
- Fig 12 :111-113: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one. A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* The mapping is the grammar that turns a state list into a route; without it you have ingredients, not a computation.

### Q3: Does the mapping add an atom?

- Fig 6 :25: *"An import adds no atom to the world."* Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*
- The mapping is a typed bond over atoms already held (states, the frame transform F). It does not add a primitive, so it is a composition, not an import that moves the wall.
- Fig 8 :50-52: *"Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence. Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."* The provenance record decides whether a given account was derived or adopted, not the contents.
- **Falsifier:** show that producing a computational ascription requires a genuinely new primitive rather than a new bond over held states.

### Q4: What does the analogue from source discrimination add?

- From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"*; *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy"*; *"It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."*
- The input/output trace is the green reading; the causal organization is the source. The instrument owed is one that discriminates among sources that produce the same trace. This maps directly onto the SEP's counterfactual and causal restrictions on mappings.
- Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* The restriction on mappings is the worse instrument already returning something; it is sharpened, not conjured.
- **Falsifier:** an argument that discriminates sources with no instrument and no residual to sharpen against.

## CONCLUSION

The rock and the gate are isomers under Fig 12: same atoms, different bond, different substance. The three terms are ordered by dependency (states, mapping, ground), and a reading below the break is a reading of nothing. The mapping adds a typed bond, not a primitive, and the provenance record is what separates a derived restriction from an adopted one. What the shape still lacks is a discriminator of sources from a shared behavioural trace, which is why the intrinsic/observer-relative binary stays `unreached` rather than `grounded`.
