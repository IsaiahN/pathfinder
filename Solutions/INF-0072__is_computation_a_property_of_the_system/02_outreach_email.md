**Subject:** Check one output on computation before you decide whether the framing is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "is computation a property of the system, or of the observer's mapping?" is the wrong cut. Any real ascription needs three things: a system with causal organization, a mapping of its states to inputs and outputs that is a frame transform requiring a pose, and a ground that settles whether the mapping is faithful. Pure intrinsic accounts drop the pose. Pure observer-relative accounts drop the ground, and under an unrestricted mapping a rock warming in the sun implements a NOT gate.
>
> The framework's own words for the two halves: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks"*; and *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."* The bond carries the information the states do not, so the rock and the gate stand as isomers, same atoms, different bond, different substance. What is offered is the shape any answer must have, not a name for the winning camp. The binary stays open until an instrument discriminates causal organization from imposed description.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that computation is purely intrinsic, that it is purely observer-relative, that this settles the philosophical debate, or that any particular mapping is the correct one. Nothing here is claimed as settled by experiment.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. redrawing the mapping changes the ascription while every physical fact and intervention outcome is held fixed;
> 2. the states alone yield a unique input/output partition with no pose or vantage;
> 3. an unrestricted mapping with no counterfactual or causal support is sufficient for a genuine computation ascription;
> 4. two systems with identical states and identical typed bonds differ in computational status;
> 5. an experiment shows computation tracking the observer's ascription rather than the system's intervention-invariant causal organization.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Computation in Physical Systems: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/computation-physicalsystems/
> - The Chinese Room Argument: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/chinese-room/
> - Computational theory of mind: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_theory_of_mind
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the local figures folder available on request. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
