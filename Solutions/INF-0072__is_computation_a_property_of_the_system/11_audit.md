## Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual line alone, before using the primary's conclusions, in the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the predicate "computation." Arity: three terms, a system with states and transitions, a mapping to inputs and outputs, and a ground that settles the mapping. Symmetry: ordered, not symmetric. The mapping runs from a pose and the frame ranges against the ground. Scale: system and frame at one scale, ground at a different logical type, since it does not update.
- **Domain 1, the system.** Actors: states, transitions, components. Currency: physical state change. Anchor: an intervention measurement. Question type: the substrate, not the property.
- **Domain 2, the mapping frame.** Actors: observer, mapping F, pose. Currency: states to inputs and outputs. Anchor: none of its own. Question type: a bond over held atoms, not a new primitive.
- **Domain 3, the ground.** Actors: instruments, experiments, researcher. Currency: residuals settled against the anchor. Anchor: the ground, which does not update. Question type: settlement, never negotiation.
- **Union, not partition.** The two offered options overlap: a computation ascription needs all three domains. This matches Fig 9 :54 and :57. Order by dependency: track states, map them, settle the mapping.

My census matches the primary's three domains and its union reading. Where the primary derived from Fig 2 and Fig 12, I derived first from Fig 9's union/partition split and Fig 3's dependency order. Different starting figures, same domain boundaries: low overlap between routes, so the agreement is weakly non-circular inside the framework. It is still one evidence pool.
