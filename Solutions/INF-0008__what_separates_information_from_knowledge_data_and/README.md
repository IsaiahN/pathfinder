# Tether outreach - figure-grounded package for INF-0008

**Question:** What separates information from knowledge, data, and evidence?

**Source:** `Questions/Information Theory.txt`, line 11, section 1 "What information is".

**Mode:** `general` on the structural separator (the four are roles in one loop, distinguished by anchor and by position relative to the bond and the ground), `specified` on the per-domain separators, and `gap` on the request for a single settled dictionary definition. Not `grounded`: no measurement settles a definition, and this whole folder is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the reading.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces.** Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* A list selected for receptiveness is a list selected to agree, which is exactly the correlation that disqualifies a verdict (Fig 2 :40).
2. **A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* So the first contact carries the residual and its refuter, never the framework's interiors.
3. **Adding people raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor.** Seat Map, failure genera: *"A room does not upgrade the label. A room reaching a conclusion without the ground is agreement, and agreement among frames is a weighted average of influence. Adding people raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor."* The ask gives the claim a budget to be tried; it does not ask anyone to endorse it.

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions, a practical failure on top of the structural one.

## Boundary

Nothing in this folder is in a repository and no repository file was read for the purpose of editing or changed. Sources used were the local canonical framework files under `FRAMEWORK/`, public web pages listed in `10_source_ledger.csv`, and the eight essays. Every world source in the ledger was opened on 2026-11-23. Sources that could not be fetched directly (the Shannon 1948 PDF returned an unsupported content type, and MDPI returned 403) are recorded honestly in `04` and the ledger and are not cited as read.
