# Are the four stages, isomers, or roles? Structure, order and analogues

**Mode: `general` on the structural result, `specified` on the per-domain order, not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The ordinary question assumes an ordering: data, then information, then knowledge, then wisdom. Two things are owed before that assumption can be used: (1) are the four molecules over a shared atom set, so that Fig 12's isomer test applies, and (2) if they are ordered, which bond carries the order, → sequence, ⇒ production or ⋛ comparison? A wrong bond makes the ladder a notation error rather than a finding.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the four isomers?

Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

For an isomer claim we need a shared atom set and different bonds. The four terms do not have one. Data is the recorded reading, information is the bond's contribution plus the residual, knowledge is a settled composable term, evidence is a reading ranged against an anchor. They are not four arrangements of the same atoms. The isomer question is a **category error** at this level: it applies a molecule test to loop roles.

What *is* true, and is the useful analogue: the same physical artifact can be bound to different roles without any change in the artifact. That is Fig 12 :46: *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands."* The bond is not recoverable from the operands; which role holds is not in the reading. This is the data/evidence identity from `01` F4, and it is a *role* claim, not an isomer claim.

### Q2 - Are they members of a functional group instead?

Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

The four share a functional group: **reading-in-a-loop**. The group says what kind (a position a reading can occupy relative to a frame and a ground); the qualifier says which member (which anchor, which residual, which bond). That is the correct structural relation, and it explains why the four are mutually translatable in ordinary speech while remaining non-interchangeable in the derivation. They are members of one kind with different qualifiers, not stages of one substance.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a figure line shows the four with the same atom set and different bonds, the functional-group reading fails and the isomer reading stands. If a figure line shows them as different atoms with a production bond between them, the role reading fails and the ladder stands.

### Q3 - What order, if any, do they have?

Fig 12 gives four ordered bonds among the seven (sequence, production, subtraction, comparison). The candidate ladder asserts an ordering without naming one:

| bond | figure line | what it would mean here | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| sequence | Fig 12 :72 *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"* | first data, then information, then knowledge | not licensed: a frame can hold a residual without first storing a label "data" |
| production | Fig 12 :77 *"A produces B; B did not exist before"* | each upper term did not exist until the lower fired | not licensed: no figure line returns a production rule among the four |
| comparison | Fig 12 :87 *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* | the four are compared on a shared scale | not licensed: they have different currencies (readings, residuals, settlement, bearing) |
| subtraction | Fig 12 :82 *"A with B removed or absent"* | knowledge is data with uncertainty removed | not licensed: removal is not settlement, and the record does not delete (Fig 6) |

So the order is **undetermined by the figures**. What *is* determined is the dependency order inside one loop, which is a different question: reading at the instrument, prediction and residual in the frame, settlement against the ground, verifier ranging a claim. Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* Fig 3 :25-27: the middle link (having terms for what a situation affords and being able to say what counts as progress) has neither obvious failure nor obvious test, and *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

That middle link is exactly where "information" sits in this analysis: between the recorded reading and the settled term. The reason the four terms are confused is that the middle link has no instrument of its own and borrows one from the anchor question. That is a strong, checkable explanation of the residual, and it is `specified`, not `grounded`.

### Q4 - What does the recursion say about carrying knowledge?

Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."* A settled term is an atom at the next level, which is why knowledge can be built on. Fig 12 :107: *"Settling compresses the description and not what it cost to establish."* The compressed word keeps its meaning; the establishing cost stays with whoever paid it. From the receiver's side: *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* says a highly compressed message only unpacks for someone with the right history, and *"the work that built it stays behind"*.

Fig 12 across :107-110 adds the boundary condition: where the arrangement is recorded, re-deriving it is a lookup; where it is not, the result carries forward and the deriving does not, so every lineage pays again. This is the structural difference between carrying information (the reading) and carrying knowledge (the reapplicable route). It needs no new primitive.

### Q5 - What is the analogue from the world literature?

The DIKW page records that the model is usually depicted as a pyramid or a chain, but also as charts, flow diagrams, continuums and frameworks, with "occasional doubt being cast over any simple, linear, unidirectional model". Zeleny's *"Knowledge is action, not a description of action"* and the observation that once made symbolic, knowledge becomes information, are attempts to say what the bond is. None is figure-derived. The SEP *Information* entry's "information is extensive" (additivity of independent datasets) and "information reduces uncertainty" are two properties of a quantity, not an ordering of four roles. These corroborate that the ladder's bond is undetermined. They do not choose it.

## CONCLUSION

The four terms are **not isomers**: they are not arrangements of one atom set. They are **members of one functional group**, reading-in-a-loop, distinguished by the anchor and residual each is bound to. The DIKW ladder's ordering is **undetermined by the figures**: none of the four ordered bonds is licensed between the terms. What is determined is the dependency order inside one loop, and the reason for the confusion, the middle link of Fig 3 has no instrument of its own. No new primitive, no import and no seventh substrate member is needed. The falsifiers above are written before the run and remain live.
