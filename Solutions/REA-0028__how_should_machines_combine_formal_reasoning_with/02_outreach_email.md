**Subject:** Check one output before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** A formal system holds a library of symbols and a checker. At some slot its prediction misses what the world returns, and no amount of search inside that library manufactures the missing structure. A second, approximate system registers structure in that slot that the formal vocabulary cannot name. The question is how to couple them so the result is settled by the ground and not by their agreement. This is a shape, not an architecture. I am not claiming a particular neural symbolic design or a system 1 / system 2 split is the answer.
>
> **The output (`general` on the barriers, `specified` on the coupling, `gap` on the named architecture):** the two systems should be held as independent frames ranged separately against one immovable ground. The fast frame may propose candidate terms. Every import into the formal frame carries provenance and names the gap it explains. The formal frame verifies, and must be unable to produce what it checks. The coupling is measured by a round trip, because what crosses up is a method and what comes down is a head start, never a recording. The sought-for shape is written down as an ordinary refutable term before the run, so the preference can be checked like anything else.
>
> **What I am explicitly not claiming:** that blending the two into one soft-prior system is enough, that their agreement counts as verification, that human approval is the anchor for a correctness question, or that the figures name a specific architecture. The figures return a shape, not a name. Which concrete architecture settles on the ground is a declared `gap`; what is owed is a source-discriminating instrument that tells a genuine new atom from a fast binding of an atom already held.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a hybrid that produces a new atom inside the formal library by internal composition alone, with provenance and reach gain recorded;
> 2. two frames with measured high evidence-pool overlap whose agreement is used as a verdict and the ground pays;
> 3. a verifier that reconstructs the claim it checks, checks it, and the claim still settles against an independent ground;
> 4. an import with no source gate and no shadow test that still expands reach;
> 5. a specific architecture derived from the figures that settles on a ground, which would close the gap and supersede this derivation.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - The figures and formula, canonical text: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in the public Project Pathfinder repository.

> - Worked narrative and the six-term derivation: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-intelligence-actually-works

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
