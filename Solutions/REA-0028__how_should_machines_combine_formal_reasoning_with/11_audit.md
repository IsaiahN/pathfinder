## 4. Round-trip gap R_T

Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

- **T_A, going up:** carry the primary's shape to a one-sentence abstraction. Result: *keep two independent judges and make them answer to something neither controls.*
- **T_E, coming back:** put that abstraction into a specific machine design. Result: *add a second model and a test set.*

**R_T = gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x)).** The gap is what the abstraction cannot hold:

1. It loses the import rule. "Add a second model" does not say that the second model's contribution must be a provenanced atom with a stated gap (Fig 6 :12-13). Coming back down, a fast binding is treated as a new primitive.
2. It loses verifier non-access. "Two judges" permits one to regenerate the other's claim. Fig 10 :42 forbids that, and the abstraction cannot see why.
3. It loses the union. "Two independent judges" reads as one condition; the primary needs both independent framing and differing closure, and a single lever clears only the region where one binds (Fig 9 :55-57).
4. It loses the source-discriminator `gap`. "Something neither controls" does not say what instrument is missing or how to recognize it when built. The abstraction reports success without a way to confirm it.
5. It loses human-instrument subordination. "Something neither controls" could be read as human preference, which Fig 2 :38 forbids for a correctness question.

This gap is not an error to eliminate; it is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold (Fig 4 :31). It is also why the outreach packet sends the generator, not the one-sentence version. The one-paragraph version in `07` is a deliberately lossy artefact and is labelled as such.
