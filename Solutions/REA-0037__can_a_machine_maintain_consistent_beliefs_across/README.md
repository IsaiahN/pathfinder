## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree that a machine can be consistent.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **Coherence is not a verdict.** *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). A list of people selected for likely agreement manufactures the very coherence whose evidential value is in question.
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). Selecting recipients for receptiveness is selecting them to share a pool.
3. **A reviewer must be able to refuse.** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). Sending the full architecture before asking for a verdict spends the only thing the outside frame contributes.

Legally: unsolicited bulk email breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions, and will get the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in venues avoid that practical failure as well as the structural one.
