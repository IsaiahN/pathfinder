## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that evolution makes every restoration fail. Some targets are static enough that the change term is momentarily negligible; the claim is that ignoring it is not a safe general design rule.
- Not that genetic diversity alone guarantees persistence. World sources record the opposite risk: too much mixing can cause outbreeding depression (Aitken and Whitlock 2013; Broadhurst et al. 2008).
- Not a specific provenance, seed mix or species list. That is the declared `gap`.
- Not a derivation of ecology from evolution, and no new primitive. Adaptive capacity is a property of an admitted entity, not a seventh substrate member.
- Not `grounded`. No restoration outcome was measured for this derivation. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding tests are:

1. **Multi-generation persistence under a documented shift.** Does a zero-adaptive-capacity design persist and function at the same rate as an adaptation-aware comparator? This fires refuter 1.
2. **Per-slot adaptive reading.** Does an instrument return a reading for adaptive capacity (variation, plasticity, gene flow) that actually changes propagule selection and intervention timing? This fires refuter 5.
3. **Anchor discrimination.** Can an independent instrument tell a self-sustaining community from one held together by continuing intervention, where both match the same reference list? This fires refuter 3.
4. **Hidden-failure detection.** Can a monitor with no adaptive term catch both Fig 11 :68-69 failure classes before the goal fails? This fires refuter 4.

Standing falsifiers from `01` section 4 are all five, written before the derivation concluded.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that evolution makes restoration fail. It says that a restoration which drops the capacity of its organisms to change has removed a load-bearing term from its own loop. Fig 4 :25 "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion"; planted material is a seed, not a small tree, and what it becomes depends on the conditions and on its capacity to change. So a restoration can hit a fixed reference list on paper, but that is a convention that has decided an outcome, not a ground-settled success. The shape of a success that survives change is a maintained generator loop: enumerate the habitat rather than composing it, instrument the adaptive term per population, and let persistence and function under a documented shift, not resemblance to a snapshot, settle the verdict. The which-provenance question is a declared gap, closed only by measurement. The falsifiers were written in advance: a zero-adaptive-capacity design that persists at the same rate across a multi-generation shift would break the claim. This is a `specified` shape from a simulated derivation, not a ground reading, and it cannot settle the question itself.
