## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Can ecological restoration succeed if it ignores the capacity of organisms to adapt and change? The answer separates two senses of success. Hitting a declared reference snapshot can succeed on paper, but that is a convention that has decided an outcome, not a ground-settled success. In the ground-settled sense, a restoration that drops the change term has removed a load-bearing generator from its own loop: planted material is a seed, not a small tree, and what it becomes depends on the conditions and on its capacity to change. The shape of success under change is a maintained, instrumented generator loop in which the organisms can change, the habitat is enumerated rather than composed, the two conditions (contact and renewal) are kept separate, and the ground, not the declared reference, settles the verdict. Mode `specified`. A narrower claim, which provenance rule wins at a given site, is a declared `gap`.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that evolution makes every restoration fail. Some targets are static enough that the change term is momentarily negligible. The claim is that ignoring it is not a safe general design rule.
- Not that genetic diversity alone guarantees persistence. Outbreeding depression and drift load are real risks recorded in the world sources (Aitken and Whitlock 2013; Stewart et al. 2017 via abstract).
- Not a specific provenance, seed mix or species list. Which rule wins at a site is a declared `gap`, closed only by measurement.
- Not a derivation of ecology from evolution, and no new primitive. Adaptive capacity is a property of an admitted entity, not a seventh substrate member.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a restoration measurement.
- Not a claim that LLM output is evidence. This packet is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Multi-generation persistence:** a zero-adaptive-capacity design versus an adaptation-aware comparator across a documented shift (fires C1's refuter).
2. **Anchor discrimination:** an instrument that tells a self-sustaining community from one held together by continuing intervention, where both match the same reference list (fires C2's refuter).
3. **Per-slot adaptive reading:** an instrument whose reading for adaptive capacity actually changes propagule selection and intervention timing (fires C3's refuter).
4. **Hidden-failure detection:** a no-adaptive-term monitor that catches both Fig 11 :68-69 classes before failure.

A refutation, with provenance, is copied into `11_audit.md` and re-evaluated as if it came from any other source.
