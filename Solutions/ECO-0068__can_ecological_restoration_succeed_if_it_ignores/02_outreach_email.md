# Outreach draft: check one output before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. No personal emails.

**Hard precondition:** the claim and refuter below must survive stage 1 before stage 2 is offered. The order is the method.

---

**Subject:** One question about restoration and adaptive capacity. What would count as a failure?

> Hello [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against your own knowledge of restoration ecology. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a restoration sets a target for a site, intervenes, then watches the gap between what it predicted and what the site actually does, population by population and function by function. The question is whether it matters that the planted organisms can adapt and change. My shape claim is that the capacity to change is load-bearing: a restoration that drops that term can reproduce a past community, but cannot produce a self-sustaining one under changed conditions. In the narrow sense of hitting a fixed reference list it can succeed on paper. That is a convention that has decided an outcome, not a ground-settled success.
>
> The distinction I want checked is between a declared target and a ground. A reference community is not an anchor, because it can move with conditions and can be satisfied while the site fails. The anchor is what persists and functions independently of the plan.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that evolution makes any particular restoration fail; that a specific provenancing rule wins at a given site; that genetic diversity alone guarantees persistence. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Which seed sources work is a declared `gap` that only measurement closes.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim** (written before this message):
> 1. A restoration that sets adaptive capacity to zero in its design persists and functions across a documented multi-generation environmental shift at the same rate as an adaptation-aware comparator, with no further intervention.
> 2. A site-only intervention with no change in the organisms' adaptive terms yields the same persistence as an adaptation-aware design under the same shift.
> 3. A declared static reference is shown to be a genuine anchor, it settles persistence and does not move with conditions, and removing adaptive terms leaves the verdict on the ground unchanged.
> 4. A monitoring design with no adaptive term detects both classes of hidden failure before the goal fails in a recorded case.
>
> If any one holds, I will narrow or withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would like from you:** what would you count as a failure here? If you have a contrary case in mind, that is the most useful possible reply. Please record your answer before I send anything else.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - The verbatim figure text, Figures 1 to 13 plus the tables: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - The architecture essay, with the citation list: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
> - The worked derivation: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-intelligence-actually-works
>
> **If you want to use the method:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a restoration problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thank you for your time.
> [name]

## Two-stage offer, stated plainly

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual, the shape claim and the four refuters. Ask what a failure would look like. Record the reply before any theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If the recipient is curious, offer the three links. The figures, not this summary, are the authority. Their stage 1 verdict is never pooled as corroboration of the later theory.
3. **Stage 3, opt-in only.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

No stage proceeds unless the previous one survived. Nothing here asks anyone to trust, endorse or forward the claim.
