## QUESTION

Can ecological restoration succeed if it ignores the capacity of organisms to adapt and change?

Restated as an effect: for each tracked slot *s* (population, species, ecosystem function), does dropping the "organisms can change" term from Γ change whether R = |Γ(b,a) − o′| settles against the ground over time? R is indexed per slot, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19). Two senses of "succeed" have different anchors: hitting a declared reference snapshot, and persisting and functioning under continuing change. The anchor test is Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."

The candidate break is vocabulary, the missing term for adaptive change.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

**The figure-derived verdict:** success splits into two verdicts that must not be averaged. (1) In the ground-settled sense, no: a restoration that ignores adaptive capacity removes the generator that keeps contact with a changing ground, so success becomes a convention masquerading as a verdict. (2) In the declared-snapshot sense, yes, it can hit its target on paper, but that is "a convention that decides an outcome" (Fig 10 :47) and not a solved state. The supporting shape: keep the generator loop intact, enumerate the habitat rather than composing it, instrument the adaptive term per slot, treat contact and renewal as two conditions with a compound overlap, and let persistence and function settle the verdict. Which provenance rule wins at a site is a declared `gap`. Mode `specified`. The world pools corroborate the structure; one conflict (Aitken and Whitlock on outbreeding risk) was returned to the census and did not flip the verdict. No filter handed the verdict (Fig 9 :60).

- **Cost if wrong:** misdirected diagnosis. A site failing the generator condition would be sold more planting effort, and a site failing contact would be told to import more variation. A single lever would be applied to a union that needs two. In outreach terms, an expert's twenty minutes would be spent refuting a shape that was never the right shape. The cost is real but bounded and largely reversible.
- **Undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails; a refutation is added and the reach falls because something was added rather than struck out (Fig 6 :42-43). The shape and its five falsifiers are recorded in a provenance trail so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-64).
- **Reversed by:** any of the five pre-registered falsifiers in `01` section 4, in particular (1) a zero-adaptive-capacity design persisting at the same rate as an adaptation-aware comparator across a documented multi-generation shift, (2) a site-only intervention with unchanged adaptive terms yielding the same persistence, (3) a declared static reference shown to be a genuine anchor with the verdict unchanged after removing adaptive terms, (4) a no-adaptive-term monitor detecting both hidden failure classes before failure, or (5) an adaptive instrument that never changes a decision across multiple projects. A source from a genuinely different evidence pool showing that per-slot persistence is fully predicted by a static reference match would also reverse it.
