## DERIVATION

### Q1. Is a restoration target a state or a route?

Fig 12 :111-113: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."

A static reference list is a state. It names what the site should contain. It cannot name the route there, because a route needs sequence (what follows what), comparison (is the observed trajectory close enough) and subtraction (how far short it falls). Fig 12 :106: "Nothing composes over what was never admitted as a part." A frame that holds only ingredient terms (species list, cover targets) can say what a site contains and cannot say what would count as getting somewhere from it.

This is the exact break in Fig 3's middle link: having terms for what a situation affords was taken for a dictionary and it is a grammar (Fig 12 :115). Ignoring adaptation removes the grammar and leaves the dictionary.

**Falsifier:** a restoration plan expressed purely as a state list that nonetheless returns a per-slot reading of progress, comparison and refusal without any sequence or threshold term. If that exists, this finding fails.

### Q2. What is the order of the compound?

Fig 3 gives dependency order: perception to vocabulary to objective to planning to learn and carry. Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

Mapped to this question:

| link | restoration reading | instrument status when adaptation is ignored |
|---|---|---|
| 1 perception | enumerate the habitat: actors, conditions, relations | present (site assessment) |
| 2 vocabulary | what do propagules afford, what can change under contact | **missing term** for adaptive change |
| 3 objective | what counts as success: persistence under change, not snapshot | degraded to a fixed list because link 2 is empty |
| 4 planning | choose propagules, interventions, monitoring | present, but aimed at the degraded objective |
| 5 learn and carry | adaptive management, carry methods not recordings | blocked, because no change term to learn from |

So the compound stops at link 2, not at planning. Fig 3 :22-23: "A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can. Which is why a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing." A critique aimed at planting technique is a reading below the break.

**Falsifier:** a restoration that ignores adaptive capacity but whose monitoring returns a per-slot adaptive reading at link 2 and uses it to reset the objective at link 3. Then link 2 is not missing and the order is different.

### Q3. Is the union a partition or a compound?

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

The four domains form a union of two conditions: **(C1) maintained contact with a settling ground** (D1 and D4) and **(C2) a generator that can change under that contact** (D2, in habitat D3). Their overlap, the eco-evolutionary feedback, is a compound with its own loop. A single lever that fixes site hydrology clears the region where only C1 holds and leaves the intersection standing, because C2 still binds. This is why "just plant more" and "just add genetic diversity" are each one lever on a union.

**Falsifier:** one intervention that restores both C1 and C2 with no categorically different residue after the first is removed, across multiple sites.

### Q4. Do Fig 12's four structures apply?

Fig 12 names four structures: isomer, catalyst, cycle, functional group.

- **Functional group applies.** Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." All restoration projects that keep the generator loop intact belong to the same functional group; the qualifier (species, site, climate) says which member. This is the honest level of generalization.
- **Isomer does not apply as written.** Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Two restorations with the same species list but different arrangement (one with gene flow and one without) do share atoms and differ decisively in behaviour. So a *weak isomer claim* is defensible: same species atoms, different bond (presence or absence of the change term), different substance. But the atoms here are not the six substrate terms; they are site-specific species. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, and recorded as a binding from the restoration frame, not a figure derivation.
- **Catalyst is tempting but strained.** Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is." Adaptive capacity does survive its own use in a sense, but calling it a catalyst imports a chemistry label onto a population property that the figure does not measure. **Strain recorded, reading declined.** Re-census returns it to the functional-group claim.
- **Cycle applies structurally.** Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Eco-evolutionary feedback is exactly a cycle: the restored community changes the selection environment, which changes the community. A chain that plants and walks away cannot close by construction. This is the strongest of the four analogues and is triangulated with Fig 11 :63's "permanent work rather than a solved state."

**Falsifier for the whole Q4:** show that a restoration can be written without the cycle bond (output never re-enters as input) and still persist under a documented multi-generation shift. That kills the cycle analogue and the "permanent work" claim together.

### Q5. Seed versus blueprint, restated in bond terms

Fig 4 :34-35: "A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes." Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."

A blueprint restoration names the tree and tries to install it. A seed restoration names what will grow a tree and leaves the conditions to decide. The bond that distinguishes them is sequence plus comparison, not conjunction. Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term (Fig 12 :92); under an ordered bond, different orders are different terms. A species list treats planting as a symmetric bundle. A restoration trajectory treats it as ordered: site preparation before propagule, propagule before monitoring, monitoring before revision. Ignoring adaptation removes the last two orders and leaves the bundle.

**Falsifier:** a restoration whose outcome is invariant to the order of site preparation, planting, monitoring and revision across documented shifts. Then sequence is not load-bearing and this finding fails.

## CONCLUSION

The compound is a **union** of maintained contact and a changing generator, with its own cyclic loop, stopped at Fig 3's link 2 (vocabulary) when adaptation is ignored. Its target is a **route**, not a state. Of Fig 12's four structures, **functional group** and **cycle** apply, a weak **isomer** claim is defensible as a binding, and **catalyst** is strained and declined. The seed/blueprint distinction is a distinction of bond type (ordered sequence and comparison versus symmetric bundle), not of ingredients.
