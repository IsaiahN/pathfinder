# Expert verification packet - the Tether on cash routing

**What this is:** a claim in a form a corporate-finance researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the number. Not `grounded`.** No market measurement is claimed. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Free cash flow is a residual indexed per firm and per use-slot, and the four named options are the tips of four overlapping conditions, not four answers to one question. A firm searches its existing reach for a paying use first; only a new capability is an import requiring provenance and a shadow test. The anchor for each condition is a market that does not update on the firm's own report. A leverage target is a violable comparison. Dividends and buybacks are isomers over a fixed atom set, distinguished by bond, one recurring and costly to cut, the other discrete and flexible. Free cash with no paying internal use is a term whose residual persists and does not trend down, so the owed move is a refusal that removes management's standing to keep composing over it. Settled routes accumulate into capital structure at the next level; only methods and head starts cross between periods. No figure line returns a ratio, and the number is a declared gap.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal plus finance-facing; checkable by reading Figure 9 and Figure 5

- **Statement:** the four options form a union of overlapping conditions with an ordered search-then-refuse spine, not a partition and not a symmetric menu.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 5 :53 "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so". Also Fig 5 :55 "What it removes is not a term but a term's standing to be composed over."
- **What to check:** can two conditions bind on the same dollar (a dividend paid while debt is retired while a project is funded)? If yes, the union reading is correct. Is the order of testing observable, with a paying internal use checked before distribution? Brav, Graham, Harvey and Michaely 2005 (JFE, opened) report that repurchases are made out of residual cash flow after investment, which is consistent with the order.
- **Refuter:** a non-updating instrument assigns an exclusive, exhaustive partition of cash flows into the four categories with an assignable shared fraction; or the order is shown to be costlessly reversible at a single moment. Either refutes C1.

### C2 - finance-facing; checkable by a corporate-finance researcher against the payout literature

- **Statement:** dividends and buybacks are isomers over the fixed atom set {cash, shares, shareholders}, distinguished by bond, and the bond is priced in the empirical record.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance." Fig 12 :5 "The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
- **What to check:** is the recurrence asymmetry real? Lintner 1956 (AER, opened): *"the dependent variable in the decision-making process is the change in the existing rate, not the amount of the newly established rate as such."* Brav et al. 2005: 94 percent of dividend payers try to avoid reducing dividends, while *"many managers now favor repurchases because they are viewed as being more flexible than dividends"*, and substitution from repurchases to dividends is asymmetric. If the bond carries no information in the relevant jurisdiction, the isomer claim fails.
- **Refuter:** equal survival rates of dividend levels and buyback programs across a shock that forces a reduction, plus identical tax treatment and identical market response. That would collapse the two into one term.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation with a ground-facing consequence

- **Statement:** ordinary reinvestment is composition inside the current reach and adds no atom; a genuinely new capability is an import that moves the wall and needs a source gate and shadow test. The anchor for "paying" is a market that does not update on the firm's report.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :21 "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."
- **What to check:** is there a case in which internal reinvestment genuinely adds a new capability with no external contact and no provenance? Is there a case in which the firm's "value created" metric moves when its own forecast changes, so the anchor has become a frame? Jensen 1986 (AER, opened) supplies the agency side: *"Managers have incentives to expand their firms beyond the size that maximizes shareholder wealth."*
- **Refuter:** a source-discriminating instrument shows internal reinvestment paying persistently with no new atom and no contact with a differing closure, against an anchor that does not update; or the metric is shown to be non-updating and still to fail. Either refutes C3.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: *"What the Spectrometer Example Reveals: The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."*

1. A route discriminator that shows which channel actually paid at the firm's own slot, separating project payment from habitat, tax change, accounting effect and unrelated market move.
2. An anchor test: does the value metric move when the firm's own forecast changes?
3. A bond test: do dividend reductions and buyback cancellations have different consequences in the relevant jurisdiction?
4. A refusal test: does persistent free cash with no paying use fail to increase value once contact is held constant?
