# Outreach draft - check the separator before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** this offer works only if stage 1 can be checked without the theory. If the recipient has already absorbed the framework, their agreement stops being evidence (Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."). Send the residual first.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about how free cash flow gets routed

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about capital allocation. I am not asking you to agree with it, and I am not asking you to read any theory first. I am asking what you would count as a failure. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a firm holds free cash flow after obligations. That cash is a residual indexed per firm and per use-slot, not a single global number. There are four visible routes: reinvest, reduce debt, pay a dividend, buy back shares. My claim is that these are not four answers to one question. They are the tips of four overlapping conditions with different anchors:
>
> 1. Reinvest is tested first, but only if there is a paying use already reachable with capabilities the firm holds. Ordinary reinvestment explores the existing reach; it does not by itself add a new atom. A genuinely new capability is an import and needs provenance and a test that it explains a real gap.
> 2. Debt reduction is a comparison against a claim the market prices. A leverage target is a goal, hence a comparison, and it must remain something the market can violate. A target no one can bring themselves to break has stopped being a convention and has become an outcome-fixing constant.
> 3. Dividends and buybacks are close to isomers: same atoms (cash, shares, shareholders), different bond. A dividend creates a recurring expectation that is costly to cut; a buyback is a discrete repurchase with no such obligation. Which bond pays is a jurisdiction-specific question, not a general verdict.
> 4. Free cash with no paying internal use is a term whose residual keeps being wrong and does not trend down. The framework says the owed term is a refusal, so paying it out removes the standing to keep composing over it.
>
> **What this explicitly does not say:** that there is a correct universal payout ratio, a target debt ratio, or a dividend amount. No figure line returns a number, and I am not supplying one by hand. That part is a declared gap, reported as unreachable with the instruments I hold, not as a silence. The conventional finance ratios are candidates with provenance owed, not outputs of this derivation.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim.** I wrote these down before running the derivation or sending this:
>
> 1. A verbatim figure line returns a universal payout ratio, dividend amount, buyback schedule or target debt ratio.
> 2. Internal reinvestment is shown to pay persistently against a market that does not update on the firm's own report, with no new capability and no contact with a differing closure, and a source-discriminating instrument confirms it.
> 3. Dividends and buybacks are shown to be identical in bond, not merely in atoms, over the horizon and jurisdiction in question.
> 4. Persistent free cash with no paying internal use is shown to increase market value persistently without changing contact.
> 5. A non-updating instrument assigns an exclusive, exhaustive partition of cash flows into the four options with an assignable shared fraction.
>
> A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **Stage 1 question:** given that residual and those five refuters, what would you expect to see in a firm's payout behaviour if the shape were true, and what observation would make you reject it? Please answer that before reading anything I have written. I will record your answer as it stands, and I will not treat later agreement as corroboration.
>
> **Stage 2, opt-in only, and only after stage 1 is recorded:** if the output survived and you want to see where it came from, the figure text and the formula are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **Stage 3, if you want to reuse the method:** run the loop on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name / role]

## Note on the venue

Use the public institutional channels in `03_public_contact_points.csv` (journal submissions, conference calls for papers, an opt-in preprint server). Do not use personal emails, do not rank recipients by likely agreement, and do not send this as a mass mailing. A call for papers or a submission portal is a ground-visible route that does not require a personal network.

## What is deliberately absent from the email

- No claim that the framework settles the payout question.
- No percentage, no target ratio, no named "best" bond.
- No biography, no credentials, no list of famous finance authors who agree.
- No request to forward, to circulate, or to endorse.
- No link until stage 1 is recorded.

The reason for the last item is Fig 4 :25 "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." Sending the architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator.
