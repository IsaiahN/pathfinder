# Isomers and order in the cash-routing question

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The question presents four uses of cash as if they were four points on one menu. Two structural questions are owed: (1) are any of the four **isomers** under Fig 12's definition, and (2) what **order** actually holds among them, sequence, threshold or undirected union? A third question follows: is the relation between retention and payout the unary refusal `¬` or the binary subtraction `−`? Getting these wrong changes the answer even if the finance facts are the same.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held in the finance frame: cash, shares, shareholders, outstanding claims, projects, capabilities. Bonds held: the seven plus negation. The market is the ground, not an atom. No new primitive is available without import. The four named options are bindings over these atoms, not members of the six-term physical substrate (see `01` retraction R1).

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are dividends and buybacks isomers? YES, over a fixed atom set

- Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance." Fig 12 :5 "The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
- Fixed atom set: {cash, shares, shareholders}. Nothing in the atom list distinguishes the two routes.
- Dividend bond: a recurring obligation to all holders, costly to cut. Lintner 1956 (opened): *"the dependent variable in the decision-making process is the change in the existing rate, not the amount of the newly established rate as such."* Brav et al. 2005 (opened): 94 percent of dividend payers try to avoid reducing dividends.
- Buyback bond: a discrete repurchase from those who sell, no recurring obligation. Brav et al.: *"many managers now favor repurchases because they are viewed as being more flexible than dividends"*; substitution from repurchases to dividends is asymmetric and rare.
- Therefore same atoms, different bond, different substance. They are **isomers**, not two spellings of one payout.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a jurisdiction and horizon are found in which the two routes have identical recurrence, identical tax treatment, identical market response to reduction and identical flexibility, the isomer claim fails and they collapse to one term. Equivalently: equal survival rates across a shock that forces a cut.
- Status: `triangulated` (Fig 12 definition plus the empirical asymmetry, low overlap).

### Q2 - Is reinvestment a composition or an import? BOTH, in different cases

- Fig 6 :21 "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Fig 6 :14 "the only operator that moves the wall".
- If the paying use can be built from capabilities the firm already holds, reinvestment is composition inside the closure. No atom is added. This is the ordinary case and it is searched first (Formula step 7 SEARCHED).
- If the paying use requires a capability the firm does not hold, it is an import. Fig 8 :51 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them" and Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." An acquisition, a licence or a new market is such a contact, and it needs a source gate (provenance) and a shadow test (does it explain a real gap in the residual?).
- A wrong import costs more than starting from primitives (Fig 6 :24 "A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."). This is why diversification-driven reinvestment is watched in the agency literature: Jensen 1986 (opened) notes that *"diversification programs are more likely to generate losses than takeovers or expansion in the same line of business"*.
- **Falsifier:** if a firm's reinvestment route adds a capability with no contact with a differing closure and no provenance, the closure account fails. If existing-capability reinvestment is shown to move the wall, Fig 6 :21 fails for this domain.
- Status: `triangulated` (Fig 6 plus Fig 8, low overlap: wall versus provenance).

### Q3 - What order holds among the four routes? SEARCH, REFUSE, RETURN. NOT A MENU

- Fig 12 :30 "A then B; B needs A to have happened" defines sequence, an ordered bond. Fig 12 :112 "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."
- Formula step 7 runs SEARCHED first, then INWARD, then OUTWARD. Applied to cash:
  1. **SEARCHED:** is there a paying use inside the current reach? This is tested before anything else because it is cheapest and the other two assume its answer.
  2. **INWARD:** if the internal instrument is too coarse, sharpen it from an instrument already returning something (Fig 6 :32 "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."). For a firm this is a better project-level measure while keeping the market anchor reachable.
  3. **OUTWARD:** if the use is not in the closure, contact a differing closure with a described residual, a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit").
  4. **REFUSE:** if no paying use is found and the residual persists and does not trend down, the owed term is a refusal, not another explanation (Fig 5 :53 "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so").
  5. **RETURN:** the residual goes to the claimant whose ground it belongs to. Between the two equity payout bonds there is no further universal order (they are isomers, Q1).
- Debt reduction sits at step 4/5 depending on the condition: it is a comparison against a claim (Fig 12 :87 "a threshold relation between two quantities") and a refusal of the standing of that claim in the firm's library (Operators :58 "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate").
- So the four options are **not** a flat sequence and **not** a symmetric menu. They are an ordered search-and-refuse spine with an isomeric branch at the end.
- **Falsifier:** if a firm's payout order is shown to be reversible without cost or condition (dividend cut, buyback cancellation and debt retirement all equally cheap and equally informative at the same moment), the ordered-spine reading fails and the menu reading stands.
- Status: `triangulated` (Formula step 7, Fig 5 refusal amendment, Fig 12 sequence and comparison).

### Q4 - Is debt reduction `¬` or `−`? BOTH, in two different frames

- Operators :73-74: *"Subtraction is the nearest bond and is not it. − is A with B removed or absent, which is the absence of an operand. Negation is the refusal of a claim."*
- In the creditor's frame, retiring debt is `−` in effect: the claim is settled and the operand is absent from the balance sheet. The lender's ledger records an extinguished obligation.
- In the firm's frame, the same event is `¬` in effect: the debt term loses its standing to be composed over. Future cash is no longer bound to that claim. Fig 5 :55 "What it removes is not a term but a term's standing to be composed over."
- This is not a contradiction. Fig 10 :68 "Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between." Two frames, one event.
- **Falsifier:** if the same event is shown to be only one of the two, with no frame transform, the two-frame note is withdrawn and the operator mapping simplified.
- Status: `single-source` on the figure side (Operators table), kept as a precise notation note, not a verdict-bearing claim.

### Q5 - Is the four-option set a chain or a union? A UNION WITH AN ORDERED SPINE

- Fig 12 :48 "A chain is the degenerate case: one bond type, no branching." A chain would mean one route followed by the next in a fixed line.
- Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." The conditions overlap, so the structure is a union.
- Both statements hold at different levels. The union is the *condition structure* (which conditions bind on this dollar); the ordered spine is the *procedure* (in what order conditions are tested). Confusing them is the error of reading the question's menu as a chain.
- **Falsifier:** if the conditions are shown to be exclusive and exhaustive by a non-updating instrument, the union collapses to a chain or partition and this distinction is unnecessary.
- Status: `triangulated` (Fig 12 plus Fig 9).

### Q6 - What crosses between periods? METHODS AND HEAD STARTS, NEVER A RECORDING

- Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Fig 4 :25 "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."
- A rule that can be reapplied (search the closure first, keep the anchor reachable, refuse persistent flat residuals) crosses from one period to the next. A specific dollar amount from last year's buyback does not.
- Fig 12 :107 "Settling compresses the description and not what it cost to establish." A smooth dividend series is a compressed description; the work that established the expectation is not carried in the series and must be maintained by continuing contact with shareholders and the market.
- **Falsifier:** if a one-off allocation can be replayed in a new period with the same payoff and no re-derivation, the method/recording boundary fails for this domain.
- Status: `triangulated` (Fig 4 plus Fig 12).

## CONCLUSION

Dividends and buybacks are **isomers** over a fixed atom set, distinguished by bond. Reinvestment is **composition** when it uses held capabilities and **import** when it contacts a differing closure, with provenance and a shadow test required in the second case. The order among the routes is an **ordered search-and-refuse spine with an isomeric branch**, not a menu and not a chain. Debt retirement is `−` in the creditor's frame and `¬` in the firm's frame. The condition structure is a **union** with an ordered test procedure. Across periods, only methods and head starts cross.

These results rescue what is structurally defensible in the question without inventing a number, a universal ranking or a seventh primitive. What remains open is which bond pays in a given jurisdiction and which route actually paid in a given firm, both of which need an instrument this folder does not hold.
