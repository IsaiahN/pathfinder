## 0. The question as a residual, stated in effect terms

The question asks us to separate two kinds of developmental regularity: those that survive a change of culture and those that change with family structure, schooling, or local work.

**Effect statement.** We hold a prediction about what a child will come to attend to, feel, expect of others, and be able to do. We observe that prediction hold in some habitats and fail in others. The residual `R` is per pattern, per habitat: the gap between what our developmental prediction says and what the child actually does in that habitat. What is owed is not a single theory of development but a rule for deciding which predictions are kernel-like (invariant under a change of habitat) and which are habitat-bound (a function of the actors the child meets).

**What an answer would look like (binding the sought-for shape as an ordinary term, per the Formula's "Direction").** An answer is a *partition rule*: for any developmental pattern P, it says whether P is composed inside the kernel and therefore invariant, or composed over habitat actors and therefore local, and it names the instrument that would settle the classification at the ground. A list of universals without such a rule is not an answer; it is a recording of which samples happened to be measured.

**Two traps named before the derivation.** First, the question is bundled: it asks about attention, emotional regulation, expectations of others, norms, agency, and timing all at once. Second, it names three candidate frames (family structure, schooling, local work) before the residual has been described. Fig 9 :27-28 warns that "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Both traps are handled in the census below.

## 1. Domain census, before any figure line is chosen

### 1a. R by arity, symmetry, scale (Fig 9 :22)

- **Arity:** many. Each developmental pattern is its own slot. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." There is no single "development across cultures" residual.
- **Symmetry:** asymmetric across habitats, symmetric across instances of the same pattern. Culture A and culture B are not interchangeable (different actors, different currency in some domains), but the *regularity* is symmetric if it recurs under the same instrument in both. Two studies of the same WEIRD sample are two readings of one event, not two frames.
- **Scale:** step (a child's learning trial and correction), cycle (a stage transition), level (the habitat or cultural niche), lineage (transmission across generations). The question spans all four, so any figure quoted must be checked for which scale it governs.

### 1b. One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor

**Domain 1, the developing child (the kernel and the loop).**
Actors: the child, caretakers, peers, teachers as the immediate cast. Currency: residuals and corrections, what the child predicts and what the world pays back. Anchor: the child's own contact with what works (the ground for that child). Nameable anchor present, so this is a domain. This is the candidate kernel.

**Domain 2, the habitat (family structure, schooling, local work).**
Actors: physical and social settings, customs of child care, caretaker psychology, plus the work and school settings the child enters. Currency: contact and introduction of actors, who is present and what the child must practise. Anchor: the environment actually rewards some behaviour and not others, so a nameable ground exists. This is the domain the question calls "family structure, schooling, or local work."

**Domain 3, the verification frame (cross-cultural developmental science).**
Actors: researchers, samples, tasks, instruments, reviewers. Currency: agreement and residuals between studies. Anchor: the ground reached through an instrument in each habitat, never agreement itself. Nameable anchor exists, but in this simulated run all frames share an evidence pool, so its independence is low. This is why the question cannot be settled from the armchair.

### 1c. Partition or union?

Union, not partition. Domains 1 and 2 overlap in a compound: the child-in-a-habitat. Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." A developmental pattern is not a property of the child alone or of the culture alone; it is a property of the pair. That overlap is its own domain with its own loop (Fig 9 :54-58: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"). Domains 2 and 3 overlap too: the habitat is described through the verification frame's instruments, which is Fig 10's seam. So the answer must be per slot and per compound, never a global average.

### 1d. Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. Enumerate the habitat before judging the child (Fig 11 :43-44). 2. Describe the per-slot residual in effect terms before naming family, school, or work as the cause (Fig 9 :27-28). 3. Range each hypothesis against an instrument in more than one low-overlap habitat (Fig 2). 4. Only then classify the pattern as kernel-like or habitat-bound. Effort spent at step 4 before steps 1 to 3 is effort above the break and cannot arrive.

## 3. Findings, each quoted before concluding

### F1 - The question is a union of slots, not one hard question

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
Fig 9 :5: "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."

**Derivation.** Asking "is development universal?" aggregates the slots. The figures require per-slot residuals. So the honest answer is a partition rule applied pattern by pattern, not one verdict for "development."

**Verdict: `general`, triangulated.** Fig 1 (slot indexing) and Fig 9 (bundle splitting) have low overlap: one is about the measurement index, the other about question structure. **Refuter:** if one mechanism with one residual accounted for attention, regulation, expectations, norms, and agency under a single instrument, the union claim would fail and a global answer would be admissible.

### F2 - The robust/local split is the kernel/conditions split

Fig 4 :20: "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."
Fig 4 :34-35: "A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."
*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "A human keeps a stable kernel (DNA) but changes as a person over time. The Tether is like a human. The kernel is fixed. The operating system grows."

**Derivation.** The child carries a kernel: the loop, the capacity to abstract from regularities, the recursion by which a settled piece becomes an operand for the next. What the child becomes is decided by the conditions. So "robust across cultures" names the kernel and the invariant cross-kind compositions; "depends on family structure, schooling, or local work" names what the conditions decide. This is the core answer shape.

**Verdict: `general` on the shape, triangulated.** Fig 4 and the essay agree from different directions (scale membrane vs. seed metaphor). **Refuter:** show an agent-only change to a child's internals that moves a developmental outcome without any change in contact. Fig 11 :40 says that cannot happen; if it did, the kernel/conditions split would be wrong.

### F3 - A developmental pattern is a property of child and habitat together

Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."
Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."
Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."

**Derivation.** Family structure, schooling, and local work are not causes with fixed valence; they are habitat actors whose valence depends on which residual we track. The same actor (an older sibling, a teacher, a workplace) is a benefactor for one pattern and a malfactor for another. Enumerate them from the world, do not compose them from a theory. Super and Harkness's three-component developmental niche is a world-side enumeration that matches this figure (see `09_watson_answer.md`).

**Verdict: `specified`, triangulated** (Fig 11 plus Fig 4 :34-35, low overlap). **Refuter:** exhibit a habitat actor whose effect on a named residual is invariant across habitats and whose valence does not depend on the residual; that would break the relativity claim.

### F4 - Same capacities, different arrangement, different outcome

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."
Fig 13 :103: "Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."

**Derivation.** Children everywhere hold the same starting capacities. Cultures differ in which bonds those capacities enter: who attends to whom, what is practised, what is rewarded. Under Fig 12, two developmental pathways can share every atom and still be different substances. This is why a pattern can be "the same capacity" and "a different outcome" at once. It also licenses the recursion: a pattern settled in one habitat becomes an atom for the next, which is how traditions and curricula accrete.

**Verdict: `specified`, single-source for the figure definition (Fig 12), with the recursion triangulated by Fig 13 :103 and Fig 12 :104.** **Refuter:** show two cultures with the same atoms and the same bonds producing different developmental behaviour, or a pattern that cannot be written as an arrangement of prior settled pieces; either would collapse the isomer reading.

### F5 - Robustness is not counted agreement

Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."
Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"
Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
Fig 7 :36: "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."

**Derivation.** A pattern is robust only if it recurs under an instrument in habitats that do not draw on the same evidence. A hundred studies of the same population, or many studies using one instrument and one author tradition, are one room wearing many names. Henrich, Heine and Norenzayan make the world-side version of this point about WEIRD samples; Callaghan et al. show what low overlap buys when a single procedure is carried to five cultures.

**Verdict: `general` on the method, triangulated** (Fig 2 and Fig 7, low overlap: anchoring vs. handoff). **Refuter:** show two frames with genuinely disjoint evidence whose agreement on a pattern still fails to survive a ground test; that would mean independence is not sufficient, and the rule needs an added condition.

### F6 - Describe the slot before naming family, school, or work

Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."
Fig 6 :30: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."

**Derivation.** The question names three candidate causes before describing what failed. That is the inversion Fig 9 forbids. The correct order is: state the pattern in effect terms (what the child is expected to do, where the prediction fails), then ask which habitat closure predicts that shape. Family, school, and work are then hypotheses to be tested, not a list to distribute the answer among.

**Verdict: `general`, triangulated** (Fig 9 and Fig 6, different moves: search order vs. description form). **Refuter:** exhibit a case where naming the frame first yields a prediction that survives an independent ground test in a second habitat; possible, but it would not overturn the rule, only show the inversion was harmless in that case. The rule itself is a discipline, not an empirical claim.

### F7 - No armchair list of universals

Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
`THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."

**Derivation.** The figure set can say what a robust pattern must look like (kernel-like, invariant under habitat change, triangulated across low-overlap habitats) and what a local pattern must look like (composed over habitat actors). It cannot say *which* of the many slots are in fact kernel-like. That requires an instrument in each habitat. "Unreached, not unreachable" is the honest status. The apparent clash between Henrich et al. (little is safely universal) and Callaghan et al. (a false-belief milestone is universal) is resolved per slot by F1: they are reporting different slots and different quantities (variation of expression versus presence and sequence of onset).

**Verdict: `general`, triangulated** (Fig 8 and Formula step 5, underivable vs. settlement). **Refuter:** derive a complete, correct list of cross-cultural universals from the figure set alone, with no instrument reading; that would refute the claim that a shape is all the framework returns.

### F8 - Contact is how the local part gets built

*Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."
Same essay: "An apprentice isn't receiving messages; they're slowly building the history that makes the messages make sense."
Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."

**Derivation.** Children are apprentices. Much of what they acquire (attention, emotional regulation, expectations of others) is tacit and rebuilt through two-way contact and correction, not transmitted as instructions. This explains *why* family structure and local work bind those patterns: they determine the contact the child has. What crosses a cultural boundary is a method or a head start, never a recording of what worked once. So schooling may refine a capacity without being necessary for its onset, which is exactly what Callaghan et al. report.

**Verdict: `specified`, triangulated** (the tacit-knowledge essay and Fig 4 :22, different domains: knowledge transfer vs. scale membrane). **Refuter:** show a pattern transmitted fully by explicit instruction with no contact, in a habitat where the child never practises it; the contact claim would then be too strong.

### F9 - Strain, re-census, and what survives

**Initial strained reading (recorded, not erased).** I first tried to map Fig 13's six physical terms (space, time, energy, spectrum, gradient, network) directly onto child development, reading "robust" as the six and "local" as their arrangements. That strains: the six are the substrate the loop assumes for physical systems, and no line in the figure text licenses identifying a child's attention with "gradient" or a child's expectation with "time." A line that fits only with a strain governs another quantity, so I re-censused.

**Re-census.** The relevant invariant is not the specific six but the *kernel* Fig 4 :19-20 names: "The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked." Fig 13 :103 (recursion) and :74 ("Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one") are then used only as structural claims about composition, not as a literal developmental vocabulary. Under that re-census the strain dissolves: F2 through F4 stand; the literal six-term map is withdrawn.

**Verdict: re-census completed, no live strain.** Recorded as a retraction in §8.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the standing rule that "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument." The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **R1, robust-slot failure.** A pattern classified kernel-like (for example the sequence of false-belief understanding) fails to appear under a standardised instrument in a genuinely low-overlap habitat. The robust claim is then refused for that slot only, not globally.
2. **R2, local-slot failure.** A pattern classified habitat-bound (for example the expression of sensitive caregiving) is invariant across low-overlap habitats under a discriminating instrument. The local-dependence claim is then refused for that slot.
3. **R3, evidence-pool failure.** The evidence for a "robust" pattern comes from one pool (one population, one instrument tradition, one author group), so Fig 2 :40 voids the triangulation.
4. **R4, contact failure.** An agent-only change to the child moves a developmental outcome without changing contact, refuting Fig 11 :40 and the kernel/habitat split.
5. **R5, composition failure.** A pattern shown by the ground to be composed entirely within one kind, with no cross-kind mediation, if the framework is claimed to govern development through cross-kind composition. This sends the claim back to census rather than flipping a figure.

Until one of these is run, no part of this answer may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Search order: SEARCHED, INWARD, OUTWARD

Kept distinct per Formula step 7.

- **SEARCHED (asked first, cheapest).** Within the figure set, is there a line that closes the residual? Yes for the *shape*: Fig 4's kernel/conditions, Fig 11's habitat, Fig 12's arrangement. No for the *inventory* of which patterns are universal: no line names a developmental slot, and the figures README forbids expecting a name. So the residual is partly closed and partly a genuine limit.
- **INWARD (sharpen our own instrument).** Build a per-slot, cross-habitat instrument that discriminates presence, sequence, and timing separately. The Callaghan standardized procedure and the Liu meta-analysis are existing instruments returning something; they can be sharpened. Inward fails if it refines forever on timing while never resolving presence.
- **OUTWARD (import from a frame whose closure predicts the shape).** Cross-cultural developmental psychology and evolutionary developmental biology supply the missing atoms: the developmental niche, reaction norms, canalization versus plasticity. These are debited against independence (Fig 6 :24), and they carry a source gate and a shadow test in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

INWARD and OUTWARD are opposed and were not read against one another.

## 8. Round-trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines `R_T` as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, and Fig 4 :30 requires it be measured rather than assumed.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 4 :20 and :34-35, Fig 11 :39 and :43, Fig 12 :117.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand "robust = kernel, local = habitat-bound, triangulate across habitats."
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete claim such as "false-belief onset is universal; caregiving sensitivity is cultural."

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip down:

- the **slot specificity** (Fig 1 :19): the shorthand is per pattern, but a concrete universal claim tends to aggregate;
- the **independence requirement** (Fig 2 :31, :40): the shorthand for triangulation across habitats silently becomes a count of agreeing studies;
- the **relativity of actor valence** (Fig 11 :49): a concrete claim treats schooling or family structure as having a fixed effect, which the figure denies.

What survives cleanly: a kernel is required, the conditions decide what it becomes, and same atoms under different arrangement yield different outcomes. R_T was recorded qualitatively as the set of dropped constraints, not measured numerically, because no cross-cultural ground reading was taken in this derivation. Treating any condensation of the figures as identical to the law set would set R_T to zero, which is the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
