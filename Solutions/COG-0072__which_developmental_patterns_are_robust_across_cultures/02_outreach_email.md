**Subject:** Check one claim about cross-cultural development before deciding whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The figures distinguish a kernel (the part that does not change with size, the loop and the rule for naming what worked) from the conditions a seed lands in, which decide what it becomes. Applied to development, the claim is a partition rule: a pattern is robust across cultures when it sits in that kernel or is an invariant composition over it; it is local when it is composed over habitat actors such as family structure, schooling, or local work. Same capacities under a different arrangement give a different developmental substance, the way an isomer differs from its formula-mate. The framework returns that shape and that rule, not a definitive list of universals.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I can name from the armchair which specific pattern is universal. That needs an instrument in each habitat. I am not claiming that many studies agreeing makes a pattern robust, because agreement from one evidence pool is the pool talking. I am not claiming that family structure, schooling, or work have fixed effects: the figures say an actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a pattern classed as kernel-like fails to appear under a standardised instrument in a genuinely low-overlap habitat;
> 2. a pattern classed as habitat-bound turns out invariant across low-overlap habitats under a discriminating instrument;
> 3. the evidence for a "robust" pattern comes from one population, one instrument tradition, or one author group;
> 4. an agent-only change to the child moves the outcome without changing contact.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the affected part. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers: local copy at `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in the project; public figures folder: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - The canonical architecture essay: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
> - Cultural structuring of child development (an independent world source, not framework material): https://chhd.media.uconn.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/3087/2024/06/Developmental-Niche-1986.pdf
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a developmental problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
