# Expert verification packet - the Tether on COG-0072

**What this is:** a claim in a form a cross-cultural developmental scientist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the sub-claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No new cross-cultural measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A developmental pattern is robust across cultures when it sits in the kernel (the loop and the invariant cross-kind compositions over it) or when it recurs under a shared instrument in habitats that do not draw on the same evidence. It is local when it is composed over habitat actors, where an actor's valence is relative to the pattern being tracked rather than fixed. Same starting capacities under different cultural bonds give different developmental substances, as isomers share a formula and differ in behaviour. The framework returns that partition rule, not a list of which patterns are universal; filling the slots is a ground job.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 4 and 12

- **Statement:** there is a kernel that does not change with habitat, and the variation is in the arrangement over it. Same atoms, different bond, different substance.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :20 - "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." Fig 4 :34-35 - "A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes." Fig 12 :117 - "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- **Refuter:** show two habitats with the same starting capacities and the same arrangement producing different developmental behaviour under a discriminating instrument; or show an agent-only change that moves an outcome without changing contact (Fig 11 :40).

### C2 - world-facing; checkable against cross-cultural developmental evidence

- **Statement:** the robust/local split is observable inside a single slot. Presence and sequence of a capacity can be robust while timing and expression are local. Habitat actors do not carry fixed valence.
- **What to check:** Callaghan et al. (2005), *Psychological Science*, used one procedure in Canada, India, Peru, Samoa and Thailand and report "synchrony in the onset of mentalistic reasoning, with children crossing the false-belief milestone at approximately 5 years of age in every culture studied." The same paper: "Schooling may help to refine understanding of false belief, but is not necessary for the onset of this understanding." Liu et al. (2008), *Developmental Psychology*, report "parallel developmental trajectories of false-belief understanding" with locales differing "by as much as 2 or more years." Rothbaum et al. (2000), *American Psychologist*: "What constitutes sensitive, responsive caregiving is likely to reflect indigenous values and goals, which are apt to differ from one society to the next."
- **Refuter:** show that the apparent onset synchrony disappears under a task that discriminates source, or that sensitivity's expression is invariant across genuinely low-overlap habitats under an indigenous instrument. Either refutes the corresponding slot classification, not the whole partition rule.

### C3 - verification-frame; checkable by reading Figure 2

- **Statement:** robustness is a property of the evidence relation. Agreement drawn from one evidence pool cannot establish it.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :31 - "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." Fig 2 :40 - "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Fig 7 :36 - "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."
- **What to check:** Henrich, Heine and Norenzayan (2010), *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*: "members of WEIRD societies, including young children, are among the least representative populations one could find for generalizing about humans." Super and Harkness (1986), *International Journal of Behavioral Development*, enumerate "the physical and social settings in which the child lives; the customs of child care and child rearing; and the psychology of the caretakers," which is the habitat that must be enumerated before a pattern is classified.
- **Refuter:** exhibit two frames with genuinely disjoint evidence whose agreement on a pattern still fails a ground test. That would mean independence is insufficient and the rule needs an added condition.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a list of universals, and not a verdict on any named developmental pattern.
- Not that culture is a fixed cause, or that any habitat actor has invariant valence.
- Not that a cross-cultural difference is a deficit; an isomer is a different substance, not a broken one.
- Not that many studies agreeing establishes robustness.
- Not that the six physical terms map literally onto child development; that reading was retracted in `01` F9.
- Not `grounded`. A source-discriminating instrument is still owed.
- Not that this derivation settles the question. It is a simulation checked for internal consistency.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. Does a pattern persist in presence and sequence across low-overlap habitats under one instrument, while rate and expression vary? If yes, the kernel/local split is confirmed for that slot.
2. Does the apparent invariance survive when the instrument is changed, so the reading no longer reflects one task tradition?
3. Does an agent-only intervention move the outcome without changing contact? If yes, Fig 11 :40 is refuted and the kernel/habitat split fails.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 4 :20 and :34-35, Fig 12 :117, Fig 11 :49 and Fig 2 :40, five short passages.
2. Check C2 against evidence you know: is there a slot where presence and sequence look robust while timing and expression vary?
3. Name one instrument that would discriminate the source of an apparent universality (maturation, universal experience, or shared method), or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
