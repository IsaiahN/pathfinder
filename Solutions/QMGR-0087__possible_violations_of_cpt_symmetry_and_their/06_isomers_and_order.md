## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Does a symmetry violation make two readings one term or two?

- Fig 12 :92: *"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing. Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term, so a code that charges"* and :93 *"for the sequence overcharges by log₂(k!) while the bond term undercharges. Both wrong, in opposite directions, and only one is usually noticed."*
- Fig 12 :23: *"this bond is symmetric, so the four are one term and nothing consults the bond to find that out"*. Under a symmetric relation, reorderings are spellings of the same term.
- Applying this to a combined transform: while CPT is a symmetry of the arrangement, the matter reading and the CPT-transformed reading are spellings of one term. The invariant is the common quantity, not either spelling. If the symmetry breaks, the relation is no longer symmetric in the relevant sense, and the two readings cannot be collapsed. They become distinct terms.
- **Result:** a CPT violation does not create matter and antimatter (the atoms were always there). It changes the bond so that two spellings become two terms. `general` structurally, `specified` for the physics application. Triangulated (Fig 12 :92 and :23 are different passages with the same consequence).

### Q2 - Are the matter and antimatter readings isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Hydrogen and antihydrogen share the same structural inventory (a proton-like and an electron-like bound state) with charge-conjugate atoms. Under the CPT-invariant reading they are one term with two spellings. Under a genuine violation with the bond changed, they are **isomers**: same atoms, different arrangement, different spectral substance. This is a checkable structural claim, and the 2025 ALPHA measurement constrains exactly this difference through the 1S-2S transition.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a measured matter-antimatter difference can be removed by a same-frame coordinate change or a Lorentz boost, the readings were spellings of one term and the isomer claim fails (the difference was a frame effect, not a bond change). This is not hypothetical: the ALPHA paper warns precisely about cross-frame comparisons.
- The chemistry corroborator is low overlap and structural only. Demming (2019) reports that mirror-image molecules (enantiomers) are isomers with a predicted parity-violating energy difference, unobserved in the Soai reaction at the required magnitude. The shape transfers; the magnitude does not.

### Q3 - What is the negation that a violation owes, and is it a bond?

- Fig 5 :56: *"Requires an operator for negation, which the seven bonds do not contain."* Operators :70-73: *"Seven of the eight are bonds: they join two operands, and both the symmetry column and the tests are questions about how two things join. Negation joins nothing, so its symmetry entry is empty rather than no, and its test asks about the operand it has rather than about an arrangement."*
- A refusal of CPT invariance takes one operand (the held invariance claim) and returns a term that denies it. It joins nothing. Writing it as a binary bond (for example, as a comparison between a particle coefficient and an antiparticle coefficient) is a notation error: the comparison reads the *signal*, the negation refuses the *claim*.
- Operators :77-78: *"The price is unchanged. |bonds| in the formula above is the choice among ways to join k operands, and negation is not one of them, so it does not enter that count. What a unary operator costs is open: an atom slot, nothing, or the choice of what to negate."*
- **Result:** do not price the refusal with the bond formula. Record the price as an open item. Triangulated (Fig 5 amendment and Operators amendment are distinct passages). `general`.

### Q4 - What is the dependency order, and where does the chain break?

| link | domain | status in this question |
|---|---|---|
| 1 perception | D2 measurement | works: antihydrogen, kaons, neutrinos, Penning traps all return readings |
| 2 vocabulary | D4 symmetry and operator terms | works: invariance, transform, bond, negation, isomer are available terms |
| 3 objective | D1 theorem | works: the theorem states what would count as a violation |
| 4 planning | D3 candidate frameworks | **the likely break:** moving from a bound to a unified theory has no instrument |
| 5 learn and carry | provenance of bindings | partial: SME data tables record provenance; theory-of-unification claims often do not |

- Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step"* (continued :21 *"that cannot use it."*).
- Fig 3 :25-26: *"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."*
- Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
- Effort spent making accelerators more precise lands above the break if the planning link (what a positive signal would license about a unified theory) has no instrument. That link is the one this solution tries to equip, with a shape and a refuter.
- **Falsifier:** if a published, checkable instrument takes an SME coefficient and returns a unique unified theory, the break is repaired and this finding is narrowed to the remaining slots.

### Q5 - What is the order of the union, and can one lever clear it?

The four domains are not ordered by importance but by dependency, and they overlap (Fig 9 :54, :57). A single lever such as "assume Lorentz violation" clears the D1/D2 intersection where the theorem premise is at issue, and leaves the D3 intersection standing where a differing closure and a provenanced import are required. Conversely, importing a string-theory mechanism clears D3 and leaves D2's same-frame measurement requirement standing. Unbundle before searching again (Fig 9 :54). Verdicts stay per domain and are never averaged.

## CONCLUSION

A CPT violation changes the bond, not the atom set: what were two spellings of one term become distinct, isomeric terms (Fig 12 :92, :117), corroborated at low overlap by the chemistry of mirror-image molecules. The refusal it owes is a unary negation, not a bond, and its price is an open item (Fig 5 :56, Operators :77-78). The chain is likely to break at the objective-to-planning link, where instruments exist for measurement but not for moving from a bound to a theory of unification (Fig 3 :25-27). One lever cannot clear the union. The name of a unified theory remains a `gap`: the framework gives the shape that any such theory would have, not a candidate's name (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134).

This framing rescues what is defensible in the question without inventing a result: the symmetry-to-isomer reading is structural and checkable; the negation requirement is a notation discipline; the break is located and instrumentable. `metric difference = CPT violation` remains an unproven binding until the same-frame test is made; `matter and antimatter are isomers under a broken CPT bond` is a checkable structural claim.
