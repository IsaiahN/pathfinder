## 0. The residual under audit, stated in effect terms

The question is: *possible violations of CPT symmetry and their implications for a unified theory*.

Restated as an effect, not as a name:

> A frame predicts that a combined transformation (charge conjugation C, parity P, time reversal T) leaves its observable predictions unchanged. For a set of comparison slots s (particle versus antiparticle mass, lifetime, charge-to-mass ratio, spectroscopic frequency, oscillation parameter, decay asymmetry), the frame predicts a zero difference. The ground returns o′ with an uncertainty. R_s = |Gamma(b,a) - o′| is measured per slot. A violation is a slot where R_s is genuinely positive where the frame predicted zero. What is the status of that residual, and what may be concluded about a "unified theory"?

- **The residual is indexed by slot, not aggregated.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A single global verdict over all CPT tests is the named error. Keep the kaon slot, the antihydrogen slot, the neutrino slot and the cosmological-asymmetry slot separate.
- **The residual is a slice.** Symbols table :38-40: *"It is the aim, not only the error signal, and it is always a slice: what cannot be perceived yet is residual and simply cannot be read."* A hypothetical violation below current sensitivity is unreadable, not absent.
- **The anchor is nameable.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Measurement and experiment settle CPT. So this is a well-formed question, not yet a `gap`.
- **The sought-for term is not bound.** "A unified theory" is the shape the question reaches for, not a term the loop holds. This is the central finding of F3 below.

**Direction, bound before the run (Formula "Direction"):** a candidate answer to this question would be (i) a classification of what a CPT violation would be in the loop, (ii) a shape any theory surviving such a violation would have, and (iii) a refuter that would fire if the violation were better described as an added atom than as a refusal of a held term. This paragraph is the binding of that shape as an ordinary term. It now predicts, and can accrue a residual.

## 1. Domain census, before any figure line is chosen

### 1.1 Arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity.** The object is a transform T_CPT acting on a theory, followed by a comparison of observable spectra before and after the transform. That is at least a transform (unary on a theory) and a binary comparison of two readings. It is not a two-place "symmetry yes/no" relation; that would lose the comparison and the transform.
- **Symmetry.** The question is explicitly about symmetry, so the symmetry question is about the arrangement rather than about an operator name. Fig 12 :92: *"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."* Under a symmetry, k spellings collapse to one term; under a broken symmetry, mirror readings become distinct terms. The relevant bond question is whether the transform maps a term to itself, not whether C or P or T is individually conserved (they are not: the weak interaction violates C and P and CP; the combined operation is the invariant).
- **Scale.** The theorem operates at the frame level (a whole theory), the test operates at the level of individual particle slots, and the "unified theory" is a cross-scale claim. The membrane between scales is lossy (Fig 4).

### 1.2 Domains, one line each (actors, currency, anchor)

X1 fails the anchor test: no nameable anchor and no bound currency. It is a preference held by the searcher, exactly the case Fig 5 warns about. X2 is not a separate domain because a violation is a property of a residual, not an actor or a currency.

### 1.3 Union or partition

**Union, not partition.** D1 and D2 share the invariance claim; D2 and D3 share the experimental ground; D4 governs the structure of all three. The overlap is a compound with its own loop. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* A single lever (for example "build a better theory") leaves the intersections standing: a null experimental bound does not settle a theorem, and a theorem does not settle a mechanism.

### 1.4 Dependency order (Fig 3)

Perception (D2 measurement) Vocabulary (D4 symmetry and operator terms) Objective (D1 states what counts as a violation) Planning (D3 candidate frameworks) Learn and carry (provenance of which bound was tested). Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* The middle link, having terms for what a violation affords and saying what would count as progress, is where the question actually sits (Fig 3 :25-27). Experiment has instruments at this link; theory-of-unification talk usually does not.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - A CPT violation is the fifth kind of residual, not a new atom (decisive for D1, D2)

Fig 5 :50-56:

> **"AMENDMENT · the gap has a fifth kind, and the pipeline has no branch for it."**
> **"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"**
> **"unexplained before. What it removes is not a term but a term's standing to be composed over. Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach."**

And the fourth route in `THE_FORMULA.md` step 2 is not enough: the first four kinds sort R by what is *missing*. A CPT violation is not missing content; the regularity "CPT invariance holds" is held and generating predictions, and a positive signal would show that regularity false. The correct bin is REFUTED. The owed term is a refusal of the standing of CPT invariance as a composition base, not an added interaction term that explains the signal.

**Refuter:** a positive CPT-violating signal that leaves every held premise (locality, Lorentz invariance, Hermiticity) intact and is fully accommodated by adding a new operator to the existing Lagrangian without removing the standing of any held term. If such a case is demonstrated, F1 is wrong and the violation is a mint (new atom), not a refusal.

### F2 - The refusal is a negation, and negation is not a bond (D1, D4)

Fig 5 :56: *"Requires an operator for negation, which the seven bonds do not contain."* Operators :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."* Fig 12 :134-136: *"AMENDMENT · a bond is a binary operator, and this figure defines the bonds. It does not define the unary ones."*

A CPT violation is a unary refusal of a held claim, not a join of two operands. This is why "CPT violation = a new fundamental interaction" is a category error in the framework's notation: it writes a unary operation as a binary bond. Operators :77-78: *"What a unary operator costs is open: an atom slot, nothing, or the choice of what to negate."* The price of the refusal is an open item, not a number produced by this derivation.

**Refuter:** show that the violation is a typed relation between two operands (for example a comparison ⋛ between a particle and an antiparticle coefficient) and that the negation is dispensable. Demonstrating that would move the case back into the bond set and F2 would be narrowed.

### F3 - "A unified theory" is an unbound preference, and the final room is forbidden (X1, decisive)

Fig 5 :61: *"BUT THE TERM MOST WORTH REFUSING MAY NOT BE IN THE LIBRARY AT ALL."* Fig 5 :62: *"A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term. It is not bound, it predicts nothing, and it accrues no residual, so nothing here can see it."* Fig 5 :65: *"The remedy is a discipline rather than a branch: make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM."*

The question asks for "implications for a unified theory". The phrase names the sought-for shape. No instrument in the loop checks the direction of the question, so nothing inside the derivation can validate it. This is why section 0 binds the shape as an ordinary term before the run.

And a unified theory in the sense of a terminal, all-explaining frame is forbidden. Fig 7 :6 names *"the limit: no last room"*; Fig 7 :30-31: *"every room closes some of the gap;"* *"no finite number of them ever arrives"*. Fig 13 :134-135: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."* The framework can characterise what a surviving theory must look like. It cannot hand over its name, and it denies the final room structurally.

**Refuter:** a derivation within the figures that returns the name of the unified theory, or a figure line that permits a last room. Either would overturn F3. Note that no world source can overturn it: figures are primary, world sources corroborate only (Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*).

### F4 - Symmetry decides whether the mirror readings are one term or two (D4)

Fig 12 :92-93:

> **"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing. Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term, so a code that charges"**
> **"for the sequence overcharges by log₂(k!) while the bond term undercharges. Both wrong, in opposite directions, and only one is usually noticed."**

Fig 12 :23: *"this bond is symmetric, so the four are one term and nothing consults the bond to find that out"*. This is the figure-grounded reading of what a CPT violation *does*: while the transform is a symmetry of the arrangement, the matter reading and the transformed reading are spellings of one term. If the symmetry is broken, the bond is no longer symmetric and the two readings become distinct terms. Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* The world corroboration is strong and low overlap: in chemistry, mirror-image molecules (enantiomers) are isomers whose parity-violating energy difference is expected to be tiny (Demming 2019, `10_source_ledger.csv`), which is exactly the "same atoms, different bond, different substance" shape at a different stratum.

**Refuter:** a case where a discrete symmetry is violated but the two readings remain the same term by every composition test the figures supply, or a case where the readings differ only by a relabelling that the bond set declares symmetric.

### F5 - The ground is experiment, and null is not absence (D2)

Fig 2 :29 (quoted in section 0) and Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Every current CPT test is a bound, not a positive signal. Greenberg's theorem and the SME framework agree that if CPT were violated, Lorentz invariance would also be violated (Greenberg 2002; SME Wikipedia 2026). But the measurements return upper limits. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The honest report is `unreached` for a positive signal, not ` CPT is exact`.

**Refuter:** a positive, independently replicated CPT-violating signal with a stated uncertainty and a published ground reading. That would convert D2 from `unreached` to `grounded` for that slot and would fire F1.

### F6 - Independence between instruments, not agreement among papers (D2, D3)

Fig 2 :31-32:

> **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are. Independence"**
> **"is what makes agreement mean something."**

The antihydrogen 1S-2S measurement, the CPLEAR neutral-kaon interferometry, the Penning-trap antiproton measurements, and the SME data compilation are different instruments pointed at the same invariance. They are genuinely low overlap in the Fig 2 sense, so their agreement is worth something. But the ALPHA paper notes a subtlety that matters here: a hydrogen-antihydrogen difference taken in different frames *"would not necessarily be a signal of CPT violation but could instead be due to a Lorentz violation in a CPT-symmetric theory"* (Baker et al. 2025). That is a warning about which quantity the instrument reads, exactly Fig 2 :37-38 (*"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."* applied at the physics stratum). The comparison must be made in the same frame, or the reading is about boost, not CPT.

**Refuter:** a demonstration that the antihydrogen, kaon and neutrino programmes draw on one shared calibration or one shared theory prior such that they are one instrument wearing three names. Then their agreement carries no independence weight.

### F7 - Per-slot verdicts, never a global average (D1, D2, D3)

Fig 1 :19 and :22 (section 0). The SME data tables list distinct coefficients for the matter, photon, neutrino and gravity sectors (Kostelecky and Russell, 2011/2026). Collapsing them into "CPT is mostly conserved" would throw away the live signal where it might actually be. Each sector gets its own verdict; none is averaged with another.

**Refuter:** a figure-grounded rule that CPT tests aggregate into a single global residual. None was found in a genuine re-census. If one is supplied, F7 is withdrawn.

### F8 - Provenance: derivation and measurement look identical in the contents (D1 vs D2)

Fig 8 :50-52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."* Greenberg's result is a derivation (Lorentz violation follows from CPT violation); the ALPHA bound is a measurement; the SME is a framework that parameterises both. Mixing them into a single "evidence for Lorentz violation" narrative erases the record. Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"* is the reason the residual was stated before the sources were read in this audit (world sources were opened only after section 1 was drafted).

**Refuter:** a case where the provenance record changes nothing about what may be concluded, that is, where a measurement and a derivation are interchangeable as witnesses. Not demonstrated; F8 stands as a discipline.

### F9 - The reach falls when a refusal is added (D1, D4)

Fig 6 :42-43:

> **"What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall. A refutation adds a term that refuses; it removes nothing, and the reach falls because"**
> **"something was added rather than because anything was deleted. So the closure and the reach are two quantities: one monotone, one not."**

A confirmed CPT violation would not delete the theorem from the record. The theorem stays, with its provenance. What falls is the *reach*: theories that depended on composing over exact CPT invariance are no longer derivable under everything held. This resolves an apparent contradiction between Fig 5 (nothing leaves the library) and the intuition that a violation "removes" a symmetry: the removal is in the reach, not the record.

**Refuter:** a demonstration that a refutation in this framework deletes a term rather than refusing it, or that the reach is monotone. Fig 6 :42-43 directly forbids both.

### F10 - The chain breaks at the objective link (D2, D3)

Fig 3 :25-27: *"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."* Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Experimental CPT tests have excellent instruments. The step from a positive signal to a statement about a unified theory has none. That is why the honest answer about the latter is a shape plus a refuter, not a result.

**Refuter:** an instrument that takes a CPT-violating coefficient and returns a unique unified theory. If one is built and works, F10 is narrowed to the remaining slots.

### F11 - A surviving theory must have a differing closure, with a gate (D3, X1)

Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Any theory whose reach can accommodate a genuine CPT violation must come from a frame whose closure differs from local Lorentz-invariant QFT. The world supplies candidates: string theory, loop quantum gravity, noncommutative field theory, brane-world scenarios (SME Wikipedia 2026). These are imports, so they carry a source gate and a shadow test. Fig 8 :12-14: *"source gate: provenance"* and *"shadow test: explains a gap"*. Without both, importing "string theory says CPT can break" is a wrong atom bought at a cost above starting from primitives (Fig 6 :24).

**Refuter:** a theory that accommodates a CPT violation purely by re-arranging the terms of the current closure, with no new atom and no import. Fig 6 :21 (*"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom"*) would then be the wrong instrument, or the violation was not a genuine one.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Required before any outreach, per the seat map: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim in this solution is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A positive CPT-violating signal is demonstrated that leaves all held premises intact and is accommodated as a new composable atom (kills F1 and F2, the "refusal" reading).
2. A figure line is produced that returns the name of a final or unified theory, or that permits a last room (kills F3).
3. A CPT violation is shown to be a binary bond rather than a unary refusal, so the negation analysis collapses (kills F2).
4. The mirror readings under a broken CPT transform are shown to be one term under every figure composition test (kills F4).
5. The antihydrogen, kaon, neutrino and cosmological-asymmetry programmes are shown to be one evidence pool, so their agreement is worth nothing (narrows F6).
6. A measurement demonstrates that a pure re-arrangement of the current closure accommodates a genuine CPT violation with no new atom (kills F11).

If any fires, the derivation is withdrawn or narrowed in the relevant domain. Until one is run, no part of this folder may be labelled `grounded`.

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is CPT a symmetry of the arrangement?** Inside local Lorentz-invariant QFT, yes, as a theorem. Fig 12 :92 governs the symmetry question; the ground is experiment (Fig 2 :29). `specified`.
2. **What would a violation be?** A REFUTED residual, the fifth kind, not a missing atom (Fig 5 :50-56). `specified`.
3. **What is owed?** A negation, a refusal of the standing of CPT invariance as a composition base (Operators :58). The price of the unary operator is open (Operators :77-78).
4. **Are the differing readings new terms?** Under a broken symmetry the bond stops being symmetric, so the mirror readings become distinct terms, i.e. isomers (Fig 12 :117). `general` structurally, corroborated by chemistry at low overlap.
5. **What about a unified theory?** It is an unbound preference (Fig 5 :62); make it an ordinary term. A final room is forbidden (Fig 7 :6). `gap` for the name.
6. **Can the current closure accommodate a violation?** Only by import from a differing closure, with a source gate and shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :12-14). `specified`.
7. **What settles it?** A same-frame, independent instrument reading at the ground (Fig 2 :45). `unreached` so far: all current results are upper bounds (Fig 8 :39).
