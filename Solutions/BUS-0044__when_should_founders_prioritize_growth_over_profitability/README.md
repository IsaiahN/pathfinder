## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not a preference between two virtues. It is a two-part control problem measured per slot:

- **b** is the founder's belief about what customers will pay for, formed from prior evidence and inherited playbooks.
- **a** is the action: spend on acquisition, hire, hold price, cut burn, or delay.
- **Γ(b,a)** is what the startup's library (product, team, playbook, capital model) predicts will happen.
- **o′** is what the market actually returns: retained customers, gross margin, cash collected, survival.
- **s** is the slot, here a customer cohort, a segment, or a time period. **R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| is measured per cohort, never aggregated into a single company-wide growth rate** (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22).

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** (Fig 1 :19)

The question therefore decomposes into an upstream **detection** loop (how to tell whether a growth action reduced R at the ground) and a downstream **decision** loop (when to keep spending on growth versus preserving cash). One cannot be answered without the other, but they are not one question. Fig 9 :54 calls such a bundle a union that must be unbundled before it is searched.

- **Option A, "growth first, profit later":** leads to the prediction that the action is justified by top-line movement and that value will be confirmed by a later round or a later market.
- **Option B, "profit first, growth later":** leads to the prediction that cash preservation is always the safer action.
- **What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"):** a pair of conditions whose conjunction makes growth a value-creating action, plus an instrument that discriminates a failure of the first condition from a failure of the second. The sought-for shape is bound here, before the run, so the refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 can fire on it. Until bound, it predicts nothing and no loop check can see it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is neither A nor B as a universal rule. It is the union of two conditions kept separate per domain and never averaged: (C1) the bracket from growth spend to realized value is sound and settling, and (C2) the channel to a paying ground stays reachable. Growth is prioritized when both hold and the company is not in the "fatal pinch"; preservation is prioritized when either fails. Which numeric threshold occupies each condition is a declared `gap`.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the founder's decision, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of startup finance.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. **A room is not an anchor.** *"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). A list of founders or investors selected for agreement manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. **Correlation has valence.** *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). Graham, the IÉSEG report and the venture-finance papers draw on overlapping startup folklore and the same population of ventures, so they are one witness, not four.
3. **A verifier must be outside the interior.** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. **Legally and institutionally:** unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets a sending domain blacklisted. Separately, the venues in `03` are explicit that AI-generated work is not acceptable authorship without disclosure: Small Business Economics states that LLMs *"do not currently satisfy our authorship criteria"* and the Academy of Management requires work to be *"created by the authors and not the product of artificial intelligence tools unless appropriate to the research question and properly cited."* This package is a checkable object to hand to a human author, not a submission.
