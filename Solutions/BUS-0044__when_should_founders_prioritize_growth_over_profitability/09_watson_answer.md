## QUESTION

When should founders prioritize growth over profitability, and how can they tell when growth is creating value? Restated as an effect: when does spending to grow reduce R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| per cohort s, and what instrument discriminates a sound growth action from an unsound one before the cash ends? R is indexed per cohort and per time, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19). Anchor: a counter and a market, named in Fig 2 :29.

Option A, growth first: the action is justified by top-line movement and value is confirmed by a later round. Option B, profit first: cash preservation is always safer. What each leads to: A risks buying a chart without a retained customer when the bracket is unsound; B risks starving a sound model when the channel is still reachable. The figure-derived answer is neither universal rule, but a conjunction of two conditions with separate instruments.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The figure-derived verdict: founders should prioritize growth when C1 (a sound, settling bracket from spend to realized value, measured per cohort) and C2 (a reachable channel to a paying ground) both hold, and should preserve cash and restore contact when either fails. Detection precedes decision, and the two conditions have different instruments and are never averaged. The universal numeric threshold is a declared `gap`. Mode `specified`. The business evidence pool corroborates the two-condition shape only after the growth-first/profit-first conflict is returned to the census and resolved as a union split. Low-overlap fields corroborate environment dependence, not the startup-level fact.

- **cost if wrong:** misallocated runway. A startup failing C2 would be sold cohort instruments while it is actually default dead; a startup failing C1 would be told to raise another round while it is buying a chart. A single lever lands on a union that needs two. In outreach terms, an expert's twenty minutes would be spent refuting a shape that was never the right shape. The cost is real but bounded and largely reversible, and it is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter (Fig 2 :42 - "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong.").
- **undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails; a refutation is added and the reach falls because something was added rather than because anything was deleted (Fig 6 :42-43). The shape and its five falsifiers are recorded in a provenance trail so a later reader can locate the error rather than only feel it (Fig 10 :62-64).
- **reversed by:** any of the five pre-registered falsifiers, in particular (1) one lever restoring both conditions with no different residue, (2) an averaged growth threshold that prospectively identifies the value-creating cohort, (3) a metric that settles value by itself, (4) growth settling no paying relation while the ground keeps paying, or (5) advantage lost while bracket fidelity and ground pressure both measure high. A source with a genuinely different evidence pool showing that per-cohort value creation is fully predicted by blended industry benchmarks would also reverse it.
