# Expert verification packet - the Tether on growth versus profitability

**What this is:** a claim in a form a venture-finance or startup-strategy researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No cohort-level measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures and cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The growth-versus-profitability decision is a union of two conditions, not one choice. Growth is the right action when (C1) the bracket from spend to realized customer value is sound and settling against a ground measured per cohort, and (C2) the channel to a paying ground remains reachable, so the company is not in the fatal pinch. When either fails, preservation and restored contact are the action. Detection is upstream of decision, a blended growth rate is a pooled reading that hides the live cohort signal, and a growth engine changes valence when the residual changes. Capital is an import from a differing closure with a debit against independence. The universal numeric threshold is not claimed; it requires a discriminator this derivation does not hold.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 2, 3 and 9

- **Statement:** R is indexed per cohort and never aggregated; the anchor is a counter or a market; detection is upstream of decision; the question is a union.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
  - Fig 1 :22 - *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
  - Fig 2 :29 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."*
  - Fig 9 :57 - *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
  - Fig 3 :20 - *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*
- **Refuter:** an averaged growth-rate threshold that predicts prospectively and per named cohort which cohort creates value, with no cohort-level measurement; or one intervention that restores both conditions in both failure types with no categorically different residue.

### C2 - business-facing; checkable against startup finance data

- **Statement:** C1 is a settlement condition, not a ratio. A growth dollar is admitted like a candidate term: support (some cohort responding), reachability (composes from what the firm has), novelty (not already in the library), then settlement against the ground (Fig 5 :32 - *"then check it against the ground, then carry it up"*). Cash spent and description state are two currencies that do not add (Fig 12 :125 - *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*).
- **What to check:** are there cohorts or startups where acquisition spend settles no repeatable paying relation yet the ground keeps paying across a market change? Conversely, is there a benchmark ratio that, without cohort measurement, prospectively identifies value-creating action across markets? The world evidence is mixed at population level: Ben-Hafaïedh and Hamelin (2022) report profitability-first firms are more likely to reach both, while Graham (2015) argues growth and cheap operation are *"far from the sharp dichotomy many founders assume."* Both are population or advice-level claims and neither settles a single company's action.
- **Refuter:** growth spend repeatedly fails to settle any new paying customer relation while the ground keeps paying across a market change; or a metric is shown that moves neither with spend nor with narrative yet settles the value question by itself.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation

- **Statement:** quick loss is channel failure; a growth engine changes valence with the residual; capital is an import with a debit.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 10 :24 - *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*
  - Fig 10 :29 - *"keep the anchor reachable"*
  - Fig 11 :49 - *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
  - Fig 12 :119 - *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."*
  - Fig 6 :23 - *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*
- **What to check:** does a documented startup lose its ability to fund growth while bracket fidelity and ground pressure both measure high and are not declining? Is an acquisition engine's value creation invariant across a documented change of residual? Does any firm renew reach purely inside a closed library across a habitat change with no import from customers, capital, technology or any differing frame?
- **Refuter:** any of those observations, sustained, breaks C3. The ecology analogue (r/K selection) is single-source and contested; it corroborates only environment dependence and is not used as evidence here.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*, an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. **Cohort discriminator:** hold out cohorts, measure retention and collected margin, compare with predicted payback. This separates bought growth from retained value.
2. **Channel test:** on current cash and recent growth, does the company reach break-even before cash ends, with a written plan B and a switch date? Budget instrument, not verdict.
3. **Fatal-pinch test:** burn, growth and remaining time, measured together. If two are wrong, C2 has failed.
4. The five pre-registered falsifiers in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4.
