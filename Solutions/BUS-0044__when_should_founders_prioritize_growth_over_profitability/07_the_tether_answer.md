# The Tether's answer on growth versus profitability

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Founders face two coupled failures. First, an acquisition action may look like success in the company's own metrics while the market returns no retained value, so the gap R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| keeps growing per cohort. Second, even a sound model can be killed by losing the channel to the capital that funds the wait. The two failures have different instruments and are a union compound, not one hard decision (Fig 9 :54-57). Detection is upstream of decision (Fig 3 :20).

## 2. The shape

1. **R is per cohort, never a blended growth rate.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* A single growth percentage is a pooled reading (Fig 10 :26) that hides the cohort whose acquisition cost is never repaid.
2. **The anchor is a counter and a market, not a metric.** Fig 2 :29 names *"A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Retention, collected margin and survival settle; a growth rate, a valuation and a benchmark move when pushed (Fig 2 :30).
3. **Two conditions, kept separate.** Growth is the right action when **C1** the bracket from spend to realized customer value is sound and settling (Fig 5 :32: *"then check it against the ground, then carry it up"*) **and C2** the channel to a paying ground stays reachable (Fig 10 :29: *"keep the anchor reachable"*). When either fails, preserving cash and restoring contact is the action. The answer is not "always grow" and not "always profit"; those are two isomers over a shared atom set (`06_isomers_and_order.md`).
4. **Quick loss is channel failure, not market decay.** Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*. The named modes are stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics and pooled readings. The practitioner name is the fatal pinch: default dead plus slow growth plus no time to fix it.
5. **A growth engine changes valence with the residual.** Fig 11 :49: *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* A paid-acquisition engine is a catalyst while it regenerates retained customers and a spent part once it stops (Fig 12 :119).
6. **Capital is an import with a debit.** Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* A round adds an atom the company could not compose itself, but a plan that depends on the next round has imported an atom it does not control. Fundraising is a plan A with a written plan B, not an anchor.
7. **The numeric threshold is a `gap`.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* The framework returns the conjunction and the instrument shape, not a universal ratio. The instrument owed is one that discriminates bought growth from retained value.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "growth at all costs," and not "profitability first, always." Both are bindings from the startup-advice frame, recorded as such (Fig 8 :50-52).
- Not that valuation is irrelevant. It is a useful instrument and a budget filter, just not the ground (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that the benchmark literature is wrong. The population-level results (Ben-Hafaïedh and Hamelin 2022; Estrin et al. 2025; Norbäck et al. 2024/2025) corroborate the shape at their own stratum and cannot decide a single company's action (Fig 9 :60).
- Not a derivation of one strategy from the other, and no new primitive.
- Not `grounded`. No cohort-level experiment settles `left(R,φ)` here.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

- **Cohort discriminator:** does the acquisition spend produce customers who pay and stay, or only a chart? Hold out cohorts, measure retention and collected margin, and compare with the predicted payback. That is the instrument F10 says is missing.
- **Channel test:** on current cash and recent growth, does the company reach break-even before cash ends (Graham's default-alive question), with a written plan B and a switch date? This is a budget instrument, not a verdict on value creation.
- **Fatal-pinch test:** are burn, growth and remaining time such that the company can still act? If two of the three are wrong, C2 has failed.
- **Standing falsifiers from the record:** one lever restoring both conditions; an averaged growth threshold that prospectively identifies the value-creating cohort; a metric that settles by itself; growth that settles no paying relation while the ground keeps paying; advantage lost while bracket fidelity and ground pressure both measure high.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The framework returns a shape and records the lineage. The r/K analogue from ecology says the paying strategy depends on the environment, but that theory is itself contested and is used only as low-overlap corroboration. The universal ratio would need an instrument that discriminates sources of a growth reading; that instrument is not held here. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not tell founders to grow at all costs or to hoard profit. It says the decision is two questions, not one: is the link from growth spend to retained, paying customers actually settling against the ground, and is the channel to that ground still reachable? The gap has to be read per cohort, because a blended growth rate hides the cohort that never pays back. Growth is the right action when both conditions hold; when either fails, preserving cash and restoring contact is the action. A growth engine can change from catalyst to liability when the market changes, and capital is an import with a debit, not a free extension of runway. What the framework offers is the shape any answer must have, five falsifiers written in advance, and the kind of instrument that would close the question. The numeric threshold stays open until the ground settles it.
