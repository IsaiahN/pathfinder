# Isomers, order and licensed analogues for EAR-0016

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Partition or union?

Fig 9 :5: *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*

The direction question and the confidence question are not disjoint, because a confidence claim about rainfall direction is a compound of both. They are a union, not a partition: the overlap, "what a community can legitimately believe about a local change", has its own loop and its own ground (the realised decision outcome). Treating it as one question would average two residuals, which Fig 1 :22 forbids: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

**Conclusion:** union with a compound in the overlap. One loop per domain, verdicts never averaged.

### Q2 — What is ordered?

Three orders are present and they are different:

1. **Dependency order (Fig 3).** Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* Describe the residual, then check per-slot structure, then independence, then scale loss, then budget versus principle, then wait for the ground. A link with no instrument is not a working link: Fig 3 :27, *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
2. **Lossy scale order (Fig 4).** Global to local is a transform, not a rank. Fig 13 :4-5: *"There is no order of rank here. The two kinds are in lockstep, a crossing is a change of role rather than a step upward."* Coarser and finer descriptions are not senior and junior; they are different frames with a measured gap between them.
3. **No order among the six (Fig 13).** States and structures alternate; none is first. Any narrative that puts "temperature first" and "rainfall second" is imposing an order the substrate disclaims, the same error the exemplar named for the `→` bond in QMGR-0037.

**Falsifier Q2:** exhibit a decision-scale rainfall claim whose dependency chain can be reversed without loss, or a derivation that ranks one of the six above another. Either breaks the ordering claim.

### Q3 — Are two regional projection pipelines isomers?

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

Take two regional rainfall projection pipelines that use the same GCM ensemble (same atoms) but differ in the downscaling relation: one dynamical, one statistical. Under Fig 12 they share atoms and differ in bond, so they are **isomers**: same ingredients, different arrangement, different resulting local estimate. This is not a metaphor about model diversity; it is the figure's own definition of a relation that the ingredient list hides.

The weaker claim is that all regional projection pipelines share a **functional group**: an external large-scale driver, a regional translation, and a local outcome. The group fixes the kind of thing produced; the qualifier (which downscaling, which region) says which member.

**Falsifier Q3, written before the run:** if the two pipelines do not share a non-trivial atom set, the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim survives. If they share all atoms *and* all bonds, they are the same term and neither claim applies. Kendon et al. 2023 is the world-side case in point: the 2.2 km CPM and the 12 km RCM share forcing and ancestry but differ in the representation of convection, and the projected exceedance frequency differs (about 4x against 2.6x), which is exactly "invisible in the formula, decisive in the behaviour".

## CONCLUSION

The question is a union compound with an overlap that needs its own loop. Dependency order is real; rank order is not. Two regional projection pipelines over the same GCM atoms but with different downscaling relations are isomers under Fig 12, with a stated falsifier, and the functional-group reading is the weaker survivor. Four analogues are licensed by figure lines or by read essays; one common analogy is refused. None of this supplies a wetter/drier direction.
