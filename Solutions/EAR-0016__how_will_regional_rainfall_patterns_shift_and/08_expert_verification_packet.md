# Expert verification packet — the Tether on EAR-0016

**What this is:** a claim in a form a regional climate researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion.

**Mode: `specified` on the structural shape, `general` on the scope of the limit, `gap` on the physical direction. Not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A regional rainfall confidence statement must be indexed per place and per decision horizon, never given as one aggregate; the loss from a global projection down to the community scale must be measured; agreement among models that share code, parameterisations or calibration data is one witness, not many; "unreachable at this budget" must be kept distinct from "underivable in principle"; and settlement waits for realised rainfall and the decision's outcome. The framework supplies this shape and the refuters. It supplies no wetter/drier direction, and declares that a `gap` rather than hiding it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — per-slot confidence (framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 1 and 2)

- **Statement:** a single aggregate confidence figure cannot decide a community-scale rainfall question; confidence is indexed per region and per decision horizon.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 — *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 — *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Symbols :43-44 — *"R is measured per slot, never as a single global number."*
- **What to check:** are those three lines present in the verbatim figure text, and does the argument use "slot" as place-timescale rather than model timestep? Is the world-side corroboration (IPCC WGI Chapter 11 confidence language, which assigns low confidence when evidence disagrees across sub-regions, time frames and models) consistent with that reading?
- **Refuter:** exhibit a case where an aggregate confidence figure decides a community question with a measured cross-scale loss of zero.

### C2 — measured scale loss and discounted witnesses (framework-internal plus physics-facing)

- **Statement:** the global-to-community transform is lossy and the loss must be measured; correlated ensemble members are not independent witnesses.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :30 — *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* Fig 2 :31-32 — *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Fig 2 :44 — *"The effective-sample-size correction for correlated observations is standard in survey statistics and meta-analysis; the collapse is the same arithmetic read as a warning."* Fig 8 :49 — *"Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."*
- **What to check:** Kendon, Fischer and Short 2023 report about 4x versus 2.6x growth in UK hourly events above 20 mm/h between a 2.2 km convection-permitting model and a 12 km regional model, so a measurable scale loss exists in a real case. Pathak et al. 2023 report that 57 CMIP6 models carry the information of about 11 independent models, and Abramowitz et al. 2019 document shared biases from shared components. Are those characterisations faithful?
- **Refuter:** show a lossless downscaling transform for regional precipitation across multiple regions, or show that two models with shared predecessor code and parameterisations contribute fully independent information about the same regional residual.

### C3 — budget versus principle, and settlement (framework-internal)

- **Statement:** "unreachable at this budget" and "underivable in principle" are different facts with different instructions; neither constitutes settlement.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :40 — *"underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts"*; Fig 6 :32 — *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* Fig 2 :29 — *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
- **What to check:** Krishnamurthy 2019 reports that chaos limits instantaneous weather prediction to under about 10 days, while Kendon et al. show that a large convection-permitting ensemble recovers a regional signal that 25 years of observations cannot. Do these two examples actually fall on opposite sides of the distinction, as claimed? The live strain is that the figures do not classify a named community's case; this is reported, not hidden.
- **Refuter:** show one observation or derivation, in figure terms, that classifies a named community's rainfall question as underivable rather than budget-limited; or show that model agreement alone settles a community question with no ground reading and identical decision outcomes under rival projections.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- No wetter/drier direction for any region. This is the declared `gap`.
- Not that a coarse global projection is useless, or that current ensembles carry no information.
- Not that internal variability makes regional change unknowable.
- Not that ensemble spread fully measures uncertainty; IPCC AR6 WGI Chapter 10 states the opposite at very high confidence.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by an observation or a model run.
- Not that the essays and figures are independent witnesses. They share an author and an evidence pool.

## 5. What would settle it

1. Direction: realised rainfall at the decision scale over the decision horizon, plus an outcome that pays.
2. Scale loss: paired coarse and fine simulations over the same region with the gap measured.
3. Independence: code and parameterisation genealogy and out-of-sample tests, not output similarity.
4. The gap verdict: a verbatim figure line or derivation from the six-term set yielding a signed precipitation direction.
