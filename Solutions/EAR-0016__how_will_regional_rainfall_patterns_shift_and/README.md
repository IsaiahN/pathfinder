## What an answer would look like (Direction, bound as an ordinary term)

Two distinct effects are being decided, and an answer to one is not an answer to the other.

1. **Direction effect.** Someone commits to a signed statement about precipitation in a named region over a named period: "wetter", "drier", "more intense heavy events". The cost of being wrong is a misdirected adaptation decision.
2. **Confidence effect.** Someone commits to a confidence statement at a named decision scale: a town, a watershed, a growing season. The cost of being wrong is false precision, either overconfidence that locks in the wrong infrastructure or underconfidence that paralyses action.

The shape claim this package derives is: *confidence must be indexed per region and per decision scale, a global or continental average cannot decide a community question, the loss across scales must be measured rather than assumed, agreement among models that share an evidence pool is one witness and not many, and "we cannot resolve this at this budget" must be kept distinct from "this is unpredictable in principle".* The framework returns that shape, not a wetter/drier name for any particular place. Where the figures are silent on physical direction, the answer is `gap`, and that is a verdict rather than a silence.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the shape claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words. A room does not add an anchor: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35). Selecting for agreement manufactures the disqualifying correlation: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). And sending the full theory before asking for a verdict spends the reader's independence: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42).
