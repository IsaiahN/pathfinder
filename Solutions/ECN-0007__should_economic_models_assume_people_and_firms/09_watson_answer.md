**Mode: `specified` on the modelling application, `general` on the rules. Not `grounded`.** Figures are

## QUESTION

Should economic models assume people and firms form expectations rationally, or use more psychologically
and institutionally realistic alternatives? Restated as an effect: a model predicts o′ from belief b and
action a, and R = |Γ(b,a) minus o′| stays live per household, firm, market and period. Which assumption
about b earns the standing to be composed over? Option A sets b to the model-consistent prediction
(Muth 1961: *"expectations are essentially the same as the predictions of the relevant economic theory"*).
Option B sets b from documented cognitive and institutional mechanisms. Option A sends a solvable model
whose belief term is a frame-internal quantity and whose definition cannot be violated. Option B sends a
set of imports that must each earn provenance and a shadow test. Neither is a verdict until the ground
settles it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or
union, and order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Three-place relation (model Γ, belief assumption b, realised outcome
  o′), indexed by slot. Arity: three, with one slot per agent, firm, market and period. Symmetry: none;
  "rational" is a threshold comparison ⋛ against a norm, not a symmetric join (Fig 12 :87). Scale:
  individual loop, market loop, macro time channel, with a lossy membrane between them (Fig 4 :27-30).
  Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
- **D1 The model frame.** Actors: modeller, Γ, expectation operator, b. Currency: description length and
  within-frame residual. Anchor: none that is a ground; internal consistency only. Admitted as a frame,
  not as a ground.
- **D2 The real economy.** Actors: households, firms, markets, institutions, central bank. Currency:
  realised outcomes, income, profit, survival. Anchor: the market and the realised outcome. Admitted, this
  is the ground.
- **D3 Cognitive and institutional knowledge.** Actors: bounded rationality, decision weights, noise
  traders, formal and informal rules. Currency: documented regularities and their prediction errors.
  Anchor: experiments and field data. Admitted, a closure that differs from D1.
- **D4 Verification and policy use.** Actors: forecasters, regulators, model users. Currency: forecast
  loss and policy outcome. Anchor: the ground, never agreement among modellers. Admitted.
- **X1 Rationality as a free-floating property.** Actors: a disembodied optimiser. Currency: none nameable.
  Anchor: none. Not a domain yet; an idealisation no real occupant meets (Fig 10 :54).
- **X2 Efficient markets imply rational expectations.** Absorbed into D2. Not a separate domain.
- **Union, not partition.** C1 ground contact and C2 reach renewal are overlapping conditions (Fig 9 :55).
  One lever leaves the intersection standing (Fig 9 :57). Dependency order: perceive the failure, have
  terms for b/a/o′, say what counts as progress, plan the test, then settle and carry (Fig 3 :4-15).

Strain test applied: Fig 8 :32 governs internal search and Fig 6 :23 governs imports from a differing
closure. Different objects, so no strain after re-census. The seat-rule reading of Fig 10 is narrowed to

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The figures do not choose between "rational" and "realistic" as wholesale packages. They decide the
meta-question. Rational expectations is disqualified as a ground-settled verdict: it sets the belief equal
to the model's prediction (Symbols table, local), so the model scores itself with a quantity it produces
(Fig 1 :27); it is an idealisation no real occupant meets (Fig 10 :54); and it cannot be violated by
observation (Fig 10 :49). Realistic alternatives are not vindicated as an orthodoxy. They are imports from
a differing closure (Fig 6 :23) and owe a provenance record and a shadow test (Fig 8 :51). The question is
a union of ground contact and reach renewal (Fig 9 :55), so tractability is a budget filter only (Fig 9 :60).
Which specific expectation rule wins is a `gap`, unreachable at this budget until a belief-discriminating
instrument exists. Mode `specified` on the application, `general` on the rules, nothing `grounded`.

- **Cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states five refuters before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a conclusion instead of a generator, which Fig 2 :42 prices: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **Undoable:** yes. No atom is deleted if a refutation lands; the refusal is added and the reach falls because *"Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach."* (Fig 5 :54). The shape and its refuters are recorded in a provenance trail (Fig 10 :62-64) so a later reader can locate the error.
- **Reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An instrument measuring beliefs independently of the model showing model-consistent beliefs matching outcomes per slot across a crisis; a figure re-census admitting frame-internal quantities as the metric; an atom that grows reach without adding an atom or explaining a gap; one intervention clearing both conditions with no categorical residue; or a test from the model's own output that violates the rational-expectations assumption. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
