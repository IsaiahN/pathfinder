## Wider literature relevant to the question

The opened sources divide into four pools. Their overlap is recorded so that agreement is not mistaken
for corroboration.

1. **Rational-expectations and macro methodology pool (one witness).** Muth 1961, the Lucas symposium
   introduction (Journal of Economic Methodology 2023, https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09538259.2022.2107365),
   Sargent 1996 and McCallum 1982 all draw on the same history. The symposium notes that Lucas himself
   acknowledged sharp downturns such as the Great Depression and 2008 as outside the scope of the
   equilibrium models that followed his 1972 paper. That is a ground-facing caveat from inside the
   rational-expectations tradition, not an outside refutation.
2. **Behavioural and institutional pool (a second witness, different evidence).** Kahneman and Tversky
   1979, Shiller 2000/2015, Shleifer and Vishny 1997, North 1991, Simon via the SEP. Overlap with pool 1
   is low but not zero: both cite expected utility and market efficiency.
3. **Economic-methodology pool (a third witness, different question).** Hausman, Mäki, the 2015 theorem
   paper. These sources disagree with each other; that disagreement is recorded in `01` section 7.
4. **Non-economics analogues (independent witnesses for the mechanism shape only).** Pierce and Ollason
   1987, Railsback et al. 2022, Smith's experimental economics as the engineering "wind tunnel" analogue.
   They corroborate the shape of the idealisation argument, not the economic fact.

No world source overrides a figure line. One apparent conflict, the methodological dispute over Friedman's
defence, was returned to the domain census and left as a recorded conflict; it did not flip the verdict
(Fig 9 :60).

## Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 4 :22-24:** *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down. A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Sending the whole framework before someone has described the residual sends a recording. Lead with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **Fig 10 :42:** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the interiors before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28:** *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*. Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before sending the figures link.
- **Fig 2 :40:** *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, especially from a methodology mailing list where everyone has read Friedman.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the residual and the shape claim; (2) the pre-registered refuter and
the question "what would you count as a failure?"; (3) record their verdict; (4) only then the three
links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer, and the third stage

1. **Stage 1:** check the output against the ground. No theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** read the source that produced it. Their stage-1 verdict is already recorded,
   so later agreement cannot be counted as evidence (Fig 10 :52; Fig 2 :45).
3. **Stage 3:** reuse the formula on another problem, with the user's own written refuter and stated mode.
   Fig 4 :22 permits sending a method, because a generator is not an answer.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 work only if the stage-1 output survives. The output in `01` is a
shape plus five refuters, not a claim that rational expectations is false everywhere. Do not strengthen
it in the email.
