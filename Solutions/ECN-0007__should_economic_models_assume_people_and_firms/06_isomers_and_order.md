# Bond structure, order and analogues: supporting derivation for ECN-0007

**Mode: `specified` on the economic application, `general` on the figure rules.** This is a simulated
derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §5).

## RESIDUAL

The question "rational or realistic?" treats the two options as rival names for one slot. Two structural
questions are owed: (1) what bond relates the belief b, the prediction Γ(b,a) and the observation o′, and
(2) are a rational-expectations model and a behavioural model isomers, or something weaker? A third,
analogue question is whether optimality assumptions in other disciplines meet the same fate.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held in the two frames: b (belief), w (weighting of private history vs inherited prior), a (action),
o′ (observation), Γ(b,a) (prediction), R (residual), plus importable atoms such as decision weights,
loss aversion, noise-trader risk and formal/informal rules. Bonds held: the seven plus negation. No new
primitive is available without import. Anchor for this derivation: the verbatim figure text; anchor for

## DERIVATION

### Q1. What bond holds between b and o′?

- Symbols table (local): b is *"What the system takes to be the case before it acts... Not the observation."*
  o′ is *"What actually happened."* Γ(b,a) is *"What the library says should happen, given belief b and
  action a."*
- Fig 12 :40: *"same two atoms, three substances"* under +, → and −. The bond carries the meaning, not the
  operands.
- Rational expectations writes b ≡ Γ(b,a), an **identity bond** (Fig 12 :67: *"two names, one referent"*).
  But the Symbols table declares b and o′ to be different objects, so an identity between b and the
  prediction collapses the residual term into a frame-internal one. The bond is not licensed by the
  notation; it is imposed on it.
- A checkable model instead writes b → a → o′, with R = |Γ(b,a) minus o′| as a **comparison** ⋛ between
  prediction and observation (Fig 12 :87: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*). That keeps
  the belief and the observation as separate referents.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a figure line licenses b ≡ Γ(b,a) while also keeping R as a
  ground-facing metric, Q1 fails and F1 of `01` is withdrawn.

### Q2. Are rational and behavioural models isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the
  formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind;
  the qualifier says which member."*
- As usually written, a rational model holds {b, a, Γ, o′} with b set by model-consistency, and a
  behavioural model adds atoms (decision weights, loss aversion, noise-trader risk, rules) that the first
  does not hold. They are **not** isomers: the atom sets differ. They are two members of a functional
  group (a belief-action-observation loop) with different occupants of the belief slot.
- The stronger form: if both models are written over the same full atom set, with the difference only in
  how b is bonded to Γ and o′, they **are** isomers, and the difference is decisive in behaviour. That is
  the claim worth testing, and it needs an instrument that discriminates the bond.
- **Falsifier:** if either model requires an atom the other cannot express even at the same level, the
  isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands.

### Q3. What is the order of the union?

- Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact
  about a level and never about the world."*
- Each first-order loop (household, firm, market) is settled or not against the ground. Only a settled
  loop can serve as an atom for the macro-level union. A model whose belief term is self-validating has
  not settled the first-order loop; it has declared it. So it cannot be composed over legitimately at the
  second order.
- Fig 13 :74: *"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."* At the level of
  this question, the two kinds are the frame side (Γ, b) and the ground side (o′, market). A composition
  that stays entirely inside the frame side is not a model of the world; it is internal bookkeeping.
- **Falsifier:** show a frame-side-only composition that reduces per-slot R at the ground. That would break
  the cross-kind rule as applied here.

### Q4. Analogues: optimality assumptions outside economics

- **Animal behaviour, low overlap.** Pierce and Ollason 1987 (Oikos 49:1, opened 2026-11-22): *"It is
  always possible to derive, retrospectively, a function with respect to which observed behaviour is
  optimal"*, and the assumption of optimality cannot be tested, so *"there can be no evidence for optimal
  foraging"*. This is F2 and F3 in another field: an unviolable optimality assumption cannot be confirmed
  by its own successes.
- **Ecology, low overlap, opposite direction.** Railsback et al. 2022 (Ecology, opened 2026-11-22):
  *"Instead of simplifying the systems we model until we can find optimal behavior, we can use theory based
  on inaccurate predictions, coarse approximations, and updating to produce good behavior in more complex
  and realistic contexts."* This is Option B stated in a field with no stake in the economics debate.
  It corroborates the **shape** (updating plus realism beats imposed optimality in complex habitats), not
  the specific economic claim.
- **Experimental economics, medium overlap.** Smith 1989 (opened 2026-11-22): *"common information is not
  sufficient to yield common 'knowledge' or expectations"* and *"institutions matter"*. The lab is a wind
  tunnel: it tests wiring before field use, which is Fig 11 :70's substituted habitat made explicit and
  therefore honest about what it proves.
- **Type caveat, still open.** The Operators table defines ⋛ as a threshold relation between two
  quantities. At the macro level the operands are settled molecules now serving as atoms. Whether an atom
  counts as a quantity at that level needs an instrument, not an argument. Named, not hidden.

## CONCLUSION

The rational-expectations move is an identity bond written onto objects the notation distinguishes. A
ground-facing model needs b and o′ held apart, with R as a comparison between prediction and observation.
Rational and behavioural models are not isomers as usually written (different atom sets); the checkable
stronger form puts them over one atom set and differs only in the bond. Order matters: a first-order loop
must settle before it can be composed over, so a self-validating belief cannot anchor a macro claim. The
non-economics analogues support the shape, not the verdict. The operand type of ⋛ at second order remains
a live strain.
