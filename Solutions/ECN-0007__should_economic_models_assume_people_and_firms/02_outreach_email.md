**Subject:** One checkable claim about expectations in economic models, before the theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question in economic methodology and would like you to check one output
> before deciding whether any of the surrounding framework is worth reading. I am not asking you to
> adopt a modelling camp, and I am not asking you to agree with me. If the output fails, there is
> nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual.** Economic models predict outcomes o′ from a belief b and an action a, and the gap
> R = |Γ(b,a) minus o′| has to be read per household, firm, market and period, not as one average.
> Two ways of filling b are on the table: set b to what the model itself predicts (rational
> expectations, Muth 1961), or set b from documented cognitive and institutional mechanisms. The
> figures I am using make the following `specified`, ungrounded claim about their standing, not about
> their truth in a particular market.
>
> **The output to check.** An expectation assumption is admissible only if (1) its belief term can be
> measured separately from the model's own prediction, (2) it can be violated by a ground reading,
> and (3) it is measured per slot. Rational expectations fails (1) and (2) by construction: b is set
> equal to the model's prediction, so the model scores itself with a quantity it also produces, and a
> definition cannot be refuted by data. The bounded and institutional alternatives are neither
> automatically right nor automatically admissible; they are imports from a different closure and owe
> a provenance record and a shadow test, that is, where the atom came from and which unexplained gap
> it explains. Tractability is a budget filter, never a witness for the verdict.
>
> **Mode and non-claims.** This is `specified` on the modelling application and `general` on the
> rules. It is not a measurement, not a claim that rational expectations is empirically false in a
> given market, not a claim that behavioural models are empirically true, and not a claim to name the
> correct expectation rule. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against a
> figure set, so it cannot settle the question it reports on.
>
> **What would refute it, written before this message:**
> 1. an instrument measuring beliefs independently of the model shows model-consistent beliefs matching
>    outcomes per slot across a documented crisis, with no updating;
> 2. a re-census of the figures admits a frame-internal quantity as the metric that settles a verdict;
> 3. an imported behavioural atom adds no new atom, explains no new per-slot residual, and yet the
>    frame's reach demonstrably grows;
> 4. one intervention clears both ground contact and reach renewal with no categorically different
>    residue;
> 5. a test built from the model's own output can violate the rational-expectations assumption.
>
> If any of these holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is far
> more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the source material follows. The figures, not my summary, are the
> authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract, path supplied on request).
> - Canonical figures README: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - THE FORMULA and the figures as SVG, branch `general-agent`:
>   https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on an expectation
> assumption you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result
> *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The framework's own rule is that a loop
> cannot check the direction of its own question, so a reported failure is more useful to me than a
> reported success.
>
> Thanks, [name].
