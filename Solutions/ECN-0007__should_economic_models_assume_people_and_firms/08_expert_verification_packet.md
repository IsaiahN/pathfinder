# Expert verification packet: the Tether on expectations in economic models

**What this is:** a claim in a form an economic methodologist, macroeconomist or behavioural economist can
check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for
and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the modelling application, `general` on the rules. Not `grounded`.** No measurement
is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

An expectation assumption earns verdict weight only if its belief term b can be measured separately from
the model's own prediction and from the realised outcome o′, if it can be violated by a ground reading,
and if the residual R = |Γ(b,a) minus o′| is read per slot rather than averaged. Rational expectations
sets b equal to the model-consistent prediction, so it fails the first and second conditions by
construction: the model scores itself with a quantity it produces, and a definition cannot be refuted by
data. Psychologically and institutionally realistic alternatives are not thereby vindicated. They are
imports from a different closure and owe a provenance record and a shadow test. The question is a union of
ground contact and reach renewal, so tractability, elegance or majority preference are filters for the
budget, never witnesses for the verdict. The framework returns the shape any admissible assumption must
have; it does not name the winning rule.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1. Framework-internal; checkable by reading the Symbols and Operators tables and Figure 1
- **Statement:** b and o′ are distinct objects; setting b equal to the model's prediction collapses the
  residual into a frame-internal quantity.
- **Source, verbatim:** Symbols table (local extract): b is *"What the system takes to be the case before it
  acts... Not the observation."*; o′ is *"What actually happened."* Fig 1 :27 *"They are frame-internal. A
  frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by
  object slot, not aggregated."*
- **Refuter:** an instrument that measures b independently of the model shows model-consistent beliefs
  matching o′ per slot through a crisis, with no updating; or a figure re-census admitting a frame-internal
  quantity as the metric that settles a verdict.

### C2. Idealisation and groundedness; checkable by reading Figure 10
- **Statement:** an expectation rule that no real occupant can meet is an idealisation, and a declared
  meaning is not a grounded one. This governs the standing of rational expectations as a verdict, not its
  usefulness as a tool.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :54 *"A rule no real occupant could meet describes an idealisation, not a
  position. Before enforcing an absolute, ask what would have to be true for a real occupant to meet it."*
  Fig 10 :55 *"The cost is not only that the rule describes nobody. An idealisation licenses inferences no
  real position would support, and those inferences will be drawn by whoever finds them useful."* Fig 10 :49
  *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."*
- **Refuter:** a test built from the model's own output that can violate the rational-expectations
  assumption; or a demonstration that a specific idealisation's falsehood never matters for the purpose
  at hand, with the ground still settling the verdict. The second refutes the application, not the rule.

### C3. Imports and the union; checkable by reading Figures 6, 8 and 9
- **Statement:** behavioural and institutional atoms are imports, licensed by a source gate and a shadow
  test; the overall question is a union, so tractability is a budget filter.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :23 *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a
  frame whose closure differs."* Fig 8 :51 *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the
  contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"* Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is
  usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the
  budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
- **Refuter:** an imported behavioural atom that adds no atom and explains no previously unexplained
  per-slot residual yet demonstrably grows the frame's reach; or one intervention that clears both ground
  contact and reach renewal with no categorically different residue.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that rational expectations is empirically false in any named market.
- Not that prospect theory, bounded rationality or institutional analysis is empirically true.
- Not a ban on idealisation as a model-building tool. The requirement is that an assumption be violable.
- Not a resolution of the methodological dispute over Friedman 1953. Hausman, Mäki and the 2015 theorem
  paper conflict; the conflict is recorded in `01` section 7.
- Not a derivation of a specific expectation-update rule. Which rule wins is a declared `gap`.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a measured belief instrument.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*
(opened 2026-11-22): an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer
resolved it. Here:

1. **Belief instrument:** measure b independently of Γ(b,a), per slot, across a crisis.
2. **Violability test:** state an observation, in the model's own terms, that would fire against the
   expectation assumption.
3. **Shadow test:** name the per-slot residual each imported atom explains that the standard model misses.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read the Symbols table entries for b, o′ and Γ(b,a), then Fig 1 :19 and :27. Are b and o′ distinct in
   the notation, and does the self-scoring rule apply as stated?
2. Read Fig 10 :49, :54, :55. Is the idealisation claim about standing, not empirical truth? If you read
   it as a truth claim, that is a misreading and the packet should be corrected.
3. Read Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :51 and Fig 9 :55, :60. Does the import/union reading survive, or can you name a
   case where a single lever clears both conditions?
4. Check C2 against your own field knowledge: does any expectation rule in use violate the violability
   requirement less than rational expectations does?
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
