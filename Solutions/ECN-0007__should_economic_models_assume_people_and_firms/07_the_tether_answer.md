# The Tether's answer: rational expectations or realistic alternatives?

**Mode: `specified` on the modelling application, `general` on the rules that decide standing. Not `grounded`.**
This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground
reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` §5).

## 1. The residual it answers

A model predicts o′ from b and a, and R = |Γ(b,a) minus o′| refuses to shrink per household, firm, market
and period. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* The question asks which
assumption about b to use. In effect it asks what gives an assumption the standing to be composed over.
It is a **union**, not a partition: ground contact (C1) and reach renewal (C2) must both hold (Fig 9 :55).

## 2. The shape, per domain, verdicts kept separate

**D1, the model frame. Verdict: `gap` for grounding; `general` for the rule.**
The frame has no ground anchor. Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a
quantity it also produces."* Within-model forecast accuracy and tractability are quantities the frame
produces. They cannot settle which expectation assumption to use. Rational expectations also writes an
identity between b and the model's own prediction (F1, F3), which understates R by construction. Nothing
in the figures licenses a frame-internal term as a verdict.

**D2, the real economy. Verdict: `triangulated` (Fig 2 anchor rule plus Fig 11 habitat rule).**
Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any
frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Fig 11 :40:
*"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* The anchor is
the realised outcome, the market, the forecast record. A clean representative-agent model is a substituted
habitat: Fig 11 :70 *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring
and never capability."* What was left out is invisible until the goal fails (Fig 11 :68).

**D3, cognitive and institutional knowledge. Verdict: `triangulated` for the import rule; `single-source`
for any specific behavioural atom; the winning atom is a `gap`.**
Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure
differs."* Fig 8 :51: *"Only the record of where each came from separates them"*. Bounded rationality,
decision weights, noise-trader risk and institutions are imports with a provenance and a shadow test, not
a replacement orthodoxy. The figures do not name which one wins in a given market. Fig 9 :32:
*"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a
silence"*.

**D4, verification and policy use. Verdict: `triangulated` (Fig 2 plus Fig 7).**
Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 7 :13:
*"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*. A verdict reached by counting methodologists is a convention.
Independent frames range separately against the ground; Fig 2 :42 *"Buy independence in proportion to the
cost of being wrong."*

**X1, rationality as a free-floating property. Verdict: `gap`, refused as a ground-settled claim.**
Fig 10 :54: *"A rule no real occupant could meet describes an idealisation, not a position."* Fig 10 :55:
*"An idealisation licenses inferences no real position would support, and those inferences will be drawn by
whoever finds them useful."* It may fund a model; it cannot settle a verdict (Fig 10 :49 *"Declaring a
meaning does not ground it."*).

**Union and method. Verdict: `triangulated` (Fig 9 plus Fig 5).**
Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*. Fig 9 :60:
*"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
Tractability is a filter. Fig 5 :43: *"A term that explains everything by saying nothing fails this, and so
does a term that explains one occasion perfectly."*

No verdict above is averaged with another. D2's ground verdict does not transfer to D3's import verdict,
and a `gap` in D3 is not a hidden yes.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that rational expectations is empirically false. Its status here is structural: idealisation,
  self-scoring term, unviolable by construction. Whether it predicts a given market is a ground question.
- Not that behavioural or institutional models are true. They are admissible imports, not settled atoms.
- Not a method ban. An idealisation can give a model a budget to be tried; Fig 10 :19 says *"install what
  can be violated"*, so the requirement is violability, not realism for its own sake.
- Not a winner in the methodology dispute over Friedman 1953. The world sources conflict; the conflict is
  recorded and did not flip the figure verdict (Fig 9 :60).
- Not `grounded`. No belief-measuring instrument was run.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*
(opened 2026-11-22): an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer
resolved it. Here the corresponding tests are:

- **Belief instrument:** measure b independently of Γ(b,a), per slot. Does the model-consistent belief
  match o′ through a crisis, or does it fail in a way a documented mechanism predicts?
- **Violability test:** state, in the model's own terms, an observation that would fire against the
  expectation assumption. If none can be stated, the assumption is a declaration (F11).
- **Shadow test for imports:** for each behavioural or institutional atom, name the per-slot residual it
  explains that the standard model misses. If none, the atom is a wrong import (Fig 6 :24).
- **Union test:** does one intervention clear both C1 and C2, and if so is the residue categorically
  different, as Fig 9 :57 requires?
- **Standing falsifiers from `01` section 5:** instrument-backed coincidence of belief and outcome across a
  crisis; a figure re-census admitting frame-internal quantities as the metric; an atom that grows reach
  without adding an atom or explaining a gap; a single lever clearing both conditions; a test from the
  model's own output that violates rational expectations.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The expectation rule that wins in a given market is not derivable from the
figures. It needs the missing instrument that discriminates among belief sources. The six-term set is a
visible choice, not nature's list; *They're Scanning Books* (exemplar record): *"Declaring the primitives
does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."* The line stops where the available instruments
stop and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not pick a side in the rational-expectations debate, and it does not claim to know which
> expectation rule is correct. It says that a model cannot score its own expectations with a quantity the
> model produces, that an assumption no real person could meet is an idealisation and cannot settle a
> verdict, and that the ground is the market and the realised outcome, not agreement among methodologists.
> Rational expectations fails the first and second tests by construction, because it sets the belief equal
> to the model's prediction and makes systematic error impossible by definition. Psychological and
> institutional alternatives are not automatically right either: they are imports from other disciplines
> and owe a provenance record and a shadow test for the gap they explain. The question is a union of
> ground contact and reach renewal, so one lever cannot clear it. What the framework offers is the shape
> any admissible expectation assumption must have, five falsifiers written in advance, and the instrument
> that would close it. The slot stays open until the ground settles it.
