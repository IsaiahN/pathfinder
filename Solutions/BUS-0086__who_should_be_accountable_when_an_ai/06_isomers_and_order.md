### Q1 - Partition or union?

Fig 9 :54: **"When the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**

The four names stand for conditions, not values of one variable:

| name | condition it names | overlaps with |
|---|---|---|
| vendor | production of the artifact and its foreseeable defects | firm (contract), manager (specification acceptance) |
| firm | authority to deploy and control of the deploying habitat | vendor, manager, decision-maker |
| manager | duty of oversight and authority to override | firm, decision-maker |
| decision-maker | authorization of the particular decision | manager, firm |

A single lever that names one actor clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55-58). *The Library of Babel* states the same instruction in plain terms: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."*

**Verdict:** union. **Falsifier, written before the run:** if the four conditions are demonstrated to be mutually exclusive and jointly exhaustive in the relevant case class, so a single lever closes the residual, the partition reading wins and this finding is withdrawn. Status: `triangulated` (Fig 9 and Seat Map are two documents with low layout overlap, plus the essay corroboration; all share an author, so this is one evidence pool).

### Q2 - What bond structure carries the attribution?

Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**
Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**
Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**
Operators table :30: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened."*
Operators table :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*

A list of four nouns is a flat formula, the conjunction case Fig 12 calls the collapse of recording ingredients and not arrangement. The attribution needs typed bonds:

- **⇒ production:** who brought the artifact into the world and what defects were foreseeable at that point.
- **→ sequence:** who acted before whom, because "B needs A to have happened" is the order of the decision (Operators :30).
- **⋛ comparison:** where the authority threshold was crossed, a threshold relation between two quantities (Operators :48).
- **¬ negation:** who could have refused, the unary operator that a list of nouns cannot write (Operators :58).

Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."** Describing the responsibility structure (bits) and settling who actually pays (actions) are two readings. Fig 12 :130: **"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."** A tidy four-box responsibility matrix prices the description and ignores the cost of the settlement action.

**Falsifier:** exhibit a case where the four roles are independent atoms whose mere enumeration fully determines the attribution without any relation among them, and where no refusal was possible. Status: `triangulated` (Fig 12 + Operators table).

### Q3 - Are the four roles isomers, or a functional group?

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

As the question writes them, the four roles share only the property "party to the decision." Under Fig 12's definition they are therefore not isomers. They are members of a **functional group**, the "party to a decision" group, with different qualifiers: vendor, firm, manager, decision-maker. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member.

The stronger form: if each role is characterized over the same atom set (authority, control, foresight, contact with the harm) and differs only in the arrangement of those atoms, then they **are** isomers under Fig 12 :117, same atoms, different bond, different substance. That is the checkable version worth taking to an expert, because it predicts that the roles differ in arrangement rather than in kind.

**Falsifier:** if one role requires an atom the others do not (for example, only the vendor has access to training data, and no other role can be said to hold it in any arrangement), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. Status: `single-source` for the figure definition (Fig 12), with the atom assignment unmeasured.

### Q4 - What order does the attribution run in?

Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**
Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

The dependency order is:

1. **Register the harm per slot.** If there is no instrument reading the harm, there is no input, and every later step is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22-23). This is the link that most often has no instrument in AI deployment: logs exist, but whether anyone reads them is a separate question.
2. **Have terms for what each role afforded.** Who could override, who could refuse, who controlled the data. This is Fig 3's middle link, the one with neither obvious failures nor obvious tests. It must be built before attribution, not after.
3. **Say what counts as an answer.** A per-slot attribution of conditions, not a single name.
4. **Refuse the premature single-name answer.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 2 lists REFUTED as a distinct bin: *"a term held is false; what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation."* The assertion "exactly one of the four is accountable" is the term held. Refusal precedes mint.
5. **Mint the per-slot attribution** from the conditions that actually held, with provenance for each.
6. **Settle at the ground.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* An external finding settles the case; an internal convention does not (Fig 10 :47).

Fig 3 :24-26 explains why the middle link is the one to instrument: perception and planning have obvious tests, but having terms for what a situation affords has neither, *"so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."*

**Falsifier:** show a case where the attribution can be settled without any record of what each role could have done, purely from the outcome, and where that settlement is stable under independent recomputation. Status: `triangulated` (Fig 3 + Formula step 2 + Fig 10 :47).

## CONCLUSION

The four-way question is a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition. The attribution is carried by typed bonds (production, sequence, threshold, refusal), not by the list of four names. As written, the four roles form a functional group; over a shared set of attributes they are a real isomer claim with a stated falsifier. The order is register, build vocabulary, refuse the forced choice, mint the per-slot attribution, then settle at an external ground.

This rescues what is defensible in the question without accepting its premise: no new actor category is needed, no import that moves a wall, and no claim that the framework can name the liable party before a ground settles it. What remains open is the atom assignment for the isomer claim and the instrument that reads what each role could have done.
