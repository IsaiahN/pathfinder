## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the offered choice: vendor, firm, manager, or decision-maker. Option A: answer with one of the four. Option B: treat the four as overlapping conditions, refuse the forced choice, and give the shape an accountability answer must have. What each leads to: A delivers a name with no unbundling, lets a party own the reading of who answers, and averages per-slot harms into one organizational statement; B delivers a per-slot attribution of conditions, an external anchor none of the parties can move, written refuters, and a clear account of what a real finding would have to settle.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain giving actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** A harm H that arrived from an AI-driven decision and was not predicted, R = |Γ(b,a) − o′|, indexed per harmed party. Arity: several overlapping conditions, so a union compound (Fig 9 :54-58), not a single hard question. Symmetry: ordered and typed (⇒ production, → sequence, ⋛ threshold, ¬ refusal), so a flat list is insufficient. Scale: first order at the decision event, second order at the institution that settles it.
- **Domain 1, harm registration.** Actors: affected people, instruments, logs, incident reports. Currency: per-slot residuals R⁺_s. Anchor: the registered harm, held fixed while frames push on it. Question type: partition, one slot at a time.
- **Domain 2, decision and habitat.** Actors: vendor, firm, manager, decision-maker plus everyone in contact with the harm and their contacts. Currency: authority, control, contact, production. Anchor: the same registered harm. Question type: **union** of overlapping conditions.
- **Domain 3, accountability settlement.** Actors: court, regulator, board, affected people; the four candidates appear only as frames. Currency: verdicts, not agreement. Anchor: human judgment and the institutional record, legitimate because the question is constitutively about people harmed (Fig 2 :36-37). Question type: a partition boundary, the anchor is not one of the parties.
- **Domain 4, verification.** Actors: outside expert readers in AI governance and accountability research. Currency: refutations. Anchor: a ground finding, never agreement. In this simulated run all seats share one evidence pool, so independence is zero.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Register the harm per slot; build the vocabulary of what each role could have refused; state what counts as an answer; refuse the forced choice before minting (Formula step 2); mint the per-slot attribution; settle at the external ground (Formula step 5).

Strain test applied: the responsibility-gap literature says no candidate meets the conditions; the abundance literature says many do. Read as opposed verdicts they strain. Re-census (Fig 9 :54-58) assigns them to two different conditions of one union, so the strain is dissolved, not smoothed over. One strain remains live: the atom assignment for the isomer claim in `06_isomers_and_order.md` is unmeasured.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The forced choice is refused as written. The question is a union of overlapping conditions, so the conditions must be unbundled before any "who" is searched. None of the four parties can own the reading of accountability, because each is measured by that reading and has a stake in it; the settlement anchor is external to all four and human-facing. Accountability is then a per-slot structure of typed relations: production, deployment authority, sequence, threshold, and the standing to refuse. Internal responsibility assignments are conventions until the external anchor checks them. This is a `specified`, ungrounded shape. There is no general answer of "the vendor" or "the firm"; a real case is settled by a ground finding, not by this derivation.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode, refuters and provenance before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a name instead of a method, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter. A second cost is failing to unbundle, which leaves intersections standing and makes the eventual finding unstable.
- **undoable:** yes. The refused single-name answer is removable without touching the derivation; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove it. No actor category has to be withdrawn, and a later ground finding can be added as a case-level result without changing the shape.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A per-slot harm reading that stays fixed when parties push on it; a record of what each role could have refused at the point the harm was foreseeable; a court, regulator or board finding with reasons and a date that is stable under independent recomputation; or a demonstration that the four conditions are mutually exclusive and jointly exhaustive. Until one of these fires, the verdict stays `candidate`. Agreement among researchers who share this evidence pool is not such a reversal.
