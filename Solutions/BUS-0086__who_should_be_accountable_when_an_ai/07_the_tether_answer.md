# The Tether's answer: who should be accountable when an AI-driven business decision causes harm

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a case result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §9), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A harm arrived from an AI-driven decision and was not predicted. R = |Γ(b,a) − o′|, indexed per harmed party, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19). The question offers four names: vendor, firm, manager, decision-maker. The effect question is which thing can settle the attribution without moving when one of the four pushes on it.

## 2. The shape

1. **The question as written is a union, not a partition.** Fig 9 :54: *"When the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* The four names stand for overlapping conditions: production of the artifact, authority to deploy, duty of oversight, authorization of the decision. Picking one is a single lever that clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. So the honest answer is not one of the four in general.

2. **None of the four can be the anchor.** Fig 10 :86: *"Outside the slice being graded. Whoever is measured by a reading cannot own it."* Fig 10 :59: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* Each candidate is a party with a stake in the reading of who answers. Fig 2 :36: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* Fig 2 :37: *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."* The settlement anchor is a human-facing institution (a court, a regulator, a board answerable to affected people) whose finding does not change because a party pushes on it. A model's correctness is a different question with a different anchor.

3. **Attribution is per slot, not an organizational average.** Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Each harmed party and each decision is a slot. A finding about one slot does not licence a statement about all of them.

4. **The habitat is enumerated, not chosen from a standing list.** Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* The offered four are a standing list assembled before the harm was described. Enumerate outward until the cascade stops mattering; Fig 11 :52-53 gives the DDT case where the residual that mattered was three steps out.

5. **The relation carries the attribution, not the list.** Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."* Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* The relevant bonds are production (⇒), sequence (→), authority threshold (⋛) and refusal (¬). Operators table :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."* A four-name list cannot say who could have said no.

6. **An internal assignment of responsibility is a convention until checked externally.** Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"* and Seat Map :167: *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."* An RACI chart or a liability clause in a vendor's terms makes the question statable. If it decides who pays without being ranged against the ground, it has become an outcome-deciding mechanism.

7. **Verdicts stay separate per domain.** Harm registration (D1), the decision and habitat (D2), institutional settlement (D3), and outside verification (D4) each have their own anchor and their own verdict. They are never averaged into a single "accountability score".

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that the vendor is never liable. F8 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` says only that the vendor's closure cannot settle the deployment decision. The production condition (⇒) remains in the attribution.
- Not that the firm, manager or decision-maker is therefore the answer. Each is a frame ranged against the anchor, not the anchor.
- Not a new theory of legal liability, and not a new actor category. This is a claim about shape and order.
- Not a verdict on any real case. No court, regulator, board, log or harm record was used as a ground here.
- Not `grounded`. `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."*

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A real finding, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

- **Per-slot harm reading:** what was the harm to this party, and is the reading stable when the parties push on it? If it moves, the channel is broken and the remedy is to restore the reading, not to adjudicate (Fig 10 :24-29).
- **Vocabulary instrument:** is there a record of what each role could have refused, overridden or changed at the point the harm became foreseeable? This is Fig 3's middle link, the one with no obvious instrument. Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
- **Unbundling check:** which of the four conditions actually held in this slot, and which intersections were live?
- **Threshold and sequence:** where was the authority threshold crossed, and in what order did the actions occur?
- **External settlement:** a court, regulator or board finding with reasons and a date, stable under independent recomputation. *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"Courts of law rule on evidence, which neither side can move."*

Standing refuters (written before this derivation, `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §5): a candidate shown to be the anchor; the four conditions shown mutually exclusive and jointly exhaustive; all four conditions met with no institution able to settle the attribution (which would make the honest verdict `unreachable`); or a grounded finding assigning exactly one of the four, stable and unmoved by pressure.

## 5. The honest stop

**Reached a shape, not a result.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."* The figures return the form the answer must have, not a name. What is missing is not a fifth actor but a ground reading per slot: an instrument for the middle link and an external finding. *How Intelligence Actually Works*: *"Alignment asks what the thing is answerable to. A ground, something that refuses, a bill that gets paid."* Until such a bill is paid, the verdict stays `candidate`. Indiscernibility is not identity.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not pick one of the four. It says the question is a bundle of overlapping conditions, not four alternatives, so the conditions must be unbundled before any "who" is searched. None of the four can be the thing that settles the attribution, because each is a party with a stake in the reading; the anchor is external to all four, a human-facing institution whose finding does not move when the parties push on it. Accountability is then a per-harm, per-slot structure of relations: who produced the artifact, who deployed it, who had the authority to refuse, and where the threshold was crossed. An internal responsibility chart is a convention until that external anchor checks it. What the framework offers is the shape any answer must have, the refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The actual name, in any real case, is owed to a ground, not to this derivation.
