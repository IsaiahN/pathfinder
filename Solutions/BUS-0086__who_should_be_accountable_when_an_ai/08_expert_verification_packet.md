# Expert verification packet: the Tether on AI accountability

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in AI governance and accountability can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No case finding is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status: simulated derivation, not a ground reading.** It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "vendor, firm, manager, or decision-maker" is a bundle of overlapping conditions masquerading as a choice among alternatives. The four names stand for production, authority to deploy, duty of oversight and authorization of the particular decision; because they overlap, picking one clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. None of the four can be the thing that settles the attribution, since each is a party with an interest in the reading of who answers. The settlement anchor is external to all four: the harm registered per slot, and a human-facing institution (court, regulator, board answerable to affected people) whose finding does not move when a party pushes on it. The attribution itself is a per-slot structure of typed relations: who produced, who deployed, in what order, where the authority threshold was crossed, and who could have refused. An internal responsibility assignment is a convention until that external anchor checks it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - about the structure of the question; checkable by a governance or responsibility researcher

- **Statement:** the four-way choice is a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition, so it must be unbundled before a "who" is searched.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54 - *"When the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :55 - *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Seat Map :211 gives the same rule in the checking arrangement document.
- **World corroboration (secondary):** Santoni de Sio and Mecacci (2021, *Philosophy & Technology*) argue the responsibility gap is *"not one problem but a set of at least four interconnected problems"*; Kiener (2025, *Journal of Applied Philosophy*) argues for *"responsibility abundance"* where *"numerous agents are responsible for the harm caused by AI"*. The gap and abundance literatures are two conditions of the same bundle, not opposed verdicts.
- **Refuter:** demonstrate that the four conditions are mutually exclusive and jointly exhaustive in the relevant case class, so a single lever closes the residual. If that holds, this sub-claim is withdrawn.

### C2 - about the anchor; checkable by anyone working on institutional responsibility

- **Statement:** none of the four parties can be the anchor that settles accountability. The anchor is external, and legitimate here because the question is constitutively about people harmed.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :86 - *"Outside the slice being graded. Whoever is measured by a reading cannot own it."* Fig 2 :36 - *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* Fig 2 :37 - *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."* Seat Map :22 - *"An anchor listed as one of the parties has already become a frame."*
- **World corroboration (secondary):** the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry on the ethics of AI notes that enforcement requires the affected party to *"identify the responsible legal entity, prove the action, perhaps prove intent, find a court that declares itself competent"* and obtain enforcement. NIST AI RMF 1.0 places governance and oversight with senior leadership and the board. EU AI Act Article 14 places the authority to disregard, override or reverse the system's output in named natural persons. The tacit-knowledge essay states the same rule in institutional terms: *"Courts of law rule on evidence, which neither side can move."*
- **Refuter:** produce a case where one of the four is itself the thing that settles the question (its answer does not move when any frame pushes on it) and the question is constitutively about that thing rather than about the people harmed. That would require reframing the question as "is this artifact correct".

### C3 - about the form of the attribution; checkable by inspecting the operators

- **Statement:** the attribution is carried by typed bonds, not by the list of four names. The relevant bonds are production (⇒), sequence (→), threshold (⋛) and refusal (¬). A vocabulary of four nouns cannot say who could have refused.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :5 - *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Fig 12 :94 - *"The arrangement is the substance."* Fig 12 :111 - *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* Operators table :58 - *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*
- **Refuter:** exhibit a case where the four roles are independent atoms whose mere enumeration fully determines the attribution without any relation among them, and where no refusal was possible.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that the vendor is never liable, nor that the firm, manager or decision-maker is always liable.
- Not that the framework can name the accountable party in a real case. It returns a shape, not a name.
- Not a new theory of legal liability, and no new actor category.
- Not `grounded`. No finding, log, measurement or case record was used as a ground.
- Not that agreement among governance researchers corroborates the claim. Agreement from a shared evidence pool is one witness, not two (Fig 2 :40).

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not more argument:

1. **Per-slot harm reading**, stable when the parties push on it. If it moves, the channel is broken and the remedy is to restore the reading (Fig 10 :24-29).
2. **A record of what each role could have refused, overridden or changed** at the point the harm became foreseeable. This is Fig 3's middle link; Fig 3 :27 - *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
3. **An unbundling of the live conditions** for the specific slot, with the intersections named.
4. **An external finding with reasons and a date**, stable under independent recomputation, assigning or declining attribution per slot.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :54-58 and Fig 10 :86, :59 (five short passages).
2. Check C1 against a case you know: are the four conditions actually overlapping, or mutually exclusive and jointly exhaustive?
3. Name the institution in your jurisdiction that can settle the attribution without moving when a party pushes on it, or say why none can.
4. Say what would count as a failure of shape, not just a failure of wording.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
