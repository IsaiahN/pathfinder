# Outreach draft: check the output before you decide whether the framing is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F1 to F3 have been checked by the recipient. The output must survive their check or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about AI accountability, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read any framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with me. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a harm from an AI-driven business decision is a residual indexed per harmed party, not a single organizational number. The four names in the usual question (vendor, firm, manager, decision-maker) are not four answers. They are four overlapping conditions: production of the artifact, authority to deploy, duty of oversight, and authorization of the particular decision. Because they overlap, the question is a union compound, and naming one of them is a single lever that clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. The conditions have to be unbundled before any "who" is searched.
>
> The claim is also that none of the four can be the thing that settles the attribution. Each is a party to the case, with an interest in the reading of who answers. The thing that settles it is external to all four: the harm as registered per slot, and a human-facing institution (a court, a regulator, a board answerable to affected people) that does not change its answer because one of the parties pushes on it. The question is about people harmed, so human judgment is the legitimate anchor. A model's correctness is a different question with a different anchor.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the vendor is never liable, that the firm is always liable, that the manager's signature decides the case, or that the decision-maker is therefore the answer. Those are bindings of a real case, and only a real ground (an actual finding, with reasons and a date) settles them. I am also not claiming a new theory of liability. This is a claim about the shape the answer must have and the order in which the question must be asked.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. show that the four conditions are mutually exclusive and jointly exhaustive, so one name closes the residual;
> 2. show that one of the four is itself the anchor, meaning its answer does not move when any frame pushes on it and the question is constitutively about that thing;
> 3. produce a real case where all four meet the usual conditions for responsibility and no human-facing institution can settle the attribution at all, which would make the honest verdict "unreachable" rather than a shape;
> 4. produce a grounded finding that assigns accountability to exactly one of the four, is stable under independent recomputation, and does not move when the parties push on it. That would settle that case, and I would record it as a case-level result.
>
> I wrote these down before running the derivation or sending this. If any holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - The eight-step loop and mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - The checking arrangement and seat rules (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_SEAT_MAP_Field_Guide_amended.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
