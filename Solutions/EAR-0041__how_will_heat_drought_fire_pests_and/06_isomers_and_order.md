## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the five conditions isomers?

Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**

As the question lists them, the five do **not** share one atom set. Heat and drought are chiefly water and energy terms; fire is fuel, heat and a return loop; pests are a host-network relation; land conversion is a change of the habitat itself. By Fig 12's definition the honest weaker reading is a **functional group**: Fig 12 :123, **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** The shared substructure is "condition acting on a stand"; the qualifiers differ.

The stronger form is a real claim: hold the atom set fixed as `{host, water, carbon, heat, fuel, insect, edge, seed source}` and vary only the bond. Heat is a gradient over water and carbon; drought is a subtraction of water (Fig 12 :80-83); fire is a cycle whose output re-enters as input (Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**); pests are a network relation that produces host change; land conversion is a habitat substitution (Fig 11 :66). Over that fixed set they are isomers: same atoms, different bond, decisive behavioural difference. This is a `specified` binding, not a derivation.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two conditions written over the identical atom set with different bonds produce the same stand outcome under the same ground reading, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. A second falsifier: if a condition requires an atom the others cannot be written with, the fixed-set claim fails.

### Q2 - What is the dependency order of the measurement chain?

Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."** The links are perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry (Fig 3 :4-15). Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

Applied to a stand, the order is:

1. **Perception:** a reading of the stand that can tell it apart from what was there before (live basal area, crown, regeneration). Fig 1 :11: *"residual R indexed by object slot"*.
2. **Vocabulary:** terms for what the conditions afford (fuel, host susceptibility, edge, moisture). This is the middle link with no obvious instrument.
3. **Objective:** a declared comparison that says what counts as survival at this stand and this horizon (Fig 12 :87, a threshold relation).
4. **Planning:** a route under incomplete information, with the recognition that introduction is available and exclusion is not (Fig 11 :61).
5. **Learn and carry:** a method that can cross to another stand, with the round trip measured (Fig 4 :22, :30).

A stand can be monitored at link 1 and still have no term at link 2 or 3. Effort spent above the break cannot rescue one below it (Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**). A reading taken below the break is *"a reading of nothing"* (Fig 3 :23).

### Q3 - What is the order of the physical cascade?

The bonds are ordered, not symmetric. Fig 12 :70: **"sequence"** with test *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*; Fig 12 :76: **"production"** with *"A produces B; B did not exist before"*.

The world sources support one recurring order: drought changes host water status and carbon balance; a weakened host is more susceptible to beetles; beetles and drought change snag and fuel loads; fire then acts on a changed fuel bed; the post-fire state changes seed source and microclimate and therefore regeneration. The *Forest dieback* page reports the first two steps directly: *"Trees can be resilient. However, that can be changed when the ecosystem is hit with a drought episode. This results in trees becoming more susceptible to insect infestations, thereby triggering a dieback event."* The *Bark beetle* page reports the host-weakening condition: *"Bark beetles often attack trees that are already weakened by disease, overcrowding, conspecific beetles, or physical damage."* The *Habitat fragmentation* page reports that *"Fires become more likely in the area as humidity drops and temperature and wind levels rise"*, giving the edge-to-fire step.

This order is `specified` and import-bound. It is not a universal sequence: in a fire-adapted stand fire precedes beetle attack in some cycles, and the sign of the loop decides whether it closes (Fig 12 :121). The naive generalisation "beetles increase fire severity" is **declined**: the *Bark beetle* page reports that *"most studies of wildfire after beetle outbreaks have found no effect of beetle-caused tree mortality on wildfire size or severity."* That conflict is returned to the census rather than used to flip the verdict.

### Q4 - How do the scales compose?

Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."** A stand whose state is settled by measurement becomes an atom for a landscape composition. The crossing is lossy: Fig 4 :30, **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."** The measured quantity is the round-trip gap `R_T`, not the apparent agreement between a stand plot and a satellite index. The *Biogeosciences* dryland-forest paper (opened on the journal home page, 2026) reports a related early-warning shape: declining carbon uptake preceded visible structural deterioration by up to 37 years. That is a source-discriminating candidate, reported as a world corroboration of the instrument claim, not as a figure line and not as a universal lead time.

### Q5 - What are the two currencies?

Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."** Fig 12 :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."** A five-interaction model is cheap to describe and expensive to refute; a two-term host-and-water model is cheap to state and may take a season to settle at a stand. Summing them into one resilience score is the error named, not the fix. This is why `07_the_tether_answer.md` returns a set of verdicts and a refuter instead of one number.

## CONCLUSION

As the question writes them, the five conditions are a **functional group**, not isomers: they share "condition acting on a stand" but not one atom set. Over an explicitly fixed stand-atom set they are a genuine isomer claim with a stated falsifier, and this is the stronger `specified` version worth testing. The licensed order is dependency first (perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, carry) and, in the physical cascade, an ordered sequence whose loop sign decides whether it closes. Scale crossing carries a measured R_T. Two currencies are held apart.

This framing rescues what is defensible in the question without inventing a coefficient: the five items differ by bond and by level, so an ingredient list cannot predict outcomes; the order is a dependency and a cascade, not a ranking of importance. `metric of heat = gradient` type mappings are bindings from the forest-science frame and are provenance-bound, not derivations and not imports that move the wall.
