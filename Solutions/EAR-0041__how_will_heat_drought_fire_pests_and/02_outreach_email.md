**Subject:** Check one forest-survival output before you decide whether the method is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output about heat, drought, fire, pests and land conversion that I would like you to check against what you already know from stands you have measured. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the five items are not five doses of one pressure. They are a union of overlapping conditions with different anchors, indexed per stand. Heat and drought couple through hydraulic failure and carbon starvation in the same tree. Drought changes host susceptibility before beetles act. Beetles and drought change fuel and snag loads before fire acts. Land conversion changes the edge microclimate around a stand, so the tree-only picture has no term for the changed contact. One lever clears the region where a single condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. That is a formal claim about composition, not a claim that all five are always harmful.
>
> **What follows from it:** fire and pests have no standing sign. In fire-adapted systems fire is a renewal process; in fire-intolerant stands it kills the dominants. Bark beetles recycle dead wood in undisturbed forests and kill healthy trees when populations outbreak. What matters is the residual against which each actor is read. A global forest survival percentage is a poor verdict object because it pools the stands that are still stable with the stands that have already flipped.
>
> **A comparison, not a stock.** "Survives" is a threshold between what a stand delivers and what the stand's goal requires, at a named horizon. Past a threshold the stand often does not merely lose forest; it acquires a different habitat with its own feedbacks, and removing the original stressor alone does not restore the former state. Restoration is introduction and permanent work, not subtraction.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I can tell you which stand dies in which year, or give you one interaction coefficient. The framework returns a shape, not a name. The coefficient is a gap I am reporting, not a number I am hiding. I also am not claiming that beetles cause bigger fires; the literature I read reports most post-outbreak studies found no effect on fire size or severity.
>
> **What would refute the shape:**
> 1. One intervention clears all five conditions and all their intersections at a stand.
> 2. Tree physiology alone predicts a converted edge stand with no habitat term and no loss of accuracy.
> 3. A single global forest metric predicts which stand flips and when, as well as per-stand readings do.
> 4. A context-free sign for fire or pests holds across fire-adapted and fire-intolerant stands.
> 5. Removing the third-order cascade leaves the stand residual unchanged.
> 6. A monotonic stock, with no threshold, orders survival across gradual and abrupt pressure regimes.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **Please record your verdict on the output before reading any framework material.** If it survives and you are curious, the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures README, canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight essays and the architecture article are listed in `04_links_and_distribution.md`; I will send the three most relevant only after you ask.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a stand you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name / project]
