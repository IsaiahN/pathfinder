# Outreach draft: check the split before you decide about the answer

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until section 5 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` has been read and the refuter is intact. The offer collapses on contact if the recipient can see a compromise answer hiding behind the split.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about the minimum wage question, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual.** A legislature sets a wage floor. Two things are then asked in one breath: does the floor lift households above a poverty line, and can it remove jobs from some workers? Those are different measurements. One needs household disposable income against a line. The other needs a per-worker employment reading against payroll. The word "or" in the question treats them as alternatives.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the slots, `general` on the structure, `gap` on the pooled sign):** split the question rather than answer it. A wage floor is a threshold relation, not a substance. It binds only where it exceeds the wage that would otherwise clear. Its valence is relative to the residual: it can raise income in the household whose head keeps work and reduce employment in the worker slot that is withdrawn. The two outcomes have different currencies and do not add into one verdict. The household condition and the low-skill condition overlap, and the overlap is a compound with its own measurement, not a harder case of either.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** a universal sign. I am not claiming that minimum wages always reduce poverty, and I am not claiming that they always cost jobs. I am not claiming a pooled net number, because no figure line returns one and no instrument here returns one. That part is declared `gap`, not silence. I am also not claiming that studies which disagree are independent witnesses; if they draw on the same data, their disagreement is one pool talking.
>
> **What would refute the output (written before this message):**
> 1. One slot, measured once against one named anchor, returns both outcomes without loss, replicated by a frame with different data and a different design.
> 2. A properly indexed reading in which the floor improves both slots for the same workers and households, with no compensating loss anywhere in the habitat.
> 3. The employment and household conditions shown to be nested, so clearing one clears the other.
> 4. An accepted common measure that prices a job slot and a currency unit on the same scale, declared before the run and checked independently.
> 5. Payroll readings from two independent sources covering every worker slot in a market, showing no heterogeneity at all.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will withdraw the affected claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would like to know:** what would you count as a failure of the split? Would you range the household reading against a headcount, a poverty gap, or something else? Which payroll source would you trust as an anchor that does not move when an economist pushes on it?
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the links are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Local verbatim figure text (with `Fig N :line` indices): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight essays by Isaiah N: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/how-intelligence-actually-works
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so a write-up that only reports a success is not a check. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - [your name]

## Two-stage discipline

**Stage 1:** send the residual, the split and the five refuters. Ask what the reader would count as a failure. Record their verdict **before** showing any framework material.

**Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40). A Stage 1 refutation is copied into `11_audit.md` with provenance and re-evaluated by the auditor seat as if it came from any other source.

**Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method. Each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode. No claim is made that this package is the only true reading.

Nothing is sent by this agent. Hosting and sending are yours to do.
