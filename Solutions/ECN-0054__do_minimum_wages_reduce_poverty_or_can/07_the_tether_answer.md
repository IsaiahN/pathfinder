# The Tether's answer: minimum wages, poverty and employment

**Mode: `specified` on the per-slot and per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the pooled net sign. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading. No payroll or household survey was read for this solution.

## 1. The residual it answers

A legislature sets a wage floor. The question asks whether it reduces poverty or can reduce employment for some workers. That is not one hard question with two opposite answers. Fig 9 :4-5 says disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working on a different subset, "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." Fig 9 :55 adds: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."

The residual is indexed per slot. Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." The two slots here are the worker's job and the household's income against a line, with the uncovered sector and household composition as the habitat between them.

## 2. The shape

1. **The floor is a comparison, not a substance.** Fig 12 :87 "a threshold relation between two quantities". It binds only where it exceeds the wage that would otherwise clear. The verb "reduce" is a subtraction and a goal comparison (Operators table, attributed). No figure line gives a standing sign to a comparison independent of the residual it is ranged against.
2. **The two outcomes have different currencies and do not add.** Fig 12 :125 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." A wage gain in currency per hour and a job loss in employment slots cannot be summed into one verdict without an imported price. Fig 12 :126 "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."
3. **The same lever has opposite valence in the two slots.** Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." A floor can raise income in the household whose head keeps work and withdraw the job of a low-skilled worker. Both readings can be true at once.
4. **The overlap is a compound.** Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound." The worker who is both low-skilled and in a poor household needs both readings. One lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing.
5. **The order is dependency order.** Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Employment is upstream: a household cannot be lifted by the wage of a job that was not read. Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." The poverty objective has no instrument until a line is named.
6. **Each slot needs its own anchor.** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." For employment that is a payroll record; for poverty it is a household income reading. Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Agreement among studies that share a survey is one pool, not two witnesses (Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool").
7. **The habitat must be enumerated outward.** Fig 11 :43-44 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Covered and uncovered sectors, compliance, informal work, household heads and secondary earners, transfers and sharing all sit in contact with the residual. Fig 11 :52-53 "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."
8. **The universal sign is a `gap`.** Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence." No figure line returns a pooled net sign or a universal magnitude, and no instrument held here returns one. The framework returns the shape any answer must have: per slot, per anchor, with a refusal written first. Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself".

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "minimum wages reduce poverty" as a universal. That is a verdict stated without an anchor.
- Not "minimum wages do not cost jobs" as a universal. That would refuse the per-slot evidence.
- Not a pooled net welfare number, not a magic floor-to-median ratio, not a universal elasticity. No figure line returns a magnitude.
- Not that the two literatures are two independent witnesses. Where they share data, they are one pool talking.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken in this derivation.
- Not a standing list of "good policies". Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." A floor and a tax credit are isomers over a shared atom set, arranged differently (`06_isomers_and_order.md`), and their valence is read per residual.

## 5. What would settle it, and the honest stop

**The ground, not agreement.** Four readings would move the verdict toward `grounded`:

1. A payroll reading per worker slot from an anchor that does not update on the researcher's expectation, with the floor's binding status recorded.
2. A household income reading per household slot against a declared poverty line, distinguishing headcount from poverty gap and heads from secondary earners.
3. Two frames with no shared data, no shared identification design and no shared author, each ranged separately against those anchors. Their convergence would then count as two witnesses rather than one pool.
4. A common scale that prices a job slot and a currency unit, declared before the run with a published source and a shadow test. That is the import Fig 6 :23 would require: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."

**Reversal conditions.** A properly indexed reading in which the floor improves both slots for the same workers and households with no compensating loss would refute F3. Nested rather than overlapping conditions would refute F4. A figure line directly returning a pooled sign would refute F10. All eight pre-registered conditions are in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 5.

**Honest stop.** The shape is settled; the sign is owed. The answer stops at the limit of the instruments available here and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *They're Scanning Books*: "A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six." The figures README: "Indiscernibility is not identity." A pooled number that no instrument distinguishes from an answer is still not an answer.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer the minimum wage question with a yes or a no. It says the question's "or" is a false choice: a wage floor is a threshold comparison, and its two outcomes are different slots with different currencies and different anchors. A household whose head keeps work can cross a poverty line while another worker loses a job; both readings can be true at once, and they cannot be added. The overlap, a low-skilled worker in a poor household, is a compound that needs both readings, and the uncovered sector and household composition decide whether the first effect reaches the second. The employment reading must come first, ranged against payroll, and the household reading against income. Studies that share the same survey are one witness, not two. What the framework returns is the shape any answer must have, five failure conditions written in advance, and the name of the instrument that would close the remaining `gap`. The pooled net sign stays open until a ground, not an agreement, settles it.
