## 0. The question under audit, stated as a residual

> Do minimum wages reduce poverty, or can they reduce employment for some workers?

The word "or" does the damage. It presents two outcomes as alternatives. Fig 9 :4-5 says what to do with a disagreement of that shape:

> **"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."**

**Residual in effect terms.** A legislature imposes a wage floor, `a`. The system predicts what happens per slot, `s`. The observation `o'` is a reading of a payroll record or a household income survey. The residual is `R_s = |Γ(b,a) − o'|_s` (Symbols table, per-slot definition, attributed to the local Symbols table). The candidate answers are:

- Option A: the floor reduces poverty. Effect to look for: household disposable income crosses a poverty threshold for some households.
- Option B: the floor can reduce employment for some workers. Effect to look for: a worker slot that had a job before the floor has none after it, conditional on the counterfactual.

What each leads to: A points at a household income survey and a poverty line. B points at payroll records and a worker-by-worker employment history. They are different instruments, different currencies and different anchors. A single pooled sentence cannot be ranged against either one cleanly.

**Binding the sought-for shape as an ordinary term.** Fig 5 :65-66 says the shape a frame seeks is a preference rather than a term, so no internal check can see it. The remedy is a discipline: write down what an answer would look like and let it accrue a residual. I bind it here, before the findings:

> An acceptable answer to this question is a set of verdicts, one per named slot, each stating (i) the quantity measured, (ii) the anchor it was ranged against, (iii) the mode of the claim, and (iv) a failure condition written before the reading. It does not state a single net sign, and it does not average a wage gain against a job loss.

That term is refusable. Findings F10 and F11 below point the refusal at it.

## 1. Domain census, before any figure line is chosen

R described by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor. No nameable anchor means it is not yet a domain.

**R (the residual).** Arity: three, one policy lever and two outcome quantities (household income against a line, employment per worker). Symmetry: "reduce" is an ordered comparison with a subtraction, not a symmetric bond. Operators table: "progress is a subtraction, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot say how far along it is" and "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one" (attributed to the Operators table). The wage floor itself is a ⋛ threshold: Fig 12 :87 "a threshold relation between two quantities". Scale: per worker-firm slot, per household slot, per sector, per policy regime. Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." and Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

**Dependency order (Fig 3).** Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." and Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." The employment link comes before the household-income link: a household cannot be lifted by the wage of a job that does not exist. Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." The objective link, what counts as "reduce poverty", has no single instrument until a poverty line is named.

**Partition or union.** A union, not a partition (Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."). The intersection is not a harder case of either condition: Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound." That compound is the low-skilled worker in a poor household, the very group a floor is meant to help and the group for which both readings must be taken.

**Strain test applied before concluding.** An early reading tried to make Fig 11 :51 ("No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual.") govern the wage floor as if it were an actor with a fixed moral valence. That strains the line: the floor is a comparison imposed on a frame, and Fig 11 governs actors in a habitat. Re-census: the floor's *valence* is read per residual through D1 and D2, while Fig 12 :87 governs the floor as a threshold. Different quantities, no strain after re-census. ## 3. Findings

### F1: The two outcomes are different slots, not two answers to one question (decisive)

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**
Fig 9 :4-5: **"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."**

A national employment rate and a national poverty rate are two different slots. A household head who keeps a job at the new floor and a teenager who loses one are different slots inside the same labour market. Pooling them into one "did the minimum wage work" number throws away the only signal that says where to look.

**Refuter:** a demonstration that one and the same slot, measured once against one anchor, returns both outcomes without loss, replicated by a frame drawing on a different data source and design, would remove the need to split. Until then the split stands.

**Mode:** `general` on the structural split, `specified` on the slot lists. Triangulated: Fig 1 (per-slot residual) and Fig 9 (non-shrinking disagreement) are low-overlap routes to the same boundary.

### F2: The wage floor is a comparison, not an atom or a substance

Fig 12 :87: **"a threshold relation between two quantities"** (Figure 12, bond table, ⋛ comparison).
Operators table: **"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"**.
Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**

A floor only binds where the floor exceeds the wage that would otherwise clear. It is a threshold relation between the floor and that wage, and then between the resulting pay and a poverty line. Asking whether the floor "is" good or bad treats a relation as an ingredient. The framework cannot name a standing sign for a comparison independently of the residual it is ranged against.

**Refuter:** a measurement showing the floor changing an outcome in a market where it does not bind, with the anchor unchanged, would break the threshold reading.

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated (Fig 12 bond table plus Operators table).

### F3: The same lever has opposite valence in the two slots

Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
Fig 11 :50: **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**
Fig 11 :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

The floor is a benefactor to the household whose head keeps work and gets a raise. It is a malfactor to the worker whose job is withdrawn. Both readings can be true at once. This is not a contradiction sent back to census; it is the predicted shape when one lever contacts two residuals. Gindling (IZA World of Labor, opened 2026-11-23) states the empirical form directly: minimum wages "pull some households out of poverty, but may push others into poverty."

**Refuter:** a properly indexed reading in which the floor improves both slots for the same workers and households, with no compensating loss elsewhere in the enumerated habitat, would refute the relative-valence claim for that policy.

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated (Fig 11 valence plus the household and payroll anchors in D1 and D2).

### F4: The intersection is a compound with its own loop

Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."**

The worker who is both low-skilled and in a poor household is the overlap. A lever that clears only the employment condition leaves the intersection standing, because the household condition still binds. One policy lever cannot be expected to clear a compound. Neumark's IZA article (opened 2026-11-23) names the mismatch of targets: minimum wages "mandate high wages for low-wage workers rather than higher earnings for low-income families." Gindling names the same seam for developing countries: a large share of workers is not covered, so the floor does not reach most poor households.

**Refuter:** showing that the two conditions are nested rather than overlapping, so that clearing one clears both, would dissolve the compound and allow a single verdict.

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated (Fig 9 union rule plus the two world sources, which share a labour-economics pool and therefore count as one witness on the figure side).

### F5: Two currencies do not add

Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**
Fig 12 :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**

A wage gain measured in income and a job loss measured in employment slots are different currencies. Adding them into one "net welfare effect" requires a price that no figure supplies. Neumark's IZA article notes that even a correctly signed elasticity cannot be read as a net gain, because "the job loss they estimate could be much higher as a percentage of workers who could potentially benefit from the higher minimum wage." That is a currency mismatch stated in the literature's own terms.

**Refuter:** an accepted common measure that prices a job slot and a currency unit against the same ground, stated in advance and agreed by frames that do not share data, would allow the addition. Without it, the no-addition verdict stands.

**Mode:** `general` on the structural rule, `specified` on the two named currencies. Triangulated (Fig 12 plus IZA, with the world source as corroboration only).

### F6: The employment verdict must be indexed per worker slot and ranged against a payroll anchor

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**
Fig 2 :31: **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."**

The claim that employment can fall "for some workers" is a claim about a subset of slots. Card and Krueger's fast-food comparison and Neumark and Wascher's payroll reanalysis are famous partly because they read different instruments: employer survey responses versus payroll records. The disagreement did not shrink with effort, which is Fig 9 :4-5's signature of several well-formed questions rather than one hard one. The anchor for the employment slot is the payroll record, which does not update on what any economist thinks.

**Refuter:** a payroll reading covering every worker slot in a market, with no subset left unresolved, returned by two frames with genuinely different data sources, and showing no heterogeneity, would refute the per-slot requirement for that market.

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated (Fig 1 per-slot plus Fig 2 anchor, with Card and Krueger and Neumark and Wascher as one pooled world witness, not two).

### F7: The poverty verdict must be indexed per household slot and ranged against an income anchor

Fig 11 :49 (above) and Fig 12 :87 (above) govern this domain. Fig 2 :36: **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."**

Poverty is defined by household income against a line, not by an individual wage. Gindling (IZA) records that in Guatemala "there are almost no minimum wage workers in the poorest two income deciles" and that 58 percent of minimum wage workers are in the top half of the household income distribution. Whether a floor reduces poverty therefore depends on which household slots it touches. The anchor is the household income reading; the poverty line is the comparison term.

**Refuter:** a household income reading in which every worker affected by the floor lives in a poor household, with no secondary-earner substitution, would collapse the targeting problem and leave one household verdict.

**Mode:** `specified`, single-source on the figure side for the household anchor (Fig 2 :36), corroborated by Gindling. Kept as `single-source`, not upgraded.

### F8: Agreement between studies is one evidence pool, not two witnesses

Fig 2 :40: **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**
Fig 8 :48: **"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."**
Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**

Much of the minimum wage literature draws on the same few sources, the US Current Population Survey and the Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages. Neumark's IZA article and the Dube review (GOV.UK, opened 2026-11-23) reach opposite summaries partly from different weighting of overlapping studies. Those are not two independent frames just because they disagree. If they quote the same estimates, their disagreement is a family quarrel inside one pool. The design effect literature (Wikipedia, "Design effect", opened 2026-11-23) states the arithmetic: effective sample size is the original sample size divided by the design effect, and cluster sampling with correlated observations inflates the design effect. Kish's correction and Fig 2's independence rule are the same quantity in two vocabularies.

**Refuter:** a pair of studies with no shared data, no shared identification design and no shared author, each ranged separately against the same payroll or household anchor, whose agreement would then count as two witnesses. The refuter is a design requirement, not an opinion.

**Mode:** `general` on the structural rule, triangulated (Fig 2, Fig 7, Fig 8 and the statistics source, with low overlap between the statistics and the economics fields).

### F9: A saturated or pooled metric severs the channel while the ground does not decay

Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**
Fig 10 :26: **"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"**

A national poverty rate is a pooled reading. A national employment elasticity is a saturated metric: it compresses heterogeneous slot outcomes into one number. When the headcount falls but the deepest households fall further, the metric can report improvement while the ground has moved the other way. Gindling (IZA) distinguishes the incidence of poverty from the poverty gap and notes that a floor can raise households near the line while reducing the incomes of the poorest. That is exactly the channel failure Fig 10 names.

**Refuter:** a metric that preserves every slot reading and still returns a single number without loss would dissolve this failure. Until one is supplied, the pooled number is a head start, not a verdict.

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated (Fig 10 plus Gindling, world source secondary only).

### F10: The universal sign is a `gap`, not a hidden answer

Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."**
Fig 13 :135: **"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."**
Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**

No figure line returns the sign or magnitude of a pooled net effect of a floor on both poverty and employment. The framework returns the shape any answer must have: per slot, per anchor, with a refusal condition. Declaring `gap` here is honest, not an omission. The correct instruction is the formula's step 7 (attributed to `THE_FORMULA.md`): ask SEARCHED first, then INWARD for a better instrument, then OUTWARD for a frame whose closure predicts the shape. Importing an off-the-shelf "consensus elasticity" into the slot would add an atom that was not needed and would price above starting from primitives (Fig 8 :43 range, attributed to the figure text).

**Refuter:** a figure line that directly returns a universal sign for this compound, found by re-census and quoted verbatim, would refute the `gap` verdict.

**Mode:** `general` on the gap verdict. Verdict: `gap`, triangulated (Fig 13, Fig 9, and the formula's step 7, with the formula attributed separately).

### F11: The ordinary answer term is refusable, and part of it should be refused

Fig 5 :66: **"Then the preference is refusable by the same test as everything else."** (from the verbatim amendment in Figure 5, attributed to Fig 5 :65-66)
Fig 5 :55: **"What it removes is not a term but a term's standing to be composed over. Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach."**

The bound answer term from section 0 contains four conditions. Two of them survive: per-slot verdicts, and a pre-written failure condition. One is refused as written: any claim that a single net verdict can range against both anchors at once is a preference the frame held before the run, now bound and refused. A refusal adds a term and removes nothing; the reach falls because something was added. This is why the answer file (`07_the_tether_answer.md`) keeps two verdicts separate instead of compromising between them.

**Refuter:** showing that the bound term accrues no residual because its four conditions are tautological would mean I bound a definition, not an answer. The four conditions are stated so they can be checked for emptiness.

**Mode:** `general`, single-source (Fig 5 amendment). The refusal is kept visible rather than smoothed over.

### F12: The room that agrees is not the room that decides

Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**
Fig 10 :52: **"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."**
Fig 9 :27-28: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**

The outreach artifacts therefore lead with the split and its refuter, and offer the figures only after a reader has recorded what they would count as a failure. This is a design consequence, not etiquette. Asking a labour economist to endorse the framework before checking the per-slot claim converts them from an outside frame into a member of the room.

**Refuter:** a demonstrated case in which showing the source first leaves the reader's independence intact would remove the ordering rule. None is supplied here.

**Mode:** `general`, triangulated (Fig 2, Fig 9, Fig 10).

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion and before any outreach, per the Seat Map: "And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." (local Seat Map :287, attributed)

The solution is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A single slot, measured once against one named anchor, returns both the household-income and employment outcomes without loss, replicated by a frame with no shared data or design. This refutes F1.
2. A properly indexed reading shows the floor improving both slots for the same workers and households, with no compensating loss anywhere in the enumerated habitat. This refutes F3.
3. The employment and household conditions are shown to be nested, so that clearing one clears the other. This refutes F4.
4. An accepted common measure prices a job slot and a currency unit against the same ground, is declared before the run, and survives independent checking. This refutes F5.
5. Payroll readings covering every worker slot in a market, from two independent data sources, show no heterogeneity at all. This refutes F6's per-slot indexing for that market.
6. A household income reading shows every affected worker in a poor household, with no secondary-earner substitution. This refutes F7's targeting seam.
7. A re-census finds a figure line that directly returns the sign or magnitude of a pooled net effect. This refutes F10's `gap`.
8. The four conditions of the bound answer term are shown to be tautological, so the term accrues no residual. This refutes F11.

If any fires, the affected finding is withdrawn in this file with the date and the reason. Nothing is silently erased.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Split the question before answering it. State the employment verdict and the poverty verdict separately, each with its slot and anchor (F1, F3).
2. Treat the floor as a threshold and name the comparison term (F2).
3. Enumerate the habitat outward: covered and uncovered sectors, compliance, household composition, transfers, and the informal sector (Fig 11 :43-44 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." and Fig 11 :52-53 "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."). Do not stop at the first-order job effect.
4. Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong. Prefer designs with different data sources and different identification over more estimates from the same survey (F8, Fig 2 :42).
5. Keep the universal sign as `gap`. Offer the shape and the refuter, not a number (F10).
6. Send the generator, not a recording: Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." The two-stage offer in `02_outreach_email.md` follows this order.

## 8. Strains, re-census, and what remains live

- **Strain S1, floor as actor versus floor as comparison.** Dissolved by re-census in section 1. Fig 11 governs valence of actors in a habitat; Fig 12 :87 governs a threshold relation. The floor is the latter, and its valence is then read per residual.
- **Strain S2, "minimum wage reduces poverty" versus "minimum wage reduces employment".** Dissolved by re-census: the two verbs have different objects and different anchors. Fig 9 :4-5 and :57 are the governing lines.
- **Strain S3, a world-source conflict.** Neumark's IZA article and the Dube GOV.UK review summarise the same literature differently. The conflict rule sent the matter back to the census, where it was re-mapped to F8 (one evidence pool, different weightings) rather than flipping a verdict. The disagreement is recorded as evidence for the union, not as a tie to be split.
- **Strain S4, live type question.** Can a payroll "job slot" and a household "income unit" ever be priced on one scale? Fig 12 :125 says they do not add; the world literature sometimes computes a net welfare measure. The measure requires a declared social welfare function, which is an import with a source gate, not a figure line. Named, not hidden.
- **Strain S5, live instrument gap.** There is no single instrument that returns the per-slot effect for a worker and the per-household effect at the same time. That is F10's `gap`, reported as unreachable at this budget, not as underivable in principle.
- After re-census: **two live items (S4, S5), three dissolved, no strain smoothed over.**

## 9. Round trip note

The shorthand carried into the answer files is: split first, read each slot against its own anchor, do not add the currencies, return a `gap` for the pooled sign. Fig 4 :30 requires the round trip: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." The concretisation drops the provenance of the instruments, the independence debit on shared data, and the untyped welfare price. `11_audit.md` enumerates the dropped constraints qualitatively as R_T. No numerical gap is claimed, because no ground reading was taken.
