# Expert verification packet: the Tether on minimum wages, poverty and employment

**What this is:** a claim in a form a labour economist or economic methodologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-slot claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the pooled net sign. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

**Standing:** this packet cannot settle the question it reports on. It asks for a refutation of the shape, not endorsement of the framework.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question's "or" presents two outcomes as alternatives when they are different slots with different currencies and different anchors. A wage floor is a threshold comparison that binds only above the wage that would otherwise clear. Its valence is relative to the residual: it can raise household income where a head keeps work and withdraw a job from a low-skilled worker, and both readings can be true at once. The two outcomes cannot be added into one verdict. Their overlap, low skill and low household income in the same household, is a compound that needs both readings. The employment reading is upstream of the household income reading, each must be ranged against an anchor that does not update on the frame's opinion, and studies that share a survey are one witness. No figure line returns a universal net sign, so that part is declared `gap`, with the instrument that would close it named.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 9 and 12

- **Statement:** the two outcomes are a union of overlapping conditions with different currencies, not two answers to one question; a pooled verdict is refused.
- **Verbatim lines, checked against `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`:**
  - Fig 9 :4-5 "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."
  - Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."
  - Fig 12 :125 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."
  - Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
- **Refuter:** show that one slot measured once against one anchor returns both outcomes without loss, replicated by a frame with different data and a different design; or supply an accepted common scale declared before the run that prices both currencies and survives independent checking.

### C2: economics-facing; checkable against the two literatures

- **Statement:** a low wage and a poor household are different targets, and the same floor can create winners and losers among poor households. This is why the household reading must be per household and must distinguish heads from secondary earners and headcount from poverty gap.
- **What to check:** Neumark, IZA World of Labor (opened 2026-11-23), says minimum wages "mandate high wages for low-wage workers rather than higher earnings for low-income families" and that "the studies that focus on the least-skilled workers find the strongest evidence that minimum wages reduce jobs". Gindling, IZA World of Labor (opened 2026-11-23), says minimum wages "pull some households out of poverty, but may push others into poverty" and that in Guatemala "there are almost no minimum wage workers in the poorest two income deciles". Dube, GOV.UK review (opened 2026-11-23), reports "a very muted effect of minimum wages on employment, while significantly increasing the earnings of low paid workers". Are those characterisations faithful? Do the two IZA articles and the review draw on overlapping estimates? If so, their disagreement is one evidence pool, not two independent frames.
- **Figure line that governs:** Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
- **Refuter:** a household income reading in which every affected worker lives in a poor household with no secondary-earner substitution; or a properly indexed reading in which the floor improves both slots for the same workers and households with no compensating loss anywhere in the enumerated habitat.

### C3: framework-internal derivation about order and independence

- **Statement:** the employment reading is upstream of the household income reading, and agreement among studies that share a survey does not count as two witnesses.
- **Verbatim lines:**
  - Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."
  - Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."
  - Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"
  - Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
- **Refuter:** show that household income can be settled without first reading whether the job exists; or provide two studies with no shared data, no shared design and no shared author whose agreement is then independent. The design-effect literature (Kish, via the opened statistics page) gives the arithmetic for the second test: effective sample size is the original sample size divided by the design effect.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a universal yes or no to the question as posed.
- Not a pooled net welfare number, a magic ratio, or a universal elasticity.
- Not that minimum wages always reduce poverty, and not that they never cost jobs.
- Not that the two literatures are independent witnesses. Shared data is declared.
- Not `grounded`. No payroll or household measurement was taken in this derivation.
- Not that the framework is the only true reading. It is a candidate generator with a written refuter.
- Not that "shape, not a name" forbids naming the payroll and household instruments. It governs missing primitives; these are bindings from available frames (correction kept visible in `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## 5. What would settle it

Ground readings, not further argument:

1. A payroll record per worker slot with the floor's binding status recorded, from an anchor that does not update on the researcher's expectation.
2. A household income survey per household slot against a declared line, distinguishing headcount from poverty gap and heads from secondary earners.
3. Two frames with different data sources and different identification designs, each ranged separately against the same anchor. Convergence under those conditions counts as two witnesses.
4. A pre-registered common scale with a published source and a shadow test, if one exists.
5. A figure re-census that finds a line returning the pooled sign directly, which would refute C1's `gap`.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :4-5, Fig 9 :57, Fig 12 :125 and Fig 1 :19, four short passages, against the local verbatim extract.
2. Check C2 against the opened IZA and GOV.UK pages: are the three characterisations faithful, and do the sources share estimates?
3. Name one anchor for the employment slot and one for the household slot that would not move if the researcher pushed on them.
4. Write down which of the five refuters they would try first, and why.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
