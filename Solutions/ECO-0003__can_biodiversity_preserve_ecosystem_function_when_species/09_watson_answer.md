# WATSON answer, ECO-0003

**Mode: `specified` on the ecological application, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the identity of a particular preserving species or trait. Not `grounded`.** Figures are primary and decide. World sources are secondary, corroborate but never overrule, and a conflict returns the derivation to the domain census. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. No ecological measurement was taken for it.

## QUESTION

Can biodiversity preserve ecosystem function when species identities and interactions are continually changing?

Restated as an effect: does R_j,k(t) = |predicted f_j,k from (S(t-1), B(t-1)) minus observed f_j,k(t)| stay in band for each function j and site k while the actor set S and relation set B turn over? Option A (preservation by a standing list of good species) predicts that a fixed set of names is sufficient and that preservation ends when those names disappear. Option B (preservation by an arrangement) predicts that preservation is carried by a functional group with response diversity, that members can be exchanged, and that the verdict is per function and per site. What each leads to: A sends a universal verdict that Fig 11 :51 structurally refuses; B sends a conditional shape, per-function verdicts, and a declared gap where the species name would go.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor, union or partition, and order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Arity: multi-slot and multi-place (function, site, time), with at least three covarying terms, so a candidate union of overlapping conditions (Fig 9 :55), not a two-place partition. Symmetry: "preserved" is a threshold comparison ⋛, not a symmetric join; "continually changing" is ordered succession →. Scale: one function-slot and its immediate habitat at the lower scale, landscape and longer time series across a lossy membrane (Fig 4 :27).
- **D1 Function ground.** Actors: functions and the instruments reading them. Currency: magnitude and variability of a measured flux, an ordering. Anchor: the measured flux, legitimate because the question is constitutively about it (Fig 2 :36). Admitted.
- **D2 Habitat / actor field.** Actors: species, conditions, relations. Currency: contact. Anchor: the ground that keeps what survived (Fig 11 :56). Admitted, enumerated never composed.
- **D3 Arrangement / bond structure.** Actors: functional groups, response and effect traits, typed relations. Currency: the bond and the arrangement it carries. Anchor: the function reading that pays for the arrangement. Admitted.
- **D4 Temporal channel.** Actors: instruments, metrics, percepts, turnover events. Currency: contact to the ground. Anchor: the ground, which does not decay (Fig 10 :24). Admitted.
- **D5 Maintainer seat (conditional, managed systems only).** Actors: managers and restorers. Currency: introduction, never exclusion. Anchor: the ground through a channel the seat keeps reachable. Admitted as conditional.
- **X1 "biodiversity" as a bare count.** No nameable actor, no anchor that settles a function verdict. Not a domain yet; moved to filter (Fig 9 :60).
- **X2 "nature's balance" as anchor.** No anchor that does not move when frames change. Refused, no nameable anchor.

Union, not partition: similarity within a group, complementarity between groups, response asynchrony, temporary selection, and spatial subsidy overlap (Fig 9 :56-57). Dependency order: perceive function per slot, acquire an affordance vocabulary, state the preservation threshold, plan an intervention, carry the result as a method (Fig 3 :20). The likely break is at the affordance vocabulary, which has no standard instrument.

Strain test: Fig 11 :51 (no standing list) versus Fig 12 :123 (functional group fixes the outcome) is not strained after re-census because the first governs named actors and the second governs arrangement kinds. Fig 11 :40 (an agent-only improvement changes nothing) versus species addition is not strained because capability is agent-and-habitat (Fig 11 :39) and a role-changing addition alters contact while a redundant one does not.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

Ecosystem function can be preserved while identities and interactions change, but the answer is a conditional shape and not a universal yes. The function reading is the ground and is per function and per site. Preservation is carried by an arrangement, a functional group with response diversity, not by a standing list of named species; members can turn over while the group and its bonds hold. The mechanisms form a union of overlapping conditions, so verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged. Richness is a filter for the budget, not a witness for the verdict. In managed systems the lever is asymmetric and the work is permanent. Which species or trait pays for a named function at a named site is a `gap` closed only by a ground reading. Mode `specified` on the ecological claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`, candidate throughout.

- **Cost if wrong:** misdirected diagnosis and misdirected measurement. A system failing the arrangement condition would be sold more species; a system failing the ground-channel condition would be sold another review. Both are expensive and both leave the actual failure untouched. In outreach terms, an expert's twenty minutes would be spent refuting a shape that was never the right shape. The cost is bounded and locatable because the claim carries five written refuters and a per-domain record (Fig 10 :62).
- **Undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails; a refutation is added and the reach falls because *"something was added rather than because anything was deleted"* (Fig 6 :42). The shape and its refuters are recorded in a provenance trail so a later reader can locate the error rather than only feel it (Fig 10 :62-64). The binding to ecology can be withdrawn without touching the structural distinction between ground and habitat.
- **Reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A source-discriminating measurement at a site showing which member paid for the function in each interval would close the gap; a universal list of named species preserving a function across residuals would fire Fig 11 :51 and break the arrangement claim; a verdict that moves with species reclassification would show the anchor moved (Fig 2 :30); a pooled score that agrees with every per-function score through a failure would remove the per-slot claim; a single lever that clears a two-condition intersection with no residue would remove the union claim. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
