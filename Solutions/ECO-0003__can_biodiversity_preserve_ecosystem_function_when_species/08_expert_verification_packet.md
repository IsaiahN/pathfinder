# Expert verification packet — the Tether on biodiversity and ecosystem function under turnover

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ecosystem-function researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the ecological application, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the identity of any particular preserving species or trait. Not `grounded`.** No site measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not an ecological result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading, and it cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Can biodiversity preserve ecosystem function when species identities and interactions are continually changing?" is a union question, not a yes/no partition. The function reading is the ground and must be taken per function and per site; the species and interactions are the habitat and update. Preservation, where it occurs, is carried by an arrangement (a functional group whose presence fixes the outcome, with response diversity among its members) rather than by a standing list of named species. Members can turn over while the group and its bonds hold. No single lever settles the union, richness is a budget filter rather than a witness, and in managed systems introduction is available while exclusion is not, so the work is permanent. Which species or trait actually pays for a named function at a named site is not returned by the framework; that is a declared gap for a source-discriminating measurement.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal plus ecology-facing; checkable by reading Fig 11 and Fig 12

- **Statement:** no standing list of good actors survives a change of residual; preservation is carried by a functional group whose members can be exchanged, not by the permanence of particular names.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49 *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :51 *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* Fig 12 :123 *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Fig 12 :94 *"The arrangement is the substance."*
- **What to check:** is there a documented case where a named species or fixed list preserved a function across several residual changes at several sites, with no functional-group substitution? That would be a standing list and would break the claim. Separately, is there a case where function persisted through turnover with no repeated functional group and no repeated bond structure, and no stable dominant or external subsidy explained it? That would break the arrangement claim.
- **Refuter:** either of those cases, verified against the primary data rather than a review.

### C2 — measurement-facing; checkable by an ecosystem-function researcher against their own trials

- **Statement:** the verdict is per function and per site; a pooled multifunctionality score can hide a function that fails. Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 10 :26 *"saturated metrics, pooled readings"*
- **What to check:** in a turnover series the researcher knows, did any single function fail while the pooled score stayed in band? Did the per-function instrument saturate, so that R stayed near zero while the function moved? If the pooled and per-function verdicts never diverge across a full turnover series, this sub-claim is not doing work.
- **Refuter:** a series in which pooled and every per-function verdict agree through a documented function failure, with a responsive (unsaturated) instrument throughout.

### C3 — framework-internal derivation about the threshold and the union

- **Statement:** "preserved" is a comparison against a threshold at one level (Fig 12 :87 *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*), and the mechanisms form a union of overlapping conditions (Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*; Fig 9 :57 *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*). Richness is a filter (Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*).
- **What to check:** does one intervention restore function where two or more conditions were missing, leaving no categorically different residue? Does species count alone predict the per-function verdict across communities with different functional-group compositions? A yes to either breaks C3.
- **Refuter:** a single lever that clears a two-condition intersection with no residue, or a count-only predictor that transfers across compositions.
- **Type question, named and not hidden:** ⋛ is defined as a threshold relation between two quantities; here the two quantities are the same function read now and the band set earlier. That holds at one level. Applying ⋛ to a flux and a species count would be a type error. If the comparison is reconstructed at a different level, the claim must be re-censused.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a universal yes, and not a universal no. Verdicts are per function and per site, kept separate and never averaged.
- Not a standing list of important or good species. Fig 11 :51 refuses it.
- Not that any named species, trait, or functional group in the reader's system is the preserver. That is the declared `gap`; it needs a site measurement.
- Not that species are interchangeable in general. They are exchangeable only at the level of a named functional group and a named function; see `06` Q1 for the distinction between a functional group and a strict isomer.
- Not that richness is irrelevant. It is a legitimate filter for where to look. It is not a witness for the verdict.
- Not that a single introduction makes a system safe. Managed habitat work is asymmetric and permanent (Fig 11 :61-63).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by an experiment, a dataset, or a field reading taken for this packet.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. Concretely:

1. **Function discriminator:** does the per-function reading stay in band through a documented identity turnover while a same-richness comparator with different functional groups fails?
2. **Source discriminator:** does a response-trait and interaction measurement identify which member actually paid for the function in each interval, rather than inferring the source from the flux alone?
3. **Anchor test:** does the verdict change when "important" species are reclassified but the measured flux does not (Fig 2 :30)?
4. **Aggregation test:** do pooled and per-function verdicts agree through a function failure, with unsaturated instruments?
5. **Standing-list test:** is there a universal list of named species that preserves a function across residuals and sites?
