## 2. Wider literature actually read

### The insurance hypothesis and its mechanism conditions

**Yachi & Loreau, 1999, PNAS** (`https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC15485`, abstract and model text opened). Quote: *"biodiversity insures ecosystems against declines in their functioning because many species provide greater guarantees that some will maintain functioning even if others fail."* Also: *"the greater the variance of the species responses, the lower the species richness at which the temporal mean of the ecosystem process saturates and the ecosystem becomes redundant."* Prediction here: preservation is a property of the distribution of responses, not of the count alone. **Corroborates F4 and F9** from within ecology; same broad evidence pool as the other BEF sources, so it is one witness.

**Loreau et al., 2021, Biological Reviews** (`https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8519139`, full text opened). Quote: *"In cybernetics, Ashby (1958) postulated that the regulation of a cybernetic system requires that it has a large enough variety of responses to counter disturbances."* Also: *"ecosystem functioning is stabilised if and only if the ecosystem contains species or phenotypes that fluctuate asynchronously."* Prediction: asynchronous response variety, not richness per se, is the condition. **Corroborates F4 and F9**, and supplies the bridge to the low-overlap cybernetics source. It also notes a mean-versus-variability trade-off, which supports keeping verdicts per function rather than averaging (F2).

### Turnover with identities changing

**Allan et al., 2011, PNAS** (`https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3193239`, abstract and Results opened). Quote: *"turnover in species promotes consistently high ecosystem function when it sustains functionally complementary interactions between species."* Also: *"species that were rare in some years became dominant and drove function in other years."* Prediction: the identity of the dominant changes while function stays high, but only where functional groups are complementary. This is the closest empirical analogue to the question as worded. **Corroborates F3, F4 and F5**. Note the conditional: turnover in functionally similar species did **not** maintain production. That is the world source most likely to be misread as "diversity always wins"; the figure-derived shape predicts the conditional, not the blanket claim.

**Elmqvist et al., 2003, Frontiers in Ecology and the Environment** (Wiley abstract page blocked direct automated fetch with 403; the abstract was returned in full by the public search index and is recorded with reduced weight). Quote: *"The diversity of responses to environmental change among species contributing to the same ecosystem function, which we call response diversity, is critical to resilience."* Prediction: response diversity inside a functional group is the preserving thing. **Corroborates F4.**

### Why function must stay per slot

**Eisenhauer, Hines, Maestre & Rillig, 2023, npj Biodiversity** (`https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11332098`, full text opened). Quote: *"the question 'redundancy for what?' should always be asked"*. Also: *"many species are needed to maintain multiple functions at multiple times and places in a changing world."* Prediction: a species that looks exchangeable for one function under one condition is not exchangeable across functions, times and places. **Corroborates F2 and F10** directly, and it is a useful warning about our own vocabulary: the figures' functional group (Fig 12 :123) is an arrangement kind, not a licence to call members expendable.

**Mori et al., 2013, Biological Reviews** (Wiley abstract returned by the public search index; direct full-text fetch was not attempted beyond the abstract). Quote: *"traditional diversity measures may often be uncoupled from measures (such as response diversity) that may be more effective proxies for ecosystem stability and resilience."* Prediction: richness can fail to track the mechanism. **Corroborates F9.**

**The Ecological Interactome, 2026, Annual Review of Ecology, Evolution, and Systematics** (publisher page returned 403 on direct fetch; abstract returned by the public search index and recorded with reduced weight). Quote: *"Interactions, governed by species traits, phylogeny, and ecological context, play a stronger role than species identity in maintaining ecosystem functionality."* Also: *"interaction extinctions often precede species loss."* Prediction: the bond goes before the actor list. **Corroborates F3 and F4** from a 2026 review, but it is still ecology, so it is the same broad pool.

### Low-overlap fields (independent witnesses for the mechanism shape)

**Ashby, 1956/1958, cybernetics**, via `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_(cybernetics)` (page opened in full). Quote: *"only variety can destroy variety"*, the law of requisite variety. Also, on the observer: *"the observer and his powers of discrimination may have to be specified if the variety is to be well defined."* Prediction: a regulator must hold enough distinguishable responses to counter the disturbance set, and the measurement must be defined relative to an observer's discrimination. **Corroborates F4 (response variety) and F7 (instrument discrimination)** from a genuinely different field, with no shared evidence pool with the grassland literature. It is a shape analogue, not an ecological measurement.

**N-version programming, software engineering**, via `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N-version_programming` (page opened in full). Quote: *"multiple functionally equivalent programs are independently generated from the same initial specifications"*, with the conjecture that *"the independence of programming efforts will greatly reduce the probability of identical software faults occurring in two or more versions of the program"*. Prediction: functionally equivalent members with *independent* failure modes preserve the service when one fails. The page also records the failure case: Knight and Leveson's 1986 experiment found that *"the assumption of independence of failures in N-version programs failed statistically."* **Corroborates F4 and F11**: the analogue supports the shape only where independence is real, and it independently demonstrates the shared-pool failure that Fig 2 :40 warns about.

## 3. Overlap audit

The BEF sources (Yachi and Loreau 1999, Loreau et al. 2021, Allan et al. 2011, Elmqvist et al. 2003, Eisenhauer et al. 2023, Mori et al. 2013, the 2026 interactome review) are **one evidence pool**, not seven witnesses: they cite the same Jena, Cedar Creek and BioDIV experiments and one another. Their agreement with the figure-derived shape is internal to ecology. The two genuinely low-overlap witnesses are Ashby's requisite variety (cybernetics) and N-version programming (engineering). Neither proves an ecological fact; both corroborate the *shape* that preservation is carried by an arrangement of independently varying members. The framework essays share the figure author's pool completely and corroborate nothing independently.

No world source conflicts with the figure verdict. The conflict rule (return to the domain census, never flip the verdict) was not triggered. The one apparent conflict, that reviews report a saturating biodiversity-function curve while long-term experiments show increasing effects, was returned to the census and resolved as two different objects: a single-function snapshot versus multiple functions over time and places (Eisenhauer et al. 2023). It is kept as evidence for the union reading, not smoothed over.

## 4. Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22-25** — *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down. A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Sending the full framework before anyone has described a residual sends a recording. Lead with the shape and its refuter; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** — *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** — *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape were true before giving them the article.
- **Fig 2 :40** — *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; and **Fig 2 :42** — *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* A list selected for receptiveness produces a convention, not corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its five refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and what function they would measure it on; (3) record their verdict; (4) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## 5. The two-stage offer, plus stage 3

1. **Stage 1:** validate the output against the measured functions the reader knows. This is Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"*, and Fig 2 :45, *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if curious, read the theory that produced it. The stage-1 verdict must be recorded first; later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method. Fig 4 :22 permits this because a generator can be given away without giving away an answer. Each user must write down what would refute their own result before running it, because the Formula's "Direction" note says a frame cannot check the direction of its own question.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 work only if the stage-1 shape survives. If a refuter in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` fires, the affected finding is withdrawn and the shape is re-censused before anything is sent.
