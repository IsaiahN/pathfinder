## 0. The residual in effect terms

The question asks whether a quantity stays put while the things that produce it change. Stated as an effect:

> A function reading f_j at site j and time t (primary production, nutrient retention, decomposition, pollination) is the quantity that must stay within a band. The actor set S(t) (species identities) and the relation set B(t) (pollination, predation, competition, dependency) both change over t. The residual is R_j(t) = |predicted f_j(t) from (S, B) at t-1 minus observed f_j(t)|, indexed per function j and per site, never pooled.

Three consequences of stating it this way, all of which drive the census:

1. **The function reading is a candidate ground.** The question is constitutively about whether function is preserved. Fig 2 :36: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* If the function reading moved when ecologists changed their minds about which species count as "good", it would not be an anchor (Fig 2 :30: *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."*).
2. **The actors and relations are habitat, not ground.** Fig 11 :73: *"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."* Species identities and interactions are exactly the things the question says are "continually changing", so they belong to the habitat side of that line. The category error to avoid is treating the species list as the answer.
3. **"Biodiversity" is not yet a domain.** It arrives in the question as a single word. Fig 9 :27-28 requires that the residual be described in effect terms before a frame is chosen: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* A species count is a spectrum reading (Fig 13 :20: *"ordering, so more and less can be said"*), and as a claimed cause it is a filter, not a witness (Fig 9 :60). It is re-census of the residual, not an actors/currency/anchor domain, until a mechanism is named.

## 1. Domain census

### R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity.** Multi-slot and multi-place. R is indexed by function j, site k, and time t. At minimum three things covary: the function reading, the actor set, and the relation set. It is therefore a candidate **union** of overlapping conditions, not a two-place partition (Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*).
- **Symmetry.** "Preserved" is a comparison against a threshold, an ordered ⋛ relation, not a symmetric join (Fig 12 :87: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*). "Continually changing" is an ordered succession, → rather than + (Fig 12 :30: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*). Swapping the time order of identity loss and function failure changes the question, so the bond is ordered.
- **Scale.** The loop runs at one scale for one function-slot (organisms and their immediate relations composing into a measured flux). Turnover is a time-channel effect, and the move from a local plot to a landscape is a membrane crossing with a lossy transform (Fig 4 :27: *"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."*). The verdict at one scale cannot be averaged into the verdict at another.

### Partition or union

Union, not partition. Preservation can come from more than one overlapping condition: many species in one functional group (similarity), several functional groups with asynchronous responses (complementarity), a particular dominant that happens to be stable in that interval (selection), and a spatial subsidy from outside the plot (migration). These overlap; a single lever that adds species clears the region where only one condition is missing and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :56: *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*). What remains after one lever is applied is *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"* (Fig 9 :57), so it must be unbundled and re-searched rather than averaged.

### Dependency order (Fig 3)

1. **Perception:** can the function be read per slot and told apart from the reading at the previous time (Fig 3 :5)? Without this, no verdict is possible.
2. **Vocabulary:** are there terms for what a species affords to a function (what it can fix, consume, pollinate, block)? This is the link that usually has no instrument (Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*).
3. **Objective:** what counts as "preserved", a threshold stated in the system's own terms (Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison."*)?
4. **Planning:** which introduction or condition change is being proposed, under incomplete information about turnover?
5. **Learn and carry:** was the result settled against the ground and carried as a method, not as a recording of one good year (Fig 4 :23: *"upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."*)?

Ordering is by dependency, not importance (Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*). Effort spent improving a diversity metric at step 1 cannot rescue a missing affordance vocabulary at step 2.

## 3. Findings

### F1 — The function reading is the ground; species and interactions are the habitat (decisive for the census)

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."*
Fig 2 :36: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."*
Fig 2 :30: *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."*
Fig 11 :73: *"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."*

The question is constitutively about whether a function continues. A flux measurement, not a species list, settles it. The species list and the interaction web are the habitat, and the question itself says they update. Assigning the species list to the ground would make the answer move whenever taxonomies or trait assignments move, which is the mutual-update collapse of Fig 2 :33-34.

Mode: `general` for the anchor/habitat distinction, `specified` for the ecological binding.
**Refuter:** a case where the function verdict changes solely because the set of species counted as "functionally important" is revised, with no change in the measured flux. That would mean the anchor moved.

### F2 — The verdict is per function-slot; an aggregate average throws away the signal

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
Fig 1 :28: *"The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."*
Fig 10 :26: *"saturated metrics, pooled readings"*

"Ecosystem function" in the singular is the aggregate that hides the result. A community can hold biomass steady while nutrient retention collapses, and a diversity index averaged over both functions will report preservation. Fig 1 :23: *"The ground enters as the only metric."* The metric is the per-function reading, and there is no single metric called "the ecosystem".

Mode: `specified`.
**Refuter:** a demonstrated case in which the verdict from a pooled multifunctionality index and the verdict from every per-function reading agree across all turnover events, including events where one function fails. If they never diverge, per-slot indexing is unjustified here.

### F3 — Changing identities refuse a standing list of good species

Fig 11 :49: *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
Fig 11 :50: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."*
Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*

The phrase "species identities are continually changing" is exactly the condition under which Fig 11 :51 fires. A claim of the form "biodiversity preserves function because species X, Y and Z are present" is a standing list, and it is structurally disqualified. Interactions change their sign with the residual, so "pollinator" and "pest" are relations in this census, not permanent kinds of species.

Mode: `specified`. Triangulated across Fig 11 (habitat valence) and Fig 12 :123 (functional group as arrangement kind). Low overlap between the two figures.
**Refuter:** a universal list of named species that preserves a given function under every residual change, demonstrated across sites rather than asserted from one experiment.

### F4 — Preservation is carried by the bond and the functional group, not by the actor list

Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
Fig 12 :46: *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."*
Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."*
Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
Fig 11 :50: *"mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds"*

This is the constructive half of F3. If function is fixed by a functional group, then the *member* filling the slot can change while the *kind* stays. Fig 12 :123 is almost a direct statement of the ecological result: the group says what kind (pollinator, decomposer, nitrogen fixer), the qualifier says which member (this bee, that fungus). A change of identity that keeps the group and the bond intact is not a change in the thing that pays for the function.

Mode: `specified`. Triangulated: Fig 12 (bond/arrangement) plus Fig 11 (valence relative to residual).
**Refuter:** a measured case where function persists through turnover with no repeated functional group and no repeated bond structure, and where the persistence cannot be attributed to a stable dominant or an external subsidy. That would break the bond claim.

### F5 — The question is a union of overlapping conditions

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
Fig 9 :56: *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*
Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

The mechanisms that can preserve function under turnover are similarity within a functional group, complementarity between groups, asynchrony of response, selection of a temporarily dominant performer, and spatial rescue. They overlap. A single intervention (add species) clears only the cases where similarity was the missing condition; where complementarity is also missing it leaves the intersection standing. This is why one universal "biodiversity preserves function" verdict cannot be returned. Verdicts must be kept per domain and never averaged.

Mode: `general` for the union structure, `specified` for the ecological mechanisms.
**Refuter:** a single lever that restores function under turnover in cases where two or more of the conditions were absent, with no categorically different residue left behind.

### F6 — In a managed system this is permanent work; introduction is available, exclusion is not

Fig 11 :56: *"Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept."*
Fig 11 :58: *"Maintained. A seat exists and can act: choose the instruments, distil the impactful actors, introduce what was not there."*
Fig 11 :61: *"The seat’s power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."*
Fig 11 :62: *"You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."*
Fig 11 :63: *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."*
Fig 11 :65: *"Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994. Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated."*

The restoration version of the question ("can we add diversity to keep function going?") is a maintained-habitat question. The seat can introduce but cannot subtract the habitat, and the introduced actor acts through a cascade that must be enumerated first (Fig 11 :44). A one-off introduction is therefore not a solution state; it is the start of permanent work. The engineering analogue (independently developed functionally equivalent versions, `04_links_and_distribution.md`) corroborates the shape from a low-overlap field but also shows the failure mode when the versions are not independent.

Mode: `specified`. Triangulated across Fig 11 (asymmetry) and Fig 10 :24 (channel maintenance), two figures with different objects.
**Refuter:** a single introduction that preserves a function across all subsequent turnover events at a site with no further maintenance and with no unenumerated cascade.

### F7 — The ground does not decay; the measurement channel does

Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*
Fig 10 :26: *"saturated metrics, pooled readings"*
Fig 10 :28: *"you have lost touch"*
Fig 10 :29: *"→ keep the anchor reachable"*
Fig 2 :30: *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."*

A surprising number of "function was preserved" results are readings of a saturated metric. If the instrument cannot register decline until a threshold is crossed, R stays near zero while the function actually moves. This is a channel failure, not a ground result. The remedy is to keep the anchor reachable (sharpen the per-function instrument from a worse one already returning something, Fig 6 :32), not to buy independence in the form of another review of the same experiments.

Mode: `specified`.
**Refuter:** a measurement series in which the per-function instrument remains responsive across the full range of turnover events and the verdict is unchanged. That would show the channel was not the limiting condition.

### F8 — Contact cascades; direct contact is not the whole habitat

Fig 11 :44: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."*
Fig 11 :48: *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."*
Fig 11 :52: *"And contact cascades."*
Fig 11 :54: *"Carson, Silent Spring, 1962. The mechanism, biomagnification through the food chain to eggshell thinning, was established over the following decade."*

A function can be lost through a second- or third-order effect that a first-order species list never shows. The figure's own instance is DDT and the eagles (Fig 11 :52-54). In ecosystem function terms, a pollinator decline can be driven by a herbicide that affects a plant two links away, and a nutrient retention failure can appear a season after the species change. Scoping the habitat to direct interactors is how the residual that matters is missed.

Mode: `specified`.
**Refuter:** a case where first-order enumeration of direct interactors predicts every function failure through a full turnover series, with no second-order effect detected.

### F9 — A diversity count is a filter for the budget, never a witness for the verdict

Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*
Fig 2 :39: *"Correlation has valence."*
Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*

Species richness tells you how much search budget was spent; it does not tell you what the arrangement was. Two communities with the same count can differ completely in functional groups and response traits, and (from F4) it is the arrangement that pays for the function. A count can be used to decide where to look first, which is its legitimate filter role. It cannot be the witness that closes the residual. The same applies to the literature: five reviews of the same grassland experiments are one witness wearing five names.

Mode: `general`.
**Refuter:** a demonstrated mapping from species count alone to per-function preservation under turnover, with the same count predicting the same verdict across different functional-group compositions.

### F10 — Which species or trait preserves which function is a declared `gap`, not a hidden answer

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*
Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*
Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*
Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

After a genuine re-census, no figure line governs the identity of the preserving species or trait. The figures return the shape (a functional group with response diversity, a non-updating function ground, a union of conditions) and stop. They do not name a species, and naming one would require a source-discriminating instrument at the site, which is a ground reading this derivation did not take. This is `unreached`, not `unreachable in principle`: the missing thing is an instrument, not a new kind.

Mode: `general` for the limit, `gap` for the object.
**Refuter:** a figure line that names a species or a trait as the preserver of a function. None was found; if one is produced, this gap closes.

### F11 — Independence is the parameter that decides whether the corroboration means anything

Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."*
Fig 8 :48: *"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."*
Fig 8 :49: *"Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."*
Fig 10 :80: *"Frames whose independence is renewed by separate experience can be maintained"*

The biodiversity and ecosystem function literature is dominated by a small set of grassland experiments (the Jena Experiment, Cedar Creek, the BioCON and BioDIV sites). Reviews that cite one another and the same primary trials are one evidence pool. To get a real second witness for the mechanism shape we have to go to fields that did not draw on those experiments: software fault tolerance (N-version programming) and cybernetics (Ashby's requisite variety). Those sources are opened in `10_source_ledger.csv` and declared low overlap. Their agreement corroborates the *shape*, not the ecological fact.

Mode: `general`.
**Refuter:** a genuinely low-overlap source whose mechanism contradicts the shape (for example, a fault-tolerance result showing that identical redundancy is as reliable as diverse redundancy across all disturbance profiles).

### F12 — What the framework returns is a shape with a refuter, not the ecological answer

Fig 5 :62: *"A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term."*
Fig 5 :65: *"Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."*
Fig 9 :53: *"Nobody chooses. The gap does, and the gap is only as good as its description."*
Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*

The sought-for answer (a list of the species that would preserve function in a given place) is a preference, not a term the framework holds. Binding the shape before the run makes it refusable. This is why the pre-registered refuter in section 6 is written before any conclusion, and why the outreach packet leads with the shape and its failure conditions.

Mode: `general`.
**Refuter:** a demonstration that the shape was fitted to the ecological result after the result was known, with no prior statement of what would have refuted it.

## 4. Strain test

Two potential strains were found and resolved by re-census; one live type question remains.

- **S1, apparent conflict between Fig 11 :51 (no standing list of good actors) and Fig 12 :123 (a functional group fixes the outcome).** Re-census: Fig 11 :49-51 governs *named actors* and their valence relative to a residual; Fig 12 :123 governs *arrangement kinds*. The first forbids a permanent list of names; the second permits a stable kind with exchangeable members. Different objects, no strain. The correction is kept visible in section 8.
- **S2, apparent conflict between Fig 11 :40 (an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing) and the idea that adding species helps.** Re-census: Fig 11 :40 is about an agent's capability, which is agent-and-habitat (Fig 11 :39). Here the "agent" is the community and the function is the ground. Adding a species that performs an already-occupied role changes no contact and changes nothing; adding one with a new response trait changes contact with the fluctuating environment and can change the reading. The figure is not strained; it discriminates the two cases.
- **S3, live type question: is "preserved" a ⋛ comparison between two quantities at the same level?** Fig 12 :87 defines ⋛ as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*. Here the comparison is between a measured flux now and a band established earlier. Both are readings of the same quantity at different times, so the type holds at this level. If the comparison were instead between a flux and a species count, it would be a type error. This is named, not hidden, and is the discriminator in the pre-registered refuter.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Required before any outreach, per the framework's own rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Standing list fires.** A universal list of named species preserves a given function across residual changes at multiple sites (breaks F3, Fig 11 :51).
2. **Bond claim fires.** Function persists through turnover with no repeated functional group and no repeated bond structure, and cannot be attributed to a stable dominant or an external subsidy (breaks F4, Fig 12 :123).
3. **Anchor fires.** The function verdict changes when the species considered "important" are reclassified, with no change in the measured flux (breaks F1, Fig 2 :30).
4. **Aggregation fires.** Pooled multifunctionality verdicts and every per-function verdict agree across all turnover events, including a function failure (breaks F2, Fig 1 :22).
5. **Union fires.** A single lever restores function where two or more missing conditions were present and leaves no categorically different residue (breaks F5, Fig 9 :57).
6. **Namespace fires.** Fig 9 :32 is shown to govern already-held bindings rather than missing primitives in a way that forbids the functional-group reading (would narrow F10).

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the shape is re-censused. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Lead with F1 and F4: the function reading is the ground, the arrangement carries preservation. These are the figure-derived core.
2. State the mode on every claim and keep the `gap` on species identity explicit (F10). Do not smuggle in a named species from the world literature as if the framework produced it.
3. Treat "biodiversity" as a filter until the affordance vocabulary exists (F9 and the Fig 3 break at link 2). A richness number is where to look, not what closes the question.
4. Keep verdicts per function and per site. Never return an averaged "biodiversity is good for ecosystem function" verdict (F2, F5).
5. If the question is the managed version, enumerate the cascade before any introduction and say plainly that it is permanent work (F6, F8).
6. Buy independence in low-overlap fields, not in more reviews of the same grassland experiments (F11).
7. Send the generator, the shape and its refuter. Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*

### Verdict of the audit

The shape survives. Ecosystem function can be preserved while identities and interactions change, but only as a per-function verdict over a union of overlapping conditions, carried by an arrangement (functional group plus response diversity) rather than by a standing list of species, and only while the channel to a non-updating function measurement stays reachable. Managed preservation is permanent work. Which species or trait preserves which function in a given place is a declared `gap`, to be closed by a ground reading, not by another review. The whole is `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.
