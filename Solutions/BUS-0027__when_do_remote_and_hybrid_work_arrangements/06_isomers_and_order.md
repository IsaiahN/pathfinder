## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the two outcomes the same quantity at different levels?

- Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
- A focus outcome and a learning outcome are measured on different slots, even when the same person and the same afternoon are involved. They are not a high value and a low value of one variable; they are two residuals with different anchors (output counter versus demonstrated transfer).
- Fig 13 :35-36: "Space, time and energy are not three readings of one quantity. Neither are spectrum, gradient and network. Each answers a question the other two cannot." That line governs the six physical primitives, not work arrangements (strain S1 in `01`), so it is used here only as the general form of the independence rule, not as a mapping.
- **Result:** no, they are not one quantity. Verdict `general` for the indexing rule; `specified` for the claim that the two workplace residuals are independent enough to diverge (Fig 1 plus F1 and F2 of `01`).

### Q2 - Are they isomers under Fig 12?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- The two workplace outcomes as commonly written share the atom set {worker, task, time span, organization Γ}, but differ in which relations are bonded: a focus slot bonds worker-to-task through a channel to an output counter; a learning slot bonds newcomer-to-expert through observation and correction to a demonstrated competence. Same atoms, different bond, different behaviour. By Fig 12's definition they are **isomers** when written over that shared set.
- If the record omits the channel and the expert from the atom set, the two share only "worker" and "time", which makes them a **functional group** (a `state → relation → ground` pattern) with different members, not isomers. This is the weaker, safer reading.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either outcome can be fully written without an atom the other requires (for example, if learning competence can be demonstrated with no expert relation, or output with no ground counter), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands.

### Q3 - What is the order of the union?

- Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- The two conditions are C1 (channel to ground maintained) and C2 (habitat reproduced and reach renewed). They are ordered by dependency, not importance (Fig 3 :20). C1 comes first because C2's instrument is itself a reading: you cannot tell whether a new relation entered the habitat unless the channel to the ground still returns a faithful observation. But C1 is not sufficient: a perfect metric on the wrong habitat reports the loss only after the goal fails (Fig 11 :68).
- Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Adding a better dashboard (above the break) cannot rescue a team that has no terms for what its new habitat affords (the middle link).
- **Falsifier:** a single intervention that restores both conditions in both failure types and leaves no categorically different residue would collapse the union into a partition. If that happens, the order claim is wrong.

### Q4 - What kind of analogue is isolation?

- Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."
- Fig 11 :67: "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat’s actors, which you did not choose."
- Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."
- Moving work home is not removing co-presence; it is exchanging one habitat for another. The useful analogue is quarantine or a test harness, not "less of the same". Fig 11 :70: "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability." A remote pilot that proves the laptops and the VPN work proves wiring, not that the learning habitat was reproduced.
- **Falsifier:** a substituted habitat that preserves every relation the goal required and introduces no new unbidden actor would break the isolation law's consequence. That would mean the analogue is misleading here.

### Q5 - Which structure explains hybrid being worse than either pure mode?

- Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
- Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."
- A shared channel is a catalyst: it regenerates across uses when team members co-attend, and it is spent when office presence varies so much that no one knows who is reachable. Nothing in a hybrid policy records whether the channel is catalyst or spent part; the behaviour differs across uses. Gibbs et al. 2024 measured precisely this: the penalty was concentrated in teams with high variation in office presence. That is `specified`, single-source for the workplace claim, triangulated for the structure (Fig 12 and Fig 7 :36).

## CONCLUSION

The two outcomes are not one quantity. Over the shared atom set they are Fig 12 isomers, and in the safer record they are a functional group with different members. The union of C1 and C2 is ordered by dependency (C1 first, but not sufficient), and a single lever that cleared both with no residue would falsify the union reading. Isolation is habitat substitution, so the valid analogue is a substituted habitat or a test harness, not a subtraction of co-presence. The hybrid penalty is a spent-catalyst failure: a channel that does not regenerate because its users are scattered. All of this is `specified` and ungrounded here.
