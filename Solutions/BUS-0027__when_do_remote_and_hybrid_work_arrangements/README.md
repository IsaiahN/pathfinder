## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree that remote work is good or bad.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **A room does not upgrade the label.** Fig 7 :13 says "agreement compounds, evidence does not". Adding more people who already like or dislike remote work raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor (Fig 2 :29).
2. **Selecting for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation.** Fig 2 :40 says "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". A list chosen for people likely to agree with our verdict is a list chosen to agree.
3. **Asking for trust before checking spends independence.** Fig 10 :42 says "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, never the framework interior.

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR/CAN-SPAM-style rules in many jurisdictions. The venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv` are public, opt-in, and institutional.
