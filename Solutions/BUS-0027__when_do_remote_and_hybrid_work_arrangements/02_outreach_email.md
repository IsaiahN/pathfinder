**Subject:** Check one output on remote work before you decide whether the framing is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a small, checkable claim about when remote and hybrid work helps and when it hurts. I am not asking you to agree with it, and I am not asking you to read any theory first. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the effect of a work arrangement is indexed by the slot being measured, not by the organization as a whole. The framework's first figure says "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated" and "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." On an independent, countable task, moving work home can shrink the gap between what the routine predicts and what actually happens: more minutes, a quieter environment, fewer sick days. On a slot whose goal requires tacit transfer, observation of an expert, or a new bridge between groups, the same move can grow that gap, because isolation substitutes one habitat for another and "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails" (Fig 11 :68). A single firm-wide "productivity" number hides both signals.
>
> **What this predicts:** hybrid arrangements should leave measured performance roughly unchanged while improving retention on experienced, graduate, team-based roles, and should weaken idea quality and cross-group bridges where teams are scattered. The first half is what Bloom, Han and Liang report in Nature (2024); the second is what Yang et al. report in Nature Human Behaviour (2022) and Gibbs et al. in Scientific Reports (2024). Those are their findings, not my derivation. I am asking you to check the shape, not to endorse the literature.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that there is one optimal number of office days; that remote work is good or bad in general; that employee satisfaction is a ground; or that a written procedure transfers a skill. The framework returns a shape, not a name. It says what an arrangement must preserve on each slot; it does not name a calendar rule.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a remote-only team whose newcomers match a co-located control on a ground measure of competence while the required mentoring and observation are documented as absent;
> 2. a hybrid team that gains new cross-team bridges over two years with no new contact from a differing closure;
> 3. a measured productivity gain that survives replacing the management metric with an independent, per-slot ground counter.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>

> - Canonical usage rule: `https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md`
