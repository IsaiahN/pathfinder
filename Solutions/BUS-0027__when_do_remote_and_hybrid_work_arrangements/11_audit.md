## Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual line before relying on the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the divergence of outcomes under one arrangement across slots. Arity: four-place, arrangement / slot / Γ / realized outcome, ordered in time. Symmetry: not symmetric; "improves" is a threshold against a baseline. Scale: individual loop, team population, organization across quarters. Fig 1 :19 makes it a per-slot quantity. Union, not partition: the two conditions have different instruments, so a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either (Fig 9 :57).
- **Domain 1, focus loop.** Actors: worker, Γ, instruments. Currency: output per unit time. Anchor: a realized output counter. Question type: can the slot be counted at all.
- **Domain 2, team habitat.** Actors: teammates, newcomers, mentors, cross-group peers. Currency: crossed knowledge. Anchor: demonstrated competence. Question type: habitat substitution (Fig 11 :66).
- **Domain 3, channel.** Actors: metrics, customer contact, percepts. Currency: contact fidelity. Anchor: the ground, which does not decay while the channel does (Fig 10 :24). Question type: instrument discrimination.
- **Domain 4, closure.** Actors: the team's library and other frames. Currency: reach. Anchor: ground settlement of a new term. Question type: import (Fig 6 :23).
- **Excluded:** culture without a behavioural measure (no anchor); a single productivity scalar (conflates D1 and D3).

Order by dependency: C1 (channel) before C2 (habitat and reach), refuse before mint (Formula step 2), describe the residual before naming a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).

My census matches the primary's four domains and its union reading. Where the primary derived from Fig 11 (agent-and-habitat) and Fig 4 (membrane), I derived from Fig 9's union/partition split and Fig 10's channel law. ## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :19, :22 (per-slot), Fig 11 :39-40 and :66-69 (habitat and isolation), Fig 10 :24-26 (channel), Fig 7 :36 (rooms).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07` and `09`: effects are per slot; a mode changes a habitat; learning weakens when the substituted habitat drops required relations; hybrid concentrates cost when scattered.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the concrete policy reading that someone could take away: give everyone three office days and two home days and the problem is solved.

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **per-slot indexing** is lost in the policy reading, which treats one schedule as optimal for every team;
- the **habitat census** is lost, so no one enumerates which relations the goal required before the move;
- the **ground discriminator** is lost, so the metric replaces the count and a saturated reading looks like a win.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a work mode changes contact, and the learning cost is silent when the required relations are not reproduced. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists here; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the formula as identical to the full figure set would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a recorded gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` states.
