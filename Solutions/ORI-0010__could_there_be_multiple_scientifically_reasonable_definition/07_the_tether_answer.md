# The Tether's answer: plural definitions of life

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on research-practice predictions, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a measurement. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The disagreement about what counts as life does not shrink with effort, and each candidate works well on a different subset. The question asks whether that plurality is a defect to be eliminated or a legitimate feature of a science. Option A (one true definition) demands a single term range over viruses, mules, RNA-world replicators, synthetic cells and hypothetical extraterrestrial life. Option B (a guarded plurality) keeps each definition scoped to a question and keeps the empirical anchor separate.

## 2. The shape

1. **Split, do not search.** Fig 9 :4: "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset," Fig 9 :5: "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." The disagreement is a union of overlapping conditions, not one hard question (Fig 9 :54-55).

2. **A definition is a convention, not a verdict.** Fig 10 :46: "A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do. The seat may author what has no truth value and nothing that does." Fig 10 :18: "a declared meaning is not a grounded one". Several scoped definitions therefore do not contradict one another. World corroboration: Benner (2010) "Any definition is intricately connected to a theory that gives it meaning"; Schoenmakers (2023) definitions "are not strictly, but rather loosely descriptive" and "Their purpose is not to be true, but to be useful to scientists".

3. **The guard is load-bearing.** Fig 2 :43: "Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one". A plurality becomes a scientific error at the moment one definition is used to settle an empirical origin question without an independent ground.

4. **Anchor by question, not by definition.** Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when" Fig 2 :30: "you push on it, it is not an anchor." For empirical origin questions the anchor is experiment and observation. For conventional questions there is no ground, and agreement about a shared meaning is the product of the convention, not corroboration (Fig 2 :41).

5. **Agreement is not evidence of truth.** Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Consensus among researchers reading the same literature is one pool talking. Do not rank people by receptiveness and do not pool later agreement as corroboration.

6. **Each definition must pay.** Fig 5 :43: "A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before. A term that explains everything by saying nothing" Fig 5 :44: "fails this, and so does a term that explains one occasion perfectly." The vacuous and the ad hoc definitions fail; a scoped one can pay.

7. **No last definition.** Fig 7 :6: "the limit: no last room". Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Every definition is a room that closes part of the gap and hands a residual to the next.

## 3. The four-slot template

A scientifically reasonable definition of life, under this shape, states:

1. **the question it is for** (origin detection, lab synthesis, biosignature search, ethics, education);
2. **the actors and scale it ranges over** (molecules, individuals, lineages, ecosystems);
3. **the ground that could settle that question**, or an explicit statement that the question is conventional;
4. **the guard** that stops it deciding an outcome it was not authored to decide.

A definition missing slot 3 or slot 4 is not reasonable in this sense, however popular it is.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that every definition is equally good. Fig 5 :43-44 excludes the vacuous and the one-case-only definition.
- Not that "life" has no referent or that biology should drop the term. The question is about definitions, not about whether living things exist.
- Not which definition any particular field should adopt. That is a convention its community authors.
- Not that consensus makes a definition true (Fig 2 :40), and not that any definition is settled by the ground.
- Not grounded. No measurement in this derivation distinguishes two definitions.

## 5. What would settle it, and the honest stop

The shape is settled by the figures, not by experiment, and is therefore `general`. The research-practice predictions are `specified` and could be checked: do communities that use scoped definitions actually make progress, and do communities that mandate one definition actually lose the union surplus (Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone.")? Those are ground questions for a historian or sociologist of science, not for this derivation.

The stop is at Fig 9 :32: a kind of thing, never the answer itself. Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." The verdict here is not that no single definition exists; it is that the right move for this residual is to split and guard, and that a final definition would be a last room. "Indiscernibility is not identity." Record the route and stop.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not pick a winner in the definitional pluralism of life. It says that the disagreement is a union of well-formed sub-questions, because it does not shrink with effort and each definition works on a different subset, so the move is to split rather than search. A definition is then a convention that makes no claim about the world, which is why several can coexist without contradicting one another, provided each states the question it is for, the scale it ranges over, the ground that could settle that question, and a guard against deciding an outcome on its own. On empirical origin questions the anchor is the experiment, not the definition; on conventional questions agreement about meaning is the product of the convention, not evidence that one definition is true. Vacuous and one-off definitions fail the same bargain any new term must pay, and there is no last definition. What is offered is the shape any reasonable definition must have, three refuters written in advance, and the disciplines that would test the predictions. The slot stays open until a community authors it.
