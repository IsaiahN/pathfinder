## 0. The question under audit, stated in effect terms

> Could there be multiple scientifically reasonable definitions of life, each useful for a different question?

Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term before searching (Formula "Direction"). An answer would look like this: a set of definitions D1..Dn, each with (a) the question it is for, (b) the actors and scale it ranges over, (c) the ground that could settle that question, and (d) a guard that stops any one definition from deciding an outcome it was not authored to decide. If the answer is "no", there is exactly one definition D* that is correct for all questions, and all other candidates are errors.

What each leads to: Option A (plural, guarded) keeps the residual visible per question and lets each ground settle its own slot. Option B (single D*) requires one definition to range over all slots, including ones it cannot measure, and turns a convention into a verdict.

## 1. The residual

**R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The persistent disagreement about what counts as "life." Arity: it presents as one slot, "the definition of life", but its symptoms occupy several distinct subsets: self-replicating RNA, metabolic networks, membrane-bound protocells, viruses, sterile individuals such as mules, synthetic cells, and hypothetical extraterrestrial life. Symmetry: the candidate definitions are not interchangeable. A replicator definition fails on a mule; a metabolic definition fails on a virus outside a host; a cell-based definition fails on the first evolving RNA. Scale: molecular replicators, individual organisms, lineages, ecosystems and entire planets are all ranged over by different candidates.

**Question type.** Union, not partition. Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." The source file itself says the competing programs (RNA-first, metabolism-first, membrane/protocell-first) "are not always mutually exclusive." This is the signature of a union.

## 4. Findings

### F1 — The disagreement is a union of well-formed sub-questions, not one hard question (governing finding)

Fig 9 :4: "First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset,"
Fig 9 :5: "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."

Applied here: the disagreement about life has not shrunk after a century of work and hundreds of proposals, and each candidate keeps working well on a different subset. The Darwinian definition works for lineages and fails on a sterile individual; the metabolic definition works for a self-sustaining cell and fails on a virion; the cell-based definition works for extant life and conflicts with an RNA world. That is exactly the pattern Fig 9 :4-5 names. Fig 9 :55 adds that it is a union of overlapping conditions, which matches the source file's own note that the research programs are not mutually exclusive.

**Verdict (D1, D2): plural is legitimate.** Mode `general`, triangulated across Fig 9 :4-5 and Fig 9 :54-55 (two distinct clauses of the same figure, low overlap with the seat rule in F2).

**Refuter:** if the disagreement demonstrably shrinks with effort toward one definition that works on all subsets (viruses, mules, RNA-world replicators, synthetic cells, extraterrestrial life), the split is wrong and Option B stands.

### F2 — A definition is a convention, not a grounded meaning

Fig 10 :45: "It does author conventions, because nothing else can see across a seam."
Fig 10 :46: "A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do. The seat may author what has no truth value and nothing that does."
Fig 10 :18: "a declared meaning is not a grounded one"
Fig 10 :49: "Declaring a meaning does not ground it."

A working definition of life makes no claim about the world. It is a tool a community authors to make a seam statable. From this it follows that several such definitions do not contradict one another, because none of them asserts a world-state that another denies. They differ in what they are for.

This is also what the world literature reports independently. Benner (2010) writes "Any definition is intricately connected to a theory that gives it meaning." Schoenmakers (2023) says of pragmatic definitions that "Their purpose is not to be true, but to be useful to scientists by guiding scientific practice." These are secondary corroboration, not witnesses that outrank the figure.

**Verdict (D1): legitimate plurality, no contradiction to resolve.** Mode `general`, triangulated across Fig 10 :45-46, :18 and :49 (convention rule plus grounding rule).

**Refuter:** show a definition making a world-claim that the ground settles true or false (for example, a definition that entails a specific chemical history and is then confirmed or refuted by the rock record). If that happens, it is a verdict in a definition's clothing and this finding fails.

### F3 — The guard: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one

Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"
Fig 2 :43: "Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."

Plurality is fine until one definition is allowed to settle an empirical origin question by fiat. At that moment the definition becomes a frame that decides its own outcome, which Fig 2 forbids. The concrete failure: declaring "life begins at Darwinian evolution" and then concluding that chemical selection before that point was not a life process, without any independent ground for the boundary.

**Verdict (D1, D2): guard is load-bearing.** Mode `specified`, triangulated across Fig 10 :47 and Fig 2 :43 (the guard is restated in a second figure).

**Refuter:** exhibit a community that uses a definition to decide which systems were alive and also supplies an independent ground for the boundary. Then the convention has not decided the outcome and the guard has not fired.

### F4 — Correlation has two valences: consensus on a definition is not truth

Fig 2 :39: "Correlation has valence."
Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"
Fig 2 :41: "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"

This resolves an apparent objection to the whole answer. "If definitions are conventions, why does anyone care which one is used?" Because a shared convention is the product of coordination, and agreement there is efficient, not corrupt. But that same agreement is disqualifying if it is offered as evidence that one definition is the true one. Consensus among researchers who read the same literature is one evidence pool talking (Fig 2 :40).

**Verdict (D3): keep convention-agreement and verdict-evidence in separate columns.** Mode `general`, triangulated across Fig 2 :39-41 and Fig 2 :35 ("Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you.").

**Refuter:** show a case where consensus on a definition is legitimately used as evidence for an empirical claim about which chemical systems existed.

### F5 — Each definition pays for its own question (the MDL bargain)

Fig 5 :43: "A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before. A term that explains everything by saying nothing"
Fig 5 :44: "fails this, and so does a term that explains one occasion perfectly."

A definition is a term and pays the same bargain. "Life is anything that exhibits organisation" fails because it explains everything by saying nothing. "Life is exactly what happened in this one flask in 1953" fails because it explains one occasion perfectly. A definition scoped to a question (for example, "a self-sustaining chemical system capable of Darwinian evolution", for origin-of-life and biosignature work) can pay, because stating it plus its leftover residual costs less than the confusion it removes. Several definitions can each pay, one per question.

**Verdict (D1): multiple definitions can each be scientifically reasonable.** Mode `specified`, single-source for the figure rule (Fig 5), corroborated by the world literature on pragmatic definitions.

**Refuter:** a definition so broad it counts crystals and fire as alive, or so narrow it fits one experiment only, that is nevertheless retained by the community. That would show the bargain is not being applied.

### F6 — Low-overlap programs have a union surplus; forcing one definition destroys it

Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."
Fig 8 :29: "low overlap: B does not draw on A's evidence"

RNA-first, metabolism-first and protocell-first programs hold different closures and draw on different evidence (Fig 8 :29). Their union has a surplus (Fig 8 :45). A single mandated definition collapses the surplus by making the programs name the same thing. The guard in F3 is the price of keeping the surplus: the frames must stay separate, and their agreement must not be pooled as corroboration.

**Verdict (D1, D2): preserve the plurality.** Mode `specified`, single-source for the figure claim (Fig 8), with the world literature on discipline-specific definitions as secondary corroboration.

**Refuter:** show two candidate definitions drawing on identical evidence such that their union adds nothing. Then there is one frame with two names and F6 does not apply.

### F7 — No last definition

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"
Fig 7 :6: "the limit: no last room"
Fig 7 :30: "every room closes some of the gap;"
Fig 7 :31: "no finite number of them ever arrives"

The framework returns a shape, not a final name. Any definition that presents itself as the complete, terminal account of life is a last room, which the figures forbid. Each definition is a room that closes part of the gap and hands a residual to the next.

**Verdict (D1): the answer is a family of definitions with stated domains, not one final definition.** Mode `general`, triangulated across Fig 9 :32, Fig 7 :6 and Fig 7 :30-31.

**Refuter:** a definition that closes the residual for all sub-questions with no remainder, demonstrably and against the ground. That would be a last room and would break Fig 7.

### F8 — Apply the anchor test to each sub-question before naming a definition

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when"
Fig 2 :30: "you push on it, it is not an anchor."
Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."
Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."

For the empirical question "did a self-sustaining evolving chemical system arise here", the anchor is the experimental and geological record, which does not move when a researcher changes definitions. A definition that changes its answer when the frame changes is not an anchor and cannot settle that question. For the conventional question "what should our community mean by life in this paper", there is no ground to consult, and a declared meaning is legitimate precisely because the question is constitutively about the convention (Fig 2 :36).

**Verdict (D2 versus D1): the two questions have different anchors and must not be merged.** Mode `general`, triangulated across Fig 2 :29-30, :36 and :45.

**Refuter:** a definition shown to be invariant across frames and to settle an empirical origin question without appeal to experiment. Then it functions as an anchor and F8 is wrong.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion and before any outreach, per the standing instruction that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument.

The shape claim (plural definitions are legitimate, bounded by the guard) is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. The definitional disagreement shrinks with effort to one definition that works on all the named subsets (breaks Fig 9 :4-5 and the union reading in Fig 9 :54-55).
2. A definition is shown to make a world-claim that the ground settles true or false, so that it is a verdict rather than a convention (breaks Fig 10 :18, :46 and :49).
3. A definition is shown to be a legitimate anchor for an empirical origin question, settling it without an independent experiment or observation (breaks Fig 2 :29-30 and the guard in Fig 2 :43 / Fig 10 :47).

Secondary falsifiers, each tied to a finding: a broad definition that survives Fig 5 :43-44 (F5); two frames with identical evidence whose union adds nothing (F6); a demonstrably last definition with no residual (F7).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, no part of this is `grounded`.

## 8. Verdict

The figures answer the question in the affirmative with a guard. Multiple scientifically reasonable definitions of life can coexist, each useful for a different question, because the disagreement is a union of well-formed sub-questions (Fig 9), a definition is a convention that makes no claim about the world (Fig 10), and agreement on a shared meaning is the product of a convention rather than corroboration (Fig 2). The guard is that no definition may be allowed to decide an empirical outcome (Fig 10 :47, Fig 2 :43). On empirical origin questions the anchor is experiment, and definitions must range against it separately (Fig 2 :29-30, :45). There is no last definition (Fig 7, Fig 9 :32).

Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the research-practice predictions, not `grounded`. Strains after re-census: none hidden; the one live type question, whether a convention can be said to "pay" under Fig 5, is a specified reading, not a measurement.
