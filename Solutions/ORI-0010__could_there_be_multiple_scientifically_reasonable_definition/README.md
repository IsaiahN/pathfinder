# Solution ORI-0010

**Question:** Could there be multiple scientifically reasonable definitions of life, each useful for a different question?

**Source:** origins of life.txt line 14

**Section:** 1. What counts as "life," and what counts as its origin?

**Mode:** `general` on the structural shape (a plurality of definitions is legitimate, bounded by a guard), `specified` on predictions about how research programs actually use definitions. Nothing here is `grounded`. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading, so it cannot settle the question it reports on.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

**Answer in one line:** Yes, with a guard. The disagreement is a union of well-formed sub-questions, each definition is a convention authored for its question rather than a claim about the world, and a plurality becomes a scientific error only when one definition is allowed to decide an empirical outcome.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree that plural definitions are legitimate.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **Agreement is not a verdict.** Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." A list selected for people who already favour definitional pluralism would manufacture the very correlation that makes agreement worthless.
2. **Correlation has two valences.** Fig 2 :41: "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it." Consensus on a definition is the product of a convention, not evidence that the definition is true.
3. **Do not spend the reader's independence.** Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Send the residual and its refuter, not the framework's interiors.

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one.
