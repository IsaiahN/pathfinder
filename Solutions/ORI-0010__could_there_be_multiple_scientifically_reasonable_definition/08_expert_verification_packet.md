# Expert verification packet: the Tether on plural definitions of life

**What this is:** a claim in a form a philosopher of biology, astrobiologist or origin-of-life researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so the reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on research-practice predictions, not `grounded`.** No experiment is claimed. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

There can be multiple scientifically reasonable definitions of life, each useful for a different question. The disagreement is a union of well-formed sub-questions, not one hard question, because it does not shrink with effort and each candidate works well on a different subset. A definition is a convention that makes no claim about the world, so several can coexist without contradiction, provided each states four things: the question it is for, the actors and scale it ranges over, the ground that could settle that question, and a guard that stops it deciding an outcome it was not authored to decide. On empirical origin questions the anchor is the experimental and geological record, not any definition. Consensus on a definition is the product of a convention, not evidence that the definition is true. There is no last definition.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 9 and 10

- **Statement:** the definitional disagreement is a union of sub-questions, so plurality is the correct move, and each definition is a convention rather than a world-claim.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :4 "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset," and Fig 9 :5 "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." Also Fig 10 :46 "A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do. The seat may author what has no truth value and nothing that does."
- **What to check:** does the history of life-definitions actually show non-shrinking disagreement with each criterion working on a different subset? Is a working definition functioning as a convention in your field, or is it being used as a discovery claim?
- **Refuter:** the disagreement demonstrably shrinks toward one definition working on all subsets; or a definition is shown to make a world-claim the ground settles true or false.

### C2 — the guard; checkable by any working origin-of-life researcher

- **Statement:** a definition is legitimate until it is used to decide an empirical outcome; then it stops being a convention and becomes a frame deciding its own result. On empirical origin questions the anchor is experiment.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one" and Fig 2 :43 "Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." Also Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when" and Fig 2 :30 "you push on it, it is not an anchor."
- **What to check:** can you name a case in your field where a definition was used to settle which systems were alive without an independent measurement? Can you name the anchor that actually settled it?
- **Refuter:** exhibit a community that uses a definition to decide an empirical origin question and also supplies an independent ground for the boundary; or show a definition that is itself a legitimate anchor.

### C3 — discipline-facing; checkable against the published literature

- **Statement:** the literature already treats definitional pluralism as normal, and the definitions are members of a functional group rather than isomers. Each definition must still pay the MDL bargain.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :43 "A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before. A term that explains everything by saying nothing" and Fig 5 :44 "fails this, and so does a term that explains one occasion perfectly." Also Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- **World support (secondary, opened 2026-11-23):** Abbott and Persson, npj Complexity 2026, "multiple technical definitions of 'life', each of which would be most useful within a specific context"; Schoenmakers, Synthese 2023, definitions whose purpose "is not to be true, but to be useful to scientists by guiding scientific practice"; Gomez-Marquez, Molecular Biology Reports 2021, definitions "biased by the research focus of the person making the definition."
- **What to check:** are those quotations faithful? Are the candidate definitions really not same-atoms-different-bond, so that the isomer claim should be refused in favour of a functional-group reading?
- **Refuter:** show the candidates share one atom set and differ only by bond (which would make the isomer claim true); or show a vacuous or one-off definition that is nonetheless retained as reasonable.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that every definition is equally good. The vacuous and the ad hoc fail Fig 5 :43-44.
- Not that "life" has no referent, or that the term should be eliminated from biology.
- Not which definition any field should adopt. That is a convention the field authors.
- Not that consensus makes a definition true. Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."
- Not that any definition is `grounded` here. No measurement was taken.
- Not a theory of everything. Fig 7 :6: "the limit: no last room".

## 5. What would settle it

The shape is `general` and is checked against the figures, not by an experiment. The `specified` predictions are checkable:

1. Do communities that use scoped definitions make more progress than communities that mandate one? This needs a historical or sociological study with an independent ground.
2. Does mandating one definition actually collapse the union surplus that Fig 8 :45 describes ("Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone")? Compare research programs before and after a definitional mandate.
3. Does the four-slot template discriminate reasonable from unreasonable definitions better than an intuition-based judgment? Test it on a set of definitions blind.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :4-5, Fig 10 :46 and :47, Fig 2 :29-30 and :43, Fig 5 :43-44. Six short passages.
2. Say whether your field's working definition is being used as a convention or as a world-claim. Name the anchor for one empirical question you work on.
3. Try the four-slot template on one definition you know. Does it expose a missing slot?
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
