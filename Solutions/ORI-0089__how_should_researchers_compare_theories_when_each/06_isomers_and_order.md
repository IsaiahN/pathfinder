## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are RNA-first and metabolism-first isomers?

Fig 12 :117:

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fig 12 :123:

> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

- Written as slogans, the theories share almost nothing: RNA-first emphasises templated replication, metabolism-first emphasises energy coupling before genetics. Under Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers** on that spelling; they are different kinds of account.
- Written over the full shared atom set listed above, differing only in which atom is bonded to which and in what order (for example, replication before compartments versus compartments before replication), they **are** isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different predicted intermediate states. That is the stronger and checkable claim.
- The weaker always-available claim is the functional group: both are `production` molecules, "A produces B; B did not exist before" (Operators :37). The group says what kind of transition is being explained; the qualifier says which arrangement is proposed.
- Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance." So the comparison should be recorded as an arrangement, not as a list of ingredients each theory mentions.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if one theory can be written only with an atom the other does not require (for example, a specific mineral surface that the other account cannot admit without changing its predictions), the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim stands.

### Q2 - What order do the stages take?

Fig 3 :20:

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**

Fig 12 :72:

> **"A then B; B needs A to have happened"**

Operators :37:

> **"A produces B; B did not exist before"**

`→` and `⇒` are both ordered, and they are not interchangeable: `⇒` requires the product to be absent before the producing step, while `→` only requires precedence. This matters here. "Compartments form, then polymers replicate inside them" is a sequence claim. "Energy coupling produces the first heritable variation" is a production claim. Folding them into one "stage order" loses the bond type, which is exactly the flat-formula collapse Fig 12 :5-17 names: "the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not".

*The Library of Babel* gives the matching instruction: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way." Combine after the split, not before.

**Falsifier:** if the observed intermediate states can appear in any order with the same downstream outcome, the dependency claim fails and the chain is a set, not a chain.

### Q3 - What kind of comparison is between two partial theories at one stage?

Fig 12 :87 and Operators :49:

> **"a threshold relation between two quantities"**

Fig 12 :112:

> **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."**

`⋛` is ordered and asymmetric: the test is "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present?" (Fig 12 :89). So a comparison must name the two quantities and the axis. Candidates here: residual left on the stage after each theory is applied (`left(R,phi)`), description cost `|φ|`, and action cost to settle the stage (Fig 12 :125-130, two currencies in different units, never added).

Fig 5 :43 states the bargain: "A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before." Applied to a theory, scope alone is not the score; the leftover counts.

**Falsifier:** if the comparison is symmetric, or the two operands are not quantities on a shared axis, `⋛` is mistyped. Operators :68: "A relation that fails all seven is not an operator you are missing; it is a claim that has not been made yet." The honest response is to state a new claim, not to smear the comparison into a conjunction.

### Q4 - Which analogue actually helps, and which is refused

World analogue, secondary only: ensemble forecasting. The verified page states that "When many different forecast models are used to try to generate a forecast, the approach is termed multi-model ensemble forecasting. This method of forecasting can improve forecasts when compared to a single model-based approach." This corroborates Fig 8 :45 "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone", but it is a different field with a different ground, so it is a witness for the mechanism shape, not for an origin-of-life conclusion. The same page warns that small ensemble spread can produce overconfidence, which is Fig 2 :35 in meteorological clothing.

Refused analogue: "inference to the best explanation as winner-picking." The SEP *Abduction* entry, which I read, notes the "best of a bad lot" problem: selecting the best member of a candidate set does not warrant truth when the set is incomplete. That corroborates the need to describe the residual before choosing frames (Fig 9 :27-28). It does not license a single winner.

### Q5 - Recursion and level

Fig 12 :104:

> **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."**

A stage settled against the ground (a demonstration that a pathway works under plausible conditions) becomes an atom for the next stage's comparison. This is the recursion the figures license, not an import. It also sets the limit: "Nothing composes over what was never admitted as a part" (Fig 12 :106). A stage that has not been settled cannot be used as a premise for the next stage. Fig 13 :115: "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."

**Falsifier:** show a second-order comparison composed over a stage that no experiment has settled. That breaks Fig 12 :106 and the whole ordering collapses to a list.

## CONCLUSION

The partial theories are **isomers only when written over the full shared atom set**; as commonly stated they are members of a functional group (Fig 12 :117, :123). Their stage order is a mix of `→` sequence and `⇒` production, fixed by dependency rather than importance (Fig 3 :20, Fig 12 :72, Operators :37), and must be split before it is combined. The comparison at each stage is an ordered threshold `⋛` over named quantities, with scope and leftover both counted (Fig 12 :87, Fig 5 :43). A stage settled against the ground becomes an atom for the next comparison (Fig 12 :104); an unsettled one cannot.

This rescues what is checkable in "compare the theories" without inventing a new primitive or naming a winner. The claim "over the shared atom set the accounts are isomers with a stated order and a stated falsifier" is a `specified` structural claim. The claim "theory X is the right account of life's origin" is not derived here.
