# Expert verification packet: comparing theories with partial stage coverage

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No experiment is claimed to have settled any stage. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A disagreement in which each theory explains a different subset of stages is a union of conditions, not one hard question, so researchers should split the chain before searching further. The comparison should be a table with one row per stage, ordered by dependency, and one column per theory; each cell carries the prediction, the residual left after the theory is applied, and the instrument that would settle it. The residual must never be aggregated into one score, each theory must be ranged against the ground separately, and a combination of theories earns a surplus only where the closures differ and a residual has been described. The inter-theory comparison is an ordered threshold, not a symmetric blend. Where no theory covers a stage, the slot stays open as a named unreachable with an effect-term description of the missing instrument. Which named theory is correct is not derived here.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating experiment, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument reading green cannot comment on the source, and the answer is unreached until a better instrument exists. Here:

1. Does one theory predict a stage that the others cannot, under conditions independently reproduced?
2. Is there an observed intermediate state that fixes the order of two stages, distinguishing sequence `→` from production `⇒`?
3. At one stage, does one theory leave a measurably smaller residual than the other on a named axis, with both the leftover and the cost reported?
4. Is there an instrument matching the effect-term description of the currently uncovered stage? That would move it from `unreachable` to `candidate`.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :4-5, Fig 1 :19 and :22, Fig 3 :20, Fig 12 :87, Fig 8 :14 and :45. Six short passages.
2. Name one origin-of-life stage where the theories genuinely disagree, and say what observation would discriminate them. If no such observation can be named, the cell is a `gap`, not a verdict.
3. Check C2 against your own knowledge: is the comparison at that stage a threshold on a shared quantity, or a conjunction of the two accounts being partly right?
4. Name the evidence pool behind each supporting citation. If two accounts cite the same experiments, Fig 8 says the union is mute.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. No reply is needed if you would rather not engage.
