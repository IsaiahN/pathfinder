**Subject:** Check one structural claim about causal sets before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about causal set theory. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the framework's minimum set has six terms in two kinds, states (space, time, energy) and structures (spectrum, gradient, network). Composition runs across the two kinds and never within one. Two figure lines bear directly here. One says *"Ordering does not produce adjacency"* and *"There is no route."* The other says a direct bond of two states is not composable: *"It is not space-time but space, a structure, time."* So the causal order (duration, precedence) cannot on its own generate spatial adjacency. The order also is not a standalone structure: *"A structure with no state is empty: an ordering over nothing, a network with nothing at the nodes."*
>
> **What the framework endorses in your program:** the continuum is not a primitive. It was considered as a seventh member and removed because *"it is a spectrum without gaps"*; discreteness is *"what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member."* So the emergence of the continuum from a discrete substrate is figure-consistent.
>
> **What the framework says is owed:** order alone is insufficient. A magnitude that fixes volume per region must be supplied from outside the order, because composition inside a closure never adds an atom. This is given as a **shape**, not a name: a quantity that assigns magnitude where the order leaves a gap. The field's own slogan, *"Order + Number = Geometry"*, is a promising match, but it comes from your frame as a provenanced import. The framework does not derive the equation and does not name a new primitive.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the framework proves or disproves causal set theory, that the Hauptvermutung is false or true, that spacetime is definitely discrete, or that the order is useless. I am not asserting that this framework is the only true one. It is a candidate generator that refuses the last room and states its own refuter.
>
> **What would refute the output:**
> 1. deriving adjacency from the causal order alone inside the six-term set;
> 2. generating geometry from a bare order with no event-elements and no counting measure;
> 3. deriving the counting measure from the order with no import;
> 4. a measurement or proof at the Planck scale showing the order alone recovers the full metric.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, I retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
