**Mode: `specified` on the verdicts, `general` on the structural shape; not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Causal set theory holds that a discrete causal order ≺ is fundamental and that spacetime geometry is recovered from it. The question asks whether the order *alone* generates the full structure (adjacency, dimension, conformal geometry, volume). This is a **union compound**, not one hard question: Fig 9 :54 says a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either, and Fig 9 :56 says to unbundle before searching again. The two conditions are order and number. They have different closures and must not be averaged.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that causal set theory is wrong. The framework endorses discrete-first emergence (D4) and the 9/10 result is exactly what the field itself teaches.
- Not that the order is useless. It is necessary; it is just not sufficient.
- Not that the counting measure is derived. It is imported, with a source gate and shadow test (Fig 6 :12-13).
- Not that number is a new primitive of the framework. It is a binding from the physics frame onto a missing item, described by its effect.
- Not that the Hauptvermutung is proved (F-SURYA: *"there is no full proof of the conjecture"*).
- Not that spacetime is discrete. That remains an empirical question (F7 strain, Fig 6 :39: *"What is unavailable is a sensor for something that has never registered anywhere, at any resolution."*).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement at the Planck scale or continuum limit settles `left(R,φ)`.
