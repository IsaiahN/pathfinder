## DERIVATION

### Q1: Which bond is the causal order?

- Fig 12 :72: **"A then B; B needs A to have happened"** defines `→` sequence, ordered, non-symmetric. Causal precedence has exactly this shape: x precedes y, and y needs x to have happened.
- Fig 12 :77: **"A produces B; B did not exist before"** defines `⇒` production. A causet element does not *produce* its successor in the figure's sense; it precedes it. So the order is `→`, not `⇒`. Marking this matters: the field's "sequential growth" is a dynamics, a separate loop, not the kinematic order itself.
- Fig 13 :23-24: **"network that entities relate, and influence travels"**. A partial order is a network: spacelike-separated elements are unordered, so the graph branches. Fig 12 :48: **"A chain is the degenerate case: one bond type, no branching."** A total order (a chain) is a special, lower-dimensional case; a generic causet is a network.
- Fig 13 :20: **"ordering, so more and less can be said"** (spectrum). The order also carries an ordering structure, which is what the dimension estimators exploit.
- Conclusion: the order is a **network/spectrum structure bound over event-states by `→` sequence**. It is not a seventh primitive and not a state. Falsifier: show the order requires a bond outside the seven (a relation failing all six tests is *"a claim that has not been made yet"*, Fig 12 :90).

### Q2: Are two causets with the same element count but different orders isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Take two causets C1 and C2 over the same N elements, differing only in ≺. Same atoms, different arrangement, hence different spacetime (different substance): by Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**. The difference is invisible if you record only "N elements" and discard the order. This is the structural content of *"same number, different geometry"*.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two non-isomorphic orders over the same element set always yield isometric continuum spacetimes, the isomer claim fails and the relation is merely a functional group (the order fixes the kind, "Lorentzian-like", but not the member). That result would not help the strong causal thesis; it would only weaken the isomer reading.
- Triangulation: single-source for the figure definition (Fig 12 :117, :123); the physics-facing check (do distinct orders actually give distinct geometry?) is corroborated by F-JAKSLAND's *"the same continuum spacetime will be approximated by many different causets"*, which is the **converse** (many orders, one spacetime), so it is consistent with, not a proof of, the isomer claim.

### Q3: Many causets to one spacetime: convergence, not blend

- Fig 13 :121: **"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."**
- Fig 13 :123: **"Two chains built from different links may compose functionally identical terms, and the converse holds too: the same ingredients under a different bond are a different substance."**
- Fig 8 :50: **"But shared atoms are not the signature. Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."**
- The many-to-one relation between causets and continuum spacetimes is the convergence case, not a blend (Fig 8 blend collapses to vocabulary, never an atom). But Fig 8 :51 adds: **"Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."** A causet obtained by sprinkling into a given spacetime and one reached independently look identical in content; only the lineage distinguishes a retrodiction from a derivation. So the Hauptvermutung, if proved, would be a convergence result, and must carry provenance.
- Note the independence caveat: in this simulated derivation the "two frames" (figure census and physics frame) draw on one evidence pool for the convergence reading, so by Fig 2 :40 their agreement is one pool talking. It is not corroboration.

### Q4: What order is the union of order and number?

- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."**
- The causal order, settled as a structure over event-states, becomes an atom at the next level. The imported magnitude (number/volume) composes with it cross-kind: states-with-count on one side, order-structure on the other, geometry as the derivative. Fig 13 :74: **"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."**
- Fig 12 :106: **"Nothing composes over what was never admitted as a part."** This is why F2 matters: a bare order with no states cannot be composed over. The events must be admitted as states first.
- **Falsifier:** if the magnitude can be shown to compose within the structural kind (order plus ordering yields volume, no imported atom), the cross-kind claim fails and F4 in `01` must be re-censed. This is the live strain to watch, not a hidden one.

### Q5: Does the order alone carry a route to the continuum limit?

- Fig 13 :90: **"One chain is one route through a space that has no last route."**
- Fig 4 :33: **"Which is why there is no top and no infinite regress."**
- A single causal order is one route; the continuum is a level it lands on, and the level remakes it (Fig 4 :34-35: *"The level a method lands on remakes it. A seed carries no small tree"*). So there is no final continuum description, which is consistent with the field's own view of the continuum as approximate. This corroborates F3, but does not by itself decide discreteness (F7 strain stays live).

## CONCLUSION

The causal order is a network/spectrum structure bound over event-states by the `→` sequence bond, not a new primitive and not a state. Two causets over the same elements with different orders are **isomers** under Fig 12 :117, with a stated falsifier (distinct orders always giving the same geometry would reduce the claim to a functional group). The many-causets-to-one-spacetime relation is convergence (Fig 13 :121, Fig 8 :50), and must carry provenance. The union of order and number is a cross-kind composition at second order, licensed by Fig 12 :104 and forbidden within one kind by Fig 13 :74.

This rescues what is checkable in the causal set program without asserting what the figures cannot: it does not derive the counting measure, does not prove the Hauptvermutung, and does not settle whether nature is discrete.
