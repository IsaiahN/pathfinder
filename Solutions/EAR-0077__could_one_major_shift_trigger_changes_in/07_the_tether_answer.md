# The Tether's answer: could one major shift trigger changes in other parts of the Earth system?

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks for one thing and actually bundles several: a source element crossing a threshold, a medium that carries influence, a receiver element with its own threshold, a loop that may or may not close, an observing channel that may or may not still return a signal, and a decision that may be made before certainty. **D2, detection. Verdict: `specified`, triangulated.** The ground does not decay, the channel does (Fig 10 :24). Instruments are improved from worse instruments already returning something (Fig 6 :32). **D3, modelling and assessment. Verdict: `general`, triangulated.** Overlapping reviews and shared authors are one evidence pool (Fig 2 :40), and a chain of agreeing assessments is one long room (Fig 7 :35). **The four verdicts are kept separate and never averaged.** "Cascades are plausible" is not a synthesis of them; it is a filter for where to look.

## 3. What the answer says in one sequence

1. Re-index "one major shift" as a vector over receiver slots. A global mean hides the live signal (Fig 1 :22).
2. Enumerate each receiver's habitat outward, reading it off the world rather than composing it (Fig 11 :43, :48).
3. Type each link before using the word trigger: `→` sequence, `⇒` production, `⋛` threshold comparison (Fig 12, Operators table).
4. Ask whether the loop closes and what sign it has. A chain cannot close by construction (Fig 12 :121).
5. Check the channel to the ground. Detection failure is channel decay, not safety (Fig 10 :24).
6. Treat reversal as substitution of a habitat, not subtraction of a condition (Fig 11 :66).
7. Keep the conditions as a union; one lever leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55).
8. Report `gap` for the named chain. The figures return a kind of thing, never the answer itself (Fig 9 :32).

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any named element (AMOC, Amazon, Greenland, Antarctic ice, coral) will tip another. Those names enter as provenanced bindings from sources opened 2026-11-22, not as figure outputs.
- Not a threshold value, a date, a probability or a timescale. No figure line returns one.
- Not that all cascades are causal. Fig 12 separates sequence from production; the field's own dispute is recorded rather than resolved by fiat.
- Not that the DDT case supplies the physical mechanism. It supplies the shape of contact propagation only.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken, and this is a simulated derivation.
- Not a final theory. Fig 13 :90: *"One chain is one route through a space that has no last route."*

## 5. What would settle it, and the honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."* The instruments that would close it:

- A source-discriminating measurement of a coupling medium, so that a common driver can be separated from direct production. The metamerism essay: a reading *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer, not an argument, resolved the analogous case.
- A receiver-side early-warning record that remains reachable through the first element's transition, so that the domino case can be distinguished from a hidden common cause.
- A return experiment showing whether a tipped element reverts by subtraction or only by habitat substitution (Fig 11 :61 to :66).
- A per-element residual vector across at least three elements, so that a global scalar can be tested (Fig 1 :19).

The stop is at the limit of available instruments, with the lineage recorded. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. Standing refuters

Written before the conclusion; repeated from `01` §4. The shape claim is withdrawn if any of these holds:

1. A threshold crossing produces no positive residual two or more links out while the channel stays reachable and receiver forcing is held constant.
2. A global scalar predicts per-element crossings with no per-slot residual, across three or more elements.
3. A claimed trigger is shown to be only a sequence, the receiver's new state having been determined before the source fired.
4. A cascade has no re-entrant loop and is fully explained by linear superposition.
5. A detector reliably signals the second element before the first fires, repeatedly, in the domino configuration.
6. A tipped element is returned by subtraction with no substituted habitat and no inherited unchosen actors.
7. One mechanism accounts for all observed cross-element changes with no residual and no overlap structure.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The figures do not name a tipping chain, and this is not a ground reading. They say that contact propagates outward beyond first-order enumeration, so scoping a tipping element to its immediate forcing is how the receiver three links out gets missed; that the residual is per element, so a global average hides the live signal; that "trigger" is a bond-type question, where a sequence and a production are different things; that amplification requires a loop that closes with a positive sign, since a chain cannot close by construction; and that failed early warning is a decayed channel, not a safe receiver. Reversal is asymmetric: you can add a condition, you cannot subtract a habitat. The conditions form a union, so one lever leaves the intersections standing. What this offers is the shape any cascade answer must have, five refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The named chain stays open until the ground settles it.
