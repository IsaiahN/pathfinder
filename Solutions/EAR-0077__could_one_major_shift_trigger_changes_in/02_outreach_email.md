# Outreach draft — check the shape first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** this draft states a shape and its refuter. Do not add the framework's interiors to stage 1. Once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about tipping cascades, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified` on the structure, not `grounded`):** a shift in one part of the Earth system can change other parts, but only under conditions that can be stated in advance. Four seem load-bearing:
>
> 1. The receiver is in contact with the source through a medium that actually carries influence, and the enumeration has to go beyond direct contact. The figure I am checking says that scoping to direct contact is how a second-order effect gets missed, with the residual that mattered three steps out.
> 2. The link has a type. A sequence, where B follows A, is not the same as a production, where B did not exist before A fired. "Trigger" means the second, not the first. A threshold crossing at the source is a third, different relation.
> 3. A change persists and amplifies only where output re-enters as input with a positive sign. A plain chain transmits; it does not close.
> 4. The observing channel can decay while the ground does not. A failed early-warning indicator is evidence about the channel, not evidence that the receiver is safe.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that this returns which element tips which, at what threshold, or when. It returns a shape. It does not settle the empirical question, and no measurement here supports a particular named chain.
>
> **What would refute it, written before this message:**
> 1. A measured case where a threshold crossing produces no positive residual two or more links out while the channel stays reachable and receiver forcing is held constant.
> 2. A global scalar that predicts per-element crossings with no per-slot residual left over, across three or more elements.
> 3. An instrument showing a claimed trigger is only a sequence, the receiver's new state having been determined before the source fired.
> 4. A cascade with no re-entrant loop and amplification fully explained by linear superposition.
> 5. A detector that reliably signals the second element before the first fires, repeatedly, in the domino configuration.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Klose et al. 2021, "What do we mean, 'tipping cascade'?", Environmental Research Letters 16 125011: https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-9326/ac3955
> - Eker et al. 2024, "Cross-system interactions for positive tipping cascades", Earth System Dynamics 15, 789-800: https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/15/789/2024/
> - Global Tipping Points Report 2025: https://global-tipping-points.org/
>
> **If you want to use the method:** write down what would refute your own result *before* you run it, and state whether your claim is general, specified or grounded. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [name]
