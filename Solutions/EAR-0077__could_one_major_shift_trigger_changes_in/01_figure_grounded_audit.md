## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

**Question as posed:** Could one major shift trigger changes in other parts of the Earth system?

**Effect restatement.** A tipping element crosses a critical threshold and leaves its prior state. Does that event change the state of other elements that are not co-located with it, through physical coupling: ocean circulation, atmospheric moisture transport, rivers, albedo, carbon cycling, ecological exchange? And can those changes themselves cross their own thresholds, so that the first event is the start of a chain rather than an isolated regional change?

**Two options and where each leads.**

- **Option A, "yes, one shift propagates."** Leads to a search for source element, coupling link, receiver element, coupling sign, timescale and threshold for each chain. Falsified by a measured case where a threshold crossing produces no positive residual two or more links out while the observing channel stays reachable.
- **Option B, "no, elements respond only to local forcing."** Leads to a search for local control parameters per element. Falsified by a measured cross-element residual that persists after local forcing is held constant.

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term.** I bind the term `cascade claim` before the run, per the formula's Direction rule: a cascade claim is a per-receiver-slot assertion, *"if element X crosses threshold c at time t, then the residual at receiver slot s becomes positive within window W through medium M."* It is indexed per slot (Fig 1 :19), it names the bond type (Fig 12), it names the medium (Fig 11 :45 to :47), and it carries a refuter. Binding it makes the sought-for shape refusable by the same test as everything else instead of an invisible preference. Unbound, a searcher can accept any anecdote as a cascade and any null as independence.

**Arity, symmetry, scale (Fig 9 :22).** Arity: many, a chain of slots rather than one cause and one effect. Symmetry: the coupling is generally ordered, not interchangeable; AMOC weakening affects Amazon rainfall through moisture transport in a way that Amazon rainfall does not equally affect AMOC on the same timescale. Scale: the chain crosses local, regional and global strata (Fig 4 membrane), and a remainder compounding through an amplifying chain is where chaos enters (Fig 13 :31).

## 1. Domain census before choosing any figure line

Describe R, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor. A domain with no nameable anchor is not yet a domain.

- **R (the residual).** A cross-element residual that the current per-element models do not predict. Arity: many slots. Symmetry: ordered coupling. Scale: crosses strata. Anchor: the observation or palaeo record. It is a slice: *"It is the aim, not only the error signal, and it is always a slice: what cannot be perceived yet is residual and simply cannot be read"* (Symbols :38 to :39).
- **D1, physical coupling and habitat.** Actors: tipping elements (AMOC, Greenland and Antarctic ice sheets, Amazon rainforest, warm-water coral reefs, permafrost, monsoons), the media that carry influence (ocean circulation, moisture recycling, rivers, albedo, carbon cycle), conditions (temperature, freshwater flux, sea ice). Currency: contact and influence travelling. Anchor: measurement and the geological record. This is the governing domain.
- **D2, detection and the early-warning channel.** Actors: indicators (critical slowing down, autocorrelation, variance), instruments, observation windows. Currency: signal against noise; channel bandwidth. Anchor: the same ground, reached through a channel that can decay.
- **D3, the modelling and assessment frame.** Actors: modelers, review authors, assessment bodies. Currency: agreement and residuals. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all seats draw on one evidence pool, so independence is low.
- **D4, decision and governance.** Actors: institutions, policymakers. Currency: action budget and prestige versus ground contact. Anchor: the outcome the ground returns, which does not consult a convention.
- **Partition or union.** A union, not a partition. D1 to D4 overlap: a detector (D2) is itself a physical actor in the habitat (D1), an assessment (D3) can change policy action (D4), and policy can change the forcing that sets D1 thresholds. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3 :20).** Describe the residual per slot (Fig 9 :27 to :28) → enumerate the habitat outward (Fig 11 :44) → type the bond and its sign per link (Fig 12) → check that the detection channel is reachable (Fig 10 :24) → settle each domain separately against the ground (Fig 2 :29) → only then decide (D4). Effort spent above the break cannot rescue a link below it (Fig 3 :19).

**Strain test applied before concluding.** A first reading wanted to force Fig 11's DDT example onto climate teleconnections. Re-census: the DDT line is an *instance* of a domain-agnostic law about contact propagation, not a constraint on which medium carries it. No strain after re-census. A second reading wanted to force Fig 12 :121 (*"a chain cannot close by construction"*) onto the domino cascade. Re-census: a domino cascade is the degenerate chain (Fig 12 :48); the two-phase and joint cascades are the loop-closing cases. The figure distinguishes them rather than contradicting them. Both re-censuses are kept visible in §7.

## 3. Findings F1 to F10

Each finding carries its verbatim figure line, its mode, its refuter, and its triangulation status.

### F1 — Contact cascades are a named figure law, and first-order enumeration misses the residual that matters (decisive for the shape)

Fig 11 :52 to :53:

> **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**

Fig 11 :44:

> **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**

Fig 13 :24 supplies the structure that makes contact propagate:

> **"that entities relate, and influence travels"**

Fig 13 :31 supplies the amplification:

> **"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise."**

The figure law is domain-agnostic. The DDT case is the instance the figure uses; the law is that contact reaches further than first-order enumeration. Applied here: scoping a tipping element to its immediate forcing misses the receiver three links out, which is exactly the question being asked. The mechanism in the figure's own citation was established over a decade, not assumed: Fig 11 :54, *"Carson, Silent Spring, 1962. The mechanism, biomagnification through the food chain to eggshell thinning, was established over the following decade."*

World corroboration, secondary: Brovkin et al. 2021, *Nature Geoscience*, abstract, *"abrupt changes arise from slow changes in one component of the Earth system that eventually pass a critical threshold, or tipping point, after which impacts cascade through coupled climate–ecological–social systems"*; Klose et al. 2021, *Environmental Research Letters*, identifies the AMOC to Amazon moisture link.

- **Mode:** `specified` on the propagation law; `general` on domain-agnostic contact.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 + Fig 13 + Fig 12 :48). Low overlap: the habitat-enumeration route and the network/amplification route are different figure lines, and the world sources are independent of the framework.
- **Refuter:** a measured case where crossing a threshold in one element produces no positive residual in any element two or more links away, while the observing channel demonstrably remains reachable and the forcing of the receiver is held constant. If that is found, the shape claim fails.

### F2 — The question must be re-indexed per element; a global shift hides the live slot

Fig 1 :19:

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**

Fig 1 :22:

> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

Fig 11 :49:

> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**

Fig 11 :51:

> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

"One major shift" is a global scalar in the question's phrasing. Under Fig 1 a global residual near zero with one live slot is a legal state, so a global mean cannot tell us where the shift is live. The right form is a vector over elements, not a single number. Closeness of an element to its threshold is not a standing property; it is relative to the residual being tracked, so a fixed list of "the most dangerous tipping elements" is a standing list of good actors and is refused.

World corroboration: the Global Tipping Points Report 2025 lists coral reefs crossing at about 1.4 °C, polar ice sheets possibly already committed, and Amazon and AMOC risks below 2 °C. Different elements, different residual slots, no single global scalar that returns the answer.

- **Mode:** `general` on the indexing rule, `specified` on the Earth-system application.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 1 + Fig 11 + world).
- **Refuter:** a single global scalar that predicts per-element threshold crossings with no per-slot residual left over, tested across at least three independent elements.

### F3 — "Trigger" is a bond-type question, and the field's terminology dispute maps onto a distinction the figures already make

Fig 12 :72:

> **"A then B; B needs A to have happened"**

That is `→`, sequence, ordered. Fig 12 :77:

> **"A produces B; B did not exist before"**

That is `⇒`, production, also ordered but with a different test: is there a B before A fires? Fig 12 :87:

> **"a threshold relation between two quantities"**

That is `⋛`, comparison, the bond of a threshold crossing.

Klose et al. 2021 write: *"This sequence of events does not necessarily have to be causal opposed to when event A directly causes event B in a domino effect."* That is exactly the `→` versus `⇒` distinction. Their taxonomy of two-phase, domino and joint cascades is a further bond and loop distinction, not a quibble about words. The question's word "trigger" commits to `⇒`: the receiver's new state did not exist before the source fired. A mere temporal sequence commits only to `→`. A threshold crossing commits to `⋛` at the source element.

Fig 12 :48:

> **"A chain is the degenerate case: one bond type, no branching."**

So a domino cascade is not a separate kind of object; it is the one-bond-type chain. This is where the field's "cascade" umbrella can hide a type error: a two-phase cascade with a shared driver and a domino cascade with direct production are different molecules.

- **Mode:** `specified`; the figure distinction itself is structural (`general`).
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 12 + Operators table + the world dispute).
- **Refuter:** an instrument that shows a claimed trigger is only `→`, with the receiver's state already determined before the source fired; or a demonstration that `→` and `⇒` cannot be discriminated by any ground reading.

### F4 — Positive feedback closes the loop; a chain cannot close by construction

Fig 12 :121:

> **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**

Fig 12 :119:

> **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."**

A tipping element is a part whose own change changes the conditions that maintain it: ice loss lowers albedo, which increases warming, which loses more ice. That is a re-entrant loop with a sign. Eker et al. 2024 identify *"underlying positive feedback loops"* as characteristic of tipping dynamics and write that cross-system secondary impacts *"called cascades, result in a much larger eventual impact."* The larger impact is the loop signature, not the chain signature. A chain transmits; a loop with a positive sign amplifies. The two-phase and joint cascades in Klose et al. are loop cases; the domino case is the degenerate chain.

- **Mode:** `general` on the structural distinction, `specified` on the Earth-system reading.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 12 + Fig 13 :31 + world).
- **Refuter:** a cascade with no output re-entering as input and with amplification wholly explained by linear superposition, so that the cycle term is empty.

### F5 — Early warning is a channel problem, not a failure of the ground

Fig 10 :24:

> **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**

Fig 10 :25:

> **"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"**

Fig 6 :32:

> **"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."**

Fig 6 :40:

> **"What is unavailable is a sensor for something that has never registered anywhere, at any resolution. That is the whole of it."**

Critical slowing down works where the channel is still returning a signal: recovery time lengthens as a threshold approaches. Klose et al. 2021 report that common early-warning indicators are sensitive to an upcoming transition *to a certain degree* in a two-phase cascade, but that in a domino cascade *"critical slowing down-based indicators fail to indicate tipping of the following element."* Brovkin et al. 2021: *"The chance of detecting abrupt changes and tipping points increases with the length of observations."* Both are channel statements. The ground did not stop; the reading stopped carrying it. The remedy is an instrument improved from one already returning something, and Eker et al. 2024 call for *"Early warning systems that harmonize the high-frequency data and monitor the key cross-system indicators."*

A genuine limit is also recorded: a system that has never registered anywhere has no edge to extend from. Fig 6 :40 states it narrowly, and it is not a warrant to invent a sensor from a description.

- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 10 + Fig 6 + three world sources).
- **Refuter:** an instrument that reliably signals the second element before the first fires, repeatedly and against the ground, in the domino configuration. If it exists, the channel limit as stated is too strong and is narrowed.

### F6 — Irreversibility is asymmetry: introduction is available, exclusion is not

Fig 11 :61 to :62:

> **"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."**

Fig 11 :66:

> **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."**

Fig 11 :68 and :69 name the two silent failures:

> **"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"**

> **"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"**

A tipped element cannot be untipped by subtracting the change; the state is maintained by a new set of contacts, and returning requires substituting a habitat and reproducing everything the prior state required. The Global Tipping Points Report 2025 states that parts of the polar ice sheets *"may also have crossed tipping points that would eventually commit the world to several metres of irreversible sea-level rise affecting hundreds of millions"* and warns, *"If we wait for certainty that tipping points have been crossed before we act, it will be too late."* Eker et al. 2024 list *"limited reversibility"* as characteristic. This is a structural asymmetry, not a claim about a specific element's exact threshold.

- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 + world).
- **Refuter:** a measured case where a tipped element is returned to its prior state by removing the introduced condition without substituting a new habitat and without inheriting unchosen actors.

### F7 — The question is a union of overlapping conditions, and one lever leaves the intersections standing

Fig 9 :55, already cited, and Fig 8 :45:

> **"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."**

Fig 8 :49:

> **"Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."**

The conditions that make one shift propagate to another include at least: a physical medium with directional coupling, a receiver near its own threshold, a loop with a positive sign, a channel that still returns a signal, and a timescale long enough to observe. These overlap. A lever that supplies a medium but not receiver proximity clears the region where only the medium is missing and leaves the compound cases standing. Klose et al. 2021: *"it remains unclear whether and how cascade-like dynamics within the Earth system is promoted by the direction and strength of the existing feedbacks."* Eker et al. 2024: *"not every cross-system interaction leads to a cascading effect."* Both are union statements, not a denial of cascades.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Status:** triangulated (Fig 9 + Fig 8 + two world sources).
- **Refuter:** a single mechanism that accounts for all observed cross-element changes with no residual left over and no overlap structure.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the derivation discipline: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device. The shape claim is withdrawn if any of the following holds.

1. A measured case where a threshold crossing in one element produces no positive residual in any element two or more links away, with the channel demonstrably reachable and receiver forcing held constant (kills F1).
2. A single global scalar that predicts per-element threshold crossings with no per-slot residual left over, across at least three elements (kills F2).
3. An instrument showing a claimed "trigger" is only `→`, the receiver's new state having been determined before the source fired, or evidence that `→` and `⇒` cannot be discriminated at the ground (kills F3).
4. A cascade with no re-entrant loop and amplification entirely explained by linear superposition (kills F4).
5. A detector that reliably signals the second element before the first fires, repeatedly, in the domino configuration (kills F5).
6. A tipped element returned to its prior state by subtraction of the introduced condition, with no substituted habitat and no inherited unchosen actors (kills F6).
7. A single mechanism that accounts for all observed cross-element changes with no residual and no overlap structure (kills F7).
8. A figure line that names a specific cascade, or a ground reading that settles one (retires F9 for that element).

If none fires, no part of this may be called `grounded`. The verdict stays `candidate`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Re-index the question per element before answering it. "One major shift" is an average that hides the live slot (F2).
2. Enumerate the habitat outward to the point where the cascade stops mattering; do not stop at direct coupling (F1).
3. Type each link as `→`, `⇒` or `⋛` before calling it a trigger (F3), and ask whether the loop closes with a positive sign (F4).
4. Treat failed early warning as a decayed channel, not as evidence that the receiver is safe (F5).
5. Keep verdicts per domain and never average them into a single "cascades are plausible" number (F7, F8).
6. Say `gap` for the named chain. The figures return a shape and a refuter, not a cascade inventory (F9).
7. Ask for a refutation, not endorsement. Use the public venues in `03`, one to one (F8, F10).

## 7. Updated verdict

The shape survives, fully triangulated. One shift can change other parts of the Earth system because contact propagates outward through an enumerated habitat, influence travels through held network structures, and a remainder compounding through an amplifying chain becomes chaos. The residual is per element, not a global average. What "trigger" means is a bond-type question, and the field's own terminology dispute is an instance of it. Failed early warning is a decayed channel, not a safe receiver. Reversal is asymmetric. The conditions form a union, so one lever leaves intersections standing.

What does not survive: any specific answer to "which shift, which parts, how fast." No figure line returns that, and none of the world sources was read as a ground settlement by this simulated derivation. F9 stands as `gap`, unreached rather than silence. Strains after re-census: **none hidden**; two dissolved and one narrowed, all recorded in §6.

Every refuter in §4 remains open. Until one is run and fires, this is `specified`/`general`, ungrounded, candidate.
