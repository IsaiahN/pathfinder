## DERIVATION

### Q1 — What separates the three patterns? A bond type and a loop, not a label

Klose et al. 2021, abstract: *"we illustrate how different patterns of multiple tipping dynamics emerge from a very simple coupling of two previously studied idealized tipping elements."* Same two elements, different coupling, three patterns. That is a structure question.

Fig 12 :48:

> **"A chain is the degenerate case: one bond type, no branching."**

The **domino cascade** is the chain. One element's crossing produces the next element's crossing, with no shared driver and no branching: `⇒` production, ordered, with the test *"is there a B before A fires? No."* (Fig 12 :79). Klose et al.: *"a domino cascade may hardly be stopped once initiated."* Once the production has fired, the chain has no off-ramp.

The **two-phase** and **joint** cascades are not chains. They are the common-cause and shared-driver cases. Fig 2 :6 to :7 names the collapse directly:

> **"collapse 2: common cause (hidden shared source)"**

Both elements move because a driver moves them, or because a slow forcing brings both near threshold at once. Their coupling bond is closer to symmetric `+` (both hold at once) or to a shared control parameter than to `⇒`. The difference between "different states of the same two elements" is therefore carried by the bond, not by the ingredients.

Fig 12 :5:

> **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**

Klose et al. write that the term cascade *"does not necessarily have to be causal opposed to when event A directly causes event B in a domino effect"* and call for *"a call to be more precise in future analyses of cascading dynamics."* The figure's answer to that call is: type the bond.

- **Falsifier:** if an instrument shows that all three patterns reduce to the same bond with the same sign and the same loop structure, the bond-type derivation fails and the field's three labels are mere descriptions of timing.

### Q2 — Chain or cycle? The loop sign decides

Fig 12 :121:

> **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**

A tipping element is not a passive node in a chain. Its own state changes the conditions that maintain it: ice loss lowers albedo, which increases absorbed energy, which drives more ice loss; rainforest loss reduces moisture recycling, which dries the remainder. That is output re-entering as input, with a sign. Eker et al. 2024: *"Conceptually, tipping dynamics are characterized by alternative stable states, non-linearity, underlying positive feedback loops, and limited reversibility."*

Fig 12 :119:

> **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."**

A maintaining feedback that regenerates itself behaves differently across uses from a spent forcing. Fig 13 :31 gives the amplifying consequence: *"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise."*

So the structural separator is: **chain transmits, positive loop amplifies, common cause synchronizes.** A "domino cascade" that amplifies only because each element is already near threshold is still a chain with a steep transfer curve, not a cycle. Calling it a loop would be a category error.

### Q3 — Are the three patterns isomers?

Fig 12 :117:

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Over a fixed atom set, the two idealized tipping elements plus the coupling medium are the atoms. The configurations differ in the bond and in whether a loop closes. By Fig 12's definition they are **isomers**: same atoms, different arrangement, different behaviour. Klose et al.'s model is exactly this: *"different patterns of multiple tipping dynamics emerge from a very simple coupling of two previously studied idealized tipping elements."* The ingredient list is fixed; the arrangement changes; the behaviour differs.

Fig 12 :123:

> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

The weaker reading is available too: the three patterns share a functional group (coupled bistable elements with a threshold) and differ only in the qualifier. Which reading applies depends on whether the atom set is genuinely fixed. If the domino case requires an atom the two-phase case does not (a directional medium absent in the shared-driver case), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group claim stands.

**Falsifier written before the run:** if either pattern requires an atom the other does not, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands; or if identical atoms under different bonds produce identical behaviour in all measured windows, the isomer claim fails on behaviour. Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."*

### Q4 — What order do the scales come in?

Fig 4 :22:

> **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**

A cascade crossing from a local shallow lake to a river network to a regional moisture system to global circulation crosses the membrane more than once. What crosses upward is not the recording of one local event but a reapplicable method: how a threshold in a bistable element propagates through a coupling. What crosses downward is a head start, not a replay: the next local case is cheaper to investigate, not predetermined.

Fig 4 :30 requires the lossy transform to be bracketed:

> **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."**

So a global cascade claim carried down to a specific basin incurs a round-trip gap `R_T`. It is not an error to eliminate; it is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold. `11_audit.md` records this qualitatively.

Fig 13 :28:

> **"They alternate, and the alternation is where both the recursion and the randomness come from."**

State, structure, state, structure. A tipping element is a state; the coupling medium is a structure; the receiver is a state. The alternation is the recursion, and the remainder at each turn is where unpredictability enters.

### Q5 — What the DDT analogue does and does not license

Fig 11 :52 to :53 (cited in `01`) names contact cascades with the DDT to eagles case. Fig 11 :54 records that the mechanism was established over the following decade. The analogue licenses only the shape: contact reaches further than first-order enumeration. It does not license the medium. Biomagnification through a food chain is not moisture transport or ocean circulation. Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* The same coupling is stabilizing for one residual and destabilizing for another; there is no standing list of good media.

- **Falsifier:** if a cascade is found whose propagation is independent of any enumerated contact relation, the habitat-enumeration requirement fails.

## CONCLUSION

The three cascade patterns are not three stories about the same mechanism. Under the figure's bond set they are: a production chain (domino), a common-cause synchrony (two-phase and joint) and, where output re-enters as input, a positive loop. Over a fixed atom set they are isomers, with a stated falsifier; if the atom set differs, they are a functional group with a qualifier. Scale order runs by methods up and head starts down, with a round-trip gap that must be measured rather than assumed. The DDT analogue licenses contact propagation, not a physical medium.

All of this is structural and `specified`. None of it names which Earth-system element is in which configuration.
