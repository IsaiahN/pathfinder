## Three findings in the wider literature that bear directly on this question

1. **Walker et al. 2004 blocks the one-number reading from inside ecology.** Return time "cannot be considered as the measure of resilience," because with multiple stable states it "doesn't measure all of the ways in which a system may fail." Four aspects (latitude, resistance, precariousness, panarchy) do not add into one scalar, and the authors say "although we do not believe in or advocate their separate measurement (especially because of their inter-dependencies)". This corroborates Fig 1 :19-28 and Fig 12 :125-130 from an evidence pool that has nothing to do with the Tether. It is a genuinely low-overlap witness.
2. **Scheffer et al. 2009 supplies the transferable pattern, not the transferable value.** Generic early-warning signals are read from a single system's own trajectory. That is per-slot work. A reader who cites this paper for a universal resilience score has changed the quantity. The conflict rule sent this source back to the domain census, where it corroborated F1 and F2 instead of flipping the figure verdict (see `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 6).
3. **Nature Ecology & Evolution, November 2026, shows the measurement problem is live.** "Global biodiversity models exhibit low predictive accuracy on local scales" and "both model types exhibit low performance when extrapolating to other contexts." That is the empirical face of Fig 4 :30: a description carried across a boundary loses what the coarser description cannot hold, and the loss must be measured rather than assumed away.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 4 :22-25**, "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down. A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." Sending the full framework before anyone has described a residual sends a recording. Lead with the shape claim and the refuter; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42**, "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who has absorbed this derivation before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28**, "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before showing the links.
- **Fig 2 :40**, "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Agreement from a list selected for receptiveness is a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 2 :42**, "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." A wrong cross-system index can misdirect restoration funding, so independence from the frame that built it is worth paying for.

## The two-stage offer

Stage 1: send the residual, the shape claim and the four refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict **before** any framework material is shown.
Stage 2, opt-in only: if they are curious, offer the three source routes (local figure text, canonical figures README, the essays). The figures, not this summary, are the authority.
Stage 3, opt-in only: offer the formula as a reusable method. Each user writes their own refuter, states their own mode, and finds their own ground.

A reply at any stage is a correction on shape, never a witness. It is not pooled as corroboration.
