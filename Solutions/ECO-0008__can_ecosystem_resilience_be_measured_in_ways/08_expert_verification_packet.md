# Expert verification packet: cross-system resilience measurement

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ecologist or measurement specialist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` structural, `specified` falsifiers. Not `grounded`.** No field measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A single resilience number comparable across a forest, an ocean and a grassland is not licensed by the framework: the residual is indexed per slot, and an aggregate hides the failing system. What transfers between systems is the method, not the value. The proposed protocol has four parts: enumerate the habitat relative to a stated residual; read the recovery residual per slot against a ground that does not update; report the round-trip gap when the protocol crosses systems; keep verdicts separate. The three systems form a union, so their intersections need their own loops, and an indicator's valence depends on the residual rather than being a property it carries.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: instrumentation and aggregation (checkable by reading Figure 1 and the Symbols table)

- **Statement:** a global resilience score hides a live slot; per-slot reading is required.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19, "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22, "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Symbols table :43-44, "R is measured per slot, never as a single global number."
- **What to check:** does a proposed cross-system index preserve `|R⁺_s|` for each member system, or does it report only the mean? A near-zero global value with one live slot is a legal state (Fig 1 :20-21).
- **Refuter:** exhibit a scalar that is itself a slot-level reading, leaves every live slot visible, and settles a recovery question without being retuned after each disturbance. If one exists, C1 fails.

### C2: what transfers (checkable by reading Figure 4 and Figure 9)

- **Statement:** the kernel transfers, the recording does not; forest, ocean and grassland are a union, not a partition.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :19-20, "The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." Fig 4 :22, "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Fig 9 :54, "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- **What to check:** name the thing that is said to transfer. If it is a number measured once in one biome, it is a recording of one occasion (Fig 4 :25). If the systems partition, one lever clears the intersections, and the union claim is wrong.
- **Refuter:** show a transferable quantity that is neither a method nor a recording of one occasion, whose value survives a change of actors, currency and anchor while still predicting recovery; or show the three systems partition. Either breaks C2.

### C3: the anchor and the round trip (checkable by reading Figure 2 and Figure 4)

- **Statement:** the anchor must be a system-specific recovery ground that does not update, and every cross-system claim owes a measured round-trip gap R_T.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29-30, "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :33, "The anchor must not update." Fig 4 :30, "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."
- **What to check:** is the stated reference state pinned to an outcome, or is it retuned until all systems score sensibly? If it moves, it is a frame, not an anchor (Fig 2 :34). Has R_T been measured for the transfer, or assumed zero?
- **Refuter:** produce a cross-system anchor that settles the same recovery question in all three systems without moving when frames push on it, and a transfer with R_T = 0. If both are demonstrated, C3 fails.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that resilience cannot be compared across systems. The method transfers; the scalar does not.
- Not that generic early-warning signals are absent. Scheffer et al. 2009 show they exist and are read per system.
- Not a theory of everything, and no final metric. The intersections stay open loops.
- Not a new primitive. Resilience is what a composition leaves behind, not a seventh member (Fig 13 :124-125).
- Not a single correct indicator list. Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :50-51).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a field measurement.
- Not a result of this derivation validating itself. The framework cannot do that (Arrival of the Fittest: "Because it is a metatheory, it cannot be validated, nor can it validate itself").

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. Concretely:

1. Run the four-part protocol in a forest, an ocean and a grassland against real disturbances and record the recovery outcomes.
2. Report the round-trip gap for each transfer.
3. Attempt each of the four refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 and publish the attempt.
4. Have an outside frame name what it would count as a failure before showing it the protocol. Fig 10 :42, "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."
