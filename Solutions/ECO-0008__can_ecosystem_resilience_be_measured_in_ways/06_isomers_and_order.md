# Union, order and two currencies: the supporting derivation

**Mode: `specified` for the falsifiable sub-claims, `general` for the structural shape. Simulated derivation, not a ground reading.** See `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7.

## RESIDUAL

The question asks whether one measurement can work across forests, oceans, grasslands and other systems. Three structural questions are owed before any verdict: (1) is the set of systems a partition or a union? (2) what is the dependency order of the measurement work? (3) what are the two prices of a proposed metric, and why can they not be added?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: the six substrate terms (space, time, energy as states; spectrum, gradient, network as structures). Bonds held: the seven, plus negation. No new primitive is available without import. Additional held terms from the world literature: recovery trajectory, disturbance regime, function at risk, round-trip gap. These are arrangements of the six, not new members. For example, a recovery trajectory is an ordering (spectrum) over time bonded to a difference that can be spent (gradient), read over a network of actors.

Order by dependency (Fig 3): perception, then vocabulary of affordances, then objective, then planning. Effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it (Fig 3 :20-21).

## DERIVATION

### Q1. Partition or union?

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
Fig 9 :55-56: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."

Forests, oceans and grasslands share conditions (temperature, water, season, nutrient supply), share disturbance types (drought, heat, harvest, fire in some grasslands), and share observers. A metric that clears the forest-only region can leave the forest-plus-drought intersection standing, because the second condition still binds. The systems are a union.

An earlier reading treated them as a partition, three separate biomes with three separate metrics. That reading is retracted, not erased: it would make the question trivial and would hide the intersections. Status: `single-source` (Fig 9 only). Walker et al. 2004 from an independent evidence pool say "There is no 'one size fits all' approach to the future," which corroborates the practical conclusion but does not make the figure verdict triangulated.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** exhibit two systems whose binding conditions are disjoint, including all intersections, so one lever clears both. Then the union claim fails.

### Q2. What is the dependency order of the work?

Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
Fig 3 :26-27: the link between having terms for what a situation affords and being able to say what would count as progress "has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments. A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

The chain for this question:

1. **Perception.** Can we tell what is in front of us and tell it apart from what was there before? This is the recovery trajectory. Its test is whether an instrument exists that registers change per slot. Fig 6 :34-35: "Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."
2. **Vocabulary of affordances.** Do we have terms for what the system affords under this disturbance? This is the habitat enumeration of Fig 11 :43-44: "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."
3. **Objective.** Can we say what would count as progress here, in its own terms? This is where Walker et al.'s four aspects live, and where most measurement work is missing an instrument.
4. **Planning.** Only now can a route to a metric be chosen under incomplete information.

A universal index is an attempt at step 4. Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." If step 3 has no instrument, adding more index machinery is motion at the wrong link.

**Falsifier:** show that step 3 already has a working, system-invariant instrument in all three domains, so an objective can be stated identically across them without enumeration. Then the order claim is empty.

### Q3. Are cross-system metrics "isomers" of one another?

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Two measurement protocols are **isomers** if they use the same atoms (same six substrate terms, same listed actors, same currency) but a different bond: for example, one reads recovery as a gradient over space (a spatial resilience pattern), the other as the same gradient over time (a temporal recovery rate). Fig 13 :76: "A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is." Same ingredients, different substance, and the formula alone cannot tell them apart.

They are **functional-group members** if they share the substructure "read a residual per slot against a ground" but use different atoms in the slots (forest species versus ocean guilds). Fig 12 :123: "the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

This distinction matters for the outreach claim. Saying "forests and oceans need different metrics" is too weak (they may share a functional group). Saying "one metric works everywhere" is too strong (the isomers are different substances). The defensible claim is: same method, different bonded terms, verdicts kept separate.

**Falsifier:** show a protocol whose behaviour is invariant under swapping which state a gradient is bonded to, so the spatial and temporal readings are the same substance. That kills the isomer claim.

### Q4. The two currencies

Fig 12 :125-126: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add. Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."
Fig 12 :128-130: "The two readings come apart in both directions. A long chain of cheap parts is expensive to describe and may cost one action to refute. A short arrangement over a costly operand is cheap to state and may take a whole budget to settle. Neither figure predicts the other, so both are read or the choice is made blind."

A global index is cheap in bits (one number fits in a report) and expensive in actions (deciding whether it holds for an ocean needs years of observation). A per-slot habitat enumeration is expensive in bits and relatively cheap to refute at one site. Choosing between them on a single "cost-effectiveness" figure is the error Fig 12 names: "Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it" (Fig 12 :130).

Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Cheapness is a budget filter. It never tells you whether the index is true.

**Falsifier:** produce a common unit in which bits and field actions are commensurable, and show that the resulting single score predicts which protocol to choose without losing either reading. Fig 12 says they do not add, so this refuter is expected to fail, but it is recorded.

### Q5. The round trip orders the comparison

Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."
Fig 4 :27-29: "Going up throws away detail; that is what makes the result reapplicable. But it means the trip is not reversible, and something carried up and brought down will not match what was sent."

Carry a method up from forest practice, bring it down into ocean practice, compare. The difference is R_T, "gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))" (Symbols table :75-77). It is not an error to remove; it is the measurement of what the common description cannot hold (Fig 4 :31-32). An index that reports comparability without reporting R_T is dead reckoning.

Note the asymmetry of the transform (Fig 4 :22): "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." A recorded score from one system is not a head start for another; it is a description of one occasion.

**Falsifier:** demonstrate a cross-system transfer whose round trip is lossless. Under Fig 4 :27-29 this cannot happen, but the attempt is what a checker should make.

## CONCLUSION

The systems form a union, so unbundle before searching. The measurement work is ordered by dependency, and the objective link is the one without an instrument. Protocols over the same atoms but with different bonds are isomers, not one metric under different labels. Description cost and action cost are two readings and cannot be summed. Cross-system claims owe a round-trip gap R_T. None of this is a measurement result; it is a structural claim checked against the figures.
