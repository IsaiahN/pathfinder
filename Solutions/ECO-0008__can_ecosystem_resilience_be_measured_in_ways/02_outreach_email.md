# Outreach draft: check the shape claim before you decide about the method

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the four refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 have been checked and the shape claim still stands. If the output fails, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about cross-system resilience measurement

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` structural shape, `specified` predictions, not `grounded`):** a single resilience number that is comparable across a forest, an ocean and a grassland is not available from the framework. The residual is indexed per slot (system, function at risk, disturbance regime, scale), and "aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average" (Fig 1 :22). What can travel between systems is the method, not the number: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down" (Fig 4 :22). So the offered deliverable is a protocol, not a score.
>
> The protocol has four parts: (1) enumerate the habitat relative to a stated residual, including conditions and relations two steps out; (2) read the residual per slot against a ground that is the actual recovery outcome, which "does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks" (Fig 2 :29-30); (3) report the round-trip gap when the protocol is carried from one system to another, because without it "you are navigating by dead reckoning" (Fig 4 :30); (4) keep verdicts per system, never averaged.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that resilience cannot be studied comparatively; that there is no generic early-warning signal. There is, and it is measured within a system's own time series. I am not claiming one number cannot be computed. I am claiming such a number is a convention, not a verdict, and that it hides the live slot. I am not claiming to know which indicator set is right. An actor's valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :50-51), so a standing list of good indicators does not survive a change of question.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written down before this message:**
> 1. a scalar that is itself a slot-level reading, leaves every live slot visible, and settles recovery without being retuned after each disturbance;
> 2. a demonstration that forest, ocean and grassland partition rather than form a union, so one lever clears the intersections too;
> 3. a cross-system transfer with a measured round-trip gap of zero;
> 4. an anchor that settles the same recovery question in all three systems and does not move when frames push on it.
>
> If any holds, I retract the shape claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the source links are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.

> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
