## Wider literature that bears on the claim

- The Amazon and boreal forests are both listed as potential tipping elements, but with large uncertainty: Lenton et al. 2008 groups them in the "intermediate sensitivity with largest uncertainty" cluster.
- Threshold estimates are not single numbers: Lovejoy and Nobre 2018 gives 20 to 25 percent deforestation for the Amazon, with fire and warming as interacting drivers; Lenton et al. 2008 gives roughly 3 to 4 degrees C global warming for Amazon dieback and roughly 3 degrees C for boreal dieback, while noting model disagreement.
- Definition itself is an active research problem: Borner and Dijkstra 2026 argues that abruptness and irreversibility are neither necessary nor sufficient, and that a change point in a time series generally does not locate a critical threshold.
- Detection is instrument-limited: Magney and Pierrat 2025 and Hirsch and Koren 2026 both state that vegetation indices are indirect proxies and do not uniquely identify a tipping point.
- The generic shape of early warning is cross-domain: Dakos et al. 2012 in ecology and Rizzi et al. 2025 in clinical neuroscience use the same slowing-down and autocorrelation structure.

## Distribution rules: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator.
- Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the theory.
- Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." A send selected for likely agreement is a convention, not corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the per-forest shape claim and its five refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and which ground would settle it; (3) record that verdict; (4) only then, if they are curious, the three links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer, plus an optional third stage

1. **Stage 1, validate the output.** Send the shape and the refuter. No framework material. Record the verdict before anything else is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in theory.** Only if they ask. The source links above. Their later agreement is not pooled as corroboration.
3. **Stage 3, reusable method.** The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer. Each user must name their own ground and write their own refuter before running it (the formula's Direction rule: a frame cannot check the direction of its own question).

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. That is why `08_expert_verification_packet.md` carries the three checkable sub-claims before any theory, and why no numerical proximity is asserted anywhere in this folder.
