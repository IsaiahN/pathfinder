# Bonds, isomers and order in a persistent forest shift

**Mode: `specified`.** Supporting derivation to `01`, `05` and `07`. Simulated and checked for internal consistency against the figures; not a ground reading.

## RESIDUAL

The shape in `01` says a persistent shift is a threshold comparison driven by a self-amplifying feedback. Two things remain to be derived: (1) which operator, among the eight, actually carries "self-amplifying"; (2) whether a forest and its shifted state are **isomers** or merely members of a **functional group**, and at what order the comparison between Amazon and boreal sits.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms available for this derivation: tree cover, water, fire, canopy structure, species composition, soil, permafrost, land use. Bonds available: the seven binary bonds and the unary negation. No new primitive is available without import. The ground is the forest's observed persistence or shift; no instrument here returns it.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Which bond is a persistent shift?

- Fig 12 :76 "A produces B; B did not exist before" (the production bond ⇒). Test at Fig 12 :78: "is there a B before A fires? No."
- Börner and Dijkstra 2026, opened 2026-11-22, defines self-amplification as "a change in the observable causes further change of the observable in the same direction." That is production: a change produces more of the same kind of change. It is **not** simple sequence →, because in sequence the second term already waits to be read; in production it is absent until the first fires.
- Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
- Therefore a persistent shift is a **positive cycle of ⇒ bonds**. A forest that recovers is the same cycle with the opposite sign; the loop closes instead of running away. Fig 12 :112 "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction." The comparison ⋛ is the threshold; the subtraction − is how much margin remains.
- Negation is not a bond. A refused shift is ¬ applied to a held term (Fig 5 :53 "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"), not the absence of an operand. This matters because a forest that does not tip is not "less forest"; it is a term that refused a composition.

**Result:** the shift is `⇒` in a positive cycle; the threshold is `⋛`; the margin is `−`; the refusal of a shift is `¬`. **Mode: `specified`, framework-internal and checkable by reading Figures 12 and 5.**

### Q2: Isomer or functional group?

- Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :123 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- **Within one biome**, a forest state and its shifted state (forest and savanna in the Amazon; dense and open forest in the boreal) share nearly all atoms: same species pool, same soil, same water, same region. They differ in arrangement and bond: canopy closure, fire frequency, moisture recycling. Under Fig 12 they are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance. Magney and Pierrat 2025 reports a bimodal tree-cover distribution shifting toward an open state, which is exactly an arrangement change rather than an atom change. Lovejoy and Nobre 2018 reports the same for Amazon forest to savanna.
- **Across biomes**, Amazon and boreal do not share the atom set (different species, different water regime, permafrost present or absent). They are not isomers. They share a **functional group**: state → structure → state, with different members in the slots. Lenton et al. 2008 lists them as two separate tipping elements with different controls and different critical values, which is consistent with the functional-group reading.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either state can be written without an atom the other requires (for example, boreal open state with no permafrost history, or Amazon savanna with no moisture-recycling legacy), the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim stands.

### Q3: What is the order of the claim?

- Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"
- First order, inside one forest: `water availability → fire → tree cover` and `tree cover → canopy structure → water availability`. Each is a legal three-term chain across kinds if a structure sits between the states. Both first-order molecules can be settled against the ground by observation, so each can become an atom at the next level.
- Second order, across forests and systems: the comparison ⋛ of two settled first-order molecules, plus the management response. This is where "how close is the Amazon compared with the boreal forest" actually lives. It is a second-order composition, not a new primitive, and it needs no import.
- Fig 13 :75 "Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state." The boreal case makes this concrete: warming (a state-like driver) does not act on tree cover (a state) without an intervening structure such as fire regime, albedo, or water availability. Magney and Pierrat 2025 lists exactly those intermediaries.
- **Strain, named:** at second order the operands of ⋛ are settled molecules now serving as atoms. The Operators table defines ⋛ as "a threshold relation between two quantities." Whether a settled molecule counts as a quantity at that level needs an instrument, not an argument. This is the same strain recorded in `01` and `09`. It is not smoothed over.

### Q4: Why flicker is not the same as tip

- Fig 12 :119 "catalyst. A part that survives its own use." Fire is such a part in the boreal system: it persists across uses and changes the substrate it acts on.
- Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input." Repeated crossing between two attractors is flicker; permanent settlement in the alternative regime is the shift. Dakos et al. 2012 treats flickering as a warning that the alternative attractor exists, not as the transition itself. Hirsch and Koren 2026 similarly flags structural change and resilience loss separately and says their study "does not attempt to directly detect ecosystem collapse itself."
- Therefore a forest that flickers is not yet a forest that has shifted. Conflating the two is a category error of the same kind as reading a change point as a threshold, which Borner and Dijkstra 2026 explicitly warns against.

## CONCLUSION

A persistent shift is a positive cycle of production bonds (⇒) closed at a threshold comparison (⋛), with the remaining margin expressed as subtraction (−) and a refusal expressed as negation (¬). Forest and shifted state are isomers within a biome and members of one functional group across biomes. The claim about Amazon versus boreal is second order, licensed by the settled-molecule rule, and carries one live type strain on ⋛'s operands. None of this returns a proximity value. It returns the arrangement any proximity value must have.
