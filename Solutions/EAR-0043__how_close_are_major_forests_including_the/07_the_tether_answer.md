# The Tether's answer on forest closeness to persistent shifts

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md`), not a ground reading. No measurement of any forest was taken here.

## 1. The residual it answers

"How close" is the gap between a forest's observed state and an answer about a persistent shift in structure or function. The question is a **union compound**, not one hard problem: warming, water stress, fire, pests, permafrost and fragmentation overlap, and a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either (Fig 9 :54). The Amazon slot and the boreal slot are different object slots with different controls and different critical values. They must not be averaged.

## 2. The shape

1. **Closeness is per forest, never a global rank.** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Any answer that combines the Amazon and the boreal into one number has already lost the signal.
2. **A persistent shift is a bound ordinary term, not an intuition.** The definition used here, from Borner and Dijkstra 2026, is a persistent nonlinear transition driven by a self-amplifying feedback process. Abruptness and irreversibility are neither necessary nor sufficient. Fig 12 :112 "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction." The threshold is a comparison ⋛; the margin is a subtraction −; the self-amplification is a production cycle ⇒ (see `06_isomers_and_order.md`).
3. **The signal of approach is a residual that persists and does not trend down.** Fig 5 :59 "a term whose residual PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN is the one to point the refusal at." In the world literature this is critical slowing down: rising lag-1 autocorrelation, variance and flicker (Dakos et al. 2012; Hirsch and Koren 2026). Rizzi et al. 2025, in clinical neuroscience, finds the same structure indexing "the distance of the system from ictal threshold" better than counting events. **This is a candidate signal, not a certificate.** Xu et al. 2023 shows regime shifts that do not exhibit it.
4. **The habitat is enumerated and the compound unbundled.** Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :44 "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Lovejoy and Nobre 2018 reports negative synergies among deforestation, climate change and fire; Magney and Pierrat 2025 lists the boreal feedback set of fire, insects, albedo, water and permafrost.
5. **Corroboration is triangulation across low-overlap frames.** Fig 2 :31 "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 8 :45 "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." Ten studies of one satellite product are one witness. Field plots, flux towers, paleo records, experiments and satellite indices must range separately against the ground.
6. **The ground is the forest, and the channel decays.** Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." A loss of long-term monitoring changes our reading, not the forest. Magney and Pierrat 2025 documents the resolution, understory, snow and space-for-time limits that degrade the channel.
7. **The answer stops at a shape.** Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". Fig 13 :138 "narrowing gets you close while the ground decides". Fig 9 :44 "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing." The framework returns the arrangement an answer must have; it does not return "the Amazon is N years away."

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a proximity value, a date, or a rank of the world's forests. Those require a ground reading this derivation cannot perform.
- Not that any single indicator is the threshold. Vegetation indices are indirect proxies; Hirsch and Koren 2026 states that temporal autocorrelation "does not uniquely indicate proximity to a tipping point."
- Not that critical slowing down is universal. Xu et al. 2023 is preserved as a live limit, not smoothed away.
- Not that abruptness or irreversibility defines a shift. Borner and Dijkstra 2026 refuses that definition.
- Not that flicker equals shift. Dakos et al. 2012 treats flicker as evidence of an alternative attractor, not as settlement in it.
- Not that agreement among similar studies is corroboration. Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool".
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." For forests the anchor is observed persistence or observed regime settlement, not an index of it. The instruments that would close this are:

1. **A source-discriminating instrument.** Does a rising index reflect slowing recovery, a trend artifact, seasonal structure or sensor change? The *Hard Problem is About Source* essay's spectrometer is the model: an instrument that can distinguish sources, not a better argument.
2. **A persistence test.** Does the new regime last beyond the transition window on the timescale the question asks about? Borner and Dijkstra 2026 makes persistence part of the definition.
3. **A feedback test.** Is the change self-amplifying (⇒ production), or merely a linear response to a nonlinear forcing? Without this, it is not the shift being asked about.
4. **An interaction test.** Does a single control variable suffice, or do fire, water and fragmentation interact? Lovejoy and Nobre 2018 and Lenton et al. 2008 favour the interaction reading; this must be tested per forest.
5. **Standing falsifiers from `01`:** a better aggregated index; a shift with no flat persistent residual; two "independent" frames sharing one pool; one control variable for both biomes; a persistent but linear shift.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 6 :40 "What is unavailable is a sensor for something that has never registered anywhere, at any resolution." Here things *have* registered: resilience loss, browning, fire feedbacks. So this is not a claim that the question is unanswerable. It is a claim that the answer is not in this derivation. Fig 7 :13 "agreement compounds, evidence does not" reminds us that another review citing the same papers adds spelling, not evidence. The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable; *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not report how close the Amazon or the boreal forest is to a shift, and it does not rank them. It says that closeness is a per-forest threshold comparison, not a global score; that a persistent shift is a self-amplifying nonlinear transition, so abruptness and irreversibility are not required; that the approach should show a residual that persists and does not trend down, which is the shape the early-warning literature actually measures; that the drivers form a union of interacting conditions whose habitat must be enumerated, not composed; and that the only thing that settles the claim is the forest itself, ranged against by independent instruments rather than by agreement among studies sharing one satellite record. The early-warning signal is a candidate, not a certificate, because some regime shifts do not show it. What is offered is the shape any answer must have, five refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The ground has not spoken in this derivation, so the answer stops as `specified`, not `grounded`.
