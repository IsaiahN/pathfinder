# Expert verification packet: the Tether on forest closeness to persistent shifts

**What this is:** a claim in a form a forest ecologist, Earth-system modeller or resilience researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No proximity value for any forest is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a measured result.

**Status of this packet:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"How close is this forest to a persistent shift in structure or function?" has the following shape. It is indexed per forest, not aggregated into a global rank. A persistent shift is a nonlinear, self-amplifying transition, so abruptness and irreversibility are not defining features. Approach to such a shift should register as a residual that persists and does not trend down (slower recovery, rising autocorrelation, rising variance, flicker), but that signal is a candidate and not universal. The drivers form a union of interacting conditions whose habitat must be enumerated, and the evidence for closeness must be triangulated across methods that do not share one data pool. The ground is the forest's observed persistence or regime settlement; no index is the ground. The framework returns this arrangement, not a number of years.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: Slope and aggregation; checkable by reading Figure 1 and any forest dataset

- **Statement:** closeness must be assessed per object slot; a global average of major forests destroys the live signal.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Also Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."
- **What to check:** Do the Amazon and the boreal forest have different controls and critical values? Lenton et al. 2008 (PNAS) lists them as separate tipping elements with different controls and different estimated thresholds. If so, a single mean "closeness" cannot be the ground for either.
- **Refuter:** an aggregated index that predicts each forest's persistent shift better than per-forest indicators on held-out cases.

### C2: The early-warning signal; checkable against time-series method

- **Statement:** the approach signal is a residual that persists and does not trend down. This is the structural form of critical slowing down, and it is a candidate, not a certificate.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :59 "a term whose residual PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN is the one to point the refusal at" (the selector's shrinking test read with the opposite sign). Fig 13 :47 "What makes a gradient a gradient is that it can be spent, and neither an ordering nor a span of space has directedness."
- **What to check:** Dakos et al. 2012 (PLoS ONE) documents rising lag-1 autocorrelation, variance and flicker as generic leading indicators; Hirsch and Koren 2026 (iScience) finds such signals in about one quarter of ecoregions, including the Madeira-Tapajos moist forests, but states that temporal autocorrelation "does not uniquely indicate proximity to a tipping point"; Xu et al. 2023 (PNAS) states "regime shifts do not all exhibit critical slowing down." Is the indicator in a given forest discriminating among recovery slowdown, trend artifact, seasonality and sensor change?
- **Refuter:** an adequately instrumented forest that makes a persistent nonlinear transition with no prior flat, persistent residual; or a rising indicator with no state change.

### C3: Union, triangulation and definition; checkable against the forest's own feedback set

- **Statement:** the drivers form a union that must be unbundled, the frames must be independent, and the shift must be defined as persistent and self-amplifying.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 2 :31 "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." Borner and Dijkstra 2026 (Earth System Dynamics) defines a tipping event as "a persistent nonlinear transition driven by a self-amplifying feedback process."
- **What to check:** Lovejoy and Nobre 2018 (Science Advances) reports negative synergies among deforestation, climate change and fire in the Amazon. Magney and Pierrat 2025 (PNAS) lists fire, insects, albedo, water and permafrost as interacting boreal feedbacks and documents satellite resolution and space-for-time limits. Are the studies being counted as independent actually drawing on one satellite product or one model ensemble?
- **Refuter:** one control variable with no interaction terms predicting shifts in both biomes at comparable skill; or two "independent" frames shown to share one evidence pool and counted twice; or an observed persistent but linear shift with no self-amplifying feedback.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a proximity value, date, or ranking of forests.
- Not that critical slowing down is universal, or that any one indicator is a threshold.
- Not that abruptness or irreversibility is required for a tipping event.
- Not that flicker is the same as a completed shift.
- Not that satellite indices are the ground; they are instruments with documented limits.
- Not that agreement among studies using the same data is corroboration. Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool".
- Not `grounded`. No measurement in this packet settles a forest's state.
- No request for agreement or trust, and no weight assigned to the reviewer's prestige.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not more agreement. Concretely:

1. An instrument that distinguishes recovery slowdown from trend, seasonal structure and sensor change (the spectrometer test from *The Hard Problem is About Source*).
2. A persistence test showing the new regime lasts beyond the transition window.
3. A feedback test showing the change is self-amplifying rather than linear.
4. An interaction test showing whether one control variable suffices.
5. Any of the five refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` firing.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19 and :22, Fig 5 :59, Fig 9 :55, Fig 11 :43 and Fig 2 :31 (five short passages).
2. Look at one forest you know: are the drivers a union, and is there one control or several interacting ones?
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish slowing recovery from a trend artifact in your system, or say why none exists.
4. Say whether "persistent nonlinear transition driven by self-amplifying feedback" is the right thing to be measuring.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
