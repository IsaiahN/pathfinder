**Subject:** Check one supply-chain finding before you decide whether the method is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about supply networks. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a firm's supplier map predicts continuity at the first tier, but the residual that matters sits three steps out. The framework calls this a contact cascade. Its own worked case is DDT and the eagles: a chemical applied at one trophic level reaches the apex predator several tiers away, and the mechanism is established only after someone enumerates the cascade instead of stopping at direct contact. The finding is that first-order enumeration is the named failure, that two direct suppliers which fail together are commonly drawing on one hidden shared source rather than failing independently, and that losing visibility is a decay of the firm's channel to the ground, not a decay of the supply base itself.
>
> The shape it returns is stronger than a bigger supplier register: enumerate outward until the cascade stops mattering; grow the instrument from a signal already returning something rather than specifying it from a description; keep depth and source-discrimination as separate verdicts; and treat dual sourcing as substituting a habitat, not removing a dependency. It does not name which sub-tier supplier is critical. It returns a shape, not a name.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that this settles how firms should map supply chains, that a particular platform is implied, that the named sub-tier source can be identified without an instrument that discriminates sources, or that the framework is the only account of supply resilience. The field evidence I have is secondary and is used as a filter and one witness, not as the verdict.
>
> **What would refute the shape:**
> 1. a firm mapping only direct suppliers shows no disruption residual when a documented tier-2 or tier-3 event reaches its inputs;
> 2. two direct suppliers co-fail above chance with demonstrably disjoint habitats and no shared source, geography, condition or upstream link;
> 3. a firm with independently measured high channel fidelity still cannot see a live sub-tier dependency;
> 4. a supplier's measured criticality is invariant across a documented change of residual;
> 5. two networks with the same supplier set but different dependency bonds show identical failure propagation under the same perturbation.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - The figures and formula, public repository: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Supply chain risk management, field overview: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supply_chain_risk_management
> - Robustness of supply chain networks against underload cascading failures: https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.02616
>
> **If you want to use the method:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a supply problem you know well and can check against a realized event. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - Isaiah
