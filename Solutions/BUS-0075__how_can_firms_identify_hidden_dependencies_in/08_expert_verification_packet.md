# Expert verification packet: the Tether on hidden supply-chain dependencies

**What this is:** a claim in a form a supply chain researcher or resilience practitioner can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified`: not `grounded`.** No realized supply event is claimed to have settled this. It is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A firm identifies dependencies beyond its direct suppliers by enumerating the habitat outward from the failed node until the cascade stops mattering, not by enlarging a tier-1 register. Co-failure of two "independent" suppliers is commonly a hidden shared source, and lost visibility is decay of the firm's channel to the realized supply event, not decay of the supply base. The instrument that resolves the source must be grown from a signal already returning something; inbound and outbound tracks are kept separate. Criticality is relative to the residual, a bill of materials records ingredients but not the bonds that decide propagation, and dual sourcing substitutes a habitat rather than removing dependency. The two failure conditions, depth and discrimination, are separate verdicts and are never averaged. The framework does not name which node is critical: it returns a shape, and the name requires an instrument that discriminates sources.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 11 and 2

- **Statement:** depth is a contact cascade; first-order enumeration is the named failure, and co-failure commonly reflects a hidden shared source rather than two independent suppliers.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :52: *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* Also Fig 11 :44: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."* And Fig 2 :6-7: *"collapse 2: common cause"*, *"(hidden shared source)"*.
- **Refuter:** a firm mapping only tier 1 that shows no residual under a disruption demonstrably reaching its tier-2 or tier-3 inputs; or two direct suppliers co-failing above chance with demonstrably disjoint habitats and no shared source, geography, condition or upstream link.

### C2: field-facing; checkable against operations and network data

- **Statement:** two supply webs with the same supplier set and different dependency bonds are isomers with different failure behaviour; the bond, not the ingredient count, carries the propagation information. Backup suppliers and surplus inventory act as recovery structure, which is why adding them improves robustness without proving the dependency is gone.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 13 :24: *"that entities relate, and influence travels."*
- **What to check:** in a real network dataset, do two topologies over the same supplier set propagate differently under the same perturbation? Yang, Scoglio and Gruenbacher (arXiv:1908.02616, Physica A 563 (2021) 125466) report that *"the adverse effects of any disruption can be amplified and spread throughout the systems"* and that *"recovery strategies of surplus inventory and backup suppliers often adopted in actual SCs can enhance the system robustness, compared with the system without the recovery process"*. That is corroboration at a different stratum, not the verdict.
- **Refuter:** identical propagation under the same perturbation for two webs with the same supplier set and materially different bonds; or a supplier substitution that reproduces no required condition, introduces no unchosen actor with a cascade, and passes a full disruption cycle with no residual.

### C3: instrument-facing; checkable by anyone who has run a mapping exercise

- **Statement:** a discriminator that names the hidden source cannot be specified from a description alone. It is grown from a signal already returning something, inward (a lead-time variance, a near-miss, a sub-tier invoice, a shared price move) or outward (a differing frame such as the supplier's own map, logistics data, a registry), and the two tracks are not read against each other. The channel decays; the ground does not.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* Fig 6 :35: *"the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."* Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*, with *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"* (Fig 10 :26).
- **Refuter:** a visibility instrument specified from a description alone, with no prior signal, that names a held-out hidden dependency later confirmed at the ground; or a firm with independently verified high channel fidelity that still cannot see a live sub-tier dependency.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a theory of everything and not a final answer. This is a shape generator applied to one question.
- Not that a named platform, framework or vendor is the answer. Prestige can fund a mapping attempt; it cannot settle it (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action... But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."*).
- Not that a bigger tier-1 register closes the question; that is the first-order enumeration Fig 11 :52 names as the failure.
- Not that a specific sub-tier supplier is critical. The framework returns a shape, not a name; the source-discriminating instrument is not held here.
- Not that dual sourcing removes a dependency. Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."*
- Not `grounded`. No realized supply event was measured for this packet.

## 5. What would settle it

A realized supply event, not agreement. In particular:

1. **Depth test:** does a tier-1-only map show a live residual when a documented tier-2 or tier-3 event reaches the firm's inputs?
2. **Shared-source test:** with provenance, do co-failing direct suppliers share a sub-tier source, geography or condition?
3. **Channel test:** with fresh, unpooled, unsaturated readings independently verified, is the dependency still invisible?
4. **Isomer test:** do identical supplier sets with different bonds propagate differently under one perturbation?

1. Read Fig 11 :52-53 and Fig 11 :43-44, then Fig 2 :6-7 and Fig 2 :18-21. Five short passages.
