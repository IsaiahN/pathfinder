# WATSON answer, BUS-0075

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** Figures are primary and decide. World sources are secondary, corroborate but never overrule, and a conflict returns the derivation to the domain census. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## QUESTION

How can firms identify hidden dependencies in supply chains several tiers beyond their direct suppliers?

Restated as an effect: why does R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| stay near zero at tier 1 and go live at tier 2 and beyond, and what changes that? R is indexed per node and per tier, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22). Anchor: the realized supply event, one of the anchors named in Fig 2 :29. Option A: hidden dependencies are a data-volume problem, solved by a bigger tier-1 register plus a visibility platform. Option B: they are a contact-and-discrimination problem, solved by enumerating the habitat outward and by an instrument that separates a shared source from independent failure. What each leads to: A enlarges the map while the residual sits outside it; B returns a shape with falsifiers and an open name until an instrument discriminates sources.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Arity: a relation over supplier nodes and directed dependencies, indexed by tier; the object of interest is the k-th order neighbour. Carries an ordered bond and a production bond, not symmetric ones (Fig 12 :72, :76). Symmetry: "hidden" and "critical" are relative to the focal firm's residual, not properties a supplier carries (Fig 11 :49). Scale: single node, tier, whole web; the membrane between tiers is lossy (Fig 4 :21, :30).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The figure-derived verdict: hidden dependencies are found by enumerating the habitat outward from the failed node until the cascade stops mattering, by keeping the channel to the realized supply event reachable with fresh and unpooled readings, by discriminating a hidden shared source from independent failure using an instrument grown from a signal already returning something, by keeping depth and source discrimination as separate verdicts, and by treating dual sourcing as habitat substitution. Criticality is relative to the residual; the bill of materials records ingredients but not the bonds that decide propagation. The two conditions are kept separate per domain and never averaged. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate. The field sources corroborate at different strata and were used as filters and at most two witnesses; the DDT overlap was declared. Which specific node is critical is a declared `gap`, not a hidden answer.

- **cost if wrong:** misdirected diagnosis and spend. A firm failing C2 would be sold a deeper map, or a firm failing C1 would be told to build a discriminator, and a single lever would be applied to a union that needs two. In outreach terms, an expert's twenty minutes would be spent refuting a shape that was never the right shape. The cost is real but bounded and largely reversible if the two verdicts stay separate.
- **undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails; a refutation is added and the reach falls because *"something was added rather than because anything was deleted"* (Fig 6 :42-43). The shape and its falsifiers are recorded in a provenance trail so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-64).
- **reversed by:** any of the pre-registered falsifiers, in particular (1) no residual in a tier-1-only map under a disruption that reached tier-2 or tier-3 inputs, (2) co-failure above chance with disjoint habitats, (3) high channel fidelity with a still-invisible dependency, (4) a description-only instrument naming a held-out dependency at ground, or (5) identical propagation for identical supplier sets under different bonds. A source-discriminating instrument at the sub-tier level would also move the name from `gap` to a held term. Prestige or agreement is not such an instrument.
