# The Tether's answer on hidden dependencies beyond direct suppliers

**Mode: `specified`: not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A firm's supplier map predicts continuity at tier 1 and goes quiet at tier 2 and beyond. The residual R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| is indexed per node and per tier, never averaged into an enterprise risk score (Fig 1 :19, :22). The anchor is the realized supply event, one of the anchors named in Fig 2 :29: *"A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* The question is a **union compound**, not one hard problem: a depth failure and a discrimination failure are two conditions, not different degrees of one (Fig 9 :55, :57).

## 2. The shape

1. **Enumerate the habitat outward; first-order enumeration is the named failure.** Fig 11 :52: *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* Fig 11 :44: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."* Include actors, conditions and dependency relations (Fig 11 :46-47), not just supplier legal entities.
2. **The ground is the realized event; the map is an instrument.** Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*, with decay modes *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"* (Fig 10 :26). Fig 2 :30: *"you push on it, it is not an anchor."* A risk score moves when pushed; a stopped line does not.
3. **Co-failure is commonly a hidden shared source, not two independent suppliers.** Fig 2 :6-7 names *"collapse 2: common cause"* and *"(hidden shared source)"*; Fig 2 :20-21: *"so the agreement is the pool talking, / not two frames confirming each other"*. Two direct suppliers that fail together are usually drawing on one sub-tier source, one raw material, one geography or one condition.
4. **Grow the instrument from a signal already returning something.** Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* Run inward (extend a weak lead-time, near-miss, invoice or price-move signal) and outward (import a supplier's supplier map, logistics data or a registry) as two tracks and *"never read one against the other"* (Fig 6 :31).
5. **The bond carries what the ingredient list omits.** Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Two webs with the same supplier set and different sub-tier bonds are isomers (Fig 12 :117), and the difference is decisive in failure behaviour.
6. **Criticality is relative to the residual.** Fig 11 :49-51: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* There is no standing list of critical suppliers; ask critical for what.
7. **Dual sourcing substitutes a habitat; it does not remove the dependency.** Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* The two silent failures are *"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"* and *"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"* (Fig 11 :68-69).
8. **Keep the two conditions separate.** C1 (contact depth) and C2 (source discrimination) have different instruments. A blended risk score is a pooled reading (Fig 10 :26) and a frame-internal quantity (Fig 1 :27).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not which specific sub-tier supplier is critical. The framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32); the source-discriminating instrument is not held here.
- Not that a bigger supplier register closes the question. That is first-order enumeration (Fig 11 :52).
- Not that a visibility platform can be specified from first principles. Fig 6 :33: *"Nobody specified a telescope from an account of Mars."*
- Not that dual sourcing removes risk. It substitutes a habitat (Fig 11 :66).
- Not `grounded`. No realized supply event settles `left(R,φ)` in this derivation.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*, an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer, not an argument, resolved that case. Here the corresponding questions are:

- **Depth test:** under a documented tier-2 or tier-3 disruption that reached the firm's inputs, does a tier-1-only map show a live residual? If no, F1 is broken.
- **Shared-source test:** do two co-failing direct suppliers share a sub-tier source, geography or condition, with provenance for the shared atom? If they co-fail with disjoint habitats above chance, F2 is broken.
- **Channel test:** with fresh, unpooled, unsaturated readings independently verified, is the sub-tier dependency still invisible? If yes, F3 is broken.
- **Proto-instrument test:** can an instrument specified from a description alone, with no prior signal, name a held-out dependency confirmed at the ground? If yes, F4 is broken.
- **Isomer test:** do two webs with identical supplier sets and different bonds propagate identically under the same perturbation? If yes, F6 is broken.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* A search that finds no hidden dependency at this budget has not proved there is none; it may be that the instrument lacks the resolution. The six-term set in the framework is *"a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes"* in the canonical reading; declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name the hidden supplier, and it does not claim that a bigger vendor list solves the problem. It says that dependency visibility is a contact cascade: a firm that enumerates only direct suppliers has done first-order enumeration, and the residual that matters is three steps out, exactly as DDT reached the eagles. Two suppliers that fail together are commonly drawing on one hidden shared source, so their co-failure is the pool talking, not two independent events. What is lost is the firm's channel to the realized supply event, not the supply base itself, and a new instrument has to be grown from a signal already returning something, not specified from a description. The bill of materials records ingredients, while the sub-tier bond carries the information that decides propagation. Dual sourcing substitutes a habitat rather than removing a dependency. What the method offers is the shape any answer must have, the two conditions kept separate, and falsifiers written in advance. The name stays open until an instrument discriminates sources.
