## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)". Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 says the system "cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it".

From *The Library of Babel*: "You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question:

- "computation is an intrinsic property of some processes" is a **name**, not a shape, because it picks a property without saying how it would be detected;
- "computation is whatever an observer maps onto inputs and outputs" is also a name, because it collapses the discriminator into a choice;
- "a process computes under a mapping whose transitions are lawfully mirrored, whose structure is non-trivial, whose description pays, and which the ground settles" is a **shape**: four conditions a candidate must satisfy, with no assertion that any particular process satisfies them.

That last form is what `07_the_tether_answer.md` offers.

## 2. How the shape is obtained: proximity, not definition

*The Library of Babel* gives the method: use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, look for recurring terms that overlap with it, and recurse. "With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary." The anchor is the question itself, not a preferred answer.

Fig 6 :30: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."

*They're Scanning Books* supplies the bootstrap vocabulary for this specific question: "A corpus is the compressed output of human minds; the capacity that produced it is a generator, and outputs do not contain their generator." That is the effect-term beginning. We are given a behaviour (a trace) and asked for its source. We cannot read the source off the trace.

*Decoding Tacit Knowledge* states the transfer rule: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." It also gives the halting-case precedent: "you can't decide from inside whether the computation ends. You need to run it, or step outside it."

The shape is obtained by asking what would have to be observable if the word "computation" were earned, then stopping when further questions stop registering against that observation. The Library of Babel's stopping condition applies: "you will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place".

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." The README: the framework stops "at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest".
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*They're Scanning Books* sharpens the second: "The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space." And: "A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six." And: "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."

So the six-term substrate is a visible guess, not nature's own list. Turning it into a definition of computation would turn a visible choice into an invisible one.

## 4. Why this question is a source question

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism, and it matches this question more closely than any other essay:

> "The Problem: You can have several underlying causes that create identical readings but the definition of the output would be read as the same."
> "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"
> "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it."

The colour case: a spectrometer reads green, but the reading cannot tell you whether the source was one green light, yellow plus blue, RGB, CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. "It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."

The computation case is the same shape. An input-output trace can be produced by a laptop running an algorithm, by a rock under a disjunctive interpretation, by a lookup table, or by a process nobody has yet classified. The trace cannot comment on the source. The question "which processes compute?" is therefore a **source question**: what is owed is an instrument that discriminates among interpretations, not a further definition. Until such an instrument exists, the honest report is "below the union horizon", not "settled".

*How Intelligence Actually Works* makes the same point for the neighbouring category: "The mapping is not a means to intelligence; the mapping is the intelligence. And that mapping is not a tree with roots and branches, it is a web, with no canonical order and no single path through." And: "indiscernibility is not identity". The computation label must be carried by the lineage of the mapping, not read off the trace.

## 5. Does "shape, not a name" forbid naming the discriminator? Correction to my own §1 and §3

I initially treated the rule as a blanket ban on naming anything in this question. That was an overstatement, and the same correction was made in the exemplar.

The README's phrase is "what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." Its subject is a primitive not held in Γ. The four conditions in `01` F9 are not a new primitive. They are a **binding** of figure-held terms (lawfulness, MDL bargain, cross-kind composition, ground settlement) onto a term from the philosophy-of-computation frame. Naming the conditions is licensed as a provenanced binding; claiming the binding adds an atom is not (Fig 6: "IMPORT adds an atom", and this one adds none).

What remains forbidden:

- naming a seventh substrate term that would make computation a new kind of thing;
- claiming the framework itself has settled the philosophical question (Fig 8: "A frame cannot certify its own limit");
- reading the source off the trace, which is the metamerism error in §4.

Both readings are kept here so neither is silently lost. The narrowed form governs the rest of the folder.

## 6. Prestige is not action

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*:

> "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly"
> "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."

A campaign that gathers famous names to agree that a definition is correct is the named collapse. *How a Society of Agents Would Work* gives the same warning in group form: "A group is only worth having while its members stay different." and "Ten copies watching the same thing are one opinion said ten times." The legitimate ask is the opposite: give the shape a budget to be tried by readers in different closures, and let an instrument settle it.

## 7. This audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

Same essay: "the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself." And: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post. It will often be better than the generic outputs that you will usually get otherwise too. However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."

*Arrival of the Fittest* adds: "Because it is a metatheory, it cannot be validated, nor can it validate itself." And the warning on simulated outputs: they "could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula."

So `01` through `09` are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are **not** a test of the claim and must be labelled as such. They cannot settle anything they report on.
