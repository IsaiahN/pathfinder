# Expert verification packet: the Tether on physical computation

**What this is:** a claim in a form a philosopher of computation, a natural-computing researcher or a physicist of information can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the structural move, `specified` on the four conditions, not `grounded`.** No measurement discriminating among interpretations of one behaviour is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a definition.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "which physical processes qualify as computation rather than merely evolving according to rules?" is the wrong question in the same sense that "what is intelligence?" is the wrong question: it asks for a property of the process alone. Rule-following is necessary but not sufficient, because every ordinary open physical system implements some automaton under some interpretation. The answerable question is what makes a process capable of sustaining a computational description that the ground pays for. The shape of the answer has four conditions: the mapping's transitions are lawful and support counterfactuals; the process has non-trivial combinatorial structure so the mapping is not a trivial clock-and-dial disjunction; the mapping pays under a description-length bargain; and the ground, not observer agreement, decides which mappings pay. Computation is a route and a composition-level remainder, not a new kind of thing; processes that qualify form a functional group with the substrate as qualifier. The framework returns this shape, not a name, and it does not say which particular process computes.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it.

1. **Counterfactual test:** does the process's causal structure support the formal transitions under inputs that did not occur on the observed run?
2. **Trace test:** is there any observable at any resolution that distinguishes a trivial disjunctive mapping from a combinatorial one? If none, the case stays below the union horizon.
3. **Bargain test:** under a declared code, does the mapping score better than the confusion it removes?
4. **Thermodynamic test:** does logical reversibility of the mapping track thermodynamic reversibility of the hardware? Landauer/Bennett say they can differ.
