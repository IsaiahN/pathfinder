## 0. The claim under audit

The question, in effect terms, asks for a discriminator. Both a falling stone and a running laptop evolve according to rules. The word "computation" is applied to one and withheld from the other. What must be present for the label to apply?

Three candidate answers, kept separate and never averaged:

| candidate | claim | where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| **A, intrinsic property** | some physical processes have a property (discreteness, symbol manipulation, a processor) that others lack; computation is detected by inspecting the process | physical dynamics frame |
| **B, observer mapping** | any process can be a computation under a chosen interpretation of its states as inputs and outputs; the label is a convention | formal / observer frame |
| **C, contact property** | a process computes relative to a mapping only when the mapping is paid by the ground: its transitions are lawful, its structure is non-trivial, and it explains more than it states | union of physical and formal frames, settled by a ground |

**What each leads to.** A makes this a detection problem (find the distinguishing property). B makes it a convention (the question dissolves into a choice of description, and every rock implements every finite automaton). C makes it a contact question (which processes can sustain a mapping that the ground pays for). The question's own wording, "rather than merely evolving according to rules", already concedes that rule-following is not the discriminator. That concession is the residual.

### 0.1 Residual stated in effect terms

R: the gap between "a process evolving according to rules" and "a process computing". Arity: two items being compared. Symmetry: the mapping from physical states to computational states is many-to-one (one trace, many formal descriptions), so the relation is not symmetric. Scale: substrate processes exist at any scale; the label "computation" is level-relative, because a settled composition becomes an atom at the next level (Fig 12 :104).

The residual is **not** "what is computation?" That is the substrate question. It is: what would have to be true of a process-and-mapping for the label to be paid by something other than agreement?

### 0.2 Domain census (before any figure line was chosen)

One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor. No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | nameable anchor? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Physical dynamics | physical states, space, time, energy, spectrum, gradient, network, the dynamical law | prediction minus observation, `R` | measurement / experiment | **yes**, an instrument that returns something |
| 2 Formal / semantic | formal states, inputs, outputs, transition table, the interpretation mapping | whether the mapping pays (description length) | none inside the frame | **no**, so domain 2 alone is not a domain yet; it becomes one only in union |
| 3 Observer / verification | the observer choosing the mapping, expert readers | agreement versus residual | the ground, never agreement | **yes** only if a source-discriminating instrument exists; otherwise the question is a convention |
| 4 Framework-internal (Tether) | six terms, seven bonds plus negation, Γ, closure, mint, import | composition, residual, settlement | figures as metalaws, plus the ground for a measurement | **yes**, the figure text and the ground |

**Partition or union?** This is a **union compound**, not a partition and not one hard question. It requires a physical process AND a mapping AND an observer who fixes the mapping. Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either". Each condition has its own loop. Unbundle before searching, or a single lever will clear the region where only one condition holds and leave the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55-56).

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Settle the substrate first (what is the process we can actually perceive), then the vocabulary (can a state afford a mapping, can its transitions be lawful), then the objective (what would count as a computation), then the check. Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28). Refuse before mint (`THE_FORMULA.md` step 2).

**Strain test applied during this census.** Fig 11 is about intelligence, not computation. Does it strain to apply it here? No: the figure's subject is any label applied afterwards to a process that met a ground, and Fig 11 :41 explicitly generalises the move ("the same move as asking what generates lift"). The specific content (selection, lineage) does not transfer; the move does. Recorded as `general`, not as a forced analogy. The one genuine fork, Fig 2 :36 (anchor legitimacy), is handled in F5 rather than smoothed over.

## 2. Findings

### F1: The question has the same form as "what is intelligence?" (decisive, `general`)

Fig 11 :34-36:

> **"What is intelligence?"**
> **"The wrong question, and this figure is the replacement for it."**
> **"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."**

Fig 11 :39:

> **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**

Fig 11 :41:

> **"Which is the same move as asking what generates lift rather than whether a thing is a bird. The substrate question dissolves; the contact question does not."**

The computation question asks for a property of the process considered alone ("which physical processes qualify"). That is exactly the substrate question the figure dissolves. The answerable question is: what makes a physical process capable of sustaining a computational description that the ground pays for? Capability is a property of process-and-mapping-and-ground, never of the process alone.

Fig 11 :40 supplies the test:

> **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

Refuter: show that computation is an intrinsic, ground-visible property of a process independent of any mapping and any ground contact. If such a property is demonstrated, F1 fails and the substrate question stands. Until then the move is `general` and structural, not a definition.

### F2: Rule-following is necessary but not sufficient (`general`, triangulated)

No figure line makes rule-following sufficient for computation. Fig 13 :115:

> **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."**

Every physical process lies in the arrangements; the label does not fall out of belonging to the set. The world source corroborates at maximum independence: Chalmers (1996, *Synthese*) reports Putnam's theorem that "every ordinary open physical system implements every finite-state automaton." If rule-following were sufficient, every rock would compute every automaton, and the question would be trivial. It is not trivial, so rule-following is not sufficient.

Triangulation status: `triangulated` (Fig 13 closure/arrangements plus Chalmers, low overlap between a framework figure and an independent philosophical derivation). Verdict: rule-following is necessary, not sufficient.

### F3: The observer's declaration does not ground the label (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 10 :18: **"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"**. Fig 10 :49: **"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."** Fig 10 :47: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**.

This is the direct answer to candidate B. An observer who declares "this voltage is bit 1, that voltage is bit 0" has authored a convention. Fig 10 :18 says the declaration is not a grounding. If the convention then decides whether the process counts as computing, it has stopped being a convention and become an ungoverned verdict.

World corroboration from a different field: the SEP entry on Turing Machines notes that to speak of a machine computing addition "we will have to provide an interpretation of the symbols recorded on the tape... then we will need to describe how to interpret the ones and zeros appearing on the tape as numbers." An interpretation is required, but requiredness is not sufficiency. Chalmers again: "the requirement that in order for a physical system to implement a formal automaton, causal relations among states of a physical system must mirror formal relations among states of the automaton." A declaration alone does not make the causal relations mirror anything.

Refuter: demonstrate a mapping whose declaration alone is sufficient, with no further condition on the process.

### F4: The discriminator is the MDL bargain applied to the mapping (`specified`, triangulated)

Fig 5 :26: **"and then: does the new term cost less to state than the confusion it removes?"**

Fig 5 :42-44:

> **"The acceptance test is a bargain, not a threshold."**
> **"A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before. A term that explains everything by saying nothing fails this, and so does a term that explains one occasion perfectly."**

Apply this to the mapping, not to the physical process. The interpretation that makes a rock implement a prime-testing automaton by adding a clock, a dial, and an input memory (Chalmers 1996, reporting Putnam) "explains everything by saying nothing": it buys the formal description by adding bookkeeping that carries no causal work. It fails the bargain. The interpretation that makes a laptop add two numbers pays: it compresses the observed trace into a rule that predicts unobserved inputs, and the causal transitions in the silicon carry the counterfactuals.

Fig 1 :27: **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."** An interpretation cannot be licensed by the fact that it makes the process look computational. That is the frame scoring itself.

Refuter: exhibit a computation whose mapping demonstrably fails the description-length bargain, or a merely-rule-governed process whose trivial mapping pays it. Either breaks the discriminator.

### F5: The anchor is question-dependent, and that is a fork, not a contradiction (`general`)

Fig 2 :29-30:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."**

Fig 2 :36: **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."**

This line initially looked like a strain. It is not. If the question is "which interpretation shall we adopt?", that is constitutively a question about a convention, and the choosing observer is the legitimate anchor. If the question is "can this process sustain lawful transitions under a non-trivial mapping?", that is constitutively about the world, and the anchor must be an instrument. The two branches answer different questions. This is exactly the system-versus-observer split in source questions 72 and 80, and it must be reported as a fork with two verdicts, never averaged into one.

Fig 2 :40: **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**. A committee of observers agreeing that a process "really computes" is a convention being negotiated, not a measurement (Fig 2 :35, cited in README).

Refuter: show a case where observer agreement about a label is itself the ground for a physical fact about the process. By Fig 2 :29 the anchor would then move when frames push on it.

### F6: Computation lives in the union, not in either frame (`general`, triangulated)

Fig 8 :45-46:

> **"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."**
> **"Composition crosses the boundary between them, and that surplus is where imports live."**

The physical frame sees dynamics without semantics. The formal frame sees a transition table without a substrate. Their union has a surplus: a process under a mapping. Fig 8 :29 requires low overlap, "low overlap: B does not draw on A's evidence". A mapping that simply restates the physical trace in new words is high overlap and produces no surplus, which is another way of stating F4's failure case.

Fig 6 :14 calls import "the only operator that moves the wall" and Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."** Asking a computation question of physics imports the semantic frame. Searching physics longer cannot manufacture it (Fig 8 :32: "no search, however long, adds an atom").

Refuter: derive the semantic frame entirely from the physical closure with no import, or show the union has no surplus in any case.

### F7: Computation is a route and a remainder, not a primitive (`general`, triangulated)

Fig 12 :111-113:

> **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**
> **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."**

A computation is a route through states under inputs, not a state. It needs ordered bonds. The Operators table defines sequence as "A then B; B needs A to have happened" and production as "A produces B; B did not exist before". A bare state-to-state mapping under a symmetric identity bond ("two names, one referent", Operators table) names a state and cannot name the route. This is the precise structural form of the metamerism error.

Fig 13 :124-125:

> **"Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are each what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member."**

Computation is the same kind of thing: what a particular composition (a lawful, non-trivial, paying mapping) leaves behind. It is not a seventh substrate term and not a new substance. Fig 13 :115 says the set is closed and the arrangements are not; computation belongs to the arrangements.

Refuter: add a seventh primitive that is load-bearing in an established law and cannot be composed from the six, or show that computation is a state rather than a route.

### F8: The sought discriminator is a preference, so it must be bound before it can be refused (`general`)

Fig 5 :61-62:

> **"BUT THE TERM MOST WORTH REFUSING MAY NOT BE IN THE LIBRARY AT ALL."**
> **"A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term. It is not bound, it predicts nothing, and it accrues no residual, so nothing here can see it."**

The framework cannot see its own preferred definition of computation. The remedy is the "Direction" discipline in `THE_FORMULA.md`: write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else. That is why the README binds the answer shape before any derivation, and why `08_expert_verification_packet.md` states its refuter before its conclusion.

Refuter: a definition of computation offered without a written-down failure condition is not checkable and is refused at this step.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Required before any conclusion, per the seat map's rule that "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device" (quoted in the exemplar's `01` §3). Written here before §4 and before the WATSON run.

The shape claim in `07` is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Intrinsic property found.** A physical property is demonstrated that is necessary and sufficient for computation and is ground-visible independently of any interpretation or measuring convention. This would make computation a detected property of the process and refute F1 and F9.
2. **Pure convention wins.** It is shown that any arbitrary mapping, including Putnam's disjunctive rock mapping, is sufficient, so the lawfulness and description-length conditions in F4 do no work. This would make computation a convention and leave candidate B standing.
3. **Lawful mirroring not necessary.** A process is demonstrated to compute under a mapping whose formal transitions are not lawfully mirrored in its causal structure, with the demonstration surviving an independent check. This would refute the Chalmers condition that F3 and F4 rely on.
4. **Figure strain confirmed.** A re-census shows that Fig 11's move governs only intelligence and cannot govern the computation question, or that the MDL bargain in Fig 5 governs only term-minting inside Γ and not the application of a term to a process. That sends the derivation back to the census, it does not soften the reading.
5. **No ground, no stop.** An instrument is exhibited that discriminates among interpretations of the same behaviour, and it returns a result that contradicts the contact verdict. The question would then move from `candidate` toward `grounded` in a direction the figures did not predict.

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or rewritten and the retraction log in §6 is updated. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Lead with F1: the question as framed is the wrong question, in the same sense that "what is intelligence?" is the wrong question. Answer the contact question instead.
2. Report F5 as a fork, not a verdict. System property and observer convention answer different questions. Do not average them.
3. Use F4's four-part discriminator as the checkable shape: lawful transitions, non-trivial combinatorial structure, paying description length, ground settlement.
4. Say plainly when the answer stops. No available instrument decides which mappings pay in general. Fig 13 :134-135: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence, It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."
5. Send the generator, not the conclusion. Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." The two-stage offer in `02_outreach_email.md` puts the refuter in front of the links.
