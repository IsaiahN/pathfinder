# Outreach draft: check the discriminator first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the stage-1 shape claim below survives the recipient's own check. If it does not survive, there is nothing else to show them.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about what makes a physical process a computation

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question in physical computation and I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to agree with anything, and I am not asking you to read a theory first. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question in effect terms.** A stone and a laptop both evolve according to rules. We call one a computation and withhold the word from the other. Rule-following is therefore not the discriminator. Three answers are on the table: (1) computation is an intrinsic physical property of some processes; (2) computation is whatever an observer chooses to map onto inputs and outputs, so any rock implements any automaton under some interpretation; (3) computation is a contact property, a process under a mapping that the ground pays for.
>
> **The stage-1 claim (`general` on the move, `specified` on the conditions, not `grounded`).** The question as framed is the wrong question. It has the same form as "what is intelligence?": it asks for a property of the process considered alone. The answerable question is what makes a process capable of sustaining a computational description that the ground pays for. The shape of that answer has four conditions:
> 1. the mapping's state transitions are lawful, meaning the causal structure of the process mirrors the formal transitions, including transitions that did not occur on the actual run;
> 2. the process has non-trivial combinatorial structure, so the mapping is not a trivial disjunction of states with a clock and a dial bolted on;
> 3. the mapping pays under a description-length bargain: stating it plus what remains unexplained costs less than the confusion it removes;
> 4. the ground settles which mappings pay, not the agreement of observers.
>
> I am not claiming this settles the philosophical question. I am claiming it is a checkable shape with a written failure condition.
>
> **What would refute it.** I wrote these down before sending this:
> 1. a physical property is demonstrated that is necessary and sufficient for computation independently of any mapping and any ground contact;
> 2. an arbitrary mapping is shown to be sufficient, so the lawfulness and description-length conditions do no work;
> 3. a process is shown to compute under a mapping whose formal transitions are not lawfully mirrored in its causal structure;
> 4. the move from the intelligence figure is shown to govern only intelligence, not computation.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the shape. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I have a new definition of computation; that this is the only account; that any particular physical system is or is not computing; that the framework has settled anything. Simulation is not measurement here.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Chalmers, "Does a Rock Implement Every Finite-State Automaton?", *Synthese* 108:309-33, 1996: https://consc.net/papers/rock.html
> - Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "The Computational Theory of Mind": https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/computational-mind/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks for reading,
> [name]

## Stage 1 and stage 2 are separate

Stage 1 is the residual, the four-condition shape, and the refuter. Record the recipient's verdict on stage 1 **before** showing any framework material. Stage 2, opt-in only, is the three links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"). A reply is a conversation, not a witness.
