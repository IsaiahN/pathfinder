# The Tether's answer: what counts as computation?

**Mode: `general` on the move, `specified` on the four conditions and the refuters, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a definition and not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A stone and a laptop both evolve according to rules. We call one a computation and withhold the word from the other. The question's own phrase "rather than merely evolving according to rules" concedes that rule-following is not the discriminator. The residual is: what would have to be true of a process-and-mapping for the label to be paid by something other than agreement?

The question is a **union compound**, not one hard question (Fig 9 :57, "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"). It needs a process, a mapping, and a ground. Each has its own loop. Its verdicts are reported separately below and never averaged.

## 2. The shape

1. **The question as framed is the wrong question.** Fig 11 :35: "The wrong question, and this figure is the replacement for it." Fig 11 :39: "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." The same move applies here: not "which processes are computation?" but "what makes a process capable of sustaining a computational description the ground pays for?" Fig 11 :41: "Which is the same move as asking what generates lift rather than whether a thing is a bird. The substrate question dissolves; the contact question does not."

2. **Rule-following is necessary but not sufficient.** Putnam's result, via Chalmers, is that every ordinary open physical system implements every finite-state automaton under some interpretation. If rule-following were enough, the question would be trivial. It is not trivial. Fig 13 :115: "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."

3. **A declaration is not a grounding.** Fig 10 :18: "a declared meaning is not a grounded one." An observer who declares which physical states are inputs and outputs has authored a convention. If that convention then decides whether the process computes, it has stopped being a convention (Fig 10 :47). This is the verdict on the observer-only candidate: it answers a convention question, not a physical one.

4. **The discriminator has four conditions.** A process computes under a mapping when:
   - the mapping's state transitions are **lawful**, meaning the process's causal structure mirrors the formal transitions, including transitions that did not occur on the actual run;
   - the process has **non-trivial combinatorial structure**, so the mapping is not a trivial disjunction of states with a clock and a dial bolted on;
   - the mapping **pays** under the description-length bargain, "does the new term cost less to state than the confusion it removes?" (Fig 5 :26); a mapping that "explains everything by saying nothing" fails (Fig 5 :43-44);
   - the **ground** settles which mappings pay, not the agreement of observers (Fig 2 :29-30, :35).
   These are a shape, not a definition. They do not tell you which process, in particular, computes.

5. **The fork is reported, not averaged.** Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." If the question is "which interpretation shall we adopt?", it is constitutively about a convention, and the choosing observer is the legitimate anchor. If it is "can this process sustain lawful transitions under a non-trivial mapping?", it is constitutively about the world, and the anchor must be an instrument. Source questions 72 and 80 are answered by reporting both branches with their own verdicts.

6. **Computation is a route and a remainder, not a primitive.** Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." Fig 13 :124-125: "Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are each what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member." Computation is the same kind of thing: what a lawful, non-trivial, paying composition leaves behind. Processes that qualify are members of a functional group (Fig 12 :123), with the substrate as the qualifier.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a new definition of computation. The framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32).
- Not a claim that any particular process, silicon, neuronal, chemical or quantum, does or does not compute. That needs a ground reading.
- Not "computation is an intrinsic physical property" and not "computation is purely in the eye of the beholder". Both are refused as blanket answers; F5 reports the legitimate fork.
- Not grounded. No instrument discriminating among interpretations of one behaviour was run here.
- Not a claim that Chalmers, the SEP authors or Landauer endorse the Tether. They are low-overlap corroboration of specific sub-claims, not witnesses for the framework.
- Not a theory of everything, and no final definition after which no further question could be asked (Fig 7 :6, "the limit: no last room").

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further definition. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

- **Counterfactual test:** does the process's causal structure support the formal state transitions under inputs that did not occur on the observed run? This is Chalmers' strong conditional, and it is a measurement of the mechanism, not of the trace.
- **Trace test:** is there any observable that distinguishes a trivial disjunctive mapping from a combinatorial one? If none exists at any resolution, the case is below the union horizon and stays open.
- **Bargain test:** does the mapping compress the observed behaviour more than it states? This can be scored under a declared code, and the score must be reported, not assumed.
- **Thermodynamic test (physics-facing):** does logical reversibility of the mapping track thermodynamic reversibility of the hardware? Landauer/Bennett say the two can differ, so this is an empirical question about a particular implementation, not a definitional one.
- **Standing refuters from `01` §3:** an intrinsic property found; an arbitrary mapping shown sufficient; lawful mirroring shown unnecessary; the Fig 11 move shown to govern only intelligence.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The six-term set is "a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes" (*They're Scanning Books*); declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral. Another sensorium's minimum set need not be these six. No available instrument decides in general which mappings pay. The answer stops there and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Fig 13 :134-135: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence, It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a new definition of computation, and it does not say which physical systems are really computing. It says the question as framed is the wrong question, the same shape as "what is intelligence?": it asks for a property of the process alone. Rule-following cannot be the discriminator, because every ordinary open system implements some automaton under some interpretation. The answerable question is what makes a process capable of sustaining a computational description that the ground pays for. The shape of that answer has four conditions: the mapping's transitions are lawful and support counterfactuals; the process has non-trivial combinatorial structure; the mapping pays under a description-length bargain; and the ground, not agreement, decides which mappings pay. Computation is then a route and a composition-level remainder, not a seventh kind of thing, and the processes that qualify form a functional group whose qualifier is the substrate. What is offered is that shape, the refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The question stays open until an instrument discriminates among interpretations of the same behaviour.
