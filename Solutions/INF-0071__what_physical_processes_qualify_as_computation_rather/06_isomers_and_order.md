## DERIVATION

### Q1: Which bond is the mapping? CORRECTION TO THE "IDENTITY" READING

- The Operators table defines identity ≡ as "two names, one referent", with the test "substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No."
- The natural first reading of "this voltage is bit 1" is identity: two names for one referent.
- But *The Hard Problem is About Source* shows that the trace cannot tell us whether the referent is one source or several: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information". Something **is** lost on substitution, so the identity test fails whenever the mapping is applied to a trace rather than to a source-discriminating measurement.
- The Operators table defines production ⇒ as "A produces B; B did not exist before", with the test "is there a B before A fires? No." Chalmers (1996) requires that "causal relations among states of a physical system must mirror formal relations among states of the automaton" and that the conditionals be lawful, including transitions not manifested on the actual run. That is production, not identity: the formal state comes into being when the physical transition fires.
- ⇒ The mapping is **production ⇒** in the general case, with identity ≡ as a limiting case only when the measurement already discriminates the source. Fig 12 :99: "A bond costs what it could have been, and nothing more." The rock case charges a symmetric identity bond for an ordered causal claim; that is the flat-formula error. It underprices the bond and overclaims the term.
- **Correction recorded.** I first wrote the mapping as identity. Narrowed to production, with identity as a limit. Triangulated (Operators table + Chalmers). Not retracted: the identity reading survives exactly where the instrument resolves the source.

### Q2: Computation is a route, not a state

- Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**
- Fig 12 :112: **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."**
- Operators table :34 warns: "the only bond a flat chain can express" is sequence →, "so every term today is a sequence whether or not it should be". So I must not claim every computation is literally a chain; it is a route built from ordered bonds (→ sequence, ⇒ production), not from a bare list of states.
- Chalmers' combinatorial state automaton makes the point independently: an internal state is a **vector** of independently variable substates, with local transition dependencies that must hold even for inputs that did not occur. That is a branching route with counterfactuals, not a trace.
- Fig 13 :130: **"A term carries the atoms it was built from and the bonds that joined them. That record is not documentation of the term. It is the term."** The lineage of the route is the term. A trace with its lineage discarded is exactly what *How Intelligence Actually Works* calls a frame of a video passed off as a photograph: "The frame and the photograph are indistinguishable as artifacts, and they carry different warrants."
- Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."** A computing loop with feedback is a cycle; a merely evolving open chain cannot close. This is a second structural discriminator, offered as `specified` and unmeasured here.

### Q3: Are a computing process and a merely evolving one isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Written over the full shared atom set {Space, Time, Energy, Gradient, Network, Spectrum}, a laptop and a stone differ not in their atoms but in the bonds that are actually present and settled. In the **descriptions** they are isomers: same atoms, different bond, different substance. Mark `specified`: the physical-atom claim is an import from physics, not a figure derivation.
- The answer to "which physical processes qualify?" is therefore Fig 12 :123: they qualify as members of a **functional group** (a process sustaining a lawful, non-trivial, paying mapping), and the qualifier (silicon, neuronal, chemical, quantum) says which member. No single substance is required. This is the figure-grounded form of the substrate-independence that source question 75 asks about.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if one case can be written without an atom the other requires, or if two cases with the same settled atoms and the same bond arrangement behave differently under a source-discriminating instrument, the isomer / functional-group claim fails and the question falls back to a name, not a shape.

### Q4: What order is the union?

- Fig 13 :75: **"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."** A physical state and a formal state cannot be joined state-to-state at first order. The mapping must be a **structure** between them, which is consistent with Q1's production bond and inconsistent with treating the formal state as a second bare state.
- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."** Chalmers' clock and dial are catalysts in this sense: each survives its own use and makes a mapping possible. But a catalyst is **not sufficient**. The clock plus dial plus input memory still fails the combinatorial-structure condition, which is why catalyst is a structural note, not the discriminator.
- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."** A computational description that the ground settles becomes an atom for the next level, so one program can be a component of another. An unsettled interpretation cannot serve as an atom, because nothing can compose over what was never admitted as a part.
- Fig 8 :50: **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."** The order that matters is the record of the mapping's derivation, not the final table. This is the provenance condition, and it is why a lookup table and an algorithm with the same input-output behaviour are not automatically the same term.

## CONCLUSION

The physical-to-formal mapping is a **production bond ⇒**, with identity ≡ as a limiting case when an instrument already resolves the source. Computation is a **route** through states under lawful, counterfactual-supporting transitions, not a state and not a bare trace. A computing process and a merely evolving one are **isomers in their descriptions** over a shared atom set, and qualify as members of a **functional group** whose qualifier is the substrate. Their order is cross-kind, settled before composition, and carried as a lineage. No new primitive is required, no import moves the wall, and no particular process is named as computing.
