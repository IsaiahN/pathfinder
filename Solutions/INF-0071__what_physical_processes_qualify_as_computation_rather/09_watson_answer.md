## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the question "what physical processes qualify as computation, rather than merely evolving according to rules?" Option A: computation is an intrinsic physical property detectable by inspecting a process. Option B: computation is whatever an observer maps onto inputs and outputs, so any rock implements any automaton under some interpretation. Option C: computation is a contact property, a process under a mapping that the ground pays for. What each leads to: A makes this a detection problem with no stated detector; B makes it a convention and trivialises the question; C makes it a four-condition shape with written refuters and named instruments, but stops short of naming any particular process.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The question as framed is the wrong shape, and the answerable question is about contact, not about the process alone. Rule-following is necessary but not sufficient. A declaration of an interpretation is a convention and does not ground the label. The discriminator is a four-condition shape: lawful counterfactual-supporting transitions, non-trivial combinatorial structure, a description-length bargain that pays, and a ground that decides which mappings pay, never observer agreement. Computation is a route and a composition-level remainder, not a seventh primitive, and qualifying processes form a functional group with the substrate as qualifier. The convention-versus-fact question is a fork with two verdicts, not one averaged answer. No available instrument settles in general which mappings pay, so the verdict stops at `candidate`, `specified` and `general`, not `grounded`.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet is mode-labelled, states its refuters before its conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by asking for agreement about a label instead of offering a checkable discriminator. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the four-condition shape and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The four conditions and their refuters are detachable from the framework; a reader can test them against Chalmers without accepting the Tether. A bad claim can be superseded by a corrected packet, and no primitive has to be withdrawn. The one thing not undoable is a verdict recorded after a recipient has absorbed the theory, which is why stage 1 must come first.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A source-discriminating instrument showing that a trace underdetermines its source in a way no available instrument resolves would move the honest stop from `unreached` to a named gap; a demonstration that an arbitrary disjunctive mapping is sufficient would refute the lawfulness and bargain conditions; a demonstration that computation is an intrinsic ground-visible property would restore the substrate question; a demonstration that Fig 11's move governs only intelligence would send the derivation back to the census. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
