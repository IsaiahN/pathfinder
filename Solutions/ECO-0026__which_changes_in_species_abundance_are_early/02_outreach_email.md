# Outreach draft: check one shape claim before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in
`03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. No personal emails are used anywhere in this
folder.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until a recipient has recorded a stage-1 verdict. If the shape
claim does not survive their check, there is nothing else to look at and the later stages are not
offered.

---

**Subject:** One claim about abundance warnings that you can check in ten minutes

> Hello,
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against your own work. I am not asking you to
> read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive,
> there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A monitoring system's error signal is indexed by the
> thing it is tracking, not by the average over things. Apply that to a community: the residual is
> per species or per functional group, not per community total. A total abundance or mean diversity
> index can sit near zero while one species carries a live residual. The figures call that a legal
> state. The same rules say that two communities with the same species list and different
> interaction arrangements are different substances, and that the difference is carried entirely by
> the bond, which a total abundance does not record.
>
> So the shape claim is: any credible early sign of collapse must be read per slot and per bond,
> against a fixed future anchor, before aggregation. It also has to survive a check on the
> monitoring channel, because transients and tail events are exactly what routine sampling tends to
> drop. This is a shape, not a measurement, and it does not name a statistic.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not saying that rising variance, lag-1 autocorrelation,
> skewness, flickering, or any other named indicator is the answer. Those names come from the
> ecological literature, not from the figures, and they are recorded as imports with provenance. I
> am not claiming that an aggregate index is useless, only that it is downstream of a per-slot call.
> I am not claiming a new theory of critical transitions, and I am not claiming that this framework
> settles which bond (sequence, production, or threshold) holds in your system.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written down before this message:**
> 1. a collapse reliably forecast by a community aggregate while every per-slot residual stays at
>    zero on held-out data;
> 2. two communities with identical species lists but different interaction arrangements behaving
>    identically under a replicated collapse experiment;
> 3. two statistics from the same time series shown to be independent frames by a low-overlap test;
> 4. an indicator transferring from microcosm to whole ecosystem with a measured round-trip gap of
>    zero.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw the corresponding part. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful
> to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **Stage 1 question:** looking only at the shape claim and its refuters, what would you count as a
> failure? Please record that verdict before reading anything further.
>
> **Stage 2, opt-in only, and only after stage 1 is recorded:** if you are curious about where the
> shape came from, the verbatim figure text and the formula are below. The figures, not my summary,
> are the authority.
> - Figures text with line numbers (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step formula (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **Stage 3, if you want to reuse the method:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a
> problem you can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run
> it, state your mode (`general`, `specified`, or `grounded`), and keep the lineage. A reported
> failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Distribution constraint

This is a one-to-one letter. It goes to a public, opt-in channel or a journal submission portal, not
to a harvested list. There is no receptiveness score, no ranking of likely believers, and no
follow-up sequence that depends on someone agreeing. A reply is a conversation or a correction, never
a witness.
