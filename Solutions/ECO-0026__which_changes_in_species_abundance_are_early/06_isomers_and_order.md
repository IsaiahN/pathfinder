## RESIDUAL

`01` establishes that a warning must be per slot and per bond. Three things remain owed: (Q1) what
kind of thing is a community with the same species but a different interaction arrangement; (Q2)
what order relates the warning to the collapse; (Q3) how to price a warning, since a cheap statistic
may be expensive to test. Each is derived below with its own falsifier.

Atoms held: the six substrate terms (states: space, time, energy; structures: spectrum, gradient,

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Two communities, same species, different bonds

Fig 12 :93 "The arrangement is the substance."
Fig 12 :95-96 "Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."
Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
Fig 12 :123 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

A community is a molecule under Fig 12. Its ingredient set is the species list; its bond set is the
interaction structure (who pollinates whom, who eats whom, who competes with whom, who depends on
whom). Two communities with the same species list and different interaction arrangements are
therefore **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance. This is not an analogy
imported from chemistry; the figure itself says the chemistry case is the established precedent and
that the rule is structural.

There is a weaker and a stronger reading. Weak: two communities share only a **functional group**
(Fig 12 :123), for example a `state → structure → state` motif or a predator-prey module; the group
fixes the kind, the qualifier fixes which species fill it. Strong: two communities are written over
the **full shared slot set**, differing only in which bonds connect which slots; then the isomer
claim applies and the difference is decisive in behaviour. Ecological practice is mostly at the weak
level (functional groups, trophic levels), which is why an aggregate index misses the difference.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two replicated communities that share every slot and differ
only in measured bond arrangement show the same collapse threshold, timing, and post-shift state,
the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group reading stands.

### Q2 — What order relates a warning to a collapse?

Fig 12 :71 "A then B; B needs A to have happened"
Fig 12 :77 "A produces B; B did not exist before"
Fig 12 :79 "is there a B before A fires? No."
Fig 12 :87 "a threshold relation between two quantities"
Operators table :52 "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"

Three distinct bonds are available:

| bond | test | ecological reading |
|---|---|---|
| `→` sequence | "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes." (Fig 12 :74) | the abundance change precedes the shift; ordering matters, causation is not asserted |
| `⇒` production | "is there a B before A fires? No." | the change produces the shift; the alternative state did not exist before |
| `⋛` comparison | "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes." (Operators table :51) | a quantity crosses a bound; this is the tipping-point reading |

The test separates them, but the figures do not say which holds in a given community. Three
ecological mechanisms from the world literature map onto the three bonds: a slow approach to a fold
bifurcation fits `⋛`; a chance excursion in a stable system fits `→` with no production; a
stepwise external shock fits neither, because B is imposed rather than produced by A. Dakos et al.
2015 lists these as distinct mechanisms with different indicator expectations. Kuehn 2013 warns that
normal-form indicators can mislead. Both are secondary corroboration that the bond must be tested,
not assumed.

A further ordering claim: **refusal before mint**. `THE_FORMULA.md` step 2 routes a gap by what is
wrong before offering a new term, and Fig 12 :139 "A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only
accumulate" gives the operator reason (negation is unary and is not one of the seven bonds). For
this question, a declining species is not automatically a new warning indicator; the existing
belief that the community is stable may need to be refused first. Negation is expressed as `¬(stable)`
rather than as a new statistic.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a single replicated system satisfies the `→`, `⇒` and `⋛`
tests simultaneously at the same measurement resolution, with no new discriminator, the three-bond
distinction collapses for this domain and this derivation is withdrawn.

### Q3 — What does a warning cost?

Fig 12 :126-127 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add. Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."
Fig 12 :129 "Neither figure predicts the other, so both are read or the choice is made blind."
Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."

A warning statistic is a composition and carries two prices. The **bits** price is how much must be
stated: a single variance trend is cheap, a multivariate sentinel-node set with a covariance model
is expensive. The **actions** price is what it costs to find out whether the statistic holds in a
new ecosystem: a cheap statistic can take a whole monitoring budget to validate, and an expensive
one can be cheap to refute in a replicate. The two currencies do not add, so ranking indicators by
either one alone is blind. Fig 10 :42 adds the verification constraint: the checker of a warning
must not be able to reconstruct it from the framework, which is why the packet in `08` gives the
shape and the refuters, not the derivation interior.

**Falsifier:** if a single scalar (bits or actions, or a weighted sum whose weights are fixed before
the run) predicts which warning survives prospective validation across ecosystems, the two-currency
warning in this derivation is wrong.

## CONCLUSION

1. Communities with the same slots and different bonds are **isomers** under Fig 12; the weaker,
   commonly measured form is a shared **functional group**. This is a structural claim, mode
   `specified`, and it is falsifiable by a replicated arrangement experiment.
2. Warning and collapse can stand in three different ordered relations (`→`, `⇒`, `⋛`), with the
   operators table supplying the test for each. Which one holds is a ground question, not a figure
   output. Refusal of the "stable" belief precedes minting a new indicator.
3. A warning has a bits price and an actions price that do not add. Cheap to state is not cheap to
   verify.
4. Nothing in this derivation names an abundance statistic. That gap is preserved rather than
   filled.
