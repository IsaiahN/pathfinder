## 0. The residual under audit, in effect terms

The question asks which changes in species abundance are early signs of a coming ecological
collapse. Stated as an effect: a monitor holds a time-indexed set of abundance readings. It must
decide, before a regime shift occurs, whether a pattern in those readings is a precursor or noise.
The cost of a false negative is an unanticipated collapse; the cost of a false positive is an
unnecessary intervention with its own ecological and political cost. The question bundles at least
five candidate change types: a monotonic decline of one species; rising variability or memory in an
existing species; change in the covariance or correlation among species; change in the interaction
structure; and loss of rare or functionally redundant species. These are not one phenomenon, so the
first job is a census, not a search.

**What an answer would look like (bound as an ordinary term before the run).** A candidate answer
has four properties: (i) it is indexed by slot s (a species, genus, functional group, or node),
never only by a community total; (ii) it is read against a fixed anchor, the realized future of the
community; (iii) it distinguishes a quantity that changes from the relation (bond) that changes;
(iv) it states its own failure condition. This is the sought-for shape, written down so it can
accrue a residual like any other term. The discipline follows `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": a frame
*cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape a frame is searching for is not a
term it holds.* Binding the shape as an ordinary term makes the preference refusable.

A preliminary warning about vocabulary. The figures use the word "collapse" for failures of a
verification arrangement (Fig 2, Fig 7, Fig 8, Fig 12), not for ecological regime shifts. Those are
homonyms. No line containing "collapse 1" through "collapse 7" is transferred to ecology in this
audit. That refusal is recorded in §3b, not silently made.

## 2. Domain census before choosing any line

### R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** many. One residual per tracked species or functional slot, `R_s`, plus residuals on the
  relations between slots. A community total is a single number and is therefore not the residual
  the figures describe. Fig 1 :11 "residual R indexed by object slot" and Fig 1 :19 "The residual is
  indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
- **Symmetry:** the time direction is ordered, not symmetric. `→` is "A then B; B needs A to have
  happened" (Fig 12 :71), `⇒` is "A produces B; B did not exist before" (Fig 12 :77), and `⋛` is
  "a threshold relation between two quantities" (Fig 12 :87). An indicator that merely precedes a
  collapse is not the same term as one that produces it.
- **Scale:** at least three. Within a population (one slot), across the community (many slots and
  their bonds), and across ecosystem types (microcosm to lake to ocean). The transform between
  scales is lossy, so a warning at one scale is not a warning at another without a measured gap.

### One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

A domain is admitted only if an anchor can be named. If no anchor can be named, it is not yet a
domain; it is an open question inside another domain.

- **Domain A, the monitored community.** Actors: species, genera, functional groups, their
  abundances, and the relations among them (pollination, predation, competition, dependency).
  Currency: per-slot abundance readings and per-slot residual mass `R⁺_s`. Anchor: the realized
  future of that community, the collapse event or its absence, which is a measurement and does not
  change because a monitor thinks otherwise. Anchor is nameable, so this is a domain.
- **Domain B, the monitoring and indicator frame.** Actors: the observation channel, sampling
  schedule, detrending choices, summary statistics (variance, lag-1 autocorrelation, skewness,
  covariance, eigenvalues), and the analysts who choose them. Currency: agreement and residual
  between prediction and observation. Anchor: the same realized future, ranged against separately.
  Anchor is nameable; this is a domain. Important: two statistics computed from the same time
  series are **one** frame, not two. Fig 2 :31 "Two people reading the same report are not two
  frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."
- **Domain C, the warning-to-collapse relation.** Actors: the change in abundance and the regime
  shift, connected by `→`, `⇒`, or `⋛`. Currency: order and threshold. Anchor: the collapse event,
  which is what fixes whether the warning actually arrived early. Anchor is nameable; this is a
  domain, but the figures supply only the test for which bond holds, not the answer.
- **Domain D, the naming of a specific indicator.** Actors: candidate statistics, each a proposed
  term. Currency: description cost and action cost (Fig 12 :126-127). Anchor: none available inside
  the figures. The figures return a shape, not a name, so there is no anchor here. By the standing
  rule this is **not a domain yet**; it is the open residue of A to C, and it is reported as `gap`
  in the verdict rather than being forced into a frame.

### Partition or union

Domains A, B and C are a **union compound**, not a partition. A warning is a statement about a
community (A) made through a channel (B) with a temporal relation to a threshold (C). Removing any
one condition leaves a different question, and a single lever that clears a region where only one
condition holds leaves the intersections standing. Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a
harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound." The question as
phrased that way bundles at least five such conditions, so unbundling is required before
search (Fig 9 :54).

### Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. **Perception:** can the channel tell the current abundance apart from what was there before
   (Fig 3 :5)? Everything downstream assumes yes.
2. **Vocabulary:** does the monitor have terms for what a species or interaction affords, and for
   the bond that changed, not just the amount?
3. **Objective:** what counts as progress, an early correct call rather than a retrospective fit?
4. **Planning:** which slots and which environmental drivers to watch, given a finite budget.
5. **Learn and carry:** name what worked, verify it against the ground, carry the method, not the
   recording, to the next ecosystem.

Effort aimed at step 4 cannot rescue a broken step 1. Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already
works cannot rescue one that does not."

## 3. Findings, verbatim line before conclusion

### F1 — A community aggregate cannot be the primary warning signal (decisive, triangulated)

Fig 1 :13: "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"
Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
Fig 1 :28: "The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."
Fig 12 :16: "and nothing signals that anything was lost"

A total abundance or mean diversity index is an aggregate over slots. Under Fig 1 it can sit at a
near-zero residual while one species slot carries positive live mass `R⁺_s`. There is no contradiction
in a community total looking stable while one species is in trouble; that is a *legal state* in the
figures' terms. The changes that can count as early signs are therefore per-slot changes, read
before the average is formed. The aggregate is not useless; it is downstream, a summary of calls
already made per slot.

This is triangulated with a second, low-overlap figure line: Fig 12 :16 says a flat recording can
lose information "and nothing signals that anything was lost." The ingredient list (total
abundance) does not record the arrangement. Overlap between Fig 1 and Fig 12 is low: Fig 1 concerns
the agent's residual indexing, Fig 12 concerns notation and bonds. Two different mechanisms, same
verdict.

**Refuter, written before the conclusion:** a documented case in which a collapse is reliably
forecast by a community aggregate while every per-slot residual remains at zero, with no slot
carrying positive mass at any sampling time. If that exists, F1 is false.

### F2 — Arrangement is the substance: which species changed matters beyond how much (triangulated)

Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."
Fig 11 :48: "You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."
Fig 11 :52-53: "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."
Fig 12 :95-96: "Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."
Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

Two communities with the same total abundance, the same species list, and different interaction
arrangements are different substances under Fig 12. A warning can therefore live in a bond rather
than in an amount: a change in who interacts with whom, in covariance structure, in the loss of a
connector species, or in the decoupling of a previously coordinated module. Fig 11 adds that the
habitat must be enumerated, not composed from a preferred theory, and that the residual that
matters can be three steps out. A monitor that only enumerates direct contact misses the cascade.

Triangulation: Fig 12 (notation: bond carries information operands do not) and Fig 11 (habitat:
contact cascades, read off the world) have low overlap. Both give the same verdict. The world
literature supplies a separate, non-figure corroboration in Masuda et al. 2024, "all the nodes do
not emit equally useful early warning signals", and in O'Brien et al. 2023, which reports different
trophic levels showing different transition mechanisms. Those are secondary only.

**Refuter:** two communities with identical ingredient sets and demonstrably different bond
arrangements showing the same collapse behaviour in a replicated experiment, with arrangement
measured and not merely assumed. If that holds, the bond carries no warning and F2 falls.

### F3 — The anchor must be fixed; an indicator tuned on the same pool is not corroborated (triangulated)

Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update."
Fig 2 :25: "pinned: it does not update on A or B"
Fig 2 :31: "Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."
Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"
Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."
Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."

The anchor in this question is the realized future of the community: did it shift, and when. That
does not move because an analyst prefers a different indicator. A warning statistic is a frame that
must range against that anchor. Two statistics (variance and lag-1 autocorrelation) computed from
the same time series draw on one evidence pool and are one frame wearing two names (Fig 2 :31,
Fig 8 :47-49). Their agreement is not two witnesses. The practical consequence for this field is
the prosecutor's fallacy documented by Boettiger and Hastings 2012: selecting historical cases
*because* they collapsed manufactures apparently good warning trends in systems that had no
approaching threshold. That is the shared-pool failure in a statistical costume.

Triangulation: Fig 2 (anchor and independence) and Fig 7 :13 "same closure, handed along; agreement
compounds, evidence does not" are separate figures with low overlap. Both give the same verdict.

**Refuter:** a demonstration, with a low-overlap test, that two indicators derived from the same
series are independent frames in the figure's sense, and that selecting cases on the outcome does
not change the estimated false-positive rate. Then F3 is false.

### F4 — Agreement among indicators cannot hand you a verdict (single-source for the figure rule, corroborated)

Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."
Fig 8 :51: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."

A panel of agreeing early-warning statistics is a room. Agreement among them selects nothing; only
the ground (a held-out collapse, a replicated experiment, a prospective test) votes. This is a
single figure line family (Fig 7, Fig 9, Fig 8), so the figure verdict is marked single-source, with
Boettiger and Hastings 2012 and O'Brien et al. 2023 as separate world corroboration. Their evidence
pool differs from the figures, but they are not independent of each other on every point; overlap is
recorded in the ledger.

**Refuter:** show a multi-indicator consensus, with no held-out ground reading, being used as the
sole basis for a management decision that later proves correct, and argue convincingly that the
consensus, not the ground, carried the warrant. That would breach the rule and require re-census.

### F5 — Before asking which signal, ask which link the monitoring chain stops at (triangulated)

Fig 3 :5: "the system can tell what is in front of it, and tell it apart from what was there before"
Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
Fig 3 :22-23: "A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can. Which is why a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing."
Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does"
Fig 10 :25-26: "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"
Fig 10 :27-28: "the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"

Early warning signals are transient, tail, and high-frequency phenomena. Fig 10 names exactly the
channel failures that erase them: dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings. When a
monitor reports "no warning", that output is not a verdict about the ecosystem; it may be a report
that the channel has lost touch with the ground. Fig 3 adds the dependency order: if the perception
link cannot tell the current state from the previous one, every later indicator is a reading of
nothing. This is why Dakos et al. 2012 notes that many sampling schemes are designed explicitly to
avoid temporal autocorrelation, the very quantity a warning test needs.

Triangulation: Fig 3 (chain dependency) and Fig 10 (channel decay) are distinct figures with low
overlap. Same verdict.

**Refuter:** a monitored system in which the channel is shown, by an independent calibration, to
preserve transients and tail events at the relevant scale, and in which no warning precedes a
collapse; then the absence is on the ground side, not the channel side, and the "lost touch"
reading is refuted for that case.

### F6 — Cross-scale transfer is lossy, and the gap must be measured (single-source, strained until measured)

Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."
Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."
Fig 6 :32: "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."
Fig 6 :38: "theory routinely precedes its instrument, and is welcome to. Neptune was calculated before it was seen, and the prediction is what told anyone where to point"

A warning validated in a chemostat or a microcosm does not transfer unchanged to a lake, a reef, or
a forest. The transform between scales loses information by construction; the honest report carries
`R_T = gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, not the bare claim. Fig 6 adds the search rule: a better instrument
is improved from a worse one already returning something, so the route is from existing imperfect
indicators to sharper ones, not from a description to a brand-new sensor. And theory may precede
the instrument: the shape derived here tells a monitor where to point, without naming the reading.

Single-source on the figure side (Fig 4 plus its echo in Fig 6, close overlap, so not counted as
two witnesses). Dakos et al. 2015 lists "measuring the wrong variable" and scale mismatch as named
operational constraints; that is separate corroboration.

**Refuter:** an indicator that transfers from microcosm to whole ecosystem with a measured round-trip
gap of zero on held-out data. Then the transfer warning is wrong.

### F7 — SUPPORT is per slot; a warning is owed only where there is live mass (single-source)

Fig 5 :20-21: "is there anything to explain?" / "the gap has real mass somewhere"
Fig 5 :33: "The three guards are a product, not a checklist."
Fig 5 :41: "Any one of them at zero stops the mint entirely. Which is why a system that is minting nothing may have nothing wrong with its minting."
Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source."

In formula terms, SUPPORT is `|R⁺_s| > 0` for some slot s before the search. A warning term is owed
only where the per-slot residual carries positive mass. This is a guard, not a reward: when no slot
is live, "no warning" is the correct output and the absence of a mint says nothing about the
quality of the search machinery. This is why the outreach material brings the characterized
residual first and the framework later.

**Refuter:** a case where no slot carries positive live mass at any resolution yet a warning term
is still owed and paid for by the ground. Then SUPPORT is mis-specified for this domain.

### F8 — The naming part of the question is a `gap`, not a silence (triangulated)

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"
Fig 9 :44: "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."
Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"
Fig 13 :135: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."
Fig 8 :39: "a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

After a genuine census, no figure line names a statistic of abundance as the early warning. Fig 9
and Fig 13 forbid deriving one; Fig 8 forbids turning that failure into a claim that no warning
exists. The correct output is `gap` for naming, with the searched space and the missing instrument
recorded. The world literature does supply names (variance, lag-1 autocorrelation, skewness,
spectral reddening, flickering, dynamic network biomarkers, sentinel-node sets), but each is an
**import** from an external frame, not a derivation from the figures. Fig 8 :51 requires the record
to separate convergent derivation from adopted import; the ledger does that per source.

**Refuter:** a derivation, from the figure set alone, of a specific abundance statistic that pays
its description cost and is then settled by a ground reading. Until one is produced, the naming
part stays `gap`.

### F9 — Order and threshold are distinguishable, and the figures give the test but not the answer (single-source)

Fig 12 :71: "A then B; B needs A to have happened"
Fig 12 :77: "A produces B; B did not exist before"
Fig 12 :79: "is there a B before A fires? No."
Fig 12 :87: "a threshold relation between two quantities"
Operators table :51: "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"

Three different claims hide inside "early sign". (i) `→` sequence: the abundance change precedes
the shift but does not cause it. (ii) `⇒` production: the change produces the shift; B did not exist
before A fired. (iii) `⋛` comparison: a quantity is compared with a threshold, which is what a
tipping point is in the dynamical-systems literature. The operators table gives the test for each,
so this is a checkable distinction, but it does not tell us which bond holds in a given ecosystem.
Dakos et al. 2015 and O'Brien et al. 2023 report that many regime shifts are not bifurcation-driven
and that EWSs often do not discriminate transition type. That is a *conflict with a naive reading*
of the figures, not with the figures themselves, and it sends us back to the census: the bond must
be settled per domain by a ground reading, not assumed.

**Refuter:** show, in a replicated system, that a precursor satisfying the `→` test also satisfies
the `⇒` test and the `⋛` test simultaneously with no change in measurement resolution. Then the
three-bond distinction collapses for this domain and F9 must be withdrawn.

## 3b. Strain tests and re-census (kept visible)

1. **The word "collapse" in the figures is a homonym.** Fig 2 :4 "collapse 1: mutual update", Fig 2
   :6 "collapse 2: common cause (hidden shared source)", Fig 7 :4 "collapse 3: the mirror chain",
   Fig 8 :4 "collapse 4: the blend", Fig 8 :6 "collapse 5: the undirected union", Fig 12 :6
   "collapse 6: the flat formula", Fig 12 :7 "collapse 7: the free bond". All seven are failures of
   verification or notation, not ecological regime shifts. An earlier draft of this audit read Fig 2's
   "common cause" as ecological confounding and Fig 2 :44 "the collapse is the same arithmetic read
   as a warning" as a statement about tipping points. **Both readings are retracted.** Fig 2 :44
   concerns effective sample size for correlated observations in survey statistics and meta-analysis.
   The ecological analogue in the census is the contact cascade (Fig 11 :52-53), not Fig 2's
   collapse panels.
2. **Fig 5 :59 "a term whose residual PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN is the one to point the
   refusal at" is not an early warning rule.** It ranks candidates for a *refusal*, the opposite
   direction to a forecast. An earlier draft read "persistent and flat residual" as a precursor of
   collapse. **Retracted.** After re-census it governs which held term to negate (Fig 5 :60 "The
   selector asks which residual is improving; this asks which one keeps being wrong and is not."),
   not which abundance change to watch. It is retained only in the pre-registered refuter, where a
   persistent-and-flat residual is a reason to refuse a claim.
3. **Fig 13 :31-32 "a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail
   a crossing discards reappears above it as noise" is structural, not a measurement.** It gives the
   *shape* of a signal that grows through an amplifying chain and of noise as discarded detail
   reappearing. It does not say that rising variance in a time series is a collapse warning. Used as
   a shape only; no empirical prediction is attached to it here.
4. **"Shape, not a name" was initially overstated.** An earlier draft said the figures forbid
   naming any indicator. The canonical wording governs a *missing* thing. A statistic from the
   ecological literature is an import with provenance, not the invention of a new primitive. The
   narrowed claim is: no statistic is figure-derived, and any named statistic must carry a source
   gate and shadow test. Both the overstatement and its correction are preserved here.
5. **Strain after re-census: none hidden.** The one live strain is F6's cross-scale gap, which is
   named and owed a measurement, not smoothed over. The F9 bond-type question is a genuine open
   test, not a strain in the figures.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Required before any outreach, with the design-instrument rationale recorded in the Seat Map (Fig 10
is the relevant seat figure; the seat guide's own line is "a falsifier written before the run is a
design instrument, not only an honesty device"). The shape claim is refuted if any of the following
holds:

1. A collapse is reliably forecast, on held-out data, by a community aggregate while every
   per-slot residual remains at zero (breaks F1).
2. Two communities with identical ingredient sets and demonstrably different bond arrangements
   show the same collapse behaviour in a replicated experiment (breaks F2).
3. Two statistics from the same time series are shown to be independent frames by a low-overlap
   test, or conditioning on observed collapse does not change the false-positive rate (breaks F3).
4. A warning transfers across scales with a measured round-trip gap of zero (breaks F6).
5. A specific abundance statistic is derived from the figure set alone and then settled by a ground
   reading (breaks F8, which is the honest way to be wrong here).

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the solution is re-censused. Until one is
run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with F1 and F5: per-slot residuals and the state of the monitoring channel. Those are the
   figure-governed parts and they are independent of any particular indicator.
2. Unbundle the question before searching (Fig 9 :54). The phrase about which changes is a union of at least five
   conditions; a single indicator cannot clear the intersections.
3. Treat the named statistics in the literature as imports with provenance (Fig 8 :51), never as
   outputs of the framework. Record author, venue, year, quote, and overlap for each.
4. Ask the ground, not the room: prospective or replicated tests with a fixed anchor (F3, F4).
5. Carry methods, not recordings, across ecosystems (Fig 4 :22). A warning recipe from one lake is
   a description of one occasion until it is re-derived with its own refuter.

Fig 9 :43: "The verdict tells you to leave. The lookup tells you where to go. Elimination is what you
do after you arrive."
