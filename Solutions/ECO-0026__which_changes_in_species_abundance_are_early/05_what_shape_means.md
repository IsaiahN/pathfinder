# What "shape" means for this question

**Provenance note, kept visible.** The eight essays are the philosophical grounding named in the
project README. Three of them most load-bearing here (The Library of Babel, The Hard Problem is
About Source, The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap) were opened and read verbatim on 2026-11-23;
their URLs and quotes are in `10_source_ledger.csv`. The canonical eight-essay mapping was read from
the completed exemplar's `05_what_shape_means.md` (a local file) in the same session. The remaining
five essays are listed below with quotes attributed to that mapping, **not** independently
re-fetched in this run. They are secondary in every case, so no figure verdict rests on them.

## 1. A shape is a constraint you can state before you can name the filler

The figures README states the canonical limit: the framework returns "what a missing thing would
have to look like (a shape, not a name)". Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"
Fig 13 :135 "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

From *The Library of Babel* (Isaiah N, adventuresinml, 2026-10-02, opened 2026-11-23):
"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it
provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question." And: "use the existing
vocabulary you do know that is closest to the words that you wish to describe and grasp the shape
of."

For ECO-0026 the shape is therefore not "use rising variance" or "watch the keystone species". It is
the set of constraints a usable warning must satisfy: per slot, per bond, against a fixed anchor,
with the channel intact and the transfer gap measured. Those constraints narrow the space of
acceptable indicators without selecting one. `SPACE(???)TIME` in the exemplar is the analogous shape;
here the analogue is `warning(???)collapse`, with the bond type and the statistic both left open.

## 2. How the shape is obtained: proximity, not prescription

From *The Library of Babel*: "With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by
definition and deepen your vocabulary." Fig 6 :30 "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened
into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30-31 "your words are borrowed too, so
describe the effect rather than the cause".

The practical version for this question: describe the residual in effect terms first (one species
slot diverges from its own history while the community total stays flat; a bond changes while the
ingredient list does not), and only then go looking for a frame whose closure predicts it. If the
frame is chosen first, any indicator will seem to fit, which is Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you
go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit".

The same essay gives the union warning directly: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation
of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually
to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way." This is the essay-side
corroboration of Fig 9 :54's unbundling rule and of the five-condition census in `01` §0.

## 4. The instrument problem: a reading does not determine its source

From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened 2026-11-23): "the instrument is speaking only about
what it measured, it cannot comment on the source." And: "If several paths create the same behavior,
then behavior is a projection with lossy information." And, the operative line: "The answer is
unreached until a better instrument exists." The essay's example is metamerism: a spectrometer, not
an argument, resolved which source produced a green reading.

Applied here: a rising variance reading is a projection. It can come from an approaching fold
bifurcation, from a change in the magnitude of environmental noise, from a chance excursion in a
stable system selected after the fact, or from a change in sampling resolution. The reading does not
determine the source. What is owed is a discriminating instrument or a replicated design, not a
name. This is the essay-side analogue of F3 and F5 in `01`, and it is why Boettiger and Hastings
2012 and O'Brien et al. 2023 matter as secondary witnesses.

## 5. Simulation, prestige, and the honest stop

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (opened 2026-11-23): "the truly testable output would need
to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." So this whole folder is a
simulated derivation, not a ground reading, and cannot settle the question it reports on.

The same essay gives the outreach rule: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget
to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it." A list of famous
ecologists agreeing with the shape claim is a prestige loop, not a check. The legitimate ask gives
the claim a budget to be tried, then lets a held-out collapse, a replicate, or a prospective test
decide.

## 6. Correction to my own conflation

An earlier reading of this file treated "shape, not a name" as a blanket ban on naming an indicator.
That overstates the canonical wording, which governs a **missing** primitive, one not held in the
library. A named statistic is not a new primitive; it is a binding from the ecological or
dynamical-systems frame onto terms the figures already hold (a slot, a residual, a bond, a
threshold). Naming one is therefore not forbidden, but calling it figure-derived is. The corrected
statement is: the figures license the shape; the literature licenses the names, with provenance.
Both the original overstatement and its correction are kept here so the error is visible.
