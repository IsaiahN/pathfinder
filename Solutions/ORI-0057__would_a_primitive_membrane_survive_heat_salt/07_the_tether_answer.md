# The Tether's answer on primitive membrane survival

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on each per-domain prediction and on the binding of "membrane" to the habitat figure. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a measurement. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks whether a primitive membrane would survive heat, salt, acidity, or other plausible early-Earth conditions. There is no named composition and no named site, so there is no single event to answer. The residual is a union compound: four named conditions plus an open list, applied to an unspecified boundary. Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either". What an answer would look like was bound before the search in `01` §0: a per-composition, per-condition verdict against an enumerated habitat, with intersections treated as compounds and an assay as the anchor.

**Domain 4, the compound.** The union is unbundled before search. The condition that seems to threaten the boundary is also a condition the boundary can use, depending on composition. **Verdict: `specified`, triangulated.**

These verdicts are not averaged into one survival score. There is no global yes/no in the shape.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that all primitive membranes survive, and not that none do. The question did not name a composition or a site.
- Not that the figures know lipid chemistry. "Membrane" is bound to the habitat figure as a declared import (Fig 8 :50: only the record separates an adopted import from a convergent derivation).
- Not that Figure 4's membrane is a lipid bilayer. Figure 4 :21 "The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction." That reading was retracted in `01` F7.
- Not that heat, salt and pH form a sequence or a single comparison. They are a co-presence with a union caveat (`06` Q1).
- Not that the six substrate terms derive NaCl, pH or temperature. They are read off the world (W4).
- Not `grounded`. No figure line returns a per-slot survival number.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A factorial assay, not a further argument. For each named composition and each condition and intersection: declare the acceptance limits before running (W5), measure retention and leakage against those limits, and include orthogonal readouts so a source is not named from one projection. Three concrete questions:

1. **Retention threshold.** Fraction of entrapped oligonucleotide retained after a declared exposure, for a declared mixture, at a declared temperature, ionic strength and pH. This is the W1 assay form.
2. **Salt and pH interaction.** At what NaCl or Mg2+ concentration does a named mixture flocculate, and does a bound nucleobase or glycerol ester change that threshold? This is the W3 assay form.
3. **Cycle persistence.** Does the mixture survive repeated exposure with no re-entry effect, or does each cycle change the next outcome? This is the W1 thermocycle form and the `06` Q3 cycle.

The world has already partially answered these for specific mixtures (W1, W2, W3), so a global `unreachable` would be wrong. Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" applies to questions with no ground; this one has one. What remains open is the particular primitive composition and the particular site, neither of which the question supplies.

## 5. The honest stop

**Reached as a shape, ungrounded as an answer.** The framework stops where the instruments stop distinguishing, and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Here it returns the form any answer must take, and the experiments fill some slots. The remaining slots are not a silence and not a gap in principle: they are under-specified inputs. An outside frame could supply them and run stage 1 without ever reading the framework. This is why `08_expert_verification_packet.md` carries the refuters before the sources.

Standing falsifiers from `01` §4: one composition with identical retention across all tested conditions; a derivation of the habitat from the six terms; a stable vote replacing an assay; one mechanism and one threshold shared by heat, salt and pH. If any fires, the shape claim is withdrawn.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say whether primitive membranes survived. It says what such an answer must look like. Survival is a property of a boundary and its habitat together, so the question must name the amphiphile mixture and the setting before it can be answered. Heat, salt and acidity are not a severity scale and not a sequence: they are a union of conditions with their own intersections, each settled by a declared retention assay rather than by agreement among specialists. No condition is intrinsically hostile; the same salt or heat that dissolves one fatty-acid boundary is tolerated or exploited by a mixed one, and bound cargo or mineral surfaces can decide the outcome two steps out. The figures return that shape and four ways to refute it. The yes or no belongs to the experiment, and some of it has already been measured.
