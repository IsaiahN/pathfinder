# Expert verification packet: the Tether on primitive membrane survival

**What this is:** a shape claim in a form a membrane researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` structural, `specified` in its binding to lipid chemistry. Not `grounded`.** No per-slot survival number is asserted by this packet.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Would a primitive membrane survive heat, salt, acidity?" is not one question with one yes/no answer. It is a union of conditions applied to an unstated composition. Survival is a property of the boundary and its habitat together, not of the boundary alone. The habitat must be enumerated from the world rather than composed from a list; the conditions must be unbundled before they are searched; each per-composition, per-condition verdict must be settled by a declared assay; and second-order contact (bound cargo, mineral surfaces, prior cycling) can decide the first-order outcome. The figures return that shape and four ways to refute it. They do not name a composition and do not return a survival number.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 11 and 13

- **Statement:** a boundary's survival is a property of boundary-and-habitat, and the valence of a condition is relative to the residual. There is no standing list of hostile conditions.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39 "And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."; Fig 11 :40 "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."; Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."; Fig 13 :76 "A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."
- **What to check:** is the binding of "agent" to a lipid boundary declared as an import? It is, in `01` F1. The claim is structural; it does not say which lipid wins.
- **Refuter:** one amphiphilic composition with identical retention across all tested temperature, ionic and pH conditions, with no interaction term. That breaks C1.

### C2: world-facing, checkable against the membrane literature

- **Statement:** the same condition has opposite valence for different compositions, and a second-order actor can decide the first-order outcome.
- **Sources read:** W1 (Mansy and Szostak, PNAS 2008): "vesicles composed solely of fatty acids are only stable within a fairly narrow pH range" and a mixed composition "retain[s] internal RNA and DNA oligonucleotides at temperatures ranging from 0 °C to 100 °C". W3 (Black and Blosser, Life 2016): "These bound building blocks stabilized fatty acid membranes against salt-induced flocculation and increased the rate of vesicle formation."
- **Figure warrant:** Fig 11 :52 "And contact cascades." and Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another".
- **What to check:** are those characterizations faithful? Are the reported composition, concentration and assay conditions comparable? The three membrane papers share one research lineage, so count their agreement as one witness.
- **Refuter:** show that for every composition tested, survival under a condition is fully predicted by that condition and the amphiphile set, with no significant contribution from bound cargo, surfaces or prior cycling. That breaks the cascade reading.

### C3: decision-theoretic, checkable by reading Figures 2, 9 and the engineering source

- **Statement:** the question is a union, not a scalar, and "survive" is settled by a declared measurement rather than by agreement.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."; Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"; Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."; Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
- **World warrant:** W5 (Creative Biolabs, 2026): "Define alert limits for particle size, PDI, zeta potential, encapsulation efficiency, leakage, assay, and impurity trends before data are generated."
- **What to check:** is one scalar severity score being used anywhere in the claim? It is not. Is there a ground that an expert could point to? Yes, a retention assay with pre-declared limits.
- **Refuter:** heat, salt and pH share one mechanism and one threshold for every composition, so a single scalar predicts all outcomes; or a survival claim is settled by a stable expert vote with no measurement. Either breaks C3.

## 5. What would settle it

A factorial retention assay with acceptance limits declared before data are generated. Three concrete slots:

1. Retention fraction after declared exposure for a named mixture at a named temperature, ionic strength and pH.
2. Flocculation threshold versus NaCl or Mg2+, with and without a glycerol ester or bound nucleobase.
3. Persistence across repeated cycles, testing whether each cycle changes the next outcome.

The world has partly answered these for specific mixtures (W1, W2, W3), so this is not a `gap`. What remains open is the actual primitive composition and the actual site, neither supplied by the question.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

2. Check C2 against your own knowledge: are the two characterizations of fatty-acid and mixed vesicles faithful, and comparable in concentration and assay?

4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement, and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
