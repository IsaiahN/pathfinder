## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the claim that financial markets direct investment to its most productive uses, versus the claim that speculation and short-term incentives distort investment. Option A: answer the question globally, choosing one side. Option B: treat the "or" as a union, keep verdicts per slot, declare the residual before assigning valence to any actor, and refuse a price as the anchor for a question in which the market is a party. What each leads to: A produces a global verdict with a self-scoring metric and an uninstrumented objective, and it spends the independence of the people checking it; B produces a checkable shape, pre-written refuters, and an open slot until a non-price ground settles it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap, per investment slot, between the allocation a mechanism produces and the allocation a non-price ground would rank as most productive. Arity: two candidate conditions, not mutually exclusive. Symmetry: asymmetric, price discovery and speculation use different inputs and horizons. Scale: from a firm's capital budget to sectoral capital flows. Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
- **Domain 1, price discovery and allocation.** Actors: dispersed investors, arbitrageurs, issuing firms, prices. Currency: capital allocated, risk-adjusted return, information compressed into a quote. Anchor: a realized long-run productive outcome measured outside the price system. Nameable, so this is a domain.
- **Domain 2, speculation and short-horizon incentives.** Actors: speculators, fund managers, managers under quarterly pressure, lenders. Currency: near-term price changes, conventional valuation, reputation. Anchor: the same real outcome plus an observable mismatch between decision horizon and payoff horizon. Nameable, so this is a domain.
- **Domain 3, verification and measurement.** Actors: researchers, data providers, regulators, reviewers. Currency: published evidence and replications. Anchor: out-of-sample real outcomes, never agreement. Nameable, so this is a domain.
- **Candidate Domain 4, market sentiment as such.** No nameable anchor inside the frame, since its only reference is the next conventional valuation, which is frame-internal. Not a domain yet. It folds into Domain 2 as a compound and gets no separate verdict weight.
- **Partition or union.** Union. Fig 9 :4-5 says disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule works on a different subset, are several well-formed questions. Fig 9 :57-58 says a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, carry. The chain most plausibly stops at the objective, because "most productive uses" is usually replaced by a price-based proxy.

Strain test applied: Fig 2 :29 lists a market as a possible anchor, but Fig 2 :36 and :47 bar it when the market is a party. Re-census resolves this by question type, not by softening either line. See `01` section 6.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The "or" is a union, not a choice. Per-slot verdicts against a named non-price anchor are the correct output, and actor valence is assigned only after a residual is declared. Speculation is not intrinsically distorting, and price cannot settle the question it is a party to. The objective phrase "most productive uses" is uninstrumented, so the chain breaks at link 3. This is a `general` shape with `specified` anchor and instrument findings, ungrounded, and reported as `gap` at the ground. The global question as posed cannot be answered with a price-based metric and a shared return-data evidence pool. No policy conclusion follows.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode, writes its four refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real avoidable cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a global verdict or a theory before the residual, which Fig 2 :42 prices as "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Leading with the shape and its refuters avoids it.
- **undoable:** yes. The refused global claims are removable without touching the derivation. The functional-group and residual-relative findings stand on their own. A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet, and no primitive has to be withdrawn.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A non-price instrument that ranks productive use and does not move when prices are pushed would either close the anchor gap or show the price proxy was adequate; a single residual giving both mechanisms the same stable verdict would turn the union into a partition; an actor with invariant valence across residuals would break Fig 11 as applied here; a lossless aggregation of per-slot residuals would break Fig 1 :19/:22. Until one of these fires the verdict stands as candidate.
