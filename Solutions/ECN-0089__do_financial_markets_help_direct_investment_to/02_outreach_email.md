# Outreach draft: check the residual before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three checkable sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` survive your own read of the figure text. If a sub-claim fails, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One check on a claim about market prices and real investment

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the anchor finding, not `grounded`):**
>
> The question "do financial markets direct investment well, or does speculation distort it?" is not one question with two answers. It is a union of two conditions that can both be live, in different investment slots and at different horizons. Fig 9 names the rule: disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are several well-formed questions, not one hard one. Fig 1 says the residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated. So the honest output is a verdict per slot, never a global "markets help" or "markets distort".
>
> The second part is about the anchor. A price cannot settle the question of where prices send real capital, because the market is one of the two mechanisms being tested. Fig 1 says a frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces. Fig 2 says a ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame, and that a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one. So a non-price measure of productive use is owed before the test can mean anything.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that speculation is intrinsically bad. Fig 11 says an actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries. The same trader is a benefactor when the residual is "does the price incorporate dispersed information?" and a malfactor when the residual is "does capital reach long-run productive use?". Calling speculation bad by nature is a category error under the framework. I am also not claiming that a better non-price instrument already exists. I am claiming only that the question as posed has no ground, and that "unreachable is a verdict, not a silence."
>
> **What would refute this, written down before I sent this:**
> 1. A single residual for which both mechanisms return the same stable verdict against a non-price anchor across independent slots, which would turn the union into a partition.
> 2. An actor whose valence is invariant across residuals, good or bad for every residual.
> 3. A price-based quantity that demonstrably does not move when the price-forming frames are pushed, and that still ranks real productive allocation.
> 4. A demonstration that per-slot residuals aggregate to a global verdict with no signal lost.
>
> If any of those holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is far more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Efficient Capital Markets (Jones and Netter, Econlib): https://www.econlib.org/library/Enc/EfficientCapitalMarkets.html
> - The General Theory, Chapter 12 (Keynes, 1936): https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/economics/keynes/general-theory/ch12.htm
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks for reading,
> [name]

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Output and refuter first, theory second | Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* |
| Do not ask for agreement or trust | Fig 2 :35, *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* |
| Pre-registered refuter | A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device. |
| Mode stated on the substantive claims | `THE_FORMULA.md`: *"Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which."* |
| Refuse a global verdict | Fig 9 :4-5 (split the union), Fig 1 :19 (residual per slot). |
| Refuse intrinsic valence for speculation | Fig 11 :49-51, *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* |
| Withhold the theory until stage 1 is recorded | Fig 10 :42, *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* |
| Links offered only after the verdict | Fig 4 :22, *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* |
| No prestige framing, no "bombshell", no "true answer" | Prestige can fund a trial but cannot settle an action; only the ground settles it. |
| Ask users to state their own ground and refuter | The formula's "Direction" limit: a frame cannot check the direction of its own question. |

## Stage sequence

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual, the shape claim and the four refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict before any theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, send the four links. The figures, not this summary, are the authority.
3. **Stage 3, opt-in only.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

A Stage 2 or Stage 3 reply is a conversation, never corroboration. Later agreement is not pooled as a witness (Fig 2 :40).
