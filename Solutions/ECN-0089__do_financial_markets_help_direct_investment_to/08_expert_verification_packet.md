# Expert verification packet: the Tether on financial markets and investment

**What this is:** a claim in a form a finance researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the anchor and instrument findings, not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Standing:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "do financial markets direct investment well, or does speculation distort it?" is a union of two conditions, not an exclusive choice. Both can be live in different investment slots, so the honest answer is a per-slot verdict and never a global score. An actor's valence is relative to the residual you declare: the same short-horizon trader can help price discovery and harm long-run capital allocation. A price cannot anchor the question it is a party to, because a frame cannot score itself with a quantity it produces, and a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one. Finally, the objective phrase "most productive uses" has no instrument independent of price, so the dependency chain breaks at the objective link. The output is a shape, written refuters and the kind of instrument that would close the question.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1. Union, not partition (framework-internal, checkable by reading the figures)

- **Statement:** two mechanisms that each work on a different subset are not one hard question with two answers. The "or" is a union, and verdicts must stay per slot.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :4-5, *"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* Also Fig 9 :57-58, *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."* And Fig 1 :22, *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
- **Refuter:** exhibit a single residual, stated in effect terms, for which both mechanisms return the same stable verdict against a non-price anchor across independent slots. That makes it a partition.

### C2. Valence is residual-relative (framework-internal plus finance-facing)

- **Statement:** no actor is intrinsically a benefactor or malfactor. Speculation is beneficial for a price-discovery residual and harmful for a long-run allocation residual.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49, *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :50, *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."*
- **What to check against your own knowledge:** does the short-horizon trader in Hayek 1945 (the arbitrageur acting on "special knowledge of circumstances of the fleeting moment") perform the same role as the speculator in Keynes 1936 (who "forecasts the psychology of the market")? They are the same activity against two different residuals.
- **Refuter:** show an actor whose valence is invariant across residuals, good or bad for every residual, or show that the two forecasts target the same thing.

### C3. Price cannot be its own anchor (framework-internal plus measurement-facing)

- **Statement:** a price-based metric cannot settle whether prices send capital to productive uses, because the market is one of the two mechanisms under test. An independent real-outcome instrument is owed.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :27, *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Fig 2 :47, *"A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame."* Fig 10 :47, *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*. The canonical figures README adds that the framework returns "a shape, not a name", so the owed instrument is characterised in effect terms, not named.
- **What to check:** can you name a real-outcome ranking of investment uses that is stable when prices are pushed, and that is not itself a function of prices or expected returns?
- **Refuter:** exhibit a price-based quantity that demonstrably does not move when the price-forming frames are pushed and that still ranks real productive allocation.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that financial markets are net good or net bad, and not that speculation is intrinsically distorting.
- Not that a transaction tax, disclosure rule or any specific policy follows. Policy is downstream of a settled anchor.
- Not that a non-price productive-use instrument already exists, and not that one is impossible (Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*).
- Not that the efficient-market and behavioural-finance literatures are two independent witnesses. They share a return-data evidence pool, so their agreement is counted once.
- Not a derivation of one mechanism from the other, and no new primitive or import that moves a wall.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

A non-price instrument plus independent witnesses, not more agreement.

1. **Anchor test:** does a real-outcome measure rank a set of investments differently from their risk-adjusted returns, stably across slots?
2. **Union test:** is there one residual for which both mechanisms give the same stable verdict on that anchor across independent slots?
3. **Cascade test:** does a short-horizon funding shock reach real capacity three steps out? Contact cascades, so a direct-contact scan can miss it.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :4-5, Fig 1 :22, Fig 11 :49-50 and Fig 1 :27, Fig 2 :47. That is five short passages, all in `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`.
2. Check C2 against your own knowledge of market psychology and real investment: are Hayek's arbitrageur and Keynes's speculator the same activity against different residuals, or the same thing?
3. Name one real-outcome ranking of investment uses that does not move when prices are pushed, or say why none is available.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
