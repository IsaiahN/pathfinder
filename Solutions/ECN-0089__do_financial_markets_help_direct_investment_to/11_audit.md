## 1. Independent domain census (written from the residual line alone)

I redid the census before reading the primary's findings, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The gap between the actual allocation of capital and an allocation ranked by long-run productive use. Arity: two candidate explanations offered as alternatives. Symmetry: low, because one operates through dispersed facts and the other through expectations of others' expectations. Scale: nested, from a single capital budget to economy-wide flows. Per-slot residual, so Fig 1 :19 and :22 apply before any aggregate verdict.
- **Domain 1, signal frame.** Actors: dispersed holders of local knowledge, arbitrageurs, issuers. Currency: capital and information compressed into a quote. Anchor: realized productive outcome outside the price system. Nameable, so this is a domain.
- **Domain 2, reflexive frame.** Actors: short-horizon traders, managers, lenders. Currency: next conventional valuation and reputation. Anchor: for the frame itself, none; for the compound, the real outcome plus a horizon mismatch. It is legitimate only when nested under the compound anchor.
- **Domain 3, measurement frame.** Actors: researchers and reviewers. Currency: evidence and replication. Anchor: out-of-sample real outcomes, never agreement.
- **Union, not partition.** Neither explanation excludes the other. I reached this from Fig 11 (valence is a relation, so the same actor can occupy both roles) and Fig 3 (a chain that stops at the objective link can be reached from more than one upstream condition). The primary reached it from Fig 9 and Fig 1. Different starting figures, same boundary. That is low overlap between *routes*, but it is still one evidence pool.
- **Order by dependency.** Describe the residual and name the anchor before assigning valence; settle the objective link before planning a test; refuse a global verdict before minting any policy claim (Formula step 2). My order came from Fig 3 and Fig 11, the primary's from Fig 9 and Fig 3. They agree on the break at link 3.

## 4. Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, and Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the figure set, chiefly Fig 9 :4-5 and :57-58 (union), Fig 11 :49-51 (valence), Fig 1 :27 and Fig 2 :47 (anchor), Fig 3 :27 (objective link).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07` and `09`: the "or" is a union, valence is residual-relative, price cannot anchor, the objective link has no instrument.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the concrete claim an expert would test: for investment slot s, rank allocations with a non-price outcome measure, declare the residual, and report whether A or B dominates.

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- **The boundary of a slot.** "Per slot" is cheap to say and expensive to operationalise. Does a slot mean a firm, a sector, a financing round, or a horizon band? The figure text does not decide, and the packet must not pretend it does.
- **The threshold at which valence flips.** Fig 11 says valence changes with the residual, but it does not say at what horizon length speculation stops helping prices and starts harming allocation. That is an instrument question.
- **The identity of the non-price anchor.** Fig 9 :32 gives a kind of thing, never the answer. Naming a specific index would be a binding from the measurement frame, and would need a source gate and shadow test.

One thing survives cleanly: a global verdict is unavailable because the two conditions can both hold and because the market is a party to its own test. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the figures" as identity would set R_T to zero and commit the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
