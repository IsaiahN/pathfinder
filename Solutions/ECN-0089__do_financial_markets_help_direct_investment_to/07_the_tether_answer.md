# The Tether's answer on financial markets and investment

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the anchor and instrument findings, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading. No measurement was taken.

## 1. The residual it answers

There is a divergence between where capital goes and where it would go under full knowledge of long-run productive use. The two offered explanations are price discovery (A) and speculation with short-horizon incentives (B). What an answer would look like: a verdict per investment slot against a named non-price anchor, with each actor's valence declared relative to the residual chosen.

## 2. The shape

1. **The "or" is a union, not an exclusive choice.** A and B can both be live, in different slots and at different horizons. Fig 9 :57-58: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* So the output is a set of per-slot verdicts, never a global score.
2. **Actor valence is relative to the residual.** Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Speculation is a benefactor for the residual "does the price incorporate dispersed information?" and a malfactor for the residual "does capital reach long-run productive use?". No actor is good or bad by kind. The question becomes decidable only after the residual is declared.
3. **A price cannot be the anchor for this question.** Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Fig 2 :47: *"A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame."* The market is one of the two candidate mechanisms, so using its prices to judge itself is a circular measurement. Keynes's "convention" and Fig 10 :47's "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one" name the same failure.
4. **The objective link is uninstrumented.** Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* "Most productive uses" is usually swapped for a return or valuation proxy, which is the mechanism's own output. The chain breaks at step 3, and better return-based work cannot fix it.
5. **The two mechanisms are a functional group, not isomers.** They share `signal -> price -> allocation` but differ in the signal atom: dispersed information (A) versus second-order belief (B). See `06_isomers_and_order.md`. This is why they need separate checks.
6. **The two main literatures are not two witnesses.** They draw heavily on the same return data and the same canonical studies. Fig 2 :40: agreement is *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Genuine triangulation needs a witness that does not use returns.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that financial markets are net good or net bad. That is the global verdict refused in F1.
- Not that speculation is intrinsically distorting. That is refused by Fig 11 :49-51.
- Not that regulation, transaction taxes or any specific policy follows from the shape. Policy is downstream of a settled anchor, and the anchor is not settled here.
- Not that a non-price measure of productive use already exists. The owed instrument is characterised in effect terms only (Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*).
- Not a derivation of one mechanism from the other, and no new primitive or import that moves a wall.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A non-price instrument that ranks investment uses and does not move when the price-forming frames are pushed, plus at least one witness that does not draw on return data. The checks that can fire:

1. **Anchor test.** Does a real-outcome measure (physical capacity, throughput, measured productivity) rank a set of investments differently from their risk-adjusted returns, stably across slots? If it never differs, the price proxy may be adequate and F3 falls.
2. **Union test.** Does one residual exist for which both mechanisms return the same stable verdict on that anchor across independent slots? If yes, the union becomes a partition and F1 falls.
3. **Valence test.** Is there an actor whose valence is invariant across residuals? If yes, F2 falls.
4. **Cascade check.** Does a short-horizon funding shock reach real capacity three steps out? Fig 11 :52 says contact cascades; a direct-contact scan can miss it.

Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* The cost here is high, because investment allocation is a real-resource decision, so independent witnesses are worth paying for.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The derivation stops at the limit of the available instrument: the objective link has no independent measure. What is owed is an INWARD instrument improvement from something already returning a reading, not a new theory and not another survey of the same return data. The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. Indiscernibility is not identity; a refused global verdict is not an absence of an answer.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that financial markets are good or that speculation is bad. It says the question is a union of two conditions that can both be live in different investment slots, so a global verdict throws away the only signal that says where to look. It says an actor's valence depends on the residual you name: a trader who helps a price incorporate dispersed information can harm long-run capital allocation. It says a price cannot be the anchor for a question in which the market is one of the parties, because a frame cannot score itself with a quantity it produces. And it says the phrase "most productive uses" has no instrument independent of price, so the objective link in the chain is uninstrumented. What it offers is a shape, written refuters, and the kind of non-price instrument that would close the question. The slot stays open until a ground settles it.

## 7. Pre-registered refuters, restated for convenience

1. A single residual for which both mechanisms give the same stable verdict against a non-price anchor across independent slots.
2. An actor with invariant valence across all residuals.
3. A price-based quantity that ranks real productive allocation and does not move when the price-forming frames are pushed.
4. A lossless aggregation of per-slot residuals into a global verdict.

Any one firing withdraws the corresponding claim. None was run here.
