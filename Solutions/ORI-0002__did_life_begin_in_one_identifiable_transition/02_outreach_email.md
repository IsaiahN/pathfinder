**Subject:** One checkable output on the "one transition or many steps" question

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not
> asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output
> does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structure, `gap` on the
> exact boundary; not `grounded`):** the question "did life begin in one identifiable transition,
> or through many gradual steps?" is a union, not a partition. In the world the origin has the
> shape of a **route**: a sequence of bonded steps, autocatalytic cycles and catalysts, in a
> chemistry whose own compositions add no new primitive. The surplus comes from contact between
> differing frames, chemistry and geology and evolution, not from one closed sequence. The
> "single identifiable transition" side has a different shape: a transition is a **threshold
> relation between two quantities**, and a boundary defined by a researcher is a convention, not
> an object in the world. Life itself reads as a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met
> its ground; what ran was selection. The order of the route is not recoverable from a modern cell,
> only from the lineage.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that there was no chemistry-to-biology change, that gradualism wins a
> debate, or that I can name the first living thing. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
> The exact boundary is a `gap`: no instrument I could find returns a discriminating reading at any
> resolution. I am reporting that as unreached at this budget, not as proof that no boundary
> exists and not as silence.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a figure line naming a single primitive "life" whose arrival closes the residual;
> 2. an experiment that returns a discriminating reading of a unique boundary event with an anchor
>    that does not move when the definition changes;
> 3. a demonstration that the route and the cut share one anchor and one currency, so the union
>    collapses to a partition;
> 4. a demonstration that gradual composition alone adds a new atom to a closed chemistry.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract
> it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather
> not engage, no reply is needed.
