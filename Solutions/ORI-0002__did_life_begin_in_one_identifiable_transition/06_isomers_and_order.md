# Route and cut: bonds, order, level crossing and analogues

**Mode: `specified` on the derivation, `general` on the structural split.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks whether life began in one identifiable transition or through many gradual steps. The supporting derivation owes four things: (1) the bond types that make a route a route rather than a list; (2) the type of the "single transition"; (3) the level at which the chemistry-to-biology change occurs; and (4) analogues that test whether the shape transfers to other fields.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: the six of Fig 13 as states and structures (space, time, energy; spectrum, gradient, network). Bonds held: the seven, plus the unary negation operator. Atoms bound from the world frames: replicators, catalysts, compartments, codes, units of selection. No new primitive is available without import.

- **D1, route domain.** Actors: molecules, cycles, catalysts, compartments. Currency: production and persistence. Anchor: experiment. Question type: which bonds compose a route.
- **D2, level domain.** Actors: replicators and collectives. Currency: heredity and selection. Anchor: selection. Question type: which crossing changes the unit of selection.
- **D3, cut domain.** Actors: the categorising frame, the boundary predicate. Currency: comparison. Anchor: an instrument, currently missing. Question type: what kind of thing a "transition" is.

Order by dependency: a cycle before a replicator, a replicator before a unit of selection, a unit of selection before a retrospective label, and a discriminating instrument before any boundary name.

## DERIVATION

### Q1. What makes "many gradual steps" a route and not a list?

- Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**
- Fig 12 :70 (sequence, →): **"A then B; B needs A to have happened"**. Ordered; swapping changes the thing.
- Fig 12 :77 (production, ⇒): **"A produces B; B did not exist before"**. The second operand is absent until the first fires.
- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**
- Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**
- A flat list of prebiotic molecules can name a state. It cannot say that the output of one reaction re-entered as the input of another, that a catalyst survived its own use, or that a new molecule appeared that was absent before. Those are four different bond facts. The gradual route is therefore a **bonded arrangement**, and the number of steps is a budget question, not the variable.
- World check (secondary): the RNA-world source describes a gradual process reaching autocatalysis; Szathmáry 2015 describes autocatalytic networks, naked genes in compartments and chromosomes as separate formation phases. These corroborate the bond shape, not the specific chemistry.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** show that the chemistry-to-biology route is expressible as an unordered ingredient list with no typed bond and no order, with the same predictions. If so, the bond claim fails. Status: `specified`, triangulated for the figure side, unmeasured on the chemistry side.

### Q2. What type is "one identifiable transition"?

- Operators :48: **"a threshold relation between two quantities"**. This is the comparison bond ⋛.
- Fig 12 :87: **"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."**
- Fig 12 :112: **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."**
- A single identifiable transition would have to be either a production (⇒), a sequence (→), a comparison (⋛) or a level crossing (Fig 4). It cannot be an atom, because no figure line admits "life" as a primitive and Fig 13 :134 returns a shape, not a name.
- The most coherent reading is: a transition is **⋛ between two quantities at a level** (for example the fidelity of heredity against the error threshold, or the coupling between replicator fitness and collective fitness), **composed with a level crossing** in which the unit of selection changes. Szathmáry 2015 maps this onto the MLS1 to MLS2 shift.
- Fig 10 :47: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**. A definition of life that decides which physical systems are alive has silently become a verdict. That is a D3 failure, not a D1/D2 discovery.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a figure line in which a transition is a primitive atom, or show that ⋛ cannot apply at the level of a unit of selection. Status: `specified`, single-source on the type, triangulated on the convention rule.

### Q3. Are RNA-first, metabolism-first and membrane-first isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :97: **"Chemistry hit it first: silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different properties"** (the figure's own example is isomerism in chemistry).
- Over the **shared atom set** {replicator, catalyst, compartment, gradient, code, unit of selection}, the three programmes are isomers: same atoms, different bonds and order, different predicted pathways. Over the **narrow** sets each programme actually cites (RNA-first emphasises the replicator and code, metabolism-first the catalyst and gradient, membrane-first the compartment), they share some atoms but not all, so by Fig 12's definition they are **not** isomers; they are members of a **functional group**, Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- The stronger isomer claim is the one worth testing: all three over the full shared atom set, differing in which bond mediates which transition. Fig 13 :121: **"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."**
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if any programme requires an atom the others cannot express even over the full shared set (for example a code that cannot be composed from a catalyst plus a compartment), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. Status: `specified`, triangulated for the definition; unmeasured on the chemistry.

### Q4. At what level is the crossing?

- Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**
- Fig 4 :34-35: **"A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."**
- Fig 10 :68: **"Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between."**
- Fig 10 :70: **"The tree of life is what a non-invertible transform looks like from outside."**
- The chemistry-to-biology change is a crossing between scales: the actors change (molecules to replicators to collectives), the currency changes (flux to heredity), and the ground changes (experiment to selection). The transform is lossy and non-invertible, which is why the tree of life has branches and why the route cannot be replayed backwards from a modern cell. Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."** A settled ribosome is an atom for the cell; it was not an atom for the prebiotic pond.
- **Falsifier:** show that actors, currency and ground are unchanged across the proposed boundary, so the crossing is only a change of description. Status: `specified`, triangulated (Fig 4, Fig 10, Fig 12).

### Q5. Do the analogues corroborate or mislead?

- **Logic and human cognition, sorites.** *"The sorites paradox ... is a paradox that results from vague predicates."* Removing one grain from a heap does not cross a boundary; repeating enough times does. This corroborates the shape "route first, cut second" and demotes the demand for a unique first-living state to a line-drawing request. It is a structure analogue, not evidence about chemistry.
- **Physics, phase transitions.** *"The identification of the external conditions at which a transformation occurs defines the phase transition point."* A phase transition is located by a threshold on a control parameter, exactly the ⋛ shape from Q2. It corroborates the type claim. Phase transitions can be non-thermodynamic, with connection probability replacing temperature, so the analogue survives the move from molecules to networks.
- **Both analogues are low overlap** with the origins-of-life pool, so they are worth more than a fifth review of abiogenesis. Neither decides the biological verdict; the figures do.

## CONCLUSION

"Many gradual steps" is the right world-side shape only when the steps are typed bonds: sequence, production, catalyst and cycle, in a closed chemistry whose own compositions add no atom, with a union of differing frames supplying the surplus. "One identifiable transition" is the right question-side shape only when it is read as a threshold relation (⋛) plus a lossy level crossing, and when the researcher's cut is declared a convention rather than a discovery. RNA-first, metabolism-first and membrane-first are isomers over a shared atom set and a functional group over the narrow sets they actually use. The exact boundary remains a `gap` with an instrument owed. This rescues what is defensible in both sides of the question without inventing a first event.
