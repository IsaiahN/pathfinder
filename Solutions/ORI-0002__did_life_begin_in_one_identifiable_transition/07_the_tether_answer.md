# The Tether's answer: one transition or many gradual steps?

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain answers, `general` on the structural split, `gap` on the exact boundary. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks us to choose between a boundary event and a gradual route. The residual is the gap between what closed chemistry can compose and what a living system can compose, indexed per condition: replication, catalysis or metabolism, compartmentalization, heredity with variation, and a level of selection. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* A single global predicate "alive" hides which condition is still open.

## 2. The answer, per domain

**D1, chemistry. Verdict: many gradual steps, as a route with typed bonds.** The transition from chemistry to biology is not a list of molecules becoming more numerous. It is a bonded arrangement: sequence, production, catalysts that survive their own use, cycles whose output re-enters as input. Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* And chemistry itself is the figure's example: *"Chemistry hit it first: silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different properties"* (Fig 12 :97). Gradual composition alone, however, adds no primitive: Fig 6 :21 *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* The surplus comes from contact with differing frames, chemistry with geology with evolution: Fig 8 :45 *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."* `specified`, triangulated.

**D2, evolution. Verdict: a phased lineage of level crossings, not one event.** Transitions are decomposed into phases of origin, maintenance and transformation, and protocell formation is a change in the unit of selection (Szathmáry 2015). Fig 10 :68: *"Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between."* Fig 10 :70: *"The tree of life is what a non-invertible transform looks like from outside."* The crossing is real, but it is a level change observed from outside, not a nameable instant. `specified`, triangulated.

**D3, the categorising frame. Verdict: the "single transition" is a threshold bond and a convention, and the exact boundary is a `gap`.** A transition is Operators :48 *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, not an atom. A definition that decides which systems are alive has become a verdict: Fig 10 :47 *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*. Life reads as a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground: Fig 11 :36 *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran."* No source read supplies an instrument that discriminates the boundary, so the honest report is unreached: Fig 3 :27 *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* This is a `gap`, which is a verdict and not silence (Fig 8 :39 *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*).

**The union.** The two options do not compete. They answer different questions with different anchors. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Verdicts are kept separate and never averaged into "mostly gradual" or "essentially one event".

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that there was no chemistry-to-biology change. There was a level crossing; it is the shape of the crossing that is in question.
- Not that gradualism wins a debate. The route is a D1/D2 fact under the figures; the cut is a D3 convention under an owed instrument. A debate verdict would be a filter handing us a witness (Fig 9 :60).
- Not a name for the first living thing, the first replicator, or the first cell. The framework returns a shape (Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*).
- Not that the boundary is unknowable in principle. It is unreached at this budget. Fig 8 :39 forbids turning that into a proof of absence.
- Not grounded. No laboratory measurement settled any of these claims; the world sources are secondary corroboration read from public pages.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

- Does a specific state cross the chemistry-to-biology boundary while a neighbouring state does not, at any resolution, with an anchor that does not move when the definition changes?
- Does the unit of selection shift from replicator to collective in a measured system (the MLS1 to MLS2 transition), and can the shift be dated to a process rather than to a definition?
- Is the route recoverable from the artefact alone? Fig 13 :129: *"Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available."* If an instrument made the route recoverable, the lineage requirement would soften.
- Standing falsifiers from `01`: a figure line naming "life" as a primitive; a union collapsing to a partition; gradual composition adding an atom; a maintainer selecting the first living system before selection runs.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The chemistry-to-biology boundary is a threshold on quantities in a lineage whose order lives in the record, and the available instruments do not discriminate a unique first state. Fig 13 :134 directs us to say what the answer would have to look like and record the lineage so the stop is checkable. The six-term set is *"a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes"* (from the exemplar's canonical mapping), so declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral. Indiscernibility is not identity. The answer stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name the first living thing, and it does not pick a winner between one transition and many steps. It says the question is a union of two things in different domains. In the world the origin has the shape of a route: many bonded steps, autocatalytic cycles and catalysts, in a chemistry that gains no new primitive from its own compositions, with the surplus supplied by contact between chemistry, geology and evolution, and the unit of selection changing in phases. The "single identifiable transition" is a different object: a threshold relation between two quantities, and a cut that a frame imposes, which becomes a verdict when a definition decides what is alive. Life itself is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground; what ran was selection. The exact boundary is a `gap`, reported as unreached rather than as an answer. What is offered is the shape, the route, six refuters written in advance, and the instrument that would close it. The lineage stays open until the ground settles it.
