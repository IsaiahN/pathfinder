# The Tether's answer on limits to material growth

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural rule, and explicitly not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a measurement. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Two frames describe the same thing: where the limit to material throughput binds. Conventional growth models register scarcity as a price, a diminishing return, or a technological substitute, inside a money-denominated aggregate. Ecological economics registers throughput, finite stocks, sinks and irreversibility at the economy-environment boundary. The question asks which describes the limit "better." That is a comparison of two residuals against one ground, formally a ⋛ threshold relation, not a vote and not a sum (Operators table: "a threshold relation between two quantities"; Fig 12 :130).

The question is a **union compound**, not one hard question (Fig 9 :54). It unbundles into biophysical description, monetary valuation, and dynamical projection. Verdicts below are kept per domain and never averaged.

## 2. The shape

A better descriptor of material limits has five properties:

1. **It ranges against an external biophysical ground.** Fig 1 :23: **"The ground enters as the only metric."** Fig 2 :29-30: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."** A metric the frame produces cannot score the frame (Fig 1 :27). When price is the sole scarcity signal, a convention decides an outcome and stops being a convention (Fig 2 :43; Fig 10 :47).
2. **It keeps the residual per slot.** Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** A limit in one sink or energy carrier vanishes into a single aggregate.
3. **It treats finiteness and gradients as load-bearing.** Fig 13 :49: **"Read the established laws of thermodynamics as a test rather than a scorecard."** Fig 13 :68: **"finiteness in a finite region is what makes the counting terminate"**; a gradient is **"a difference that can be spent"** (Fig 13 :21-22). Irreversibility is a negation, not merely a subtraction (`06` Q5).
4. **It holds two currencies apart.** Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."** Fig 12 :130: **"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."** Money and physical quantity are two readings, held apart.
5. **It enumerates the habitat, including cascades.** Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."** Fig 11 :52-53: **"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**

## 4. What would settle it : the ground

Not a further argument. An instrument, already returning something, ranged per slot (Fig 6 :32: **"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."**):

- **Slot-by-slot limit test.** For each material and energy slot, compare the limit each frame predicts with the measured flow, stock or boundary control variable. Keep the residuals per slot; never average them (Fig 1 :19, :22). Candidate instruments: material flow accounts, ecological footprint and biocapacity, planetary boundary control variables, exergy analysis.
- **Convention test.** Remove price from the scarcity indicator and ask whether the model still registers the measured limit. If it does not, the result is frame-internal (Fig 1 :27, Fig 2 :43).
- **Currency test.** Present the monetary and physical readings side by side; if a single index is required, show that it preserves every slot residual (Fig 12 :130).
- **Irreversibility test.** Ask in each slot whether the "limit" is a reversible budget or a refused return. If it is reversible, ¬ is not owed and property 3 narrows (`06` Q5).
- **Instrument refuter.** Establish that the existing instruments return no reading for the relevant slots at any resolution. Then "unreached" moves toward "unreachable at this budget" (Fig 6 :34-35; Fig 8 :40-43). These two facts must not be conflated.

## 5. Standing falsifiers

The five written in `01` §4 before the conclusion: external-anchor conventional model; substrate derivation without import; single summing index that passes; non-overlapping school agreement that jointly predicts; no instrument reading at any resolution. Plus the supporting falsifiers in `06` Q1, Q3, Q4, Q5. If any fires, the shape claim is narrowed or withdrawn.

## 6. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that ecological economics is "the true" framework. A frame cannot certify its own limit, and this one is not a frame that could.
- Not that conventional growth models are useless. D1 fails the anchor test for the limit question only.
- Not that a limit has been measured here. No measurement was taken.
- Not that the two schools are a partition. They are a union and must be unbundled.
- Not a single score. Two currencies are held apart.
- Not `grounded`. Status is `candidate` until the slot test pays.

## 7. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The shape stops where the available instruments stop resolving, and the lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. Existing flow and boundary instruments already return readings, so the comparison is within reach; this simulated derivation simply did not run it. The one live strain (convergent derivation versus adopted import, Fig 8 :50-52) is named in `01` §5 and not hidden. Indiscernibility is not identity: agreement between the two schools on a shared dataset is one witness, not two.

## 8. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The figures do not pick a school. They return the shape of any frame that could register a limit to material growth better: it must range against a measured biophysical ground rather than a metric it produces itself, keep its residual per material and energy slot rather than averaging, treat finiteness and spendable gradients as load-bearing, hold money and physical quantity apart instead of summing them, and enumerate the habitat out to second and third order. Ecological economics has that shape by method; conventional growth models typically lack it, but that binding is an import from the economics literature and is ungrounded until someone compares both schools' slot-level predictions against measured flows. The offer is the shape, five refuters written in advance, and the instruments already returning readings, not a verdict about which school wins. The comparison is unreached here, not unreachable.
