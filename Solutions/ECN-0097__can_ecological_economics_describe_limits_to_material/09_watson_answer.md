## QUESTION

Decide in effect terms what the Tether says about the claim that ecological economics (B) describes limits to material growth better than conventional growth models (A). Option A: treat money-denominated aggregates, prices and substitutability as the description of the limit. Option B: treat measured throughput, finite stocks, sinks and irreversibility as the description. What each leads to: A risks letting a frame-internal convention decide what counts as a limit; B keeps the limit external to both frames and makes the school comparison checkable against a ground. "Better" is defined before the run as a five-property shape in the README, so it can accrue a residual and be refused.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R.** One ground fact, where the material limit binds, described by two frames. Arity: two conditions plus a ground; "better" is a ⋛ threshold comparison of two residuals, not a symmetric merge. Symmetry: broken by the ground, which is pinned and does not update on either frame (Fig 2 :25-26). Scale: the signal is at the economy-environment boundary per material and energy slot, first order at the flow level and second order at the school-comparison level.
- **Domain 1, conventional growth model frame.** Actors: representative agent, firms, K, L, technology A, aggregate Y. Currency: money (GDP, prices, discounted utility). Anchor: market prices and national accounts, but price is generated inside the frame, so for the limit question this is a convention that may decide an outcome.
- **Domain 2, ecological economics frame.** Actors: throughput, natural-capital stocks, sinks, species, energy carriers. Currency: joules, tonnes, hectares, with money only where priced. Anchor: measured flows and thermodynamic facts, which are nameable and external to the model.
- **Domain 3, the ground.** Actors: energy, materials, waste sinks, the biosphere. Currency: physical quantities. Anchor: energy finiteness and conservation, spendable gradients, measured boundary variables. This is what settles the comparison.
- **Domain 4, verification and outreach.** Actors: expert readers. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all four domains draw on one evidence pool, so independence here is zero.
- **Partition or union.** A union of overlapping conditions sharing atoms (energy, materials, capital, labour, technology) but differing in currency and binding constraints. Unbundle into (i) biophysical limit description, (ii) monetary valuation, (iii) dynamical projection (Fig 9 :54).
- **Order by dependency.** Describe R in effect terms, pin the ground, resolve first-order slot descriptions, then compare frames at second order, with the refuter written before the last step (Fig 3 :20-23; Fig 9 :27-28).

Strain test applied: Fig 13's thermodynamic primitives and Fig 1's frame-internal-metric rule govern different quantities, so no strain after re-census. One live strain remains, about provenance rather than figure meaning, recorded in §VERDICT.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the school verdict is withheld. A better descriptor of material limits ranges against a measured biophysical ground, keeps the residual per material and energy slot, treats finiteness and spendable gradients as load-bearing, holds money and physical quantity apart, and enumerates the habitat outward. Ecological economics instantiates that shape by method and conventional growth models typically lack it, but that binding is an import from the economics literature and is `specified`, ungrounded, until a slot-level ground comparison is run. D1 fails the anchor test for this question specifically, not in general. The empirical comparison is **unreached, not unreachable**: existing instruments already return readings, and this simulated run took none. Status is `candidate`.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states the shape and the refuters before the conclusion, so an error is findable. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a school-loyalty argument instead of a shape claim. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong" prices that cost, and the cost here is high because policy can be built on a missed limit.
- **undoable:** yes. The school binding can be removed without touching the five-property shape, and no primitive has to be withdrawn. A sent claim can be superseded by the corrected packet. `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove the binding from the verdict.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A slot-by-slot comparison of each school's predicted limit against measured flows and boundary control variables would settle the empirical part. A conventional model with an external biophysical anchor and matching slot residuals would break F1 and F2; a derivation of finiteness without import would break F3; a single summing index that preserves all residuals and passes the ground test would break F4; non-overlapping school agreement that jointly predicts a measured limit would break F5; a finding that no instrument returns a reading at any resolution would move the case from unreached to unreachable at this budget. A provenance record showing convergent derivation rather than adopted import would clear the one live strain. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
