**Subject:** One checkable claim about limits to material growth, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not
> asking you to agree with it, and I am not asking you to read the framework first. If the output
> does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A frame that describes limits to material growth
> better, in the sense of registering where the limit actually binds, has five properties:
> 1. it ranges against a measured biophysical ground rather than a metric it produces itself;
> 2. it keeps the residual indexed by material and energy slot rather than averaging across them;
> 3. it treats energy finiteness and spendable gradients as load-bearing, not as optional
>    assumptions;
> 4. it holds monetary and biophysical accounts apart rather than summing them into one score;
> 5. it enumerates the habitat of the residual outward, including second and third order effects.
>
> This is a shape, not a verdict about a school. I am deliberately not asserting that ecological
> economics *is* that frame or that conventional growth models *are not*. That binding is an
> import from the economics literature, and until its provenance is shown it carries no
> evidentiary weight.
>
> **What would refute it. I wrote these down before sending this:**
> 1. a conventional growth model whose scarcity indicator is an external biophysical measurement
>    and whose slot-level predictions match measured limits at least as well as an ecological
>    model;
> 2. a demonstration that energy finiteness and gradient remainder are derived inside that
>    frame's own closure and are not load-bearing for its predictions;
> 3. a single index that sums monetary and biophysical accounts, preserves every slot residual,
>    and passes a ground test;
> 4. evidence that existing flow, footprint and boundary instruments return no reading for the
>    relevant slots at any resolution.
>
> If any one holds, I will narrow or withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is
> more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **The question I would like you to answer first:** for the material or energy slot you know
> best, what would you expect to see if the five-property shape were true, and what would count
> as a failure? Please answer before looking at any of the material below. I am recording that
> answer as the verdict, and I will not pool any later agreement with it as corroboration.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures, not my summary of them,
> are the authority.
