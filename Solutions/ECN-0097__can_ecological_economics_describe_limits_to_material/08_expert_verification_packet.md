# Expert verification packet : the Tether on limits to material growth

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ecological-economics researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural rule, not `grounded`.** No measurement in this packet compares the two schools. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A better descriptor of limits to material growth has five properties: it ranges against a measured biophysical ground rather than a metric it produces itself; it keeps the residual per material and energy slot rather than averaging; it treats energy finiteness and spendable gradients as load-bearing; it holds monetary and biophysical accounts apart rather than summing them; and it enumerates the habitat out to second and third order. Ecological economics instantiates this shape by method, conventional growth models typically do not, but that school binding is an import and is withheld from the verdict until a ground comparison is run. The packet offers the shape, five refuters written in advance, and the instruments already returning readings.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 : Framework-internal; checkable by reading the figures

- **Statement:** the anchor must be external to both frames, and the residual must be kept per slot. A frame-internal money metric and an aggregated average both fail for this question.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29-30 : **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."** Fig 1 :23 : **"The ground enters as the only metric."** Fig 1 :19 : **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22 : **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** Fig 2 :43 : **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."**
- **Refuter:** exhibit a conventional growth model whose scarcity indicator is an external biophysical measurement, not a price, and whose slot-level predictions match measured limits at least as well as an ecological model. If it exists, C1 narrows from structural to typical.

### C2 : Economics-facing; checkable against the two literatures

- **Statement:** as ordinarily written the two frames are a functional group, not strict isomers (Fig 12 :123 : **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**). Rewritten over the full shared atom set `{energy, materials, capital, labour, technology, sinks, time}` they become a real isomer claim (Fig 12 :117 : **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**).
- **What to check:** can both frames be expressed over that shared atom set with only the bonding changed? Does either require an atom the other cannot express, for example a sink with no monetary proxy or a price with no physical referent?
- **Refuter:** if either frame requires an atom the other cannot express, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. This falsifier was written before the run.

### C3 : Measurement-facing; checkable against existing instruments

- **Statement:** two currencies cannot be summed into one yardstick, and irreversibility is a negation rather than a subtraction, so finiteness must be load-bearing.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :125 : **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."** Fig 12 :130 : **"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."** Fig 13 :68 : **"finiteness in a finite region is what makes the counting terminate"**; Operators table: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."
- **Refuter:** exhibit a single index that sums monetary and biophysical accounts, preserves every slot residual and passes a ground test; or show that every relevant "irreversibility" is a reversible budget given enough energy. Either result breaks C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that ecological economics is the true framework, or that conventional models are useless. D1 fails the anchor test for the limit question only.
- Not that a limit has been measured in this packet. No measurement was taken.
- Not that the schools form a partition. They are a union and must be unbundled into biophysical description, monetary valuation and dynamical projection (Fig 9 :54).
- Not a single score. Two currencies are held apart.
- Not `grounded`. Status is `candidate`.
- Not that this second seat is independent. It shares the primary's evidence pool; its agreement is internal consistency, not corroboration (Fig 2 :40).

## 5. What would settle it

An instrument, not an argument (Fig 6 :32: **"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."**):

1. **Slot test:** for each material and energy slot, compare each frame's predicted limit with a measured flow, stock or boundary control variable; keep residuals per slot.
2. **Convention test:** remove price from the scarcity indicator and see whether the model still registers the measured limit.
3. **Currency test:** show that any single index preserves every slot residual, or keep the two readings apart.
4. **Irreversibility test:** decide per slot whether the limit is a reversible budget or a refused return.
5. **Instrument refuter:** establish that no existing instrument returns a reading for the relevant slots at any resolution, which moves the case from unreached to unreachable at this budget.
