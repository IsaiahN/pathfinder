## Wider literature that bears on the question

1. **Thermodynamic foundation.** Georgescu-Roegen's *The Entropy Law and the Economic Process* (1971) is the source of the claim that economic scarcity is rooted in physical reality and that low entropy is irreversibly degraded into high entropy. This is secondary corroboration for F3, not part of the figure verdict. The Wikipedia article read above also notes that his thermodynamics contained errors disputed by physicists, so treat the *framework's* substrate claim (Fig 13) as primary and the historical argument as a witness with a known blemish.
2. **Measurement critique.** Goodhart's law is the outside-field witness for F1 and F4: a money metric produced by the frame and then used as its own yardstick is a measure turned target. Fig 1 :27 makes the same point structurally.
3. **Existing ground instruments.** Material flow accounts, ecological footprint and biocapacity, planetary boundary control variables and exergy analysis are already returning readings. Under Fig 6 :32 this makes the school comparison reachable rather than underivable. The 2020 Herrington study and the CSIRO comparison of *The Limits to Growth* with thirty years of data are examples of ground-facing model evaluation, though both are contested and neither is used here as a verdict.
4. **The bundle warning.** *The Library of Babel* essay explicitly raises the possibility that a question is "a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one," which is the figure's union-not-partition finding (Fig 9 :54) in a different register.
