**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and the five refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` sections 3 to 4 are stated cleanly. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** Check one discriminator shape before you decide whether the ancestry claim is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** suppose we had a confirmed biological signature from Mars, Venus, or an icy moon. The question is whether that biota shares ancestry with Earth life. The structural claim is that **the molecular contents cannot decide this**. Two lineages that never shared an ancestor can converge on the same molecules: the same genetic polymer, the same handedness, even a similar genetic code. A lineage that did share an ancestor and was transported to a new world can look identical in those same respects. Convergence and adopted ancestry look the same in the contents. Only the *record* separates them.
>
> So the discriminator is not a molecule. It is a **kind of character**: an arbitrary, historically contingent feature that function or physics does not force. In practice that means things like a shared non-optimal codon assignment, or sequence homology in regions that are not functionally constrained, rather than a shared catalytic site or a shared set of ingredients. A catalytic site can evolve independently more than twenty times; a shared arbitrary quirk is much harder to converge on.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that DNA means relatedness, that left-handed amino acids mean relatedness, or that a physically possible panspermia route is evidence. Each of those is consistent with both hypotheses. Handedness in particular can be produced by deterministic seeding from space, so it is an ambiguous slot, not a verdict. I am also not claiming that we have a returned sample with the relevant measurement. This is a shape and a route, not a laboratory result.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written before this message):**
> 1. Two independently derived biota are shown to share an arbitrary, non-functionally-forced marker with high probability, so contents do discriminate after all.
> 2. A discriminating instrument shows identical arbitrary codon quirks and non-functional sequence homologies, confirmed by independent transfer physics, settling shared ancestry at the ground. That would retire the "indiscernible by contents" framing.
> 3. A documented Earth-derived contamination route fully accounts for the shared markers. The claim is withdrawn as an artifact.
> 4. Someone reports "independent origin" from a null result without first excluding budget and depth. That is the no-proof-of-absence error; the finding reverts to `unreached`.
> 5. The supposed independent character classes turn out to draw on the same underlying fact, so triangulation collapses to one witness.
>
> I wrote these down before contacting you. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
