# The Tether's answer on shared ancestry with Earth life

**Mode: `specified` on the discriminator shape and on every per-domain verdict, `general` on the structural constraints, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading. Figure quotes are verbatim from `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` and cited as `Fig N :line`.

## 1. The residual it answers

A biological signature has been found on Mars, Venus, or an icy moon, and what is undecided is the **source of its lineage**. Whether the signature is biological is sibling question ORI-0087 and is granted here. The residual is per character: polymer identity, monomer handedness, codon assignment, shared complex machinery, energy currency, membrane lipids, isotopic fractionation.

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

The two hypotheses are Option A, shared ancestry with Earth life (an adopted import across habitats), and Option B, a second independent origin (a convergent derivation). This is a **union compound**, not one hard question (Fig 9 :54-58), so it is unbundled before search. Each character gets its own verdict, never averaged with another.

## 2. The shape

1. **Contents do not decide ancestry; the record does.** Fig 8 :51: **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."** A biota with DNA, left-handed amino acids and a near-standard codon table is precisely the case where both hypotheses return the same reading. Fig 8 :50: **"But shared atoms are not the signature. Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."**

2. **The discriminator is a kind of character, not a molecule.** It is arbitrary, historically contingent, and not forced by chemistry or physics. In practice: a shared non-optimal codon assignment or variant quirk, or sequence homology in non-functionally-constrained regions. Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."** A catalytic motif will not do: identical catalytic triads evolved independently more than twenty times (convergent evolution entry, 2026).

3. **The residual is read per slot and the verdicts are never averaged.** Fig 1 :19 and Fig 1 :22. A single reported percentage of identity to Earth life is the named error, not an answer; it aggregates away the very slot that would discriminate.

4. **The low-overlap character classes are ranged separately against the sample.** Chemistry (handedness), molecular information (codon map, non-functional sequence), phylogenetics (shared derived characters), planetary dynamics (transfer routes), and mission operations (contamination chain). Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."** Fig 8 :45: **"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."** Two labs reading the same report are one frame; two instruments pointed at the same sample are two.

5. **Transfer is physically possible, so shared ancestry is a live option.** Worth et al. (2013) conclude that any life found on the outer moons cannot be assumed to be of independent origin. But possibility is a budget filter, never a witness: Fig 9 :60 **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

6. **Contamination is a habitat-substitution failure.** Fig 11 :70: **"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."** The spacecraft, the laboratory and the sequencer are part of the habitat of the residual and must be enumerated (Fig 11 :43-48), not subtracted. Fig 11 :69: **"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts."**

7. **A null is `unreached`, not `independent origin`.** Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** Fig 6 :27: **"Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."** Three questions are asked in order: was it in the closure and unfound, can the instrument be sharpened from one already returning something, and only then is genuine absence considered.

8. **The held term must be refusable.** Operators table :58: **"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."** If shared ancestry is false, the owed term is a refusal (a second origin), not a more elaborate homology story. The exact negation stays open if partial ancestry with later divergence is allowed; that is declared, not hidden.

9. **Instrument reach grows from an existing reading.** Fig 6 :32: **"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."** A sequencer lineage exists; an ancestry-discriminating reading is a sharpening of it, not a new instrument specified from an account of Mars.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that DNA, RNA, or any particular polymer implies relatedness. Identical contents are the *problem*, not the evidence (Fig 8 :51).
- Not that left-handed amino acids or right-handed sugars decide ancestry. A deterministic extraterrestrial seeding mechanism predicts the same reading (homochirality entry, 2026).
- Not that panspermia happened anywhere. Transfer feasibility is a plausibility filter with zero verdict weight (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that a similarity percentage, a shared gene, or a shared enzyme is a verdict. All are ingredient-level or function-level readings (Fig 12 :94).
- Not that an unreadable record is an absent record. Fig 8 :39 and Fig 9 :44 forbid that promotion.
- Not `grounded`. No returned sample with the relevant source-discriminating measurement exists in this derivation.
- Not an answer to ORI-0087 (is the signature biological?). That is granted here.
- Not a claim that the framework is the only true account of biological relatedness.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green cannot comment on the source; the spectrometer settled that case, and the answer stays unreached until a better instrument exists. Here the corresponding tests are:

- **Record test:** does the alien codon map share *non-optimal, arbitrary* assignments with Earth life, beyond what function predicts? A shared arbitrary quirk supports Option A; a systematically different but viable map supports Option B.
- **Non-functional homology test:** do sequences in weakly constrained regions align beyond chance, after contamination control? This is the tree-of-life record (Fig 10 :70: **"The tree of life is what a non-invertible transform looks like from outside."**).
- **Contamination exclusion:** enumerate the full contact chain (spacecraft bioburden, reagents, adapters, handlers, return capsule) and show that shared markers cannot be Earth-derived artifacts (Fig 11 :68-70).
- **Mechanism discriminator:** identify whether the shared handedness is a forced sign (cosmic chiral field) or a local accident. Only the latter regains discriminating power for Option A.
- **Three-way null discipline:** before naming Option B, exclude budget failure (SEARCHED), then depth (INWARD), and only then consider genuine absence (Formula step 7).

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The union horizon is the current state of instruments, and being below that horizon is a report that nobody has reached it yet, not a settled verdict (How Intelligence Actually Works, 2026). No Mars, Venus or icy-moon sample with a measured arbitrary-record comparison is in evidence here. The answer stops at the shape of the discriminator and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Indiscernibility is not identity.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say whether a particular Martian, Venusian or icy-moon biota is related to us, and it gives no similarity percentage. It says that molecular contents cannot decide ancestry: a second, independent origin can converge on the same DNA, the same handedness and a similar genetic code, so a shared biota and a shared ancestor look the same in the ingredients. What separates them is the record, arbitrary historical choices that function did not force, such as a non-optimal codon assignment or homology in sequence regions that are not constrained. So the right question for a mission is not whether the biota uses DNA, but whether it carries the same arbitrary record after contamination has been excluded. Transfer between worlds is physically possible, so shared ancestry stays on the table, but possibility is not evidence. A null result is reported as unreached at the current instrument and budget, never as proof of a second genesis. What the framework offers is the shape of the discriminator, five refuters written in advance, and the names of the instruments that would have to return a reading. The lineage stays open until the ground settles it.
