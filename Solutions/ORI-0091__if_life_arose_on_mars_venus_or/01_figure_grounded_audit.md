## 0. The residual in effect terms

The question is not whether a signature is biological. That is sibling question ORI-0087. The residual here is the **source of the lineage** behind a signature that is already granted to be biological.

Let Γ be the molecular library a biota can express. Let the hypothesis H_A (shared ancestry) predict a library Γ_A and H_B (independent origin) predict Γ_B. A mission returns o′. Define per character slot s:

> R_s = |Γ(b,a)_s − o′_s|

Figure 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Figure 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** So the answer cannot be a single "percentage similarity to Earth life." It is a vector of per-character verdicts: genetic polymer, monomer handedness, codon-to-amino-acid mapping, genetic machinery (ribosome, tRNA), energy currency, membrane lipids, and isotopic fractionation. Each slot is ranged separately against the anchor.

Figure 1 :23: **"The ground enters as the only metric."** The anchor here is the measured sample and its repeatable readings, not the prior probability of panspermia and not the consensus of interpreters (Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."*).

**What would count as an answer, bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"):** a *shape for a discriminator*, that is a character class whose readings differ under H_A and H_B at a resolution an instrument already returns something at. Binding that shape as a term is what makes it refusable; an unbound preference for "DNA means related" predicts nothing and no check in the loop can see it (Fig 5 :62-63: *"A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term"*).

## 1. Domain census, before any figure line is chosen

### R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** two hypotheses (H_A shared ancestry, H_B independent origin) evaluated over a vector of many character slots. Not one measurement.
- **Symmetry:** asymmetric. Shared ancestry is a *historical record* (an adopted import in the figures' vocabulary); independent origin is a *convergent derivation*. The two can produce identical contents, so the relation is not symmetric in what it licenses: finding the record settles H_A, while failing to find it does not settle H_B (Fig 8 :39).
- **Scale:** molecular (monomer chirality, codon mapping) to cellular (machinery, membranes) to planetary (habitat) to Solar-System (transfer) to deep time (lineage divergence). A verdict at one scale does not copy to another (Fig 10 :68: *"Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between."*).

### One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor

1. **D1, molecular biochemistry.** Actors: nucleic acids, amino acids, proteins, lipids, ATP, homochirality, the codon table, ribosome and tRNA. Currency: sequence and bond-arrangement information. Anchor: a measured polymer structure and its reading. Nameable, so this is a domain.
2. **D2, lineage and phylogeny.** Actors: lineages, a last common ancestor, horizontal transfer, descendants. Currency: heritable variation and descent; shared *derived* characters. Anchor: a rooted tree inferred from characters that are not functionally forced. Fig 10 :70: **"The tree of life is what a non-invertible transform looks like from outside."** Nameable.
3. **D3, planetary transfer and habitat.** Actors: impact ejecta, radiation, the interplanetary medium, Mars, Venus, Europa, Enceladus. Currency: viable material crossing between habitats. Anchor: measured ejection, survival and re-impact data (Worth et al. 2013; Frontiers 2020). Nameable.
4. **D4, instrument and reach.** Actors: mass spectrometer, chromatograph, sequencer, isotope-ratio instrument, sample-return chain. Currency: observation o′ and residual R. Anchor: the instrument reading. Fig 6 :32: **"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."** Nameable.
5. **D5, verification and interpretation.** Actors: independent instruments and labs, peer review. Currency: independent residuals, not agreement. Anchor: the sample, never the consensus. Fig 2 :35. Nameable.

No domain lacks a nameable anchor, so no domain is declared a `gap` at the census stage.

### Partition or union

**Union, not partition.** "Shares ancestry" is a bundle of overlapping conditions: arbitrary-record match, molecular homology, chirality, mechanistic plausibility of transfer, and contamination control. Figure 9 :55: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*; Fig 9 :54: *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* A single lever (for example, "both use DNA") clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. The domains above are therefore unbundled, then ordered by dependency.

### Dependency order (Fig 3)

1. Perception and vocabulary: can the instrument read a polymer at all? Fig 3 :5, :7. Below this break a reading is of nothing (Fig 3 :22).
2. Describe R in effect terms before naming a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).
3. Cheap, discriminating readings first: handedness, elemental and isotopic composition (D1, D4).
4. Sequence and codon mapping (D1, D2), which require step 3 to have returned something.
5. Phylogenetic reconstruction and transfer physics (D2, D3).
6. Settlement at the ground (D5), where elimination compounds and the ground decides (Fig 9 :38, :42).

## 3. Findings F1 to F11

### F1 - Convergence and adopted import are indiscernible by contents (decisive, `general`)

Figure 8 :50-51:

> **"But shared atoms are not the signature. Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."**
> **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."**

Map the figures' vocabulary onto the biological question. H_A (shared ancestry) is an **adopted import**: one lineage's library crossed into a new habitat. H_B (independent origin) is a **convergent derivation**: a second closure reached similar atoms separately. Figure 8 says the *contents* cannot tell them apart. A biota with DNA, L-amino acids, D-sugars and a near-standard codon table is exactly the case where the two hypotheses deliver the same reading.

Figure 13 :121: **"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."** The figure gives the chemistry precedent directly: *"In chemistry this has been established since Liebig and Wöhler found two substances with the same composition and different properties... and Berzelius named it isomerism in 1830"* (Fig 12 :97-98). Figure 8 :50 and Fig 13 :121 share that chemistry example, so this is one evidence pool, not two independent witnesses; the triangulation is with the *convergent evolution* literature (Wikipedia, 2026) and the *homochirality* entry (Wikipedia, 2026), which are genuinely separate fields.

**Consequence:** the verdict is not carried by any ingredient list. It is carried by the **record**: arbitrary, historically contingent features that convergence does not reproduce. This is the whole answer in one line, and it is `general`.

**Refuter:** exhibit a case where two independently derived biota reliably share an arbitrary, non-functionally-forced marker with high probability. Then contents would discriminate after all and F1 fails.

### F2 - The residual is per character, never a similarity score (`general`)

Figure 1 :19 and :22, quoted in section 0. A mission that reports "70 percent identical to Earth life" has committed the named error: it has averaged across slots and thrown away the signal that says *which* character discriminates.

World anchor: the genetic code entry (Wikipedia, 2026) reports *"The genetic code is highly similar among all organisms and can be expressed in a simple table with 64 entries"* and separately that variants exist. "Highly similar" is an aggregate. The discriminating information is *which* codons changed and whether the changes are shared derived quirks. That is a per-slot question.

**Refuter:** show a validated assay whose output is a single scalar similarity and whose per-character decomposition adds no discriminating information. Until then, per-slot verdicts are mandatory.

### F3 - The shape of the discriminator: arbitrary record, not function (`specified`)

Figure 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."** Figure 9 :27-28: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."**

Binding the shape (Formula "Direction"): a discriminating character is one where (i) the reading differs between H_A and H_B, (ii) the difference is not forced by chemistry or physics (otherwise convergence reproduces it), and (iii) an instrument already returning a reading can be sharpened to resolve it (Fig 6 :32, :34-35: *"Nobody specified a telescope from an account of Mars. A wandering point in the sky was already a reading, and every sharper instrument was built on the residual the last one produced."*).

Candidate slots, each with its own verdict and never averaged:

| slot | what a match would mean | why it is not decisive alone | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| identical codon-to-amino-acid mapping with the same variant quirks | shared historical accident, strong for H_A | a functional optimum could in principle be reached twice; check whether the quirks are non-optimal | candidate, `specified` |
| sequence homology in non-functional or weakly constrained regions | shared derived characters, strong for H_A | needs enough divergence-aware phylogeny; horizontal transfer complicates the tree | candidate, `specified` |
| same molecular machinery (ribosome, tRNA, synthetases) | shared complex record | complex machines can have a single origin and then diverge beyond recognition | supporting only |
| same handedness (L-amino acids, D-sugars) | consistent with transfer | deterministic extraterrestrial seeding can produce the same bias (homochirality entry, 2026) | ambiguous, see F4 |
| same genetic polymer (DNA/RNA) | consistent with transfer | convergent chemistry can arrive at the same polymer (convergent evolution, molecular level, 2026) | supporting only |

**Refuter:** the shape fails if no character class can be shown where H_A and H_B predict different readings at any resolution an instrument already returns something at. That is a genuine `gap` verdict and must be stated plainly if reached.

### F4 - Homochirality is an ambiguous slot, not a verdict (strain recorded)

The homochirality entry (Wikipedia, 2026) states that *"abiotic chemical processes typically produce racemic mixtures of chiral compounds"* while *"many biologically-synthesized compounds are homochiral,"* with 19 of 20 proteinogenic amino acids left-handed and biological sugars right-handed. It also records that *"Some analyses of organic molecules in extraterrestrial materials... identified small enantiomeric enrichments in a few L-amino acids and D-sugars, leading to the proposition that initial enantiomeric bias could have been seeded on Earth from space,"* and that the amino acids in Bennu and Ryugu show no chiral bias.

So the same observation (L-amino acids on two worlds) is predicted by H_A (descent) **and** by a deterministic abiotic seeding mechanism that is not ancestry. Figure 8 :51: the contents are identical; the record decides. Figure 9 :32: a reading gives a kind of thing, not the answer.

**Handling:** chirality is a candidate binding from the chemistry frame with zero standalone verdict weight. It becomes evidence only when combined with a source-discriminating observation (Fig 9 :32; sibling ORI-0087's metamerism rule). This strain is named here and returned to the census, not used to flip the verdict.

**Refuter:** a demonstrated mechanism that makes the *sign* of the enantiomeric excess contingent on an arbitrary local accident rather than on a universal chiral field. Then identical handedness across two worlds would regain discriminating power for H_A.

### F5 - The frozen accident is the discriminating record, with a convergence caveat

The genetic code entry (Wikipedia, 2026) records the *"frozen accident" argument for the universality of the genetic code*, while noting that Crick in 1968 *"still stated that the universality of the genetic code in all organisms was an unproven assumption"* and that variants were subsequently found. A shared arbitrary mapping is therefore exactly the kind of non-forced record F3 asks for.

Caveat from a different field: the convergent evolution entry (Wikipedia, 2026) reports that *"The chemical and physical constraints on enzyme catalysis have caused identical triad arrangements to evolve independently more than 20 times in different enzyme superfamilies"* and that convergence occurs at the DNA and amino-acid sequence level. So identity at a *functional* site is not identity of record. The discriminator must read the **arbitrary mapping and the non-functional sequence**, not the catalytic motif.

This is why the figure's bond distinction matters: Fig 12 :94 **"The arrangement is the substance."** Two biota with the same molecular ingredients arranged under a different historical record are Fig 12 :117 *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance."*

**Refuter:** a quantified model showing that the observed codon map is the unique physical optimum, so that a second origin would converge on it. Then the codon map loses verdict weight.

### F6 - Transfer is physically possible, so the prior cannot be set to zero (`specified`)

Worth, Sigurdsson and House (2013, *Astrobiology*) conclude from n-body simulations: *"searches for life on these objects should keep in mind the necessity of determining whether life arose independently or descended from common ancestors to Earth life. Any life found there cannot be assumed to be of independent origin."* They estimate transfer of viable material from Earth and Mars to the terrestrial planets and, rarely, to the moons of Jupiter and Saturn, with significant re-impact in the first million years. The Frontiers news item (Sammicheli, 2020) reports that *"The results suggest that radioresistant Deinococcus could survive during the travel from Earth to Mars and vice versa, which is several months or years in the shortest orbit."*

Figure 11 :66: **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."** Panspermia is precisely a substitution of habitat: the lineage crosses, the habitat changes, and the lineage inherits actors it did not choose. Figure 11 :68-69 names the two silent failure modes: **"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"** and **"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts."**

**Consequence:** H_A cannot be dismissed a priori. The transfer domain supplies *plausibility*, never a verdict. A physically possible route is a budget filter, not a witness (Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*).

**Refuter:** a demonstrated physical barrier that makes transfer of viable material between the relevant bodies impossible at the relevant epoch. That would remove H_A as a live option and change the question.

### F7 - Contamination is a substituted-habitat failure, not a footnote (`specified`)

Figure 11 :70: **"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."** A laboratory or spacecraft carrying Earth-derived nucleic acid into a sample is a substituted habitat: a positive reading proves the wiring of the assay, not the capability of an alien biota.

The planetary protection entry (Wikipedia, 2026) defines *"Forward contamination is the transfer of viable organisms from Earth to another celestial body"* and records COSPAR Resolution 26's warning that *"contamination of this planet would make such a search far more difficult and possibly even prevent for all time an unequivocal result."* It also notes that the Mars Sample Return receiving facility must protect samples from terrestrial contamination *"that could confuse the sensitive life detection tests,"* and even flags gene transfer agents that *"could potentially transfer DNA from martian microorganisms to terrestrial microorganisms if they have a shared evolutionary ancestry."*

Figure 11 :43-48: enumerate the habitat, do not compose it; *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."* The contamination chain is part of the habitat of the residual and must be enumerated: spacecraft bioburden, laboratory reagents, sequencing adapters, human handlers, and the return chain.

**Refuter:** a demonstrated contamination route that fully accounts for the observed sequences. Then the ancestry claim is withdrawn as an artifact, not decided either way.

### F8 - A null result is unreached, not absence (`general`)

Figure 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** Figure 9 :44: **"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."** Figure 6 :27: *"Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."*

Failure to find shared arbitrary markers has three distinct readings and they must not be merged:

1. **Unreachable at this budget** (Formula step 7): the term is in the closure and was not found; the remedy is more search with independent searchers (Fig 6 :50).
2. **Depth**: the instrument cannot yet resolve the character; the remedy is to sharpen an instrument already returning something (Fig 6 :32, :34-35).
3. **Genuine absence**: the term is not in the closure; H_B is supported, but only after the first two are excluded.

Importing a new hypothesis against a budget limit adds an unneeded atom and costs more than starting from primitives (Fig 6 :52; Fig 8 :40-43). This is why the shape must be bound before the run.

**Refuter:** a sealed, fully swept search space at a stated resolution. Since Figure 9 :44 says no frame is a sealed room, claiming one is itself a refutable claim.

### F9 - No single test decides; triangulation with independent instruments does (`general`)

Figure 2 :31-32: independence is *"how little they draw on the same evidence"* and *"is what makes agreement mean something."* Figure 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."** Figure 8 :45: **"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."**

The low-overlap frames available here are: chemistry (handedness, monomer distribution), information/molecular biology (codon map, non-functional sequence), phylogenetics (shared derived characters), planetary dynamics (transfer routes), and mission operations (contamination chain). Each is ranged separately against the same sample. Two labs reading the same report are one frame; two instruments pointed at the same sample are two (Fig 2 :31-32).

Figure 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."** A consensus statement signed by many origin-of-life researchers is a room, not an anchor.

**Refuter:** a demonstration that two supposedly independent character classes draw on the same underlying physical fact, so their agreement carries no information (Fig 13 :38-39). That is what the F4 strain tests.

### F10 - The held term must be refusable (`general`)

The Operators table :58: **"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."** Figure 5 :53: **"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so."**

If we hold "shared ancestry" and it is false, what is owed is a **refusal** (a second independent origin), not a more elaborate explanation of similarities. The refusal is a negation, not a seventh bond, and it removes the standing of the held term to be composed over (Fig 5 :54-56). Negation is the one unary operator (Operators :70-74). A framework that can only accumulate homology-like similarities cannot carry the correction.

**Refuter:** show that "independent origin" is not the negation of "shared ancestry" but a different claim (for example, partial ancestry with later divergence). Then the refusal must be restated more carefully. This is recorded as an open item, not hidden.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per Fig 5 :62-66, the sought-for shape is bound as an ordinary term and committed before the run. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Record collapse.** Two independently derived biota are shown to share an arbitrary, non-functionally-forced molecular marker with high probability, so contents discriminate after all. F1 and F3 fail.
2. **Attribution reversal.** A discriminating instrument shows identical arbitrary codon-table quirks and non-functional sequence homologies in both biota, and independent transfer physics is confirmed. Then H_A is settled and the "indiscernible by contents" framing is refused at the ground.
3. **Contamination fire.** A documented Earth-derived contamination route fully accounts for the shared markers. The ancestry claim is withdrawn as an artifact.
4. **Null promoted to absence.** Anyone reports "independent origin" on the basis of no detected homology without first excluding budget and depth. That is the Fig 8 :39 error and the finding is retracted to `unreached`.
5. **Union failure.** The character classes turn out not to be independent (Fig 13 :38-39), so triangulation collapses to one witness.

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the retraction is kept visible here. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with F1: contents do not decide; only the record decides. Do not send a claim that DNA or handedness implies relatedness (Fig 8 :51).
2. Bind the discriminator shape before the run and publish the five refuters above (Fig 5 :62-66).
3. Read every character in its own slot; never publish a single similarity scalar (Fig 1 :19, :22).
4. Enumerate the contamination habitat before interpreting any shared sequence (Fig 11 :43-48, :68-70).
5. Report nulls as `unreached at this instrument and budget`, distinguishing depth from genuine absence (Fig 6 :27, :38-43; Fig 8 :39).
6. Triangulate across low-overlap character classes ranged separately against the sample (Fig 2 :31-32, :35; Fig 8 :45).
7. Figure 4 :25: **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."** Send the generator, the discriminator shape and the refuter, not a list of suggestive similarities.

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is ancestry decided by contents?** No. Fig 8 :51. Only the record separates convergence from import.
2. **Which record?** Arbitrary, non-forced characters: codon quirks, non-functional sequence homology. Function-level identity is not record-level identity (Fig 12 :94, :117).
3. **Is chirality decisive?** No. A deterministic seeding mechanism predicts the same reading; ambiguous slot (homochirality entry 2026).
4. **Is transfer possible?** Yes, and rare but nonzero to the icy moons (Worth 2013; Frontiers 2020). Possibility is a filter, not evidence (Fig 9 :60).
5. **Can we read the record?** Only by instruments improved from ones already returning something (Fig 6 :32, :34-35). A sequencer lineage exists; an ancestry-discriminating reading is a sharpening of it.
6. **Is the null decisive?** No. Depth and genuine absence look the same from inside (Fig 6 :27). Three questions, not one (Formula step 7).
7. **What must be refused?** The held term "shared ancestry" if contradicted; the owed term is a refusal (second origin), a negation, not a bond (Operators :58; Fig 5 :53).

## 7. Updated verdict

The shape survives: **biological relatedness is a property of a lineage record, not of a molecular ingredient list; the discriminator must be an arbitrary, non-functionally-forced character read by an instrument that can already resolve something, with the two hypotheses ranged separately against the sample and the null reported as unreached rather than absent.** Everything specific that would promote a candidate character to a verdict without provenance does not survive: DNA identity, handedness, transfer possibility, and similarity scalars are all bindings or filters with zero standalone verdict weight. Strains after re-census: **one live** (the chirality slot, deterministic seeding versus descent) and **one open** (the exact negation of "shared ancestry" if partial ancestry is allowed). Both are named, not hidden. No verdict is `grounded`; the status is `candidate` throughout.
