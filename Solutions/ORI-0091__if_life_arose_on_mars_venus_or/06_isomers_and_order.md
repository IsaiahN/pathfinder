## RESIDUAL

Audit finding F1 established that shared ancestry (an adopted import in the figures' vocabulary) and independent origin (a convergent derivation) are indiscernible in the molecular contents. Two supporting questions are owed before the answer in `07` can be stated:

1. Under Fig 12's definitions, are the two biota **isomers**, that is the same atoms in a different arrangement, or members of one **functional group**, that is the same kind with different qualifiers?
2. In what **dependency order** must the readings be taken, and at which link is this chain most likely broken?

---

## DOMAIN CENSUS

- **Atoms held in the biology frame:** nucleic acid, amino acid, lipid, codon mapping, handedness, energy currency. These are already bound atoms of the frame, so naming them is a provenanced binding, not an import that adds a seventh atom.
- **Bonds held:** the seven typed relations and negation, as defined in Fig 12 and the Operators table. The relation between two biota is not a new atom; it is an arrangement of the atoms already held.
- **Symmetry:** the two hypotheses are not related by an ingredient-set permutation. They differ in the *record* of which arbitrary assignments were adopted. This is exactly the case the bond definition governs.
- **Scale:** molecular (a codon assignment), cellular (a translator plus a membrane), lineage (a rooted record), planetary (a transfer route). A verdict at one scale does not copy to another (Fig 10 :68).
- **Partition or union:** union. A shared arbitrary record and a shared function are overlapping conditions, not a list of separate questions (Fig 9 :54-58).

No claim of `grounded` status is made anywhere in this derivation.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Isomer or functional group?

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

Two readings are licensed, and they are not the same claim.

- **Strong isomer reading.** If both biota are written over the same full atom set (nucleic acid, protein, lipid, codon mapping, handedness, energy currency) and differ only in which arbitrary assignments and non-functional sequence arrangements are present, then they are Fig 12 isomers: same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in an ingredient list and decisive for ancestry. This is the figure-level restatement of the discriminator shape, and it is the claim worth testing.
- **Weak functional-group reading.** If the biota use different polymer chemistries or different energy currencies but share the substructure `heritable polymer -> regulated energy -> bounded compartment`, they are members of one functional group. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member. Function-level resemblance then carries **zero** ancestry verdict weight, because the kind is reached by convergence.

Which reading applies to an actual sample is an empirical question, not a figure question. The strong reading carries information only if the arbitrary assignments are genuinely not forced by chemistry.

### Q2 - Does a shared function tell us anything about a record?

Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**

Fig 12 :95-96: **"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."**

The convergent evolution entry (Wikipedia, 2026) supplies the world-side check: identical catalytic triads *"evolve independently more than 20 times in different enzyme superfamilies."* A shared catalytic function is therefore the flat-form failure named at Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**

This is why the audit refuses catalytic sites as discriminators and asks for arbitrary assignments and non-functionally-constrained sequence instead. Those are the bond-level information the flat ingredient list drops.

### Q3 - Is the codon table a record or a function?

Both, and the two roles must be held apart rather than collapsed.

- **As function:** a mapping from codons to amino acids is a working translator. Molecular convergence is documented, so a translator of some kind is expected under a second origin as well.
- **As record:** *which* codon maps to *which* amino acid, including variants that are not optimal, is historically contingent. The genetic code entry (Wikipedia, 2026) records the *"frozen accident" argument for the universality of the genetic code*, together with Crick's 1968 caution that universality was unproven and the later discovery of variants. A shared non-optimal quirk is the right shape of record-level evidence.

Fig 12 :99-101: **"A bond costs what it could have been, and nothing more. The price of an arrangement is the choice it records, so it is logarithmic in the number of bonds available."** A codon map that could have been otherwise records a choice; a catalytic triad forced by chemistry records almost none.

**Falsifier:** a quantified demonstration that the observed codon map is the unique physical optimum. Then the recorded choice falls to zero and the codon map loses verdict weight. Recorded before the run, not hidden.

### Q4 - Is handedness a symmetry case?

Fig 12 :92: **"Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term, so a code that charges for the sequence overcharges by log₂(k!) while the bond term undercharges."**

Handedness in a biota is a two-valued global property, not a symmetric bond in the figure's sense. Under a deterministic chiral field the sign is forced by an external influence, so two worlds match for a reason that is not ancestry. Under a chance symmetry-breaking mechanism the sign is a local accident, and a match across worlds regains meaning. The homochirality entry (Wikipedia, 2026) records both mechanism classes, notes small enantiomeric enrichments in some extraterrestrial amino acids, and notes that Bennu and Ryugu amino acids show no chiral bias.

The slot therefore stays an ambiguous binding until the mechanism is identified (audit F4).

**Falsifier:** a demonstrated universal deterministic chiral influence that fixes the sign everywhere. Then identical handedness has zero ancestry value and the slot leaves the discriminator set.

### Q5 - Dependency order and the likely break

Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**

The ordered chain for this question:

1. **Perception:** can the instrument read a polymer and its sequence at all? Fig 3 :5: **"the system can tell what is in front of it, and tell it apart from what was there before"**
2. **Vocabulary:** do we have terms for what the reading affords, for example arbitrary versus forced assignment? Fig 3 :7: **"it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked"**
3. **Objective:** can we say what would count as progress, in our own terms, namely a character on which the two hypotheses diverge? Fig 3 :10: **"it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms"**
4. **Planning:** which character is measured first under an incomplete sample?
5. **Learn and carry:** name what worked, verify it, carry the method to the next sample. Fig 3 :15.

Fig 3 :18: **"The break is drawn between three and four as an illustration. Where a given chain stops is a reading, never a property of the chain."** For this question the likely break is at links 2 to 3, not at perception: a sample can be sequenced, but the arbitrary-versus-forced distinction has not been bound as an operational mission term. Effort spent on more sequencing arrives at a step that cannot use it until the objective is stated.

Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** That is the honest reason to bind the shape before commissioning more readings. A reading taken below the break is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22-23).

### Q6 - Analogues, kept as analogues

- **Chemistry isomerism.** Fig 12 :97-98 records the precedent directly: *"silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different properties"* and the naming of isomerism by Berzelius in 1830. Fig 13 :121: **"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."** The figure itself warns that these two citations share one chemistry example, so they count as one evidence pool, not two witnesses.
- **Perceptual metamerism.** *The Hard Problem is About Source* (Isaiah N, 2026) gives the instrument that reads green and cannot comment on the source. That is the same structure as a biota's ingredient list: a lossy projection, resolved only by a source-discriminating instrument. This is an outside analogue, used for corroboration only, never to flip a figure verdict.
- **Lineage as proof.** Fig 13 :127-129: **"The array cannot be checked by inspection, so the record is what makes it verifiable."** The artefact does not say which route produced it; the lineage does. This is the figure-level backing for the whole answer.

### Q7 - Recursion after settlement

Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."**

Once a biota's status is settled at the ground, it becomes an atom for the next question: the frequency of origins, the direction of transfer, the shape of a universal biology. Until then it cannot be composed over. Composing over an unsettled term mistakes library growth for reach growth (Formula step 8). This is why the answer stops at the lineage verdict and claims no census of life's frequency.

## CONSOLIDATED FALSIFIERS

Written before the conclusion:

1. A quantified result showing the codon map is the unique physical optimum kills the arbitrary-record reading (Q3).
2. A demonstrated universal deterministic chiral field kills the handedness slot (Q4).
3. A case where two independently derived biota reliably share an arbitrary non-forced marker with high probability kills the strong isomer reading's discriminating power (Q1).
4. A measurement showing the chain's break is at perception, not vocabulary or objective, reverses the order in Q5.
5. A demonstration that function-level identity is reliably record-level identity collapses the Q2 distinction.

---

## CONCLUSION

The two biota are a **strong isomer claim** when they share the atom set and differ in arbitrary arrangements, and a **weak functional-group claim** when they share only a function-level substructure. Only the strong reading carries ancestry information. The dependency chain most likely breaks at vocabulary and objective, not perception, so the discriminator must be bound before more sequencing is commissioned. Codon and chirality readings each carry an explicit falsifier. Recursion is licensed only after a verdict is settled at the ground.
