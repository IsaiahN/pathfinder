**Subject:** Check one output on inclusive institutions before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working with a figure-grounded framework and have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the framework treats a society's institutional library as a frame that predicts how a political contest will go. The ground is the event record: was the powerful actor actually constrained, was the violation actually punished, did power actually turn over. The gap is measured per institutional slot, never averaged into a country score. From that setup it follows that an inclusive institution is not a stock a polity owns. It is a maintained relation with two conditions that must both hold:
>
> 1. the channel to the constraint record is kept reachable, so the rule still binds the people who wrote it;
> 2. reach is renewed by contact with a closure that differs, here the economic and social arrangements the question asks about: property, inequality, civil society, informal norms.
>
> If either condition fails, the result is categorically different, not just a smaller degree of inclusion. A polity failing the first needs a different instrument from a polity failing the second. The framework also says the transition has a threshold shape rather than accumulating smoothly, and that a coalition or court that once constrained can change sign when the underlying problem changes. It does not say which particular economic or social arrangement is required in a given country. It returns a shape, not a name.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that this settles whether inclusive institutions cause prosperity, that any named arrangement is the answer, that the framework has measured a country, or that it is the only account of institutional emergence. Those names are bindings from other frames, and until their provenance and instruments are shown they carry no evidentiary weight here.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a polity whose inclusive constraint slots survive a habitat substitution that removes the material and social base that sustained them;
> 2. a single intervention that restores both conditions in both failure types and leaves no categorically different residue;
> 3. a polity that loses effective constraint while independent measures of monitoring fidelity and ground pressure both stay high and non-declining;
> 4. a polity that renews its reach entirely inside its own political closure, with no atom from a differing economic or social closure, and still passes the ground test across a change of habitat.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Local verbatim figure text (Fig N :line citations): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in the Project Pathfinder workspace
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Public figures folder: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on an institutional problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it - the framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question - and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
