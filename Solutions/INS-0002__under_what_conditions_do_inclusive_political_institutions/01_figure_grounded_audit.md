## 0. The residual in effect terms

The loop defines R = |Γ(b,a) − o′| per slot s (Formula step 1; Symbols table, `R`). Here:

- **b** is the society's belief about who may rule and who may be constrained, formed from its own history and inherited priors (Symbols table, `b`, `w`).
- **a** is what actors do: form a coalition, protest, repress, draft a constitution, enforce a rule, comply or defect.
- **Γ(b,a)** is what the society's institutional library predicts will happen: whether the powerful will actually be constrained, whether rights will be respected, whether the rule will bind its authors.
- **o′** is what actually happens: the event record of constraint, punishment, compliance and turnover.
- **s** is the institutional slot. R is indexed by slot and by time, never aggregated into a country score.

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** (Fig 1 :19)
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** (Fig 1 :22)

The phenomenon to explain is not whether a country carries the label "inclusive" but **the duration for which a constraint slot keeps R small**. Call it `D_s`. Observed: some constraint arrangements bind powerful actors for decades; others close quickly after a short opening. A frame that predicts all constitutions are equally durable, or that a declared right simply persists, has a residual here.

State the residual as: *why does the gap between what a polity's institutions predict about constraining power and what its event record actually returns stay small for decades in some slots and grow quickly in others, and which atoms from economic and social arrangements must cross into the political frame for that to happen?* This is an effect description. It names no theory, no "state capacity", no "pluralism", no "civil society". Those are candidate names and are withheld until the census.

**Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term** (Formula, "Direction"), before the run: the answer would be (a) a conjunction of conditions whose conjunction keeps `D_s` large, (b) a statement of which imports from a differing closure are structurally required, and (c) an instrument that discriminates a failure of one condition from a failure of the other. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no loop check can see it. It is now bound, so the refuter in section 4 can fire on it.

**Anchor test.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* The same line names candidates directly: *"A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* For this question the viable anchor is the **event record of constraint**: did the powerful actor actually get sanctioned, did the court ruling actually bind the executive, did power actually turn over. Institutional self-description, expert ratings and constitutional text are instruments at best; Fig 2 :30: *"you push on it, it is not an anchor."* Fig 2 :37-38 adds the instrument caveat: judgment is the right anchor only when a question is constitutively about people, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure.

**Important separation (strain S2, resolved in section 6).** Economic and social arrangements are **habitat**, not ground. Fig 11 :73: *"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."* The question asks how much inclusive institutions depend on prior economic or social arrangements. The answer is not that those arrangements settle inclusion. They are the habitat that supplies actors and imports; the ground is the constraint event record. Keeping these apart is what prevents the question from collapsing into economic determinism.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### 1.1 Arity, symmetry, scale of R

- **Arity.** R is a multi-slot relation: at minimum an elite-constraint slot and a popular-participation slot, plus a state-capacity slot where the question's "political choices" half requires enforcement. It is a sequence rather than a set (constitution then enforcement then transmission), so it carries an **ordered** bond (`→`, sequence: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*, Fig 12 :72) and a **comparison** bond for "inclusive" (`⋛`, *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*, Operators table :48). It is not symmetric: elites and non-elites are not interchangeable.
- **Symmetry.** "Inclusive" is a threshold relative to a distribution of power, not an absolute quantity (Fig 12 :85-89). Persistence is asymmetric in time: a closed institution is not reopened by reversing the same action.
- **Scale.** Four scales are in view: the individual decision, the coalition, the institution, and the society across generations. The membrane between scales is *"Not a wall: a filter with a direction"* (Fig 4 :21), and *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* (Fig 4 :22). The transform is lossy, so the round-trip gap R_T is owed (Fig 4 :30).

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

A domain is admitted only if an anchor can be named. Where none can be named, it is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor, nameable? | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 The political contest at a moment** | elites, non-elites, state officials | power exercised, residual reduction | yes: realized constraint events (who was actually bound) | admitted |
| **D2 The institutional library** | rules, franchises, courts, norms as atoms and bonds | reach (what can be composed and enforced), cost \|φ\| | yes: settlement, does the rule bind the powerful against the ground | admitted |
| **D3 The economic and social habitat** | property regimes, markets, inequality, class, ethnicity, civil society, informal norms | material and organizational power, survival | yes: material outcomes, who controls resources and who can organize | admitted |
| **D4 The temporal channel** | enforcement instruments, records, legitimacy, memory | contact with the ground | yes: the ground, *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"* (Fig 10 :24) | admitted |
| **X1 The great founder or legislator as free-floating cause** | the leader | talent | **no** anchor independent of the habitat's verdict (the same founder yields different institutions in different habitats) | not a domain yet |
| **X2 Geography, disease or resource endowment as sole direct cause** | terrain, climate, pathogens, resources | material conditions | conditions are habitat elements, already inside D3: *"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"* (Fig 11 :46) | absorbed into D3, not separate |

X1 fails the anchor test and is excluded before any figure line is applied to it. This is a structural exclusion, not a claim that leaders never matter. X2 is not discarded; it is read as habitat, which is exactly where the question's "prior economic or social arrangements" live.

### 1.3 Partition or union

D1 and D2 overlap: the institutional library is inside the political contest. D2 and D3 overlap: institutions are composed inside a habitat that supplies their atoms. D4 overlaps all three as the channel. The whole question is a **union**, not a partition.

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** (Fig 9 :54)
> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** (Fig 9 :55)
> **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."** (Fig 9 :57)

The unbundled conditions are: (C1) constraint of power is actually maintained, the contact and channel condition; (C2) reach is renewed by contact with a closure that differs, the import condition, which is where economic and social arrangements enter. A polity failing only C1 and a polity failing only C2 are not different degrees of one failure; they are different compounds and need different instruments. The essay *The Library of Babel* states the same stop condition: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."*

### 1.4 Dependency order (Fig 3)

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."** (Fig 3 :20)
> **"Why the middle link is where chains usually break."** (Fig 3 :24)
> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** (Fig 3 :27)

Order for this question: 1 perception (can actors read the distribution of power and the violation record?) → 2 vocabulary (are there terms for what a political situation affords, who counts, what can be constrained?) → 3 objective (can the polity say what progress would count as, namely constraint on the powerful?) → 4 planning (coalition and reform sequence) → 5 learn and carry (constitutional settlement and transmission). The middle link, having terms for what a situation affords and saying what would count as progress, is the one with no instrument. Fig 3 :29: *"Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."* Most reform effort lands above the break because that is where the measurements already exist (Fig 3 :30).

## 3. Findings

Mode tag on each: `general` if structural, `specified` if a falsifiable prediction about institutions, `grounded` only if settled by a measurement. None is `grounded`.

### F1: Inclusive institutions are a property of polity-and-habitat, not of the political frame alone

**Mode: `specified`. Domains D1, D2, D3. Status: triangulated (Fig 11 and Fig 4, low topical overlap: habitat vs membrane).**

> **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."** (Fig 11 :39)
> **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."** (Fig 11 :40)
> **"it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."** (Fig 4 :20)

The two structural facts: capability is located in the relation, not the polity considered alone (Fig 11), and the kernel is constant across sizes while actors, currency and anchor change (Fig 4). Therefore an answer to "under what conditions" cannot be a list of political attributes considered in isolation. The habit of answering with a constitution, a court or an election alone is an agent-only improvement that may not change contact. Fig 11 :72 gives the standing refuter in the figure's own words: an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim.

**Refuter:** a polity whose inclusive constraint slots persist unchanged across a habitat substitution that demonstrably removes or reverses the material and social base that sustained them. If it survives, F1 is false.

**World bearing, secondary only.** Acemoglu and Robinson, *Paths to Inclusive Political Institutions* (2016, MIT working paper): *"inclusive political institutions emerge from a balanced increase in state capacity and the distribution of power"* and *"we emphasize the crucial nature of informal institutions and social norms which put Athens and England onto this path."* That corroborates the two-condition and habitat reading. It is the institutional-economics pool, so it is one witness, not three (Fig 2 :31).

### F2: The question is a union of two conditions, not one lever

**Mode: `general` with a `specified` consequence for reform. Domains all. Status: triangulated (Fig 9 and the Library of Babel essay; the essay is framework-internal so the external witness count is one).**

Fig 9 :54, :55 and :57 are quoted in section 1.3. The two conditions C1 (constraint maintained) and C2 (reach renewed through a differing closure) have different instruments:

- A C1 failure shows up as saturated metrics, pooled readings, a bracket that no longer matches the violation record (Fig 10 :26, Fig 7 :38).
- A C2 failure shows up as a closure handing itself the same room, no import, and a reach that stops moving while the library keeps growing (Fig 6 :21, Fig 7 :12).

A single lever, for example "write a better constitution", clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing, because the other condition still binds (Fig 9 :55). This is the reason the verdicts in `09_watson_answer.md` are kept separate per domain and never averaged. It also answers a naive reading of the question: "how much dependence on prior arrangements" is not a single coefficient, it is a claim about which imports are owed in which compound.

**Refuter:** a single intervention that restores both conditions in both failure types with no categorically different residue left over. That would collapse the union into a partition.

### F3: Quick closure is a failure of the channel, not a decay of the ground

**Mode: `specified`. Domain D4. Status: triangulated (Fig 10 and Fig 7, low overlap: ground maintainer vs room chain).**

> **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"** (Fig 10 :24)
> **"saturated metrics, pooled readings"** (Fig 10 :26)
> **"you have lost touch"** (Fig 10 :28)
> **"→ keep the anchor reachable"** (Fig 10 :29)

An institution loses inclusiveness quickly when its contact with what pays, namely the event record of constraint, degrades while the ground keeps paying for constraint elsewhere. The named decay modes are stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics and pooled readings. "Pooled readings" is the institutional version of aggregating across slots (Fig 1 :22): the signal that says where the constraint is failing disappears into an average.

Fig 7 adds the two-factor test:

> **"Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:"** (Fig 7 :37)
> **"the bracket's fidelity across the handoff"** (Fig 7 :38)
> **"the ground's pressure on the room"** (Fig 7 :39)

A decade of binding constraint is a chain of handoffs. If either the bracket's fidelity or the ground's pressure goes to zero, the polity runs in place. The world corroborates at a different stratum. Ostrom's Nobel popular information page: *"active participation of users in creating and enforcing rules appears to be essential. Rules that are imposed from the outside or unilaterally dictated by powerful insiders have less legitimacy and are more likely to be violated."* The same page: *"Likewise, monitoring and enforcement work better when conducted by insiders than by outsiders."* That is Fig 10 :24 and Fig 7 :38 in a different field, with no shared evidence pool with the institutional-economics papers.

**Refuter:** a documented case in which a polity loses effective constraint while bracket fidelity and ground pressure are independently measured as high and are not declining. If both stay high, F3's mechanism is not the cause.

### F4: Renewal of reach requires contact with a differing closure (the dependence answer)

**Mode: `general` for idempotence, `specified` for the institutional application. Domains D2, D3. Status: triangulated (Fig 6, Fig 8 and Fig 13, three figures with distinct roles).**

> **"the only operator that moves the wall"** (Fig 6 :14)
> **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."** (Fig 6 :21)
> **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."** (Fig 6 :23)
> **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."** (Fig 8 :32)
> **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."** (Fig 13 :115)

A political frame can keep optimizing its own rules forever and never add an atom. To keep a constraint slot live across a change of habitat, its reach must be expanded by contact with frames whose closures differ. Here those frames are the economic and social arrangements the question names: property regimes, the structure of the economy, inequality, civil society, informal norms. This is the figure-grounded content of the second half of the question. Dependence is **structural** (a differing closure must be in contact) but it is **not monocausal** (the figures return a condition of import, not a coefficient and not a name of which arrangement).

The world supplies the names from its own frame. The Cambridge description of Acemoglu and Robinson's *Economic Origins of Dictatorship and Democracy* lists six dependencies: *"the strength of civil society, the structure of political institutions, the nature of political and economic crises, the level of economic inequality, the structure of the economy, and the form and extent of globalization."* Ostrom supplies a different-frame witness for user participation in rule making. Both are bindings from their frames onto the shape; by Fig 8 :51, *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them,"* so their names carry no figure-authority.

**Refuter:** a polity that renews its inclusive reach solely by composing inside its own political closure, with no new atom entering from a differing economic or social closure, and that still passes the ground test across a change of habitat. That breaks idempotence and F4 with it.

### F5: Inclusive emergence is a threshold relation, not a stock

**Mode: `specified`. Domains D1, D4. Status: triangulated (Operators table and a low-overlap computational social science model, with the figure as primary and the model as secondary).**

The Operators table defines `⋛` as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*. Applied to this question, "inclusive" reads as a threshold between the power that can be exercised by a few and the power that can constrain them, not as an amount of constitutional text. This is a **binding** of the comparison bond onto a political quantity, not a derivation; the operand type is an open strain (S3).

A low-overlap witness makes the threshold shape concrete. Calmettes and Weiss, *The emergence of egalitarianism in a model of early human societies* (Heliyon 3(11), 2017): *"egalitarianism emerges suddenly as the optimal power-sharing arrangement in a population of selfish individuals without any inherently altruistic qualities."* Their model has a critical gossip parameter at which anti-dominance coalitions destabilize the alpha position: *"At this threshold, any individual alpha can be toppled by an anti-dominance coalition of resentful lower-ranking individuals."* That is a phase transition in the distribution of constraint, reached at a critical value rather than accumulated smoothly. It corroborates the shape from a physics-inspired model with no shared evidence pool with institutional economics.

**Refuter:** a documented emergence of inclusive constraint that is smooth and monotone in the same measured quantity with no threshold, in a system where the threshold prediction was pre-registered. That would weaken the ⋛ binding to a metaphor.

### F6: The valence of a mobilized group is relative to the residual

**Mode: `specified`. Domains D2, D3. Status: triangulated (Fig 11 and Fig 12, low overlap: habitat relations vs composition structures).**

> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** (Fig 11 :49)
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."** (Fig 11 :50)
> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."** (Fig 11 :51)
> **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."** (Fig 12 :119)

This is the mechanism behind "civil society helps democracy" stated in effect terms. A coalition, a court or an informal norm is a catalyst if it regenerates across uses and a spent part if it does not, and nothing in its description records which. When the residual changes, a former benefactor becomes a malfactor without anything about the actor changing. That is why a standing list of "good" institutional ingredients cannot be the answer.

Two independent low-overlap witnesses. Redhead and von Rueden, *Coalitions and conflict: A longitudinal analysis of men's politics* (Evolution and Human Sciences 3, 2021), report that across many primate species *"subordinates also form 'levelling' coalitions to weaken higher ranking individuals' privileged access to resources or mates"* and that the egalitarianism of small-scale human societies *"may owe in large part to levelling coalitions that are larger, more effective, and longer-lasting"*. Calmettes and Weiss put the same relation in their model's terms: *"In effect, a coalition of the weak enforces a moral code that keeps aggressive upstarts from selfishly dominating the group to monopolize reproductive and other resources."* Neither shares an evidence pool with institutional economics.

**Refuter:** an institution whose measured valence is invariant across a documented change of the polity's residual. If no such change of valence can be found anywhere, F6 weakens to `general`.

### F7: Never answer by aggregating across polities or averaging slots

**Mode: `general` with a `specified` methodological consequence. Domains all. Status: triangulated (Fig 1 and Fig 9, low overlap).**

> **"The ground enters as the only metric."** (Fig 1 :23)
> **"predicates minted, and credibility accrued"** (Fig 1 :26)
> **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."** (Fig 1 :27)

A cross-country index that reports "inclusive institutions explain X percent of growth" scores the frame with a quantity the frame produces. It is a filter for where to look and a budget instrument, not a witness. Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* The specified consequence: any claimed answer to INS-0002 that is an average across countries has already thrown away the only signal that says which constraint slot is failing and where.

**Refuter:** an averaged cross-country statistic that predicts, prospectively and per slot, which named institution will close next. If one exists, F7's methodological consequence is false.

### F8: Conflict between the modernization frame and the institutional frame, and how it was resolved

**Mode: `general`. Status: figure-governed; conflict returned to the census.**

Papaioannou, *Institutions, history, antagonisms, and development* (Scandinavian Journal of Economics, 2025), reports: *"Acemoglu et al. (2008, 2009) re-examine the role of income (and education) on democracy, the cornerstone of the modernization hypothesis. They first show that the statistically significant – but implying modest effect – cross-country correlation weakens considerably once geography and culture are accounted for"*. That reads as a conflict with a simple "prior economic arrangements determine inclusion" story, which is one plausible answer to the question as posed.

A conflict between world and figures sends the derivation back to the census; it never flips the verdict. Re-census R2 (section 6) resolves it: economic arrangements are habitat (D3) and can be import sources (F4), not a single dominant cause. Fig 11 :73: the habitat updates, the ground is what settles and does not. Under that mapping the modernization result is a filter on which material conditions to examine, not a verdict on emergence. The binding is recorded with no source gate for the institutional claim, so it carries zero evidentiary weight for the verdict.

### F9: Which particular prior arrangement is required is a `gap`

**Mode: `general`. Status: figure-governed, triangulated for the limit.**

> **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"** (Fig 9 :32)
> **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"** (Fig 13 :134)
> **"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."** (Fig 13 :135)
> **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** (Fig 8 :39)

The framework can say that a differing closure must be in contact and that the constraint channel must be kept reachable. It cannot say which specific economic or social arrangement will do the work in a given society, because that is a source-discriminating question and the instrument is not held here. This is a `gap`, not a silence and not a refusal to answer. The world's named candidates (informal norms, civil society, inequality, economic structure) are bindings from the institutional-economics frame, provenance owed and ungrounded here (Fig 8 :51).

**Refuter:** a source-discriminating instrument that, prospectively and per slot, identifies which prior arrangement supplied the atom that reopened a constraint channel, and that survives independent replication. That would convert part of this gap into a grounded name.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the design-instrument rule: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* The shape claim is refuted if any of the following is observed:

1. A polity's inclusive constraint slots are unchanged across a habitat substitution that removes the material and social base that sustained them (breaks F1, Fig 11 :39-40).
2. A single intervention restores both C1 and C2 in both failure types and leaves no categorically different residue (breaks F2, Fig 9 :57).
3. A polity loses effective constraint while bracket fidelity and ground pressure both measure high and non-declining (breaks F3, Fig 7 :37-39).
4. A polity renews its inclusive reach purely by internal composition with no new atom from a differing economic or social closure and still passes the ground test across a change of habitat (breaks F4, Fig 8 :32).
5. Inclusive emergence is smooth and monotone in the same measured quantity where a threshold was pre-registered (breaks F5's ⋛ binding).
6. A mobilized group's measured valence is invariant across a documented change of the residual (breaks F6, Fig 11 :49-51).
7. Fig 13's six terms are derived from five of them, or Fig 6's idempotence fails inside a closed set (breaks the figures themselves, not just this reading).

No part of this solution may be upgraded to `grounded` until at least one of these has been run and has failed to fire, and until a constraint-event ground measurement has paid for the shape.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the residual and the refuter, not with a theory of institutions (Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"*).
2. Keep C1 and C2 separate in every report. A blended "inclusiveness score" is a pooled reading (Fig 10 :26) and destroys the diagnostic.
3. Ask, at each handoff, which link the chain stops at and whether that was measured (Fig 3 :29). Do not fund above the break.
4. Treat any list of institutional attributes as Γ, that is, as a candidate library whose reach must be tested against the ground, never as the answer (Fig 1 :27).
5. Use the institutional-economics literature as one corroborating witness and as a budget filter, not as the verdict. Acemoglu and Robinson, Ostrom, and the primatology sources draw on different pools, so they are not automatically one pool; the two institutional-economics items are one witness (Fig 2 :31, :40).
6. Offer the formula as a method after a verdict is recorded (Fig 4 :22). Each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.
7. Stop at the shape. Do not name which specific prior arrangement a society must have; the framework returns *"a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (Fig 9 :32).
