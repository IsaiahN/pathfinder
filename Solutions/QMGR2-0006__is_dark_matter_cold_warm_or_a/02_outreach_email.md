# Outreach draft: check one shape before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the findings in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are repaired and the refuter in section 3 of that file is still standing. The stage-1 output must survive, or the two-stage offer collapses on contact (Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked").

**Subject:** One checkable claim about the cold/warm dark-matter question, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific shape claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the shape does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **Stage 1, the claim (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The observational residuals in this question are indexed by probe, not averaged. Large-scale structure is already fit by cold collisionless dark matter, while dwarf satellites, inner halo profiles and the Lyman-alpha cutoff leave live, scale-dependent residuals. The figure-governed output is therefore not a label but a shape: a suppression in the matter power spectrum above some cutoff, with a redshift evolution that could discriminate a thermal intergalactic-medium source from free-streaming dark matter. "Cold," "warm" and "mixed" are regions and combinations of that ordering, not three answers to be voted on. This is a prediction of a shape, not a measurement.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming that dark matter is a 7 keV sterile neutrino, that any particular lower bound is a detection, or that the small-scale problems are solved. The Lyman-alpha cutoff can be fit by cold dark matter with a thermal history and by warm dark matter with free streaming. Naming the particle from that projection would be reading a source off a lossy instrument.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before this message:**
> 1. Two probes with genuinely independent systematics return suppression scales whose discrepancy does not shrink with more data.
> 2. A source-discriminating measurement (the cutoff's redshift evolution, or 21 cm at z greater than 15) shows no cutoff where the shape predicts one, or shows a thermal scaling with no warm component.
> 3. Every independent slot resolves to baryonic feedback with no dark suppression at all.
> 4. Any of the three options requires a seventh primitive beyond the six-term set.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract or narrow the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If the shape survives and you are curious, I will send the sources and the derivation. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them. Your verdict on stage 1 is recorded before any of that is shown, and your later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
>
> **Stage 3, if you want to use the machinery.** The formula is a method, not an answer. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get: `general`, `specified` or `grounded`.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Contact discipline

- Public, opt-in, institutional venues only, from `03_public_contact_points.csv`.
- No personal emails, no purchased lists, no automation, no misleading subject line, and an opt-out line in every message.
- Track A only here: these are people who can refute the shape, not people selected because they are likely to agree. Evidence and contact costs stay in separate columns.
- A reply that refutes stage 1 is copied into `11_audit.md` with provenance and re-evaluated by the auditor seat. It is never quietly dropped.
