# The Tether's answer: can network structure predict conservation priority?

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claim, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

We can rank species and interactions by their position in an ecological network, but the ranking does not tell us what to protect until we say what we are trying to preserve. The residual is per object slot (Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated"), it is objective-relative, and it has depth: direct partners, neighbours of neighbours, and effects three steps out. It is a **union compound** (Fig 9 :54-56), not one hard question, so it must be unbundled before it is searched.

## 2. The shape

1. **A network is a structure, not a state.** Fig 13 :29-30: "A structure with no state is empty: an ordering over nothing, a network with nothing at the nodes." So a centrality score is a description of an arrangement. It does not carry a priority magnitude by itself.
2. **Priority is relative to a declared objective.** Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." Fig 11 :50: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." Change the goal and the ranking can reverse. This is the strongest figure-grounded point and it leads the answer.
3. **The objective is a missing dependency link.** Fig 3 :25-27: "The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments." Network metrics supply terms for what nodes afford; they do not say what counts as progress. Operators table :51: "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one." Cagua et al. 2019 independently name the gap: centrality "says little about how these species could be used to attain or preserve a particular community state."
4. **Cascade depth is part of the claim.** Fig 11 :52-53: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." A first-order degree ranking and a third-order cascade ranking are different terms over the same nodes.
5. **The habitat is not the ground.** Fig 11 :73: "The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not." Centrality ranks are filters for the budget, not witnesses for the verdict (Fig 9 :60). A removal experiment, a monitored persistence outcome, or a forecast scored against withheld data settles the candidate.
6. **The answer is a shape, not a species list.** The framework returns "a shape, not a name." What it licenses is the constraint: per slot, objective-relative, multi-order, ground-settled. The species name is earned only by D2 and recorded as a binding.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that network structure is useless. It is the habitat census and the budget filter, without which no candidate list exists.
- Not that any particular species is a priority. No ground reading was run here.
- Not that centrality, modularity, nestedness, or any other index is sufficient. Those are filters (F5 in `01`).
- Not that a structural ranking is `grounded`. Its mode is `specified` until a ground pays.
- Not that the question is one question. It is a union and must be unbundled.
- Not that a list of central species survives a change of objective. Fig 11 :51 forbids that reading.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating reading, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (mapped in `05` §4): an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; it was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved it. Here the corresponding tests are the five pre-registered refuters in `01` §5. Restated so they can fire:

- Declare two different objectives and ask whether one structural ranking is settled as correct for both. If yes, Fig 11 :51 breaks.
- Run a removal or manipulation experiment. Does the top centrality node fail to affect the target while a low-degree node does? If yes, the filter-to-verdict step breaks.
- Enumerate to second and third order. Does the ranking change? If never, the cascade claim narrows.
- Use the public forecasting challenges: submit a structural-priority forecast before the target data are released and let the withheld data score it. The NEON challenge already makes "All forecasts and scores... publicly available", so the anchor does not move when the forecaster argues.
- **Standing falsifiers from `01`:** derive a structure with no states that carries magnitude; show the conditions are a partition; show all effects are first order.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The claim stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. The six-term set is a visible guess, not nature's own list (*They're Scanning Books*, mapped in `05` §3: "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."). Another frame may hold the atom this one lacks. Indiscernibility is not identity: a structural ranking and a persistence verdict that look the same in a report may be different things. No part of this is settled.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> Network structure can predict the *shape* of a conservation priority, not the priority itself. In the figures an ecological network is a structure, and "a structure with no state is empty: an ordering over nothing, a network with nothing at the nodes." So a centrality ranking is a description of an arrangement, not a verdict about which species to save. "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual": the same node is a benefactor for one objective and a hazard for another. What structure gives you is the habitat of contact, a per-species candidate list, and a first-order map, with effects that can reach three steps out, as in the DDT case. What it cannot give you is the objective or the settlement. The objective is a separate dependency link that usually has no instrument; the ground, an experiment, a monitoring series, or a forecast scored against withheld data, decides which candidate survives. Centrality is a filter for the budget, not a witness for the verdict. This is a `specified` shape with five refuters written in advance, not a list of species and not a result.
