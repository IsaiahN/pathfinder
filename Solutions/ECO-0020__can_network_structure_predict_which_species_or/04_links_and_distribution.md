## Four things in the wider literature that bear on the question

1. **Structure alone is not dynamics.** Jordán 2009 says "Understanding the linkage between structure and dynamics is a condition to test the results of topological studies." This is the ecological version of Fig 13 :29-30: a structure with no states at the nodes is empty. Present it as corroboration from a different discipline, not as a figure line.
2. **The objective changes the answer.** Cagua et al. 2019 distinguish predicting which extinctions cause the largest change from "how these species could be used to attain or preserve a particular community state." That is Fig 3's missing objective link, independently named in the ecological literature.
3. **Layers and cascade depth change the ranking.** Domínguez-García & Kéfi 2024 report that whole-community importance is sometimes "emergent", resembling neither single-layer ranking. That is Fig 11 :52-53's cascade point inside the structural frame.
4. **The field already has a ground arrangement.** The NEON challenge scores forecasts against data withheld at submission time and makes "All forecasts and scores... publicly available." That is an anchor that does not move when a forecaster argues (Fig 2 :29). It is the natural venue for testing whether a structural priority ranking predicts persistence.

**Overlap warning.** Sources 1 to 3 all sit in the ecological-network citation pool. They are one witness on the "structure is not sufficient" claim, not three. Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Their value is that they show the figure-derived shape is seeable from inside ecology; they do not confirm it from outside.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22** - "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Sending the full framework before someone has described their objective and their ground sends a recording, not a generator.
- **Fig 10 :42** - "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A reader who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** - "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what they would count as a failure before giving them the links.
- **Fig 2 :42** - "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." A conservation decision can cost species and habitat, so independence is worth buying here.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its five refuters; (2) ask what objective they would declare and what they would count as a failure; (3) record the verdict; (4) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (5) only then the formula as a reusable method with a ground and a refuter per problem.

## The two-stage offer

- **Stage 1.** The recipient checks the shape claim without holding the framework's interior. Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source."
- **Stage 2, opt-in.** After the stage-1 verdict is recorded, offer the three source links. Their later endorsement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
- **Stage 3, opt-in.** Offer the formula. It is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer. Each user writes their own ground and refuter first.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. If a recipient names an objective that reverses the ranking, that is a successful check, not a failed one. Record it and narrow the claim.
