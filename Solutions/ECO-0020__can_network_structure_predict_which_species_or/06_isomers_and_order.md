## DERIVATION

### Q1 - What kind of thing is a network?

- Fig 13 :24 names it: "that entities relate, and influence travels." Fig 13 :18-19 defines structures as "how those quantities are arranged."
- Fig 13 :29: "A state with no structure has no magnitude: three of what, ordered how, varying across what?" and "A structure with no state is empty: an ordering over nothing, a network with nothing at the nodes."
- So a network is a **structure**, in lockstep with states. A network with no species at the nodes is empty; a list of species with no relations has no magnitude as a network. The species are the states; the edges are the arrangement.
- **Conclusion:** "network structure" is not a state and cannot by itself carry a priority magnitude. It becomes a usable term only when bonded to states (which species) and to a comparison (which is more important for what).
- **Falsifier:** show a network, with no named states at its nodes, that carries a priority magnitude under the six-term set. This is refuter 3 in `01` §5.

### Q2 - Union or partition?

- Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- Fig 9 :56: "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."
- Priority = (large effect on the target) union (the target is declared). A centrality lever clears the first condition alone and leaves the second binding. So the two must be searched separately and recombined.
- **Falsifier:** show the conditions are a partition, so one lever clears both. Refuter 4 in `01` §5.

### Q3 - What order does the cascade have?

- Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :44: "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."
- Fig 11 :52-53: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."
- Degree is first order. Betweenness over shortest paths is second order at most. Effects that matter can be third order. So a ranking's **order** is part of the claim, and a first-order ranking is not the same term as a third-order one even if they use the same atoms.
- Jordán 2009 (opened 2026-11-23) supports the mesoscale step: "key species may be important interactors even if they only have a small number of direct partners in the web" (*Pisaster*), and the paper builds an index of "indirect effects... up to a three-step length."
- Dominguez-Garcia & Kefi 2024 (opened 2026-11-23) show that at whole-community level the importance ranking can be "emergent", resembling neither layer alone. Order and layer are both part of the answer.
- **Falsifier:** show that all priority-bearing effects for a declared objective are first order, so increasing the enumeration depth never changes the ranking. Refuter 5 in `01` §5.

### Q4 - Are two networks isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Two ecological networks over the **same species set**, differing only in which interactions connect them, satisfy the figure's definition: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance (different dynamics). Two networks that share only a few species and a motif type are not isomers; they are members of a **functional group** ("state - structure - state", or "hub-and-module") with different occupants.
- Jordán 2009 gives the ecological precedent without using the word: alternative connection patterns change which node is central, and "the importance rank of species depends on n (the maximum length of indirect effects)." Change the arrangement or the depth and you have a different substance.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two networks over the same species set, with different interaction wiring, show identical persistence outcomes for the declared objective under every removal sequence tested, the isomer claim fails for that objective and the functional-group reading stands instead.

### Q5 - What carries across a level, and what does not?

- Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."
- Fig 12 :109-110: "Where it is not [recorded], the result carries forward and the deriving does not, so every lineage pays again. Which boundary matters is the one the record does not cross."
- A structural ranking that has been settled for one objective becomes an atom for the next objective only if its lineage is recorded: which species set, which layers, which cascade depth, which ground. Without the record, the next team re-derives it against a different goal and calls the disagreement a contradiction.
- Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." The round trip gap R_T for this condensation is enumerated in `11_audit.md`.
- **Falsifier:** show that a structural ranking transfers to a new objective with no loss and no record of its original layers, depth, or ground. That would set R_T to zero and break Fig 4's rule.

## CONCLUSION

A network is a **structure**, not a state, so it cannot carry a priority magnitude alone (Q1). The question is a **union**, so the objective must be unbundled before a ranking is searched (Q2). The ranking has an **order**, and first-order centrality is not interchangeable with third-order cascade enumeration (Q3). Two networks over the same species set with different wiring are **isomers** under Fig 12's definition; networks that share only a motif are members of a **functional group** (Q4). Settling makes a ranking reusable only with its lineage attached (Q5).

Nothing in this derivation names a species. That is the point: the shape constrains any future answer, and the ground decides which name it earns.
