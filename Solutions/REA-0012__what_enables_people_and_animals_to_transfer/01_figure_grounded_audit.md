## 0. The claim under audit, stated as an effect

The question asks what enables a person or animal to carry learning into a situation that differs substantially from the one in which the learning occurred. Stated in effect terms, the learner's library Γ predicts well in the source situation and fails in the target situation. The residual is the gap between what Γ predicts and what happens there.

Three candidate answers are audited, none assumed:

- **A, stored rule.** Transfer happens because a general abstract rule was stored and then applied. Effect: the learner holds a term that predicts across both situations, and no new term is owed on arrival.
- **B, similarity.** Transfer happens because the situations share identical elements. Effect: the residual after arrival is a function of overlap between source and target.
- **C, single enabler.** One variable, internal to the learner, accounts for far transfer. Effect: a single effect size or score selects success across all target situations.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run (formula step "Direction"): a **carryable generator**, that is, a description of something held by the learner that (i) predicts in the source situation, (ii) survives the crossing into a substantially different situation, and (iii) is settled again by what happens there, not by the learner's agreement with itself. Committing this shape before the run makes it refusable by the same test as every other term: *"Committing a prediction before a run is this, already working."* (Fig 5 :66).

The candidate answers are sorted, not averaged. Fig 9 :4-5 says to split a disagreement that does not shrink with effort: *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*

## 1b. Domain census, written before choosing any figure line

**R by arity, symmetry and scale.** Arity: at least three conditions in a union, the learner's held library, the structure of what is carried, and the habitat entered. Symmetry: the enablement claim is ordered, source to target, so a symmetric correlation between source success and target success cannot choose the direction. Scale: one crossing within a lifetime or training run (the membrane between situations), and one crossing across generations or populations (the membrane between agents).

**Per-domain lines. A domain needs actors, a currency and a nameable anchor.**

- **D1, the individual learner's closure.** Actors: a person or animal with library Γ, belief b, weighting w, object slots s. Currency: per-slot residual R and description length. Anchor: what actually happens in the target situation, which does not change because the learner predicts otherwise. Nameable, so this is a domain.
- **D2, the structure of the carried item.** Actors: atoms and the bonds between them. Currency: two incommensurable prices, bits to describe and actions to test. Anchor: whether the ground settles the arrangement. Nameable, so this is a domain.
- **D3, the habitat entered.** Actors: conditions, relations, other actors, including ones the learner did not choose. Currency: contact. Anchor: the ground of the new situation, which stays put while the habitat updates. Nameable, so this is a domain.
- **D4, the social or population frame, present for people and patchy for animals.** Actors: peers, a shared library, something that ranks what members produce. Currency: methods and head starts crossing a membrane. Anchor: the same non-updating ground. Nameable, so this is a domain. Where a species has no such frame, the domain is absent and the verdict for it is not imported from the human case.

**Partition or union.** A union of overlapping conditions, not a partition. A learner can hold the right method and still fail because the habitat was substituted; can enter a reproduced habitat and still fail because only a recording crossed; can carry a bond and still fail because the ground it ranges against moved. One lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55-57). So verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged into a single transfer quotient.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3) :** perception of the new situation, then terms for what it affords, then a statement of what progress there would be, then a route, then learning and carrying. Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 8 :53), refuse before mint (formula step 2), settle before composing over a term (Fig 12 :104).

**Strain test.** Candidate A read as "the abstract rule is the enablement" strains against Fig 4 :23 and Fig 12 :47. That strain governs a different quantity, namely what kind of item crosses a boundary, so the census sends us back to D2 rather than softening Fig 4. Candidate B read as "similarity causes transfer" strains against Fig 11 :49, where valence is relative to the residual. That strain governs D3, not D1. Both strains are productive and are recorded in section 9.

## 2. Findings

### F1: What crosses is a method or a head start, not a recording (decisive for the shape)

Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**
Fig 4 :23: **"upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."**
Fig 4 :24: **"downward: something that makes the next search cheaper. Never a replay of what worked once."**
Fig 4 :25: **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."**

A method is reapplicable in a new situation; a recording is a description of one occasion. Transfer to a substantially different situation is precisely the case where replay of one occasion cannot pay. This is the strongest figure-grounded result and it decides between candidate A and the carryable generator. If what is carried is a stored rule that only ever reproduced the source occasion, it is a recording under another name.

**Refuter, written before the conclusion:** exhibit a case where a verbatim recording of one success, with no re-derivation and no head start, produces a novel success in a genuinely substituted habitat, and where the ground pays for it. If that holds, F1 is withdrawn.

### F2: The bond carries the relation the ingredient list drops

Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**
Fig 12 :47: **"recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself."**
Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**
Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**

This governs D2. What enables transfer is not a list of features shared by source and target. It is the typed relation between them, which is why a situation can look quite different and still afford the same move. Fig 12 :117 defines the isomer: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** Two situations can share every atom and differ only in the bond, so surface ingredient overlap is the wrong measure.

World corroboration, low overlap with the framework: structure-mapping theory, opened via Wikipedia 2026-11-23, quotes Gentner's systematicity principle, *"A predicate that belongs to a mappable system of mutually interconnecting relationships is more likely to be imported into the target than is an isolated predicate."* The Library of Babel essay, opened 2026-11-23, says analogies work because they are *"translating structural relationships and problems through language and scenarios that you are already familiar with, so that the knowledge transfers."* These are corroboration only; the verdict comes from Fig 12.

**Refuter:** show a transfer that is fully predicted by counting shared object attributes while holding relational structure constant, or show a bond that is recoverable from its operands alone. Either breaks F2.

### F3: Far transfer is a union of conditions; a single effect size cannot decide it

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**
Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

Candidate C is refused on two independent grounds. The residual is a slice, so a pooled transfer score throws away the only signal that says which slot failed. And the question is a union, so one score cannot select across intersections. Barnett and Ceci 2002, abstract read 2026-11-23 (direct PubMed fetch returned a security notice): *"Estimation of a single effect size for far transfer is misguided in view of this complexity."* The taxonomy names nine dimensions, which is the world's version of the union verdict.

**Refuter:** exhibit a single scalar that predicts transfer success across all target situations and all object slots with no residual left on any slot, verified against a ground that does not update on the scalar. If it holds, F3 is withdrawn and candidate C revived.

### F4: The description has to travel in effect terms

Fig 6 :30: **"outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."**
Fig 9 :30: **"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect"**
Fig 9 :31: **"rather than the cause"**

D1 and D4 can only differ usefully if the carried description can be read in the target closure. A description sharpened into the source's private vocabulary fails even when the right thing is held. The tacit-knowledge essay, opened 2026-11-23, states the contact-free case: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."*

**Refuter:** show a successful transfer where the carried description is intelligible only inside the source frame and no effect-level statement of it can be given. If so, F4 is narrowed to a preference for reportability, not a condition.

### F5: The new situation's ground settles it; agreement does not

Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**
Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**
Fig 11 :39: **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**
Fig 11 :40: **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

This governs D1 and D3 together. A learner that succeeds on a benchmark and then fails under a small contextual change has not lost a faculty; its contact to the ground changed. The capability claim is agent-and-habitat, so "what enables the animal" is the wrong grammar. What is enabled is the pair.

**Refuter:** improve an agent in isolation, with no change of contact to any ground, and show a durable rise in transfer to substantially different situations. That would break Fig 11 :40 and F5 with it. The same essay records that this is an open, refutable claim, not a settled law.

### F6: A substantially different situation is a substituted habitat, with two silent failures

Fig 11 :66: **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."**
Fig 11 :67: **"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."**
Fig 11 :68: **"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"**
Fig 11 :69: **"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"**
Fig 11 :70: **"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."**

This is the figure-most-specific answer to "differs substantially." The difference is not a larger distance on a similarity scale. It is a change of habitat, which carries two failures that cannot be seen until they act. A synthetic solve proves wiring. This is why a benchmark success is weak evidence for transfer.

**Refuter:** show a target situation that is genuinely substituted, with unchosen actors present, where neither a failed reproduction nor an unintended introduction is detectable even after the goal is scored. If both failures are invisible in principle, F6's checkability claim is withdrawn.

### F7: Where the atom sets differ, an import is owed; re-arrangement adds nothing

Fig 6 :21: **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."**
Fig 6 :22: **"NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world. IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure."**
Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**
Fig 8 :32: **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."**
Fig 8 :53: **"Residual first, frame second."**

If the target situation contains something the learner's closure cannot represent, more practice in the source closure cannot manufacture it. What is owed is a contact with a differing closure, with provenance and a shadow test, and it is debited against the independence of the frame imported from. This is why the metacognitive and social channels matter for people and why the question cannot be answered by more of the same trials.

**Refuter:** derive a new atom of the target situation purely by composing the learner's existing atoms, with no import, and have the ground settle it. That breaks the barrier claim for this case. A mere re-naming of a held atom is not such a derivation.

### F8: The round-trip gap is the honest measure of what abstraction gave up

Fig 4 :27: **"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."**
Fig 4 :30: **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."**
Fig 4 :31: **"The gap is not an error to eliminate."**

A compressed method is reapplicable because detail was thrown away. But the detail does not come back on concretisation. The difference between what was sent up and what came down, R_T, is the report of what the abstraction cannot hold. A transfer claim that reports only success, without the gap, is dead reckoning. The tacit-knowledge essay makes the same point from the human side: *"The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold, and it is the only honest report of how much was given up"* (quoting Fig 4 :31-32).

**Refuter:** show a compression of a method into an effect description whose round trip to a substituted habitat is lossless on every slot, measured against the ground. If R_T is zero in general, F8 collapses.

### F9: The chain usually breaks at vocabulary and objective, not at planning

Fig 3 :25: **"Perception has obvious failures and obvious tests. Planning has obvious failures and obvious tests."**
Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**
Fig 12 :112: **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."**
Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**

D1's common failure is not that the learner cannot act. It is that the learner has no term for what the new situation affords, or cannot say what would count as progress there. That link has no instrument, so effort spent above it arrives at a step that cannot use it. The meta-learning literature makes the same structural point in its own vocabulary: *"A learning algorithm may perform very well in one domain, but not on the next,"* because each algorithm carries an inductive bias that has to match the problem (Wikipedia, opened 2026-11-23).

**Refuter:** show a case of failed far transfer where the learner demonstrably had terms for the target's affordances and a working comparison for progress, and the break is elsewhere. That would narrow F9 to a subset.

### F10: Negative transfer is the mirror image, not the absence of the mechanism

Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
Fig 11 :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**
Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."**

A stored item that paid in the source can interfere in the target. Negative transfer, opened via Wikipedia 2026-11-23, is *"the interference of the previous knowledge with new learning, where one set of events could hurt performance on related tasks."* In comparative cognition the design requirement is stricter: *"Transfer stimuli must be novel to release behavior from a prior reinforcement history that could confound transfer performance"* (abstract read via search index, 2026-11-23). A spent part can be carried as though it were a catalyst, and nothing in the record marks which it is. So enablement is not a property of the stored item; it is a relation between that item, the new residual and the ground.

**Refuter:** exhibit a stored item whose valence is invariant across every change of residual and every habitat. That would break the valence claim and make a standing enablement list possible.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter, written before the conclusion

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following is demonstrated, with a ground that does not update on the claim:

1. A verbatim recording of one success, with no re-derivation, produces a novel success in a genuinely substituted habitat with unchosen actors present (breaks F1).
2. A transfer is fully predicted by shared object attributes with relational structure held constant, or the typed bond is recovered from its operands alone (breaks F2).
3. A single scalar predicts transfer across all object slots and all target situations with zero residual left anywhere (breaks F3).
4. Improving an agent with no change of contact raises its transfer to substantially different situations (breaks Fig 11 :40 and F5).
5. A compression of a method into effect terms has a lossless round trip to every substituted habitat, so R_T is zero in general (breaks F8).
6. A new atom required by the target is derived from the learner's old closure alone, with no import, and settled by the ground (breaks F7).

Until one of these is run, nothing in this folder may be labelled `grounded`. A failure condition is more useful to us than an endorsement.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Lead with the union shape and the refuter, not with a mechanism name. The figures return a kind of thing, not a name (Fig 9 :32).
2. Report transfer per slot and per domain. Do not pool into a transfer score; a global near-zero with one live slot is a legal state (Fig 1 :13-14).
3. State what crossed. If it is a recording, do not call it transfer even if the benchmark score rose (Fig 4 :25).
4. Measure the round trip. An abstraction without a measured gap is dead reckoning (Fig 4 :30).
5. Treat a substantially different situation as a substituted habitat and enumerate what was failed to reproduce and what was unintentionally introduced before scoring (Fig 11 :68-69).
6. Ask a checker for a failure, not for agreement. Send the residual and the refuter first, the theory only after their verdict is recorded (Fig 10 :52).

## 6. Seven-question recursive derivation, condensed

1. **What fails?** The prediction of the held library in the target slot (Fig 1 :19, Symbols :38-40).
2. **Is it one question?** No. Union of library, carried structure, habitat and frame, with per-slot residuals.
3. **What kind of item crosses?** A method or a head start, never a recording (Fig 4 :22-25).
4. **What makes it carry?** The typed bond and the relation, not the ingredient list (Fig 12 :5, :94).
5. **Can more trials supply what is missing?** Only inside the closure. A genuinely new atom requires import with provenance and a shadow test (Fig 6 :21-23, Fig 8 :32).
6. **Who settles it?** The ground of the target situation, separately for each frame. Agreement is disqualified when the frames share a pool (Fig 2 :29, :35, :40).
7. **What is owed before asking again?** A source-discriminating observation, a measured round-trip gap, and a refuter written in advance. The slot for the cognitive mechanism remains marked `???` (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134).
