## 4. Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed zero. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 4 :22-25 (method crossed, recording replayed), Fig 12 :5 (bond carries the relation), Fig 9 :55-57 (union) and Fig 11 :66-69 (substituted habitat).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07_the_tether_answer.md`: carryable generator, effect terms, reproduced habitat, non-updating ground, per-slot residual.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the concrete research claim: a single mechanism or score explains far transfer, measured by a benchmark in a new situation.

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **directional filter**, which the concrete claim flattens into a symmetric similarity measure;
- the **bond**, which the concrete claim replaces with an ingredient or attribute list;
- the **union and per-slot structure**, which the concrete claim aggregates into one effect size;
- the **silent habitat failures**, which the concrete claim treats as a harder version of the same task.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: what crosses must be reapplicable and the ground in the target must settle it. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no experiment was run. It is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is a condensed version of the figures" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
