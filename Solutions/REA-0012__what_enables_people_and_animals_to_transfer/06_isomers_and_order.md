## RESIDUAL

A learner succeeds in a source situation and is tested in a target situation that differs substantially. The residual is not "did it succeed" but which slot fails and what crossed the boundary between the two. Three questions are owed: (1) is naming a method a forbidden name or a binding onto held terms, (2) what is the order of the conditions, and (3) are the source and target situations isomers, a functional group, or something with a changing atom set?

### Q2: What is the ordering of the crossing?

Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**
Fig 4 :23: **"upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."**
Fig 4 :24: **"downward: something that makes the next search cheaper. Never a replay of what worked once."**
Fig 4 :25: **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."**

The order is not a symmetric similarity relation. It is a directional filter. A recording carried in the wrong direction still looks like knowledge, which is why it is dangerous. Machine learning gives a low-overlap analogue: Model-Agnostic Meta-Learning, opened 2026-11-23, trains parameters so that *"a small number of gradient steps with a small amount of training data from a new task will produce good generalization performance on that task,"* which the paper summarizes as *"trains the model to be easy to fine-tune."* That is a head start, not a replay of one occasion. Corroboration only; the figure decides.

- **Falsifier, written before the run:** show a case where a verbatim recording of one occasion, with no re-derivation and no head start, produces a novel success in a substituted habitat and the ground pays. If so, the directional filter claim fails.

### Q3: Are the source and target situations isomers?

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**

Two cases must be separated:

1. **Same atom set, different arrangement.** If source and target contain the same actors, conditions and relations but with a different bond, they are isomers over a fixed atom set. Same ingredients, different behaviour. This is the strong and checkable version.
2. **Different atom set.** If the target requires an actor or condition absent from the source, the relation is not isomerism. It is composition plus import, and the import needs a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :23). Calling this isomerism would hide exactly the difference that makes the transfer hard.

World analogue, low overlap: structure-mapping theory, opened 2026-11-23, reports Gentner's finding that analogies map relations rather than object attributes, and the systematicity principle that interconnected predicates are imported more readily than isolated ones. Comparative cognition adds an instrument: *"Transfer stimuli must be novel and dissimilar from training stimuli to rule out stimulus generalization as the sole basis of transfer performance"* (abstract read via search index, 2026-11-23). Without that instrument, an apparent isomer result may just be stimulus generalization.

- **Falsifier, written before the run:** show that the source and target have different atom sets while still calling them isomers, or show that a bond is recoverable from its operands. The first is a category error and the second breaks the figure's definition.

### Q4: What order are the conditions in?

Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**
Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**

The conditions are: a carryable method, an effect-level description, a reproduced habitat, and a non-updating ground. They are not ranked by importance. They are ordered by dependency: you cannot describe what crossed until you know what the carried item is; you cannot reproduce a habitat until you know what the goal required; you cannot read a ground that has been moved by the learner's own report. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. This is why a transfer program that only adds training exemplars can improve source performance while the target remains untouched.

Barnett and Ceci 2002, abstract read 2026-11-23: *"Estimation of a single effect size for far transfer is misguided in view of this complexity."* Their nine dimensions are the world's version of the same union. Negative transfer is the mirror case: *"the interference of the previous knowledge with new learning, where one set of events could hurt performance on related tasks"* (Wikipedia, opened 2026-11-23). A spent part carried as a catalyst explains why more prior experience can lower target performance (Fig 12 :119 - *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."*).

- **Falsifier, written before the run:** exhibit one intervention that removes far-transfer failure in every condition simultaneously, with the ground unchanged and per-slot residual reported. If it holds, the union verdict is withdrawn.

## CONCLUSION

The crossing is ordered and lossy. What should cross is a method or a head start with its bond intact; a recording crosses as a description of one occasion and cannot generate a novel success. Source and target situations are isomers only over a fixed atom set, where same atoms under a different bond give different behaviour; when the atom set differs, the relation is composition plus import, not isomerism. The four conditions form a union ordered by dependency, and one lever does not clear their intersections. The specific cognitive mechanism that occupies the method slot is not named here; it stays a `gap` reported as a verdict.

This framing rescues the defensible part of the naive answers without their failures: relational structure rather than stored rules, directional method transfer rather than similarity, and a union of conditions rather than one enablement variable. Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**
