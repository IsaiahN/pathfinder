**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 2 and the pre-registered refuter in section 3 are both in front of you. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Mode stated up front:** `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the per-domain claims, not `grounded`. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

**Subject:** Check one output before you decide whether the shape claim is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question: what enables a person or an animal to carry learning into a situation that differs substantially from the one it learned in. I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` shape, `specified` per domain, not `grounded`):** far transfer is not one faculty and not one number. It is a union of at least four conditions with different anchors. The residual has to be read per slot, not as a global score, because a global average can hide one live failure. What crosses a boundary usefully is a reapplicable method or a head start, never a recording of one occasion. A recording replayed in a new habitat repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one. The carried item is a typed relation, not a list of shared ingredients. Two situations can share every atom and differ only in the arrangement, which is exactly where the behaviour diverges.
>
> A substantially different situation is not a harder version of the old one. It is a substituted habitat. You fail to reproduce some of what the goal needed, and you inherit actors you did not choose. Both failures are invisible until the goal fails or the introduced actor acts. A synthetic solve proves wiring, not capability. Capability belongs to the learner and its habitat together, so improving the learner alone, with no change of contact, changes nothing.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** I am not naming a cognitive mechanism. The framework I am testing returns a shape, not a name, so the slot for the mechanism stays marked `???`. I am not claiming that similarity causes transfer, or that one effect size selects success across situations, or that more training in the same frame supplies what a new situation requires. I am not claiming this is settled by experiment.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before sending this:**
> 1. A verbatim recording of one success, with no re-derivation, produces a novel success in a genuinely substituted habitat with unchosen actors present.
> 2. A transfer is fully predicted by counting shared object attributes while relational structure is held constant, or the typed relation turns out to be recoverable from its operands alone.
> 3. A single scalar predicts transfer across every slot and every target situation with zero residual left anywhere.
> 4. Improving the learner with no change of contact to any ground raises its transfer to substantially different situations.
> 5. A compression of a method into an effect description has a lossless round trip to every substituted habitat.
> 6. A new atom required by the target is derived from the learner's old closure alone, with no import, and the ground settles it.
>
> If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I will send the sources. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority. Please record your verdict on the output above before you look at them, so that a later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
>
> **If you want to use the method:** run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
