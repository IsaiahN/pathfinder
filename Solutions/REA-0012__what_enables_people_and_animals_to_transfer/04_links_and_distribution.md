## What in the wider literature bears on this question

1. **Gentner's systematicity principle bears directly on the bond finding.** Relations embedded in a connected system are imported into a target more readily than isolated attributes. This is the world's version of Fig 12 :5 and Fig 12 :117. It is a genuinely low-overlap witness relative to the framework, coming from cognitive science rather than from the metatheory.
2. **Barnett and Ceci bear directly on the union and per-slot finding.** Their nine-dimensional taxonomy and their refusal of a single far-transfer effect size match Fig 1 :19 and Fig 9 :55-57. It is the closest world source to the question as asked.
3. **The comparative-cognition literature bears on the habitat-substitution finding.** Requiring novel, dissimilar transfer stimuli and equal baseline performance is an instrument for the two silent failures of Fig 11 :68-69. A transfer score with repeated stimuli can be learned in the test itself.
4. **MAML and meta-learning bear on method versus recording.** The model is trained so that a few steps on a new task work, which is a head start, not a replay. This corroborates Fig 4 :22-24 from a field that rarely cites cognitive science.
5. **Negative transfer is the mirror case.** Prior knowledge can hurt performance on a related task, which is what a spent part carried as a catalyst looks like (Fig 12 :119, Fig 11 :49).

Overlap declaration: the two Wikipedia transfer pages, the meta-learning page and the MAML paper are all machine-learning sources and count as one broad pool, not four independent witnesses. The structure-mapping and analogy pages share Gentner 1983 and count as one witness. The four framework essays share the framework author and carry no independent weight. The Barnett and Ceci paper and the comparative-cognition material are the two genuinely low-overlap corroborators. No world source conflicts with the figure verdict, so the conflict rule was not triggered.
