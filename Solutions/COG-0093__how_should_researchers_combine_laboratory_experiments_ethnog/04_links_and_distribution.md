## Wider literature that bears on the question

Four strands, with overlap declared.

1. **Comparative method and animal behaviour (W1, genuinely low overlap).** Tinbergen's four questions are complementary rather than competing. The schema is used as a "periodic table" whose empty cells mark gaps in knowledge. This corroborates the figure verdict that four frames answer four different questions and that keeping the answers separate is the point. It is an analogue, not a figure line.
2. **Social-science methodology (W2 and W3, one evidence pool).** The triangulation entry supplies the navigational sense, in which two bearings are taken on a fixed point, and the convergence sense, in which researchers search for regularities across sources. Those are two different quantities. The figures decide for the navigational sense and against treating convergence as a verdict when the sources share a pool. The multimethodology entry supplies the method-specific analytic interests and dialectical pluralism's refusal to require convergence. These two Wikipedia entries count as one witness, not two.
3. **Anthropology and the target field (W4 and W5, partly shared pool with strand 2).** Emic and etic are insider and outsider frames whose complementarity is widely recognised, which is the low-overlap union in Fig 8 at the human scale. The cultural psychology entry grounds the question's field and supplies a real case: Nisbett and Cohen combined a laboratory aggression measure with crime-rate and demographic statistics, and Shweder reports repeated failure to replicate Western lab findings in non-Western settings. That last point is a residual at the contact of methods, not a reason to average them.
4. **Physics (W6, genuinely low overlap).** In the proton radius puzzle, two methods converged for decades and a third, differing method found a 4 percent discrepancy. Later measurements and reanalyses resolved it. This instantiates the warning that two instruments can never talk and still agree, so the agreement is the pool talking, and that the ground settles, not the consensus.

The framework essays are all by the same author as the figures, so their overlap is maximal. They are used only for the canonical reading of "shape" and for the simulation-status declaration, never as independent world witnesses.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 4 :22** - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full architecture before a recipient has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the separator and its refuter.
- **Fig 10 :42** - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27** - *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"* - ask what they would expect to see if the separator were true before offering the links.
- **Fig 2 :40** - *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* - a list selected for people who already agree produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 11 :51** - *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* Do not rank venues or people by how receptive they are likely to be; rank nothing by expected agreement.

## Submission constraint

This packet is an LLM-assisted, simulated derivation. It is not a research report and must not be submitted as one. ORPC's author guidelines require APA 7th edition, proposal-first submission, and blind peer review; MMIRA and IACCP venues expect author-owned work. A human researcher who wants to use any of this must own, rewrite, and check the submission, and must run the refuter against a real ground first. Putting a preprint on a public server does not make a simulated derivation a ground reading.
