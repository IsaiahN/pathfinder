## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the four methods an ingredient list or a molecule?

Fig 12 :5: *"The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
Fig 12 :46: *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands."*
Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."*

"Lab + ethnography + history + population" under a single symmetric `+` records the ingredients and nothing about how they are joined. But the ordered bonds are available and they make different substances:

- `D3 → D1`: the historicalrecord precedes and constrains the lab task. Sequence.
- `D2 ⇒ D1`: the ethnographic category produces a lab measure that did not exist before. Production.
- `D1 ⋛ D4`: the lab effect and the population distribution compare at a threshold. Comparison.
- `D1 − D4`: the population reading is absent or subtracted from the lab reading. Absence of an operand.

Under `≡`, two differently named procedures that reach the same referent would be one term. Under `+`, they would be two. The choice is not cosmetic: Fig 12 :92-93 says a code that charges for sequence under a symmetric bond overcharges by log₂(k!), while the bond term undercharges, and only one of the two errors is usually noticed.

**Result:** the combination is a molecule, not a list. Four methods with the same names under different bonds are different objects, and a mixed-methods report that records only the method list has *"recorded half of itself."*

**Falsifier:** two arrangements with identical method sets but different bonds that behave identically under every available ground. If that is shown, Fig 12 :94 does not govern this domain and Q1 is withdrawn.

### Q2 - Are they isomers?

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

Two readings.

**Weak reading.** As `01` lists them, D1, D2, D3 and D4 share no method atom at all; each is a different atom. So they are not isomers under Fig 12 :117. Each instance of "a reading against a ground" is a **functional group**: the group fixes the kind (a reading), the qualifier names the member (lab, field, archive, population). This is the defensible claim.

**Strong reading.** If each method is written over a shared atom set, for example {question, instrument, case, observation, ground}, differing only in which atom is manipulated and which is held fixed, then two methods that share all five atoms but differ in the bond are **isomers**: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. The manipulation-versus-observation contrast is then a bond difference, not an atom difference. That is the stronger, checkable structural claim.

**Falsifier:** show that one method requires an atom the other does not (for example that historical evidence requires a dated record that no lab method can hold even as a parameter). The isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. This is the same falsifier form used in the exemplar's Q3, applied here.

**Status:** functional-group reading `triangulated` (Fig 12 plus the multimethodology source W3 that assigns each method a distinct analytic interest); isomer reading `single-source` on the figure side and conditional on the shared-atom set.

### Q3 - What order is licensed?

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*
Fig 3 :25: *"Perception has obvious failures and obvious tests. Planning has obvious failures and obvious tests. The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."*
Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."*

The figures license one order, the dependency chain: perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry. Mapped onto methods, this is not "do ethnography first, then lab". It is:

1. **Perception:** what is in front of the study, and how it differs from what was there before. Field observation is often the instrument here, but the figure names the link, not the method.
2. **Vocabulary:** terms for what the situation affords, what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked. This is where emic categories enter, with provenance (Fig 6 :12).
3. **Objective:** what would count as progress in our own terms, stated before the design is fixed. Operators :51: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one."*
4. **Planning:** a route chosen under incomplete information.
5. **Learn and carry:** name what worked, verify it, carry it as a method (Fig 4 :22).

The chain usually breaks between 2 and 3, not between 1 and 2 or 3 and 4 (Fig 3 :24-26). A team can perceive the case and plan the analysis and still have no instrument for what the case affords or no comparison that counts as progress.

**Falsifier:** a mixed-methods project that fails at planning while the affordance vocabulary and the objective comparison both measure high and non-declining. That would relocate the break and break Fig 3 :25.

### Q4 - What crosses, and what stays

Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."*
Fig 12 :107: *"Settling compresses the description and not what it cost to establish."*
Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*

A settled combined result can serve as an atom in the next loop, which is recursion. But the cost of settling it is not carried in its description. Where the arrangement is not recorded as a method, every lineage pays again (Fig 12 :108-110). So the transferable object is the molecule *plus* its lineage: which methods, which bond, which ground paid, and what would refuse it.

**Falsifier:** showing that a combined result can be composed over without its bond type and ground record, with no loss of refutability. That breaks Fig 12 :107-110.

### Q5 - Catalysts, cycles, and the two currencies

Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."*
Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input."*
Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*
Fig 12 :126: *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*

A method that survives its own use is a catalyst: an interview protocol that can be reapplied without being consumed by one field season. A spent part is the one-occasion recording. The research loop is a cycle in Fig 12 :121's sense, with the sign of the loop deciding whether it closes: if the residual re-enters as the input to the next method selection, the chain closes (Fig 13 :88-89); if it does not, the methods are a line that runs in place.

The price has two currencies. A four-method design may be cheap in bits (one paragraph in a methods section) and extremely expensive in actions (years of fieldwork plus archival access plus a national survey). Fig 12 :128: *"The two readings come apart in both directions."* Reading only one is how a proposal proposes arrangements it cannot afford to test.

**Falsifier:** a demonstration that description bits and settling actions are convertible at a fixed rate for this domain. That would break Fig 12 :125-130.

## ANALOGUES (secondary, not figure lines)

- **Animal behaviour (W1).** Tinbergen's four questions are complementary, not rival: mechanism, ontogeny, function, phylogeny. The "periodic table" use highlights gaps. This is a low-overlap analogue for keeping four kinds of answer separate. It does not license a mapping from any one question to any one method.
- **Anthropology (W4).** Emic and etic are insider and outsider frames whose complementarity is widely recognised. The insight for order is Pike's: the dichotomy was proposed as a *methodological solution*, not an epistemological verdict, which is the vocabulary link of Fig 3 in human form.
- **Physics (W6).** In the proton radius puzzle the disagreement was between methods, and the resolution came from new measurements and reanalysis. The methods were not averaged into a consensus; the ground moved the verdict. This is Fig 7 :35 in another field.

Each analogue corroborates the mechanism shape only. Under Fig 8 :50-52, convergent derivation from a different evidence pool is at maximum independence; an adopted import is not. The record of where each came from is the only separator, so these are listed as analogues, not as extra figure lines.

## CONCLUSION

The four methods are a molecule, not an ingredient list, and the bond carries the information the method names do not. Over a shared atom set, two methods with the same atoms and different bonds are isomers; as currently named they are members of one functional group. The only order the figures license is the dependency chain, and it breaks at vocabulary or objective, not at planning. What crosses between methods is a generator plus its lineage, never a recording of one occasion, and the two prices, bits and actions, must be read separately. Every claim here is `specified` or `general`, none `grounded`, and each has a falsifier written above.
