## The question restated as an effect

The object to be explained is the gap between what any one method predicts about a phenomenon and what that phenomenon actually does, indexed per case and per time: R = |Γ(b,a) − o′| per slot (Fig 1 :19). A laboratory experiment, an ethnography, a historical reconstruction, and a population analysis are four bounded frames. Each has different actors, a different currency, and a different anchor (Fig 4 :20). The question asks what combination makes R shrink at each slot without manufacturing agreement among the frames.

Restated this way, the question is not "which method is best" and not "what fixed percentage of each method should be used". It is: under what conditions does contacting a second, third, or fourth frame add reach rather than just add spelling? An answer would look like a separator with checkable conditions, plus a statement of what no figure can return. That sought-for shape is bound as an ordinary term in `01` and `07`, so it can accrue a residual and be refused like anything else.

## Headline verdict

The four methods are four frames with different currencies and anchors, so they must be combined by triangulation, not by negotiation: each is corrected separately against a ground that does not update on what any method thinks, and their verdicts are kept per slot and never averaged (Fig 2 :29, :35, :45, :46; Fig 1 :19, :22). Independence is the design variable to buy (Fig 2 :31, :42); a union of differing closures is mute until a residual is described and the frame that predicts its shape is chosen (Fig 8 :19-21, :53). The whole is a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition and not one lever (Fig 9 :54-57). No figure line returns a universal mixing ratio, method order, or sample allocation. That is a declared `gap`, reported as a verdict rather than a silence (Fig 13 :134).

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own terms. First, agreement among frames that share an evidence pool is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other (Fig 2 :18-21, :40). Second, selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures exactly the correlation that disqualifies a verdict (Fig 2 :39-40). Third, showing a potential checker the whole architecture before taking their verdict lets them reconstruct the claim, and a verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it (Fig 10 :42).
