## 0. The claim under audit

> Combine laboratory experiments, ethnography, historical evidence, and population-level data by triangulating them against a non-updating ground, keeping each method's verdict indexed per case and separate, buying independence between them in proportion to the cost of being wrong, describing the residual before selecting any method, and treating the four as a union of overlapping conditions rather than a partition or a weighted average. No universal mixing ratio, method order, or sample allocation is returned by the figures. That is a `gap`, not a hidden answer.

The sought-for shape is bound here as an ordinary term before the run, so it can accrue a residual and be refused by the same test as everything else (`THE_FORMULA.md`, Direction). This is the pre-commitment discipline: the figures cannot see the direction of a question whose answer shape is held only as a preference.

**Arity, symmetry, scale of R.** Arity: at least three, a frame's prediction Γ(b,a) against an observation o′ at a slot s, ordered in time (Symbols table :33-40). Symmetry: not symmetric. The ground does not update on the frame (Fig 2 :25); "better" is a threshold comparison against a fixed reference, not a conjunction (Fig 12 :85-89). Scale: the slot is one case or one cultural moment, while "population-level" is a different stratum, so the residual is intrinsically cross-scale and passes through a lossy membrane (Fig 4).

## 2. Findings

### F1 - The verdict stays indexed per case; averaging throws away the signal (decisive for the unit)

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*

A pooled "mixed-methods effect size" is the aggregate Fig 1 forbids. The question of how to combine methods is not a question about a single global score; it is a question about what to do when one slot is live and the others are quiet. A global near-zero residual with one live slot is a legal state (Fig 1 :13). So the first rule is: each method reports its residual per case, and the combination keeps those four readings separate.

**Refuter:** a demonstration that a single pooled composite predicts the ground better than the per-slot readings, across a change of residual, against a ground none of the frames can move. If that holds, Fig 1 :19/:22 is wrong for this domain and the verdict falls.

**Status: triangulated** (Fig 1 and Symbols table :42-43, which says R is measured per slot, never as a single global number).

### F2 - The four methods are four frames: same loop, different cast

Fig 4 :20: *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."*
Fig 11 :46: *"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"*
Fig 11 :47: *"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"*
Fig 11 :48: *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."*

D1 to D4 are not four versions of one instrument. They have different actors, currencies, and anchors, which is exactly what Fig 4 :20 says changes across a boundary. The habitat of a cultural phenomenon must be enumerated from the world, not composed from a preferred list of methods (Fig 11 :43-48). The framework does not hand you a standing four-method checklist; the question happens to name four, and their admissibility is earned by a nameable anchor for each.

X1 and X2 fail that test. "Consensus among methods" has no anchor independent of the frames, and "culture" has no referent separate from the four readings. They are not domains yet (Fig 2 :29; Fig 11 :73).

**Refuter:** name an anchor for consensus, or for culture, that does not move when a frame pushes on it. Until then, admitting them as domains reintroduces the mirror collapse.

**Status: triangulated** (Fig 4 and Fig 11, low overlap in their roles).

### F3 - Triangulate by ranging against a fixed ground, never by negotiating agreement

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*
Fig 2 :46: *"Each frame is corrected by it separately, and what makes the correction sound is that neither frame can move it."*

This is the load-bearing finding. The ordinary methodological word "triangulation" is used in two senses. In the navigational sense, two bearings are taken on a fixed landmark (world source W2). That is Fig 2 :35. In the social-research sense, triangulation often means seeking convergence or "regularities in the research data" among methods (W2), which is Fig 2 :13-21's collapse if the methods draw on the same pool. The figures decide between them: agreement with a shared source is the source talking. So "the four methods agree, therefore the finding is robust" is not a verdict; it is a convention at best (Fig 2 :41).

Practical consequence: a mixed-methods design must name, for each claim, the thing that would change each method's reading independently. If the ethnography and the survey item were both written from the same lab theory, they are one frame wearing two names.

**Refuter:** a case where two methods demonstrably sharing an evidence pool still contribute independent verdict weight against a ground neither can move. That would break Fig 2 :31/:40.

**Status: triangulated** (Fig 2, plus the navigational sense in W2 as secondary corroboration only).

### F4 - Independence is bought, not assumed; it is one quantity read twice

Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."*
Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*
Fig 8 :47: *"Independence is one quantity read twice."*
Fig 10 :80: *"Frames whose independence is renewed by separate experience can be maintained"*

The four named methods start with plausible low overlap: a manipulation, an in-context observation, a dated record, and a distribution. But overlap is a property of evidence, not of method names. It rises whenever the same theoretical frame writes the lab stimulus, the interview schedule, the archival coding scheme, and the survey item (world source W5 on W.E.I.R.D. dominance). It also decays with use: frames that only check against each other rotate rather than refresh (Fig 10 :80-81). Independence must be renewed by separate experience, and purchased in proportion to what an error would cost.

The same parameter decides two things, which is why it is one quantity read twice (Fig 8 :47-49): whether agreement means anything, and whether the union has any surplus.

**Refuter:** show that increasing genuine evidence-level independence never changes either the weight of agreement or the size of the union surplus. That breaks Fig 8 :47.

**Status: triangulated** (Fig 2 and Fig 8 and Fig 10, three distinct roles).

### F5 - Residual first, frame second; an undirected union is mute

Fig 9 :27: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*
Fig 9 :30: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"*
Fig 8 :19: *"closures differ, so a surplus exists"*
Fig 8 :21: *"nothing was aimed; the union is mute"*
Fig 8 :53: *"Residual first, frame second."*

The question as posed invites the echo-without-shadow error: pick four prestigious methods and assert that combining them is better. Fig 8 :19-21 forbids that inference. A union of differing closures has a surplus only when a residual is described and a frame is selected because its closure predicts that residual's shape. Otherwise four methods in a room produce motion, not progress (Fig 7 :32).

So the working order is: state R in effect terms (arity, symmetry, scale), then ask which method's closure already predicts its shape. For a lab effect that fails to travel across populations (W5), the owed frame is population-level data; for a practice no instrument has named, it is ethnography; for the origin of a present arrangement, it is historical evidence. The choice follows the residual.

**Refuter:** a case where a method selected before any residual description produces the same reach as one selected after, with no provenance difference. That would make Fig 8 :53 idle.

**Status: triangulated** (Fig 8 and Fig 9, plus the Library of Babel essay on steering by the question).

### F6 - It is a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition and not one lever

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

"How should researchers combine four methods" is one lever aimed at a union. The conditions overlap: a phenomenon may be lab-visible, field-visible, documented, and distributed at once, and the overlap is a compound with its own loop. One lever, for example adding a fifth method or increasing sample size, clears only the region where a single condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. Unbundling is not bookkeeping; it is the second-order value of the split, because the residue becomes describable (Fig 9 :59).

**Refuter:** a single intervention that restores all four conditions with no categorically different residue left over. If that is shown, the union reading is wrong for this domain.

**Status: triangulated** (Fig 9 and Fig 3, with Tinbergen's four complementary questions in W1 as low-overlap corroboration).

### F7 - The chain is ordered by dependency and usually breaks at vocabulary or objective

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*
Fig 3 :25: *"Perception has obvious failures and obvious tests. Planning has obvious failures and obvious tests. The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."*
Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
Fig 3 :30: *"Most improvement effort in practice lands above the break, because that is where the measurements already exist."*

A team can run all four methods competently and still fail because it has no term for what the situation affords or no way to say what would count as progress in its own terms. That is not a weak reading; it is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22-23). In mixed-methods practice the broken link is typically the objective: methods are combined because a grant or a paradigm requires it, not because a stated progress measure requires the second method.

Practical consequence: before choosing a combination, write down what each method would count as progress and what the case affords. If neither can be stated, more method is effort above the break.

**Refuter:** a study that shows the mixed-methods chain failing at planning while the vocabulary and objective links measure high and non-declining. That would relocate the break and break Fig 3 :25.

**Status: triangulated** (Fig 3 and Fig 12 :111-115, which notes that a vocabulary with only ingredients can name a state and not a route to one).

### F8 - What crosses between methods is a method or a head start, never a recording

Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*
Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*
Fig 6 :12: *"source gate: provenance"*
Fig 6 :13: *"shadow test: explains a gap"*
Fig 6 :14: *"the only operator that moves the wall"*

When ethnography supplies a category to the lab, what may travel is the procedure that produced the category and the conditions under which it was produced, not the category as a finished object. A finished category replayed in a lab task is a recording of one occasion; it produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one (Fig 4 :26). If a genuinely new primitive crosses, it needs a source gate (where did it come from) and a shadow test (which gap does it explain). Without them, convergence and adopted import look identical in the contents (Fig 8 :50-52).

**Refuter:** show that a replayed category from one method adds the same reach as a transferable method, with provenance held constant. That breaks Fig 4 :22-26.

**Status: triangulated** (Fig 4 and Fig 6, with Fig 8 corroborating the provenance half).

### F9 - Adding a method is a handoff between rooms, and the next room must differ

Fig 7 :13: *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*
Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*
Fig 7 :36: *"The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."*

Adding a fifth study in the same closure is booking a room like the last one, which is how a chain stops travelling (Fig 7 :42). Progress per handoff has two factors, bracket fidelity across the handoff and ground pressure on the room (Fig 7 :37-39); zero either and the chain runs in place. So "we used more methods" is not evidence of progress. The next method must be selected against the residual the last one surfaced. This is the operational form of F5 at the level of a research programme.

**Refuter:** a chain of same-closure handoffs whose ground-predictive power rises monotonically with no change in either factor. That would break Fig 7 :37.

**Status: triangulated** (Fig 7 and Fig 8, with W6 proton radius as low-overlap corroboration that a third, differing method surfaced the residual).

### F10 - A combined result is a molecule with a priced bond, not an ingredient list

Fig 12 :5: *"The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."*
Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."*
Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*
Fig 12 :126: *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*

"Lab plus ethnography plus history plus population" records the ingredients and not the arrangement. The bond matters: does the historical record precede the lab task (sequence), does the ethnographic category generate a new lab measure (production), or do the readings compare against a threshold (comparison)? Same four methods under different bonds are different substances (Fig 12 :117). Once a combined result settles against the ground, it becomes an atom for the next loop, which is the recursion, not an import.

The two currencies, description bits and actions spent to find out whether the arrangement holds, do not add (Fig 12 :125-128). A mixed-methods proposal can be cheap to describe and unaffordable to settle; this is why "just add a method" is not a cost-free answer.

**Refuter:** two arrangements with identical method sets but different bonds that behave identically under every available ground. That would break Fig 12 :94/:117.

**Status: triangulated** (Fig 12 and Fig 4, since the bond is what crosses).

### F11 - The channel decays, the ground does not; a method's valence flips with the residual

Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*
Fig 10 :26: *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*
Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*
Fig 11 :73: *"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."*

Methods are not good or bad in themselves. Ethnography is a benefactor when the residual is an unnamed category and a malfactor when a dated count is needed and only thick description is supplied. Population data is a benefactor for transportability and a malfactor when it pools away the live slot (Fig 10 :26). So a standing *best combination for cultural psychology* is refused by Fig 11 :51, the same line that refuses a standing list of good actors.

The ground, by contrast, is whatever settles the particular question. A population distribution is an instrument reading and part of the habitat, not automatically the ground (Fig 11 :73). Confusing the two is a live strain, recorded in section 6 as S4.

**Refuter:** an invariant ranking of the four methods across two documented changes of residual. That would break Fig 11 :49/:51.

**Status: triangulated** (Fig 10 and Fig 11, different objects: channel versus valence).

### F12 - No figure line returns a mixing ratio, a method order, or a sample allocation: `gap`

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*
Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*
Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

After a genuine re-census, no line governs the universal ratio, the fixed sequence, or the allocation of effort among the four methods. That is a `gap` of the shape-not-a-name kind, not a hidden number waiting for a cleverer reading. What the figures return is the separator in F1 to F11 plus the instruments that would close the remaining question. Reporting this as a gap is required: unreachable is a verdict.

**Refuter:** derive a unique ratio or order from the figure lines alone, with no binding from a particular field. If that is possible, this `gap` is withdrawn.

**Status: single-source on the limit (the limit is stated in the figures README and Fig 13); declared `gap`.**

## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion and before outreach, per the framework's rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A pooled composite across the four methods predicts a non-updating ground better than per-slot separate verdicts, across a change of residual (breaks F1, Fig 1 :19/:22).
2. Two methods with demonstrably shared evidence still contribute independent verdict weight (breaks F3, Fig 2 :31/:40).
3. A cross-method category imported with no provenance and no shadow test earns verdict weight (breaks F8, Fig 6 :12-14).
4. The ground changes its answer because of what a method thinks (breaks Fig 2 :25/:33-34).
5. A universal mixing ratio, method order, or sample allocation is derived from figure lines with no field binding (breaks F12; closes the declared gap).
6. A single lever restores all four conditions with no categorically different residue (breaks F6, Fig 9 :57).
7. A standing ranking of the four methods survives two documented changes of residual (breaks F11, Fig 11 :51).

If any fires, the separator is wrong and the claim is withdrawn or narrowed visibly. Until one is run, nothing here is labelled `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Write the residual first, in effect terms and per case. Do not name methods until the frame whose closure predicts its shape is identified (F5).
2. Name each method's anchor and check that it does not move when the method is pushed. Drop "consensus" as a verdict (F3).
3. Buy independence where the cost of being wrong is high; renew it with separate experience, not with more of the same team (F4).
4. Keep four verdicts, not one average. Use filters such as effect size, sample size, and cost for the budget only (F1, Fig 9 :60).
5. Unbundle the union before searching; treat lab-visible and field-visible cases at the intersection as a different kind of case (F6).
6. State what counts as progress and what the case affords before adding a method. That is the broken middle link (F7).
7. Transfer methods and head starts, never recordings of one occasion; gate every import with provenance and a shadow test (F8).
8. Select the next method against the residual the last one surfaced; do not book the same room twice (F9).
9. Price the bond and both currencies; decide in actions as well as description bits (F10).
10. Publish the gap and the refuter. Do not invent a ratio (F12).

## 7. Round trip and honest stop

Carry the separator up (Fig 4 :30): keep each method's verdict separate against a ground that cannot move, and buy independence. Bring it down to a specific study: run four methods, do not average them, and check whether they share a pool. The gap R_T (Fig 4 :31) is what the one-sentence form loses: the per-slot indexing, the source gate on imported categories, the union structure, the dependency order, and the two currencies. That loss is measured, not eliminated, and it is why the outreach packet sends the generator rather than the one-paragraph version.

**Verdict:** the shape survives, with the re-census corrections attached. It is a `specified` separator, `general` on structure, not `grounded`, and it stops at a declared `gap` rather than inventing a ratio. Strains after re-census: none unresolved.
