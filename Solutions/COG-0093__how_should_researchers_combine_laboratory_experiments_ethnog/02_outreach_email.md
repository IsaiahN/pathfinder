# Outreach draft - check the separator before deciding about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** send only after the verdict in stage 1 has been recorded by the recipient. Nothing about the framework is shown before that verdict.

---

**Subject:** One methodological claim to check before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim about combining research methods that I would like you to check against your own work. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, stated first.** For one case at a time, each method predicts something and the phenomenon returns something. The gap is indexed per case, not averaged (Fig 1 :19). A laboratory experiment, an ethnography, a historical reconstruction, and a population analysis have different actors, different currencies, and different anchors. The question is what makes a second or third method add reach instead of just adding agreement.
>
> **The shape claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** combine the four by ranging each separately against a ground that does not change because of what any method thinks. Keep four verdicts, not one average. Buy independence between methods in proportion to what an error would cost, because two methods drawing on the same evidence are one witness. Describe the residual before choosing a method, and treat the four as overlapping conditions rather than a partition. No figure line returns a universal mixing ratio or method order, and the claim does not invent one.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the methods agree more when combined, that a pooled effect size is the answer, that there is a standard sequence such as ethnography then lab then survey, or that culture is a variable with its own anchor. Agreement among frames that share an evidence pool is the pool talking, not corroboration.
>
> **What would refute it:**
> 1. a pooled composite that predicts a non-updating ground better than per-case separate verdicts, across a change of residual;
> 2. two methods that demonstrably share evidence still contributing independent verdict weight;
> 3. a category imported from one method into another with no provenance and no shadow test still earning verdict weight;
> 4. a universal mixing ratio or method order derived from the framework alone;
> 5. one intervention that restores all four conditions with no categorically different residue left.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - The figures and formula, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - The architecture essay: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether
> - The worked derivation: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-intelligence-actually-works
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a methods problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

| Residual and shape before theory | Fig 10 :52 - *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Fig 8 :53 - *"Residual first, frame second."* |

| Offer the formula as a method to reuse | Fig 4 :22 - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* A generator can be given away without giving away an answer. |
| No standing ranking of methods | Fig 11 :51 - *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* |
| No personal emails, no receptiveness rank | Fig 2 :40 - *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; selecting for likely agreement manufactures the disqualifying correlation. |
