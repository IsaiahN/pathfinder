# Outreach draft: check the output before deciding about the claim

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` have been checked against the figure text by the sender. The two-stage offer collapses on contact if the stage-1 output does not survive.

**Subject:** One checkable output on transparency and corruption, before the theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "does transparency reduce corruption when enforcement is weak?" is not one lever. It is a compound of a disclosure operation, a sanction operation, and an outcome. The structural prediction is that disclosure extends perception, but reduction requires the residual to be settled against an anchor that can act. When the sanction path is weak, the anchor has become another frame, so information cannot settle the residual. Bits price the description; actions price finding out whether it holds; they are in different units and a system that accounts only in bits will not notice the shortfall (Fig 12 :125). The expected effect on objective corruption is therefore approximately zero or small, with any measured effect concentrated in perception measures.
>
> **What would refute this output, written before sending:**
> 1. a controlled or credible quasi-experimental study showing a robust fall in an *objective* corruption measure from a transparency intervention in a weak-enforcement setting with no concurrent strengthening of sanctions;
> 2. a demonstration that bits and actions are commensurable, that is, that a fixed information input reliably purchases a fixed sanction output with no additional actor or budget;
> 3. a single anchored dataset on which subjective and objective corruption measures move together under transparency.
>
> If any holds, I will retract the structural claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that transparency never helps. With a pinned anchor, the same information becomes usable. I am not claiming a measured effect size, because no measurement in this package settles it. I am not asking you to trust a framework, and I am not asking for a verdict on the theory.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Lindstedt and Naurin, "Transparency is not Enough", *International Political Science Review* 31(3), 2010: "Just making information available will not prevent corruption if such conditions for publicity and accountability as education, media circulation and free and fair elections are weak."
> - Bauhr and Grimes, "Transparency to curb corruption?", *Crime, Law and Social Change* 68, 2017: transparency measures should not be stretched to include sanctioning.
> - Chen and Ganapati, "Do transparency mechanisms reduce government corruption? A meta-analysis", *International Review of Administrative Sciences* 89(1), 2023: a significant but small effect, larger on subjective than objective measures.
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the eight-step loop is in the formula. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> [name]
