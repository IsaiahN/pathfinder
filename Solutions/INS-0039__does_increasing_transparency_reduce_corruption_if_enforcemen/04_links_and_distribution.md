## What the wider literature contributes, and its overlap problem

- **The accountability condition.** Lindstedt and Naurin (2010) is the closest world source to the figure-derived verdict: information reaches an effect only if there is a publicity condition and a sanctioning mechanism. **Prediction here:** no reduction in objective corruption when enforcement is weak. Corroborates F2 and F3.
- **The measurement distinction.** Chen and Ganapati (2023) find a small overall effect, larger on subjective than objective measures. **Prediction here:** apparent success in perception indices without a matching objective change. Corroborates F7.
- **The conceptual separation.** Bauhr and Grimes (2017) insist that disclosure, answerability and sanctioning are different things and that conflating them produces false positives. **Prediction here:** a transparency index containing enforcement items will look effective by construction. Corroborates F1 and F8.
- **The organisational mediator.** IBP (2010) adds that parties and civic associations are needed to keep an issue on the agenda. This is a habitat-enumeration point and corroborates F6.
- **Perverse effects.** The Wikipedia synthesis records "spectacular transparency" producing defensive behaviour and transparency producing the opacities it claims to remove. **Prediction here:** adaptation within the habitat, not reduction. Corroborates F6 at low overlap (medical regulation and anthropology rather than political economy).

**Overlap declaration.** The four scholarly sources all work in the principal-agent / good-governance tradition and cite one another (Bauhr and Grimes cite Lindstedt and Naurin; Chen and Ganapati cite Bauhr and Grimes). By Fig 2 :40, *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*, their agreement is one witness, not four. The Wikipedia source is lower overlap but is an encyclopedia synthesis, not a primary study. No world source conflicts with the figure verdict, so the conflict rule was not triggered.
