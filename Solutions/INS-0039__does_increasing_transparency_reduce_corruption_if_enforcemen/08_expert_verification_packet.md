# Expert verification packet: transparency under weak enforcement

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher or practitioner in transparency and corruption can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified`: not `grounded`.** No measurement in this packet settles the empirical magnitude. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Does transparency reduce corruption when enforcement is weak?" is not one lever but a union compound of disclosure, sanction and outcome (Fig 9 :54). Disclosure extends perception and is paid in bits; reduction requires an anchor that can act, and that action is paid in a different currency (Fig 12 :125). Where enforcement is weak, the sanctioning body has become a frame rather than a pinned anchor, so information produces coherent drift, not correction (Fig 2 :33-34). Consequently the expected effect of disclosure alone on objective corruption is approximately zero or small, with any measured effect concentrated in perception measures. This is conditional: with a pinned anchor the same information becomes usable.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 2, 3 and 12

- **Statement:** disclosure cannot substitute for a broken action link, and bits cannot pay the action bill.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 12 :126: *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*
- **Refuter:** demonstrate commensurability, that is, show a setting where a fixed information input reliably purchases a fixed sanction output with no additional actor or action budget.

### C2: world-facing; checkable against the empirical literature

- **Statement:** the empirical finding is conditional on publicity and accountability conditions, and the reported effect is larger on subjective than on objective measures.
- **What to check:** Lindstedt and Naurin (2010), *International Political Science Review* 31(3), 301-322: *"Just making information available will not prevent corruption if such conditions for publicity and accountability as education, media circulation and free and fair elections are weak."* Chen and Ganapati (2023), *International Review of Administrative Sciences* 89(1), 257-272: *"transparency has an overall significant, though small, effect size in reducing corruption. Transparency is more effective for reducing subjective than objective corruption measures."* Bauhr and Grimes (2017), *Crime, Law and Social Change* 68, 431-458: transparency measures should not be stretched to include sanctioning or general state capacity.
- **Refuter:** a controlled or credible quasi-experimental study showing a robust fall in an *objective* corruption measure from a transparency intervention in a weak-enforcement setting with no concurrent strengthening of sanctions.

### C3: measurement-facing; checkable by anyone with an anchored dataset

- **Statement:** a perception index and an objective corruption record are different sources of one reading; a source-discriminating instrument is owed before claiming a reduction.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 10 :26: *"saturated metrics, pooled readings"*. Fig 1 :23: *"The ground enters as the only metric."*
- **Refuter:** on one anchored dataset, show subjective and objective corruption measures moving together under transparency, with no divergence. Then the source objection does not bite.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that transparency never helps. With a pinned anchor and an action path, the same information is usable.
- Not a measured effect size, not a causal estimate, and not a general verdict. All substantive claims are `specified`, ungrounded.
- Not that enforcement is the only missing primitive. The habitat may be missing an instrument, a relation, or a source-discriminating measure.
- Not that a perception-index improvement is a corruption reduction.
- Not an endorsement of any specific transparency instrument (open budgets, FOI, e-procurement, asset disclosure). The framework returns a shape, not a name.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. In order of decisiveness:

1. A pre-registered comparison of objective corruption records before and after a disclosure intervention in a weak-enforcement setting, with no concurrent enforcement change.
2. A test of anchor invariance: does the sanctioning body's decision move when disclosed actors push on it?
3. A source-discriminating instrument that separates "people noticed more" from "malfeasance fell".
4. A re-census that shows bits and actions are commensurable, or that the compound is a partition rather than a union.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :54, Fig 3 :19-20, Fig 12 :125-127 and Fig 2 :33-34, four short passages.
2. Check C2 against their own knowledge of the empirical literature. Is the conditional reading fair to Lindstedt and Naurin and to Chen and Ganapati?
3. Name one objective corruption measure that would discriminate a real reduction from a perception change, or say why none is available.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
