## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

R = the gap between the prediction *"more transparency reduces corruption"* and the observation in a setting where the sanction path is weak.

Describe it by arity, symmetry and scale before choosing any figure line (Fig 9 :21-22, *"R, described / arity · symmetry · scale"*):

- **Arity:** three operands, not two. A disclosure operation, an action operation (sanction), and an outcome. This is a **union compound**, not one hard question. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific"* compound. The compound is *transparency with weak enforcement*, and it has its own loop and its own ground.
- **Symmetry:** the two operations are not interchangeable. Swapping disclosure and sanction changes the meaning. This is an ordered relation, not a symmetric one (Fig 12 :72-74, sequence: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"* / *"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes"*). Importantly, the order is not merely temporal: disclosure alone does not produce sanction; sanction can act without disclosure. The bond is gated, not automatic.
- **Scale:** perception and action run at the institutional scale; the residual settles at the outcome scale. The chain is ordered by dependency, not importance (Fig 3 :20).

**Direction bound as an ordinary term (Formula, "Direction").** What would an answer look like? An answer must say (a) what transparency changes, (b) what enforcement changes, (c) what happens to corruption when (a) is present and (b) is weak, and (d) what measurement would discriminate this from its negation. Writing this down makes the sought-for shape refusable by the same test as everything else. Without it, the frame holds the answer as a preference that accrues no residual (Fig 5 :62, *"A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term."*).

## 1. Domain census (before any figure line is chosen)

One line per domain with actors / currency / anchor. A domain without a nameable anchor is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 Disclosure / perception** | publishing agencies, auditors, journalists, media, citizens, oversight bodies | information: visibility, inferability, signal | no anchor of its own; it borrows the outcome anchor | does this extend perception? |
| **D2 Sanction / enforcement** | courts, prosecutors, anti-corruption agencies, voters, parties, complainants with a remedy | actions: prosecutions, recoveries, removal, punishment | the sanction outcome, which must not move when frames push on it (Fig 2 :29, :33) | can the residual be settled? |
| **D3 Outcome and measurement** | officials, patronage networks, firms, citizens, measurement instruments (records, surveys, indices) | corruption level R per slot s | objective corruption readings (convictions, recoveries, audit findings, bribe incidence), never agreement | does R actually fall? |

**Partition or union:** union, not partition. The three domains overlap: the media is in D1 and D3; voters are in D2 and D3; an audit is a disclosure and often the precursor to a sanction. An overlap is a compound with its own loop, so it is kept as a fourth item below rather than quietly merged.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3):** D1 perception produces what D2 planning/action consumes; D2 produces the change that D3 measures. Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step"* that cannot use it. If enforcement is the broken link, disclosure effort lands on a step that cannot consume it.

**Anchor test applied.** D1 has no nameable anchor (information does not settle anything by itself), so D1 alone is not a complete domain for the question. It is a necessary sub-loop. D2 has an anchor only when the sanctioning body does not update on what the disclosees think. D3 has an anchor only if the corruption measure is not produced by the reform being tested. Where the measure is a perception index influenced by the transparency campaign, the instrument is inside the frame it is meant to grade (Fig 1 :26-27, *"predicates minted, and credibility accrued / They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*).

## 3. Findings F1 to F9

### F1: Transparency is a perception extension, not a settlement (governs D1)

Fig 1 :15: *"where to look: broken vs novel"*. Fig 5 :12: *"new: extend perception, aim the probe here"*. Fig 1 :23: *"The ground enters as the only metric."*

Disclosure changes what can be perceived. It does not, by itself, change the ground. This is `general` (structural) and triangulated across two low-overlap figures (Fig 1 on residuals, Fig 5 on the mint pipeline).

**Refuter:** show a case where a reading changes the outcome with no action path at all, that is, where perception alone settles R. Under the figures that would require the ground to update on a frame, which Fig 2 :33 forbids.

### F2: Under weak enforcement the anchor becomes another frame (decisive, governs D2)

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."* Fig 2 :34: *"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."* Fig 2 :47: *"A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame. Which is panel 1, arrived at by a different route."*

Weak enforcement means the sanctioning body is not a pinned anchor: it can be influenced, captured, or simply unable to act. In the figures' terms it has become a frame. Adding information to a system whose anchor has become a frame produces coherent drift, not correction. This is the structural reason the answer to the question is no under the stated condition, and it is `specified` (it predicts what will happen when the condition holds), triangulated across Fig 2 (anchor) and Fig 10 (channel).

**Refuter:** exhibit an enforcement institution whose decisions are invariant to pressure from the disclosed actors and that still fails to reduce corruption. That would show the anchor is pinned and the failure lies elsewhere, which would move the verdict to D3.

### F3: The chain breaks below the perception link (governs the relation D1 to D2)

Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

Perception (link 1) and vocabulary (link 2) are the links transparency strengthens. Planning and action (link 4) and learn and carry (link 5) are where sanction lives. Fig 3 :22: *"A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can."* Effort spent above a break arrives at a step that cannot use it. So a transparency program in a weak-enforcement setting is effort above the break.

**Refuter:** show that the sanction link is not broken but merely slow or under-measured, so the perceived failure is a measurement lag. That would convert this from a structural verdict to an instrument problem (inward, not outward).

### F4: Bits and actions are two currencies and do not add (governs the whole compound)

Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 12 :126: *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them"* Fig 12 :127: *"and will not notice the shortfall. It proposes arrangements it cannot afford to test, and the budget is what decides how much of the space is reached at all."*

This is the crispest figure-grounded statement of the question. A transparency reform spends bits (publish, disclose, open). Reducing corruption requires paying actions (investigate, prosecute, sanction, recover). A system that accounts only in bits cannot compare the two and will not notice the shortfall. It will keep proposing disclosure arrangements it cannot afford to test. Fig 12 :130: *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it. A price in bits and a price in actions are two readings, held apart."*

**Refuter:** demonstrate commensurability, that is, show a setting where a fixed quantum of disclosure reliably purchases a fixed quantum of sanction without any additional actor or action budget. That would falsify the two-currency claim.

### F5: More search at the same reach adds no atom (governs "more transparency")

Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."* Fig 6 :49: *"more search at the same reach: is it in here and unfound? The closure does not change. Same atoms, same wall, more of the space actually visited."* Fig 6 :14: *"the only operator that moves the wall"* is import.

"Increase transparency" is an instruction to search more inside the same closure. If the sanction atom is absent, no amount of disclosure manufactures it. The honest instruction is Formula step 7: SEARCHED first (is the sanction actor already present but unfound?), then INWARD (sharpen the instrument), then OUTWARD (import the missing actor from a frame whose closure differs). Importing against a budget limit adds an atom that was not needed, so distinguish *"underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are DIFFERENT FACTS"* before acting.

**Refuter:** show that disclosure creates a new primitive in the institutional closure, for example a previously nonexistent capability to sanction that is composed entirely from information. If so, the idempotence claim does not govern this domain and D1 must be re-censused.

### F6: The habitat must be enumerated; the reform is permanent work (governs D3)

Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*

The relevant habitat for corruption is not the disclosing agency. It includes officials, courts, prosecutors, complainants, parties, media, firms, patronage networks, and informal payment channels. The same actor (media, auditor, court) can be a benefactor or a malfactor depending on the residual, so there is no standing list of good actors. Fig 11 :61: *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* One can add an enforcement actor; one cannot subtract the corrupt habitat by decree. Fig 11 :63: *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."*

This also explains why transparency can be absorbed: with no new contact, corrupt actors adapt within the same habitat. Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Moving a residual (a scheme migrates to another agency) reproduces the conditions.

**Refuter:** exhibit a reform that changes contact for every actor in the enumerated habitat without adding any enforcement actor. If corruption falls, capability is not agent-and-habitat in this domain and F6 is wrong.

### F7: Subjective and objective readings are different sources of one behavior (governs measurement in D3)

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 10 :26: *"saturated metrics, pooled readings"*. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (essay, 2026-08-28): *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* And: *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."*

A perception index and an objective corruption record can both read "improvement" while having different sources: the campaign changes what people notice (a projection), or it changes actual malfeasance (the source). The instrument that cannot distinguish them is inadequate for the question. Chen and Ganapati (2023) report exactly this asymmetry: transparency's effect is *"more effective for reducing subjective than objective corruption measures"* (see `10_source_ledger.csv`). Under the figures that is a reason to demand a source-discriminating instrument, not a reason to accept the average effect as a settlement.

**Refuter:** show that, on a single anchored dataset, subjective and objective corruption measures move together under transparency with no divergence. Then the metamerism concern does not bite and the average effect may be reported as a settlement.

### F8: A transparency metric that decides the outcome has stopped being a metric

Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*. Fig 10 :49: *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."* Seat Map :163: *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."*

When compliance with a transparency index becomes the target, the index stops measuring disclosure and starts measuring the institution's ability to produce the appearance of disclosure. Bauhr and Grimes (2017) make the same conceptual point from inside the field: measures that bundle in sanctioning, answerability, or general state capacity *"cast a wide net"* and yield *"empirical findings that are ambiguous in terms of interpretation"*. The correct figure-grounded response is to keep the two readings apart: a bit-count for disclosure and an action-count for enforcement.

**Refuter:** produce a transparency index whose publication does not alter the behavior of the institution it grades, demonstrated by a pre-registered comparison. If no such index exists, the convention has indeed decided the outcome and F8 stands.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written BEFORE the conclusion)

Required by Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The structural verdict in §6 is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Ground-level falsifier.** A controlled or credible quasi-experimental study shows a robust reduction in an *objective* corruption measure from a transparency intervention in a setting with demonstrably weak enforcement and no concurrent strengthening of sanctions. This directly breaks D2/D3.
2. **Anchor falsifier.** A sanctioning body is shown to be invariant to pressure from disclosed actors and yet corruption does not fall, while disclosure alone produces the fall. This breaks the claim that the anchor must be able to act (Fig 2 :33).
3. **Currency falsifier.** Bits and actions are shown to be commensurable: a fixed information input reliably purchases a fixed sanction output with no additional actor or budget. This breaks Fig 12 :125-130.
4. **Measurement falsifier.** On one anchored dataset, transparency moves subjective and objective measures by the same amount and in the same direction. This breaks F7.

If any fires, the finding is copied into `11_audit.md` with provenance and re-evaluated. Until one is run, no part of the verdict may be labelled `grounded`. Note the asymmetry: falsifier 1 is the only one that would settle the *empirical* question; the others would only repair the derivation.

## 5. Strains and re-census

- **Fig 6 (closure idempotent) versus the obvious fact that disclosure reveals new facts.** Re-census: a new *reading* is not a new *atom*. Fig 6 :22 distinguishes them: *"NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world."* Disclosure is more search at the same reach (Fig 6 :49). No strain after re-census.
- **Fig 2 (anchor must not update) versus the empirical fact that enforcement bodies can be captured.** Re-census: a captured enforcer is not an anchor; it is a frame. Fig 2 :47 says exactly this. The figure diagnoses the condition rather than contradicting it. No strain.
- **Fig 11 (introduction available, exclusion not) versus the policy rhetoric of "weeding out corruption."** Re-census: exclusion of an actor is unavailable; removal of an actor is introduction of a counter-actor (prosecution, replacement oversight). The habitat is substituted, not subtracted (Fig 11 :66). No strain.
- **Bits/actions (Fig 12) versus "filters for the budget" (Fig 9 :60).** Re-census: a filter spends budget; it does not become a witness. Disclosure is a filter and is correctly priced in bits. No strain.
- **Residual: none after re-census.** Strains were resolved by describing different quantities, not by softening a reading.

## 6. Recommendation (the verdict, after the refuter above)

1. Do not present transparency as a stand-alone anti-corruption lever in a weak-enforcement setting. Present it as a necessary perception input whose effect is gated on an action path.
2. State the compound explicitly: disclosure plus sanction path plus anchored outcome. Cite Fig 9 :54 and Fig 3 :19.
3. Demand a source-discriminating instrument (objective corruption records) before claiming a reduction. A perception index is a projection, not a source (F7).
4. When the effect fails to appear, run Formula step 7 in order: SEARCHED (is the sanction actor already present?), INWARD (sharpen the corruption measure), OUTWARD (import an enforcement actor from a frame whose closure differs). Do not answer "more transparency" to a question about a missing action atom.
5. Keep the two currencies in separate columns of any evaluation. Summing them is the named error (Fig 12 :130).

### Round trip note

Condensing the figures into "transparency helps only with enforcement" loses three constraints that must be re-attached before the sentence is used: the union structure (Fig 9 :54), the two currencies (Fig 12 :125), and the anchor-must-not-update rule (Fig 2 :33). `11_audit.md` measures this gap qualitatively as R_T.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives and the universal claim does not. Under weak enforcement, transparency extends perception but cannot settle the corruption residual, because the anchor that would act on the information has become a frame, the dependency chain breaks below the perception link, and bits cannot pay the actions bill. The expected effect on objective corruption is approximately zero or small, with any measured effect concentrated in perception measures. All of this is `specified`, ungrounded, and candidate. Strains after re-census: none.
