# The Tether's answer: transparency under weak enforcement

**Mode: `specified`: not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A reform adds information about malfeasance to an institution where the capacity or will to sanction is weak. The question is whether that information changes the corruption outcome. Stated in effect terms, R is the gap between *"information added"* and *"outcome unchanged"*. It is a **union compound** of disclosure, sanction and outcome (Fig 9 :54), not one hard question and not two independent variables.

## 2. The shape

1. **Disclosure extends perception; it does not settle anything.** Fig 1 :15, *"where to look: broken vs novel"*; Fig 5 :12, *"new: extend perception, aim the probe here"*; Fig 1 :23, *"The ground enters as the only metric."* Transparency raises the live mass of the residual, that is, it makes corruption visible. `general`.
2. **Under weak enforcement the anchor has become a frame.** Fig 2 :33, *"The anchor must not update."* Fig 2 :34, *"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."* Fig 2 :47, *"A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame."* Information delivered to a system whose anchor is capturable produces coherent drift, not correction. `specified`, triangulated.
3. **The chain breaks below the perception link.** Fig 3 :19, *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Disclosure strengthens links 1 and 2; sanction lives at links 4 and 5. Effort above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it. `specified`, triangulated.
4. **Bits and actions are different currencies.** Fig 12 :125, *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 12 :126, *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."* A disclosure reform pays in bits and cannot pay the action bill. `general` on the structure, `specified` on the prediction.
5. **Capability lives in the habitat, not the reform.** Fig 11 :40, *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Fig 11 :43, *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* The actors include courts, prosecutors, media, parties, firms and patronage networks. The same actor can help or harm depending on the residual (Fig 11 :49). `general`.
6. **Expectation at the ground.** Because of 2 to 5, the expected effect of transparency alone under weak enforcement is approximately zero on objective corruption, or small; any measured effect is expected to concentrate in subjective (perception) measures. `specified`, ungrounded, world-corroborated from one evidence pool.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that transparency never helps. With a pinned anchor that can act on the record, the same information becomes usable. The verdict is conditional, not universal.
- Not a measured effect size. No ground reading in this package settles `left(R,φ)`.
- Not that enforcement is the only missing thing. The habitat may be missing an instrument, an actor, a relation, or a source-discriminating measure. Fig 13 :134 says something must be imported, not what.
- Not that a perception-index improvement is a corruption reduction. They are different sources of one behavior (F7).
- Not a single-lever policy. The compound has its own loop and its own ground.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. **Objective outcome test:** a pre-registered comparison of objective corruption records (convictions, recoveries, audit findings, bribe incidence) before and after a disclosure intervention in a weak-enforcement setting, with no concurrent enforcement change.
2. **Anchor test:** is the sanctioning body's decision invariant to pressure from disclosed actors? If it moves, it is a frame and not an anchor (Fig 2 :33-34).
3. **Source test:** do subjective and objective measures diverge after the intervention? If they move together on one anchored dataset, the metamerism concern does not bite.
4. **Standing falsifiers from `01` §4:** the three additional refuters, copied there before the conclusion.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The figures narrow the shape of the answer without supplying the empirical magnitude. The framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32, *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*), and it cannot name the missing primitive without already holding it. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* This derivation stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that transparency is useless, and it does not say that transparency alone reduces corruption. It says the question is a compound: disclosure, an actor able to act on the disclosure, and a corruption outcome. Disclosure extends perception, and perception is cheap in bits. But corruption falls only when the residual is settled against an anchor that can act, and an anchor that can be moved by the people being disclosed is not an anchor. Under weak enforcement the chain breaks below the perception link, so more disclosure is effort arriving at a step that cannot use it. The expected effect on objective corruption is therefore approximately zero or small, and any measured effect is expected to sit in perception measures. What the framework offers is the shape any answer must have, the refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The magnitude stays open until the ground settles it.
