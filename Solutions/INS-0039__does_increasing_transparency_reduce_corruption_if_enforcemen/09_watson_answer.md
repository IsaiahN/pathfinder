**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.**

## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what follows when a disclosure operation is added to an institution whose sanction operation is weak. Option A: the stand-alone lever reading, more transparency reduces corruption regardless of enforcement. Option B: the compound reading, disclosure extends perception but reduction requires an anchor that can act, so under weak enforcement the expected effect on objective corruption is approximately zero or small and any measured effect concentrates in perception measures. What each leads to: A recommends a cheap bits-only reform and reads perception improvement as success; B recommends an action path plus a non-updating anchor and demands a source-discriminating instrument before claiming success.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between "information added" and "outcome unchanged". Arity: three operands, disclosure, sanction, outcome. Symmetry: the operations are not interchangeable, so the bond is ordered rather than symmetric. Scale: perception at the institutional scale, settlement at the outcome scale. Fig 9 :54 says unbundle before searching when the split lands on a union; Fig 9 :57 says a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either but a specific compound.
- **Domain 1, disclosure and perception.** Actors: publishing agencies, auditors, journalists, media, citizens, oversight bodies. Currency: information, visibility, inferability. Anchor: none of its own; it borrows the outcome anchor. Question type: a necessary sub-loop, not a complete domain for the question.
- **Domain 2, sanction and enforcement.** Actors: courts, prosecutors, anti-corruption agencies, voters, parties, complainants with a remedy. Currency: actions, prosecutions, recoveries, removal, punishment. Anchor: the sanction outcome, which must not move when frames push on it. Question type: the settlement link.
- **Domain 3, outcome and measurement.** Actors: officials, patronage networks, firms, citizens, measurement instruments. Currency: corruption level R per slot s. Anchor: objective corruption readings, never agreement. Question type: the ground.
- **Order by dependency.** Disclosure produces what action consumes; action produces the change the outcome records. Refuse the stand-alone lever before minting (Formula step 2); describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).

Strain test applied: the apparent conflict between closure idempotence and the fact that disclosure reveals new facts is resolved by re-census, because a new reading is not a new atom (Fig 6 :22). No strain after re-census. See `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §5.

## WORLD (secondary sources read)

World sources corroborate only; they never overrule. All were opened on 2026-11-23.

- **Political science / accountability conditions.** Catharina Lindstedt and Daniel Naurin, "Transparency is not Enough: Making Transparency Effective in Reducing Corruption", *International Political Science Review* 31(3), 2010: "Just making information available will not prevent corruption if such conditions for publicity and accountability as education, media circulation and free and fair elections are weak." Also: "the public must possess some sanctioning mechanism. This is the accountability condition." Predicts no reduction under weak enforcement; corroborates the anchor and dependency verdicts.
- **Public administration / meta-analysis.** Can Chen and Sukumar Ganapati, "Do transparency mechanisms reduce government corruption? A meta-analysis", *International Review of Administrative Sciences* 89(1), 2023: "transparency has an overall significant, though small, effect size in reducing corruption. Transparency is more effective for reducing subjective than objective corruption measures." Predicts apparent success in perception measures; corroborates the measurement verdict.
- **Political science / measurement.** Monika Bauhr and Marcia Grimes, "Transparency to curb corruption? Concepts, measures and empirical merit", *Crime, Law and Social Change* 68, 2017: "at the policy level, transparency measures refer only to access to information, and do not require answerability or sanctioning." Warns that broad measures conflate transparency with state capacity; corroborates the two-currency and measurement verdicts.
- **Development practice / commentary.** International Budget Partnership, "Transparency Is Not Enough", 2010: "Transparency, publicity and accountability are not enough." Adds organisations as mediators; corroborates the habitat-enumeration verdict.
- **Cross-disciplinary synthesis / critical transparency studies.** Wikipedia, "Transparency (behavior)": "It is not clear however if this provides less opportunity for the authorities to abuse the system for their own interests"; records "spectacular transparency" with perverse effects in medical regulation and the claim that transparency "produces the very opacities it claims to obviate". Predicts adaptation within the habitat; corroborates the habitat verdict at low field overlap, but it is an encyclopedia synthesis, not a primary study.

**Overlap declaration.** The four scholarly sources work in one principal-agent / good-governance tradition and cite one another. By Fig 2 :40, agreement from a shared evidence pool is one witness, not four. The Wikipedia source is lower overlap but is secondary. No world source conflicts with the figure verdict, so the conflict rule was not triggered; a conflict would have sent the analysis back to the domain census, never flipped the verdict.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

Under weak enforcement, increasing transparency does not reduce corruption through the transparency mechanism alone. Disclosure extends perception and raises the live residual, but reduction requires the residual to be settled against an anchor that can act. When enforcement is weak that anchor has become a frame, the dependency chain breaks below the perception link, and bits cannot pay the action bill. Expected effect on objective corruption is approximately zero or small, with any measured effect expected in perception measures. This is a `specified`, ungrounded, candidate verdict about the shape. With a pinned anchor and an action path, the same information becomes usable, so no universal claim in either direction is made. The empirical magnitude is unreached, not unreachable, and is owed to a source-discriminating instrument.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The verdict is a recommendation shape with three pre-registered refuters, so an error is findable rather than invisible. If it is wrong, the cost is an under-recommended low-cost reform, that is, a policy opportunity cost. Fig 2 :42 says *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* That cost is avoided by buying one independent ground reading: a controlled comparison with objective corruption measures.
- **undoable:** yes. Nothing here is an irreversible action. The claim can be superseded by a single anchored study, and no primitive or institution has to be withdrawn. A transparency program already launched is not undone by revising the verdict; the revision changes what is claimed for it.
- **reversed by:** a controlled or credible quasi-experimental study showing a robust fall in objective corruption from a transparency intervention in a weak-enforcement setting with no concurrent enforcement change; a demonstration that bits and actions are commensurable; a single anchored dataset on which subjective and objective measures move together; or a re-census showing the compound is a partition rather than a union. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
