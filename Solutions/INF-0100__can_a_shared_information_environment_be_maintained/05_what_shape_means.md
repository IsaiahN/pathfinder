## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical rule: the framework reports "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)". Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 :134-135: "It can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands... It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

From *The Library of Babel*: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer that can be stated before it can be named. It constrains, it does not fill. For this question, the shape is:

> a shared environment whose convention is checkable and does not decide outcomes; frames that range separately against non-updating anchors; independence bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong; a standing refusal operation; and methods, not recordings, crossing between communities.

That is a shape. "Use platform X" or "adopt standard Y" would be a name.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: relevance is residual-relative. "The problem you have determines what makes a book relevant to you or not." The rest of the library "might as well be incoherent noise". Use the vocabulary already held that is nearest to the thing, then recurse on recurring new terms: "With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."
- Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit". Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause".
- *Library of Babel* on the tether: "When traversing through an infinite library, you need something to tether and anchor you to reality." The anchor is the question itself: "If the question is answered by your present collection of books from your search you know whether to stop or continue and which direction to turn next."
- *Library of Babel* on bundles: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?" This is the essay's version of Fig 9 :54-57 and it is the exact situation in INF-0100 (sources, standards and context are three conditions).
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: "You will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place" and the paradox: "you have already composed the answer through the process of locating it."

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape:

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." The canonical README: the framework "stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." An instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer, not an argument, resolved it. "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy." Applied here: a metric such as "trust in information" reads one value for many different underlying arrangements (one source, many sources, shared standards, disputed standards). Naming a cause from that reading is the metamerism error. What is owed is a discriminating instrument, not a name.

## 4. The multi-agent reading of "shared"

*How a Society of Agents Would Work* is the essay closest to this question. It states the figure rule in plain terms:

- "Each agent is a separate point of view, and they all answer to the same judge: the world itself."
- "A group is only worth having while its members stay different." And: "Ten copies watching the same thing are one opinion said ten times." This is Fig 2 :31 and Fig 8 :49 in plain language.
- "The group's agreement is never the verdict." The two collapses are restated: agents converge on "whoever had the most influence", or they "never talk but share one source, so 'the agreement is the pool talking.'"
- "What gets shared, and what never does": "The group shares methods, never recordings." Fig 4 :22. The room shares everything for one problem; the commons holds only methods "distilled from finished rooms".
- "Votes come from outcomes, not popularity." "Independent discoveries count most." "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."
- "Things fade, so they can be tried again": "an old verdict slowly loses weight until it is worth re-testing. The record is never deleted." This is the refusal operator of Fig 6 amendment :42-46 and Operators table :58, expressed as maintenance practice.
- "Bad news travels in the same lane as good news": "knowledge of what fails is shared, voted on and faded just like knowledge of what works."
- "Who looks after the shared store": the maintainer "sets conventions, never verdicts"; "it may author what has no truth value and nothing that does". And: "A carefully chosen room is still a chosen room." That is Fig 10 :73-79 applied to the question's venue problem.

This gives the second half of the shape: a shared information environment is a commons plus rooms, not one room. The commons carries methods with provenance; each room is opened for one question and closed with it. A permanent "room" that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure Fig 10 :71-72 names.

## 5. What "maintained" means

The essays and figures agree that maintenance is work on contact, not work on agreement.

- *Society of Agents*: "A crash is a broken channel, not a broken world. The remedy is to restore contact, not to give up." Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does".
- Fig 11 :63-64: "a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state". So the correct answer to "can it be maintained" is not a one-time yes. It is a specification of the work that keeps it.
- *Society of Agents*: "Deadlines are filters, never verdicts." Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."
- *Society of Agents*: "The two stores: room, commons, newborn, one ground. The ground sits under all three: no store's agreement replaces it."

## 6. Two consequences for this solution

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it." A campaign that gathers prestigious names to agree that a shared environment exists is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask is to give the claim a budget to be tried and let the ground settle it. "You may all agree and be wrong."

**This audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: "the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself." And: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." So every substantive file in this folder is labelled as a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. No file here settles whether any real information environment is maintained.
