# Isomers, union and order: supporting derivation for INF-0100

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading.

## RESIDUAL

The main audit concluded that "shared information environment" names two objects with opposite answers. This file tests the structural claim behind that conclusion: are the convention and the verdict genuinely different substances built from the same atoms (an isomer pair), or merely two descriptions of one thing? And if they are different, what is the dependency order for trying to maintain the environment?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are "shared convention" and "shared verdict" isomers?

- Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
- Fig 12 :46: "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."
- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

Write the two readings over the same atom set:

| reading | composition | bond | what it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| convention | A `+` S `+` B, with S authored by the seat | `≡` for the shared meaning; S is "two names, one referent" for the parties | agreement about a shared meaning is the product (Fig 2 :41) |
| verdict | (A ranges against G) `+` (B ranges against G), G pinned | `⋛` comparison of two independently produced predictions, with G as the non-updating reference | agreement from one evidence pool is worthless (Fig 2 :40) |

Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The formula "A and B share S" looks identical in both cases; only the bond records which object it is. This is the structural reason the question cannot be answered once. Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance."

**Falsifier, written before the run:** show that the two readings are related by a symmetric bond alone, so that swapping convention for verdict changes nothing checkable. Equivalently, show a case where a convention deciding an outcome and a verdict corroborated by independent frames produce the same refuter and the same ground test. Under Fig 2 :39-41 and Fig 10 :46-47 that should be impossible; if it is possible, the isomer claim fails and the two readings collapse into one.

### Q2 - Is the union of the three conditions a partition or a compound?

- S1 = source, S2 = evidence standard, S3 = relevant context. Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- Fig 9 :55-56: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."
- Fig 9 :57: "Which is the second-order value of a big lever, and usually the larger one: not that the pile is smaller, but that the residue is finally describable."

A single intervention (publish a source list, or mandate a standard) clears S1-only or S2-only regions. The S1-and-S2 and S2-and-S3 intersections remain. The residue after the lever is a **specific compound**, not a harder case of S1. This is why the audit refuses to score "the information environment" as one number (Fig 1 :19, :22).

**Falsifier:** a case where a single lever clears all three conditions and the residual stays down on held-out slots. Then the union reading is wrong and the environment can be treated as one condition.

### Q3 - What is the dependency order?

- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
- Fig 10 :16-17: "the convention does not fix the bug / it makes the bug statable".
- Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

Order derived:

1. **Convention (statability).** Without an authored convention for what counts as a disagreement, no residual can be described.
2. **Per-slot residual (perception).** Separate S1, S2 and S3; never aggregate (Fig 1 :28).
3. **Anchor test (vocabulary/objective).** For each slot, name what settles it and check that it does not update (Fig 2 :29-30). If nothing can be named, the slot is not yet a domain.
4. **Refusal (mechanism).** Before minting a remedy, refuse the term that keeps producing a flat residual (Formula step 2; Fig 5 :58-60).
5. **Independent checkers (room selection).** Select the next room against the surfaced residual, and keep it low-overlap (Fig 7 :36, Fig 8 :53).
6. **Composition of the second-order answer (settle then compose).** Only settled first-order molecules become atoms for the cross-community comparison (Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it.").

Effort spent at step 5 before step 1 arrives at a step that cannot use it: selecting expert reviewers before the disagreement is statable just produces a room of people who agree about the wrong thing. This is the Fig 3 break, and it is the usual place chains stop (Fig 3 :24-26).

### Q4 - What do the analogues say, and where do they stop?

Three low-overlap analogues were opened this session:

- **Engineering (Byzantine fault):** broadcast consistency and correctness are different properties. A system can make everyone receive the same value without making the value true. Predicts the convention/verdict split. Stops at the boundary: Byzantine systems have a protocol and a known fault threshold; communities do not have an agreed protocol.
- **Survey statistics (design effect):** effective sample size divides n by the design effect; clustered, correlated observations carry less information than the same count of independent ones. Fig 2 :44 says the same arithmetic "is standard in survey statistics and meta-analysis; the collapse is the same arithmetic read as a warning." Predicts that volume of agreement is the wrong currency. Stops at the boundary: the correlation ρ must be estimated, which itself requires an instrument.
- **Social choice (belief aggregation):** the pooling rule depends on whether experts share information. Geometric aggregation is licensed when beliefs are based on the same information; multiplicative when based on private information. Predicts that "shared environment" requires knowing which information regime is in play before choosing a rule. Stops at the boundary: these are axiomatic results under stated domains, not measurements of a community.

These analogues corroborate; they do not decide. Where one of them seems to conflict with a figure, the census is re-run, not the figure softened.

### Q5 - Bits and actions are two currencies

Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."

For this question: writing down the convention (bits) is cheap; checking that it has not begun deciding outcomes (actions, an instrument) may be expensive. A proposal that only prints a longer standards document has paid in bits and not in actions. Reading the two in one number is the error Fig 12 :130 names: "Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."

## CONCLUSION

The convention and the verdict are a genuine isomer pair: same frames, same shared symbol, different bond, different refuter. The three disputed conditions are a union compound, not a partition, so a single lever leaves intersections standing. The dependency order runs convention, residual, anchor, refusal, checker selection, settled composition. Every step has a falsifier; none has a ground reading in this run. The answer stays `specified`.
