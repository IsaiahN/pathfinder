# Expert verification packet: INF-0100, shared information environments

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in information integrity, social epistemology, distributed systems or science studies can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so the reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` shape, `specified` domain verdicts, not `grounded`.** No measurement of a real information environment is claimed. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Can a shared information environment be maintained when communities disagree about sources, standards of evidence and relevant context?" has two answers, because "shared" names two objects. A shared **convention**, meaning agreed rules about what counts, can be maintained, and agreement inside it is productive. A shared **verdict** about the world cannot be maintained by agreement drawn from one evidence pool, because agreement compounds while evidence does not. The shape of a maintainable environment is: an authored convention that makes the disagreement statable and never decides an outcome; communities ranging separately against anchors that do not update; independence bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong; a standing refusal operation; and methods, not recordings, crossing between communities. Removing the disagreeing communities substitutes a habitat rather than maintaining one. This is permanent work, not a solved state.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 2 and 10

- **Statement:** agreement has opposite valence depending on whether the object is a convention or a verdict. A convention that decides an outcome stops being a convention.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :39 "Correlation has valence." Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Fig 2 :41 "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it". Fig 10 :46 "A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do. The seat may author what has no truth value and nothing that does." Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one".
- **Refuter:** exhibit a shared convention that decides an outcome and remains refusable by the same test, or a shared verdict whose supporting frames demonstrably draw on disjoint evidence and whose agreement survives independent replication.

### C2 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 2 and Figure 8

- **Statement:** independence, not volume of agreement, is the resource that makes a shared environment worth having. Two frames with the same evidence pool are one frame wearing two names.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :31 "Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are." Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Fig 2 :44 "The effective-sample-size correction for correlated observations is standard in survey statistics and meta-analysis; the collapse is the same arithmetic read as a warning." Fig 8 :49 "Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names." Fig 7 :13 "agreement compounds, evidence does not".
- **Refuter:** measure source overlap between two frames and show that their agreement produces a correct novel claim that no independent frame reaches, on a held-out slot. If overlap is high and the result is novel and correct, C2 fails.

### C3 - world-facing; checkable against the field's own literature

- **Statement:** the structure reported by three low-overlap literatures matches the figure split: broadcast consistency is not correctness (distributed systems), correlated observations reduce effective sample size (survey statistics), and the correct opinion-pooling rule depends on whether experts share information (social choice).
- **Sources, read and quoted:** "Byzantine fault" (Wikipedia, accessed 2026-11-23): "Byzantine fault tolerance is only concerned with broadcast consistency... This kind of fault tolerance does not encompass the correctness of the value itself." "Design effect" (Wikipedia, accessed 2026-11-23): "The effective sample size, defined by Kish in 1965, is calculated by dividing the original sample size by the design effect." "Belief aggregation" (Wikipedia, accessed 2026-11-23): "Geometric aggregation is justified when the experts' beliefs are based on the same information, and multiplicative aggregation is justified when the experts' beliefs are based on private information."
- **Prediction:** each source predicts that a shared value and a warranted value come apart, and that the regime of evidence sharing determines the right pooling rule.
- **Status:** corroborates C1 and C2. Overlap is declared: all three are public secondary reference pages sharing a broad evidence pool, so together they are one weak witness, not three. Figure verdicts do not depend on them.
- **Refuter:** a peer-reviewed result showing that pooling rule choice is independent of evidence overlap, or that broadcast agreement entails correctness under a stated fault model. That would conflict with the world sources and send the census back to the figures, not flip the verdict on its own.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that agreement is always a defect. It is the product for a convention (Fig 2 :41).
- Not that any platform, source list or standard is the answer. The figures return a shape, not a name.
- Not that the three disputed conditions are one condition. They are a union and must be unbundled (Fig 9 :54-57).
- Not that excluding disagreeing communities maintains the environment (Fig 11 :61, :66).
- Not that the live strain is resolved. The anchor for "relevant context" is open when the affected community is a party (Fig 2 :36 and :47).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here has been measured against a real environment.

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Overlap intervention:** measure shared-source overlap, then break the pool and retest. Agreement that collapses with the pool confirms Fig 2 :40.
2. **Convention test:** check whether a standard is cited as a reason a claim must be accepted. If yes, Fig 10 :47 has fired.
3. **Slot test:** apply one lever and measure S1, S2 and S3 separately. A lever that clears only one slot confirms the union reading.
4. **Refusal test:** remove the ability to refuse and measure whether errors still fall. If they do, the negation claim fails.
5. **Handoff test:** select each next room against the residual the last one surfaced, with low overlap. If the residual does not trend down, Fig 7's two factors are insufficient.
