**Subject:** One checkable claim about shared information environments, and what would refute it

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` shape, `specified` per-domain verdicts, not `grounded`):** the question "can a shared information environment be maintained when communities disagree about sources, standards of evidence, and relevant context?" names two different objects, and they have opposite answers. If "shared" means a **convention** (shared meaning about what counts), then agreement inside it is the product, not a corruption: *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"* (Fig 2 :41). If "shared" means one **verdict** about the world, then agreement drawn from one evidence pool is worthless as evidence: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A convention that starts deciding an outcome stops being a convention (Fig 10 :47).
>
> The shape of a maintainable environment is therefore: an authored, checkable convention that makes the disagreement statable; each community ranging separately against an anchor that does not update; independence bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong; a standing operation for refusal; and methods rather than recordings crossing between communities. It is not maintainable as one agreed body of fact, because *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"* (Fig 7 :13), and it is not maintainable by removing the communities that disagree, because introduction is available and exclusion is not (Fig 11 :61).
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that this settles the real-world question. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against a set of figures, not a measurement of any platform. I am not claiming that a particular platform, standard or moderation policy is the answer. I am not claiming that all agreement is bad: the figures explicitly permit it for conventions. I am not claiming that the three disputed conditions (sources, evidence standards, relevant context) are one thing; I treat them as a union that must be unbundled before searching (Fig 9 :54).
>
> **What would refute the shape claim** (written down before this message was composed):
> 1. A shared environment in which the anchor is one of the disputing parties and outcomes are still corrected by something outside all frames.
> 2. A shared convention that decides an outcome and remains refusable by the same test.
> 3. Two high-overlap frames (same sources, same dossier) producing a correct novel verdict that no independent frame reached, on a held-out slot.
> 4. An environment that corrects errors monotonically with no refusal operation.
> 5. Evidence that source, standard and context are one condition, so the union reading is wrong.
>
> If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
