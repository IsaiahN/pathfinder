# The Tether's answer: can a shared information environment be maintained?

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the three domain verdicts, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks whether a condition can persist. Scored as one yes/no, it is malformed. The disputed terms (sources, standards of evidence, relevant context) are three slots, not one variable (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22). And "shared" names two different objects, a convention and a verdict, which have opposite answers (Fig 2 :39-41). The residual is the failure of a claim to range against an anchor that does not move, when the parties dispute what the anchor is, what evidence counts and what context is relevant.

## 2. The shape

1. **Two answers, not one.** A shared **convention** (shared meaning about what counts) can be maintained, and agreement inside it is productive: Fig 2 :41 "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it". A shared **verdict** about the world cannot be maintained by agreement drawn from one evidence pool: Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". These are isomers, not descriptions of one thing (`06_isomers_and_order.md` Q1).
2. **The convention must not decide an outcome.** Fig 10 :46 "A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do." Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one". Fig 10 :49 "Declaring a meaning does not ground it." An authored standard is legitimate only while it remains checkable and refusable.
3. **The anchor must not update and must not be a party.** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :33 "The anchor must not update." Fig 2 :45 "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." Fig 2 :47 "A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame." A community's preferred source cannot be the shared anchor for a dispute about sources.
4. **Independence is the budget parameter, not a virtue signal.** Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Fig 8 :49 "Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names." Two communities citing the same dossier are one frame wearing two names; their consensus carries neither verdict weight nor union surplus.
5. **More of the same frames is motion, not progress.** Fig 7 :13 "agreement compounds, evidence does not". Fig 7 :36 "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced." Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
6. **A shared environment must be able to refuse.** Operators table :58 "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate". An environment that only adds sources, context and evidence can grow forever without correcting anything.
7. **Maintenance acts on the channel, not the ground.** Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does". Fig 10 :29 "keep the anchor reachable". Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
8. **You cannot maintain it by removing the disagreeing communities.** Fig 11 :61 "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." A curated clean space proves wiring, not capability (Fig 11 :70).
9. **It is permanent work.** Fig 11 :63-64: "a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state". The honest answer to "can it be maintained" is a specification of the work, not a one-time yes.

## 3. Domain verdicts, kept separate and never averaged

| domain | verdict | status | mode |

**Overall:** yes for the convention under the nine conditions above; no for a single shared verdict; unreached for the real-world question because no measurement was taken. The overall shape is `general`; the domain verdicts are `specified`. Nothing is `grounded`.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that agreement is always bad. It is the product for a convention (Fig 2 :41).
- Not that a single source, standard or platform would settle anything. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that the three disputed conditions are one thing. They are a union compound and must be unbundled (Fig 9 :54-57).
- Not that exclusion of bad actors maintains an environment. Exclusion substitutes a habitat (Fig 11 :66).
- Not that this settles the real-world question. It is a simulated derivation.
- Not that the live strain is resolved. The anchor for "relevant context" remains open when the affected community is a party to the dispute (Fig 2 :36 and :47).

## 5. What would settle it: the ground

A discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a reading "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved the case. Here the corresponding tests are:

1. **Overlap test:** measure source overlap between two communities (shared citations, shared dossier) and test whether their agreement survives an intervention that breaks the pool. If it does not, Fig 2 :40 is confirmed at the ground.
2. **Convention test:** find a convention currently in use and check whether it has begun deciding an outcome. If a party can cite the convention itself as the reason a claim must be accepted, Fig 10 :47 has fired and the convention has stopped being one.
3. **Slot test:** run a lever (a new source list, a new standard) and measure the residual on S1, S2 and S3 separately. If the lever only clears one slot and the intersections stand, the union reading is confirmed.
4. **Refusal test:** remove the ability to refuse from an environment and measure whether errors still fall. If they do, Operators table :58 is refuted.
5. **Handoff test:** replace each room with a low-overlap room selected against the surfaced residual and measure whether the residual trends down. If it does not, the two factors in Fig 7 :37-39 are not sufficient, which is an open possibility (Fig 10 :83).

Standing falsifiers from `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 also apply. Any firing sends the shape back to the census.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The figure text can specify the shape a maintainable environment must have. It cannot name a platform, and it cannot report on any actual environment without contact. One strain remains live: for S3 (relevant context), the affected community may be the legitimate anchor because the question is constitutively about it (Fig 2 :36), but a party to the disagreement cannot be the anchor (Fig 2 :47). That is recorded rather than smoothed (Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"). The lineage of this stop is in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` and the round-trip gap is measured in `11_audit.md`.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The shared information environment question has two answers because "shared" names two things. A shared convention, meaning agreed rules about what counts as a source, evidence and context, can be maintained, and agreement inside it is the product. A shared verdict about the world cannot be maintained by agreement drawn from one evidence pool: agreement compounds while evidence does not. The shape of a maintainable environment is an authored convention that never decides an outcome, communities ranging separately against anchors that do not update, independence bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong, a standing right of refusal, and methods rather than recordings crossing between groups. You cannot keep it by removing the people who disagree; that substitutes a habitat. It is permanent work, not a solved state. What is offered here is that shape, five refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The real-world question stays open until a ground settles it.
