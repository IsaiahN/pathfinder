## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

> Can a shared information environment be maintained when communities disagree about sources, standards of evidence, and what counts as relevant context?

**What is being decided.** Whether a condition called "shared information environment" can persist, and what would have to be true for it to persist. Two naive options:

- **Option A (yes, as one agreed body of fact and method):** leads to a convention that has begun deciding outcomes, and to a chain of agreeing frames in which agreement compounds while evidence does not.
- **Option B (no, because the reference is destroyed):** leads to a bare plurality of closed rooms with no shared reading and no way to state the disagreement.

**What an answer would look like.** Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term before searching (Formula, "Direction"): an answer is a specification of (i) what is shared, (ii) what is not shared, (iii) what each side ranges against, and (iv) what would make the arrangement fail. That shape is the tether. It is written down here before the figure census, so it can accrue a residual like any other term.

**Residual by arity, symmetry and scale.**

- **Arity:** three disputed conditions (source, evidence standard, relevant context) plus a fourth (what the anchor is). This is a union of overlapping conditions, not one hard question. Fig 9 :4-5: "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- **Symmetry:** the frames are interchangeable with each other but not with the ground. Fig 2 :39: "Correlation has valence." The valence differs by object: agreement about meaning is productive, agreement about the world from one evidence pool is not.
- **Scale:** first order inside one community (frames range against that community's ground); second order across communities (a convention lets different brackets travel). The convention is a composition over settled first-order terms, not a new primitive: Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."

**The live slots.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." The three slots are S1 (which sources count), S2 (which evidence standards count), S3 (which context is relevant). A global measure such as "trust in information" collapses them and throws away the only signal that says where to look (Fig 1 :28).

## 1. Domain census, before choosing any figure line

One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor. A domain with no nameable anchor is not yet a domain.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | partition or union |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1, frames inside one community | claimants, sources, instruments | per-slot residual R, prediction versus observation | the ground for that claim, when nameable (measurement, counter, experiment). If no anchor can be named, D1 is not yet a domain for that slot | partition at the anchor test; each slot separately |
| D2, the convention layer | the seat that authors conventions, the communities | shared meaning, statability of a disagreement | procedural, not empirical: the rule that the convention must not decide an outcome and must be checkable (Fig 10 :47-48) | convention is not a claim about the world, so it is neither corroborated nor refuted by D1's ground |
| D3, cross-community verification | outside frames, expert checkers | agreement versus residual | the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all seats draw on one evidence pool, so D3's independence is approximately zero | union with D1 and D2 by dependency, not by identity |

**No anchor, no domain.** For S3 (relevant context) the anchor is not automatically nameable. Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Relevance is partly constitutive of the question, so the affected community can be the legitimate anchor, but a community that is a party to the disagreement cannot also be the anchor: Fig 2 :47 "A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame." S3 is therefore a genuine open item, not a missing datum. It is recorded as such in section 7.

**Partition or union.** Union. The three conditions overlap: a dispute about sources often entails a dispute about standards and context. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55-57). Unbundle before searching.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Make the disagreement statable (D2) before ranging frames against the ground (D1); settle each first-order molecule before composing the cross-community comparison (D3). Fig 10 :16-17: "the convention does not fix the bug / it makes the bug statable." Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." D2 is the middle link with no obvious instrument, which is exactly where Fig 3 :27 says chains usually break: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Refuse before mint (Formula step 2); describe the residual before picking a frame (Fig 8 :53).

## 2. Figure census and findings

Each finding quotes the figure text verbatim before concluding. Quotes carry `Fig N :line` against `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`.

### F1 - Two collapse modes, not one disagreement (triangulated)

Fig 2 :13: "no anchor; each updates on the others". Fig 2 :14-15: "they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence". Fig 2 :18: "one evidence pool". Fig 2 :19-21: "they never talk, and still agree / so the agreement is the pool talking, / not two frames confirming each other".

> Conclusion (`general`, figure-internal): a "shared environment" produced by mutual updating is coherent drift, and one produced by a single common source is the pool talking. Neither is corroboration. Fig 2 :34: "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."

**Refuter:** show two communities that never share an evidence pool and whose agreement survives an intervention that breaks the pool, with the same result on a held-out slot. If agreement vanishes with the pool, F1 stands. Triangulation: Fig 2 (collapse panels) and Fig 7 (mirror chain), low overlap between the mechanics of drift and the mechanics of handoff.

### F2 - Correlation has valence: the hinge of the whole question (triangulated)

Fig 2 :39: "Correlation has valence." Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Fig 2 :41: "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it". Fig 2 :43: "Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."

Fig 10 :46: "A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do. The seat may author what has no truth value and nothing that does." Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one". Fig 10 :49: "Declaring a meaning does not ground it."

> Conclusion (`general`): the question has two answers because it names two objects. A shared **convention** (what counts as a source, what counts as evidence, what counts as context) can be maintained, and high agreement inside it is the product rather than a defect. A shared **verdict** about the world cannot be maintained by agreement drawn from one evidence pool. Averaging the two answers produces a category error.

**Refuter:** exhibit a shared convention that decides an outcome and remains refusable, or a shared verdict whose supporting frames demonstrably draw on disjoint evidence and whose agreement survives an independent replication. Either breaks the valence rule as applied here. Triangulation: Fig 2 and Fig 10, different figures, different vocabulary (evidence pool versus convention/verdict).

### F3 - The anchor must not update and must not be a party (single-source for the exact claim)

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :30: "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update." Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." Fig 2 :46: "The anchor is not a third party to the disagreement and it is not the tie-breaker in a vote. Each frame is corrected by it separately, and what makes the correction sound is that neither frame can move it." Fig 2 :47: "A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame."

> Conclusion (`specified`): if a community treats its own preferred source as the shared anchor, that source becomes a frame and the environment is back in panel 1. Maintenance requires an anchor that each community ranges against separately and none can move. This is the condition that fails first in the question as posed, because "disagree about sources" is precisely a dispute about what the anchor is.

**Refuter:** name an anchor for S1 that is itself a party to the source dispute and still cannot be moved by that party. By Fig 2 :30 that is contradictory, so the refuter is a test of the claim that the environment is currently maintainable without re-anchoring. Single-source status is recorded honestly: the exact non-updating rule is stated in Fig 2, corroborated only weakly by world sources (see W1 below).

### F4 - Independence is bought, not assumed (triangulated)

Fig 2 :31: "Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are." Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Fig 2 :44: "The effective-sample-size correction for correlated observations is standard in survey statistics and meta-analysis; the collapse is the same arithmetic read as a warning." Fig 8 :47: "Independence is one quantity read twice." Fig 8 :48-49: "How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus. Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."

> Conclusion (`general`): the maintenance budget should be spent on independence, not on volume of agreement. Two communities that cite the same dossier are one frame wearing two names; their apparent consensus has zero verdict value and zero union surplus. Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." That surplus exists only where the closures differ.

**Refuter:** an experiment in which two high-overlap frames reach a novel, correct claim that no low-overlap frame reaches, with overlap measured by shared sources rather than self-report. Triangulation: Fig 2 :31-32, :42, :44 and Fig 8 :47-49, independent figure lineages.

### F5 - More agreement is motion, not progress (triangulated)

Fig 7 :12-13: "same closure, handed along; / agreement compounds, evidence does not". Fig 7 :14: "blending: novelty swamped to the mean". Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Fig 7 :36: "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced." Fig 7 :37: "Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:"

> Conclusion (`specified`): a platform, forum or standards body that keeps handing the same disagreement to more people like the last group has not maintained an information environment; it has built one long room. The next room must differ from the last and be selected against the residual the last one surfaced. Fig 7 :38-39 names the two factors: "the bracket's fidelity across the handoff" and "the ground's pressure on the room".

**Refuter:** a handoff chain where each new room is deliberately low-overlap and selected against the surfaced residual, yet no residual decreases. That would show the two factors are not sufficient, which is a live possibility (the selection test "has not been tested forward", Fig 10 :83). Triangulation: Fig 7 and Fig 8 :45-46 (surplus lives at the boundary).

### F6 - A pooled environment without a residual is mute (triangulated)

Fig 8 :19-21: "closures differ, so a surplus exists / but no residual is described / nothing was aimed; the union is mute". Fig 8 :27-28: "1: describe R, then find the frame / that already predicts its shape". Fig 8 :53: "Residual first, frame second." Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or / any frame you pick will seem to fit". Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself".

> Conclusion (`general`): simply putting disagreeing communities into one shared space produces a surplus only if a residual has been described. Without a stated R, any source standard selected afterwards will seem to fit, which is apophenia rather than maintenance.

**Refuter:** a shared environment that produced a specific, checkable correction without any party first writing down what failed to resolve. Triangulation: Fig 8 and Fig 9, different figures and different steps (lookup versus leaving).

### F7 - A shared environment must be able to refuse (single-source)

Operators table :58: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate". Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."

> Conclusion (`general`): a nominally shared environment whose only operation is addition (more sources, more context, more evidence) can accumulate forever without correcting anything. Maintenance needs a ¬ operator: the standing to refuse a claim, a source or a standard, which is not the same as subtracting it. Fig 6 amendment :42-43: "A refutation adds a term that refuses; it removes nothing, and the reach falls because something was added rather than because anything was deleted."

**Refuter:** an environment with no refusal operation whose error rate falls monotonically on held-out slots. Single-source: the operator claim lives in the Operators table and Fig 12 amendment, not independently in the numbered figures. Recorded as `single-source`.

### F8 - The channel decays; the ground does not (triangulated)

Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does". Fig 10 :25-26: "stale percepts, dropped transients, / saturated metrics, pooled readings". Fig 10 :27-28: "the output is not a verdict, it is / you have lost touch". Fig 10 :29: "keep the anchor reachable". Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."

> Conclusion (`specified`): most reported failures of a shared environment are channel failures, not ground failures. The named modes, stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics and pooled readings, map directly onto outdated source lists, lost provenance, engagement metrics used as truth proxies, and aggregation of distinct slots. What crosses between communities should be methods, not recordings of one occasion.

**Refuter:** an environment whose ground changes while its channel is held fixed, and whose correction still succeeds. That would breach the non-decaying-ground premise. Triangulation: Fig 10 and Fig 4, low overlap between seat maintenance and scale crossing.

### F9 - You cannot subtract a disagreeing community (single-source)

Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone". Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Fig 11 :70: "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."

> Conclusion (`general`): the only available maintenance move is introduction (a new method, instrument or actor), not exclusion of the communities that disagree. Removing them substitutes a habitat, and the substitution hides both what failed to reproduce and what was unintentionally introduced. A curated "clean" information space proves the wiring, not the capability.

**Refuter:** demonstrate exclusion of an actor from a habitat with no substitution, and show the residual still discharged. By Fig 11 :66 that is not possible in principle, so the refuter targets whether the environment being discussed is a habitat at all. Single-source (Fig 11), corroborated weakly by the echo-chamber/bubble distinction in W5.

### F10 - Do not score the environment as one number (triangulated)

Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." Fig 1 :28: "The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look." Fig 9 :4: "First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset," Fig 9 :5: "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."

> Conclusion (`general`): "is the information environment shared?" is not a well-formed yes/no measurement until the slots are separated. Source, standard and context each have their own residual and their own anchor test. Fig 9 :57 gives the second-order value of the split: "not that the pile is smaller, but that the residue is finally describable."

**Refuter:** show that S1, S2 and S3 always co-vary perfectly across cases, so the union collapses to one condition. Triangulation: Fig 1 and Fig 9.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A shared environment is demonstrated in which the anchor is one of the disputing parties and outcomes are still corrected by something outside all frames (breaks Fig 2 :47 and :33).
2. A shared convention is shown to decide an outcome and remain refusable by the same test (breaks Fig 10 :47).
3. Two frames with measured high overlap (same sources, same dossier) produce a correct novel verdict that no independent frame reached, on a held-out slot (breaks Fig 2 :40 and Fig 8 :49).
4. A failure is corrected monotonically in an environment with no refusal operator (breaks Operators table :58).
5. The three conditions S1, S2 and S3 are shown to be one condition, so the union reading and the unbundling instruction are wrong (breaks Fig 9 :54-57).

Until one of these is run, every substantive verdict stays `candidate`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Answer the question twice, once for the convention and once for the verdict, and never average the two (F2).
2. Publish the residual per slot before selecting any remedy or venue (F6, F10, Fig 8 :53).
3. Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong; measure overlap by shared sources, not by self-description (F4, Fig 2 :44).
4. Select the next room against the residual the last one surfaced; if the next room resembles the last, the chain has stopped travelling (F5, Fig 7 :36).
5. Keep the refusal operation explicit and keep provenance for every ruling, because a perfectly provenanced wrong ruling is still wrong (F7, Fig 10 :62-66).
6. Introduce methods and instruments; do not propose removing the disagreeing communities as a maintenance strategy (F9, Fig 11 :61-66).
7. Treat channel failures as the default diagnosis and keep the anchor reachable (F8, Fig 10 :24-29).

The strongest single figure-grounded point for outreach is the limit: "no last room" (Fig 7 :6). A shared information environment is permanent work, not a solved state (Fig 11 :63-64).

## 7. Verdict summary

The shape survives; the naive yes and the naive no do not. A shared information environment is maintainable as an authored, checkable convention that makes disagreement statable, with independent low-overlap frames ranging separately against non-updating anchors, with a standing refusal operation and with methods rather than recordings crossing between communities. It is not maintainable as one agreed verdict, and it is not maintainable by adding more of the same frames. Domain verdicts are kept separate in `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `09_watson_answer.md` and are never averaged. One strain (the anchor for relevant context) is live and recorded.
