## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, whether a shared information environment can be maintained when communities disagree about sources, standards of evidence and relevant context. Option A: yes, as one agreed body of fact and method. Option B: no, because disagreement destroys the common reference. Option C (the figure-derived option): yes for the convention, no for a single verdict, and only under stated conditions. What each leads to: A produces a convention that has begun deciding outcomes and a mirror chain where agreement compounds while evidence does not; B produces closed rooms with no shared reading and no way to state the disagreement; C produces a checkable shape, five pre-registered refuters and an explicit live strain.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor. Partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The failure of a claim to range against a non-updating anchor when the parties dispute which sources count, which evidence standards count and which context is relevant. Arity: three slots plus the anchor question, a union of overlapping conditions, not one hard question (Fig 9 :4-5, :54-57). Symmetry: frames are interchangeable with each other but not with the ground; correlation has valence by object (Fig 2 :39). Scale: first order inside a community, second order across communities where a convention lets brackets travel.
- **Domain 1, frames inside one community.** Actors: claimants, sources, instruments. Currency: per-slot residual and prediction versus observation. Anchor: the ground for that claim when nameable; if no anchor can be named, the slot is not yet a domain. Question type: partition at the anchor test, slot by slot.
- **Domain 2, the convention layer.** Actors: the seat that authors conventions and the communities. Currency: shared meaning and statability. Anchor: procedural, not empirical. The convention must not decide an outcome and must be checkable (Fig 10 :47-48). Question type: neither corroborated nor refuted by D1's ground, because a convention makes no claim about the world.
- **Domain 3, cross-community verification.** Actors: outside frames and expert checkers. Currency: agreement versus residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all seats draw on one evidence pool, so independence is approximately zero. Question type: union with D1 and D2 by dependency.
- **No anchor, no domain.** For relevant context, the anchor is not automatically nameable: the affected community may be legitimate because the question is constitutively about it (Fig 2 :36), but a party to the dispute cannot be the anchor (Fig 2 :47). Recorded as a live strain.
- **Order by dependency.** Make the disagreement statable, then separate the slots, then run the anchor test, then refuse before mint, then select checkers, then compose the settled first-order molecules. Convention first because it makes the bug statable (Fig 10 :16-17); select rooms last because effort spent above a break arrives at a step that cannot use it (Fig 3 :20).

Strain test: the apparent conflict between Fig 2 :41 (agreement productive for a convention) and Fig 8 :49 (overlapping frames are one frame) was resolved by re-census. They govern different objects: shared meaning versus claims about the world. Fig 2 :39 is the rule that separates them. One strain remains live (the anchor for relevant context). No reading was softened to remove it.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

Yes for the convention, no for a single verdict, unreached for the real world. A shared information environment can be maintained only as an authored, checkable convention that makes disagreement statable and never decides an outcome, with independent low-overlap frames ranging separately against anchors that do not update, a standing refusal operation, and methods rather than recordings crossing between communities. It cannot be maintained by adding more agreeing frames, and it cannot be maintained by removing the communities that disagree. The three disputed conditions form a union compound and require separate loops. The relevant-context anchor remains a live strain. This is a `general` shape with `specified` domain verdicts, ungrounded, and `candidate` until a ground settles it.

- **cost if wrong:** if the convention/verdict distinction is wrong, a real environment could be judged by the wrong currency. The concrete cost is treating a shared source list or shared metric as evidence of truth, which both disqualifies the verdict and lets the convention silently decide outcomes (Fig 10 :47). A second cost is spending the independence of outside checkers by asking for agreement before a check (Fig 2 :42).
- **undoable:** yes. Conventions can be re-authored, methods can be re-introduced, and provenance can be kept so an error is locatable rather than invisible (Fig 10 :62-66). No atom has to be withdrawn and no community has to be excluded to revise the arrangement.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An overlap-intervention showing agreement that survives the breaking of the shared pool; a convention shown to decide an outcome yet remain refusable; a lever that clears all three slots at once; an environment that keeps correcting without a refusal operator; or a low-overlap handoff chain whose residual does not trend down. Any of these sends the shape back to the census. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
