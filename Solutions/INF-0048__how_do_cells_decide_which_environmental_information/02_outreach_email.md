# Outreach draft: check the separator before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. Not for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** this draft reports a simulated derivation, not a measurement. Do not present it as a result about a named cell. If a reader asks for the ligand list, say plainly that the framework does not return one and that the list is an empirical task.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about how a cell routes signals, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one output I'd like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` structure, `general` shape, not `grounded`):** the three words in the question, ignore, retain, act, are not three bins. They are a union of overlapping conditions with five routes, not three. A signal can be held with nothing owed, novel and parked pending an import, mis-attached and needing a re-fit, broken and needing new machinery, or refuted because a regularity the cell held is false. The last two look like "act" and "ignore" from outside but have opposite signs. One lever, for example raising a receptor's affinity, clears the cases where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.
>
> The residual has to stay indexed per signal channel. Fig 1 :19, verbatim: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A cell can be globally quiet with one live channel, and that is a legal state, not an inert one. The biological counterpart is receptor cross-talk: when one ligand saturates a strongly coupled receptor cluster, the cluster loses the ability to respond to a second ligand. Keeping methylation ligand-specific restores the per-channel reading.
>
> **What the shape does not claim:** it does not name which ligands a particular cell ignores. It returns a separator that assigns a route per channel and states what would have to be true for that route to change. The ligand list is a `gap` that experiment, not the framework, has to fill. I also am not claiming that "the cell decided" is a literal verdict. At the lineage scale selection settled the repertoire; the word decision is a label applied afterwards. At the cellular moment there is a controller doing work. Those are two scales with different anchors, not one claim.
>
> **What would refute it, written before this message:**
> 1. Response to one ligand cannot be measured independently of others in the same cluster under any perturbation, so the residual is genuinely pooled.
> 2. A single criterion sorts every signal into exactly one of the three bins with no overlap, so the union reading is wrong.
> 3. A new ligand specificity arises without modification of a sensor that already returned a reading for some ligand.
> 4. Two different routings cannot be discriminated by any growth or fitness measurement, so the anchor does not settle between them.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you'd rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not asking:** I am not asking you to trust the framework, to endorse it, or to say it is true. Those would make your agreement worthless as a check. I am asking what you would count as a failure.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Local figure text extract, with `Fig N :line` citations: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in this project folder.
> - Lan and Tu 2016, "Information Processing in Bacteria: Memory, Computation, and Statistical Physics", Reports on Progress in Physics 79(5):052601: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4955840
> - Perkins and Swain 2009, "Strategies for cellular decision-making", Molecular Systems Biology 5, MSB200983: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/msb.2009.83
> - Gavira et al. 2020, "How Bacterial Chemoreceptors Evolve Novel Ligand Specificities", mBio 11(1):e03066-19: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6974571
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** write down what an answer would look like before you run it, state what would refute it, and label the mode of what you get. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape it seeks is a preference rather than a term it holds. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thank you for your time,
> [name]
