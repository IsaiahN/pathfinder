# The Tether's answer on modularity and cascades

**Mode: `specified` on the conditions, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A failure fires on one slot. It either stays there or appears on many. The question asks for the conditions under which the first happens. The framework's answer is not a number and not a design rule of thumb. It is a shape with a dependency order, and a refusal of the single-lever reading. The question as asked is a **union compound**, not one hard question (Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"). It has its own loop and its own ground.

## 2. The answer, per domain kept separate

### Domain 1, framework-internal structure

1. **A cascade is a contact path, not a collection of independent failures.** Fig 11 :52-53: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." Fig 13 :32: "a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos." The residual stays per slot (Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."). A global robustness score can be near zero while one module is live (Fig 1 :13).
2. **The module boundary is a filter with a direction, not a wall.** Fig 4 :21: "The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction." So containment is always relative to a failure class. Modularity does not prevent cascades in general; it prevents the classes it does not pass.
3. **Independence is measured, not drawn.** Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." Fig 8 :49: "Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names." A partition on a diagram creates no barrier if the two sides range against one source.
4. **A hidden common cause defeats the barrier.** Fig 2, collapse 2 "(hidden shared source)" and :20-21: "they never talk, and still agree" and "so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other." Correlated failure crosses a boundary without travelling through it.
5. **Closure makes containment the default absent contact.** Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom." Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." A cascade is evidence that a channel existed, whether drawn or not.
6. **Isolation substitutes a habitat; it does not delete one.** Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." An isolator inherits actors it did not choose (Fig 11 :67-69). So an instruction to raise modularity is really an instruction to replace one coupling pattern with another, and the unchosen part matters.

**Domain 1 verdict:** `specified`, `triangulated` for the contact-path and independence findings (two figures each, low overlap), `single-source` for the membrane and common-cause findings (one figure each). Not grounded.

### Domain 2, the network-resilience frame (secondary corroboration only)

- Newman (2006), PNAS: modular structure is "densely connected groups of vertices, with only sparser connections between groups" and gives groups "some degree of independence". Predicts that modularity is an independence claim, not a count. Corroborates F3.
- Thébault and Fontaine (2010), Science: "the stability of trophic networks is enhanced in compartmented and weakly connected architectures", while "a highly connected and nested architecture promotes community stability in mutualistic networks." Predicts that connection helps or hurts by interaction type. Corroborates the union reading F6.
- Mou and Hayashi (2025), PLoS One: "the robustness is weakened by strong local communities in spatial networks" and long-distance links mitigate it. Predicts that the bridge, not the module, is the decisive structure for targeted attacks. Corroborates the filter reading F2.
- Buldyrev et al. (2010), Nature: "failure of nodes in one network may lead to failure of dependent nodes in other networks. This may happen recursively and can lead to a cascade of failures." Predicts coupling as the carrier of cascade. Corroborates F1 and F7.
- Albert, Jeong and Barabási (2000), Nature: "although key components regularly malfunction, local failures rarely lead to the loss of the global information-carrying ability of the network", while "error tolerance comes at a high price in that these networks are extremely vulnerable to attacks". Predicts a tradeoff between failure classes. Corroborates F2.
- Zelbi, Ialongo and Thurner (2026), Scientific Reports: rewiring reduced systemic risk "by 16-50% without reducing the production output of firms", yet "We find no particular topological measure that explains systemic risk mitigation." Predicts that the lever works but the single global metric cannot see why. Corroborates F5 and F8.

**Domain 2 verdict:** corroborating, mixed by design, not a verdict source. The disagreement between Thébault/Fontaine and Mou/Hayashi is a union signature, not a failure of the figure verdict. Overlaps are recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv`; the four framework essays share an author and an evidence pool with the figures and carry little independent weight.

## 3. The conditions stated positively

Containment obtains when all of the following hold together:

1. the residual is measured per slot, so the live module is visible before the system-wide score moves (Fig 1 :19, :22);
2. the contact chain from the failing slot is enumerated outward until the cascade stops mattering, including links not drawn on the diagram (Fig 11 :44, :52-53);
3. the propagation class is named (overload, exposure, threshold, or common cause), because a boundary cannot be said to filter "failure" in general (Fig 4 :21);
4. the modules are genuinely independent, meaning low overlap of the channel that carries that class (Fig 2 :31; Fig 8 :29, :48-49);
5. the boundary's filter passes the classes needed for local repair and blocks the class that carries the cascade (Fig 4 :22-24);
6. there is no hidden shared source that makes the modules fail together without communicating (Fig 2, collapse 2).

If any one is missing, the compound is not cleared. This is the union, not a checklist whose items can be traded off against one another.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that modularity always prevents cascades. Fig 4 :21 makes the boundary a filter.
- Not that more modularity is better. Mou and Hayashi (2025) report the opposite for spatial connectivity robustness, and the isomer reading in `06_isomers_and_order.md` says why that is not a contradiction.
- Not a numeric Q threshold, an optimal coupling strength or a named partition algorithm. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."
- Not that topology alone predicts containment. Fig 2's common-cause collapse and the supply-chain "no particular topological measure" result rule that out.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement of a named network with a per-slot residual and a recorded channel was taken here.

## 5. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"; the colour case "was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." Here the corresponding instruments are:

1. a per-slot residual trace through a cascade, showing which slot goes live and when;
2. a channel discriminator that distinguishes overload redistribution from exposure/threshold propagation from a common source;
3. a boundary test that records which failure classes a given partition passes and blocks;
4. an overlap measure between modules on the specific channel, not on the generic graph.

Falsifiers, written in advance in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4: a cascade with no contact chain and no shared source; a boundary that passes the wrong class; overlapping modules that fail together from an internal cause; a single global Q that predicts containment across interaction types and failure modes.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a modularity threshold. It says the question is a union of two conditions, structure and failure dynamics, that cannot be averaged. A module boundary is a filter with a direction, not a wall, so containment is always relative to the class of failure the boundary passes or blocks. The residual has to be read per slot, because a global score hides the one live module. Independence has to be measured on the channel that carries the failure, not drawn as a box, and a hidden common source makes two modules fail together without any link between them. That is why weakly coupled compartments can stabilise one kind of network while strong local communities weaken another. What the framework offers is the shape any answer must have, four refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The number stays open until the ground settles it.
