**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about modularity and cascades, no theory attached

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about cascades in networks. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, the conversation ends there.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "under what conditions does modularity prevent local failures from becoming systemic cascades?" is a *union of two conditions*, not one question with a stronger or weaker lever. The two conditions are (1) structure, meaning genuine independence between modules, and (2) dynamics, meaning the channel the failure actually travels on (overload redistribution, exposure, threshold, or a hidden common cause). A single lever, for example raising modularity Q, clears only the cases where structure alone binds. It leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds. That is why compartmented, weakly connected architectures are reported to stabilise trophic networks while strong local communities are reported to weaken connectivity robustness in spatial networks. Those are not contradictory results; they are different cells of the union.
>
> **The shape the framework returns:** a boundary between modules is a filter with a direction, not a wall. Containment holds where the specific channel the failure needs is absent or filtered at that boundary at the moment the failure fires. It does not hold merely because a partition was drawn, and it does not hold when two modules range against one hidden shared source. The framework gives the shape of the conditions, not a numeric threshold of Q; a number would have to come from a measurement on a named network.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that modularity always prevents cascades; that a higher Q is always better; that the topology alone predicts containment; or that any of this is `grounded`. The outside sources I read are secondary checks, not witnesses for the verdict.
>
> **What would refute it, written down before this message:**
> 1. a systemic cascade with no enumerable contact chain from the failing slot and no hidden shared source;
> 2. a boundary that passes the failure class it should filter, or blocks every class including the head start needed for repair;
> 3. two modules with measured near-zero channel overlap that still fail together from an internal cause in one of them;
> 4. a single global modularity value that predicts containment across interaction types, failure modes and substrates, with no per-slot data and no dynamics term.
>
> If any of those holds, I withdraw the shape. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
