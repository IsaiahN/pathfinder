## What in the wider literature bears on the question

These were read through the pages above (their titles, years and quoted abstracts appear on the fetched pages). They are named here as the field's own ground, not as independently opened full texts.

1. **Holling (1973), "Resilience and stability of ecological systems", Annual Review of Ecology and Systematics 4: 1 to 23.** The origin of the two meanings of resilience. The engineering meaning (time to return to equilibrium) and the ecological meaning (capacity to absorb and reorganize) are different quantities; answering this question with the wrong one is a category error.
2. **May (1977), "Thresholds and breakpoints in ecosystems with a multiplicity of states", Nature 269: 471 to 477.** The threshold and breakpoint framing that the bifurcation source corroborates from mathematics.
3. **Scheffer and Carpenter (2003), "Catastrophic regime shifts in ecosystems: linking theory to observation", Trends in Ecology and Evolution 18: 648 to 656.** The theory-to-observation link, relevant to whether an early-warning indicator is an instrument or the ground.
4. **Folke, Carpenter, Walker, Scheffer, Elmqvist, Gunderson and Holling (2004), "Regime shifts, resilience, and biodiversity in ecosystem management", Annual Review of Ecology, Evolution, and Systematics 35: 557 to 581.** Three anthropogenic ways to reduce resilience: losing diversity and functional groups, altering the physico-chemical environment, and modifying disturbance regimes. Each maps onto a different condition in the union.
5. **Mumby, Hastings and Edwards (2007), "Thresholds and the resilience of Caribbean coral reefs", Nature 450: 98 to 101.** Hysteresis case: urchins rebounded but coral cover did not return.
6. **Isbell, Tilman, Polasky, Binder and Hawthorne (2013), "Low biodiversity state persists two decades after cessation of nutrient enrichment", Ecology Letters 16: 454 to 460.** Persistence of a shifted state long after the driver is removed.
7. **Carpenter, Ludwig and Brock (1999), "Management of eutrophication for lakes subject to potentially irreversible change", Ecological Applications 9: 751 to 771.** Management under irreversibility, the applied version of F7.

Corroboration status: the ecology sources (1 to 7) share a literature pool with each other, so their agreement is one witness, not seven. The mathematics, human-cognition and business pages are lower-overlap analogues. They corroborate the shape's structure (threshold, contact, process not trait) but cannot settle an ecological trajectory. No fetched source conflicts with the figure verdict; if one did, the rule is to return to the census, not to flip the verdict.
