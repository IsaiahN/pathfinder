## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the two states isomers?

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

A regime shift in which the same species and the same conditions persist but the interaction network changes (a predator released, a grazer suppressed, a foundation species replaced in role) is an isomerization: same atoms, different bond, different substance. The clear-water and turbid states of a shallow lake are the textbook candidate; the species list can overlap heavily while the dominant feedback loop differs.

Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." The weaker claim is that both states share a functional group (a state, a mediating structure, a state) with different qualifiers. This holds even when the atom pools differ.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a shifted state requires an atom that was not in the old pool (an invaded species, a novel condition, an introduced actor), the isomer claim fails for that case. It becomes an arrival under Fig 11 :65 "Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994", not an isomerization. Keep both readings visible; the falsifier decides between them.

### Q2 - Which bond holds between the two states?

- **⋛ comparison** (Fig 12 :87 "a threshold relation between two quantities") is the threshold at which one arrangement loses and the other settles. It is ordered, which is what makes hysteresis the expected shape.
- **→ sequence** (Fig 12 :70 "A then B; B needs A to have happened") describes the forward route only. Writing the return as the same → is the error. Fig 12 :73 tests it: "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes." For a hysteretic system, yes.
- **− subtraction** (Fig 12 :82 "A with B removed or absent") is the false restoration model: remove the disturbance and expect the old state. Fig 11 :61 "Introduction is available; exclusion is not" rules this out as a general management move.
- **¬ negation** (unary, not a bond) is the refusal of the old arrangement once it no longer pays. A vocabulary with only bonds could report the new state and not the refusal of the old one.

### Q3 - Order and closure

Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." A degraded state held by a positive feedback loop is a closed cycle; a chain of external forcing cannot close by construction. This is the structural difference between "the system flipped because an internal loop closed" and "the forcing was large."

Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it." Once the new arrangement settles, it becomes an operand for the next disturbance. Successive disturbances are therefore not repetitions of the same experiment: each turn begins from the residue the last one left (Fig 13 :88-89). This is why repeated disturbances differ from one disturbance of the same total intensity (adjacent question 37).

### Q4 - What is the analogue, and where does it stop?

Low-overlap analogue: bifurcation theory. "a bifurcation occurs when a small smooth change made to the parameter values ... causes a sudden 'qualitative' or topological change in its behavior" (Bifurcation theory, Wikipedia, fetched 2026-11-23). This corroborates the threshold and the sudden switch from mathematics. It does **not** name the ecological atom or supply the habitat enumeration, so it is a shadow, not a substitute. Supply-chain resilience (business, fetched same date) corroborates "persist, adapt, or transform" as a three-way outcome, but its system is authored by managers in a way an ecosystem is not; Fig 11 :61-63 makes that difference load-bearing.

## CONCLUSION

The old and new states are isomers when the shift rearranges a shared atom pool, and members of a shared functional group when it does not. The forward and return paths are different ordered bonds, not one symmetric transition. A positive feedback cycle closes the new state; a settled arrangement becomes an atom for the next turn. The stated falsifier (a shifted state requiring a new atom) decides the isomer question case by case and hands it to the arrival/engineering reading if it fires.
