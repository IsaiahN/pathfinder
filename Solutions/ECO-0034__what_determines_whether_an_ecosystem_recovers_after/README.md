## The question restated as an effect

A system meets a disturbance. Two outcomes are observed: (A) the pre-disturbance arrangement re-forms (recovery, resilience), or (B) a different arrangement forms and persists (regime shift, alternative state). The residual R is: given the same disturbance, why do some systems return and others flip? A and B are not two answers to one question. They are the two branches of a threshold comparison, and each branch has its own ground.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run: a shape stating (i) the determinants are properties of the agent-and-habitat contact, not the species set alone; (ii) the habitat is enumerated, not composed, and contact cascades reach beyond direct effects; (iii) recovery versus shift is a threshold comparison over two settled arrangements, ordered (hysteresis), not a symmetric pair; (iv) the question is a union compound of overlapping conditions, so it must be unbundled before it is searched; (v) the framework returns a shape, not the name of a particular species or a numeric threshold. That shape is the prediction committed in advance, so it can be refused by the same test as everything else.
