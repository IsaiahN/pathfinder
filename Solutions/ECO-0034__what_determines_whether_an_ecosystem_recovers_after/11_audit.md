## 4. Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, and Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 11 :39-44 and :49-53, Fig 12 :87/:104/:121, Fig 9 :54/:57/:32.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the five-point shape carried in `07` and `09`: contact not species set; enumerate outward; no standing valence; ordered threshold comparison; union of overlapping conditions; shape not name.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a specific answer for a specific lake, for example "phosphorus loading above X flipped this lake to a turbid state."

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **depth of the cascade**: the shorthand says "outward" but a concretisation must name which step carries the determinant, and first-order enumeration is the named failure;
- the **per-condition and intersection structure**: the shorthand says "union" but the concretisation must report which condition holds alone and which intersection remains after the lever;
- the **ordered type of the comparison**: the shorthand says "expected to differ" but the concretisation must measure both crossings with the same state variables, or the claim is untested;
- the **relativity of valence**: the shorthand says "relative to the residual" but the concretisation must name the residual, or a species becomes a standing good actor by accident.

One thing survives cleanly: a structure of contact must be enumerated before any determinant is named, and the two outcomes are not a symmetric pair. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the five-point shape as identical to the figures would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
