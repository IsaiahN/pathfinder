## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what determines whether an ecosystem recovers after a disturbance or shifts into a new state. Option A: answer with a list of intrinsic species-set traits that confer recovery. Option B: answer with a shape over contact, enumeration, threshold and order, with the name left to that system's ground. What each leads to: A produces a standing catalog of good actors, which the figures forbid, and a universal score that hides the live slot; B produces checkable sub-claims, per-condition verdicts and an open slot until a trajectory settles it. A and B are not two answers to one question, because the determinant is a union of overlapping conditions rather than a single quantity (Fig 9 :54).

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Arity: a comparison of at least two quantities across one disturbance (Fig 12 :87 "a threshold relation between two quantities"). Symmetry: not symmetric; forward and return crossings can differ, so the bond is ordered. Scale: first order at species and interaction level, second order at habitat and regime level, with cascades several steps out (Fig 11 :52-53). **Union compound**, not a partition: disturbance magnitude, feedback sign, connectivity, cascades and the observer window overlap.
- **Domain 1, the ecological habitat.** Actors: species, conditions (light, water, temperature, season, terrain), relations (pollination, predation, competition, dependency). Currency: contact and cascade. Anchor: which configuration actually persists after the perturbation, measured as a trajectory. A system with no nameable anchor is not a domain yet.
- **Domain 2, threshold and state.** Actors: the six substrate terms and the bonds, especially ⋛ comparison and → sequence. Currency: composition inside `closure(Γ)` then settlement. Anchor: the ground settles which arrangement formed. Distinct from D1 because D1 enumerates actors and D2 compares arrangements.
- **Domain 3, observation.** Actors: observer and instruments (early-warning indicators, monitoring). Currency: residual R and anchor. Anchor: the trajectory, not agreement among models. Adjacent questions 35 and 36 live here.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Enumerate the habitat first, then acquire terms for what actors afford, then state what counts as recovery, then choose a lever, then carry what worked. The middle link has no instrument (Fig 3 :27), so effort above the break cannot rescue it (Fig 3 :19).

Strain test applied: union (Fig 9) and dependency order (Fig 3) describe different quantities, so no strain. Fig 11's management rule and Fig 12's settled-molecule rule describe different objects, so no strain. After re-census: none hidden.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; no particular determinant is named. Recovery versus a new state is decided in the agent-and-habitat contact, enumerated outward until the cascade stops mattering; actor valence is relative to the residual; the two outcomes are an ordered threshold comparison between settled arrangements, with the return path expected to differ; and the headline question is a union of overlapping conditions that must be unbundled. A name, such as a nutrient threshold or a decisive species, is licensed only by a specific system's measured trajectory or by a provenanced import with a shadow test. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate. This is a `specified` shape, not an ecological result.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable if the packet is sent as written, because it states modes and refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable. The real cost is a wrong restoration decision made from a first-order habitat scope or from a symmetric single-threshold model. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is reduced by measuring the trajectory and by keeping the shape claim in front of the links.
- **undoable:** yes. The five-point shape can be revised without withdrawing the derivation, and a particular system's name can be added when its ground settles it. `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove every trait-catalog and universal-threshold claim. Nothing in the habitat has to be composed or subtracted.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A measured trajectory showing return at the same forcing as the forward crossing would fire the ordered-comparison refuter; a case where the determinant is demonstrably first order with all cascades inert would fire F2; a clean partition of conditions would fire F5; a source-discriminating instrument identical to the trajectory decision would fire F6; an actor class beneficial for every residual would fire F3. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
