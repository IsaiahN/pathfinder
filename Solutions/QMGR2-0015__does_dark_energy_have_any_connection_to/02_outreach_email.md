# Outreach draft: check the output first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the recipient can see the residual and the refuter without any framework vocabulary. If the stage 1 output does not survive their check, there is nothing else to show them.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about dark energy and vacuum energy, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question as an effect:** quantum field theory returns a family of estimates for the energy density of the vacuum, from about (200 GeV)^4 at the electroweak scale up to about (10^18 GeV)^4 with a Planck cutoff. Cosmology observes a smooth component of order (10^-3 eV)^4 that drives accelerated expansion. The standard move declares them the same quantity and tunes a counterterm to make the numbers agree. My output is about the *relation* between them, not about what dark energy is.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the relation is an ordered comparison, not an identity. I used a figure that defines identity as "two names, one referent" with the test "substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No." Substituting the field theory estimate for the observed component loses everything: the predicted expansion would tear galaxies apart before they form. So "dark energy equals vacuum energy" is not an identity under that test. The relation that does fit is a threshold comparison, which asks which is larger. The question also bundles at least four sub-questions (does a vacuum contribution gravitate, why is the observed value small, is the acceleration that contribution, and is the vacuum number a prediction or a renormalization convention), and these have different anchors, so they should be split before anyone searches them.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I know what dark energy is; that a named mechanism such as supersymmetry, sequestering or unimodular gravity is the answer; that the framework settles the 120-order discrepancy; or that a consensus of agreeable readers would count as evidence. The framework returns a shape, not a name. The shape of what is owed is a term that suppresses every vacuum contribution to the observed scale without a hand-set counterterm, and that is settled by observation. I cannot name that term from what I hold.
>
> **What would refute the output, written before this message:**
> 1. an instrument that returns a regulator-independent vacuum energy density equal to the observed dark energy density;
> 2. a demonstration that substituting the field theory estimate reproduces the observed expansion history;
> 3. a supplied import with a checkable provenance and a shadow test that closes the discrepancy against the ground;
> 4. a demonstration that the four sub-questions share one anchor, so the bundle was one question after all.
>
> If any holds, I retract the output. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would like from you at stage 1:** just your verdict on that output and, if it fails, which of the four refuters fired. Please send that before reading anything I have written.
>
> **If, and only if, it survives:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The loop and mode rule: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - Public figures repository, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Distribution rule

One to one only. Do not send to a list selected for likely agreement: Fig 2 :40 says agreement within a shared evidence pool is disqualifying for a verdict. Do not pool later agreement as corroboration. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
