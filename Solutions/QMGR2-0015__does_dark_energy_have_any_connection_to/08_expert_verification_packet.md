# Expert verification packet: the Tether on dark energy and vacuum energy

**What this is:** a claim in a form a physicist or philosopher of physics can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the shape. Not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed here. This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The connection between dark energy and quantum vacuum energy is an ordered comparison, not an identity. The framework's identity bond is defined as "two names, one referent" and its test is "substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No." Substituting the field-theory estimate for the observed component loses the observed universe, so identity fails as stated. The question is also a union of four sub-questions with different anchors, so it must be split before it is searched; the counterterm tuned to the observed value is a convention that has become an outcome; and each known vacuum contribution fails separately. No mechanism is named. What is offered is the shape any candidate answer must have, refuters written in advance, and the observations that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 12

- **Statement:** the correct bond is comparison ⋛, not identity ≡. Identity fails its own substitution test; comparison is an ordered threshold relation.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :67 "two names, one referent"; Fig 12 :69 "substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No."; Fig 12 :87 "a threshold relation between two quantities"; Fig 12 :89 "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes." Also Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance."
- **What to check:** is the substitution test applied correctly? The QFT estimates (electroweak ~ (200 GeV)^4, Planck cutoff ~ (10^18 GeV)^4) would produce expansion that forbids galaxy formation (Weinberg 1989/2000; NASA 2025). The observed component is of order (10^-3 eV)^4.
- **Refuter:** an instrument or regulator-free calculation returning a value of ⟨rho⟩ equal to the observed density, so that the identity test passes; or a demonstration that substituting the estimate reproduces the observed expansion history.

### C2 — framework-internal plus physics-facing; checkable by reading Figure 9 against the literature

- **Statement:** the question is a union compound that must be split. Its four sub-questions have different anchors: a regulator-dependent family of estimates in QFT, a ground reading from cosmology, a source question, and a convention question. They overlap only in the word "vacuum energy".
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :4-5 "First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."; Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."; Fig 8 :50 "But shared atoms are not the signature. Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."
- **What to check:** is the anchor asymmetry real? Koberinski (2021) shows the QFT value depends on regulator and scheme, with no instrument returning ⟨rho⟩. Carroll (2001) anchors the cosmological side in observation and lists the contributions separately. Weinberg (1989/2000) notes the gap has not shrunk after decades of work.
- **Refuter:** a demonstration that all four sub-questions share one anchor and one closure, so the bundle was one question after all; or a regulator-independent measurement of ⟨rho⟩.

### C3 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 10

- **Statement:** tuning the counterterm to the observed value is a declared meaning, not a settlement. It makes the bug statable but does not fix it, and by deciding the outcome it has stopped being a convention.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :18 "a declared meaning is not a grounded one"; Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"; Fig 10 :49 "Declaring a meaning does not ground it."
- **What to check:** Carroll 2001 writes the effective cosmological constant as a bare term plus potential and zero-point contributions, and notes that a cancellation is imaginable "other than the fact that it seems ridiculous." Koberinski 2021 states that renormalized quantities "aren't taken as predictions of some physical quantity" and are "tuned to give the measured value." Is that a convention by the figure's definition?
- **Refuter:** a parameter-free derivation of the observed value from the Standard Model, or a settlement by the ground that removes the need for the counterterm.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that dark energy is definitely not vacuum energy. The identity fails **as stated** under the figure's test; whether a common substrate exists is for the ground.
- Not that supersymmetry, sequestering, unimodular gravity, quintessence or anthropic selection is the answer. Each is a candidate import without a source gate or shadow test here.
- Not a new physical theory, not a derivation of general relativity from quantum field theory, and no new primitive.
- Not a claim about which of the four split questions is true. They are split, not answered.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken in this derivation.
- Not a verdict about the number 120. That number is reported from Weinberg, Carroll and NASA; it is not produced by the figures.
