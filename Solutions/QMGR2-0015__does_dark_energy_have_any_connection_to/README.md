## The question restated as an effect

Two frames each speak of an energy density of empty space. In quantum field theory the vacuum expectation value of the stress-energy tensor is estimated from known field contributions and from a cutoff, giving values as large as roughly 10^74 GeV^4 (about 120 orders of magnitude above the observed value). In cosmology, dark energy is an observed component with density of order 10^-47 GeV^4 that drives accelerated expansion. The question asks whether these are the same quantity. Three options, and what each leads to:

- **Option A, identity:** they are one quantity under two names. This leads to the cosmological constant problem as a failed calculation: the predicted number is wrong by ~120 orders and must be tuned to observation.
- **Option B, comparison:** they are two quantities that can be ranged against the same ground and compared, but not substituted for one another. This leads to a union compound that must be split into separate loops before it is searched.
- **Option C, compound:** the question bundles an identity claim, a magnitude claim, a source claim and a convention claim. This leads to an unbundling before any mechanism is minted.

The derivation below concludes B and C, refuses A as stated, and records `gap` at the level of a mechanism. It does not claim to say what dark energy is.
