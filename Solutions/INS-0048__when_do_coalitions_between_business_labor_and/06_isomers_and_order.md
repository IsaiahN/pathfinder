## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Union or partition?

- Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
- Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- C1 (open closure) and C2 (population-wide channel) can hold independently. A coalition can welcome new members while measuring only its own; it can measure everyone while keeping its membership sealed. A single lever clears the intersection only, which is why "add more partners" fails when the real break is the metric, and "improve the metric" fails when the real break is the wall.
- ⇒ **Union.** Two loops, two grounds, two instruments. This is the re-census that refuses the naive "inclusiveness scale" answer.

### Q2 - Are two coalitions with the same cast the same substance?

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."*
- Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*
- Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

Two national bargains can have the same atoms (employer association, union, ministry, wage pact, training levy) and differ in the bond. If one uses a sequence (→: the pact precedes the levy, and the levy cannot exist without the pact) and the other uses a threshold comparison (⋛: access is granted above a contribution level), they are Fig 12 **isomers**: same ingredients, different arrangement, different substance. The published ingredient lists look the same, and nothing in the list records the difference.

- A third case is a **functional group** rather than an isomer: two coalitions that share the substructure "state → structure → population-wide outcome" but fill the slots with different actors. The group fixes the kind; the actors say which member.

- **Falsifier, written before the comparison:** if two coalitions are shown to have identical atoms and identical bonds yet different population-wide outcomes with no difference in habitat, contact or instrument, the isomer/functional-group framing fails and the difference must come from a new atom, which F8 says is a gap the figures do not name.

### Q3 - What is the dependency order?

- Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*
- Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
- Order for this question: (1) perceive who is in and out, per slot; (2) build a vocabulary for what the coalition affords outsiders (access, cost, representation, cascade); (3) bind "broad development" as a comparison with a per-group measure; (4) plan the opening of the wall and the repair of the channel; (5) learn and carry only methods that paid against the ground.
- The likely break is at link 3. Organisations typically have excellent link 1 (membership lists) and link 4 (bargaining plans), and no instrument at link 3. Fig 3 :26: the middle link "has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments." A reading taken below that break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :23).

## CONCLUSION

The two conditions form a union, so they are analysed in separate loops and never averaged (Fig 9 :54-57). Coalitions with the same cast are not automatically the same substance: under Fig 12 they can be isomers, differing in the bond, or members of one functional group differing in the atoms that fill the slots. The dependency order puts the definition and measurement of "broad development" at the break, before planning. Single-lever explanations fail for a stated figure reason each; the two-condition remedy survives with a falsifier attached.

This framing rescues what is defensible in the ordinary question without importing a named institution. It needs no new primitive and no claim that the framework can name the winning design. What remains open is exactly what F8 records: which arrangement satisfies both conditions in a given habitat is `gap`.
