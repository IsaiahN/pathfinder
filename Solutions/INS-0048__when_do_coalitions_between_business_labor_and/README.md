## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not the *composition* of the coalition (which three actors sit at the table) but the *contact the coalition keeps with the ground of broad outcomes*. In effect terms: the coalition holds a library Γ that predicts returns to its members; market and state returns o′ arrive per person and per group; R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| must be read per slot, never averaged across a population (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22). One live slot can be an excluded group whose loss is hidden inside a healthy national average.

- Option A, "a coalition of the right actors produces broad development": leads to the prediction that development is a property of the coalition's cast, and that adding the right partners is sufficient.
- Option B, "broad development is a property of coalition-and-habitat, conditional on two things": leads to the prediction that the same cast can produce broad development or exclusion depending on whether its closure stays open to outside frames and its channel to a population-wide ground stays unpooled (Fig 11 :39, Fig 11 :40).

What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a **shape** naming two conditions that must both hold, plus an instrument that would discriminate a failure of the first condition from a failure of the second. That shape is the sought-for term; until it is bound it predicts nothing and no check in the loop can see it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B in `specified` mode, kept per domain and never averaged. Which exact institutional design satisfies the two conditions in a given country and decade is a declared `gap`, not a hidden name.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the institutional question, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of coalitions and development.

Four reasons:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable development scholars manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
