## 0. The question under audit

> "When do coalitions between business, labor, and government support broad development, and when do they exclude outsiders?" (institutional questions.txt line 59, section 5)

**Residual in effect terms.** A coalition is a bounded frame with a library Γ. It predicts that its bargains produce "development." The ground returns outcomes per person and per group, not to the coalition as a whole. The residual is R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| indexed by slot, and the slots that matter most here are the ones the coalition does not count: informal workers, non-union labour, firms outside the bargain, consumers, future entrants, and second-order groups three steps out. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A coalition can post a healthy national growth average while one slot carries all the exclusion.

**What would count as an answer, bound before the run.** A shape with two conditions and a discriminator: (C1) the coalition's closure is open to a frame whose closure differs, so an outside actor can move its wall; (C2) the coalition keeps a channel to a population-wide ground that neither the coalition nor its researchers can move. Broad development when both hold; exclusion when either fails, in categorically different ways. This is the "Direction" discipline of `THE_FORMULA.md`: write the sought-for shape as an ordinary term so it can accrue a residual like anything else.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.** Arity: a three-place coalition plus a fourth class it does not bind (the outsiders), so four slots at minimum, and more once contact cascades. Symmetry: the relation between insiders and outsiders is not a symmetric bond. It is a threshold (who is inside the wall) and an absence (who is not at the table), carrying ⋛ and −, not +. Scale: the bargaining table (micro), the sector or nation (meso), and the distribution of outcomes across the population over a decade (macro). The membrane between them is lossy (Fig 4 :30).

**One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor. No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.**

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 The bargaining coalition | firms and employer associations, unions, state agencies | concessions, wage pacts, policy commitments, credibility | realized population-wide outcomes (employment, productivity, distribution) that do not move when the coalition calls them development | admitted |
| D2 The outsider habitat | informal and non-union workers, excluded firms, consumers, future entrants | access, opportunity, costs borne, second-order effects | their realized outcomes, measurable separately from the coalition's average | admitted |
| D3 The closure and membership mechanism | Γ, the seven bonds, the imported atom, the wall | reach, imports, description length | settlement against the ground; what composes is decided by what pays | admitted |
| D4 The verification and research frame | development scholars, institutes, reviewers, voters | agreement and residual | the ground, never agreement; independence is how little this frame draws on the coalition's evidence | admitted |
| X1 "coalition quality" as a free-floating property | the coalition alone | talent, trust, "social capital" | none nameable apart from the coalition's own self-report | not a domain yet, absorbed into D1/D3 |
| X2 "strong unions cause development" as a standing law | one actor class | membership density | already inside D1, and its valence flips with the residual (Fig 11 :49-51) | absorbed, not a domain |

**Partition or union.** A union, not a partition. C1 and C2 are overlapping conditions, and a coalition can satisfy one and fail the other. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* A sealed coalition with good data and an open coalition with a broken metric are not two degrees of the same failure. They need different levers. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*

**Dependency order (Fig 3).** perception of who is in and out -> vocabulary for what the coalition affords -> objective ("broad development" stated as a comparison, not a slogan) -> planning -> learn and carry. The middle link is where chains break: a coalition can perceive its members perfectly and plan skilfully, yet never bind "broad development" as a term that can accrue a residual. Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Effort spent above the break (more summits, more pacts) arrives at a step that cannot use it.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The question is a union, not a scale (decisive for the census)

Fig 9 :54-57, quoted in section 1. The question embeds two conditions, not one continuum from "broad" to "exclusive." A coalition can be open to new actors yet measure only its members, or measure the whole population yet seal its membership. The naive answer, "it depends on how inclusive the coalition is," collapses the union into one lever and leaves the intersection standing.

- Refuter: one intervention is shown to clear both conditions at once, and the residue after it is not categorically different from the starting case. Then it was a scale after all.

### F2 - The cast does not carry the verdict (re-census of the obvious reading)

Fig 4 :20: *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :50: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*

Business, labor, and government are roles, not valences. The same employer association can widen training and access under one residual and capture a licensing regime under another. The same union can broaden a wage bargain to uncovered workers or defend an insider wage floor at their expense. A figure line that appeared to say "include labor, get development" would fit only with a strain, so it governs a different quantity, namely the cast of a particular bargain, not the condition of broad outcomes. Re-census sends us back to C1 and C2.

- Refuter: a standing list of actor types is shown to predict broad, population-wide outcomes across genuine changes of residual, with the same sign each time.

### F3 - Coalition agreement is not a verdict

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* and Fig 2 :41: *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*

A joint statement by business, labor, and government is a convention about shared meaning. It is efficient for coordinating the coalition and worthless as evidence that outsiders benefited. Fig 2 :43: *"Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* The anchor here is the separately measured outcome of outsiders, not the communique. Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*

- Refuter: a coalition's own agreed metric is shown to be unmoved by its members' interests and to track independently measured outsider outcomes across a change of residual. Then it is an anchor, not a convention.

### F4 - Two failure modes, not one: sealed closure and channel failure

**Sealed closure (C1 fails).** Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."* More bargaining inside the same three actors re-arranges the same library. Fig 7 :13: *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"* and Fig 7 :36: *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."* This is the captured coalition: internally successful, externally inert.

**Channel failure (C2 fails).** Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"* and Fig 10 :26: *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*. Fig 1 :22 again: the average hides the live slot. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information" and "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it." A coalition that reads "development" from an aggregate growth figure cannot tell broadly shared gains from concentrated gains plus an excluded periphery; the reading does not comment on the source.

- Refuter for sealed closure: reach renewed by internal composition only, with no new atom and no outside frame, demonstrated against a ground that moved.
- Refuter for channel failure: an instrument that discriminates broadly shared from concentrated gains, and the coalition's metric is shown to track it after the source changes.

### F5 - Exclusion is a substitution, not a subtraction

Fig 11 :61: *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* A coalition can add an actor, a programme, a seat at the table. It cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult the coalition. Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* What the world calls "excluding outsiders" is always the construction of a substituted habitat: an enclave, a special economic zone, a formal-sector benefit, a gated training scheme. Fig 11 :68: *"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"* and Fig 11 :69: *"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"*.

This is a non-obvious prediction, so it gets its own refuter: a clean subtraction is demonstrated, with an actor removed from a habitat and no substituted habitat, no silent introduced actor, and no delayed cost. Under the figures that cannot happen, so such a demonstration would break Fig 11 :61 and :66.

### F6 - The habitat must be enumerated, not composed

Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 11 :44: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."* Fig 11 :52: *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*

For this question the direct slots are the bargaining partners. One step out are uncovered workers, informal firms, and consumers. Three steps out are, for example, the families of workers whose formal-sector wage floor raised the price of services the excluded group buys, or the regions whose tax base funds the bargain without representation in it. A coalition that enumerates only its signatories has scoped the habitat to direct contact and will call the resulting exclusion a side effect.

- Refuter: a documented second- or third-order effect on an outsider slot is shown to be absent where the model predicted it, across a change of residual.

### F7 - Capability is coalition-and-habitat

Fig 11 :39: *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40: *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

A "strong coalition" is not a property of the three signatories considered alone. Reforming the interior of a secretariat, or electing a more capable convenor, changes nothing if contact with the outsider habitat and the population-wide ground does not change. This dissolves X1 in the census: "coalition quality" has no anchor independent of contact, so it is not a domain.

- Refuter: sustained, ground-verified broadening of outcomes is produced by an interior reform that demonstrably changes no contact and no reach.

### F8 - Which exact institution works is a gap

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* and Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*. No figure line, after a genuine re-census, governs which specific bargaining design produces broad development in a given country and decade. The framework returns C1 and C2 as a shape. Naming corporatism, a specific wage-setting institution, or an "inclusive institution" as *the* answer is a binding from the political-economy frame, provenanced as such, not a derivation from the figures. From *They're Scanning Books*: "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."

- Refuter: a figure line is produced that names a specific institutional arrangement and its membership rule, with no import from the political-economy frame. Until then, `gap` stands.

### F9 - The research frame can spend the independence it is meant to supply

Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Seat Map :171: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"*. From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."

Development-coalitions research is often funded by, embedded in, or methodologically committed to the same institutions it studies. That is a shared evidence pool, so agreement from within it is one witness. This is why the contact CSV lists public, opt-in venues and why the first contact carries only the two-condition shape and its refuter, never the framework.

- Refuter: a research community with genuinely different data, methods, and incentives, demonstrably not drawing on the coalition's evidence pool, returns a ground-based verdict on stage 1. That would be a witness, not a mirror.

### F10 - The condensation has a round trip gap

Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*

Condensing this derivation to "inclusive institutions good, extractive institutions bad" discards exactly what the union required: which of C1 and C2 failed, whether exclusion was a substitution, and which slot carried the residual. The Nobel 2024 account of Acemoglu, Johnson, and Robinson supplies the broad institutional contrast as world corroboration, not as the derivation. Treating the shorthand as identical to the figure-derived shape would set the round trip gap R_T to zero, which is the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 names.

- Refuter: the shorthand is shown to predict which of the two conditions failed, with no loss of slot-level information. Then the gap is zero and the condensation is lossless.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Required before any outreach, Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The two-condition shape is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A coalition produces broad, population-wide, per-slot gains while its closure is demonstrably sealed against every outside frame and its metric reads only member outcomes (breaks C1 and C2).
2. "Exclusion of outsiders" is demonstrated to be a clean subtraction, with no substituted habitat, no silent introduced actor, and no delayed cost (breaks Fig 11 :61 and :66).
3. A standing list of actor types predicts broad outcomes with the same sign across genuine changes of residual (breaks Fig 11 :49-51).
4. One lever clears both conditions at once and leaves a residue that is not categorically different from the start (breaks Fig 9 :54-57).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Ask which condition failed before asking who should be at the table. Cast-only diagnosis is the strain F2 refuses.
2. Bind "broad development" as an ordinary term with a per-slot instrument before further bargaining analysis (Fig 3 :27).
3. Enumerate the habitat two or three steps out, not just the signatories (Fig 11 :44, :52).
4. Treat exclusion as habitat substitution and look for what was not reproduced and what was unintentionally introduced (Fig 11 :68-69).
5. Send the generator: the shape, the refuter, and the lineage. Keep the figures, not this summary, as the authority (Fig 4 :23).

## 7. Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is the coalition a single thing with a degree of inclusiveness?** No. Fig 9 :54-57 names a union of two overlapping conditions. Unbundle first.
2. **Does the cast decide?** No. Fig 4 :20 and Fig 11 :49-51 make valence relative to the residual. The same actor changes sign when R changes.
3. **What closes R when C1 fails?** Only an import, Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :32. More of the same three actors is more spelling.
4. **What closes R when C2 fails?** A better instrument, improved from one already returning something (Fig 6 :32-34). The per-slot outcome measure is already returning a reading; it just cannot discriminate source.
5. **What is "exclusion" mechanically?** A substituted habitat, Fig 11 :61 and :66, with two silent failure modes at :68-69.
6. **Can we name the winning institution?** No. Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134. Shape only. A specific name would be an import with no source gate and no shadow test.
7. **Where is the seat in all this?** D4. Frames range against the ground, they do not negotiate with it (Fig 2 :45). A research venue that shares the coalition's evidence pool supplies a convention, not a witness.

**Strains after re-census: none hidden.** One live strain is recorded and not smoothed: Fig 11 :61 says exclusion is unavailable while ordinary language says coalitions exclude. The resolution is that substitution is available and subtraction is not, which is why F5 predicts silent costs rather than clean boundaries. R_T for the shorthand "inclusive versus extractive" is nonzero and enumerated in F10.
