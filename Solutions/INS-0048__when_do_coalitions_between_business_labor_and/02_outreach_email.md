# Outreach draft - validate the shape first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until section 6 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` has been checked and the two-condition shape still stands. If the shape does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about business, labor and government coalitions

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about development coalitions. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "when do these coalitions help and when do they exclude outsiders" is not one question with a scale from broad to narrow. It is a union of two conditions that can fail separately, so it has to be unbundled before looking for a case.
>
> 1. **Open closure.** The coalition has to stay in contact with a frame whose closure differs from its own: uncovered workers, informal firms, consumers, future entrants, or a different discipline's data. If the same three actors only re-arrange what they already hold, their agreement compounds while their evidence does not move. An open coalition can add an actor. It cannot cleanly subtract one, because "exclusion" is always the substitution of one habitat for another, with costs that stay invisible until the goal fails or the introduced actor acts.
> 2. **A population-wide channel.** "Broad development" has to be bound as an ordinary term with an instrument that reads outcomes per group, not as a national average. A coalition can read healthy aggregate growth while one slot carries all the exclusion. The average hides the live signal.
>
> Broad development requires both. Exclusion follows from either failing, but the two failures are categorically different and need different levers: the first needs an import from an outside frame, the second needs a better instrument, sharpened from one already returning a reading.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular cast of actors carries the result. Business, labor and government are roles whose valence depends on the residual. The same employer association or union can widen access under one question and defend an insider rent under another. I am also not naming the winning institution. The framework returns a shape, not a name, so "which specific bargaining design works in this country and decade" is an open gap, not an answer I am smuggling in.
>
> **What would refute the shape, written before this message:**
> 1. a coalition produces broad, population-wide, per-group gains while its closure is demonstrably sealed against every outside frame and its metric reads only its own members;
> 2. "excluding outsiders" is shown to be a clean subtraction, with no substituted habitat, no unintended introduced actor and no delayed cost;
> 3. a standing list of actor types predicts broad outcomes with the same sign across real changes of the question;
> 4. one lever clears both conditions at once and leaves a residue that is not categorically different from the start.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract the shape. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula, public repository (branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - The architecture, with the figure set and citation list: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a coalition you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The architecture's own note says LLM outputs can be "contrived to fit the formula," so a reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> [your name]

## The two-stage offer, explicitly

1. **Stage 1:** send the two-condition shape, its four refuters, and the demand for a per-slot ground. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record that verdict **before** showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt in only:** if they are curious, send the three links. Seat 4 must not hold the interiors; once a recipient has absorbed the framework, their agreement stops being evidence. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40, *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method, with the instruction that each user names their own ground, writes their own refuter, and states their own mode.
