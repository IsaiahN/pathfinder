# Expert verification packet - the Tether on development coalitions

**What this is:** a claim in a form a development scholar or political economist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the two-condition shape and the failure modes, `general` on the structural claims. Not `grounded`.** No country-level measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Broad development" and "excluding outsiders" are not ends of one inclusiveness scale. They are a union of two conditions that can fail separately. First, the coalition's closure must stay reachable by a frame whose closure differs from its own; if only the same three actors re-arrange what they already hold, agreement compounds while evidence stands still. Second, development has to be read per group, not as a national average; an aggregate figure cannot tell broadly shared gains from concentrated gains plus an excluded periphery. Broad development requires both. Exclusion follows from either failing, and is never a clean subtraction of a group but a substituted habitat with costs invisible until the goal fails or the introduced actor acts. The actor list itself carries no fixed verdict, because the valence of business, labor and government depends on the residual. The framework does not name the winning institution; which design satisfies both conditions in a given place and decade is an honest `gap`.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 9 and 8
- **Statement:** the question is a union, so the two conditions must be analysed separately and never averaged into one degree of inclusiveness.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57 *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Fig 9 :54 *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 8 :32 *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."*
- **Refuter:** one lever is shown to clear both conditions at once and leaves a residue that is not categorically different from the starting case. Then it was a scale after all.

### C2 - measurement-facing; checkable by a development economist or statistician
- **Statement:** the per-slot residual is the only reading that can support a breadth verdict, and an aggregate cannot discriminate the source of a gain.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 10 :26 *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*
- **What to check:** can a national income or employment series be constructed that tracks member and non-member outcomes separately, and does it change the sign of any conclusion about breadth? From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."
- **Refuter:** an aggregate metric is shown to discriminate broadly shared from concentrated gains across a documented change of source, with no per-group breakdown. Then the channel is not the break.

### C3 - institutional-facing; checkable by a political economist or economic sociologist
- **Statement:** exclusion is habitat substitution, and the cast of the coalition has no standing valence.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :61 *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* Fig 11 :66 *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Fig 11 :51 *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* Fig 11 :68 *"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"* and Fig 11 :69 *"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"*
- **What to check:** in a coalition you know well, name the substituted habitat for an excluded group and the two silent costs. Then ask whether the same actor class had the opposite sign under a different question.
- **Refuter:** a clean subtraction is demonstrated with no substituted habitat, no introduced actor and no delayed cost; or a standing actor list predicts broad outcomes with the same sign across changes of residual.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a named winning institution. Corporatism, social partnership, growth coalitions and inclusive institutions are bindings from the political-economy frame, not derivations from the figures. Which one works where is `gap`.
- Not that actors are irrelevant. Their valence is relative to the residual, so a cast-only answer cannot close the question.
- Not that aggregate statistics are useless. They cannot discriminate a source, which is a different objection.
- Not that exclusion is impossible. Substitution is available; subtraction is not.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by a measured coalition outcome.

## 5. What would settle it

A per-group, source-discriminating instrument applied to a real coalition and its outsiders, not a further argument. Concretely:

1. Per-slot outcomes for members, uncovered workers, informal firms, consumers and second-order groups, reported separately (Fig 1 :19).
2. A wall test: does a new actor from a differing closure change the coalition's predictions (Fig 6 :23)?
3. A substitution ledger for each excluded group: the new habitat, what was not reproduced, what was unintentionally introduced (Fig 11 :66-69).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :54-57, Fig 1 :19-22 and Fig 11 :61, :66, :68-69. Six short passages.
2. Check C1 against a coalition you know: are the wall and the metric actually independent failures?
3. Check C3 by naming one substituted habitat and its two silent costs. If you cannot, say so, that is data.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
