## What the wider literature bears on the question

1. **The institutional economics witness (Nobel 2024).** The inclusive/extractive contrast names the same surface distinction as the question, and the committee's line that "no one will trust their promises of future economic reforms" while a political system guarantees control is a close world-level restatement of sealed closure. It corroborates F4 at the level of a broad institutional shape. It does not tell us which condition failed in a given case, and it shares the broad institutional-economics evidence pool with the rest of that literature, so it is one witness, not several.
2. **The governance witness (Nobel 2009).** Ostrom's user associations and Williamson's conflict-resolution structures show that a coalition can be a governance structure rather than a fixed cast. The figure-level reading (F2, F7) says capability is a property of coalition-and-habitat. The 2009 account corroborates that at the level of institutional design, from a different set of laureates and mechanisms. Low overlap with the 2024 source.
3. **The resilience witness (Ecology and Society 2022).** A "rigidity trap" is highly connected, rigid and inflexible, with diminished novelty, and too much redundancy can cause stagnation. That corroborates F4's sealed-closure branch from a field with a genuinely different evidence base. It is an analogue, not a measurement of coalitions. The inverted-U result also warns against the naive prescription "add more partners": addition can reduce adaptability past a point.
4. **Framework-internal essays ( maximal overlap, used for the canonical reading only).** The eight essays are by the author of the figures. Their agreement with the figures is expected and carries no independent weight. They are used in `05_what_shape_means.md` for what "shape", "source" and "prestige" mean inside the framework.

- **Fig 4 :22-25**, *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down. A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Sending the whole architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording. Lead with the two-condition shape and the refuter.

- **Fig 9 :27-28**, *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before showing the article.
- **Seat Map :163**, *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* A send list selected for people likely to agree produces a convention, not corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the two conditions and their four refuters; (2) ask what the recipient would count as a failure and how they would measure each group separately; (3) record that verdict; (4) only then, if they ask, the three links, with the note that the figures and not the summary are the authority.
