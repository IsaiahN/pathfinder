## Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual line alone, before reading the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency. The residual line I used was the question text itself, restated in effect terms: given stabilization, does each ice quantity retrace its loss, continue to a smaller equilibrium, or stay on a one-way path?

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Arity: the residual names "ice-related changes" but the world returns four distinguishable response laws, so this is a **union compound** (Fig 9 :55). Symmetry: near-symmetric for the thin seasonal reservoir, asymmetric where a grounding-line feedback or a carbon burial bottleneck exists. Scale: seasons to millennia. I did not assume three classes at this stage; the three-class shape came from the figure census below.
- **Domain 1, fast seasonal ice.** Actors: atmosphere and ocean surface. Currency: enthalpy. Anchor: satellite extent and thickness. Fast, no crossing for summer sea ice.
- **Domain 2, grounded ice.** Actors: accumulation, ablation, bed and debris. Currency: mass balance. Anchor: stakes and terminus length. Slow, but reversible if cooled enough.
- **Domain 3, marine ice sheets and shelves.** Actors: grounding line, retrograde bed, buttressing shelf, ocean heat. Currency: ice flux. Anchor: altimetry, gravimetry, bed topography. Crossing-sensitive.
- **Domain 4, frozen ground.** Actors: ground thermal regime, snow, microbes, burial. Currency: temperature and carbon. Anchor: boreholes and inventories. Internally a union: area and carbon.
- **Order by dependency.** Fast extent, then grounded ice, then marine ice sheets, then frozen-ground carbon. I derived this from what each domain consumes from the previous one, not from the primary's ordering.

**Where my census differs in route.** The primary derived the union from Fig 9 and the asymmetry from Fig 4, Fig 12 and Fig 11. I derived the union from the incompatibility of the anchors themselves (an extent record cannot settle a carbon-stock question), and the asymmetry from the sign of the feedback in D3 and the absence of a reverse operator in D4. Different starting figures, same domain boundaries. Low overlap in route, but one evidence pool, so the agreement is internal consistency, not corroboration.

## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 9 :54-57 (union), Fig 4 :28-29 (irreversible transform), Fig 12 :119 and :121 (spent parts, loop sign), Fig 11 :61 (asymmetry).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07_the_tether_answer.md`: three classes, two quantities per subsystem, no averaging, numbers imported.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the concrete claim about sea ice, glaciers, grounding lines and permafrost carbon, with the specific timescales.

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- **Which crossing has been crossed in a given basin.** The shape says a crossing makes retreat self-sustained; it cannot say whether the Amundsen basin has crossed. That needs grounding-line observations.
- **Whether a named glacier can regrow at all.** The shape says regrowth is slower than retreat; flat, thick, low-latitude glaciers may vanish entirely, which is a different outcome than slow regrowth. Bed geometry settles it.
- **The rate of carbon re-burial.** The shape says carbon is a spent stock; the numerical irreversibility comes from a model of burial and decay.
- **The ocean-ice separation inside D3.** The shape's spent/regenerating rule predicts the ocean returns faster than the ice sheet. The 40-year figure is a model result, not a figure output.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: the residual is a union, and the three outcome classes are distinct. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists here; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the figures" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a recorded gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` states.
