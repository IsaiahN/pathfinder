# Expert verification packet: EAR-0040 ice recovery and reversibility

**What this is:** a claim in a form a cryosphere researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape and class assignments, `general` on the invariants, `grounded` only for named world measurements quoted with provenance. Not a ground reading.**

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "which ice changes can partly recover if temperature stabilizes?" is a union of four conditions, not one question. Sea ice and seasonal snow can partly recover on timescales of years. Mountain glaciers are committed to decades-to-centuries of further change and regrow more slowly than they retreat. Marine ice sheets and ice shelves can enter self-sustained retreat, after which returning temperature to its prior value does not restore the prior configuration; this is a centuries-to-millennia class. Permafrost must be split into AREA, which largely follows temperature back down, and CARBON, which is not re-buried on a human timescale. The structural claim is that recovery is not the loss path run backwards once a crossing or a spent stock is involved. The figures supply that shape; the numerical timescales are world measurements, imported with provenance and not derived from the framework.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - The question is a union of conditions with different reversal behaviour, not one recovery time

- **Statement:** no single time constant with no crossing term classifies sea ice, glaciers, marine ice sheets and permafrost. They must be unbundled before they are searched.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Also Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Also Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- **World check:** IPCC AR6 WG1 Chapter 9 separates the components and states "Because of their lagged response, glaciers will continue to lose mass at least for several decades even if global temperature is stabilized," while its sea-ice section states "There is no tipping point for this loss of Arctic summer sea ice (high confidence)." Two different reversal laws in one assessment.
- **Refuter:** a single first-order law with one time constant and no crossing term predicts the post-stabilization trajectory of all four domains, and a measurement pays for it.

### C2 - Once a crossing occurs, returning the forcing does not return the prior configuration

- **Statement:** a positive feedback that closes a loop makes retreat self-sustained; the return path is not the loss path reversed. Marine ice sheet retreat is the physical case.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :121 "The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Also Fig 4 :28-29 "it means the trip is not reversible, and something carried up and brought back down will not match what was sent." Also Fig 11 :61 "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."
- **World check:** Garbe et al. 2020, Nature: "the currently observed ice-sheet configuration is not regained even if temperatures are reversed to present-day levels," and "the West Antarctic Ice Sheet does not regrow to its modern extent until temperatures are at least one degree Celsius lower than pre-industrial levels." Mathiot et al. 2026, The Cryosphere: "The ocean conditions reverse within 40 years when the atmospheric perturbation ceases, while the ice sheet's response is much slower."
- **Refuter:** a grounding line that, after entering self-sustained retreat, returns to its prior position under the same boundary conditions at the same rate it retreated.

### C3 - Spent and regenerating stocks must be kept in separate accounts

- **Statement:** permafrost AREA and permafrost CARBON are different substances under the same forcing and must not be averaged into one reversibility verdict.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :119 "Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is." Also Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- **World check:** Cui et al. 2025, Earth System Dynamics: "In overshoot scenarios, permafrost area shows effective recovery" but "In contrast, permafrost carbon loss remains largely irreversible." Schaefer et al. 2014, Environmental Research Letters: "The PCF is irreversible on human time scales because in a warming climate, the burial mechanisms described above slow down or stop, so there is no way to convert CO2 into organic matter and freeze it back into the permafrost."
- **Refuter:** released permafrost carbon re-buried and refrozen on the same decadal timescale as permafrost area under stabilized temperature.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

## 5. What would settle it

The anchors are measurements, not agreement (Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks.").

1. Extent and thickness records for fast ice (satellite passive microwave, altimetry).
2. Mass-balance stakes and terminus length records for glaciers, with response time from thickness over terminus ablation.
3. Altimetry, gravimetry and bed topography for grounding-line position after a forcing reversal.
4. Borehole temperature and soil-carbon inventories, with AREA and CARBON in separate accounts.

One further point of method: agreement among cryosphere groups reading the same assessment is one evidence pool, not two witnesses (Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"). A failure from one checker is worth more than ten endorsements.
