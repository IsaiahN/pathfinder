**Subject:** One checkable claim about which ice changes can recover

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read any framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** "Which ice changes can partly recover if temperature stabilizes?" is not one question. It is a union of four conditions with different response times and different crossing behaviour. Sea ice and seasonal snow mostly track temperature on both the way up and the way down, so they can partly recover in years. Mountain glaciers keep losing mass for decades after stabilization and regrow more slowly than they retreat, so they sit in a decades-to-centuries class. Marine ice sheets can enter self-sustained grounding-line retreat and ice shelves can collapse, after which returning temperature to its prior value does not return the prior configuration; this is a centuries-to-millennia class. Permafrost must be split: its AREA largely follows temperature back down, but its CARBON, once emitted, is not re-buried on a human timescale.
>
> The structural claim is that recovery is not the loss path run backwards once a crossing or a spent stock is involved, and that the four conditions cannot be averaged into one recovery score.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the framework predicts ice physics. It does not supply the numbers 2 years or 1500 years. Those come from measurements and models, and I treat them as secondary. The framework supplies the shape of the classification: union, asymmetry, spent versus regenerating, and continuing commitment under stabilization.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. A single first-order law with one time constant and no crossing term predicts the post-stabilization trajectory of sea ice, glaciers, marine ice sheets and permafrost, and a measurement pays for it.
> 2. A marine ice sheet that has crossed into self-sustained retreat returns to its prior grounding-line position under the same boundary conditions, at the same rate it retreated.
> 3. Released permafrost carbon is re-buried and refrozen on the same decadal timescale as permafrost area.
> 4. Stabilized temperature immediately freezes a slow domain at its current extent with no further mass change.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Chapter 9, Ocean, Cryosphere and Sea Level Change, IPCC AR6 WG1: https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/chapter/chapter-9
> - The hysteresis of the Antarctic Ice Sheet, Garbe et al., Nature 2020: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-020-2727-5
> - Permafrost response and feedback under temperature stabilization and overshoot, Cui et al., ESD 2025: https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/16/1809/2025/
