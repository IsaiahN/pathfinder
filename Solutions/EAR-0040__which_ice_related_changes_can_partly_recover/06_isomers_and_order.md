# Isomers, functional groups and order in the ice question

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The three-class shape says that two systems under the same forcing can have opposite reversal behaviour. Two questions are owed: (1) is that an **isomer** relation under Fig 12, and (2) what is the **order** of the four domains, and of the two quantities inside D4, if the chain is ordered by dependency rather than by importance?

## DOMAIN CENSUS (recap, re-derived from the residual line alone)

Atoms held for this derivation are not the six substrate terms; they are the measured quantities: extent, mass, grounding-line position, ground temperature, soil carbon. Bonds available: sequence (→), production (⇒), subtraction (−), comparison (⋛), symmetric conjunction (+), and negation (¬). No new primitive is introduced. The forcing (temperature) is the shared operand.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are permafrost area and permafrost carbon isomers?

Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
Fig 12 :124: **"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

- Both quantities sit in the same domain D4, share the same actors (ground thermal regime, snow, microbes, burial) and have the same forcing operand (temperature). Same atom set.
- They differ in the bond to the forcing. AREA bonds through a near-reversible thermal relation: it *follows* temperature down and back up. CARBON bonds through a production relation that is effectively one-way on human timescales: thaw produces emission, and the reverse production (burial and refreezing) is astronomically slow.
- Different bond, different reversal behaviour, invisible if you record only "permafrost responds to temperature." By Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers of the permafrost compound**, not two readings of one thing.
- The functional-group reading is the weaker, safer claim: both are members of the class "frozen-ground quantity that responds to temperature" (Fig 12 :124), and the qualifier (area versus carbon) decides the reversal class.

**Triangulation status:** Fig 12 definition is figure single-source. The physical discrimination is world-triangulated across Cui et al. 2025 (area recovers, carbon does not) and Schaefer et al. 2014 (burial mechanism stops), which draw on different models but overlapping literature. Mark `specified`, not `grounded`.

### Q2 - Are glaciers and sea ice isomers, or different functional groups?

- Fig 12 :124 again: the group says what kind. Both are "cryospheric extent that responds to temperature," so they share a functional group.
- They do **not** share the full atom set: sea ice is seasonal and thin, with no bed geometry and no multi-decadal mass reservoir; glaciers have thickness, slope, hypsometry and a terminus. Fig 12 :117 requires the *same* atoms under a different bond. These do not qualify as isomers as the evidence is currently written.
- The stronger isomer claim would require writing both over the full shared set (temperature forcing, phase change, albedo feedback, extent, thickness) and showing that only the bond differs. That is a checkable structural claim, but it is not established here. **Refused as written.**

### Q3 - What is the order of the domains?

Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**
Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."**

Order by what each domain consumes from the one before:

1. **D1 Fast seasonal ice** is the first link. Its anchor (satellite extent) settles directly and quickly; it has no reliance on a slower subsystem to register a change. It is the shortest response time and the cleanest ground.
2. **D2 Grounded ice** consumes the same atmospheric forcing plus bed geometry. Its response time is longer because the mass reservoir is larger, but it still regrows if forcing reverses (Fig 12 :104, settled first-order molecule becomes an atom at the next level).
3. **D3 Marine ice sheets** consume D2-like surface mass balance plus ocean forcing and bed topology. When a grounding line retreats onto a retrograde slope, the output (more flux) re-enters as input (more retreat). Fig 12 :121: **"The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."** A chain cannot close; only a cycle can. This is where the third class becomes structurally necessary.
4. **D4 Frozen ground** is last in this ordering because its carbon quantity is not just slow but spent: the reverse operator (burial) is not available on the same timescale. Fig 12 :119: **"Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**

Dependency is not a claim that D4 is more important. The order tells us which ground to check first and which link cannot be diagnosed from below a break (Fig 3 :22-23).

### Q4 - What is the order inside D4?

AREA first, CARBON second. Area has a fast thermal channel and a measurement anchor (boreholes plus extent). Carbon has a slow burial channel and a stock anchor (inventory). The carbon verdict cannot be read off the area verdict: the area recovered in the Cui et al. 2025 overshoot runs while the carbon loss remained largely irreversible. A reading taken below that break would be a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22-23). So the order is area then carbon, and the two verdicts are never averaged.

### Q5 - What about the ocean in D3?

A useful discriminator, kept separate. Mathiot et al. 2026 found ocean conditions reversed within 40 years while the ice-sheet response was much slower, with 14 ice streams still retreating centuries later. This is Fig 12 :119 again: the ocean is the regenerating part, the grounded ice the spent part, in the same coupled domain. It also warns against using a fast-recovery ocean reading as a witness for ice-sheet recovery. Different quantity, different bond.

## FALSIFIER, written before the run

The isomer and order claims fail if:

1. Permafrost AREA and CARBON are shown to reverse on the same timescale under the same boundary conditions, so the different-bond claim collapses (kills Q1).
2. Sea ice and glaciers are written over the full shared atom set with only the bond differing, and their reversal classes still coincide (would establish, not kill, Q2; the claim as written is already refused).
3. A domain lower in the dependency order is shown to settle a higher domain's class without importing anything from it, so the chain can be reordered or collapsed (kills Q3).
4. Area and carbon in D4 are shown to be one quantity with one reversal law (kills Q4).

If any fires, the corresponding sub-claim is withdrawn. None has fired in this simulated run.

## CONCLUSION

The permafrost area/carbon split is a real isomer-style discrimination: same compound, different bond, decisive difference in behaviour. Sea ice and glaciers are only a functional group as currently written, not isomers. The domains order by dependency from fast extent to spent carbon, and the marine ice sheet is the link where a cycle closes and a chain cannot. The order is a guide to which ground to check first, not a ranking of importance.
