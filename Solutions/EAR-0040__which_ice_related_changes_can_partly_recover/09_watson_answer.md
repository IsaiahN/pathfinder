## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, which ice-related changes can partly recover if temperature stabilizes and which take centuries or longer to reverse. Option A: treat "ice" as one system and give one recovery time. Option B: unbundle the residual into four conditions, keep the per-domain verdicts separate, and distinguish a crossing-sensitive or spent class from a fast tracking class. What each leads to: A hides the differences that decide the answer and cannot be checked against a single anchor; B gives a checkable shape, four anchors, and refuters written in advance. The sought-for shape was bound before the run: three outcome classes, two quantities per subsystem (a response lag and a crossing sensitivity), and no averaging across domains.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Arity: four conditions in contact with temperature. Symmetry: near-symmetric tracking in the fast, near-linear case; asymmetric where a crossing or a spent stock exists. Scale: years (seasonal ice) to millennia (marine ice sheets, released carbon). Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." This is a union, not one hard question.
- **Domain 1, fast seasonal ice (sea ice, snow, lake and river ice).** Actors: atmosphere, ocean surface, seasonal insolation. Currency: surface energy balance and enthalpy. Anchor: satellite extent and thickness records. Response: years. No crossing for summer sea ice.
- **Domain 2, grounded ice (mountain glaciers, ice caps).** Actors: accumulation, ablation, bed slope, debris. Currency: mass balance and response time (thickness over terminus ablation). Anchor: stake mass balance and terminus length records. Response: decades to centuries, with regrowth slower than retreat.
- **Domain 3, marine ice sheets and ice shelves.** Actors: grounding line, retrograde bed slope, ice shelf buttressing, ocean heat, bedrock uplift. Currency: ice flux across the grounding line and basal melt rate. Anchor: altimetry, gravimetry, bed topography. Response: centuries to millennia once a crossing occurs.
- **Domain 4, frozen ground.** Actors: ground thermal regime, snow, microbes, carbon burial. Currency: ground temperature and soil carbon stock. Anchor: borehole temperatures and carbon inventories. Itself a union: AREA tracks temperature, CARBON does not. Unbundled before search per Fig 9 :54.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3 :20).** Fast extent first, grounded ice second, marine ice sheets third, frozen-ground carbon last. Order is dependency, not importance.
- **Strain test applied.** Fig 4 governs a lossy transform between scales; Fig 12 :121 governs the sign of a feedback loop. A first reading treated these as competing accounts of recovery. Re-census shows different objects, so no strain remains. The figures are metalaws about frames and closures; their bearing on physical ice is structural, not a physical derivation. Stated openly.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

An ice question is a union, not one question. Fast seasonal ice can partly recover on years. Mountain glaciers are committed over decades to centuries and regrow more slowly than they retreat. Marine ice sheets and ice shelves are effectively irreversible on human timescales once a crossing occurs, on centuries to millennia. Permafrost AREA largely follows temperature back down; permafrost CARBON does not return on a human timescale. Recovery is not the loss path run backwards where a crossing or a spent stock exists. The figures give that shape; the numerical timescales are imported world measurements, not figure outputs. This is a `specified`, ungrounded-by-this-derivation result.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The shape claims are few, each has a refuter written before the run, and the class assignments carry their anchor and provenance. The real cost is classification error in adaptation planning: treating a crossing-sensitive grounding line as fast-reversible, or treating permafrost carbon as if it followed area back down. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The avoidable additional cost is spending outside readers' independence by sending a framework before the shape has been checked.
- **undoable:** yes. A class assignment can be moved by a named measurement without withdrawing the shape. Unbundling permafrost and separating ocean from ice-sheet response are corrections already made in this folder and recorded visibly. No primitive has to be withdrawn; refusals add to the record rather than erase it.
- **reversed by:** ground readings, not agreement. A single-law fit to all four domains that a measurement pays for would break the union claim. A grounding line that returns to its prior position under the same boundary conditions at the same rate would break the asymmetry claim. Decadal re-burial of released permafrost carbon would break the spent-stock claim. Stabilized temperature immediately freezing a slow domain would break the committed-work claim. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
