# Expert verification packet - the Tether on the rate of adaptive evolution

**What this is:** a claim in a form an evolutionary biologist or quantitative geneticist can check. Mode, derivation, provenance and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural claim. Not `grounded`.** No number of generations is asserted and no measurement was taken in this derivation.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The response to selection is indexed per trait, not averaged into a species-wide rate. Each per-trait response per generation is a product of transmission fidelity and environmental pressure, so it stalls when either factor is zero. Heritable variance precedes pressure in the dependency order, so more selection cannot rescue a population with no variance for the trait. Vulnerability is a property of organism and habitat together. A population tracking a moving optimum trails by a lag. The breeder's equation `R = h²S` is credited to quantitative genetics and is treated here as an imported law placed inside a larger shape, not as a figure-derived discovery. What is offered for checking is that placement, five written refuters, and an honest statement of what only a measurement can close.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 1

- **Statement:** the rate must be reported per trait slot, never aggregated into one number for the species.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
- **What to check:** does a single scalar rate predict heat tolerance, phenology and disease resistance in the same population as well as three separate rates do? The figures predict no.
- **Refuter:** a scalar rate that matches each trait's response as well as the per-trait vector in a real dataset.

### C2 - biology-facing; checkable by a quantitative geneticist

- **Statement:** per-generation response is the product of narrow-sense heritability and the selection differential, and is zero if either is zero.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 7 :26 "progress per handoff: R = h² × S". Fig 7 :37-39 "Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:" / "the bracket's fidelity across the handoff" / "the ground's pressure on the room".
- **Independent corroboration:** *Heritability / Breeder's equation* (Wikipedia, 2026): "the expected response to selection of a trait with known narrow-sense heritability (h2) can be estimated using the breeder's equation: R=h2S", and "the Selection Differential (S) is defined as the average difference between the parent generation and the selected parents."
- **What to check:** is the figure's "bracket's fidelity" a faithful structural reading of `h²`, and "the ground's pressure" of `S`? Estimate both for one trait and compare the observed response with the product.
- **Refuter:** an additive relation (`R = h² + S`), a proportionality to pressure alone, or a heritable directional response at zero additive variance.

### C3 - ecology-facing; checkable by an evolutionary ecologist

- **Statement:** heritable variance is the prerequisite link; vulnerability is a contact property; the response trails a moving optimum.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Fig 11 :39 "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40 "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 7 :30-31 "every room closes some of the gap;" / "no finite number of them ever arrives".
- **Independent corroboration:** Alexander, Martin, Martin and Bonhoeffer (2014), *Evolutionary Applications* 7(10):1161-1179, open access: "essentially equivalent parameters are highlighted, including severity of environmental change (e.g. drug dosage or rate of temperature increase), population size, and genetic variance or mutation rate"; "Response to selection allows the population mean trait value to track the moving optimum, asymptotically establishing a constant lag distance." *Evolutionary rescue* (Wikipedia, 2026): "persists due to natural selection acting on heritable variation".
- **What to check:** do the rescue models in fact put variance on the same footing as the rate of environmental change? Is the lag a general result or an artefact of the quantitative-genetic assumptions? Would a trait change that alters no habitat contact reduce vulnerability?
- **Refuter:** adaptation at zero variance; a moving optimum held with zero lag; a contact-free reduction in vulnerability.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a number of generations for a named species. Not a species-wide scalar rate.
- Not a new biological law. `R = h²S` is the breeder's equation, "borrowed from breeding" by the framework's own record (*How a Society of Agents Would Work*, 2026) and credited to quantitative genetics (Fig 8 :50-51: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them").
- Not that evolution always rescues a population; rescue is conditional on variance, population size, variant timing and the rate of change.
- Not that all trait change is genetic; plasticity and movement to another habitat are different residuals.
- Not `grounded`. Not a final theory, and no claim that this is the only framework.

## 5. What would settle it

The ground: estimate `h²` and `S` for one trait in one population, then observe the per-generation response. Five refuters are pre-registered in `01` §4 and repeated in `07` §4. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. Averaging many expert opinions about the shape is not a substitute for the measurement (Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you.").

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, Fig 7 :26 and :37-39, Fig 3 :19, Fig 11 :39-40, Fig 7 :30-31. Six short passages.
2. Check C2 against the breeder's equation you already know: is the figure's two-factor product faithful, and does the stall condition follow?
3. Name one dataset where `h²` and `S` were estimated for the same trait and population, or say why none exists.
4. Say whether the lag under a moving optimum is general in the rescue models or an assumption of their quantitative-genetic form.
5. Send the refutation, if there is one. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
