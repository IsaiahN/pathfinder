## Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual line alone before using the primary's conclusions, in WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the gap between a population's trait distribution and the values that keep it clear of mortality under changed conditions. Arity: many slots, so a vector and not a scalar. Symmetry: ordered, since the response moves toward an optimum rather than joining two peers. Scale: one generation (one handoff), compounding across generations, with a further crossing from allele to individual to population. Union, not partition: several conditions bind at once.
- **Domain 1, population genetics.** Actors: individuals, alleles, additive variance, heritability, selection differential. Currency: trait change per generation. Anchor: measured heritability and selection in a population. Question type: partition of variance and pressure into a response.
- **Domain 2, habitat.** Actors: conditions, competitors, predators, contaminants. Currency: pressure and contact. Anchor: fitness and mortality, which settle. Question type: union of conditions; the environment that changes is the habitat, not the ground.
- **Domain 3, generational scale.** Actors: generations, inherited distributions, settled traits. Currency: per-handoff response and its compounding. Anchor: the lineage record. Question type: ordered by dependency, inherit then select then carry.
- **Domain 4, verification.** Actors: expert readers. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Independence in this simulated run is zero.

Order by dependency: variance before pressure (Fig 3 :19); describe the residual before naming a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); settle a generation's distribution before composing over it (Fig 12 :104).

My census matches the primary's four domains and its union reading, but I reached it from Fig 9's union/partition split and Fig 11's habitat/ground distinction, whereas the primary reached it from Fig 1's per-slot rule and Fig 7's product. Different starting figures, same boundaries: low overlap between routes, so the agreement is weakly non-circular inside the framework. It is still one evidence pool.

One thing survives cleanly: a per-trait response is a product of two factors and stalls when either is zero. That is the residue worth sending.
