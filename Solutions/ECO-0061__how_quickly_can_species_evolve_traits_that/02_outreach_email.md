**Subject:** One checkable claim about the rate of adaptation, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at. I am writing to you individually because your public work touches the residual, not because you are likely to agree.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** Ask how quickly a species can evolve a trait that lowers its vulnerability to a changing environment. The answer the figures license is a shape, not a number:
>
> 1. There is no single species-wide rate. The response is indexed per trait and per environmental axis. A global average can be near zero while several trait-level responses are live.
> 2. The per-trait response per generation is a **product** of two factors: the fidelity with which the trait is transmitted across the generational handoff, and the pressure the changed environment exerts on that trait. If either factor is zero, the chain runs in place and there is no directional response.
> 3. Each generation closes part of the gap. No finite number of generations arrives at a perfect match, and under a continuously moving optimum the population trails by a lag rather than catching up.
> 4. Heritable variance comes before pressure in the dependency order. Increasing pressure cannot rescue a population with no variance for the trait, and a link nobody has measured is not a link that works.
>
> This is close to the breeder's equation `R = h²S`, and I am not claiming to have derived that equation or to have found a new biological law. The question is whether the surrounding shape, per-slot vector, stall condition, dependency order and lag, adds anything checkable beyond the textbook product.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** a number of generations for any named species; that all adaptation is genetic rather than plastic or behavioural; that evolution always rescues a population; that a species-level scalar rate is meaningful; or that the framework is a final theory. Those are ground questions and this is not a ground reading.
>
> **What would refute it (written down before this email):**
> 1. the response is additive in fidelity and pressure, or proportional to pressure alone, rather than their product;
> 2. a population with zero additive variance for the trait still shows a heritable directional response under constant pressure;
> 3. a single scalar rate predicts each trait's response as well as the per-trait vector;
> 4. a population holds a continuously moving optimum with zero lag, or adapts beyond the critical rate set by its variance and the fitness width.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract or re-census the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **Stage 1, before any theory:** what would you expect to see in the data if the shape were true, and what would count as a failure? Please record that verdict before reading anything below.
>
