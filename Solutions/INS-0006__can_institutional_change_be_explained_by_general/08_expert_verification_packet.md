# Expert verification packet — the Tether on general principles versus historical events

**What this is:** a claim in a form a scholar of institutional change can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the separator and per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the numerical split, not `grounded`.** No institutional case was measured. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Standing:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question's either/or is a union of conditions, not a choice between rival answers. General principles govern the kernel, that is the loop and the rule for naming what worked, which persist while actors, currency and anchor change. Historically specific events are not an extra variable in that kernel: they are the conditions a seed lands in, the ordered selection of one path among alternatives, and the atom imported from the historical record that no closed set of principles can derive by further search. A settled institution becomes an atom for the next layer, which is the recursion the framework calls path dependence. Whether a shock is critical depends on the residual being tracked, so no standing list of critical junctures survives a change of residual. The allocation between kernel and route is a learnable per-frame weighting, not a universal number.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framing; checkable by reading Figure 9 and the local residual description

- **Statement:** the question is a union of overlapping conditions, not a two-item partition; verdicts must be kept per domain and never averaged into a percentage.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54 — *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :55 — *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57 — *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Fig 1 :19 — *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*

### C2 — the general/specific relation; checkable by reading Figures 4, 8 and 12

- **Statement:** the kernel recurs and the cast does not; the specific cannot be derived from the general by search; the route is carried by an ordered bond and a loop sign, not by an ingredient list.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :19-20 — *"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* Fig 4 :34-35 — *"The level a method lands on remakes it. A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."* Fig 8 :32 — *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."* Fig 12 :111 — *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."*
- **What to check:** is the seed-and-conditions relation faithful to how a general principle actually enters an institutional case? Is the event selection a distinct bond from the persistence mechanism? Can the loop sign be measured rather than inferred from the outcome?
- **Refuter:** derive a dated event from a closed set of principles with no import; or show that the selection event and the persistence mechanism are one bond; or show that the loop sign cannot in principle be determined.

### C3 — the limit and the anchor; checkable by reading Figures 2, 9, 11 and the Symbols table

- **Statement:** no universal percentage and no standing list of decisive events; the allocation is a learnable per-frame weighting, and the anchor is the material outcome rather than the scholarly narrative.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :32 — *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 11 :51 — *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* Fig 2 :29 — *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Symbols table, entry `w`: *"How far the belief leans on private history versus inherited prior"* and *"w is learnable from the outcomes of past weightings."*
- **What to check:** does the framework really leave the value open rather than imply a constant? Is there an institution where the outcome was read independently of the account of it?
- **Refuter:** show the weighting is a fixed constant; validate a context-independent list of critical junctures across residuals; or show the material outcome moves when the describing theory changes while the facts do not.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a universal design cookbook, not a list of critical junctures that work everywhere, not a percentage split such as "60 percent history."
- Not that history is noise around a deterministic core, and not that principles are rhetoric over a unique sequence.
- Not that a surviving institution is thereby explained. Settling is necessary and not sufficient (Fig 5 :69-70).
- Not that the six physical terms of Fig 13 are six institutional primitives. At this stratum the invariant is the loop and the naming rule; the currency and anchor change.
- Not that any actual country, reform or revolution has been settled. Nothing here is `grounded`.
- Not a second witness. This packet and its audit share an evidence pool with the figures and essays.

## 5. What would settle it

A material-outcome reading independent of any account of it. Concretely:

1. Read persistence, constraint, compliance and resulting power distribution per slot without using the historian's narrative as the instrument.
2. Separate the ordered selection event from the self-reinforcing loop and measure the loop's sign.
3. Apply the import test to a named critical juncture: source gate, provenance, and a stated gap it explains.
4. Test whether the weighting moves when outcomes arrive.
5. Run the six pre-registered falsifiers in audit section 5. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
