# Outreach draft — check the separator before deciding about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim, its mode labels and its refuters are intact. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one separator before you decide whether the framing is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim about a hard question in institutional analysis, and I would like you to check it against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question as posed is a false choice.** "Can institutional change be explained by general principles, or is it largely shaped by historically specific events?" treats the two as competing answers. The claim is that they are different quantities with different anchors, and that the allocation between them is learnable per case rather than a universal percentage.
>
> **The separator (`general` on its structure, `specified` on any application, not `grounded`):** general principles govern the kernel, that is the rule that a composing system keeps a loop and a rule for naming what worked while the actors, currency and anchor change. Historically specific events supply the landing conditions, the order of the bonds, and the imported atom that no closed set of principles can derive. A settled institution then becomes a building block for the next layer, which is what path dependence looks like from inside the framework. The framework's own weighting parameter leans on private history versus inherited prior, is learnable from outcomes, and is the reason two frames with identical architecture diverge.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that a fixed list of design principles explains institutional change everywhere; that a standing list of critical junctures works across cases; that the framework returns a number such as "60 percent history"; that any particular institution has been settled by this analysis. A particular event being critical is relative to the residual being tracked, and naming one is a provenanced binding, not a derivation.
>
> **What would refute the claim, written down before this message:**
> 1. deriving a specific dated event from a closed set of general principles with no import and no new residual;
> 2. finding two institutions with identical kernels, landing conditions, histories and weightings that nevertheless diverge;
> 3. validating a context-independent list of critical junctures across different residuals;
> 4. showing that the material outcome moves when the describing theory changes, while the underlying facts do not;
> 5. producing a figure-grounded derivation that selects a unique institutional form without an import.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - The eight-step formula and mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - Arrival of the Fittest: The Tether Architecture: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on an institutional case you know well and can check against a material outcome. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
