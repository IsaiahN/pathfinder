## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* The local figure text adds that the framework *"cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel* (opened directly): *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. In this question, the shape is: a kernel that recurs, a habitat that supplies the landing conditions, a bond order that carries the route, and a weighting that is learnable from outcomes. The name would be a particular design principle, a particular critical juncture, or a number such as "60 percent history." The framework returns the former, not the latter.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. *"With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."* The essay also gives the steering rule: *"books that you are returning in your searches need to be tested or ranged against the problem you want answered in order to steer the direction of the next search."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."*
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* (carried): *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."*
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: *"You will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place, as there is nowhere else to search and nothing else that can describe the answer better."* The paradox is that by then *"you have already composed the answer through the process of locating it."*

For institutional change, this means the derivation must start in effect terms: not "which theory is right," but "what would I expect to observe if regularities were kernel versus if the route were decisive." Only then is a frame selected. Fig 9 :28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."*

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays."* The README: the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."* For institutions, the ground is a material outcome, not a narrative.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test. Naming a critical juncture is exactly this: the event comes from the historical record, a different closure, and must be marked as borrowed.

*They're Scanning Books* (carried) sharpens this: *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space."* And: *"A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six."* And: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."*

So a list of "universal institutional design principles" is a visible guess elevated into an invisible one if its provenance is dropped. Fig 13 :130: *"A term carries the atoms it was built from and the bonds that joined them. That record is not documentation of the term. It is the term."*

## 4. The essay that most directly governs this question

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (opened directly this session) contains a passage that is almost an answer to INS-0006:

> *"You cannot recreate humanity. The exact lineage of events is lost. You would need to know every natural event and interaction with every other species, all the way back, and even then you would need the exact sequence. The combinatorial possibility exceeds exponential. Even if you had the rules, even if you had a computer that could run the simulation, you would not get the same result without the exact events playing out."*

Three things follow, all in `general` mode:

- Having the rules is not having the lineage. The general principles are the rules; the historically specific events are the exact sequence. Neither is reducible to the other.
- The route is not recoverable from the artefact. Fig 13 :129: *"Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available."* An institution you can observe does not tell you which of several possible histories produced it.
- What is owed is a record of the route, not a better guess from the outcome. The same essay: *"This essay is my provenance record."*

This is the strongest world-side corroboration of Fig 4 :34-35, and it comes from the framework's own author, so it carries high overlap with the figures and little independent witness weight. It is recorded because it is the governing reading, not because it is independent.

## 5. Why the "both are needed" answer is not a middle compromise

A reader might take the separator as a polite split the difference. It is not. The same essay names the structural reason a lineage cannot be regenerated from rules alone: the combinatorial space of sequences exceeds what any simulation can traverse, and the loss of detail at every level is enough to make distant things look unconnected. Fig 13 :31-32 says the remainder that re-enters the loop is where randomness comes from: *"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise."* Historical contingency is not an absence of law; it is what the recursion leaves behind when detail is discarded at a crossing.

So the answer is not "half principles, half events." It is: principles govern what is composable, the lineage governs what was composed, and the two are different objects with different anchors. A `specified` allocation is learnable per case; a `general` split is a category error.

## 6. Correction to my own §1 and §3 (see `06_isomers_and_order.md` Q1)

I initially wrote the shape-not-a-name rule as if it forbade naming any binding. That overstates it. The README's phrase is *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* The subject is a missing primitive, one not held in Γ. A design principle, a weighting or a juncture type is not a missing primitive; it is a binding from the institutional-theory frame onto atoms and bonds the framework already holds. Naming one is provenance work, not the invention of a seventh term.

What remains wrong in a naive account is different: calling such a binding a derivation, or claiming it moves the wall. Fig 6 :25: *"An import adds no atom to the world."* A binding from a historical frame gives a frame access; it does not add a primitive. Both the original overstatement and this narrowing are preserved so neither is quietly lost.

A second correction, same section: *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*'s point about contact applies to the *instrument* that would read outcomes independently of narratives (audit S4). It does not license treating the narrative itself as the ground. Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."*

## 7. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (opened directly): *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."* A list of famous institutional theorists assembled to agree with this separator is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask is a budget to try the claim against a material outcome.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of the institutional question and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on.
