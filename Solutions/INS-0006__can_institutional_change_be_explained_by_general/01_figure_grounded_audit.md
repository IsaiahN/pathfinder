## 0. The question under audit

> Can institutional change be explained by general principles, or is it largely shaped by historically specific events?

Restated as an effect: a reader is asked to choose between two explanatory currencies. One currency is the repeatable structure (the principle that would show up in any society, any century, any organisation). The other is the singular route (the particular sequence of events, the individual decision, the accident, the date). Choosing one determines what the reader will look for, what they will count as an explanation, and what they will be unable to see.

Two failure modes are already visible before any figure is read. If the answer is "general principles alone," a concrete trajectory has to be derived from principles that the framework itself says return a shape and not a name; that is an import with no source gate. If the answer is "specific events largely," the account becomes a narrative with no pre-registered failure condition, which the seat map calls an unfalsifiable claim with a clean provenance record. The residual is therefore not "which answer is right." It is: *what is the correct object of explanation, and which quantity is each candidate actually describing?*

The sought-for shape is bound as an ordinary term before the run (formula, "Direction"): **an answer would say which regularities are kernel and which are bindings, would name the condition under which each is owed, and would state what observation would reverse the allocation.** This makes the preference refusable by the same test as everything else.

## 1. Residual in effect terms (before choosing any figure line)

- **Arity.** The question presents two candidates, so it looks binary. It is not a two-item partition. Institutional change is a compound of at least three conditions that overlap in every real case: a compositional structure (what can be built from what), a historical route (which option was selected, in which order, under which conditions), and a ground (whether the resulting rule actually persists and does what it claims). Fig 9 :54 says *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
- **Symmetry.** The question's phrasing is symmetric: swapping "general principles" and "specific events" gives the same question. The explanation relation is not symmetric. Principles constrain the space of forms (a structure), while events select one form and carry it forward (an ordering plus a bond). Under a symmetric bond the operands would be interchangeable; here they are not. The Operators table identifies sequence as ordered with the test *"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."* So the two candidates cannot be averaged into a percentage without destroying the very relation being asked about.
- **Scale.** The general-principles claim lives at the invariant level. The specific-events claim lives at the level where a seed lands in conditions. Fig 10 :68: *"Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between."* The transform between the two levels is lossy, so a verdict obtained at one level cannot be read at the other without a round trip. Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

**Verdict on the framing:** the question as asked is a false dichotomy. It is a union of conditions with different anchors. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A global answer such as "institutions are mostly principles" or "mostly history" would throw away the only signal that says where to look.

## 2. Domain census (written before selecting figure lines)

One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor. No nameable anchor means it is not yet a domain.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1, the compositional frame** (what general principles govern) | the invariant loop, the rule for naming what worked, the reusable methods that cross a membrane | description length and reachability inside `closure(Γ)` | the figures as metalaws; internal consistency, plus the ground for any settlement | partition question: what composes across kinds and what cannot |
| **D2, the historical frame** (the habitat and the route) | specific events, critical junctures, actors, conditions, relations, the order they arrived in | causal influence, path dependence, increasing returns, the bond that carries the route | the observed trajectory in the record, but only when it can be read without updating on the theorist's own account | union: a case in two conditions, not a list of events |
| **D3, the outcome frame** (what actually happened) | implementations, compliance, persistence, distribution of power, material effects | whether the rule pays for itself at the slot | a material outcome that does not change because a theorist or historian re-describes it | per-slot verdict, never global |
| **D4, the verification frame** (the literature that answers the question) | researchers, theories, reviews, citations | agreement and residual | the ground, never agreement; low overlap between frames | mirror chain if the frames cite one another |

**No anchor means no domain.** D2 is admitted only after its anchor is named. A historian's narrative alone is not an anchor: if it moves when a new interpretation is published, it is a frame (Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*). The anchor for D2 is the *reconstructed sequence plus the material trace it left*, read separately from any single account of it. D3's anchor is the outcome itself. D1's anchor is the metalaw set for internal questions and the ground for settlement questions. D4's anchor is the ground, and this simulated run has no independent outside frame, so D4's independence is zero.

**Partition or union.** Union. D1 and D2 overlap in every real institution: the principle is instantiated only in a route, and the route is only describable using principles. D3 cuts across both, since the outcome is what settles a claim made in either. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* A single lever, choosing one answer, clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** The chain runs: 1 perception (what happened, in what order), 2 vocabulary (terms for what the event affords), 3 objective (what counts as institutional change here), 4 planning (which lever to pull), 5 learn and carry (what persists). Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* General principles occupy links 2 and 3; the historical record occupies link 1; the ground is what settles link 5. If link 1 is broken, principles applied at link 4 arrive at a step whose input never came: Fig 3 :23: *"It is a reading of nothing."* This is why "general principles explain institutional change" cannot be assessed before the sequence has been perceived. Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

## 4. Findings

### F1 — The either/or is a union, not a partition (decisive for the framing)

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*

Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*

Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

The two candidate explanations do not divide institutional change into two piles. Every real case contains a compositional structure, a route through a habitat, and an outcome that settles whether the rule held. Asking "which one explains it" is asking for a single lever to clear a region where three conditions bind. Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* A preference for elegant general theory or for rich narrative is a filter on the budget of attention, not a witness for either.

**Per-domain verdict:** D1 `specified`, D2 `specified`, D3 `specified` per slot. The union reading is `general` and triangulated across Fig 9, Fig 4 and Fig 11. **Refuter:** show that the three conditions are separable, that is, that an institution can have a structure with no route, or a route with no outcome that could settle anything.

### F2 — The framework has a general/specific parameter, and it is learnable

Symbols table, entry `w`: *"How far the belief leans on private history versus inherited prior."* And: *"w_A + w_B = 1, and w is learnable from the outcomes of past weightings."* And the decisive sentence: *"Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge."*

This is the closest the figures come to answering the question directly. It does not return a universal percentage. It returns a *mechanism*: the architecture (the general part) is shared, and divergence comes from history and from the weighting between private history and inherited prior. The weighting is itself learnable from outcomes, which means the general/specific allocation is a quantity a frame can update, not a doctrine it must declare.

**Mode:** `general` on the existence of the parameter, `specified` on its value in any given case, `gap` on the value itself. **Triangulated** with Fig 4 :19-20 (kernel invariant, cast variable) and Fig 10 :68 (levels are not copies). **Refuter:** a frame with identical architecture, identical history and identical weighting that nevertheless diverges would break this claim, since the framework would then have no parameter left to account for the divergence.

### F3 — The kernel recurs; the cast does not

Fig 4 :19-20: *"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."*

This is the figure-grounded core of the "general principles" answer. There is something that recurs at every scale and in every case: the loop, and the rule for naming what worked. What does not recur is the cast. Applied to institutions, the claim is not that a particular constitution or design principle is universal. It is that the *relation* between a rule, the ground it ranges against, and the record of what worked is universal. Naming a particular design principle as the universal is a binding, not a derivation (see F6 and the correction in `05_what_shape_means.md`).

Fig 4 :33: *"Which is why there is no top and no infinite regress."* So the general side of the question is licensed only at the level of the invariant loop, never at the level of a final institutional form. A "general theory of institutions" that claims to name the final form is a terminal room, which the framework forbids.

**Mode:** `general`. **Triangulated:** Fig 4, Fig 13 :115, the metalaws note. **Refuter:** exhibit a composing system at some stratum with no loop or no rule for naming what worked.

### F4 — A seed carries no small tree: the landing conditions decide the outcome

Fig 4 :34-35: *"The level a method lands on remakes it. A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."*

This is the figure-grounded core of the "historically specific events" answer. The general principle is the seed. It does not contain the tree. The habitat into which it lands (the prior institutions, the distribution of power, the order of events, the accident of timing) is part of what the thing becomes. This is not a rhetorical concession to history; it is the framework's rule about what crosses a scale membrane. Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* A recording of one occasion does not cross.

Applied to the question: "institutions are shaped by historical events" is true in the specific sense that the landing conditions are in the composition, not an external garnish. But it is not true in the sense that general principles are absent. The seed still carries what will grow one. Both halves are required.

**Mode:** `specified` on institutions, `general` on the seed rule. **Triangulated:** Fig 4, Fig 11 :66, Symbols `w`. **Refuter:** a case where the same seed in two different habitats produces the same institution with no difference in contact or conditions, and where the framework's own `w` cannot register a difference.

### F5 — The specific cannot be derived from the general by more search

Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."*

Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*

Fig 13 :115: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."*

A general-principles frame searching its own closure will never produce a particular event. It can enumerate arrangements forever and not arrive at the date, the decision or the accident, because those are atoms held in a different frame (the historical record, the habitat). Fig 13 :118: *"infinite effort inside a finite reach."* So the historical side of the question is not "another variable to add to the model." It is an import, and imports require a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :23, :24).

Fig 6 :24: *"Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."* A narrative that imports the wrong event is not a harmless enrichment; it is a wrong atom that degrades the frame's reach and its independence.

**Mode:** `general`. **Triangulated:** Fig 8, Fig 6, Fig 13. **Refuter:** derive a specific dated event from a closed set of general principles with no import and no residual. If that succeeds, closure is not idempotent and this finding fails.

### F6 — The framework returns a shape, not a name

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*

Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*

Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

Applied here: a figure-grounded derivation can say that an institution which composes at all must have a structure between a state of affairs and the rule that orders it, that the route is carried by a bond rather than by an ingredient list, and that the landing conditions are in the composition. It cannot say *which* design principle will prevail in a given country or *which* event will be critical. Naming one is a binding from the institutional-theory frame onto an atom the framework already holds, which is provenance, not a derivation. See the correction in `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6: the rule governs missing primitives, not the selection among atoms already held.

**Mode:** `general` limitation, `gap` on the particular. **Triangulated:** Fig 9, Fig 13, canonical figures README. **Refuter:** produce a figure-grounded derivation that selects a unique institutional form without an import.

### F7 — The route is carried by the bond, not the ingredients

Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*

Fig 12 :46: *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands. A recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself."*

Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."*

Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."*

This explains why two institutional accounts that list the same factors can disagree completely. One lists the ingredients of change (power, interests, ideas, shock); the other records the bonds (which event preceded which, which choice closed off alternatives, which rule was refused). The first cannot say what would count as getting somewhere; the second can. "Critical juncture" is not an extra ingredient. It is an ordered bond: the selection event that could have gone otherwise and after which return became harder. Drift and layering are different bonds over similar atoms.

**Mode:** `general` structural, `specified` when applied to a named institution. **Triangulated:** Fig 12, Fig 13 :129-130, Fig 3. **Refuter:** show two institutions with identical atom sets and identical bonds that behave differently with no difference in habitat or landing conditions.

### F8 — Path dependence is recursion, not a special substance

Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"*

Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*

An institution that has settled becomes an atom at the next level; the next layer composes over it. That is path dependence in the framework's own terms: not a mystical weight of the past, but the recursion rule applied to a settled composition. The "sign of the loop" decides whether the feedback closes (increasing returns, lock-in) or opens (reform, reversal). The handbook language of critical junctures and self-reinforcing sequences maps onto this without importing a new primitive.

**Mode:** `general`. **Triangulated:** Fig 12, Fig 13 :110-112, metalaws note on nested composition. **Refuter:** a settled rule that cannot be composed over, or a composed institution whose loop sign cannot in principle be determined.

### F9 — No standing list of historically critical events survives a change of residual

Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*

Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*

This is the boundary on the "historically specific events" answer. The fact that events matter does not license a catalog of events that are always critical. Whether a shock is a critical juncture or noise depends on the residual being tracked and on the system it hits. The same event is a reform opportunity for one residual and a reinforcement of entrenched power for another. Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* The list of conditions is read off the world; it is not assembled from principles.

**Mode:** `general`, with a `gap` on which events are critical in a given case. **Triangulated:** Fig 11, Fig 9 :53 (*"Nobody chooses. The gap does, and the gap is only as good as its description."*). **Refuter:** validate a context-independent list of critical junctures across residuals.

### F10 — The anchor for institutional change is the outcome, not the narrative

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*

Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."*

Fig 2 :34: *"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."*

The material outcome (did the rule persist, did it constrain the powerful actor, was it complied with, what distribution of power resulted) is the anchor. A scholarly narrative about the institution is a frame. If the narrative is used as the anchor, the arrangement becomes a mirror chain: Fig 7 :12-13: *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not."* Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*

This matters for the question because both candidate answers are theories about the world. Neither is settled by how many scholars cite it. Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."*

**Mode:** `specified`. **Triangulated:** Fig 2, Fig 7, seat map. **Refuter:** an outcome that changes when the describing account changes, with no change in the underlying material facts.

### F11 — Agreement between the two literatures is one pool if they share evidence

Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*

Fig 8 :50-51: *"But shared atoms are not the signature. Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence. Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*

Historical institutionalism and rational-choice institutionalism sometimes converge on the same account. That convergence is corroboration only if they reached it from separate evidence. If they read the same case studies and cite the same canonical works, their agreement is the pool talking. The same applies to this simulated derivation: the primary seat and the second seat share the figure text and the same public sources, so their agreement is internal consistency, not a second witness.

**Mode:** `general`. **Triangulated:** Fig 2, Fig 8, seat map law on correlation. **Refuter:** demonstrate independent evidence streams behind two apparently agreeing frames.

### F12 — The universal percentage is a genuine gap, not a hidden number

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*

Fig 13 :136-138: *"It can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands, so something has to be imported before it is asked again."*

No figure line returns a split between general principles and historical events. Symbols `w` says the weighting is learnable from outcomes, which means the split is case-specific and revisable rather than a constant the framework could assert. The honest report is therefore `gap` on the number, of the "shape not a name" kind, not a silence and not an invitation to invent a number. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

**Mode:** `gap`. **Refuter:** a figure line that governs the numerical allocation, or a ground measurement that settles it for a named institution.

### F13 — Settling is necessary and not sufficient for an institution to be composed over

Fig 5 :69-70: *"settling is necessary for a term to be composed over, and it is not sufficient."*

Fig 5 :71: *"So a system can hold terms it purchased and cannot use, and a library that grows is not a reach that grows."*

An institution can survive (settle) and yet be unavailable as a building block for the next arrangement, because how it composes is a separate fact. This blocks a common move in institutional argument: that persistence proves explanatory power. It does not. Fig 1 :24-27 disqualifies the frame-internal score: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Counting the survival of rules as evidence for a theory that predicted their survival is exactly that error.

**Mode:** `general`. **Triangulated:** Fig 5, Fig 1, Fig 12 :104. **Refuter:** a settled term that is always composable regardless of its arrangement.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the seat map: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* The shape claim advanced by this audit is: **general principles govern the kernel and the reach; historically specific events supply the landing conditions, the bond order and the imported atom; the allocation between them is learnable per case and is not a universal number.** That claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A closed set of general principles, with no import and no new residual, derives a specific dated event of an institutional trajectory. (Breaks F5, Fig 8 :32.)
2. Two institutions with identical kernels, identical landing conditions, identical histories and identical `w` diverge anyway. (Breaks F2, Symbols `w`.)
3. A context-independent standing list of critical junctures or design principles sufficient for institutional change is validated across residuals. (Breaks F9, Fig 11 :51.)
4. The material-outcome anchor moves when the describing theory changes, with no change in the underlying facts. (Breaks F10, Fig 2 :33.)
5. A figure-grounded derivation selects a unique institutional form without an import. (Breaks F6, Fig 9 :32.)
6. The general/specific weighting is shown to be a fixed constant across all frames and cases. (Breaks F2 and the `gap` in F12.)

If any fires, the derivation is withdrawn or re-censused. Until one is run, no part of the answer is `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. **Refuse the either/or as a partition.** Report D1, D2 and D3 separately and never average them into a percentage (F1, Fig 9 :60).
2. **State which level each claim lives at.** Kernel claims are `general`; binding claims are `specified` and need provenance; the number is `gap` (F2, F3, F12).
3. **Treat the event side as an import, not a variable.** Require a source gate and shadow test for any named critical juncture (F5, Fig 6 :24).
4. **Send the generator, not the conclusion.** Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* The generator here is the separator plus its refuter.
5. **Bring a characterised residual, never the source.** Fig 10 :52. The first contact states the residual and its refuter; the framework is offered only after a verdict is recorded.
6. **Declare the stake.** Fig 10 :59: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* The author of this derivation gains nothing from which answer holds, but the derivation prefers a figure-grounded resolution, and that preference is recorded rather than hidden.

## 8. Strains and re-census

- **S1, Fig 13 physical substrate versus institutional terms.** Live at first pass. Re-census: Fig 13 governs the physical minimum set; the institutional stratum inherits the *loop* and the *rule for naming what worked*, not the six physical atoms. Fig 4 :19-20 governs the invariance claim at a new scale. Different objects, so no contradiction. Kept visible because the temptation to name six "institutional primitives" is exactly the forbidden move.
- **S2, "structure constrains" versus "events select."** Apparent conflict dissolved by re-census: the two phrases govern different quantities. The structure sets the closure and the reach; the event selects which composition is actually instantiated and becomes an atom for the next level. Fig 12 :104 + Fig 6 :23. Low overlap between routes, so the agreement is weak but not circular inside the framework.
- **S3, `w` learnable versus no universal split.** Dissolved: `w` is per frame and learnable from outcomes; it is not a global constant. The `gap` in F12 is about the number, not about the existence of the parameter.
- **S4, anchor versus narrative.** Not dissolved. Kept as a live design constraint. The material outcome is the anchor, but in practice the outcome is usually reached through a record compiled by frames, so the channel can decay (Fig 10 :24). An instrument that would read outcomes independently of accounts is owed.
- **S5, path dependence applied to physical and social systems.** Re-census: Fig 12 recursion is stratum-agnostic; the *currency* of the loop changes (energy and settlement versus power and compliance). Fig 10 :68. Not strained after re-census, but the currency rename must be stated each time.

After re-census: one live constraint (S4), four dissolved strains, none hidden.

## 9. Verdict

The question as posed is a union of conditions, not a choice between two answers.
