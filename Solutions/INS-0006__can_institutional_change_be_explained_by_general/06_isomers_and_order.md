# Order, isomers and analogues in institutional change

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the bond definitions, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## RESIDUAL

The main audit establishes that general principles and historical events are different quantities with different anchors, and that the allocation is learnable per case. Three questions are still owed before that can be used:

1. What *kind* of relation is "an event selects a path"? Is it just another ingredient, or is it an ordered bond?
2. If two institutions are built from the same general atoms, what is the relation between them? Are they isomers, or merely members of a family?
3. Do low-overlap fields return the same shape, and if so, is that corroboration or a shared metaphor?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Does "shape, not a name" forbid naming a design principle or a juncture type? Correction to my own `05` sections 1 and 3

- The README: *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* The subject is a primitive not held in Γ.
- A design principle (boundaries, congruence, collective choice) and a juncture type are not missing primitives. They are bindings from the institutional-theory frame onto arrangements the loop can already express.
- ⇒ Naming one is not forbidden. What is forbidden is calling it a wall-moving import when it adds no atom (Fig 6 :25: *"An import adds no atom to the world."*) and claiming the framework derived a name it can only bind.
- **I overstated this earlier** in `05` sections 1 and 3. Retracted as applied to held atoms. Triangulated (README + Fig 13 :115).

### Q2 — What bond is "an event selects a path"?

- Operators table: sequence is ordered, with the test *"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."*
- A critical juncture is not an extra factor added to a list. It is the moment at which one of two or more institutional arrangements is selected, after which return becomes progressively harder. That is `→` sequence plus a threshold: alternatives exist before, and only one path continues after. Fig 12 :72: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened."*
- By contrast, path dependence itself is a **cycle**: Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Increasing returns close the loop; reform or reversal opens it.
- ⇒ "History matters" is not one claim. It is at least two different bond claims: the selection is an ordered sequence, and the persistence is a loop whose sign is empirically decidable. Conflating them is why the literature can say both that the initial choice is contingent and that the path is unavoidable. The figures do not assert either globally.
- **Falsifier (written before the run):** if the selection event and the persistence mechanism are shown to be one bond rather than two, the distinction fails; if the loop sign cannot in principle be determined, the path-dependence claim is a narrative, not a structure.

### Q3 — Are two institutions with the same general atoms isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- **Weak reading:** an inclusive and an extractive institution share only a family resemblance, the way GR and QM share only "state-structure-state" in the exemplar. By Fig 12's definition they are not isomers; they are members of a functional group (a rule that constrains a powerful actor, with different qualifiers).
- **Strong reading:** if both are written over the same atom set (a rule, a distribution of power, a compliance channel, a ground that does not update on the ruler's own report, a seat able to refuse), differing only in which bond connects which atoms, then they **are** isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different behaviour. This is the claim worth testing.
- World corroboration, secondary: Ostrom's design principles are *"well supported empirically"* (Cox et al. 2010, fetched directly) yet Principle 2 is explicitly *"congruence with local conditions."* General principles whose content includes local fitting are functional groups, not recipes. The authors also record the critique that the principles risk being treated as *"a magic bullet or institutional panacea."*
- **Falsifier:** if either institution can be written without an atom the other requires (for example one with no compliance channel at all, or one with no non-updating ground), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands instead.

### Q4 — Do the analogues corroborate, or are they borrowed metaphors?

- **Evolutionary biology.** Mahoney 2000 (search-indexed, reduced weight): *"path dependence characterizes specifically those historical sequences in which contingent events set into motion institutional patterns or event chains that have deterministic properties."* This is almost a restatement of the figure verdict in a different field: contingent events (the specific) set in motion deterministic properties (the general). Its independence is limited because institutional theory borrowed path dependence from economics and evolutionary biology, so the pool overlap is real.
- **Replay experiments.** The Science review (via MSU release, fetched directly) found *"a complex picture of evolutionary change in which both contingency and determinism are evident."* Losos's reframing is the sharpest corroboration: *"Why does convergence occur sometimes and not others?"* That is the same question as "when do general principles explain institutional change and when does the route dominate." Low overlap with institutional theory: microbes, anoles and the fossil record share no evidence pool with political science. Corroborates the *shape* only.
- **Animal behaviour.** Whiten et al. 1999 (search-indexed abstract, reduced weight): 39 behaviour patterns are customary or habitual in some chimpanzee communities and absent in others *"where ecological explanations have been discounted."* Same general learning capacity, different local traditions built by transmission. This corroborates the seed-and-landing-conditions shape (Fig 4 :34-35) without saying anything about parliaments or constitutions. Low overlap.
- **A genuine conflict, returned to census.** Thelen's critique, reported in the fetched Wikipedia map, says applications of QWERTY-style path dependence to politics are *"too contingent and too deterministic."* Read as a direct conflict it would flip the verdict. Re-census: the figures do not assert a fluke initial choice followed by an unavoidable path. The loop sign can change (Fig 12 :121) and the weighting is learnable (Symbols `w`). So Thelen's target is a specific misuse, not the framework. The verdict was not flipped; the source was narrowed to mechanism-shape corroboration.

### Q5 — What is the general/specific allocation, formally?

The Symbols table gives the framework's own parameter: *"How far the belief leans on private history versus inherited prior."* And: *"w_A + w_B = 1, and w is learnable from the outcomes of past weightings."* And: *"Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge."*

- `w` is not a universal constant. It is a per-frame quantity that can be updated from outcomes.
- Applying it to institutions: a reform body's belief leans partly on its own history (the specific route) and partly on inherited prior (the general principle). Two bodies with identical mandates can diverge because their histories and their weightings diverge.
- **No figure returns the value of `w` for a named institution.** That is the declared `gap` (audit F12), of the shape-not-a-name kind, reported as unreachable at this budget rather than as silence. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
- **Falsifier:** show that `w` is fixed across all frames and all cases, or that the value can be read off a figure line without a ground measurement.

### Q6 — Two currencies, not one

Fig 12 :125-126: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add. Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*

A general-principles explanation is cheap in bits (one rule covers many cases) and may be expensive in actions (checking whether it actually holds in a given case). A detailed historical narrative is expensive in bits and may cost one action to refute. Summing them is an error. This is why asking "which explanation is more powerful" has no answer without saying which currency is being counted. Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

## CONCLUSION

"Event selects a path" is not an ingredient. It is an ordered sequence (`→`) coupled to a loop whose sign decides whether the path closes. Over a fixed atom set, two institutions that differ only in arrangement are isomers under Fig 12's definition; over different atom sets they are a functional group. The lowest-overlap witnesses (replay experiments in evolution, chimpanzee local traditions) corroborate the *shape* that both the kernel and the route are needed, and they return the same open question the institutional literature returns: when does convergence occur and when does divergence? No figure line answers that for a named case. The `gap` is recorded.

This derivation rescues what is defensible in both candidate answers without inventing a seventh term: the principles are the seed, the events are the conditions and the bond order, and the allocation is learnable.
