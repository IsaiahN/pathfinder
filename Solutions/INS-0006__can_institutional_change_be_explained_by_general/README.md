## The question restated as an effect

Two candidate answers are offered: (A) institutional change follows general principles that would recur anywhere, or (B) it is largely shaped by historically specific events that would not recur. What each leads to: A licenses prediction, design and transfer, but if pressed past its reach it names a structure the framework says it cannot name and imports a history it does not hold. B preserves the particular, but if pressed past its reach it becomes a list of one-off narratives with no refuter and no way to say which account would fail. The derive-first move is to refuse the either/or as a partition, run a domain census, and keep the verdicts separate.

## What is bound as an ordinary term

The sought-for shape is written down before the run, per the formula's "Direction" rule: *an answer would be a statement of which regularities are kernel (recur at every scale and every case) and which are bindings (the particular lineage, the event order, the conditions the institution landed in), plus a stated rule for which is owed when the residual persists.* Binding this as an ordinary term lets it accrue a residual like anything else instead of remaining an invisible preference.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with this answer.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claimed numerical split such as "institutions are 60 percent history." No figure returns such a number, and Fig 9 :32 says *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **A room does not upgrade the label.** Seat Map: *"A room reaching a conclusion without the ground is agreement, and agreement among frames is a weighted average of influence. Adding people raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor."*
2. **Selecting for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation.** Seat Map: *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."*
3. **An outside frame that holds the interior stops being outside.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*

Legally: unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in and institutional submission venues avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
