**What this is:** a claim in a form a reasoning or institutions researcher can check. Mode, derivation, and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the verdict, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No strategy frequency was measured. This is a shape and a route to a ground test.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The reasoning loop and the rule for naming what worked are invariant across markets, schools, and courts. What changes is the actors, the currency, and the anchor, so which strategies become common is conditional on the setting. A strategy's value is relative to the residual in its habitat, not carried by the reasoner, and the same move can be a benefactor in one setting and a malfactor in another. The three settings form a union compound, not a partition, and must be split before their frequencies are compared. They are best read as functional groups over shared reasoning moves, with the setting's qualifier saying which member counts. The framework does not predict a frequency table; it gives the shape and the instruments that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal plus world-facing; checkable by reading Fig 4 and testing one setting

- **Statement:** the loop is invariant; the currency and anchor vary with the setting. Frequencies are conditional, forms are not.
- **Source, verbatim:** `Fig 4 :19-20` "The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." Supporting: `Fig 2 :29-30` "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."
- **What to check:** in a setting you know, name the ranking currency and the anchor. Do they differ from the neighbouring setting while the reasoning loop stays the same? Does the anchor move when participants push on it?
- **Refuter:** a form unavailable in one setting for a reason that is not an actor, currency, or anchor difference; or a setting whose metric moves when pushed yet still settles the question.

### C2 - world-facing; checkable against the researcher's own field

- **Statement:** a strategy's valence is relative to the residual in its habitat, so a ranking of strategies independent of setting is not available. The settings are distinct rooms, not one sample.
- **Source, verbatim:** `Fig 11 :39` "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." `Fig 11 :49` "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." `Fig 7 :35` "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
- **What to check:** find a move that is rewarded in your setting and penalised in another, with the same actor performing it. If none exists, the valence claim is weak for that move. Check whether pooling your setting with another would change what counts as a correct answer.
- **Refuter:** a single strategy retaining the same frequency and success criterion across markets, schools, and courts after each currency and anchor is matched, with the frames kept independent; or a single scoring rule that settles all three without changing what counts as correct.

### C3 - framework-internal structure

- **Statement:** the settings are functional groups over shared reasoning moves; the strong isomer reading is checkable but unmeasured. The derivation order is anchor, currency, vocabulary, frequency.
- **Source, verbatim:** `Fig 12 :124` "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." `Fig 12 :117` "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." `Fig 3 :20` "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."
- **What to check:** does your setting share a substructure such as observe, compare, decide with the others, differing only in the qualifier (precedent, price, rubric)? Does a frequency claim in your literature get made before the anchor is defined?
- **Refuter:** two settings with disjoint atom sets, so neither can express a move the other requires. That kills the functional-group and isomer readings and leaves only the disjoint-atom reading.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a frequency table, not an average over settings, not a ranking of reasoning strategies by quality independent of setting.
- Not that any named strategy is *the* strategy of a market, a school, or a court. That is a binding from the setting's record and needs provenance.
- Not that the settings use different logical forms. The functional-group reading says they share moves.
- Not that the loop changes with size. Fig 4 says it does not.
- Not that this is `grounded`. No measurement was taken.
- Not a claim that the figures decide empirical frequency. They govern shape and route only.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading per room, not a further argument.

1. **Anchor test:** name what settles the question in the setting and show it does not move when participants push on it.
2. **Currency test:** identify what the ranking rewards and show that the strategy persists because it pays there, not because it is a recording of one success (`Fig 4 :22` "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down.").
3. **Habitat enumeration:** list actors, conditions, and relations in contact with the residual before counting strategies (`Fig 11 :43` "The habitat is enumerated, never composed.").
4. **Independence:** keep the rooms' evidence pools separate (`Fig 2 :40` "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool").

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 4 :19-20, Fig 11 :39 and :49, Fig 7 :35, and Fig 12 :124. Four short passages.
2. Name the currency and anchor of your own setting. Say whether the anchor would move under pressure.
3. Give one example of a move rewarded in your setting and penalised in another, or say why none exists.
4. Apply the five pre-registered refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4. Send the failure. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
