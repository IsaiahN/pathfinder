## Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual line alone, before reading the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry, and scale; one line per domain with actors, currency, and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the unevenness of strategy frequency across settings. Arity: two-place at minimum (strategy, setting), three-place with the reasoner. Symmetry: the mapping is asymmetric, since a setting selects and rewards. Scale: one-agent reasoning and institutional population, with a membrane between them. Union, not partition, because the three named settings overlap.
- **D1, individual frame.** Actors: one reasoner with belief, action, observation, and a library. Currency: residual per slot. Anchor: what actually happened. Status: invariant kernel.
- **D2, institutional frame.** Actors: many agents, shared library, a ranking. Currency: what the ranking pays. Anchor: each setting's ground, legitimate only when the question is constitutively about it. Status: union compound; three rooms.
- **D3, habitat.** Actors: roles, rules, evidence standards, instruments. Currency: contact. Anchor: the ground, not the habitat. Status: valences relative to the residual.
- **D4, verification.** Actors: readers and claims. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Status: independence zero in this simulated run.

Order by dependency: anchor → currency → vocabulary → frequency. A frequency taken before the anchor is a reading below the break.

## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, the gap between what was sent up and what came back down. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed zero. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figures, chiefly Fig 4 :19-20 (kernel versus actors, currency, anchor), Fig 11 :39 and :49 (agent-and-habitat, valence relative to the residual), Fig 9 :4-5 (split the union), Fig 2 :29-30 (anchor test).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried through the folder: the loop is invariant, the distribution is setting-conditioned, the settings are three rooms, and the framework returns a shape.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete empirical claim of the form "strategy X is more common in setting Y because that setting rewards X."

**R_T is nonzero.** Three constraints held by the shorthand do not survive the trip back down:

- the **anchor test**. A frequency is only interpretable if the setting's anchor does not move when pushed. The shorthand does not carry the per-setting test; a concrete claim can silently use a movable metric.
- the **union split**. A pooled frequency over markets, schools, and courts discards the compound structure and treats three conditions as one sample.
- the **shape-not-a-name limit**. Naming X as *the* strategy of Y converts a binding from that setting's record into a derivation the figures did not make.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: the loop is invariant and the strategy set is selected by the habitat. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the formula as identical to the law set would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a recorded gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` states.
