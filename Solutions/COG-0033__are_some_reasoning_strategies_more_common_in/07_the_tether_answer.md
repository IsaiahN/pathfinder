# The Tether's answer: are some reasoning strategies more common in particular institutional settings?

**Mode: `specified` on the verdict, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a frequency table. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks whether the distribution of reasoning strategies is conditional on the setting or whether the same forms appear everywhere and only content changes. The settings named, markets, schools, and courts, are not a clean partition. They overlap, so this is a union compound with its own loop and its own grounds, not one hard question about "institutions."

## 2. The shape

1. **The loop is invariant; what varies is the actors, the currency, and the anchor.** `Fig 4 :19-20` "The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." So "universal reasoning" and "institution-specific reasoning" are not opposites. The loop is universal; the strategies minted and rewarded under it are conditional.
2. **A strategy's value is relative to the residual in its habitat.** `Fig 11 :39` "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." `Fig 11 :49` "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." The same move is a benefactor to one setting's residual and a malfactor to another's. There is no standing list of good strategies that survives a change of residual.
3. **Split before searching.** `Fig 9 :4-5` "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." `Fig 7 :35` "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Three settings are three rooms. Pooling them into one sample is a chain of mirrors.
4. **Each room needs its own anchor test.** `Fig 2 :33` "The anchor must not update." `Fig 2 :36` "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." A grade is an anchor for "did the student meet the standard" and only an instrument for "did the student learn". That decision is live and must be made before a school frequency is interpreted.
5. **Settings are functional groups over shared moves.** `Fig 12 :124` "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." `Fig 12 :117` "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." The stronger isomer reading is checkable but unmeasured here; the functional-group reading is the one that survives the census.
6. **The framework returns a shape, not a frequency.** `Fig 9 :32` "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Which strategies are actually more common in each setting is a ground question, answered by instruments in that setting, not by the figures.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any named strategy is *the* strategy of a market, a school, or a court. Naming is a binding from that setting's record and needs provenance.
- Not that the three settings use different logical forms. The functional-group reading says the opposite: shared moves, different arrangement.
- Not that the loop changes across settings. `Fig 4 :19-20` says it does not.
- Not a frequency table, not an average over settings, and not a ranking of reasoning strategies by quality independent of setting.
- Not `grounded`. No strategy frequency was measured in this run.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A ground reading per room, not a further argument. Concretely:

- **Anchor test:** for each setting, name what settles the question and check it does not move when participants push on it. A metric that moves has no anchor, and that room drops out of the frequency comparison.
- **Currency test:** identify what the setting's ranking actually rewards. A strategy is common where it pays, and `Fig 4 :22` "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." distinguishes a reusable method from a recording of one success.
- **Habitat enumeration:** list the actors, conditions, and relations in contact with the residual in that setting before counting strategies. `Fig 11 :43` "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."
- **Independence:** keep the rooms' evidence pools separate. `Fig 2 :40` "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool".
- **Standing refuters from the audit:** a single strategy retaining the same frequency and success criterion in all three settings after currencies and anchors are matched; a form unavailable for a non-actor, non-currency, non-anchor reason; a movable metric that still settles; disjoint atom sets; a single scoring rule that works everywhere without changing what counts as correct.

## 5. The honest stop

**Reachable in principle, not reached at this budget.** `Fig 13 :134` "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence," and `Fig 8 :39` "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." The framework can hand over the shape and the instruments. It cannot hand over the frequencies, because they are not in the closure of the figures. What remains is to take the shape into each room and let that room's ground settle it. The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> Yes, but with a split that matters. The reasoning loop (bet, observe, predict, name what worked) does not change from market to school to court. What changes is the actors, the currency, and the anchor, so a strategy's value is relative to the residual in that habitat rather than carried by the reasoner. The same move can be a benefactor in one setting and a malfactor in another. The question is therefore a union compound: three rooms with their own grounds, not one sample to be averaged. Markets, schools, and courts look like functional groups over shared reasoning moves, with the setting's qualifier saying which member counts. What the framework cannot give is the actual frequency of each strategy in each room; that needs an anchor test, a currency test, and a habitat enumeration in every setting. The shape, the refuters written in advance, and the route to the ground are offered; the frequency table is not.
