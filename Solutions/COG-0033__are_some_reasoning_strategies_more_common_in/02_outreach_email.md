# Outreach draft: check the shape before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the anchor decision for schools in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F9 is made explicit. The claim must survive a per-setting anchor test or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about reasoning in markets, schools, and courts

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question in reasoning and institutional settings, and I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual.** Are some reasoning strategies more common in particular institutional settings, such as markets, schools, or courts? Two readings are possible. Either the forms of reasoning are the same everywhere and only the content differs, or the loop is invariant while the strategies that get rewarded and transmitted vary with the setting.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The figure-derived shape is a split. The loop (bet, observe, predict, name what worked) and the rule for naming what worked do not change with size. What does change is the actors, the currency, and the anchor. A reasoning strategy is not carried unchanged by a person from one setting to another: a strategy's value is relative to the residual in the habitat where it is used, and capability is a property of the reasoner and the habitat together. So the honest answer is that forms are universal and frequencies are setting-conditioned, with a gap where the actual frequencies would go. That is a shape and a route to a measurement, not a frequency table.
>
> The question also has to be split before it is searched. Markets, schools, and courts are not three samples from one population; they are three rooms with different grounds, and a grade is an anchor only when the question is constitutively about the grade. I am not claiming a single scoring rule covers all three.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any named strategy is *the* strategy of a market, a school, or a court; that the framework predicts a number for how common a strategy is; that courts and markets reason with different logical forms; or that this is measured. Naming a strategy as characteristic of a setting is a binding from that setting's own record, and it needs provenance. The framework returns a kind of thing, never the answer itself.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before this message:**
> 1. one strategy keeps the same frequency and the same success criterion across markets, schools, and courts after each setting's currency and anchor are matched, with the three frames kept independent;
> 2. a reasoning form is unavailable in one setting for a reason that is not an actor, currency, or anchor difference;
> 3. a setting's ranking metric moves when participants push on it, yet the setting still settles the question;
> 4. two settings use disjoint reasoning atom sets, so that neither can express a move the other requires;
> 5. a single scoring rule settles strategy frequency in all three settings without changing what counts as a correct answer in any of them.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract or split the claim again. A one-line note of the form "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Precedent and Analogy in Legal Reasoning": https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/legal-reas-prec/
> - Mata et al., "Ecological Rationality: A Framework for Understanding and Aiding the Aging Decision Maker" (2012, open access): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3278722/
> - Gallagher, Mastrogiorgio and Petracca, "Economic Reasoning and Interaction in Socially Extended Market Institutions" (2019): https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.01856/full
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the figures and formula are public at https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures . Run them on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Residual and refuter first, theory last | `Fig 10 :52` "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Frames range against the ground separately. |
| Do not ask for agreement | `Fig 2 :35` "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." |
| Pre-registered refuter | "A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." |
| Mode stated on the output | "Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which." |
| Settings split, not pooled | `Fig 9 :4-5` "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." |
| Frequency table withheld | `Fig 9 :32` "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself" |
| Strategy names are bindings, not derivations | Source gate and shadow test apply to imports; provenance is what separates a convergent derivation from an adopted import. |

| Later agreement is never pooled | `Fig 2 :40` "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool" |
