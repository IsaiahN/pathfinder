# Are institutional reasoning practices isomers? Structure, order, analogues

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The main audit concludes that the loop is invariant and the strategy distribution is setting-conditioned. Two supporting questions are owed. (1) Can the reasoning practices of markets, schools, and courts be written as different arrangements over the same stock of reasoning moves, so that Fig 12's structure claims apply? ## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Is a setting an isomer or a functional group?

`Fig 12 :117` "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

`Fig 12 :124` "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Three readings are possible:

1. **Strong isomer reading.** All three settings are written over the full shared atom set and differ only in which bonds connect which moves. Then they are isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different practice.
2. **Functional-group reading.** The three settings share a substructure, for example `observe → compare → decide`, that fixes what kind of reasoning counts. The qualifier (precedent, price, rubric) says which member. This is the weaker, safer claim.
3. **Disjoint-atom reading.** The settings share no substructure and use different moves. Then neither Fig 12 claim applies.

The exemplar's own re-census rule applies here: a figure that only fits with a strain governs another quantity. The strong reading strains when a setting's reputed practice turns out to need an atom the others do not. The functional-group reading is the one that survives a genuine census of the three rooms. It is `specified`, not `grounded`.

### Q2 - What is the bond difference, in effect terms?

- A court chains observation to comparison with a prior case (`observe → ⋛ precedent`) and then to subtraction, the practice of distinguishing (`−` the material fact). This is captured in the SEP entry: precedent and analogy are central forms of reasoning in legal systems.
- A market chains perception of a price signal to a bet and then to an observation of clearing, with a minted heuristic retained when it paid. The Frontiers paper describes prices as offloading devices and economic reasoning as scaffolded by the institution.
- A school chains observation to comparison with a standard (`⋛`) and then to a grade, with the measured retention of a strategy depending on what the rubric rewards. The longitudinal education study reports that different disciplines teach different kinds of reasoning to different degrees.
- Same atoms, different bonds. The arrangement, not the ingredient list, is the substance.

### Q3 - What order is the derivation?

`Fig 3 :20` "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."

The order is anchor → currency → vocabulary → frequency:

1. **Anchor.** `Fig 2 :33` "The anchor must not update." Ask what settles the question in this setting, and check that it does not move when participants push on it.
2. **Currency.** `Fig 4 :20` "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." Ask what the setting's ranking rewards.
3. **Vocabulary.** Enumerate the habitat. `Fig 11 :43` "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." List actors, conditions, and relations in contact with the residual, then outward until the cascade stops mattering.
4. **Frequency.** Only now ask which strategies are common. `Fig 3 :22-23` "a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing."

A frequency measured at step 4 before steps 1 to 3 is not a weak measurement; it is a measurement of nothing, because it does not know what it is counting against.

### Q4 - Why the settings cannot just be averaged

`Fig 7 :36` "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."

`Fig 9 :54` "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."

Markets, schools, and courts overlap, so they form a union rather than a partition. A compound case in two settings is "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound" (`Fig 9 :57-58`). Averaging their strategy frequencies would dissolve the compound back into a mixture and lose the only thing that made it describable.

## FALSIFIER, WRITTEN BEFORE THE RUN

The isomer and functional-group claim fails if either of the following holds:

1. Two settings can be written without an atom the other requires, so no shared substructure exists. Then the disjoint-atom reading stands and the Fig 12 claims are refused for this question.
2. A single bond and a single scoring rule reproduce the strategy distribution in all three settings without changing what counts as a correct answer in any of them. Then the settings are one room and the bond-difference claim is refused.

If either fires, `07_the_tether_answer.md` is revised and the refutation is copied into `11_audit.md` with provenance.

## CONCLUSION

The strongest defensible form is the **functional-group** reading: the three settings share an `observe, compare, decide` substructure, and the qualifier supplied by the setting (precedent, price, standard) says which member. The stronger **isomer** reading, over a full shared atom set, is a real and checkable claim but requires a census of each room's atom set that this simulated run did not perform. The derivation order is anchor, currency, vocabulary, frequency, and a frequency taken out of that order is a reading of nothing.
