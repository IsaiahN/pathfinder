## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"How do stellar flares affect atmospheres and habitability?" is a union compound, not one question. It splits into at least six slots: upper-atmosphere heating and ionization, ozone and NOx chemistry, thermal and non-thermal escape, surface UV and particle dose, prebiotic chemistry yield, and persistence of surface liquid water. The residual is indexed by slot, not aggregated, so no single "flare effect" number is admissible. A flare's valence is relative to each slot: a malfactor for ozone retention, a potential benefactor for UV-driven prebiotic chemistry. That opposition is a threshold comparison, not a sum. The causal chain is a legal cross-kind composition of energy and a gradient into a flow, and its dependency order is stellar deposition, then atmospheric response, then surface habitability. The middle link is where a refuter should check first. Each slot needs its own anchor, and agreement among models sharing a photochemical code is one evidence pool, not corroboration.

### C2 — physics-facing; checkable by a specialist against the literature

- **Statement:** the benefactor and malfactor results are answers to different slots, not conflicting verdicts on one question. Caldiroli et al. (A&A 2025) find flaring *"does not significantly affect the atmospheric retention of close-in planets"* for their modelled systems; Tilley et al. (Astrobiology 2019) find the ozone column *"can be depleted by 94% in 10 years"* for an unmagnetized Earth-like planet; Segura et al. (Astrobiology 2010) find *"flares may therefore not present a direct hazard for life on the surface."* GJ 273 (A&A 2020) states *"the right balance of flaring, not too much and not too little."*
- **What to check:** are those characterizations faithful? Is the "right balance" a threshold relation between two quantities, as the Operators table defines comparison (*"a threshold relation between two quantities"*), or is it an additive quantity? Does atmospheric depth change the sign, as Yamashiki et al. (ApJ 2019) report when 1 bar is protective and 0.1 bar is near-fatal?
- **Refuter:** show that the beneficial UV chemistry and the harmful escape or ozone loss combine additively in one measured quantity with no threshold; or show that atmospheric depth does not change the sign of the flare effect for the same stellar spectrum.

- **Statement:** the chain is a legal cross-kind composition, `energy(state) → gradient(structure) → flow`, and the dependency order is deposition, atmospheric response, surface habitability. Fig 13 :76, **"Energy and a gradient compose a flow."** Fig 13 :74, **"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."** Fig 3 :20, **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."** Fig 3 :27, **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. - **Refuter:** demonstrate escape with no gradient term; or produce a habitability verdict that does not consume a measured atmospheric-response value. Either breaks the composition or the dependency order.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: the spectrometer resolved the metamers where argument could not.

1. A time-resolved upper-atmosphere temperature and electron-density profile during a flare, separating model from measurement.
2. An ozone column measured across a proton event, with a spectrum that discriminates UV photolysis from particle-driven NOx production.
3. An escaping-ion flux measured at the planet, separating quiescent XUV escape from flare-driven escape, with the quantity (absolute or relative) stated.
4. A surface or near-surface dose measurement with atmospheric depth measured at the same time.
5. A laboratory prebiotic yield under the measured flare spectrum, not an assumed blackbody.
