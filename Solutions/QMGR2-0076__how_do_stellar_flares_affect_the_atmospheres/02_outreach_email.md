**Subject:** Check one shape claim about M-dwarf flares before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "how do stellar flares affect atmospheres and habitability?" is not one question. It is a union of at least six slots: upper-atmosphere heating and ionization, ozone and NOx chemistry, thermal and non-thermal escape, surface UV and particle dose, prebiotic chemistry yield, and persistence of surface liquid water. The framework's rule is that a residual is indexed by slot, not aggregated. A single "flare effect on habitability" number averages away the live signal.
>
> The second part of the output: a flare is an actor whose valence is relative to the residual. For the ozone column, an associated proton event is a malfactor. For prebiotic chemistry, the same flare's UV can be a benefactor. The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another. So "do flares help or hurt habitability" is the wrong question in effect terms. What is owed is a threshold comparison per slot, not a sum.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that flares sterilize red-dwarf planets, that they are required for life, or that any particular planet is habitable. Those are physical verdicts for an instrument to settle. The framework returns a shape, not a name. It cannot say which mechanism dominates for a specific planet. I am also not claiming this is a theory of everything. There is no last room.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. one scalar flare effect is shown to predict measured outcomes across all six slots with no live per-slot residual;
> 2. the same flare is shown to have identical valence for atmospheric retention and for prebiotic chemistry;
> 3. atmospheric response is fully accounted for at first order with no measurable cascade into surface dose or water loss;
> 4. a measured column, escape rate or surface dose is shown to move when the modelling frame changes.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The verbatim figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/docs/01-FOUNDATIONS/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md
> - Figures and formula for direct download (SVG, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
