## What in the wider literature bears on this question

1. **The apparent disagreement is a union, not a contradiction.** Caldiroli et al. (thermal escape of primordial H/He), Tilley et al. (ozone under repeated proton events) and Segura et al. (single-flare surface dose) answer different slots. Fig 9 :5: *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* Do not average them into a single answer.
2. **The "right balance" line is the strongest corroboration of the shape.** GJ 273's *"not too much and not too little"* is a threshold relation, and the Operators table defines comparison as *"a threshold relation between two quantities."* It corroborates F2 without supplying a verdict for a specific planet.
3. **The two orderings are opposite.** Caldiroli et al. report greatest absolute mass-loss enhancement at closest separation but greatest relative enhancement at larger separation. This is Fig 1 :19 in a real dataset: the slot decides the ordering.
4. **Atmosphere depth itself is the gate.** Yamashiki et al. find 1 bar protective and 0.1 bar near-fatal. This is the habitat-contact rule: the same actor's valence changes with the state of the atmosphere, consistent with Fig 11 :49.
5. **The essays are not independent evidence.** They are by the framework's author and share an evidence pool with the figures. They explain method and limits; they do not corroborate a physical verdict.
