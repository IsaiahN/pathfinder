# The Tether's answer: how do stellar flares affect the atmospheres and habitability of planets orbiting red dwarfs?

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that flares strip atmospheres on every red-dwarf planet. Caldiroli et al. (2025) find flaring *"does not significantly affect the atmospheric retention of close-in planets"* for their modeled systems.
- Not that flares are harmless. Tilley et al. (2019) find the ozone column *"can be depleted by 94% in 10 years"* for an unmagnetized Earth-like planet.
- Not that flares are required for life, nor that they preclude it. GJ 273 and Yamashiki et al. give the two sides of the threshold; Tilley et al. add *"this does not preclude habitability, as a safe haven for life could still exist below an ocean surface."*
- Not a verdict on Proxima b, TRAPPIST-1 e or any named planet. That requires a measurement at that planet.
- Not a theory of everything. Fig 7 :6 names *"the limit: no last room."*
- Not `grounded`. The framework returns a shape; naming a dominant mechanism is a provenanced binding from the astrophysics frame, not a derivation (Fig 9 :32, *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*).

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a spectrometer, not a debate, distinguished metamers. Here:

- **Slot 1, heating and ionization:** a time-resolved measurement of upper-atmosphere temperature and electron density during a flare, not a model profile.
- **Slot 2, ozone and NOx:** a measured column before and after a proton event, with a spectrum that discriminates UV photolysis from particle-driven NOx production. Without that discriminator the source is unnamed (metamerism).
- **Slot 3, escape:** an escaping-ion flux measured at the planet, separating quiescent XUV-driven escape from flare-driven escape. Caldiroli et al. show that absolute and relative enhancements order planets differently, so the choice of quantity is part of the measurement.
- **Slot 4, surface dose:** a surface or near-surface dosimeter, with atmospheric depth measured at the same time. Yamashiki et al. show 1 bar protective and 0.1 bar near-fatal, so depth is not a free parameter.
- **Slot 5, prebiotic chemistry:** a laboratory yield under a measured flare spectrum, not an assumed 9000 K blackbody.
- **Slot 6, water:** a measurement of water loss or retention over a baseline long enough to separate a flare contribution from quiescent evolution.
- **Standing falsifiers:** derive one of the six substrate terms from the other five; admit a direct two-state composition that cannot be refused; show one scalar predicts all six slots; show valence is intrinsic; show a measured anchor moves when the modelling frame changes.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that stellar flares make red-dwarf planets uninhabitable, and it does not say they are good for life. It says the question as posed is a bundle, not one question: six slots must be checked separately, from upper-atmosphere ionization through ozone chemistry and escape to surface dose and prebiotic chemistry. A flare is an actor whose sign depends on which slot you are measuring. For ozone and atmospheric retention it can be a malfactor; for UV-driven prebiotic chemistry it can be a benefactor. That opposition is a threshold comparison, not a sum, and the causal chain is a legal composition of flare energy and an atmospheric gradient into an outflow, with no new primitive required. The framework returns that shape and a list of falsifiers written in advance, not a verdict about any particular planet. Each slot stays open until an instrument settles it. Prestige and agreement among models are not such an instrument.
