# Outreach draft - check the separator before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the four domains and the `gap` in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are stated plainly in your own words. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** One separator to check before you decide whether the trade-off claim is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on each domain, `general` on the structure, `gap` on the aggregate; not `grounded`):** the question "do global supply chains make economies more efficient but more vulnerable" bundles two conditions with different anchors. Efficiency is read against a market or a measured cost. Disruption exposure is read against a realized loss and a recovery outcome. Fig 12 puts them in two currencies that do not add. So the honest output is not a net percentage. It is a per-slot separator: which actor, which link, which anchor, which currency, and what would have to be measured before a net verdict is even well posed.
>
> Three things follow, and I would like you to check each rather than accept any:
> 1. **"Global" changes the habitat.** New cross-border actors and contact are added; the habitat cannot be subtracted. A cost improvement that does not change contact with a disruption ground has not changed the vulnerability signal.
> 2. **A link's valence flips with the residual.** A single low-cost source is a benefactor under a cost residual and a chokepoint under a disruption residual. There is no standing list of good suppliers.
> 3. **Efficient and resilient configurations over the same actor set are isomers.** Same ingredients, different bond, different substance. This is a type question at least as much as a degree question.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that there is a universal trade-off coefficient; that global chains are *always* more efficient or *always* more fragile; that one net number for "the economy" exists; or that any of this is settled by experiment. No figure line returns that number. I record it as `gap`, which is a verdict and not silence.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. One instrument measures both effects in one currency against one anchor, with a stable exchange rate across slots.
> 2. A global network adds contact without changing exposure to any shock at any slot, under an anchor that does not update on its own efficiency reports.
> 3. Over a fixed actor set, no change of bond changes both cost and exposure.
> 4. An instrument returns a net per-economy verdict with a non-updating ground and the two effects trend together.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, the separator is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.

>

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Stage boundaries

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual, the four refuters and the shape claim. Ask what they would count as a failure. Record their verdict before showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, send the three local framework files. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because they have now seen the interior (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a generator, with the instruction that each user names a ground and writes a refuter before running it.

If a reply contains a real refutation, copy it into `11_audit.md` with provenance and re-evaluate it there as if it came from any other source. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
