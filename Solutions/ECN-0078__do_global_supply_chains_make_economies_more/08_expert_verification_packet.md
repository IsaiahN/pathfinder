# Expert verification packet - the Tether on global supply chains

**What this is:** a claim in a form a supply chain researcher or operations economist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on each per-domain sub-claim, `general` on the structural rules, `gap` on the aggregate verdict. Not `grounded`.** No disruption dataset was measured. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question is a union of two conditions with a shared habitat, not one dial with two signs. Efficiency is a comparison against a paying market; disruption exposure is a comparison against a realized loss and recovery. The figures hold them in two currencies that do not add, so no net exchange rate is licensed and no figure returns a verdict for "economies" as one aggregate. "Global" changes the habitat by adding cross-border contact that cannot be subtracted, and a link's valence flips with the residual: the same low-cost source is a benefactor under a cost residual and a chokepoint under a disruption residual. Over a fixed set of actors, lean and resilient configurations are isomers: same ingredients, different bond, different substance. What is offered is a per-slot separator, four failure conditions written in advance, and the instrument that would be needed to name a winner.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal and world-facing; checkable by reading Fig 9 and any shock dataset

- **Statement:** the two effects form a union of overlapping conditions, so verdicts must be kept per domain and never averaged into a net percentage.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 - *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57 - *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*. Fig 12 :130 - *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it. A price in bits and a price in actions are two readings, held apart."*
- **What to check:** does any dataset you know return a stable exchange rate between, say, a unit-cost saving and a recovery-time increase at the same slot? If yes, the union reading is weakened.
- **Refuter:** a single instrument measures both effects in one currency against one anchor with a stable cross-slot exchange rate.

### C2 - operations-facing; checkable against sourcing practice

- **Statement:** a link's valence is relative to the residual, and over a fixed actor set the lean and resilient configurations are isomers.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49 - *"valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :5 - *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
- **What to check:** take one real chain. Hold suppliers, routes, inventory and capacity fixed. Change only the bonds (single to dual source, zero to positive buffer, just-in-time to reserved capacity). Do cost and exposure both change? Then take a case where a genuinely new supplier was added. If only those cases change exposure, the isomer claim fails and the import claim stands.
- **Refuter:** no change of bond over a fixed actor set changes both readings, or every observed change requires a new actor.

### C3 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Fig 11, Fig 12 and Fig 4

- **Statement:** "global" changes the habitat, contact cascades past the first tier, and the firm-to-economy round trip is lossy.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :61-62 - *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."* Fig 11 :52-53 - *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* Fig 4 :30 - *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*
- **What to check:** did any disruption you know register first at a second- or third-tier supplier while first-tier enumeration looked clean? What was lost when a firm-level result was quoted as an economy-level result?
- **Refuter:** a global network adds contact without changing exposure at any shock class, or first-tier enumeration fully predicts realized loss, or a carried-up and carried-back-down firm result matches exactly.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a net efficiency-versus-vulnerability coefficient, and not a universal yes or no.
- Not that every global chain is fragile or every local chain is resilient.
- Not that redundancy is free; Fig 12 :125 - *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*
- Not that global participation always beats regional participation. Review of World Economics 2025 finds the significant TFP gain at the regional level in its sample; the "global" qualifier is not interchangeable.
- Not that complexity alone sets stability. May 1972 and Allesina and Tang 2012 disagree, and both were re-censused rather than used as verdicts.
- Not a named winner among sourcing arrangements. The figures return a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32 - *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer, not an argument, resolved it. Here:

1. Does a shock at tier 3 produce a loss that first-tier enumeration missed?
2. Does changing only the bond, with actors fixed, change both cost and exposure?
3. Does the channel to the disruption ground stay open while the cost metric saturates (Fig 10 :24, *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*)?
4. After the firm-to-economy round trip, what is `R_T`?

The four standing refuters in `01` §4 and the one-paragraph version in `07` §7 travel with this packet.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :55-57, Fig 11 :49/:61-62 and Fig 12 :117/:125-130, six short passages.
2. Check C2 against one chain you know: hold the actors fixed, change only the bonds, and say whether both readings move.
3. Name one shock class for which first-tier enumeration would miss the material loss, or say why none exists in your domain.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
