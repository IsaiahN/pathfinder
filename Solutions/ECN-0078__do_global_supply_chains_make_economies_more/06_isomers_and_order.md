# Union, isomer and bond order in the supply chain question

**Mode: `specified` on the structure, `general` on the bond rules, not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks whether one intervention produces two effects. Before answering, we must decide whether the two effects form a partition (separate questions with separate answers) or a union of overlapping conditions sharing one habitat. We must also decide what relation holds between a lean configuration and a resilient configuration over the same physical actors: the same substance in different degree, or a different substance with the same ingredients.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Actors held: suppliers, lead firm, logistics links, inventory, capacity, contracts, shocks. Bonds held: the seven, in particular → sequence, ⇒ production, − subtraction, ⋛ comparison, + conjunction; plus ¬ negation. Currency: cost (bits) and settlement actions. Anchors: a paying market for D1, a realized disruption and recovery outcome for D2, observed topology for D3. D4 has no common anchor and is unbundled.

Order by dependency: set the habitat (Fig 11), read each effect per slot (Fig 1 :19), then compose, then attempt the aggregate and refuse it (Fig 9 :54).

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Partition or union?

- Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*.
- Both effects are produced by the same network rearrangement. A single lever that clears the cost condition leaves the exposure condition standing, because the second still binds. Therefore **union**, and the overlap (D3, the habitat) needs its own loop.
- World check: World Bank 2023 reports reduced domestic-shock vulnerability and increased external-shock sensitivity from the same integration. Two signs from one intervention, so a partition would misdescribe the case.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a lever is demonstrated that improves cost without changing any exposure measure at any shock class, the union reading fails and the two effects are separable into a partition. **Verdict: union, `specified`, triangulated (Fig 9 plus the World Bank source).**

### Q2 - Are lean and resilient configurations isomers?

- Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
- Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."*
- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Take a fixed set of suppliers, routes, inventory lots, capacity reservations and contracts. Configuration A bonds them as single source, zero buffer, just-in-time sequence. Configuration B bonds the same set as dual source, positive buffer, reserved capacity. The ingredient lists are identical; the bonds are not. Under Fig 12 these are **isomers**, not different amounts of one substance. Fig 13 :121: *"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."* The converse also holds: the same ingredients under a different bond are a different substance.
- This is a **type** claim, not a degree claim. Ivanova 2025 frames the question as *"if a supply chain can be both efficient and resilient"* and looks for a tipping point; Lücker et al. 2025 separate dual-purpose from dedicated levers. Both are bond questions in Fig 12 language.
- **The fixed-set condition is load bearing.** If a configuration requires a genuinely new supplier, route or capability, the relation is composition plus import, not isomerism. Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 6 :24: *"A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."* The WTO 2025 report's call for *"sufficient redundancy"* is an introduction, not a re-bonding of the same atoms, so it falls under Fig 11 :61-62 rather than under the isomer claim. **This condition is recorded as a live strain, not hidden.**
- **Falsifier:** if every observed change from lean to resilient requires at least one new actor that was not in the original set, the isomer claim fails and only the import claim stands. **Verdict: isomer `specified` conditional on a fixed actor set; single-source on the figure definition.**

### Q3 - What is the bond order?

- Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* Operators :52: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one."*
- "More efficient" reads as ⋛ comparison against a cost or output quantity (Fig 12 :87: *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*). "More vulnerable" also reads as ⋛ against a loss or recovery quantity. These are two comparisons, not one symmetric bond joining two instances of a thing. The word "but" in the question is a rhetorical contrast; it is not a figure-licensed operator.
- **→ sequence** appears inside the chain: one stage precedes another. Fig 12 :72: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*. The bullwhip effect (Lee, Padmanabhan and Whang 1997) shows that a pure → chain cannot carry the feedback that amplifies the order variance. Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* A linear supplier list is a chain; a chain cannot close by construction, so it is the wrong notation for the cascade.
- **− subtraction** appears in the buffer: a buffer is capacity held back from current use. Fig 12 :82: *"A with B removed or absent"*. Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."* A maintained buffer is a catalyst; a stockpile that is spent once is a spent part. The ingredient list records neither.
- **¬ negation** is needed for the refusal: "this source is no longer viable." Operators :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*. A sourcing vocabulary with only + and → can record additions and sequences, and cannot record that a link has been refused. This is why a chain that only accumulates suppliers looks resilient on paper.
- **Verdict:** the two effects are ordered comparisons; the chain needs a cycle and a negation to carry the cascade and the refusal; the configuration difference is an isomeric bond difference. `general` on the operator rules, `specified` on their application.

### Q4 - Does the aggregate compose?

- Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"*.
- A firm-level efficiency result is settled against a market many times over. A firm-level exposure result is settled against disruptions, less often and with thin samples. Both may become atoms at the economy scale. Whether an economy-level composition over them is legal depends on whether the two settled molecules can serve as operands of one operator against one ground. Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*
- Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* Carrying the firm-level shorthand up to "economies" and back down to a policy loses the per-slot indexing and the two-currency separation. The round trip gap is nonzero (see `11_audit.md`).
- **Falsifier:** produce one ground at the economy scale that pays for both readings simultaneously, with a measured `R_T`, and the aggregate composes. Until then it does not. **Verdict: `gap`, unreachable at this budget, not silence (Fig 13 :134).**

## CONCLUSION

The question is a union of overlapping conditions in a changed habitat, not a partition and not one dial. Over a fixed set of sourcing atoms, lean and resilient configurations are isomers: same ingredients, different bond, different substance. The supporting derivation needs a cycle (the cascade), a subtraction (the buffer) and a negation (the refusal), none of which a flat supplier list can express. The firm-level results may settle and become atoms at the economy scale, but no figure composes them into a net verdict, and the round trip from firm to economy is lossy. The isomer claim is conditional on a fixed actor set; where a new source is introduced the relation is import, and that strain is named.
