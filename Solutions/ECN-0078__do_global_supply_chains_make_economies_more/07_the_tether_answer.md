# The Tether's answer on global supply chains, efficiency and disruption

**Mode: `specified` on each per-domain verdict, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the aggregate "economies" verdict. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a separator, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Global supply chain integration lowers delivered costs and raises measured productivity where a market pays, and the same rearrangement changes the paths along which a shock travels. The question packages these as "efficient but vulnerable" and asks for one verdict about "economies." The residual is the unstated coupling: what is the relation between the two effects, at which slot, against which anchor, and can they compose into a net economy verdict. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*

## 2. The shape, per domain

**D1, efficiency.** Read against a paying market or a measured cost. Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* The effect is per slot (Fig 1 :19, *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated"*). It is a bit-price: a cheap description of how inputs become outputs (Fig 12 :126). WTO 2021 and World Bank 2023 corroborate it; Review of World Economics 2025 narrows the specifically global part. Verdict `specified`, triangulated. **Not averaged with D2.**

**D2, disruption exposure.** Read against a realized loss and recovery outcome, an anchor that does not update on the chain's own efficiency reports. It is an action-price: what it costs to settle the arrangement under a shock (Fig 12 :126). The same link that is a benefactor under a cost residual is a chokepoint under a disruption residual (Fig 11 :49, *"valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries"*). UNCTAD 2020, Ivanova 2025, Lücker et al. 2025 and Albert et al. 2000 corroborate. Verdict `specified`, triangulated. **Not averaged with D1.**

**D3, the habitat.** "Global" is not a label on the same local chain. It adds cross-border actors and contact, and the habitat cannot be subtracted (Fig 11 :61-62: *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."*). Capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the firm alone (Fig 11 :39). Contact cascades past the first tier (Fig 11 :52-53). Over a fixed set of actors, lean and resilient configurations are Fig 12 :117 isomers: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance."* Verdict `general` on the rules, `specified` on the application, triangulated across Fig 11, Fig 12 and the network physics source.

**D4, the aggregate.** No figure and no opened source returns a net economy verdict. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*. The two currencies are held apart (Fig 12 :130: *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it"*), and the firm-to-economy transform is lossy (Fig 4 :30). Verdict `gap`.

## 3. So, yes or no?

The honest answer has the shape of a separator, not a yes or a no.

- **Yes, with conditions:** global chains make the measured cost lower where a market is the anchor and the slot is named; and they change exposure, usually increasing sensitivity to external shocks while sometimes reducing sensitivity to domestic ones (World Bank 2023).
- **No, as stated:** there is no single dial on which efficiency goes up and vulnerability goes up, no universal exchange rate between them, and no economy-wide net verdict that the figures license. "But" is a rhetorical contrast, not a bond.
- **The stable core:** a global chain is a habitat whose contact has been extended. Every cost saving and every new exposure are properties of that habitat, read per slot against different anchors. Verdicts stay separate and are never averaged.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that all global chains are fragile, or that all local chains are resilient. Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :50).
- Not that redundancy is free. Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*
- Not that "more global" is "more efficient." Review of World Economics 2025 finds the significant gain at the regional level, not the global level, in its sample.
- Not that complexity alone decides stability. May 1972 and Allesina and Tang 2012 disagree, and both were re-censused rather than used as verdicts.
- Not a net percentage for an economy, and not a named winner among sourcing arrangements (Fig 9 :32).
- Not `grounded`. No disruption dataset was opened that measures the two effects at the same slots with a non-updating anchor.

## 5. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not another argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: the instrument reading green *"cannot comment on the source"*; it was the spectrometer that resolved the case. Here the corresponding instrument would record, per slot and per shock: cost saving, shock class, cascade depth, recovery time, and the bond that connected the actors. Two low-overlap frames would then read it separately, an engineering network frame and a trade or macro frame, each with an anchor the other cannot move (Fig 2 :31, :45). Then:

- Does a shock at tier 3 produce a loss that first-tier enumeration missed? (Fig 11 :52-53)
- Does changing the bond from single to dual source alter both cost and exposure over the same actor set? (Fig 12 :117)
- Does the channel to the disruption ground stay open while the cost metric saturates? (Fig 10 :24-26)
- After carrying the firm result up and back down, how large is `R_T`? (Fig 4 :30)

Standing falsifiers, written before the conclusion: one currency and one anchor for both effects; contact added with no exposure change at any slot; no bond change over a fixed set moves both readings; a net economy instrument that pays for both; targeted removal that leaves a concentrated network intact.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable in principle.** The separator is stateable now; the instrument that would name the winner is not in the opened sources. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The answer stops at the limit of what the available instruments distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Agreement among the many papers that assert a trade-off is one evidence pool (Fig 7 :13: *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*). Prestige can fund the dataset; it cannot settle the claim.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not return a net number for "economies." It says the question bundles two conditions with different anchors: efficiency is read against a paying market, disruption exposure against a realized loss and recovery, and Fig 12 holds them in two currencies that do not add. "Global" changes the habitat by adding contact that cannot be subtracted, so the same low-cost source is a benefactor under a cost residual and a chokepoint under a disruption residual. Over a fixed set of suppliers and buffers, lean and resilient configurations are isomers: same ingredients, different bond, different substance. What is offered is the shape any answer must have, the per-slot separator, four failure conditions written in advance, and the instrument that would be needed to name a winner. The aggregate stays open until the ground settles it.
