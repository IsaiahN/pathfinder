## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

The question asks whether one intervention, global supply chain integration, produces two effects: increased efficiency and increased vulnerability to disruption. The word "but" asserts that the two are coupled, as opposite signs on one dial.

We must decide three things before concluding anything:

1. Are efficiency and vulnerability one quantity with two readings, or two quantities in two currencies?
2. Does "global" change the object being measured, or only its label?
3. Does any figure license a verdict about "economies" as a single aggregate, or only per-slot verdicts?

Stated as an effect: an increase in cross-border fragmentation lowers delivered cost where a market pays, and simultaneously rearranges the contact structure through which a shock travels. What is owed is not one net number but a separator that says which anchor is being read, at which slot, under which disruption.

**R by arity, symmetry and scale.**

- **Arity:** at least two conditions (efficiency, disruption exposure) plus a scope qualifier ("global"). Fig 9 :4-5 says disagreements that do not shrink with effort, *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* So R is a union compound with its own loop, not a partition.
- **Symmetry:** "more efficient" is a comparison against a cost or output quantity; "more vulnerable" is a comparison against a realized loss and recovery time. Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* Operators :52: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one."* Both effects are comparisons, but of different quantities, so the coupling "A but B" is not a symmetric bond joining two instances of one thing. It is two threshold readings (Fig 12 :87, *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*) against two anchors.
- **Scale:** the effect registers at the firm, link and tier scale first, and only then, if at all, at the economy scale. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Symbols :43: *"R is measured per slot, never as a single global number."* The transform between the two scales is lossy (Fig 4 :30), so a firm-level efficiency gain cannot be promoted to an economy-level verdict without a measured round trip.

**What an answer would look like (binding the sought-for shape as an ordinary term).** A satisfactory answer would be a per-slot separator: for each named actor and link, which anchor is read (market or disruption outcome), in which currency (bits of description or actions to settle), with what valence relative to the residual, and what instrument would have to exist before a net verdict is even well posed. If no such separator exists and only a single net percentage is returnable, that is the shape failing, not an answer.

**Partition or union.** Union, not partition (Fig 9 :54, :55, :57). The same network rearrangement that lowers cost also changes exposure, so the conditions overlap. Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"* and becomes a specific compound. The overlap is the object that needs its own loop, which is D3.

**Dependency order (Fig 3 :20, *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance"*).**

1. D3 habitat first: which nodes and links exist, and what cascades through them.
2. D1 efficiency second: read cost against the market anchor, per slot.
3. D2 vulnerability third: read exposure against a disruption anchor, per slot, using the same topology.
4. D4 last: attempt the aggregate verdict, and refuse it if the anchors do not compose.

Refuse before mint (Formula step 2); describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28, *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*). Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* The missing instrument at step 4 is the main finding of this audit.

**Strain test applied before concluding.** An early reading tried to make the two-currency line govern the whole trade-off directly. That strains the line: it governs two currencies of a *composition*, not two social outcomes. Re-census assigns it to D1/D2 as the reason the two readings cannot be added, which is a better fit and needs no forcing. Recorded in §5.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The question is a union of two conditions, not one dial (decisive for the framing)

Fig 9 :4-5: *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*.

The two effects overlap in the same network, so the overlap is a compound with its own loop (D3). Asking for one answer that covers both is asking a single lever to clear the intersections, which Fig 9 :54-57 says it cannot. Verdicts stay per domain and are never averaged. **Mode: `general` on the structure, `specified` that this particular question is a union. Triangulated across Fig 9 and Fig 1 :19 (per-slot indexing), low overlap between the split rule and the indexing rule.**

### F2 - Efficiency and vulnerability are two currencies that do not add

Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 12 :126: *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."* Fig 12 :130: *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it. A price in bits and a price in actions are two readings, held apart."*

A lean arrangement is cheap to describe and cheap to run in normal conditions (bits). Resilience is what it costs to settle the arrangement when a disruption arrives (actions). The literature itself keeps them apart: Lücker, Timonina-Farkas and Seifert (2025, *Production and Operations Management*) distinguish *"dual-purpose"* levers, which promote efficiency in expectation, from *"dedicated"* levers, which guarantee resilience against particular disruptions without paying in normal conditions. That is two readings held apart, not one number. The retail-logistics study by RDEA likewise reports that most stores *"sacrifice significant portions of efficiency for an increased level of resilience"* while some align the two, which is exactly the two-currency picture. **Mode: `specified`. Triangulated across Fig 12 and two independent operations-management papers that do not share an author with the figures.**

### F3 - The two effects have different anchors

Fig 2 :29 gives the anchor test: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* For "more efficient" the anchor is a paying market or a measured cost. For "more vulnerable" the anchor is a realized disruption and recovery outcome. They are different instruments. Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* When a single dataset is used to certify both, the frames share an evidence pool and their agreement is worthless as a verdict (Fig 2 :40): *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*.

The strongest corroboration is the World Bank (2023) line: GVC integration *"has also been associated with reduced vulnerability of economic activity to domestic shocks, although it has come with increased sensitivity to external shocks."* Two opposite signs, two different shock classes, two different readings. That is Fig 11 :49 in world language. **Mode: `specified` on the per-anchor verdicts. Triangulated (Fig 2 anchor test plus World Bank economics), low overlap between the framework and a public policy report.**

### F4 - "Global" changes the habitat, and you cannot subtract it

Fig 11 :61-62: *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."* Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"*. Fig 11 :73: *"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."*

Global sourcing adds cross-border actors and contact. It does not merely relabel the same local chain. This is why the efficiency gain and the exposure change arrive together: both are properties of the rearranged habitat, not of a firm considered alone. Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* A cost improvement that leaves contact with the disruption ground untouched has not changed the vulnerability residual at all.

World corroboration: the WTO *Global Value Chain Development Report 2025* notes that specialization *"has led to the offshoring of production processes to where they are most efficiently executed"* and that this often *"points towards a few state actors, thereby leading to considerable concentration of production capacities,"* creating *"bottlenecks and choke points, especially during periods of high uncertainty."* The report's remedy is to *"introduce sufficient redundancy into the system,"* which is an introduction, exactly as Fig 11 :61 licenses. **Mode: `specified` on the habitat claim, `general` on the introduction/exclusion asymmetry. Triangulated (Fig 11 plus WTO report), with Fig 6 :23 as the reachability route.**

### F5 - A link's valence flips with the residual

Fig 11 :49: *"valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* Fig 11 :50: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."*

A single low-cost source is a benefactor under a cost residual and a chokepoint under a disruption residual. There is no figure-licensed standing list of "good suppliers." This dissolves the common phrasing that a particular sourcing arrangement is simply efficient or simply risky: it is efficient *relative to* one residual and fragile *relative to* another. The flip is not a contradiction; it is the same actor read against two anchors.

The cleanest low-overlap witness is Albert, Jeong and Barabási (2000, *Nature* 406:378-382), who found that scale-free networks display *"an unexpected degree of robustness"* to random failures, but that *"error tolerance comes at a high price: these networks are extremely vulnerable to attacks, i.e. to the selection and removal of a few nodes that play the most important role in assuring the network's connectivity."* Same topology, two residuals, opposite verdicts. **Mode: `general` on the valence rule, `specified` on its application to sourcing. Triangulated (Fig 11 plus a physics source with no shared author or evidence pool).**

### F6 - Contact cascades; first-order enumeration misses the material effect

Fig 11 :52-53: *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*

A tier-1 supplier is direct contact. The material disruption may sit at tier 3, as the semiconductor shortages did. Enumerating only direct suppliers is a first-order census of a second-order effect, so the vulnerability residual is read at the wrong slot. Lee, Padmanabhan and Whang (1997, *MIT Sloan Management Review*) show the related intra-chain amplification: *"the variabilities of an upstream site are always greater than those of the downstream site, a simple, yet powerful illustration of the bullwhip effect."* Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* The bullwhip is a cycle with a positive sign; a chain cannot represent it, which is why a linear supplier list understates it. **Mode: `specified`. Triangulated (Fig 11 cascade plus Fig 12 cycle plus the operations source).**

### F7 - The efficiency metric can saturate the channel to the disruption ground

Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*. Fig 10 :25-26: *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*. Fig 10 :27-28: *"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"*. Fig 1 :27: *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*

Just-in-time cost metrics are excellent at what they measure. Their success is the risk: a fully utilized, low-inventory chain has no slack signal left to carry a disruption warning. The channel decays while the ground does not. Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A cost model that also certifies its own resilience is a frame scoring itself with a quantity it produces. **Mode: `specified`. Single-source on the figure side for the channel mechanism; corroborated by the lean-versus-resilient literature without using it as a verdict.**

### F8 - Efficient and resilient configurations over one actor set are isomers

Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."* Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

Given a fixed set of suppliers, routes, inventory and capacity, a lean configuration and a resilient configuration can share every ingredient and differ only in the bonds: single source versus dual source, zero buffer versus buffer, just-in-time versus capacity reservation. Under Fig 12 they are isomers, not different quantities of one substance. This is why the trade-off is a *type* question (which bond) at least as much as a *degree* question (how much). Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."* A buffer that is maintained and regenerated is a catalyst; a one-off stockpile that is spent is not, and nothing in an ingredient list records which it is. Fig 13 :121: *"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."* **Mode: `specified`, conditional on a fixed actor set. Single-source for the figure definition; the fixed-set condition is named as a live strain in §5 and in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.**

### F9 - No figure returns a net verdict for "economies"

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."* Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

There is no figure line that converts two currencies and two anchors into a single net welfare number for an economy. The closest world result, the Review of World Economics (2025) firm-level study, reports that GVC participation raises TFP by about 4.7 percent but adds that *"the only consistently significant and positive effect is observed for firms participating at the regional European level"* and that global participation is not significantly positive in that sample. That is a scope nuance, not a contradiction: it says the anchor for "global" is not the anchor for "regional." It cannot be promoted into a universal net verdict. The honest output is `gap` of the shape-not-a-name kind: a separator plus the instrument that would be owed, not silence and not a fabricated percentage. **Mode: `gap`. Triangulated on the refusal (Fig 9 and Fig 13, low overlap between the lookup rule and the reach rule).**

### F11 - Emergence of a general verdict from agreement is the named collapse

Fig 7 :13: *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*. Fig 7 :35: *"agreement is variation, not a verdict"*. Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* A large body of papers agreeing that the trade-off exists is one evidence pool if they reuse the same case studies and the same cost metrics. The legitimate ask is two low-overlap frames, each ranging against its own non-updating anchor: an engineering network frame reading cascade outcomes, and a trade or macro frame reading realized cost and recovery, with the overlap between them recorded rather than pooled. Fig 8 :45: *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."* Fig 8 :51-52: *"Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."*

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim fails if any of the following holds:

1. **One-currency refuter.** A single instrument is shown to measure efficiency and disruption exposure in one currency against one anchor, with a stable exchange rate across slots. This breaks Fig 12 :125-130 and F2.
2. **Habitat refuter.** A global network is shown to add nodes or contact without changing exposure to any shock at any slot, under an anchor that does not update on the network's own efficiency reports. This breaks Fig 11 :61-63 and :40.
3. **Isomer refuter.** Over a fixed actor set, no change of bond (single to dual source, zero to positive buffer) changes both cost and exposure as Fig 12 :117 predicts, or a change of actors is required for every such effect. This breaks F8.
4. **Aggregate refuter.** An instrument is produced that returns a net per-economy verdict with a non-updating ground, and the two effects are shown to trend together at the economy scale. This closes the `gap` in F9.
5. **Cascade refuter.** Targeted removal of the most connected nodes leaves connectivity unaffected in a concentrated network, or first-order enumeration fully predicts realized loss. This breaks Fig 11 :52-53 and the Albert et al. analogue.
6. **Simulation refuter.** The derivation is shown to be a ground reading rather than a simulated derivation, which would change the mode line on every file.

Until at least one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with F1 and F4: a union of conditions in a changed habitat, not one dial. Keep the two verdicts separate and never average them.
2. State the currency of each claim (Fig 12 :125-126) before quoting any number. A TFP percentage is a bit-price; a recovery time is an action-price.
3. Use the valence flip (Fig 11 :49) to explain how one source can be efficient and fragile without contradiction.
4. Report the aggregate as `gap`, with the four conditions of refuter 4 written down, rather than a net percentage (Fig 9 :32).
5. Send the generator and refuter, not a recording of this analysis (Fig 4 :22, Fig 10 :52).
