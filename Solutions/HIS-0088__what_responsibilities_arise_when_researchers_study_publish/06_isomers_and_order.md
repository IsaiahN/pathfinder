## DERIVATION

### Q1 — One act or a union, and in what order?

Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**
Fig 12 :71: **"A then B; B needs A to have happened"**

Study → publish → commercialize is an ordered chain (→), and publication and commercialization each produce a new artifact (⇒: a paper, a product). The chain is a union, not a partition: the same community habitat is contacted three times, and the obligations overlap. Fig 9 :56 says a single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds. So a consent form obtained at the study stage does not discharge the commercialization stage; the intersection (use of the same knowledge for profit) is a specific compound with its own loop.

- Falsifier, written before the run: a single governance instrument is shown to settle all three acts for the same knowledge and the same habitat, with no categorically different residue at the intersections.

### Q2 — Are the two accounts isomers?

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

As commonly written, a research paper and a community account share only the bare observations (the atoms). Under Fig 12 they are not isomers; they are two members of a **functional group** (claims about the world) with different qualifiers. The stronger claim: if both are written over the full shared atom set `{holders, place, protocol, observations, transmission relations, benefit}`, differing only in which bond attaches which atom, then they **are** isomers, same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. Under the strong reading, a paper that drops holders, protocol and benefit is not a truncated community account; it is a different substance whose formula looks identical.

- Falsifier: a demonstration that holders, protocol and benefit carry no information beyond the observations for this knowledge, so the rearrangement is lossless. Then the functional-group reading stands and the isomer reading fails.

### Q3 — Does refusal come before or after the governance term is minted?

`THE_FORMULA.md` step 2 routes a REFUTED gap first: *"a term we hold is false; what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation."* Fig 12 :140: **"A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate."**

The held term is "community knowledge is free-floating data." A process that adds a benefit-sharing clause (mints a governance term) before allowing the community to refuse the underlying term has the order backwards. Refusal is ¬, a unary operator that acts on one operand and joins nothing; it is not the subtraction −, which is "A with B removed or absent" (Fig 12 :78 to :84). Adding a clause while keeping the extractive relation is subtraction-shaped; refusing the extractive term is negation-shaped. A vocabulary with only − can be corrected and cannot carry the correction.

- Falsifier: a research process shown to carry a community's refusal (including withdrawal) using only composition, substitution and subtraction, with no negation operator anywhere. If that works, the unary-negation requirement is unnecessary.

### Q4 — Is consent a comparison or an identity?

Fig 12 :85: **"a threshold relation between two quantities"**
Fig 12 :67: **"two names, one referent"**

"Consent" written as ≡ (identity) says the researcher's account and the community's account are two names for one referent: substitute one for the other and nothing is lost (Fig 12 :69, **"substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No."**). That is the flattening error. Consent is structurally ⋛, a threshold relation between two quantities: does the community's own ground still hold under the proposed action? Comparison is ordered and asks which, not merely both (Fig 12 :89: **"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."**).

- Falsifier: a case where substituting the research account for the community account loses nothing at the ground, so ≡ is the correct bond and ⋛ is over-engineering.

## CONCLUSION

The three acts form an ordered union with overlapping conditions, not one action. The research and community accounts are isomers only under the strong reading over the full shared atom set; as written they are a functional group. Refusal precedes minting, and it is a negation, not a subtraction. Consent is a comparison, not an identity. These are structural findings with modes attached: `general` for the bond rules, `specified` for the research application. None is `grounded`.
