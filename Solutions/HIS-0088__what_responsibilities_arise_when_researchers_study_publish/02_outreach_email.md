# Outreach draft — check the shape before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to an institutional submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` have survived your own check. The output must stand on its own or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about responsibilities to community-held knowledge

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a figure-grounded derivation of what responsibilities arise when researchers study, publish, or commercialize knowledge held by communities. I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the derivation says that any legitimate contact between a research frame and community knowledge must satisfy six constraints, and that these can be stated before naming any particular governance protocol.
>
> 1. The community is the anchor for questions constitutively about the community, not an instrument the researcher uses. Its answer must not move when the researcher's prestige or framing changes.
> 2. The knowledge is a property of holders and habitat, not of the extracted item. You have to enumerate place, practitioners, season, transmission relations and cascades several steps out, not just the fact, compound or observation.
> 3. Provenance is part of the term itself, not a footnote. Holder, place, protocol, consent and agreed benefit travel with the knowledge, or the knowledge does not travel.
> 4. Refusal must be real. Consent that cannot register a no, or cannot be withdrawn, is a process that can only accumulate.
> 5. Stakes must be declared. Prestige, citation count and committee size give a claim a budget to be tried; they cannot settle whether the research was responsible.
> 6. Study, publish and commercialize are three overlapping loops over one habitat, not one action with one rule. A single consent form clears only the places where one condition holds.
>
> This is a prediction of a **shape**, not a measurement, and not a claim that any one protocol (CARE, OCAP, Nagoya, FPIC) is the answer. The framework returns a kind of thing, never the answer itself. Naming a protocol is a binding from a different frame, and until its provenance is shown it carries no evidentiary weight here.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written down before this was sent):**
> 1. a community verdict that tracks the researcher's reputation rather than the community's own ground;
> 2. a knowledge claim that keeps its full validity and bearing with place, holders and transmission relations removed;
> 3. a knowledge claim whose rightful control is unaffected by erasing origin, holder and protocol;
> 4. a research process that reaches a sound result while offering the community no way to refuse or withdraw;
> 5. a demonstration that re-arranging community observations into a paper or a patent is lossless in the arrangement;
> 6. a figure line that directly selects one governance protocol after a fresh census.
>
> If any of these holds, I withdraw the corresponding obligation. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not asking:** I am not asking you to trust the framework, to endorse it, or to say that you already knew this. I am asking what you would count as a failure of the shape above. Your verdict on this stage is recorded before any theory is shown, and I do not pool later agreement as corroboration.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in the solution folder's project tree
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Nagoya Protocol summary (official CBD page, open 2026-11-23): https://www.cbd.int/abs/about/
> - CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance (open 2026-11-23): https://datascience.codata.org/articles/10.5334/dsj-2020-043/
> - The First Nations Principles of OCAP (open 2026-11-23): https://fnigc.ca/ocap-training/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures folder. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and its author warns that *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."* A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
