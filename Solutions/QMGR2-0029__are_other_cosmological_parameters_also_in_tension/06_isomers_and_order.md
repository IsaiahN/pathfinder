## RESIDUAL

The question asks whether the disagreement vector R has support on one slot or several. Two derivations are owed: (1) are the parameter slots structurally distinct, or are they different spellings of one quantity, and (2) is the multi-slot problem a partition or a union, and in what order should its loops run?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Are H0 and S8 different terms or two names for one?

Fig 13 :76: *"A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."*

- H0 is a time gradient: it is the fractional change of the scale factor per unit time, so a structure (a gradient) bonded to the state time.
- S8 is an amplitude of clustering over space, a structure bonded to the state space, with Omega_m inside its definition (S8 = sigma8 times the square root of Omega_m/0.3).
- Under Fig 13 :76 these are **different terms**, not two spellings of one. They cannot be collapsed by a symmetric identity bond.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** exhibit an exact one-to-one map between the H0 residual and the S8 residual under a single model and a single dataset, so that one is a reparameterisation of the other. Then they are one term and the two-slot reading fails.

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 13 and the definitions in the physics frame (low overlap between a substrate figure and a measurement convention).

### Q2 — Are the slots isomers, a functional group, or distinct families?

Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

- If the slots are written over the full shared set {space, time, energy, gradient, spectrum, network} and differ only in which structure mediates which state pair, they are **isomers** in the figure's sense: same atoms, different arrangement, different behaviour under a proposed import.
- If they share only the model and the likelihood formalism, with different atoms in the slots (H0 uses time and a gradient; S8 uses space and a clustering amplitude), they are members of a **functional group**, not isomers. The group fixes the kind of comparison; the qualifier fixes the member.
- The empirical fact that a single mechanism can move several slots at once (Wang and Guo, 2026) is the behavioural signature of shared atoms, which is what the isomer reading predicts.

**Falsifier:** show that two slots share no atom other than the model itself, yet a single import moves both. Then the shared-atom explanation is wrong and the functional-group reading carries the whole case.

### Q3 — Partition or union, and what is the order?

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
Fig 9 :55-56: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*

- H0-only region: late-universe distance measurements versus early-universe inference at fixed sound horizon.
- S8-only region: weak-lensing and redshift-survey amplitudes versus CMB-inferred clustering.
- Intersection: both depend on Omega_m, on the model, and for early-universe chains on r_d. A lever that shrinks r_d raises the inferred H0 and changes the predicted clustering, so it clears the first region and acts on the intersection.

The dependency order is therefore: measure each slot (perception), establish what each slot affords (vocabulary), define a persistent residual as progress (objective), choose an assumption-isolating null test (planning), then carry the method (Fig 3). The middle link is the one with no routine instrument, which is why the cosmic tetrarchy channels matter.

**Falsifier:** show that the intersection is empty, that is, no shared atom between any two live slots. Then the union collapses to a partition and the unbundling step is unnecessary.

### Q4 — What does an import do to the order?

Fig 6 :14: *"the only operator that moves the wall"*
Fig 8 :50: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*

An import adds an atom to the inference closure. In this question a candidate import must be recorded with (a) the slot it was aimed at, (b) the effects on every other slot, and (c) its provenance. Without (b) it is a single-lever claim, and Fig 9 :55 says a single lever leaves the intersections standing. Without (c) a convergent derivation and an adopted import are indistinguishable in the contents.

**Falsifier:** produce an import with a complete per-slot effect table that closes one slot and leaves all others strictly inside their prior errors. Then the union's intersection was not binding and the import is a genuine wall move.

## CONCLUSION

The parameter slots are structurally distinct terms under Fig 13 :76, not spellings of one quantity. They are best read as a functional group, and as isomers under the stronger writing where both use the same atoms in different arrangements. Their problem is a union with a nonempty intersection, so unbundling precedes search and every proposed import must be scored against all slots. The order is perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, carry, with the vocabulary/objective link as the likely break. Mode `specified`, ungrounded.
