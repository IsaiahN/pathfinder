## What in the wider literature bears on this question

1. **The scope is already a two-slot literature.** Abdalla et al. (2022) review both H0 and sigma8/S8 and explicitly recommend fitting a full array of data with a single model rather than one parameter at a time. This corroborates the union reading in F3. It does not decide it: the figures do.
2. **Independence has to be audited, not assumed.** Verde, Treu and Riess (2019) emphasise that the H0 result does not depend on one method or team. Wong et al. (2019) supply a genuinely different closure through time-delay lensing. Against that, several early-universe chains share the sound horizon and several late chains share the supernova calibration. Fig 2 :31 decides how to treat each pair.
3. **Correlated shifts are the normal failure mode of a fix.** Wang and Guo (2026) say that proposals reduce the nominal tension at the cost of shifts elsewhere. This is the physics-side echo of Fig 6's import rule: an atom that moves one slot and disturbs another has not explained a gap.
4. **A null test can localise the assumption.** Martinelli and Sapone (2026) decompose the distance consistency test into four channels with different assumption sets. This is a world-side instance of Fig 3's missing middle link: a way to say what a situation affords and what would count as progress, with an instrument.
5. **Agreement from one pool is one witness.** All the cosmology sources above draw on the same published datasets and reviews. Their agreement with each other is expected and carries little independent weight. The figure verdict does not rest on it.
