## 2. Wider literature that bears, recorded but not over-read

- Meadows et al. 2018, "Exoplanet Biosignatures: Understanding Oxygen as a Biosignature in the Context of Its Environment", Astrobiology, https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1089/ast.2017.1727. The public page was seen in search results with its section 1.1 discussion of O2 reliability and abiotic sources. It is recorded here as a search-index finding, not as a fully opened source, and no quote from it is used in the verdict.
- Young et al. 2024, "Inferring chemical disequilibrium biosignatures for Proterozoic Earth-like exoplanets", Nature Astronomy 8, 101-110, https://www.nature.com/articles/s41550-023-02145-z. Abstract and citation record were opened. It reports that retrieval constraints on O2 and CH4 depend on SNR and abundance. Used only as an instrument-limit note in `01` F9, marked partial in the ledger.
- Young et al. 2025, "Modern Earth-like Chemical Disequilibrium Biosignatures are Challenging to Constrain through Spectroscopic Retrievals", ApJ 986 206, https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.3847/1538-4357/addbd7. Abstract accessed via the public journal page. Its conclusion that O2-CH4 inference is difficult at SNR below 40 is used as an instrument statement, not as a world verdict.
- Lederberg 1965 (Nature 207, 9-13), Lovelock 1965 (Nature 207, 568-570), Hitchcock and Lovelock 1967, Sagan et al. 1993. Cited in the Schwieterman and Rein papers as the historical origin of the disequilibrium proposal. They are not reopened here and no quote is taken from them directly.

**Overlap declaration.** Schwieterman, Krissansen-Totton, Wogan, and the Young papers share the NExSS workshop series, several authors and the Earth analogue record. They are one evidence pool, one witness. Rein et al. 2014 is partly independent (instrument modelling), metamerism is color science, and the SEP entry is philosophy of science; those three are the low-overlap corroborators, and even they corroborate only the *shape*, not the existence of life anywhere.
