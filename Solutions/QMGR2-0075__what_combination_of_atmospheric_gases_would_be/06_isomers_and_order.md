# Isomers and order in a "combination of gases"

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading. See `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6.

## RESIDUAL

The question asks for a *combination* of atmospheric gases. Three structural questions are owed before any name is given:

1. What kind of bond does a "combination" name, and is it symmetric?
2. Are two candidate biosignature gas pairs isomers over a fixed set, or only members of a functional group?
3. Does order carry information here, and if so, what is ordered?

## DERIVATION

### Q1. Does "shape, not a name" forbid naming a gas pair? Correction to my own first pass

- The canonical rule: *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*. The subject is a missing primitive.
- Fig 13 :133: "what it returns is a shape rather than a name."
- O2, CH4 and CO2 are not among the six and are not missing primitives of the substrate. They are terms of the planetary-science frame.
- Therefore naming a candidate pair is a **binding** from that frame onto an atmospheric vocabulary. It is not the invention of a seventh member and not a figure output.
- What remains wrong is presenting the binding as a derivation, or claiming it moves the wall (Fig 6: "IMPORT adds an atom" adds none here).
- **I overstated this earlier** by reading the rule as a ban on any gas name. Retracted as applied to candidate pairs. Triangulated (canonical rule plus Fig 13).

### Q2. What bond does a "combination" name?

- Fig 12 :59, the conjunction test: "swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No." A list `O2 + CH4` is one term under a symmetric bond; `CH4 + O2` is not a new substance.
- Fig 12 :87, the comparison test: "a threshold relation between two quantities." A disequilibrium asks which species is in excess relative to equilibrium. Swapping operands changes the claim if the magnitudes differ.
- Symbols table :97: "A difference that can be spent. Not merely a distribution: what makes it a gradient is directedness."
- Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
- **Verdict:** the combination is a molecule. The bond that carries the evidential information is comparison plus gradient, not conjunction. Conjunction names the ingredient list; comparison names the departure from equilibrium.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if the evidential force of a pair is invariant under exchanging the two species and under replacing the pair with both species at equilibrium, then conjunction was correct and this verdict fails.

### Q3. Are O2+CH4 and CH4+CO2 isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- As the literature writes them, the pairs do not share an atom set: `{O2, CH4}` versus `{CH4, CO2}` share only CH4. By Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**; they are two members of a **functional group**, a reduced species coupled to an oxidized species, with different atoms in the slots.
- The stronger version: write both over the full shared atmospheric atom set `{O2, O3, CH4, CO2, N2, H2O, CO}`. They are then the same inventory under different maintained couplings, and the difference in evidential meaning is carried entirely by which gases are coupled to which flux. That **is** an isomer claim in Fig 12's sense.
- The world literature itself supplies the reason the weaker reading fails: Krissansen-Totton et al. 2018 propose CH4 plus CO2 with absent CO for anoxic atmospheres, while Schwieterman et al. 2018 discuss O2 plus CH4 for oxic atmospheres. Different habitats, different atoms, different functional-group members.
- **Falsifier:** if either pair can be written without an atom the other requires, across all habitats, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. As written, that falsifier has already fired against the strong isomer claim; the finding is retained with that outcome visible.

### Q4. The chemistry analogue is not an analogy

- Fig 12 :97: "This is not an analogy. Chemistry hit it first: silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different properties, which is what forced structure into the notation."
- The figure explicitly excludes treating this as a metaphor.
- So the gas-pair case is not a metaphor borrowed from chemistry. Two atmospheres with the same gas inventory and different maintained couplings are different substances in exactly the sense the figure defines. The spectral formula (a list of bands) cannot tell them apart; the bond does.
- Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance."

### Q5. What is ordered, and what is not

- Conjunction is symmetric (Fig 12 :59), so listing order among gas names carries no information.
- Comparison is ordered (Fig 12 :87; Operators table :51, "a threshold relation between two quantities"). The order is not "O2 before CH4" as written; it is "which species is in excess relative to the equilibrium state".
- Sequence `→` is the ordered bond that expresses a route: "A then B; B needs A to have happened" (Fig 12 :72). It would be the wrong bond here, because the two gases are simultaneous rather than successive. Using `→` would add an ordering the figure does not license, the same defect the QMGR-0037 exemplar found in its own chain.
- Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." A list of band depths names a state. A maintained departure from equilibrium names a route.
- **Falsifier:** if a time-ordered sequence, rather than a simultaneous threshold, is what actually discriminates the sources, then `→` or a temporal bond governs and this Q5 verdict fails. This is an open instrument question, not a settled one: temporal monitoring is one of the INWARD moves in `01` F9.

### Q6. Catalysts and functional groups in the habitat

- Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."
- In an atmosphere the catalyst analogue is a surface or a photolytic pathway that is not consumed by the reaction it enables. Two atmospheres with identical gas inventories can differ by which catalytic surface is present, which is again a bond difference, not an atom difference.
- Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." A catalyst is a benefactor to one residual and irrelevant to another, so no standing list of catalytic surfaces is a biosignature either.

## CONCLUSION

1. A "combination of gases" is a molecule, not a list. The bond is the maintained departure from equilibrium; conjunction carries none of the evidential information (Q2).
2. The two famous pairs are members of one **functional group**, not isomers as written. Over a shared full gas inventory they become a real isomer claim, whose strong form is falsified by the different atom sets the literature actually uses (Q3).
3. The chemical analogue in Fig 12 is explicitly not an analogy: same inventory, different arrangement, different substance (Q4).
4. Listing order carries no information; the ordered quantity is which gas is in excess relative to equilibrium. Sequence would be the wrong bond (Q5).
5. Naming candidate pairs is a provenanced binding, not a forbidden invention and not a wall-moving import (Q1).

This rescues what is checkable in the question without inventing a gas list the figures do not return. The literal list remains a `gap`.
