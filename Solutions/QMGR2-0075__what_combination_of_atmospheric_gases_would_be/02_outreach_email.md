# Outreach draft: check the shape claim before deciding whether the framework is worth reading

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three checkable sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` have been re-checked by the sender. The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, never the framework's interiors.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about biosignature gas combinations, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a small, checkable output about how atmospheric gas combinations could count as evidence of life. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A gas list is not evidence. Evidence lives in the bond between the gases: a maintained departure from chemical equilibrium whose persistence requires a renewed flux. Under the six-term substrate, three structural consequences follow. First, a combination with no bond is a flat formula, so the pair O2 and CH4 carries no more information than its two band depths until the maintained relation between them is specified. Second, the evidential relation is a threshold and a directed gradient, not a symmetric list; swapping the operands does not preserve the claim. Third, no standing list of gases works across habitats, because an actor's valence is relative to the residual. A large H2, CO2 and water disequilibrium can be an anti-biosignature where chemotrophic life would consume it.
>
> **What the output does not say.** It does not say that O2 plus CH4 is the answer, that CH4 plus CO2 is the answer, or that any particular gas has been detected on any planet. Those are bindings from planetary science onto an atmospheric vocabulary, not outputs of the derivation. The framework returns a shape, not a name. The specific list is a `gap`, reported as unreachable at this budget rather than as silence, because the source of the gases on an exoplanet is not an anchor we can currently reach.
>
> **What "persuasive" means after re-grounding.** Persuasion is a fact about a room, not a fact about a planet. I am using "would survive contact with an anchor that does not update when opinion changes", not "would convince most researchers". Agreement about a shared meaning is efficient for a convention; agreement about the world is worthless as a verdict when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim. I wrote these down before sending this.**
> 1. A figure line that licenses a standing list of gases as sufficient evidence independent of habitat.
> 2. A single gas reading that uniquely determines its source against a non-updating anchor in more than one habitat.
> 3. A re-census showing the governing bond is symmetric conjunction rather than comparison plus directed gradient.
> 4. A measured atmosphere in which a pair's co-occurrence has no abiotic source in a fully enumerated habitat and a non-updating anchor confirms life (this would close the gap, not break the shape).
>
> If any of these holds, I will withdraw the corresponding finding. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would actually like to know:** what would you count as a failure of the bond claim? Would an unresolved planet plus moon, with O2 on one body and CH4 on the other, already be a counterexample at foreseeable resolution? I am asking because I want the discriminator, not the endorsement.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Schwieterman et al. 2018, Exoplanet Biosignatures: A Review of Remotely Detectable Signs of Life, Astrobiology: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6016574/
> - Rein, Fujii and Spiegel 2014, Some inconvenient truths about biosignatures involving two chemical species on Earth-like exoplanets, PNAS: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4024890/
> - Krissansen-Totton, Olson and Catling 2018, Disequilibrium biosignatures over Earth history, Science Advances: https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.08211
> - Wogan and Catling 2020, When is chemical disequilibrium a biosignature versus an anti-biosignature, ApJ: https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.06852
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the formula is in the public figures repository. Run it on a problem you can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thank you for reading this far.
>
> [name]
