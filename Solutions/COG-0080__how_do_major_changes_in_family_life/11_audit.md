## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 11 :39-43 and :52, Fig 4 :22 and :25, Fig 9 :55, Fig 1 :19, Fig 2 :36-38.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the five-point shape in `07_the_tether_answer.md`: child-and-habitat, methods not recordings, union compound, per-child residual, outcome anchor.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete intervention claim, for example "install this device in every classroom, report the cohort mean gain, conclude development improved."

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **per-slot requirement**, replaced by a cohort mean that hides the live child;
- the **contact requirement**, replaced by a claim about a device in isolation;
- the **union reading**, replaced by one lever applied to the intersections;
- the **substitution warning**, replaced by a claim that adding a tool is the same as adding capability.

One thing survives cleanly: a change in conditions acts through the habitat, so the question is what the tool changes about contact, for whom. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists here; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the shape is the condensed version of the figures" as identity would set R_T to zero and commit the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
