# Expert verification packet: the Tether on development across generations

**What this is:** a claim in a form a family-studies or developmental-science reader can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the derived predictions, not `grounded`.** No measurement was taken in this derivation. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Development is a property of the child and the habitat together. Major changes in family life, technology and education reshape development by changing contact, not by rewriting the child's internal machinery. What one generation hands to the next is a method or a head start that makes the next search cheaper, never a recording of past answers, which is why an early boost can fade while a reusable practice lasts. The three changes overlap, so they form a union compound that must be unbundled before any lever is tried; the developmental signal lives in individual children, not in a cohort average; and the thing that settles a claim about people is an outcome for a person, not agreement among researchers who read the same literature. Substituted digital habitats fail silently in two ways: what they fail to reproduce and what they introduce. The framework returns this shape and refuses to name the specific mechanism. Five refuters were written before this packet.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - The contact claim (checkable by a developmental scientist against their own data)

- **Statement:** a change that does not alter contact does not move the developmental ground, however much it improves the child in isolation.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39, "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40, "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
- **What to check:** in a study you know well, did the effective intervention change something about the child's contact (caregiver interaction, peer interaction, correction, sleep, safety), or did it only change a property of the child or the device? If the latter and the outcome still moved reliably, the claim fails. Also check the cascade: was the residual that mattered first order, or was it three steps out, as in Fig 11 :52?
- **Refuter:** an improvement to the child in isolation, with no change in contact, that reliably moves the measured outcome.

### C2 - The transmission claim (checkable by a family studies researcher)

- **Statement:** what crosses a generation is a method or head start, not a recording; the receiving generation must re-test it in its own habitat.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :22, "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Fig 4 :25, "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." Fig 4 :34, "The level a method lands on remakes it. A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."
- **What to check:** in an intergenerational study, classify what was transmitted: a reusable practice the receiver can reapply, or a fixed prescription tied to the original occasion. Does the transmitted item produce new capability in the receiving generation, or only a repeat of a past success? The early-childhood programme record is the natural test: early boosts that fade alongside long-term returns in some cohorts. Which transmitted item survived, and why?
- **Refuter:** a recording that produces new capability in the receiving generation without that generation re-testing it against its own ground.

### C3 - The verification claim (checkable by reading three short figure lines)

- **Statement:** the three changes are a union to be unbundled; the residual is per child; the anchor is an outcome for a person, not agreement.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55, "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 1 :19, "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 2 :36, "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."
- **What to check:** does a single lever clear the intersections of family, technology and education, or only the single-condition regions? Does a reported cohort mean preserve the per-child signal that steers a successful intervention? Does the claimed outcome move when the measuring researchers change their mind about the theory? If the answer to any of these defeats the corresponding clause, that clause is withdrawn.
- **Refuter:** any of (a) one lever clears the whole union; (b) a cohort average preserves the steering signal; (c) agreement among studies sharing one evidence pool is shown to be independent corroboration of a world claim.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a finding about a specific device, curriculum, screen-time dose or parenting style. That is content requiring a ground; recorded as `gap`.
- Not a literal derivation of development from the six-term substrate. That application strained and was re-censed as `gap` (F9 of `01`).
- Not that one of the three changes is the cause and the others are noise. They are a union compound.
- Not that cohort averages are useless for administration. They are disqualified only as witnesses for where the signal lives.
- Not that agreement among researchers is corroboration. Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."
- Not grounded. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating observation, not a further argument (see *The Hard Problem is About Source*: the instrument that reads green "it cannot comment on the source" was resolved by a spectrometer, not by a debate). For this question:

1. **Contact test:** is contact with a specific child measurably changed, and does the outcome move only when it is?
2. **Recording test:** does the receiving generation hold a capability it did not have before the handoff, and does it survive re-testing in that generation's habitat?
3. **Union test:** does one lever clear the intersections, or only the single-condition regions?
4. **Slot test:** does a cohort average preserve the per-child signal that steers a successful intervention?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :39-40 and :52, Fig 4 :22 and :25, Fig 9 :55, Fig 1 :19, Fig 2 :36. Seven short passages.
2. Check C1 against one study you know: was the effective ingredient contact, or was it a property of the child in isolation?
3. Check C2 against one intergenerational finding you know: method or recording, and how would you tell from the data?
4. Name one observation that would distinguish a method from a recording in your field, or say why none exists.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. Record the verdict before asking for the derivation.
