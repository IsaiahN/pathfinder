## What is being decided, in effect terms

Option A: treat development as a property of the individual child that can be improved in isolation by a better device, a better curriculum, or a better parenting script, delivered as a recording. This leads to interventions whose early gains fade, to a cohort average that hides which children moved, and to expert agreement that is a convention rather than corroboration.

Option B: treat development as a property of the child and the habitat together, where major changes reshape development by changing contact, where what crosses a generation is a head start and a method rather than a recording, where the three named changes form a union that must be unbundled, and where the residual is measured per child against an outcome that cannot be moved by agreement. This leads to a checkable shape, written refuters, and an honest stop.

The solution derives B and states what would settle it.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the shape.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No developmental finding presented as settled by the figures. The figures constrain the shape of an answer; they do not contain a developmental psychology and do not say how much screen time is harmful or which curriculum works.

Three reasons, in the framework's own words:

1. A list selected for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation. "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention." (Seat Map :163)
2. Sending the framework before asking for a verdict spends the checker's independence. Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."
3. A falsifier is a design instrument. Seat Map :287: "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."
