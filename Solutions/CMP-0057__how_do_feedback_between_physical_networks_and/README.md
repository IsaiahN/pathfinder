## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **Agreement is not an anchor.** Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." A list selected for receptiveness is a list selected to agree.
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** The Seat Map rule recorded in the exemplar: "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention." Selecting recipients for likely agreement manufactures the correlation that would make their agreement worthless.
3. **Showing the interiors spends independence.** Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, never the framework's interior.

Legally: public opt-in and submission venues avoid the failure mode of unsolicited bulk outreach under GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules. That is a practical reason on top of the structural one.
