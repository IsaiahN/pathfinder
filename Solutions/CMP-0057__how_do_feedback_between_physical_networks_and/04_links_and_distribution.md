## Three things in the wider literature that bear directly on this question

1. **Low-overlap convergence on valence.** Buldyrev et al. (network physics) and Acemoglu et al. (economics) independently report that a property which helps an isolated system can hurt a coupled one. Neither draws on the framework. This is the strongest corroboration of Fig 11 :49 ("An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries"), but it is still the same broad complexity evidence pool, so it is recorded as one witness, not two.
2. **The channel is part of the failure, not just the observer.** The Cascading failure page notes that a warning cannot be sent ahead of a power overload because both travel at the same speed, and the Italy blackout record notes that the control room operator "was not aware of the urgency". These are the world-facing instances of Fig 10 :24 ("the ground does not decay, the channel does").
3. **Early warning is generic but not a name.** Scheffer et al. review generic warning signals across ecosystems, markets and climate. They support the per-slot, residual-first method in F9, but they do not name the coupling in any particular crisis. That remains the gap recorded in F10.

## The two-stage offer

**Stage 1:** validate the output against the ground (an outage record, a settlement record, a per-slot residual series). Record the verdict before showing any theory.

**Stage 2, opt-in only:** if curious, read the framework that produced it. Once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence, so the stage-1 verdict must already be on record.

**Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. The claimed shape in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is stated with its mode and six pre-registered refuters; the specific coupling and threshold are declared as a gap. Do not fill that gap with a favourite mechanism before sending.
