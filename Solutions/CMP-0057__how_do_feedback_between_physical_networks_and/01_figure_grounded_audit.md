## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

`R = |Γ(b,a) − o′|` per slot `s` (Fig 1 :11, Symbols table). The question asks why, when a physical network (power, water, transport, telecom) and a social or financial network (payments, markets, institutions, behaviour) are coupled, a small missed prediction in one slot returns enlarged at the next turn instead of dying out.

What is being decided:

- **Option A:** amplification is a property of the networks themselves, located in their size or density. Leads to a single lever, "reduce connectivity", applied everywhere.
- **Option B:** amplification is a property of the *composition*: a cycle whose sign and cross-scale residue decide whether a disturbance is damped or compounded. Leads to per-slot diagnosis, unbundling of the union, and a search for the state that carries the coupling.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run (formula step "Direction"): *a shape with (i) a loop and its sign, (ii) a cross-kind state that carries the coupling, (iii) a per-slot residual that compounds over turns, and (iv) a channel that can still range against the ground while the cascade runs.* That shape is the sought-for term, written down so it can accrue a residual like anything else.

Scale: component, single network, coupled system of systems, and society. Arity: at least two networks plus the state between them. Symmetry: a cascade is ordered (A then B then back to A), so it carries a sequence bond and a cycle, not a symmetric join.

## 2. Findings

### F1 — The figure that governs "feedback amplifies" is the cycle, and its sign decides (decisive, `general`)

Figure 12 :121:

> "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."

A cascade is not a chain that got longer. A chain cannot close; a cycle can. The question "how do feedback between networks amplify crises" is, in figure terms, "what is the sign of the loop formed when a dependency returns its output to its input?" Amplification is the positive sign; damping is the negative sign. A chain, by construction, reaches neither verdict because it never returns.

The world literature says the same thing in its own words. The Cascading failure page, opened 2026-11-22, defines the event as "a failure in a system of interconnected parts in which the failure of one or few parts leads to the failure of other parts, growing progressively as a result of positive feedback." That is the world-facing instance of Fig 12 :121, not a rival explanation.

**Mode:** `general` for the cycle-sign rule. Refuter: a measured persistent oscillation in which the loop is demonstrably negative (the output returns to reduce the input) and the amplitude still grows without any second positive loop or external drive. Fix any term you like; if it holds, this finding fails.

### F2 — The amplification mechanism is a remainder that compounds (decisive, `general`)

Figure 13 :28-32, quoted at the point where the text names the remainder:

> "comes from: a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise."

The residual of one turn is the input to the next. In a coupled crisis, the unserved megawatt, the unsettled payment, the missed control signal is exactly that remainder. If each crossing leaves a remainder and the arrangement amplifies, a small slot residual becomes a large one without any new primitive being added. This is why "very small" initiators can produce system-wide failure: the Buldyrev et al. abstract, opened 2026-11-22, reports that "a failure of a very small fraction of nodes in one network may lead to the complete fragmentation of a system of several interdependent networks."

Note the honest limit: the figure gives the *shape* of compounding, not an amplitude or a threshold. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Which coupling has the positive sign, and at what value it crosses, is `gap` until a ground settles it.

**Mode:** `general` for compounding, `specified` for the crisis reading. Refuter: a documented coupled failure in which the residual is strictly non-increasing at every cross-network handoff and the damage still grows, with no hidden slot carrying the mass.

### F3 — Two networks are two structures; they can only join through a state (structural, `general`)

Figure 13 :24:

> "that entities relate, and influence travels"

Network is a **structure** in the six-term set, not a state. Figure 13 :74-75:

> "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."
> "Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state. Energy"

So a physical network and a financial network, being two structures, do not bond directly. They compose through a state: power flow, a payment obligation, a fuel delivery, a control signal, a service level. The coupling medium is not an optional detail; it is the only legal joint. This is the domain census result of the strain test, now quoted.

The Interdependent networks page, opened 2026-11-22, describes exactly this medium: "the power stations which form the nodes of the power grid require fuel delivered via a network of roads or pipes and are also controlled via the nodes of communications network." Its warning is the same as the figure's: "If the two networks were treated in isolation, this important feedback effect would not be seen and predictions of network robustness would be greatly overestimated."

**Mode:** `general`. Refuter: a demonstrated coupling between two network structures with no intervening state, in which influence travels with no carrier at all.

### F4 — This is a union compound, so a single lever answers the wrong question (method, `general`)

Figure 9 :54-57:

> "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
> "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."

"Physical network risk", "financial network risk" and "the feedback between them" are not three items in a list. The third is the intersection with its own loop. Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." A single lever, for example hardening lines only, clears the physical-only region and leaves the coupled intersection standing. The Acemoglu, Ozdaglar and Tahbaz-Salehi abstract, opened 2026-11-22, independently reports the same shape: "the same factors that contribute to resilience under certain conditions may function as significant sources of systemic risk under others."

**Mode:** `general` for the union rule, `specified` for crisis management. Refuter: one intervention that restores both the physical-only and the coupled-intersection regions with no categorically different residue left over.

### F5 — Contact cascades run further than first-order enumeration (scope, `specified`)

Figure 11 :52-53:

> "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three"

The extract continues on the next line: "steps out." The point is direct: enumerating only immediate dependencies misses the residual that matters. The 2003 Italy blackout record, opened 2026-11-22, is the instance: "the shutdown of power stations directly led to the failure of nodes in the Internet communication network, which in turn caused further breakdown of power stations." Three steps, not one.

The Interdependent networks page adds the warning that matters for instruments: "the existence of a first order transition implies that the system can go from a relatively healthy state to complete collapse with no advanced warning." That is a channel problem (F7), not evidence that no residual existed.

**Mode:** `specified`. Refuter: a post-event analysis in which first-order (direct-neighbour) enumeration fully accounts for every observed cross-network failure, with no third-order residual.

### F6 — A coupling's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries (mechanism, `general`)

Figure 11 :49:

> "An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."

Figure 11 :51:

> "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

The same interconnection is a diversifier when the residual is small and a propagation channel when the residual is large. Dense connections help at one scale and hurt at another. The Acemoglu et al. abstract states the world-side version: "as long as the magnitude of negative shocks affecting financial institutions are sufficiently small, a more densely connected financial network ... enhances financial stability. However, beyond a certain point, dense interconnections serve as a mechanism for the propagation of shocks." The Buldyrev et al. abstract reports the physical-side counterpart: "a broader degree distribution increases the vulnerability of interdependent networks to random failure, which is opposite to how a single network behaves."

This is a genuine low-overlap convergence: an economics result and a network-physics result, neither drawing on the framework, both matching the figure's valence rule.

**Mode:** `general` for valence, `specified` for the two domains. Refuter: an invariant beneficial or harmful value of one coupling across a documented change of residual in both directions.

### F7 — In a crisis the channel decays, not the ground (verification, `general`)

Figure 10 :24-26:

> "the ground does not decay, the channel does"
> "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"

What fails first in a cascading crisis is the *reading*: sensors saturate, alerts are pooled, transient warnings are dropped. The Cascading failure page gives the engineering form: "the speed of the control signal is no faster than the speed of the propagating power overload, i.e. since both the control signal and the electrical power are moving at the same speed, it is not possible to isolate the outage by sending a warning ahead to isolate the element." The Italy blackout record reports the human form: "the Swiss control room operator was not aware of the urgency."

Fig 10 :27-28 names the output of this state: "the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch." So a crisis that seems to appear "with no advanced warning" is often a failed channel, not an absent residual.

**Mode:** `general`. Refuter: a crisis in which the monitoring channel remains unsaturated, unpooled and faster than the propagating overload, and the collapse still arrives with no readable residual.

### F8 — The anchor is the ground, never the agreement (verification, `general`)

Figure 2 :29:

> "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."

Figure 2 :35:

> "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

For this question the anchor is the physical instrument (frequency, pressure, flow) and the settlement event (a payment clears or does not). Models that agree with one another about "systemic risk" are one pool talking. Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

**Mode:** `general`. Refuter: a verification procedure in which the verdict changes because two expert panels agreed, with no new instrument or settlement record.

### F9 — Risk must be indexed per slot, not averaged into a single number (method, `general`)

Figure 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Figure 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

A portfolio-level systemic-risk index is a filter for allocating attention. It cannot be a witness for what amplified a particular crisis, because the live slot is exactly what the average removes. The Scheffer et al. review, opened 2026-11-22, is relevant here: "Complex dynamical systems, ranging from ecosystems to financial markets and the climate, can have tipping points at which a sudden shift to a contrasting dynamical regime may occur." Generic early-warning signals are filters on a time series, not a per-slot ground.

**Mode:** `general`. Refuter: a measurement in which an aggregated index identifies the failing slot with no loss and no second per-slot reading.

**The gap.** No figure line names the coupling that carries a particular crisis, gives the sign of its loop in that instance, or supplies the threshold at which damping becomes amplification. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence," and Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." The missing thing is a source-discriminating, per-slot instrument: a reading that distinguishes a positive-feedback dependency from a merely correlated one. Characterised in effect terms, not held. "A search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence" means this is a stop, not a denial that an answer exists.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the exemplar's Seat Map rule: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A persistent amplification is measured in a loop whose returned signal demonstrably reduces its input, with no second positive loop and no external drive (breaks F1).
2. A coupled failure is documented in which the residual is non-increasing at every cross-network handoff and the damage still grows, with no hidden slot (breaks F2).
3. Two network structures are shown to couple with no intervening state carrying the influence (breaks F3).
4. One intervention restores both the single-network and the coupled-intersection regions with no categorically different residue (breaks F4).
5. A coupling keeps the same valence across a documented change of residual in both directions (breaks F6).
6. First-order enumeration fully accounts for a cross-network cascade with no third-order residual (breaks F5).

If any fires, the shape is wrong in that domain and the affected claim is withdrawn. Until one is run, nothing here may be labelled `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Lead with F1 and F2: the cycle and its sign, then the compounding remainder. Those are the figure lines that actually govern the question.
2. State the coupling medium as an unknown to be measured, not assumed (F3). "Interconnection" is too vague to have a sign.
3. Unbundle the coupled system before running any stress test; a single lever clears the wrong region (F4).
4. Instrument the channel first (F7, F8). A crisis that appears warning-free may be a saturated meter, not an absent signal.
5. Publish per-slot residuals, not one averaged index (F9).
6. Say `gap` for the specific coupling and threshold (F10). Do not name a favourite mechanism and call it a derivation.

## 6. Verdict of this audit

The shape survives; a coupling-specific answer does not. Under the figures, a crisis is amplified when a cross-network dependency closes a cycle with the positive sign, so that the remainder of each turn re-enters enlarged (F1, F2). The joint is a state, not a direct structure-to-structure bond (F3). The system is a union compound, so unbundling precedes search (F4), coupling valence flips with the residual (F6), the channel degrades before the ground does (F7), and the residual must be read per slot (F8, F9). Which coupling, which sign and which threshold is a declared `gap` (F10), reachable only by an instrument that discriminates sources, not by more agreement. Mode: `general` for the shape, `specified` for the crisis reading, never `grounded`. Final refuters are in section 3.
