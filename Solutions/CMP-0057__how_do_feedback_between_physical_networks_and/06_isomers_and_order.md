# Structure, order and analogues: cycle versus chain in coupled networks

**Mode: `specified` for the crisis application, `general` for the structural rules. Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading** (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks "how do feedback ... amplify crises". Two sub-derivations are owed beyond `01`:

1. **Structure and order:** what is the bond that closes a cascade, and what is the order of the composition that produces a system-of-systems failure?
2. **Analogue:** under Fig 12's definitions, are a physical network and a financial network isomers, a functional group, or something else? Does that change what can be concluded?

## DERIVATION

### Q1: What kind of bond is a feedback loop?

- Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
- Fig 12 :48: "A molecule is a bonded arrangement. A chain is the degenerate case: one bond type, no branching."
- The sustained cascade in the Cascading failure page, opened 2026-11-22, is "growing progressively as a result of positive feedback"; the Italy blackout entry describes communication failure that "produced a positive feedback on the power grid".
- ⇒ The cascade is a **cycle**, not a chain. A chain of failures is the degenerate spelling; it cannot return and so cannot be signed. The "feedback" in the question is the cycle, and its sign is the quantity that decides amplification or damping.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** a measured persistent growth in a system whose failure path is a genuine chain with no returned signal. If it holds, the cycle reading fails and a chain-based account is owed.

### Q2: What is the order of the composition?

- Fig 13 :74: "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."
- Fig 13 :75: "Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."
- A physical network and a financial network are both instances of the structure "network". Their coupling is therefore a **state** (power flow, settlement, a service level), and the legal first-order molecule is `network(state-carrier)network`, one crossing through a state.
- Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."
- The single-network cascade (a line trips, load shifts, another line trips) settles at first order against the ground and becomes an atom. The cross-network cascade is the **second-order** composition: (settled physical cascade) joined through a state to (settled financial cascade). It needs no new primitive.
- Fig 11 :52 gives the observed order: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three" (continuing "steps out"). So the order is not merely two; the residual that matters is several steps out.
- **Falsifier:** a documented coupled failure that is fully accounted for at first order, with no second-order composition and no third-order residual.

### Q3: Are the two networks isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Reading the two networks at the level of the substrate, both are the structure "network": entities relate and influence travels (Fig 13 :24). They share every structural atom. What differs is the state they carry and the bond that closes their loops (flow redistribution in the physical case, obligation propagation in the financial case). On the figure's definition they are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible if you record only that "both are networks", and decisive in behaviour.
- Reading them at the level of their concrete inventories (generators, lines, substations versus banks, payments, clearinghouses), they share almost no atoms and are **not** isomers; at best they share a functional group, the `state → structure → state` crossing.
- The stronger claim is the checkable one: *over the shared structural atom set, the two cascades are isomers whose different bonds decide the outcome*. The weaker claim is a taxonomy label. The exemplar's `06` makes the same move for GR and QM, so the method carries across domains; that is a reuse of the method, not a result imported into this question.
- **Falsifier:** a demonstration that one cascade can be written with an atom the other does not hold at the structural level (for example, one requires a state the other provably lacks), which kills the isomer claim and leaves the functional-group claim standing.

### Q4: Analogues named by the figure, and what they do and do not license

- **Catalyst** (Fig 12 :119): "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is." A clearinghouse, a grid operator or an emergency protocol can be a catalyst in a cascade: it persists across uses and changes what a later failure does. Whether a given institution is spent or regenerating is a ground question, not a figure verdict.
- **Cycle** (Fig 12 :121): already governed in Q1. The sign is the decision; the figure does not supply the sign for any particular loop.
- **Functional group** (Fig 12 :123): governs the weaker taxonomy in Q3.
- **Convergent derivation versus adopted import** (Fig 8 :50-52): "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them." The Buldyrev et al. and Acemoglu et al. results were derived independently in network physics and economics. They look like corroboration of the figure's valence and cycle rules; they count as convergence only because their provenance is recorded. Had we borrowed the terms "positive feedback" from them first and then mapped onto Fig 12 :121, that would be an adopted import with no shadow test.
- **Not licensed:** naming which institution is the catalyst, or assigning a numeric loop gain, from the figure. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."

## CONCLUSION

The cascade is a cycle (Q1), so the sign of the returned signal, not the length of the chain, decides amplification. The coupling is a state-bearing joint and the system-of-systems failure is a second-order composition whose residue is observed several steps out (Q2). The physical and financial networks are isomers at the structural atom level and a functional group at the inventory level (Q3). Catalyst, cycle and functional group are the analogues that transfer; none names a particular link or supplies a sign (Q4). The method carries from the exemplar, the verdicts do not.
