# Expert verification packet, the Tether on standards of evidence

**What this is:** a claim in a form a historian or philosopher of science can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the discriminator and per-domain verdicts, `general` on the convention structure, not `grounded`.** No archive reading was done for this packet. It is a shape and a route, not a history.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A standard of evidence is a convention until it decides an outcome. The question "discovery or pressure?" is a union, not a binary, so the answer is a discriminator with three outputs, ground-driven (G), pressure-driven (P), and compound, plus `gap` where the dated record is missing. A standard changes legitimately when the reference it ranges against changes, through an inward instrument already returning something or through a provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs. It changes illegitimately when it moves under a push while no new ground contact is recorded, through mutual drift, a shared hidden source, or a mirror chain. Because both causes can be written as "the field revised its evidentiary bar", only the dated record separates them. The framework returns that discriminator and refuses the name of any specific case.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 2 and 10

- **Statement:** the discriminator is the anchor test plus the convention rule. If the reference moves when frames push on it, it is not an anchor. If the standard decides an outcome, it has stopped being a convention.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29-30: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." Also Fig 2 :34: "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices." And Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one".
- **Refuter:** produce a standard of evidence that is itself the anchor, not moving when frames push, with the ground ranging against it. Or show a standard change with neither the reference changing nor any frame pushing and no third term available. Either result collapses F1 of the audit and the whole discriminator falls with it.

### C2: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 5, 6 and 9

- **Statement:** discovery reaches a standard by two opposed routes, INWARD (sharpen an instrument already returning something) and OUTWARD (import a standard from a frame whose closure differs, with source gate and shadow test). These are not to be read against each other, and an unattributed discovery names nothing.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :31: "Neither answer tells you much about the other. Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other." Fig 6 :32: "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something." Fig 5 :14: "mechanism broken: a new term is owed". Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."
- **What to check:** does a standard change attributed to discovery in your own case name a prior reading, or a donor frame, or neither? If neither, it is a label, not a cause.
- **Refuter:** show a discovery-driven change where no prior instrument was returning anything and no donor frame held the standard. That breaks the inward/outward pair.

### C3: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 2, 7 and 8

- **Statement:** pressure changes standards through three distinct collapses, mutual update, common cause and mirror chain, and only the dated record separates them from ground-driven changes.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." Fig 2 :13-15: "no anchor; each updates on the others / they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence". Fig 7 :12-13: "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not". Fig 8 :51: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."
- **What to check:** in a case you know, did the evidence pool shared by the agreeing parties come before their agreement, or was the agreement manufactured by the institution? That ordering is what the discriminator asks for.
- **Refuter:** show a standard changed under open institutional pressure where the change improved contact with an independently nameable anchor and the ground reading is dated before the decision. Reclassify that case as compound and P falls for it. Or show a complete dated record where both causes are visible yet no reader can order them; the case is then `gap` and the discriminator is inadequate for it.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that any named case, Lysenkoism, Deutsche Physik, the ASA p-value statement, evidence-based medicine or the replication crisis, has been correctly sorted. Only their public records were read, and only as secondary corroboration.
- Not that institutions are always corrupting, or that discoveries are always clean. A committee may follow a ground reading; a discovery may be framed by the standard it overturns.
- Not that prestige, agreement or the number of historians endorsing a reading is evidence. Fig 2 :40 disqualifies correlated agreement for a verdict.
- Not a new standard of evidence for historians, and not a periodisation of the history of science.
- Not `grounded`. No archive, instrument or dated case check was performed.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not more agreement.

1. **The decisive refuter for the shape:** a standard of evidence that does not move when frames push on it and against which the ground ranges. Then the convention/anchor distinction fails.
2. **For a case:** a dated record showing which came first, the ground reading or the rule change, what proto-instrument was already returning something or what donor frame held the new standard, and whether the old rule was refused (¬S) or merely supplemented.
3. **For the inward/outward pair:** name the worse instrument the better one was built from, or the frame whose closure differed. An unattributed discovery does not fire the discriminator.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29-30, :34 and Fig 10 :47. Three short passages. Do you accept the anchor test as a discriminator between a rule that tracks a reference and a rule that moves under a push?
2. Take one standards change you know. Write down the date of the relevant reading and the date of the rule change, in that order. If you cannot, the case is `gap`, which is a permitted answer.
3. Check C2 against that case. Was there a prior instrument already returning something, or a donor frame? If neither, is "discovery" doing any work?
4. Check C3 against that case. Which of the three collapses, if any, is visible in the record you just wrote down?
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement, and later agreement will not be pooled as corroboration.
