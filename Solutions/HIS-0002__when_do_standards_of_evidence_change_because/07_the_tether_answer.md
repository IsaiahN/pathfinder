# The Tether's answer: when standards of evidence change

**Mode: `specified` on the discriminator and the three per-domain verdicts, `general` on the convention structure, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a history. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A field's standard of evidence S changes. The question asks which cause fired: a new discovery (the ground returned something the old S could not register) or political and institutional pressure (frames pushed on S). The two produce the same sentence, "the field revised its evidentiary bar", so the reading cannot name the source. This is a union compound (Fig 9 :54), not one hard question and not a clean binary.

## 2. The answer, one loop per domain, verdicts kept separate

**D1, ground and discovery. Verdict: `triangulated`, legitimate re-anchoring when the discriminator returns G.** A standard changes because of a discovery when the thing it ranges against changed. The change comes by one of two opposed routes, which must not be read against each other (Fig 6 :31): INWARD, where an instrument already returning something is sharpened until the old rule fails (Fig 6 :32, Fig 5 :14), or OUTWARD, where a standard is imported from a frame whose closure differs, with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 6 :24). The frame's own claim to have discovered the new standard is not enough: an unattributed discovery names no instrument and no donor frame.

**D2, institutional and political. Verdict: `triangulated`, capture when the discriminator returns P.** A standard changes because of pressure when it moves under a push and no new ground contact is recorded. The figures give three named failure modes: mutual update, where frames converge on whoever had the most influence (Fig 2 :13-15, :34); common cause, where frames never talk and still agree because they share a pool (Fig 2 :18-21); and the mirror chain, where "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not" (Fig 7 :12-13). The deepest point is Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." A committee that defines the standard and then scores work by it is doing exactly this. And Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." D2 has no nameable anchor. That is the finding, not a gap.

**D3, the record and the reader of it. Verdict: `triangulated`, but only a record settles any specific case.** From inside a frame, a ground-driven change and a pressure-driven change look the same (Fig 6 :27). They are distinguishable only by lineage. Fig 8 :51: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ." Fig 10 :62-63 requires the ruling to carry its residual, reasoning and date. Where that record is absent, the honest verdict is `gap`, not a guess.

**Union verdict.** Because one episode can contain both causes, the discriminator returns three values: G, P, **compound**, plus `gap` where the record will not close. The question's forced choice is refused. This is not averaging, and it is not a fourth cause: compound means both causes are separately visible in the dated record.

## 3. The discriminator itself

1. Is there a dated change in S, and is it one rule or several bundled? Split if several (Fig 9 :4-5, :54).
2. Which changed first in the record, the ground reading g or the frame decision d? (Fig 9 :27-28.)
3. Did the reference A move when pushed? If yes, A was never an anchor (Fig 2 :29-30, :34). Did S decide an outcome? If yes, S had stopped being a convention (Fig 10 :47).
4. If G: inward or outward? Name the proto-instrument already returning something, or the donor frame (Fig 6 :29-31).
5. If the old S was shown false, is the new term a negation of it (¬S) or merely an addition? An addition without a refusal is an accumulation (Fig 5 amendment, Operators table :57).

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any particular episode was discovery-driven or pressure-driven. No dated case was checked here.
- Not that institutions are always corrupting. A committee can follow a ground reading; the discriminator orders them in time. Where the ground is too consequential to ignore, pressure stops (the Lysenko record's Judt quotation, a world source).
- Not that prestige or agreement is evidence. Fig 2 :40 disqualifies correlated agreement for a verdict.
- Not a periodisation of the history of science and not a new standard of evidence for historians. It is a test for a historian to run.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here has been settled by an archive or an instrument.

## 5. What would settle it, and what would settle a case

**The shape** would be settled by the decisive refuter: a standard of evidence that is itself the anchor, not moving when frames push and against which the ground ranges. That would collapse the convention/anchor distinction.

**A case** is settled by a dated record, not by more argument. The record must show which came first, the reading or the rule; what instrument was already returning something or what donor frame held the new standard; and whether the old rule was refused or merely supplemented. Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."

## 6. The honest stop

The figures return the discriminator and refuse the name. Fig 13 records that the framework can say the answer is out of reach and "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"; it cannot say which primitive is missing without already holding it. Here the missing thing is not a primitive but a record. Where the record exists, the discriminator can fire and the case is checkable. Where it does not, `gap` is the answer. This derivation stops at that limit and records the lineage so the stop is checkable.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not decide whether a given change in standards of evidence was a discovery or a power move, and it does not take sides in that argument. It says the question is a union, not a binary, so the answer is a discriminator with three outputs. A standard is a convention until it decides an outcome; you tell a legitimate change from a captured one by asking whether the thing the standard ranges against moved, or whether the standard itself moved when frames pushed on it. Discovery reaches a new standard through one of two opposite routes, a sharpened instrument or a provenanced import from another field's frame, and you must not read one against the other. Pressure works through three named collapses: mutual drift, a shared hidden source, and a mirror chain where agreement compounds while evidence does not. Because both causes write the same sentence, only a dated record separates them, and where that record is missing the answer is `gap`, not a guess. What is offered is the shape any answer must have, three refuters written in advance, and an instruction to bring the record, not more agreement.
