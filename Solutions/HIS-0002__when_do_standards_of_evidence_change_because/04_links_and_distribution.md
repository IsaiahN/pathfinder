## Two things in the world record that bear directly on the question

1. **The same source can carry both causes.** Ioannidis (2005) is a ground result (simulations) that changed standards, and it also names interests and prejudice as bias sources. The replication-crisis record adds "publish or perish" and publication bias while also recording replication failures as the trigger. This is why the answer returns a third verdict, compound, rather than a forced binary (Fig 9 :54, "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again.").
2. **Pressure has a bound.** The Lysenko record quotes Tony Judt noting that nuclear physicists were left alone because "Stalin may well have been mad but he was not stupid", and the Deutsche Physik record notes the movement's science was "not accepted, supported, or used by the Third Reich". Where the ground was too consequential to ignore, the anchor was not captured. This is corroboration, not a figure line, and it is a reason to ask how much contact a standard actually has before declaring it captured.
