## 0. The question in effect terms

> When do standards of evidence change because of new discoveries, and when do they change because of political or institutional pressures?

Restated as an effect: a field holds a standard of evidence S, a rule in its library that decides what counts as settled. We observe S change. The residual is **what caused the change**. Two candidate causes are offered:

- **Cause G (discovery):** the ground returned something the old S could not register. A measurement, a failed replication, a refutation. The change tracks a new contact with an anchor.
- **Cause P (pressure):** frames pushed on S. An institution, a committee, a funder, a room, a prestige gradient. The change tracks agreement or influence rather than a new contact with the ground.

What each leads to:

- If G, the new S is a **re-anchoring**. The rule now registers something the ground pays for. The reach grows.
- If P, the old S was **not an anchor**. It moved when pushed, so it was a convention that had come to decide an outcome. The drift is coherent and, by Fig 2 :34, nobody notices from inside.

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term (Formula, "Direction"):** a *discriminator*, that is, a test that sorts a dated case of standard change into G, P, or both, using the record of which changed first and what the standard was ranging against. Without binding this, the question is a preference for one cause, and the loop cannot check its own direction. The discriminator is stated before the findings and is refutable.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** two candidate causes for one observed change. This is a **union compound**, not one hard question. Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." A single episode can be both. Fig 9 :55-56: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."
- **Symmetry:** asymmetric. Cause G runs one way: ground to frame, and the ground does not update on what frames think (Fig 2 :25-26). Cause P is symmetric among frames: mutual update, "they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence" (Fig 2 :14-15). A test that assumes symmetry would miss the difference, which is why the discriminator compares the direction of travel.
- **Scale:** the change itself happens at the level of a field's convention, a rule in Γ. Its cause can be below that level (an instrument, an observation) or at the same level (a committee, a room). The change of a standard is therefore a **cross-level event**, and the round trip between levels loses information (Fig 4 :27-30).

### One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1, ground and discovery** | instruments, experiments, failed replications, refutations | residual R and settlement, "verified only by what pays" | the measurement, the experiment, the counter, the patient outcome | a domain; the anchor is nameable and does not update |
| **D2, institutional and political** | committees, editors, funders, states, prestige, rooms | agreement, influence, convention | **none nameable**: the standard moves when pushed, so it is not an anchor | this is the collapse, not a second ground; see F3 |
| **D3, the record and the seat that reads it** | historians, dated decisions, provenance, citations, outside frames | provenance, "the record of where each came from" | the dated record, which does not move when a later frame reinterpret it | a domain; the only place the two causes can be separated |

D2 has no nameable anchor. By the census rule this means it is not a ground domain. That is the finding, not a gap: political pressure is precisely the absence of an anchor. Claiming an anchor for D2 (the institution's own procedure, the party line, the editor's rule) would be to promote a frame to an anchor, which is the failure Fig 2 :34 describes.

### Partition or union

Union. G and P overlap. Ioannidis (2005) records a case where a ground result (bias simulations) and interests ("financial and other interests and prejudices") act in the same field at the same time. The discriminator must therefore report three outcomes, not two: G, P, or compound. Unbundle before searching (Fig 9 :54).

### Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. Establish that there is a fact to sort: is there a dated change in S at all, and is it one change or several? Fig 9 :4-5: "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."
2. Ask which changed first, the ground contact or the rule. Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit". The words "discovery" and "pressure" are borrowed from the case; describe the effect first.
3. Decide whether S was an anchor or a convention at the time it changed (Fig 2 :29-30, Fig 10 :47). This precedes any judgement about whether the new S is good.
4. Only then assess settlement. Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." A better argument for a standard cannot repair a record that never had a ground reading.
5. Refuse before mint (Formula step 2). If the old S is shown false, the owed term is a negation, not a replacement convention. Fig 12 amendment: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate" (Operators table :57).

## 3. Findings

### F1: A standard of evidence is a convention until it decides an outcome, and the discriminator is the anchor test

Fig 2 :29-30:

> "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."

Fig 2 :25-26:

> "pinned: it does not update on A or B / both range against it; it is not a third party to the disagreement"

Fig 10 :47:

> "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"

A standard of evidence is not itself the ground. It is the field's rule for ranging against the ground. So the figure line does not govern the standard directly as an anchor; it governs the thing the standard is about. When S changes, ask two separate questions:

1. Did the thing S ranges against change (a new instrument, a new observation)? Then S was tracking an anchor and the change is a re-anchoring.
2. Did S itself change when frames pushed on it? Then S had stopped being a convention and had become a verdict-bearing instrument, and the change is a capture.

**Strain test.** Reading "anchor" as "standard of evidence" strains, because a standard is a rule in Γ and the anchor is the immovable reference outside Γ. Re-census: two quantities, the rule and the reference. Fig 2 governs the reference; Fig 10 :47 governs the rule. No strain after re-census.

**Verdict: `triangulated`** across Fig 2 (ground and alignment) and Fig 10 (maintainer seat), two figures with low overlap in subject matter. The anchor test and the convention-collapse line are independently stated.

**Refuter:** produce a standard of evidence that does not move when frames push on it and against which the ground itself is said to range. Then the anchor/convention distinction collapses and F1 is withdrawn. A second refuter: show a case where S changed without either the reference changing or any frame pushing on it, with no third term available.

### F2: Discovery changes a standard by one of two opposed routes, never by reading one against the other

Formula step 7 and Fig 6 :28-31:

> "Which makes reachability two questions, not one. The instruments are opposed."
> "inward: is our representation adequate to hold it? The instrument extends access."
> "outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels."

> "Neither answer tells you much about the other. Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other."

And Fig 6 :32:

> "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."

**INWARD:** a new observation fails to resolve under the old S, the instrument is sharpened from a worse instrument already returning something, and S is refitted to what the better instrument can now read. Fig 5 :14: "mechanism broken: a new term is owed." In the history of scientific standards, the replication failures catalogued in the replication-crisis literature are exactly such a return: the old standard (p < 0.05 on a single study) kept producing readings that did not resolve, and the response was preregistration, larger samples and replication, not a new argument alone.

**OUTWARD (IMPORT):** a new standard arrives from a frame whose closure differs. Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Evidence-based medicine is a documented case of this shape: Eddy (1990, quoted in the EBM record) tied policy to evidence "instead of standard-of-care practices or the beliefs of experts", importing formal guideline method from a different frame into clinical decision making. Such an import carries a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 8 :9-13) and is "debited against that frame's independence" (Fig 6 :24).

**Important limit.** Neither route is a "new discovery" in the naive sense. The inward route needs a proto-instrument already returning something; the outward route needs a frame that already holds the standard. Fig 6 :34-35: "the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there." So a standard change attributed to "discovery" must name the prior reading or the donor frame. An unattributed discovery is not a cause, it is a label.

**Verdict: `triangulated`** for the two-route structure (Fig 5 and Fig 6, plus Formula step 7). `single-source` for each specific historical case, since the figure supplies only the shape and the case must be checked against a dated record.

**Refuter:** show a standard change driven by a discovery where no prior instrument was returning anything and no donor frame held the new standard. Then the inward/outward pair is incomplete.

### F3: Pressure changes a standard through named collapses, and the field cannot see them from inside

Fig 2 :4-7 names the first two collapses:

> "collapse 1: mutual update / (coherent drift)"
> "collapse 2: common cause / (hidden shared source)"

Fig 2 :13-15:

> "no anchor; each updates on the others / they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence"

Fig 2 :34 gives the mechanism of invisibility:

> "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."

Fig 7 :12-13 and :35:

> "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"
> "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."

Fig 1 :27 is the deepest point for a field setting its own evidentiary bar:

> "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."

A committee that both defines what counts as evidence and scores work by that definition is scoring itself with a quantity it also produces. The 1948 VASKhNIL session in the Lysenko record declared one theory "the only correct theory" after the Central Committee approved the report; the same closure was then handed along to Poland, Czechoslovakia, East Germany and China. That is the mirror chain of Fig 7, not a discovery. Lysenko's own reported rejection of "control groups and statistics" is a direct attack on the instrument, which is how a pressure-driven change in S becomes self-protecting. The Deutsche Physik record supplies the parallel in physics: relativity was rejected with "They were not convinced by the empirical evidence for relativity", and the campaign relied on institutional appointment power rather than a new measurement.

**A useful counter-case from the same record.** The Lysenko article quotes Tony Judt on why Stalin left nuclear physicists alone: "Stalin may well have been mad but he was not stupid." Where the ground was too consequential to ignore, pressure stopped. This is corroboration, not a figure line, and it is recorded as such.

**Verdict: `triangulated`** across Fig 1, Fig 2 and Fig 7 (three figures, different mechanisms: frame-internal metric, anchor update, mirror chain). Noaveraging; the three are separate failure modes that can co-occur.

**Refuter:** show a case where a standard changed under open institutional pressure and the change *improved* contact with an independently nameable anchor, with a dated record showing the ground reading came first. Then the pressure verdict for that case is overturned and it is reclassified as compound.

### F4: Only the record can separate the two causes, because from inside they look the same

Fig 6 :27:

> "Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."

Fig 8 :50-52:

> "But shared atoms are not the signature. Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."
> "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."

Fig 10 :62-63:

> "A ruling carries provenance like an atom."
> "The residual it answered, the reasoning, the date, in a record the seats below can read."

This is why the question is a history question and the deliverable is a discriminator. A standard of evidence that changed for ground reasons and one that changed for pressure reasons can be written in identical prose: "the field revised its evidentiary bar." Only the lineage, the dated order of the reading and the decision, separates them. A historian who reports only the new standard has reported the artefact without the route, which Fig 13 forbids: the lineage is the proof.

Fig 10 :59-60 adds the standing condition:

> "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"
> "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."

A historian of standards who also wants a particular standard to win has a stake in the reading and must declare it.

**Verdict: `triangulated`** across Fig 8 and Fig 10. low overlap (union/provenance versus seat discipline).

**Refuter:** show a case where the dated record is complete and both causes are visible, yet a competent reader still cannot tell which changed first. Then the discriminator is inadequate and the case is a `gap` for that record, not a verdict.

### F5: The two causes are a union, so the honest third verdict is compound

Fig 9 :4-5 and :54-58 (quoted in §1) require unbundling. Ioannidis (2005) states Corollary 5: "The greater the financial and other interests and prejudices in a scientific field, the less likely the research findings are to be true." The same paper is itself a ground result (simulations showing that "for most study designs and settings, it is more likely for a research claim to be false than true") that was used to change standards. One source therefore carries both causes. The replication-crisis record adds the institutional side: "publish or perish" culture and publication bias are named causes of the crisis, and the same record names "Emphasize triangulation, not just replication" as a remedy, which is Fig 2 :35 in different words.

So the answer to the question as posed is a refusal of the forced choice. The right output is a three-way sort with a provenance requirement. A case assigned to "discovery" or "pressure" without a dated record is a preference, not a finding.

**Verdict: `general`** for the union claim, `specified` for the three-way sort. Triangulated across Fig 9 and the world record; the figure alone does not decide any particular case.

**Refuter:** a census of standard changes in which every case sorts cleanly to exactly one cause with a complete dated record. Then the union reading is unnecessary for that census, though it would still be figure-supported.

### F6: What survives, and what is a gap

| claim | mode | figure |
|---|---|---|
| A standard of evidence is a convention until it decides an outcome | `general`, structural | Fig 10 :47; Fig 2 :43 |
| The discriminator is whether the reference moves when pushed | `specified`, checkable in a record | Fig 2 :29-30; Fig 2 :34 |
| Discovery changes standards inwardly or by import, never by conflating the two | `specified` | Fig 6 :28-31; Fig 5 :14 |
| Pressure changes standards by anchor-update, common-cause and mirror-chain collapses | `specified` | Fig 1 :27; Fig 2 :4-7; Fig 7 :35 |

The last row is not a silence. The figures return the shape of the test and refuse the name. Fig 13 :134 states it plainly: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". The framework cannot say which primitive is missing without already holding it, and it cannot say which cause fired in 1948 or 2016 without the record. That is an honest stop.

## 4. Strains and re-census

**Strain 1, resolved.** Reading Fig 2's "anchor" as the standard of evidence. Re-census separates the rule (Fig 10 :47) from the reference (Fig 2 :29-30). Two quantities, no strain.

**Strain 2, live and named.** A discovery that changes a standard is itself described in the vocabulary of the old standard. Kuhn's SEP entry records that anomalies turn up inside normal science and that a paradigm change "typically changes goals, standards, linguistic meaning, key scientific practices". So the "new discovery" is not vocabulary-free. Formula step 2 states the limit: "Every check here runs on the MECHANISM and none on the DIRECTION OF THE QUESTION." The remedy is the discipline, not another figure: bind the discriminator as an ordinary term and make the *record*, not the vocabulary, carry the separation. This strain is not smoothed.

**Strain 3, live and named.** Institutional changes in standards often follow ground findings (ASA 2016 followed the metascience literature; EBM followed Cochrane and Eddy). A committee decision is not automatically Cause P. Re-census: the seat that decides is not the same as the cause that moved it. The discriminator must order the reading and the decision in time. This is why F5 returns compound as a live third outcome.

After re-census: three strains, two named as live, none hidden.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per Seat Map :287, "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device". The shape is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A standard of evidence changes and a dated record shows a new instrument or experiment the old standard could not register, with the ground reading preceding the rule change. This confirms Cause G; the shape is refuted only if no such separation can be shown in principle.
2. A standard changes by a recorded committee, editorial or state decision, the standard moves when pushed, and no new ground contact is recorded. This confirms Cause P; the shape is refuted only if the same decision is shown to have been forced by a reading the old standard could not register.
3. **Decisive refuter.** Show a standard of evidence that is itself the anchor: it does not move when frames push on it, and the ground ranges against it. Then the convention/anchor distinction fails and F1, F2 and F3 all fall.
4. **Record refuter.** Show a standard change for which no dated lineage exists and no instrument or donor frame can be named. The cause is then `gap` for that case, and the discriminator cannot fire. This is a limit, not a silent failure.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Lead with the discriminator, not the framework. Ask which changed first in the record, the reading or the rule.
2. State the mode on every claim (F1 to F6). Nothing here is `grounded`.
3. Keep the three verdicts separate. Never average "discovery" and "pressure" into a single percentage of influence.
4. Require the dated record. A standard change without a lineage is a shape with no route, and Fig 13 makes the lineage the proof.
5. Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Send the discriminator and its refuter, not the full architecture.
6. Declare the historian's stake where it cannot be removed (Fig 10 :60).

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is there a fact?** Yes, a dated change in S. If several rules changed on different subsets, split first (Fig 9 :4-5).
2. **Which changed first?** The reading or the rule. Fig 9 :27-28: describe the effect before naming a frame.
3. **Was S an anchor or a convention?** If it moved when pushed, convention (Fig 2 :30, Fig 10 :47).
4. **What route did G take?** INWARD instrument or OUTWARD import. Never read one against the other (Fig 6 :31).
5. **What collapse did P take?** mutual update, common cause, or mirror chain (Fig 2 :4-7, Fig 7 :35).
6. **Settlement?** Only a dated record plus a ground reading settles it. Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."
7. **Gap?** Which specific case is not decided by any figure. Recorded, with the instruction that the record, not another frame, is owed.

## 8. Updated verdict

The figures decide the **shape**, not the history. A standard of evidence is a convention until it decides an outcome. It changes legitimately when the reference it ranges against changes, by an inward instrument or a provenanced import. It changes illegitimately when it moves under pressure, by anchor update, common cause or mirror chain, and the field cannot see this from inside. Because the two produce identical prose, only the dated record separates them, and the framework must declare `gap` for any case where that record is absent. The question's forced choice is refused: the three-way discriminator is the answer. Mode `specified` on the discriminator and per-domain verdicts, `general` on the convention structure, nothing `grounded`. Strains after re-census: three named, two live, none hidden.
