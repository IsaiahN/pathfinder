# Are the two causes isomers? Structure, order and analogues

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

Two standards changes can be written with the same sentence: "the field revised its evidentiary bar". One is ground-driven, one is pressure-driven. Are they two spellings of one thing, or two different substances sharing the same surface description? And what order does the discriminator impose on the evidence?

## DOMAIN CENSUS (supporting derivation)

Atoms held for this derivation: a standard S (a term in Γ), a reference A (the anchor, outside Γ), a ground reading g, a frame decision d, a record r with dates, and negation ¬ (refusal). Bonds held: the seven plus ¬, in particular → sequence, ⋛ comparison, − subtraction, ¬ negation. No new primitive is available without import.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Are "discovery-driven" and "pressure-driven" isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Write the two cases over the shared atom set `{S, A, g, d, r}`:
  - **G case:** `g → S`. The reading arrives, then the rule is refitted. The bond is → sequence, and ¬ may be present if the old rule is refused.
  - **P case:** `d → S`. The decision arrives, then the rule is refitted. The bond is also →, but the operand is a frame decision, not a ground reading.
- Same atoms, same bond type, different operand at the head. By Fig 12 :117 they are not the same substance: the behaviour differs (one re-anchors, one drifts), and the formula "a standard changed" cannot tell them apart.
- **Stronger form:** if the P case also contains `A → d` (the anchor was made to update on the decision), then the two cases share every atom and differ in the direction of the bond to A. That is the Fig 2 panel-1 versus panel-3 difference. Fig 2 :34: "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if the G case and the P case can be reduced to the same ordered molecule over `{S, A, g, d, r}` with the same operand at every slot, they are one substance and the three-way discriminator is a distinction without a difference. Alternatively, if a case can be written both ways with no dated record to separate them and both readings make identical predictions, the isomer claim fails and the case is `gap`.

### Q2: What order does the discriminator impose?

- Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
- Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
- The chain for this question:
  1. **perception:** is there a dated change in S? (If not, no residual.)
  2. **vocabulary:** do we have terms for what the change affords? Convention, anchor, instrument, import. Fig 3 :7: "it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked".
  3. **objective:** what would count as progress here? Answer: sorting the case into G, P, compound or `gap`.
  4. **planning:** order the record, check which of g or d came first.
  5. **learn and carry:** keep the dated lineage so the next case can be compared.
- The middle link (2 to 3) is the one that usually breaks: "having terms for what a situation affords was taken for a dictionary and it is a grammar" (Fig 12 :115). A field that has the word "evidence" but no term for the direction of the rule's movement cannot say what would count as a standard change. That is a vocabulary failure, not a data failure.
- Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit". So step 2 precedes the choice of Kuhn, Ioannidis or Lysenko as an analogue.

### Q3: Negation: what a refutation changes

- Operators table :57: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate".
- Fig 5 amendment: "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so". A term that refuses another "removes not a term but a term's standing to be composed over".
- When a ground reading shows the old standard S is false, the owed term is `¬S`: S loses its standing to be composed over, and the reach falls even though nothing was deleted from the record (Fig 6 :42-43). This is the inward route of F2 in structural form.
- A pressure change that merely replaces S with S' without refusing S is an accumulation, not a correction. It is the "flat formula" failure of Fig 12 :14-17: "the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not / nothing downstream can tell them apart, and nothing signals that anything was lost". A new evidentiary bar that does not say what was wrong with the old one carries no correction.

### Q4: Analogues, and where each stops

| analogue | what it carries | where it stops |
|---|---|---|
| Courts of law | "Courts of law rule on evidence, which neither side can move" (*Decoding Tacit Knowledge*, 2026). This is Fig 2 :24-25 in an institution. | A court is itself an institution that can be captured. It is an instance of the anchor rule, not the rule. |
| Replication and Registered Reports | APS states that replicability is "a cornerstone of science" and that reports are "published regardless of the size of the effect or statistical significance of the result". This is Fig 2 :35 made into a publication rule. | Replicating the same protocol is not triangulating with a low-overlap frame. Fig 8 :50-51 requires closures that differ. |
| Kuhnian paradigm change | SEP: a paradigm change "typically changes goals, standards, linguistic meaning, key scientific practices". This is the shape of a standard change driven by an internal reorganization. | Kuhn's account also makes the choice social, which is why it cannot by itself sort G from P. It is a bridge source, not a witness for either side. |
| The metamerism instrument | *The Hard Problem is About Source*: the spectrometer resolved green where the eye could not. The analogue is the dated record resolving discovery from pressure. | A record is not a sensor. It can be forged, lost or written after the event. It needs a source gate like any import (Fig 6 :12-13). |

All four analogues are secondary. None overrules the figure lines.

## CONCLUSION

The two causes are **isomers over a shared atom set**, differing in which operand sits at the head of the sequence: a ground reading or a frame decision. They are invisible in the surface sentence "the standard changed" and decisive in behaviour. The discriminator is a dependency-ordered chain whose middle link is vocabulary, not data. A genuine ground-driven change carries a negation of the old rule; a pressure change that only adds a new rule is an accumulation, and Fig 12 names that failure. The four analogues are useful but each stops before the figure rule it instantiates.
