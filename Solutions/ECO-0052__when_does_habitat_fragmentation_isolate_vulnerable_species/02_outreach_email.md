# Outreach draft - check the separator first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter below survive the reader's own check. If the separator fails on contact, there is nothing else worth showing them.

---

**Subject:** One checkable separator for the fragmentation "good or bad" dispute

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about habitat fragmentation. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the "isolate versus create opportunity" question is a union of overlapping conditions, not one dial with a right amount of fragmentation. From a habitat rule I hold, four things follow:
>
> 1. Fragmentation substitutes a habitat; it does not subtract one. A patch has new edge, a matrix, new microclimate and new actors. So "when does it isolate" becomes: when does the substituted habitat fail to reproduce the contact a species' persistence required? The failures are silent: what was not reproduced stays invisible until the population fails, and what was introduced stays invisible until it acts.
> 2. "Vulnerable" is a relation to that residual, not a property a species carries. The same edge that costs an interior specialist can help a generalist. So verdicts are per species and per scale. An averaged "fragmentation is good or bad" is the wrong object.
> 3. The opportunity is a different slot's result, not compensation paid to the isolated population. A landscape-scale richness gain from habitat heterogeneity or turnover does not repair the local population that lost its contact. Both can be true at once.
> 4. The two habitat modes differ. Where no one maintains the habitat, what survives is what the ground kept. Where a seat acts, introduction is available and exclusion is not: you can add a corridor, you cannot subtract the fact of contact. That is permanent work, not a solved state.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that there is a universal patch size, edge distance, corridor width or habitat fraction at which the sign flips. The framework returns a shape, not a number. I am also not claiming that the opposed reviews are two independent witnesses; they largely draw on the same studies, so their disagreement is a fact about scale and measure, not a triangulation.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before this message:**
> 1. a measured case where a population's required contact is preserved unchanged across a substitution and it persists;
> 2. a list of vulnerable species whose membership is invariant across residuals and scales;
> 3. a ground reading where a landscape diversity gain restores the same population's own local residual;
> 4. a derivation from the figures that returns a universal numerical threshold.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the affected claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Canonical figures and formula (local extract with line numbers): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` and `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md` in the public repository branch.
> - The habitat figure (Fig 11) is the one that governs this residual: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - The architecture essay that states the habitat and ground rules: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, the framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
