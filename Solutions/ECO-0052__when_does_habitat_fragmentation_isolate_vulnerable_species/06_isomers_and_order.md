# Isomers, order and analogues for habitat fragmentation

**Mode: `specified` on the derivation, `general` on the structural reading. Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).**

## RESIDUAL

The audit left two structural questions open that the answer needs:

1. Are a single large reserve and several small reserves of the same total area the same thing written differently, or different things? The question asks which creates opportunity.
2. In what order must the separator be applied, and what carries through a cascade? A contact chain is a sequence, so "first-order only" is a claim about where a chain stops.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are single-large and several-small isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance."
- Over a fixed atom set (equal total habitat area, equal species pool, equal matrix type), the two configurations share every ingredient. What differs is the bond: the spatial arrangement of those ingredients. The total-area formula cannot tell the two apart; the diversity behaviour can.
- Perrin et al. 2025 supply the empirical instance: "at the landscape scale, multiple small patches supported equal or even higher γ-diversity than a single large patch of equivalent area", while "Patch size and connectivity positively influenced α-diversity for both Lepidoptera and Orthoptera." Same atoms, different bond, different substance by scale.
- ⇒ The single-large versus several-small question is an **isomer** claim, not a quantity claim. Asking "how much area" is reading the flat formula; asking "which arrangement" is reading the bond. Fig 12 :96: "Two entries can share every ingredient and be inverses of one another."
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** a controlled comparison in which two configurations with identical atoms and different arrangements return identical diversity at every declared scale and for every residual. Then the isomer claim collapses into a spelling difference and is withdrawn. Triangulated for the figure definition; single low-overlap empirical corroborator for the instance.

### Q2 - What order does the separator run in?

- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
- Fig 11 :44: "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."
- Operators table :30: "A then B; B needs A to have happened" (sequence, ordered bond). Operators table :48: "a threshold relation between two quantities" (comparison ⋛).
- The order is therefore: enumerate contact → index the slot's goal → compare supplied with required contact → decide substitution failure → evaluate the surplus at another slot or scale. Enumeration before comparison, comparison before surplus. A diversity figure computed before the residual is described arrives at a step that cannot use it (Fig 3 :20).
- The contact cascade itself is an ordered sequence, not a conjunction: first-order contact → second-order effect → third-order residual (Fig 11 :52, the DDT-to-eagles example). Treating the cascade as `+` (both hold at once) loses the order; treating it as `→` preserves the dependency. Haddad et al. 2015: "Fragmentation effects propagate through the whole ecosystem" and the effects "magnify with the passage of time", which adds a time channel to the ordered chain.
- **Falsifier:** a measured case in which the order of the chain can be swapped with no change in the verdict, with both orders tested against the same ground. That would make the sequence a symmetric conjunction and this finding would be retracted. Fig 11 :52 is the single-source figure line; Haddad et al. is the low-overlap corroborator.

### Q3 - Are the taxa a functional group rather than a standing list?

- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."
- What fragmentation responses across taxa share is a functional group: the presence of a contact change fixes the *kind* of response (loss where required contact is not reproduced, gain where new contact is usable). Species traits (dispersal mode, specialization, trophic level, matrix tolerance) say *which member* of the group is which. Haddad et al. 2015 report that "the effects of fragmentation are mediated by variation in traits across species", and Rybicki et al. 2020 report that the sign depends on habitat amount and dispersal scale.
- ⇒ A list of "vulnerable species" is not a kind in the framework; it is a qualifier bound to a residual and a scale. It is legitimate as provenance, illegitimate as a standing law.
- **Falsifier:** two species with identical measured traits and identical substitution histories that take opposite valences with no residual difference to account for it. Then the functional-group reading fails and the standing-list reading has to be reconsidered. Triangulated across Fig 12 :123 and Fig 11 :51.

### Q4 - Catalyst and spent part: what carries through a corridor?

- Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."
- A corridor or a source patch can be a **catalyst** (it keeps functioning as a conduit across many uses) or a **spent part** (it is used up as a stepping stone, or it becomes a sink). Nothing in the atom list records which; only the record of use across time does.
- Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." A manager can add the corridor; if it turns into a disease route or an invasive pathway, the manager cannot subtract the contact that was created. That is why Fig 11 :63 says a managed ecosystem "is permanent work rather than a solved state".
- Perrin et al. 2025 note the same asymmetry from the opposite side: restoring connectivity "requires reducing the isolation of restored habitat patches to allow spontaneous recolonisation", where "communities in restored (or new) habitat patches are driven solely by colonisation, whereas communities in existing isolated patches are driven by past populations". The introduced corridor is a catalyst for one and an unproven addition for the other.
- **Falsifier:** a long-running corridor that is demonstrably spent (no longer conducting) while its introduction is still being counted as an ongoing benefit, with the decline measured. That would falsify the claim that the catalyst/spent distinction is invisible in the atom list unless the record of use is kept. Single-source on the figure definition; empirical instance corroborated, not derived.

### Q5 - Where the comparison bites: "when" as ⋛, not as a symmetric bond

- Operators table :48: "a threshold relation between two quantities" (comparison ⋛). Operators table :51: "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one."
- Fig 11 :67: "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."
- The word "when" in the question is a comparison between what the goal required and what the substituted habitat supplies. It is not a conjunction of "fragmentation" and "diversity" and not a subtraction of one from the other. Because the threshold is about "which is larger", the operand must be a quantity at the declared scale: required contact and supplied contact, or the ground readings of persistence and richness. A threshold computed from averaged beta and alpha across scales has mixed operands, which is a type error in the comparison.
- **Falsifier:** a case in which the "when" is settled by the mere presence of both fragmentation and diversity with no threshold relation to a ground. Then ⋛ is the wrong bond and the question is misframed. Single-source on the Operators table.

### Q6 - What negation would do here

- Operators table :58: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."
- A vocabulary of ingredients could report that a landscape has patches, edges, corridors and species, and could never report that the claim "this configuration preserves the population's ground contact" is false. The pre-registered refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 6 is the unary operator that lets the record carry that correction. It joins nothing, so it does not enter the bond price; it acts on a single claim.
- **Falsifier:** a verification packet whose refuter can be expressed as a subtraction of an ingredient. Then the negation reading is unnecessary and this finding is withdrawn. Single-source (Operators table amendment).

## CONCLUSION

Single-large and several-small are **isomers** over a fixed atom set: same ingredients, different bond, different substance by scale (Q1). The separator runs in dependency order, enumeration before comparison and comparison before surplus, and the cascade is an ordered sequence whose third-order effect is easily scoped out (Q2). Taxa form a **functional group** whose qualifier is a trait bound to a residual, not a standing list (Q3). Corridors and source patches are **catalysts or spent parts** depending on a record of use that the atom list cannot carry (Q4). The word "when" is a **comparison** against a ground, with a type constraint on its operands (Q5), and the correction is carried by **negation**, not by subtraction (Q6). Each claim has a falsifier written before the run. No new primitive and no universal number is produced.
