## The question restated as an effect

A landscape process, the splitting of continuous habitat into smaller and more isolated patches separated by a matrix, produces two outcomes that look opposed. For one species slot the substitution of habitat breaks the contact its persistence required, and the population declines or is lost locally. For a different species slot, or the same landscape at a different scale, the same rearrangement introduces new contact, heterogeneity and edges that increase some measure of diversity. The question asks for the boundary condition that selects between these outcomes. An answer must say what is being decided (which residual, at which scale), and what each option leads to (loss of contact for one slot versus a surplus of new settled terms for another).

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run: a separator that names the anchor (measured persistence and measured richness at a declared scale), keeps verdicts per species slot, refuses a standing list of vulnerable species, and returns a shape rather than a number. See `07_the_tether_answer.md` section 2 and the pre-registered refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 6.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with this derivation.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the framework's own words: a room does not add an anchor (Seat Map :283), selecting for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict (Seat Map :163, Fig 2 :40), and showing a recipient the full interior before asking for a verdict spends the independence they contribute (Fig 10 :42). Scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach also breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions, a practical failure on top of the structural one.
