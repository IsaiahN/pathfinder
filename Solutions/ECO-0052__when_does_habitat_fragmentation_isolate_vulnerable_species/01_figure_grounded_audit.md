## 0. The question under audit, stated as a residual

> "When does habitat fragmentation isolate vulnerable species, and when can it create new opportunities for diversity?"

The residual R in effect terms. A landscape process, the conversion of continuous habitat into smaller and more isolated patches within a matrix, is followed by two different readings of the same event. In one reading a species' population loses contact with what its persistence required (food, mates, breeding sites, dispersal routes, microclimate, mutualists) and declines or disappears locally. In another reading the same spatial rearrangement is followed by more species at a landscape scale, more turnover between patches, more edge habitat or more habitat heterogeneity. The question asks for the boundary condition that selects between these readings. The effect to be decided is: does the change in spatial configuration break a particular species' contact with its ground, or does it open contact that a different residual can settle into?

R by arity, symmetry and scale:

- **Arity: two conditions, not one dial and not a two-way partition.** Isolation and opportunity are not opposite answers to one question. Fig 9 :4: "First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." The two outcomes overlap on the same landscape and the same configuration can produce both at once at different slots or scales. Union, not partition.
- **Symmetry: the valence is not carried by the fragment; it is assigned by the residual.** Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :50: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." The power over the habitat is also asymmetric: Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." A manager can add a corridor; a manager cannot subtract the fact of contact.
- **Scale: patch, local landscape, region; and alpha, beta, gamma.** Fig 4 :21: "The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction." A conclusion at one scale is a lossy transform of a conclusion at another. Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."
- **Anchor:** the question is constitutively about measured persistence and measured diversity, so a population count, a survival record, a richness estimate or an experiment is a legitimate anchor (Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."). Agreement among reviewers is not that anchor (Fig 2 :35).

**Partition or union.** D1, D2 and D3 form a union of overlapping conditions on the same landscape, not a partition (Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."; Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."). Within D2 the slots are a partition (one species, one goal, one verdict), because R is indexed by object slot (Fig 1 :19). D4 is a frame over the others, not a fifth ecological domain; it governs what may count as a verdict.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance.").** First enumerate the habitat and index the residual per slot (D1 to D2, Fig 11 :43-44). Then decide whether the substituted habitat failed to reproduce the goal-required contact (D2). Only then evaluate the diversity surplus (D3), because a landscape-scale richness gain is a different slot's residual, not a repair of the isolated population's own residual. The frame (D4) writes its refuter before any of these verdicts are read as conclusions. Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :28: "any frame you pick will seem to fit").

## 3. Findings

### F1 - Fragmentation is a habitat substitution, not a removal (decisive for D1)

Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."
Fig 11 :67: "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."
Fig 11 :68: "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"
Fig 11 :69: "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"

A fragment is not a smaller copy of the continuous habitat with a piece subtracted. It is a new habitat with a different actor set: more edge, a surrounding matrix, altered microclimate, new competitors and predators, and a changed distance to mates and resources. The question "when does it isolate" becomes, in effect terms: when does the substituted habitat fail to reproduce the contact the species' goal required? The two failure modes are silent by construction. What was not reproduced stays invisible until the goal fails (an extinction debt, an immigration lag); what was introduced stays invisible until it acts (an edge predator, an invasive, a disease vector). Haddad et al. 2015 measured the first mode at scale: "habitat fragmentation reduces biodiversity by 13 to 75%" and "Effects are greatest in the smallest and most isolated fragments, and they magnify with the passage of time." The framework's isolation law predicts the shape of that result: a delayed, contact-dependent loss, not an instantaneous subtraction. **Mode: `specified`; triangulated** (Fig 11 :66-69, plus Fig 11 :52 contact cascade, plus the experimental ground in Haddad et al. which is a different evidence source, low overlap with the framework).

**Refuter for F1:** a documented case in which a population's goal-required contact is preserved unchanged across the substitution, and the population persists, with the mechanism measured rather than assumed. That would show isolation is removal of habitat after all, and Fig 11 :66 would be wrong as applied here.

### F2 - "Vulnerable" is a relation to a residual, not a property of a species (D2)

Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
Fig 11 :50: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."
Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."
Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

The same fragment is lethal to an interior specialist and beneficial to an edge generalist. Rybicki, Abrego and Ovaskainen 2020 report exactly this sign flip at the community level: "When the total amount of habitat is large, fragmentation per se tends to increase species diversity, but if the total amount of habitat is small, the situation is reversed: fragmentation per se decreases species diversity." Zhang, Chase and Liao 2024 report a unimodal response: "When habitat loss is low, habitat fragmentation promotes coexistence by suppressing competitively dominant species, while habitat fragmentation at high levels of habitat loss can shape many smaller isolated patches that drive extinctions of superior competitors." These are not contradictions to be averaged. They are different slots and different habitat amounts. Aggregating them into one "fragmentation is good or bad" percentage is precisely the move Fig 1 :22 forbids. A global richness near zero is compatible with one live population residual (Fig 1 :13: "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"). **Mode: `specified`; triangulated** (Fig 11 valence rule, Fig 1 per-slot rule, two independent ecology model/meta-analysis sources that share data pools with each other but not with the framework).

**Refuter for F2:** a standing list of vulnerable species whose membership survives a change of residual and scale, with the invariance measured. That would break Fig 11 :51.

### F3 - Opportunity is a union surplus, not a compensation paid to the isolated species (D3)

Fig 8 :19: "closures differ, so a surplus exists"
Fig 8 :21: "nothing was aimed; the union is mute"
Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."
Fig 8 :51: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."
Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."

A diversity gain following fragmentation is a surplus that arises where the new configuration brings into contact species and habitats that were previously separated. It exists only if the closures differ (new edges, new matrix, new microhabitats, a maintained introduction such as a corridor). An undirected union with no described residual is mute (Fig 8 :21): more patches do not by themselves license a richness claim. The gain is settled at a different slot, and usually at a different scale, from the loss in F1. Perrin et al. 2025 found both at once in one dataset: "habitat fragmentation negatively affects α-diversity at the patch scale, it can enhance overall β- and γ-diversity at the landscape scale." That is the union, not a contradiction and not a repair. Where a maintaining seat exists, the opportunity can be worked on: introduction is available (corridors, stepping stones, new habitat) and exclusion is not, so a managed landscape is "permanent work rather than a solved state" (Fig 11 :63). **Mode: `specified` on the union reading; single-source on the figure side (Fig 8) with low-overlap world corroboration from the ecology sources.**

**Refuter for F3:** a ground reading in which a landscape-scale diversity increase is shown to restore the same population's own per-slot residual, with the mechanism measured. That would break the union reading and Fig 1 :19.

### F4 - The two modes of habitat make "when" a comparison against a ground, not a symmetric choice (D1 and D4)

Fig 11 :56: "Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept."
Fig 11 :58: "Maintained. A seat exists and can act: choose the instruments, distil the impactful actors, introduce what was not there. That is the garden, and it is how arrival is worked on rather than waited for."
Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
Fig 12 :89: "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes." (the test for ⋛, comparison)

In the unmaintained mode there is no one choosing which species is vulnerable: what survives is what the ground kept. In the maintained mode a seat can introduce, but cannot subtract, and every improvement that does not change contact changes nothing. So the boundary "when does it isolate" is a threshold relation (⋛) between the goal-required contact and the contact the substituted habitat supplies, read against a ground at a declared scale. It is not a symmetric join of two equal options. Fahrig 2017 supplies the ecology wording for the threshold: most significant fragmentation responses were positive, yet "24% of significant responses to habitat fragmentation were negative", and the sign depends on the measure, the scale and the taxon, not on a single dial. **Mode: `general` on the two-mode structure, `specified` on the comparison; triangulated** (Fig 11 two modes, Operators comparison test, Fahrig 2017 review as a world source).

**Refuter for F4:** a demonstration that a maintained-seat introduction improved a population's persistence without changing any contact in the habitat (for example by changing only a label or a metric), which would break Fig 11 :40.

### F5 - Contact cascades, so first-order enumeration is how the effect is missed

Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."
Fig 11 :44: "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."
Fig 11 :48: "You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."
Fig 11 :52: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."

The fragmentation literature's own history is a contact-cascade example. A patch may lose a pollinator, which changes seed set, which changes a plant population, which changes a seed disperser three steps out; or an edge may raise predation, which changes nest success, which changes a bird population. Haddad et al. 2015 frame this directly: "Fragmentation effects propagate through the whole ecosystem" and "Reduced fragment area, increased isolation, and increased edge initiate changes that percolate through ecosystems." Enumerating only direct contact is how the effect that matters is scoped out. What cannot yet be perceived is residual by definition (Symbols table: R "is always a slice: what cannot be perceived yet is residual and simply cannot be read"), so the absence of an instrument is not evidence that the cascade stops. **Mode: `specified`; triangulated** (Fig 11 :43-52, Haddad et al. whole-ecosystem experiments, low overlap).

**Refuter for F5:** a system in which the cascade provably stops at first contact for the residual in question, established by an instrument that can see second-order effects, not by failing to look.

### F6 - "Shape, not a name" forbids a universal number, not a provenanced binding

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"
Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"
Fig 13 :135: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

No figure line returns a patch-size threshold, an edge-distance cutoff, a corridor width, the identity of the vulnerable species, or the fraction of habitat at which the sign flips. The framework returns the separator (substitution, residual-relative valence, per-slot indexing, union surplus, two modes, contact cascade), not the number. The ecology literature itself reports that the number is context dependent. Rybicki et al. 2020: "our results do not directly convey specific values for 'low' and 'high' amounts of habitat, as these will be context-dependent." Perrin et al. 2025: "determining whether the effect of fragmentation on biodiversity is positive or negative is a difficult task and ultimately there is no single answer because the term biodiversity covers different aspects that do not respond in the same way." So the numerical boundary is a declared **gap**, of the shape-not-a-name and unreachable-at-this-budget kind, not a silence and not an invitation to invent a percentage.

**Correction kept visible (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).** The canonical phrase governs a *missing* primitive. Naming an already-held binding (edge, corridor, heterogeneity, matrix) is a provenanced binding, not a forbidden invention and not an import that moves the wall. The first draft of this audit wrote "the framework cannot name an edge or a corridor"; that was overstated and is retracted in section 9.

**Refuter for F6:** a derivation from the figures that returns a universal numerical threshold while respecting Fig 13 :135. We predict none is available; if one is produced, the gap verdict is withdrawn.

### F7 - The opposed reviews are one evidence pool, not two frames

Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"
Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."
Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
Fig 7 :36: "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."

Fahrig 2017 (76% of significant responses positive) and Haddad et al. 2015 (13 to 75% biodiversity reduction) are routinely presented as two opposed camps. For this derivation they are one broad fragmentation evidence pool: both reviews draw on the same experimental and observational studies, with different inclusion rules and different response variables. Their disagreement is a fact about scale and measure, not a triangulation. Treating their opposition as two independent witnesses would manufacture the very correlation Fig 2 disqualifies. Zhang et al. 2024 and the conciliation paper by Valente et al. 2023 are further attempts to unbundle the union; they too cite the same core studies. Genuine low overlap here is between the framework's metalaw reading and the ecology measurements, not between two ecology reviews. **Mode: `general` structural; triangulated on the union reading, single witness on the ecology pool.**

**Refuter for F7:** a demonstration that the two reviews draw on non-overlapping primary datasets and independent instruments, such that their agreement or disagreement would carry independent evidence. That would change the witness count, not the figure verdict.

### F8 - The question's own "when" is a preference held as a term

Fig 5 :62: "A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term. It is not bound, it predicts nothing, and it accrues no residual, so nothing here can see it."
THE_FORMULA.md, Direction: a frame "cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it holds"; the remedy is to write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual.
Fig 9 :27: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"

The shape "isolating versus opportunity-creating" is what this search wants. Left as a preference it is invisible to every internal check, so the derivation could refuse the wrong term and still aim at the outcome it wanted. The boundary object was therefore written down in section 0 and bound as an ordinary term before the findings: a separator with an anchor, per-slot verdicts, no standing vulnerable list, a union reading and no universal number. That allows the refuter in section 6 to fire on it like anything else. **Mode: `general` discipline; single-source (Fig 5 and the Formula are the same framework), stated as such.**

**Refuter for F8:** evidence that the bound separator was written after the findings and tuned to them. The order of writing in this folder is the audit trail; that order is the only available check, and it is internal.

### F9 - Isomers: single large versus several small is a bond difference, not an area difference

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance."
Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Over a fixed atom set (the same total habitat area, the same species pool, the same matrix type), a single large patch and several small patches share every ingredient. What differs is the bond, the arrangement of those ingredients in space. By Fig 12 :117 they are isomers, not spellings: the area formula cannot tell them apart, and their alpha and gamma behaviour differs decisively. Perrin et al. 2025 report this directly: "at the landscape scale, multiple small patches supported equal or even higher γ-diversity than a single large patch of equivalent area", while at patch scale "Patch size and connectivity positively influenced α-diversity". This is developed with a falsifier in `06_isomers_and_order.md`. **Mode: `specified`; single-source on the figure definition, with one low-overlap empirical corroborator.**

**Refuter for F9:** a controlled comparison in which two configurations with the same atoms and different arrangement return identical diversity at every declared scale and for every residual. That would make the isomer claim a spelling difference and it would be withdrawn.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the shape and its refuter, not with a verdict about which land configuration is better. The two outcomes are a union compound (F1, F3, F7).
2. Index every claim to a species slot and a scale. Never average an alpha loss with a gamma gain into one number (F2, F3).
3. When a stakeholder says "this fragments the habitat", translate it into effect terms: which goal-required contact was in the substituted habitat, and which introduced actor is now acting (F1, F5).
4. Treat introduction as available and exclusion as unavailable. A corridor is an import that needs provenance and a shadow test; a claim that an actor was removed is usually a claim that a different actor was added (F3, Fig 6 :11-14).
5. Record the numerical boundary as a gap and name the instrument that would close it: a manipulative, replicated experiment at a declared scale with an independent frame reading the same ground (F6, F7).
6. Send the generator (the separator and the refuter), not a recording of one reserve's outcome. Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."

## 6. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion of this audit and before any outreach. A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument (Seat Map :287).

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Threshold fire.** A derivation from the figures returns a universal patch-size, edge-distance, corridor-width or habitat-fraction number that respects Fig 13 :135. We predict none exists. If one does, the `gap` finding F6 is withdrawn.
2. **Substitution fire.** A measured case in which a population's goal-required contact is preserved unchanged across a habitat substitution and the mechanism is observed, while the population persists. This breaks Fig 11 :66-67.
3. **Standing-list fire.** A list of vulnerable species whose membership is invariant across changes of residual and scale, established by measurement. This breaks Fig 11 :51.
4. **Compensation fire.** A ground reading in which a landscape diversity gain restores the same population's own per-slot residual, with the causal path measured. This breaks Fig 1 :19 and the union reading in F3.
5. **Same-slot fire.** A manipulative experiment at a single declared scale in which isolation and opportunity co-occur for the same residual with no union explanation, and the effect does not shrink when habitat amount and scale are separated. This breaks F7.
6. **Asymmetry fire.** A demonstrated maintained-seat operation that subtracts habitat or contact rather than adding an actor, with the subtraction measured rather than renamed. This breaks Fig 11 :61-62.
7. **Cascade fire.** A system in which the fragmentation residual provably stops at first contact, established by an instrument able to resolve second-order effects. This breaks Fig 11 :52.

If any fires, the affected finding is withdrawn and the audit is re-censused, not softened. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.
