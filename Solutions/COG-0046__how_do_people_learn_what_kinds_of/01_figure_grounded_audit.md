## 0. The claim under audit

> People learn which desires and ambitions are acceptable or admirable through contact with a social habitat. The learning is indexed per kind of desire, not averaged into a single "well adjusted" score. What crosses between generations is a method and a head start, not a recording of a particular past success. The specific contents, which desire, which weight, which age, are not given by the figures and must be settled by a ground.

Two initial readings were retracted during this audit and are preserved in §6 rather than erased: (1) treating "acceptable" and "admirable" as one domain, and (2) treating Fig 13's cross-kind composition rule as a direct figure line for this question.

## 1. Residual, arity, symmetry, scale

### 1.1 The residual in effect terms

Let R be the gap, per kind of desire or ambition, between what the learner currently treats as acceptable or admirable and what the surrounding ground actually rewards, sanctions, models and ignores. The learner bets (expresses a desire, pursues an ambition), observes (approval, admiration, correction, sanction, imitation, neglect), and must refit the evaluative vocabulary. R is a slice, not a total: what cannot yet be perceived as a kind of desire is simply not readable (Symbols table: R is *"always a slice: what cannot be perceived yet is residual and simply cannot be read"*).

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
Fig 1 :28: **"The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."**

So a global sentence such as "this child has learned the norms" throws away the only signal that says which slot is still live. A global residual near zero with one live slot is a legal state (Fig 1 :13, :20). Mode `general` for the structure of R, `specified` for its application to desire-kinds.

### 1.2 Arity

At least two conditions, and they overlap: "acceptable" is a threshold relation (a desire must clear a norm), while "admirable" is an ordering (a desire ranks above others). The learner's own frame is a third term in contact with the ground. Fig 9 distinguishes a partition from a union: when the split lands on a union, unbundle before searching, because "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either" (Fig 9 :55-58). This question is a **union compound**, not one hard question. The unbundling is derived in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

### 1.3 Symmetry

The two predicates are not interchangeable. "Acceptable" is an ordered threshold (the comparison bond ⋛, "a threshold relation between two quantities", Operators table :47-52). "Admirableness" is an ordering (spectrum, "ordering, so more and less can be said", Fig 13 :19-20). The learner-ground relation is asymmetric: in the unmaintained case the ground does not update on the learner; in the maintained case a seat can act on the habitat (caregivers, educators) but still cannot subtract the habitat (Fig 11 :61-64). Mode `specified`.

### 1.4 Scale

Individual learning (one scale) and enculturation across generations (population scale). Fig 4 describes the same machine at two sizes: "many agents, a shared library, and something that ranks what they produce" (Fig 4 :5). The filter between scales has a direction. Mode `general` for the membrane rule, `specified` for its application here.

## 4. Findings

### F1 — Learning is per desire-kind, never a global score (governs D1)

Fig 1 :19 and :28 (quoted in §1.1) are literal: the residual is indexed by object slot, and the gap is measured per object. Applied to this question, the object slot is a kind of desire or ambition. The only signal that says where to look is the per-slot one. A study that reports a single "moral development score" averaged over all desires has, in the figure's words, "thrown away the only signal that says where to look."

The world literature converges on the need to split the domain. Haidt, Koller and Dias (1993) found that "cultural norms and culturally shaped emotions have a substantial impact on the domain of morality and the process of moral judgment", with different effects for different kinds of act (flag-cleaning, dog-eating, promise-breaking). Haidt (2007) reports that "Morality is universal, yet culturally variable." These are secondary corroboration, not the verdict.

**Verdict:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 1 :19/:28 and the world sources, but with low independence because both world sources are moral psychology (one evidence pool). Refuter: a demonstration that evaluative learning is well modeled as a single global scalar with no per-slot structure, so that Fig 1 :19 does not apply.

### F2 — The anchor splits with the question, and this is a re-census, not a strain (governs D1/D4)

Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**
Fig 2 :36: **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."**
Fig 2 :37: **"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."**

At first reading this strains against Fig 2 :33, "The anchor must not update", because human judgment is made of people who do update. Re-census resolves it: there are two different questions with two different anchors. For "what do people in this group in fact admire", the question is constitutively about people, so human judgment is the legitimate anchor. For "is our account of the learning mechanism correct", human judgment is only an instrument, and "instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure" (Fig 2 :38). The same page says "It is only an instrument for is this code correct." The strain is dissolved by splitting the question, not by softening either line. Mode `specified`.

**Verdict:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 2 :29/:33/:36/:37. Refuter: a demonstration that one and the same anchor must serve both sub-questions, or that human judgment can settle the mechanism question without an independent instrument.

### F3 — "Acceptable" and "admirable" unbundle; the middle link is where learning fails (governs D1/D2)

Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
Fig 9 :57-58: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."**

Treating the question as one hard question is the wrong census. "Acceptable" (threshold, ⋛) and "admirable" (ordering, spectrum) are two conditions that overlap. The unbundling and the resulting functional-group / isomer claim are derived in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

Fig 3 :25-27 (quoted in §2) adds the failure mode: the middle link, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what counts as progress, has neither obvious failures nor obvious tests, so it attracts the fewest instruments. A child may perceive approval perfectly well and still lack a term for the kind of ambition being rewarded. That is a vocabulary failure, not a motivation failure.

Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** The figure gives a shape, not a list: there is a norm-slot structure, and which desire fills it is culture-specific. Refuter: a demonstration that the two predicates reduce to one scalar, so the union unbundling is wrong.

### F4 — The habitat is enumerated and the child is a lineage (governs D2/D3)

Fig 11 :39: **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**
Fig 11 :40: **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**
Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**
Fig 11 :44: **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**

The question "how do people learn what desires are admirable" cannot be answered by inspecting the learner alone. The habitat must be enumerated: caregivers, peers, institutions, media, and the second-order effects of each. Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** and :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."** A parent is a benefactor for one desire and a malfactor for another.

Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** and :23: **"upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."** What transmits between generations is a way of doing and a head start, not a replay of a specific success. Fig 4 :34: **"A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."**

The essays say the same thing about the exact case. *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "The cognitive development I was thinking about was not actually a blank slate. Children had parents who taught them, nurtured them, corrected them. The blank slate was a half-truth. The child is a lineage." *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "what passes down is ways of doing things and head starts, never the experience itself. Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."

World corroboration comes from genuinely different closures: Bandura's modeling page says "modeling influences can alter motivation, emotional dispositions, and value systems"; Richerson and Boyd define culture as "information capable of affecting individuals' behavior that they acquire from other members of their species through teaching, imitation, and other forms of social transmission"; Whiten, Horner and de Waal (2005) found in chimpanzees "a conformity bias that is regarded as a hallmark of human culture." These are separate research programs, so the agreement is not merely shared authorship, but they all study the same broad phenomenon, so independence is moderate, not maximal. Fig 2 :31-32: independence is how little frames draw on the same evidence.

**Verdict:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 11 and Fig 4 (low overlap between the habitat rule and the membrane rule), with moderate-independence world corroboration. Refuter: an agent-only improvement that moves the ground, which Fig 11 :40 says is impossible; or a case where only a recording, not a method, crosses the membrane.

### F5 — The specific content is a gap, and the direction of the question is outside the mechanism (governs D1/D2/D3)

Fig 5 :62-63: **"A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term. It is not bound, it predicts nothing, and it accrues no residual, so nothing here can see it."** Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."**

The figures return a shape. They cannot say which desire, which weight, which age, which institution. Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."** An isolated learner cannot manufacture a desire-kind its frame lacks; contact with a different closure is required. Fig 8 :53 (the table of Fig 9 :53) says "you get a kind of thing", and Fig 9 :27-28 says **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."** So the residual is described first and the research frame chosen second.

Formula step 7 separates three questions that are often collapsed: SEARCHED (is it in here and unfound?), INWARD (extend the instrument), OUTWARD (import a frame whose closure predicts the shape). For this question: SEARCHED applies to the individual's existing desire vocabulary; INWARD is the under-instrumented middle link of Fig 3; OUTWARD is the import of developmental and cross-cultural frames. They are not the same instruction and must not be read against each other.

**Verdict:** `gap` for the specific content, `specified` for the shape. Refuter: a figure-derived naming of a particular desire or a particular weight, which would contradict Fig 9 :32; or a demonstration that the shape itself is not invariant across the domains censused.

### F6 — Verification is triangulation, not a room of agreeable readers (governs D4)

Fig 2 :35 and :40 (quoted above), Fig 7 :13: **"agreement compounds, evidence does not"**, Fig 10 :42: **"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."**, Fig 10 :52: **"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."**

A list of readers ranked by how likely they are to find the shape agreeable manufactures the disqualifying correlation. The two-stage offer in `02_outreach_email.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` therefore sends the residual and its refuter first, records the verdict before showing theory, and never pools later agreement as corroboration. Fig 10 :59: **"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."** The stake is declared, not hidden.

**Verdict:** `general` structural rule, `specified` for the outreach design. Refuter: a checkable case in which agreement among a shared-pool set settled a factual question about a ground.

### F7 — Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence (governs the honest stop)

Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**
Fig 13 :115: **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not."**
Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"**

The figures do not contain the answer to "which desires are admirable in this culture and how exactly were they learned." That is a `gap` at this budget and with these instruments, not an invitation to invent a name. Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."** The tier or the field of a source is a filter for what to read, never a reason the shape is true.

**Verdict:** `general` on the stop rule, `specified` on the claim that the specific content is unreachable with the held library. Refuter: an instrument that returns a per-desire signal and a frame whose closure differs, producing a ground-settled name.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the standing rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. Evaluative learning is well modeled as a single global scalar with no per-slot structure, so that Fig 1 :19/:28 does not apply to desires.
2. A learner's evaluative capability is improved in isolation with no change in contact, which Fig 11 :40 rules out.
3. "Acceptable" and "admirable" reduce to one condition, so the union unbundling of Fig 9 :54 is wrong.
4. A culture is observed in which only a recording of a specific past success crosses generations, with no method or head start, contrary to Fig 4 :22.
5. A figure line is found that names a specific desire or weight for this question, contradicting Fig 9 :32.

If any fires, the shape claim is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Lead with the shape claim and the refuter, not with the framework (Fig 10 :52).
2. State the mode on every substantive claim, and keep the four domain verdicts separate and unaveraged.
3. Design any follow-up study at the level of desire-kinds, with an instrument for the middle link of Fig 3, and with at least two frames whose evidence pools do not overlap.
4. Enumerate the habitat outward to second-order contact before naming actors (Fig 11 :44).
5. Use the public opt-in venues in `03`. Ask what would refute the shape, record that first, and offer sources only after (Fig 9 :27-28).

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **What is the residual?** A per-desire-kind gap between evaluative vocabulary and what the ground rewards. Fig 1 :19, :28.
2. **One question or several?** A union of "acceptable" (threshold) and "admirable" (ordering). Unbundle. Fig 9 :54-58.
3. **Who or what learns?** Agent and habitat together, not the agent alone. Enumerate the habitat outward. Fig 11 :39-44.
4. **What crosses generations?** Methods and head starts, never a recording of one success. Fig 4 :22-23.
5. **What fails first?** The middle vocabulary link, which has no instrument. Fig 3 :25-27.
6. **Can the shape name a desire?** No. It returns a kind of thing, never the answer itself. Fig 9 :32.
7. **What settles it?** A ground reading, not agreement. Bring a characterised residual, keep the lineage, report `unreachable` honestly. Fig 10 :52, Fig 13 :134.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives: learning is per desire-kind, agent-and-habitat, limited by an under-instrumented vocabulary link, and transmitted across generations as methods and head starts; the two predicates unbundle into a threshold and an ordering; verification is triangulation rather than a room of agreeable readers. The specific content does not survive as a claim: the figures return a shape, not a name, and no ground reading was taken. Strains after re-census: one live strain, the "only methods cross up" rule versus the plain observation that specific habits are copied. It is named in `11_audit.md`, not hidden.
