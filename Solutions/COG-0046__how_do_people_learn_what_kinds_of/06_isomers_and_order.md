The two predicates are yoked by "or," but the figures require a census before a figure line is chosen. ## DERIVATION

### Q1 — One question or a union? CORRECTION TO THE FIRST READING

- *Library of Babel*: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"
- Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
- Fig 9 :55-56: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."
- "Acceptable" carries a **threshold**: does this desire clear the norm? That is ⋛, an ordered comparison, not a symmetric bond.
- "Admirable" carries an **ordering**: where does this ambition rank? That is spectrum, "ordering, so more and less can be said" (Fig 13 :19-20).
- The two overlap. A desire can be acceptable but not admirable, admirable but not acceptable (the necessity distinction), both, or neither. There is an intersection where the second condition still binds.
- ⇒ This is a **union compound**. Treating it as one scalar is the miscensus. Fig 9 :57-58: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."**
- **I initially treated them as one domain.** Retracted: the union split is required by Fig 9 :54-58. Triangulated (Fig 9 + the essay's bundle warning). Mode `specified`.

### Q2 — What structure do the two members share?

- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Both predicates fix an outcome by the presence of a norm-structure: a comparison against a standard. The group is "a desire appraised against a standard"; the qualifier (threshold versus ordering) says which member.
- Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** Across two cultures that hold the same set of desire-kinds but arrange them differently (one ranks assertiveness as admirable, the other ranks it as merely acceptable), the atoms are shared and the bond differs. Under Fig 12's definition those cultures are **isomers** at the level of the norm-set.
- Within one culture, "acceptable" and "admirable" are not isomers of each other: they do not share the full atom set, they share the functional group. The stronger isomer claim is cross-cultural and is checkable only if the same desire-kind set is enumerated in both frames.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a culture is found in which the threshold and the ordering are implemented by the very same predicate with no separable intersection case, the union claim fails and the two collapse into one condition. If two cultures cannot be shown to share the same enumerated desire-kind set, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group claim stands instead.

### Q3 — What is the dependency order of the learning chain?

- Fig 3 :4-17 lists the links: 1 perception, 2 vocabulary, 3 the objective, 4 planning, 5 learn and carry. The chain is "ordered by dependency, not by importance" (Fig 3 :20).
- For this question:
  1. **Perception:** can the learner tell what is rewarded and admired? This has obvious failures and obvious tests.
  2. **Vocabulary:** does the learner have a term for the kind of desire being rewarded? This is the middle link. Fig 3 :25-26: **"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."**
  3. **Objective:** can the learner say what would count as progress toward being admirable in its own terms?
  4. **Planning:** a route to that objective under incomplete information.
  5. **Learn and carry:** "name what worked, verify it, and carry it to the next problem" (Fig 3 :15).
- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** Effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it. Most practical "character education" effort lands above the break, where the measurements already exist (Fig 3 :29-30).
- Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** The derivative claim is that the vocabulary link is the likely break. That is `specified`, not measured.
- **Falsifier:** a study that shows the vocabulary link already has a reliable instrument and is not the binding link in the population sampled would move the break to another link and narrow this claim.

### Q4 — What crosses the scale between person and culture?

- Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**
- Fig 4 :23: **"upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."**
- Fig 4 :34: **"A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."**
- A child inherits a way of appraising desires and a head start, not a recording of which ambitions were admired in a previous generation. The conditions of the habitat decide what the seed becomes. This is the scale rule that governs enculturation, and it is why "the child is a lineage" (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*) rather than a blank slate.
- **Live strain, named:** specific habits plainly are copied, and that looks like a recording crossing generations. Fig 4 :25 says a recording replayed downward "produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one." The strain is between the figure's filter and the observation that both method and recording appear to travel. It is resolved only by an instrument that distinguishes a reapplicable method from a replayed success in a given case. Not smoothed over here; carried to `11_audit.md`.

### Q5 — Why can the figures not name the admired desire?

- Fig 12 :111-113: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one. A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."
- The figures supply the bonds (comparison, ordering, sequence) and the level rule (Fig 12 :104, **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"**). They do not supply the content of a culturally specific goal.
- Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."** So the answer here is: a per-slot, habitat-bound, method-transmitted comparison structure. The specific desire remains an import awaiting a ground.

## CONCLUSION

"Acceptable" and "admirable" are a union compound, not one scalar: a threshold ⋛ and a spectrum ordering that overlap. They share a functional group (a desire appraised against a standard), and cross-culturally they can form a genuine isomer claim if the same desire-kind set is enumerated in both frames. The learning chain is ordered by dependency, and the likely break is the vocabulary link at position 2, which has no instrument. What crosses generations is a method and a head start, not a recording. None of this names a desire, and none of it is `grounded`. The specific content remains a `gap`.
