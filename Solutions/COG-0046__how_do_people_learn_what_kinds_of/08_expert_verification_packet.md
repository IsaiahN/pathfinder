# Expert verification packet: the Tether on learning acceptable and admirable desires

**What this is:** a claim in a form a moral-development researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape claims, `general` on the bare structural rule, not `grounded`.** No per-desire measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status of this packet:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

People learn which desires and ambitions are acceptable or admirable in a way that is indexed per kind of desire, not averaged into one score; that belongs to the person and their habitat together, not to the isolated person; that splits into a threshold ("acceptable") and an ordering ("admirable") which overlap; and whose binding failure is a missing vocabulary link, with methods and head starts, not recordings of past successes, crossing generations. The figures do not name the desire: they return a shape, not a name. What is offered is that shape, four refuters written in advance, and the kind of instrument that would close the gap.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal plus cognitive; checkable by reading Figures 1 and 3

- **Statement:** the learning residual is per desire-kind, and the likely break is the vocabulary link between perception and objective.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 — **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :28 — **"The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."** Fig 3 :25-26 — **"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."** Fig 3 :27 — **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**
- **What to check:** does the literature actually show per-domain structure in moral evaluation, as Haidt, Koller and Dias (1993) report across act types and cultures? Is there an instrument for "has a term for this kind of desire", or is that link genuinely unmeasured?
- **Refuter:** a study in which a single global scalar predicts evaluative learning as well as a per-slot model with no loss of signal; or a validated instrument showing the vocabulary link is not the binding link.

### C2 — habitat-facing; checkable by a developmental or cultural-evolution researcher

- **Statement:** capability is a property of agent and habitat, and what transmits between generations is a method and a head start rather than a recording of one success.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :40 — **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."** Fig 11 :44 — **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."** Fig 4 :22 — **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** Fig 4 :23 — **"upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."**
- **What to check:** is the habitat enumerated to second-order contact (media, institutions, peers, and their downstream effects), or do accounts stop at the parents? Does the observed transmission carry a reapplicable method, or only a replayed practice? Bandura's modeling page and Richerson and Boyd's transmission definition bear on this; Whiten et al. (2005) provide a low-overlap animal comparison.
- **Refuter:** an isolated change in the learner that moves the ground without any change in contact; or a documented case in which only a recording, with no method or head start, crosses generations.

### C3 — framework-internal derivation; checkable by reading Figures 9 and 12

- **Statement:** "acceptable" and "admirable" are a union compound, not one scalar. They share a functional group; cultures with the same desire-kind set arranged differently are isomers.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :58 — **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."** Fig 12 :124 — **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** Fig 12 :117 — **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- **What to check:** is there an intersection case in which a desire is acceptable but not admirable, or the reverse? Can two cultures be shown to share an enumerated desire-kind set so the isomer claim is testable?
- **Refuter:** a single predicate implements both threshold and ordering with no separable intersection case, which collapses the union; or no two cultures share a desire-kind set, which kills the isomer claim and leaves only the functional-group claim.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a developmental mechanism, stage sequence, age, or causal weight for any specific actor.
- Not "parents determine ambitions" or any standing list of good actors; valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49-51).
- Not a claim that the six physical primitives of Fig 13 govern this social question. That reading was retracted as a strain.
- Not that human judgment settles the mechanism. Fig 2 :37 makes it the right anchor only where the question is constitutively about people; for the mechanism it is a fallible instrument.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a measurement; the derivation is a simulation.
- Not a name for the admired desire. Fig 9 :32 — "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. The checks:

1. A per-desire instrument that shows whether the residual is indexed per slot (tests C1).
2. A contact manipulation that shows whether an isolated learner change moves the ground (tests C2).
3. An intersection-case test and a cross-cultural atom enumeration (tests C3).
4. A method-versus-recording discriminator for transmitted practices (resolves the live strain carried in `11_audit.md`).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, :28 and Fig 3 :25-27 — four short passages.
2. Check C1 against your own knowledge: is moral evaluation per-domain in the data you know, and is the vocabulary link actually unmeasured?
3. Check C2 against one case you know well: was the transmitted practice a method or a replay?
4. Name one instrument that would distinguish "acceptable" from "admirable" in a single case, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.

**Recording rule:** the verdict on C1-C3 is recorded before any framework is shown. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40 — "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool").
