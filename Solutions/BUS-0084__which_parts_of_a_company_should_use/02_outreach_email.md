# Outreach draft - validate the output first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. No personal emails are used; the recipient must have an opt-in or public institutional route.

**Hard precondition:** the stage-1 output must survive the recipient's check before any theory is shown. The output here is a `specified` shape with no ground measurement, so the legitimate ask is a refutation of the shape, never endorsement of it. If the recipient cannot be shown a failure condition they can actually test, do not send.

---

**Subject:** Check one claim about AI decision rights before you decide whether any of this is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one narrow claim about AI in organisations that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. I am asking for a failure. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the usual question, "which departments should recommend and which should decide autonomously?", is the wrong unit. The unit is a recurring decision slot, and the answer is a set of conditions on that slot, not a department list.
>
> 1. The slot needs a ground that does not change its answer when the AI's prediction pushes on it. If the "outcome" is stakeholder approval, or a metric the AI itself shapes, there is no anchor and no basis for autonomy.
> 2. What the decision is constitutively about decides who or what should anchor it. For a question about a person, human judgment is the right anchor and the AI is an instrument. For a checkable state of the world, a measurement is the anchor and autonomy is a candidate.
> 3. Whether the decision is a verdict or a convention decides how much independence to buy. In a verdict, agreement from a shared data pool is worthless. In a convention, agreement is the product. A convention that starts deciding outcomes has stopped being a convention.
> 4. The person checking the decision must not be able to reconstruct it. If the reviewer sees the same features and the same model output and can reproduce the recommendation, the reviewer is a playback, not a check.
>
> These are four different failure modes with four different instruments. A single rule such as "keep a human in the loop everywhere" clears only the fourth case and leaves the others standing. The further claim is that the model's accuracy is not the deciding quantity, because accuracy is a property of the model and a benchmark is a score the model's own frame produces. The anchor has to be the realized outcome.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I can name which departments or roles are safe to automate, that model accuracy is irrelevant, that any particular company is a case in point, that this is settled by evidence, or that it is the only account of AI adoption. I am offering a shape any candidate answer would have to have. A shape is not a name. The missing instrument is a per-slot measurement that distinguishes a real outcome from a pooled reading, and I do not have it.
>
> **What would refute it, written down before I sent this:**
> 1. a slot whose "ground" moves when the AI's prediction pushes on it, yet autonomy there is still justified by that ground;
> 2. a documented case where an instrument is the legitimate anchor for a question constitutively about a person, with no human judgment required;
> 3. a verdict slot where agreement from a shared evidence pool is sufficient to settle the verdict;
> 4. a convention that keeps counting as a convention after it decides a consequential outcome for a named person;
> 5. an agent-only autonomy grant that lowers the ground-verified residual and keeps it down across a habitat change;
> 6. a reviewer who can fully reconstruct the decision and still refutes at the same rate as an independent reviewer;
> 7. one scalar governance score that validly predicts both specification cost and real settlement cost across slot types;
> 8. one intervention that restores all four conditions in all four failure types and leaves nothing categorically different behind.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the claim. A single line saying which one holds and why is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send the three public sources below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
>
> - Figure text with line numbers (I can point to the exact lines): the local canonical figure text in this solution folder.
> - Formula and usage rule: the `figures` folder on the public `general-agent` branch of the `tether` repository.
> - The essays on source, search shape and the prestige/action separation: my Substack archive.
>
> I will send URLs only after you tell me what you would count as a failure.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is public. Run it on a decision slot you know well and can check against a real ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

| Do not ask for agreement | *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35) |
| Four failure modes kept separate | *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"* (Fig 9 :57); a single blanket reviewer rule is one lever on a union |
| The unit is the slot, not the department | *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* (Fig 1 :19) |
| Pre-registered refuter | Seat Map field guide: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device"* |
| Mode stated on the claim | `THE_FORMULA.md`: *"Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which."* |
| Anchor named before accuracy | *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* (Fig 2 :29); accuracy is frame-internal (Fig 1 :27) |
| No name for the roles | *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (Fig 9 :32) |
| Independence bought in proportion, not maximised | *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* (Fig 2 :42) |

## Sending rules

- One recipient at a time. No distribution lists, no mail-merge, no forwarding chain.
- Use only a public institutional contact, a call for submissions, or an address the person has published themselves. Record the source and date.
- A reply is a conversation, never corroboration. If a recipient returns a refutation, copy it into `11_audit.md` with provenance and have the auditor seat re-evaluate it as if it came from any other source.
- Do not follow up with the theory after a "no". *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"* (Fig 10 :59).
- Include an opt-out line in every message. AOM's publishing page states that work submitted must be authored and not a product of AI tools unless appropriate to the research question and properly cited. This packet is an LLM-assisted simulated derivation, so a human must own and run the check before anything is submitted.

---

## Stage sequence

**Stage 1.** Send `08_expert_verification_packet.md` sections 1 to 3 only: the one-paragraph claim, the three checkable sub-claims with their verbatim figure lines and refuters, and the non-claims. No provenance bundle, no essay links.

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If the recipient is curious and has already recorded a verdict on stage 1, offer the three public links: the figure text, the formula and usage rule, and the essays. Their later agreement is a conversation, not corroboration (Fig 2 :40).

**Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method, never as an answer (Fig 4 :22). Each user writes a refuter before running it and states the mode of what they get.

**Stop rule.** If stage 1 fails, the theory is not offered. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
