## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The offered split by "part of the company" is the wrong unit. The unit is a recurring decision slot, and a slot may select its action autonomously only when four conditions hold at once. First, the slot has a nameable ground that does not change its answer when the AI's prediction pushes on it. Second, the question is constitutively about a legitimate anchor: for a question about a person, human judgment is the anchor and the AI is an instrument; for a checkable state of the world, a measurement is the anchor. Third, the decision is classified as a verdict or a convention, because agreement from a shared evidence pool is worthless in a verdict and is the product in a convention. Fourth, a checker exists that did not author the decision and cannot reconstruct it. These are four different failure modes with four different instruments, not degrees of one AI-readiness variable. Capability is a property of the AI-and-slot, so granting decision rights changes authority and not contact; quick failure is usually the channel going stale, saturated or pooled rather than the ground changing. The claim is a shape that any candidate allocation must have; it does not name which roles or departments should be autonomous.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 2 and 9

- **Statement:** the unit is the decision slot, not the department; the anchor decides autonomy, not model accuracy; what the question is about decides whether the anchor is human judgment or a measurement.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
  - Fig 2 :29 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."*
  - Fig 2 :30 - *"you push on it, it is not an anchor."*
  - Fig 2 :36 - *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."*
  - Fig 2 :37 - *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."*
  - Fig 2 :37-38 - *"It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."*
  - Fig 9 :54 - *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- **Refuter:** a slot whose ground moves when the AI's prediction pushes on it yet still justifies autonomy; or a documented case where an instrument is the legitimate anchor for a question constitutively about a person with no human judgment required; or a department-wide rule that keeps R small on every slot across a habitat change with no categorically different residue. Any one fires against C1.

### C2 - Framework-internal plus one organizational-design check

- **Statement:** verdict and convention are different regimes; buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong; a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention. An oversight checker must not be able to reconstruct the decision.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 2 :40 - *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*
  - Fig 2 :41 - *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*
  - Fig 2 :42 - *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*
  - Fig 2 :43 - *"Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."*
  - Fig 10 :42 - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*
  - Fig 10 :52 - *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*
  - Fig 10 :59 - *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."*
- **What a researcher can check against their own knowledge:** Kellogg, Valentine and Christin 2020 write that algorithmic control operates through six mechanisms, with employers able to *"restrict and recommend"*, *"record and rate"* and *"replace and reward"*. Does the distinction between recommending and replacing not track the verdict or convention boundary, and is "a human approves the model's output" not simply a recording-and-rating layer when the human sees the same features? A "no" to both questions is a real refutation of the mapping. EU AI Act Article 14 :4(b) requires awareness of over-reliance *"in particular for high-risk AI systems used to provide information or recommendations for decisions to be taken by natural persons"*. Is that not a regulatory instance of C1 and C2 together?
- **Refuter:** a verdict slot where agreement from a shared pool is sufficient; a convention that keeps counting as a convention after deciding a consequential outcome for a named person; or a checker who can fully reconstruct the decision and still refutes at the same rate as an independent checker. Any one breaks C2.

### C3 - Structure, order and competing explanations

- **Statement:** recommendation and autonomy are different bonds over the same atoms, so a model inventory cannot distinguish them; the conditions bind in dependency order with the vocabulary and objective links the usual breaks; description cost and settlement cost are two currencies that do not add.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 12 :5 - *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
  - Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
  - Fig 12 :124 - *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
  - Fig 3 :27 - *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
  - Fig 12 :125 - *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*
  - Fig 12 :130 - *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."*
- **Refuter:** two slots with identical atoms and identical bonds whose ground-verified residuals diverge with no difference in contact or imported atoms; a slot whose chain works at perception, vocabulary and objective and fails only at planning; or a single scalar governance measure that validly predicts both specification cost and settlement cost with no unit conversion. Any one breaks C3.

- Not `grounded`. No measurement has paid for any part of this.
- Not a department list, a maturity stage or a risk-tier label.
- Not a theory of everything about organizations, and no final or unique theory claim.
- Not an average. No claim about what fraction of decisions should be autonomous.
- Not that accuracy, adoption or trust are irrelevant. They are Γ and channel readings; the claim is that they do not settle the anchor question.
- Not "a human in the loop everywhere". That is one lever on a four-condition union.
- Not a claim that a specific role or department is the answer. That is a `gap` at the instrument boundary, not a hidden answer.
- Not that the management literature independently corroborates this. Kellogg et al. is one evidence pool. Human factors, regulation and labour economics corroborate the mechanism, not the corporate fact.

## 5. What would settle it

A prospective, per-slot study that names the anchor, classifies the question's constitution and verdict status, records the bond as well as the model inventory, and reports which condition failed before each decline. Concretely:

1. **Anchor test:** does the "outcome" change when the AI's prediction shapes the input? A moving outcome is not an anchor (Fig 2 :30).
2. **Constitution test:** for each slot, is the answer constitutively about a person, a checkable state, or a convention? Does the proposed anchor match (Fig 2 :36-38)?
3. **Independence test:** in verdict slots, do the checker and the AI draw on separate evidence? In convention slots, is agreement the product rather than a verdict in disguise (Fig 2 :40-43)?
4. **Channel test:** before blaming the model, measure stale percepts, saturated metrics and pooled readings, and check bracket fidelity times ground pressure (Fig 10 :24-28, Fig 7 :37-39).
5. **Source-discriminating instrument:** can the study distinguish a genuinely anchoring ground from a pooled reading and from luck when all three produce the same dashboard number? The source essay says a reading alone *"cannot comment on the source"*.

## 7. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, Fig 2 :29-30 and :36-43, Fig 9 :54, Fig 10 :42 and :52, Fig 12 :117 and :125. About ten short passages, roughly eight minutes.
2. Check C1 and C2 against your own knowledge of an organization you know: name one decision slot where the ground is a person's judgement and one where it is a counter, and say whether the proposed anchor matches. About five minutes.
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish a genuinely anchoring outcome from a pooled metric produced by the same AI, or say why none exists. About five minutes.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
