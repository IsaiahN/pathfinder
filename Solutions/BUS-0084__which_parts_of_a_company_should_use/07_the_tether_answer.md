# The Tether's answer on AI recommendation versus autonomous decision

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 8), not a ground reading. No per-slot ground measurement pays for any claim here.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks for a division of a company into parts that use AI for recommendations and parts that allow autonomous decisions. An answer would explain why the gap between what an AI system predicts a decision will return and what the world actually returns stays small when the system selects the action itself in some slots and grows when it does so in others. R is indexed by decision slot, never by department and never averaged (Fig 1 :19, :22). The question is about the **decision rights on a slot**, not about the model alone and not about the org chart.

The union must be unbundled before it is searched (Fig 9 :54). It is four conditions, not one hard question and not a binary partition:

- **C1:** the slot has a nameable ground that does not update when the AI's prediction pushes on it.
- **C2:** the question is constitutively about a legitimate anchor, human judgment for questions about people, a measurement for checkable states.
- **C3:** the decision is a verdict or a convention, which sets how much independence must be bought.
- **C4:** a checker exists that did not author the decision and cannot reconstruct it.

## 2. The shape

1. **The unit is the decision slot, not the department.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* A department is a bundle of slots with different grounds, hence a union (Fig 9 :54-57). A department-wide rule clears the single-condition cases and leaves the intersections standing.
2. **The anchor decides autonomy, not the accuracy score.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :30: *"you push on it, it is not an anchor."* Model accuracy, adoption and trust are frame-internal (Fig 1 :25-27) or move when pushed. The realized outcome is the anchor.
3. **What the question is about decides who or what should anchor it.** Fig 2 :36: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* Fig 2 :37: *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."* For a checkable state, an instrument is the anchor: *"It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."* (Fig 2 :37-38)
4. **Verdict or convention decides what agreement means.** Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; :41: *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*; :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* And :43: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."*
5. **Capability is a property of the AI-and-slot, not the AI alone.** Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Granting decision rights changes authority, not contact.
6. **The checker must not be able to reconstruct the decision.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Declare unavoidable stakes: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"* (Fig 10 :59).
7. **Fast failure is channel failure, not ground failure.** Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; :26: *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*; :29: *"→ keep the anchor reachable"*. Fig 7 :37-39 gives the two-factor form: bracket fidelity times ground pressure, and zero either stops the chain.
8. **Recommendation and autonomy are different bonds over the same atoms.** Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* A model inventory cannot see the bond. Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Description cost and settlement cost are held apart.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not `grounded`. No prospective per-slot measurement has paid for this shape.
- Not a department list, a maturity stage or a risk-tier label. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- Not a claim that accuracy, adoption or trust are irrelevant. Accuracy is Γ; adoption and trust are channel readings. None is the anchor (Fig 1 :27, Fig 2 :30).
- Not "humans in the loop everywhere". That is a single lever on a four-condition union (Fig 9 :55).
- Not an average. No claim about the percentage of decisions that should be autonomous. Such a number would be a pooled reading (Fig 10 :26) and a filter for budget, not a witness (Fig 9 :60).
- Not a claim that the framework is the only account of AI adoption. A uniqueness claim over all management frameworks has no census and no last room (Fig 7 :6, *"the limit: no last room"*).
- Not corroboration by agreement. Kellogg, Valentine and Christin 2020 is one management evidence pool; the human factors, regulation and labour economics sources corroborate mechanism, not the corporate fact (Fig 2 :31, :40).

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. The source essay's metamerism rule: an instrument reading green *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* Here the readings are dashboard outcomes, adoption scores and audit results; the candidate sources are a genuinely anchoring ground, a pooled metric the AI itself shapes, a favourable habitat, or luck.

Nine falsifiers, pre-registered in `01` and repeated in `08` and `09`:

1. a "ground" that moves when the AI's prediction pushes on it, yet still justifies autonomy;
2. an instrument as the legitimate anchor for a question constitutively about a person, with no human judgment required;
3. a verdict slot settled by agreement from a shared pool;
4. a convention that keeps counting as a convention after deciding a consequential outcome for a named person;
5. an agent-only autonomy grant that lowers the ground-verified residual and keeps it down across a habitat change;
6. a checker that can reconstruct the decision and still refutes at the same rate as an independent checker;
7. a single scalar governance score that validly predicts both specification cost and settlement cost;
8. one intervention restoring all four conditions in all four failure types with no categorically different residue;
9. one of the six terms derived from five, or closure idempotence failing inside a closed set.

A prospective per-slot study that names the anchor, classifies the question's constitution and verdict status, records the bond as well as the model inventory, and reports which condition failed before each decline would turn this from `specified` toward `grounded`.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The shape stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. The missing thing is a discriminator of sources, and it is owed. Naming a winner from a dashboard reading would be the metamerism error the source essay forbids. The eight conditions and comparisons in section 2 are a shape; the ninth thing, which named decision slots should be autonomous in a given company, is not held and is not pretended.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not produce a list of departments that should recommend and departments that should decide. It says that the unit is the decision slot, and that a slot may decide autonomously only when four conditions hold. The slot needs a ground that does not move when the AI's prediction pushes on it; the question must be constitutively about a legitimate anchor, which for a question about a person is human judgment and for a checkable state is a measurement; the decision must be classified as a verdict or a convention, because agreement is worthless in the first and is the product in the second; and the checker must not be able to reconstruct the decision. The model's accuracy is not the anchor, because accuracy is a score the model's own frame produces. Capability lives in the AI-and-slot, so granting decision rights changes authority and not contact. Quick failure is usually the channel going stale, saturated or pooled, not the ground changing. Recommendation and autonomy are different bonds over the same atoms, invisible in a model inventory and decisive under error. What is offered is that shape, nine falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. Which named slots to automate stays open until a source-discriminating per-slot instrument exists, and the stop is recorded rather than filled.
