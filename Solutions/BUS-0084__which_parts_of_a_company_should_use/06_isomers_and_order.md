## Q1 - Are two decision slots with the same AI isomers?

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** (Fig 12 :117)
> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** (Fig 12 :124)
> **"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."** (Fig 12 :92)
> **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."** (Fig 12 :5)

Two slots with an identical model, identical data, identical staff and identical interface have the same atoms. If one composes them as `model -> candidate -> human -> action -> outcome` and the other as `model -> action -> outcome` with feedback, the bonds differ. Under Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different behaviour, with the difference invisible in the model card, the data catalogue and the resource inventory. This is the figure-grounded version of the intuition that it is not what AI you have but how it is wired into the decision, and it is stronger, because the arrangement is itself a substance that carries information the ingredients do not.

If the two slots share only a pattern (`perception -> model -> decision`) while differing in which actors occupy the slots, the weaker claim holds: they are members of the same **functional group** (Fig 12 :124), the qualifier saying which member. The presence of the pattern fixes the kind, not the autonomy outcome.

The distinction is testable. A model inventory comparison that finds no difference between two slots cannot distinguish isomer from functional-group; the bond has to be recorded. Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."* A governance audit that lists models and datasets has recorded the ingredients and not the decision-rights arrangement, which is the half nothing was recording.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two slots with identical atoms and identical bonds nevertheless diverge in ground-verified residual with no difference in ground contact or imported atoms, the isomer framing is insufficient and the difference must be habitat (D2), which moves the explanation out of D3.

**Status:** `specified`, triangulated for the legality of the reading (Fig 12 and Fig 11 :50, different figures), single-source for the organizational application. The world corroboration is Kellogg, Valentine and Christin 2020, whose *"restricting and recommending"* versus *"replacing and rewarding"* is the management-pool name for a bond difference. It is one witness.

### Q1b - What is `⋛` doing here?

The threshold "should this be autonomous?" is a comparison bond `⋛`, defined in the Operators table as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*. Its operands are not two models; they are the slot's estimated residual under a recommendation arrangement and the same slot's estimated residual under an autonomy arrangement. The bond is about which is smaller and by how much, not about both being present. Fig 12 :85-89 gives the identifying test: *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."*

This is the one place where the framework can say something about the decision without naming a department: it defines the **comparison** but not the **answer**. The residual estimates require a settlement action each, which is F9's second currency.

**Falsifier:** a slot where the threshold cannot be stated as a comparison of two quantities under a declared code. Then `⋛` is mistyped here and the autonomy question is not a comparison at all.

## Q2 - What order do the conditions bind in?

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."** (Fig 3 :20-21)
> **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** (Fig 3 :19)
> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** (Fig 3 :27)

Order for a decision slot facing a new AI option:

1. **Perception.** Can the system read the outcome at all? Failure here is visible and has obvious tests. This is where most governance budget lands, because outcome dashboards already exist.
2. **Vocabulary.** Does Γ have terms for what the situation affords, and can it say what would count as progress there? This is the middle link. Fig 3 :26: *"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."*
3. **Objective.** Can it state a comparison that would count as getting somewhere? Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* A vocabulary with only ingredients cannot state a goal.
4. **Planning.** A route to that objective under incomplete information.
5. **Learn and carry.** Name what worked, verify it, carry it to the next slot.

The four conditions map onto this chain:

- **C1 (anchor)** is a failure of link 1 in its ground-facing sense: the outcome signal does not exist or moves with the prediction.
- **C2 (anchor legitimacy)** is a failure of links 2 and 3: the model has terms for the inputs but not for what "good for this person" would count as.
- **C3 (verdict or convention)** is mostly a failure of link 3: the system cannot tell whether agreement is the product or a verdict about the world.
- **C4 (checker non-access)** is mostly a failure of link 5: the company names what worked but the checker is a playback rather than an independent reading.

This mapping is why a single lever cannot clear all four (Fig 9 :55). Recommendation is robust to a break at link 3, because a person can supply the objective the model cannot state. Autonomy is not: with no objective link, the system selects an action it cannot say counts as progress. That is the dependency reason for the allocation, and it is independent of the anchor argument in F2.

The order also decides which instrument to buy first. Link 1 has accounting and outcome data; links 2 and 3 have almost none. So budget piles up at link 1 while the break sits at 2 and 3.

**Falsifier:** a slot whose chain demonstrably works at links 1, 2 and 3 and fails only at planning (link 4). That would falsify the "usually middle" claim of F8 in `01`, though not the dependency ordering itself.

## Q3 - Competing explanations, and what each can and cannot reach

|---|---|---|---|
| **Model accuracy / benchmark performance** | a property of Γ | Fig 1 :25-27: frame-internal, *"A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces"* | Why the same accuracy keeps R small in one slot and not another. No ground variable, so it conflicts with F2 until re-mapped as Γ |
| **AI maturity stage / high-risk tier** | a labelling convention over domains | close to C3's convention pole and to Fig 10 :46 (*"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"*) | It is a management or regulatory binding, not a derivation; it does not say what would refute it and it does not separate C1 from C3 |

| **Automation bias / misuse (Parasuraman and Riley)** | channel and design failure | matches Fig 10 :24-28 and F6 | Human factors at a different stratum; it corroborates mechanism, not the corporate allocation |
| **EU AI Act risk classification** | a regulatory convention with oversight duties | close to C3 and F3, and corroborates context-dependent oversight | A regulation, not a figure line and not a measurement; it imports no source gate here and carries zero verdict weight until provenanced |

No candidate is discarded by prestige or familiarity. Each is ranged against the same residual in the order Fig 8 :53 requires. The point is not to pick one; it is to notice that the first five are largely bindings from one management or organizational pool (Fig 2 :31), while human factors and regulation are genuinely low-overlap corroborators of mechanism only.

## Q4 - Does the shape generate the literature's answers?

Partly. The shape can generate:

- the maturity-model emphasis on staged readiness, as a convention over C3 with provenance owed;
- the human-in-the-loop emphasis, as C4 and Fig 10 :42;
- the high-risk-tier emphasis, as C2 and Fig 2 :36-38;
- the algorithmic-control emphasis on recommendation versus replacement, as Fig 12 :117;
- the automation-bias emphasis on oversight design, as Fig 10 :24-28.

It cannot generate the *name* of the specific slot that should be autonomous. That is the `gap` recorded in F11 of `01`. It is a gap at the instrument boundary, not a silence: Fig 13 :134 (*"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*) and Fig 8 :39 apply. The source essay states why: an instrument that reads a result *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."*

## Q5 - Which analogue is legitimate, and which near-miss is refused?

**Legitimate:** the membrane between scales (Fig 4). A rule validated at one decision slot is a method that can be carried up to a population of slots, but the transform is lossy, so the round trip gap R_T must be measured (Fig 4 :30). A department-wide policy is a recording carried upward and replayed downward, which *"produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one"* (Fig 4 :25). This analogue governs the move from slot to department and is used in `11_audit.md` for R_T.

**Refused:** the ecological-niche analogue. The lead essay's "Three mappings offered and declined" records that the habitat as an ecological niche was declined: *"A niche is defined by an organism's relation to its environment. A habitat here is defined by contact with a residual, and the residual moves so the same actors constitute a different habitat when the question changes."* The resemblance is real and the definitions do not align. A company's "AI risk environment" is not a niche in that sense, because the residual moves when the decision right changes. The analogy is cited so a reader can check the resemblance rather than take it as a derivation.

**Also refused:** the "human in the loop as Popperian critic" mapping declined in the lead essay. The critic is inside the community and draws on its evidence; the oversight seat's defining property is that it cannot reconstruct the claim. A reviewer who shares the model's data is not a Popperian critic of it in this framework's sense.

## CONCLUSION

Two slots with the same model, data and people but different decision-rights bonds are isomers under Fig 12 :117; two slots sharing only a `perception -> model -> decision` pattern are members of one functional group under Fig 12 :124. The threshold between the two arrangements is `⋛`, a comparison of residual estimates under a declared code, and it defines the question without naming the answer. The conditions bind in dependency order, with the vocabulary and objective links and the carry link the usual breaks, which is why recommendation tolerates a break autonomy cannot and why C1 to C4 need different instruments. The standard adoption explanations are mostly bindings of one evidence pool and are generated by the shape only up to the point where a name would be required. The membrane analogue governs the slot-to-department move; the ecological-niche and Popperian-critic analogues are refused. The isomer claim has a written falsifier, the order claim has one, the threshold has one, and the naming gap is declared rather than filled.
