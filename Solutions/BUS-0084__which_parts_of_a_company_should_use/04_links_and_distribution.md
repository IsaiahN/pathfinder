## 2. What the wider literature bears on the question

**One pool, one witness.** Kellogg, Valentine and Christin 2020 is the organizational-design source. Its six Rs distinguish *"restricting and recommending"* from *"replacing and rewarding"*, which is the management name for the recommendation versus autonomy split. It treats the split as a mechanism of control, not as a department property, which corroborates F1 and F2. It shares the management evidence pool with any strategy answer, so it counts as one witness (Fig 2 :31, :40).

**Low-overlap witnesses**, each from a different field and each read directly:

1. **Regulation.** EU AI Act Article 14 :3 requires oversight *"commensurate with the risks, level of autonomy and context of use of the high-risk AI system"* and :4(b) requires awareness of automation bias *"in particular for high-risk AI systems used to provide information or recommendations for decisions to be taken by natural persons"*. This is the regulatory name for a per-slot, context-dependent allocation. It corroborates F2 and F3, and its two-person verification rule for remote biometric identification is an instance of buying independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42). It is a regulation, not a measurement, so it sets a floor rather than settling the figure verdict.
2. **Labour economics.** Acemoglu and Restrepo 2019 centre their framework on *"the allocation of tasks to capital and labor, the task content of production"*, not on occupations. That corroborates the per-task unit of F1. It says nothing about corporate decision rights, so it is a mechanism analogue rather than this ground.
3. **Human factors engineering.** Parasuraman and Riley 1997 distinguish misuse (over-reliance and automation bias), disuse (underuse from false alarms) and abuse (automation of functions *"without due regard for the consequences for human performance"*). This corroborates F6 at the level of mechanism: fast failure is a channel and design problem, not a ground problem.
4. **Judgement and decision making, two different results.** Dietvorst et al. 2015 find algorithm aversion: *"people are especially averse to algorithmic forecasters after seeing them perform, even when they see them outperform a human forecaster"*. Logg et al. 2019 find algorithm appreciation: *"lay people adhere more to advice when they think it comes from an algorithm than from a person"*, and that expertise reduces reliance on algorithmic advice *"which hurt their accuracy"*. These are different labs and tasks, so they are two witnesses to the instability of trust, not one pool. They corroborate F3's conclusion that trust cannot be the anchor, because a quantity that flips with framing and experience cannot settle a verdict. They corroborate mechanism, not the corporate fact.

**Framework-internal essays** are used for the canonical reading of "shape" in `05_what_shape_means.md` only. Their author wrote the figures, so overlap is maximal and they carry no independent witness weight. The lead essay's own warning applies: the triangulation claim *"fits four cases already known and has not been tested forward."*

## 3. Distribution rules

Figure-grounded reasons for how material is offered:

- **Fig 4 :22-25:** *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Send the generator, the four conditions and the refuter, not a department list. A recording carried upward *"looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion"* (Fig 4 :25).
- **Fig 10 :42:** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28:** *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the four-condition shape were true before showing the source.
- **Fig 2 :40:** agreement from a shared pool is worthless as a verdict. Later agreement about the framework is never pooled as corroboration.
- **Fig 10 :59:** *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* Declare the stake; do not ask for belief.
- **Fig 2 :42:** *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* One-to-one contact for consequential slots, not a broadcast.

**A hard practical constraint.** The AOM Publishing page states: *"Artificial Intelligence: Work submitted to AOM must be created by the authors and not the product of artificial intelligence tools unless appropriate to the research question and properly cited."* This whole solution is a simulated derivation produced with an LLM and is labelled as such everywhere. It must **not** be submitted as an authored empirical or theoretical article. The legitimate use is as a private checking instrument: post the residual and the refuter, invite a human to run the check, and let that human author whatever they conclude. Any submission must be rewritten and owned by a human author who can defend the derivation, with the AI contribution disclosed under the venue's policy.

**Order in any reply:** (1) the shape claim and the pre-registered refuter; (2) the question "what would you count as a failure here?"; (3) only after a verdict is recorded, the three source links with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (4) the formula as a reusable method, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

## 4. The two-stage offer, compressed

**Stage 1.** Send `08_expert_verification_packet.md` sections 1 to 3 only: the one-paragraph claim, the three checkable sub-claims with their verbatim figure lines and refuters, and the non-claims. No provenance bundle, no essay links.

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If the recipient is curious and has already recorded a verdict on stage 1, offer the three public links: the figure text, the formula and usage rule, and the essays. Their later agreement is a conversation, not corroboration (Fig 2 :40).

**Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method, never as an answer (Fig 4 :22). Each user writes a refuter before running it and states the mode of what they get.

**Stop rule.** If stage 1 fails, the theory is not offered. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.

**Hosting.** Nothing here is hosted or sent by this agent. The human operator hosts the packet on an opt-in page and sends one to one. Prefer a download page that lets a recipient decline contact over an attachment pushed to a list.
