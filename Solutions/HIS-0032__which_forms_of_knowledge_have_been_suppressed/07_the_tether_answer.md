# The Tether's answer: which forms of knowledge have been suppressed, appropriated or excluded

**Mode: `specified` on the mechanisms, `general` on the shape, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

An official history can be globally coherent and still wrong at particular slots: an uncredited contributor, a craft that worked but never entered the archive, a tradition held in a closure the archive never contacted, a correction that could be stated but never entered the canon. The question asks which forms occupy those slots. Stated in effect terms, the answer is a **typology by mechanism over a union of three overlapping conditions**, not a roster of names.

## 2. The shape

1. **The three verbs are a union, not three lists.** Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* A case can be suppressed, appropriated and excluded at once; Fig 9 :57 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"* but a specific compound needing its own loop.

2. **Suppression is a per-slot averaging failure.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A smooth canon and a live slot are compatible (Fig 1 :13). The form of knowledge suppressed is whatever occupies a slot whose residual the aggregate swallowed.

3. **Appropriation is a provenance failure, not a content failure.** Fig 8 :50: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents."* Fig 13 :127: *"Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available."* The form appropriated is the route: who derived it, from what, in what order. Fig 13 :130: *"A term carries the atoms it was built from and the bonds that joined them. That record is not documentation of the term. It is the term."*

4. **Exclusion is a closure effect.** Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 6 :25: *"An import adds no atom to the world."* The excluded knowledge was present in the world; the archive lacked contact with the frame holding it. Import gives access, not new atoms.

5. **Tacit methods and routes are lost when only products are archived.** Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* A token mention is a recording, not a method (Fig 4 :25).

6. **The archive is not the anchor.** Fig 2 :47: *"A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame."* Fig 10 :86: *"Outside the slice being graded. Whoever is measured by a reading cannot own it."* The ground is what happened and what worked, not the published record.

7. **Consensus cannot settle any of this.** Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 7 :12 to :13: *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"*

8. **The framework returns a shape, not a roster.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"* Named cases (Franklin, Lacks, ethnomathematics) are world illustrations, zero verdict weight. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a list of suppressed traditions. That list requires ground-level historical work, not figure derivation.
- Not a claim that every omission is an injustice. Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49); the same actor can be a benefactor to one history and a malfactor to another.
- Not a claim that adding names repairs the mechanism. A canon without negation can only accumulate (Operators table :58).
- Not a claim that the archive is deliberately deceitful. It occupies a position that cannot own the reading (Fig 10 :85).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement of an archive was taken.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A provenance-discriminating instrument, not more agreement. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: *"the instrument is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source."* Here the corresponding checks are:

1. **Slot test:** does a given archive show a near-zero global residual with one or more live per-slot residuals, and is the slot information lost in aggregation? (Fig 1)
2. **Provenance test:** for a claimed appropriation, does the artifact's contents fail to determine the route, while a separate record settles it? (Fig 8 :50, Fig 13 :127)
3. **Closure test:** is the knowledge reachable only through contact with a frame whose closure differs, with a source gate and a shadow test? (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8)
4. **Contact test:** does the knowledge fail to travel as a written artifact but travel as a method through apprenticeship or practice? (Fig 12 :111, Fig 4 :22)
5. **Standing falsifiers:** empty intersections between the three conditions; full recoverability of slot data from the aggregate; a surviving provenance record with identical contents; composition outside a closure without import.

## 5. The honest stop

**Reached in shape, unreached in roster.** Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"* Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The framework can say what any recovered knowledge must look like and what would have to be imported; it cannot say which tradition fills a slot, because naming that would already require holding it. The six-term set and the figure rules are a visible guess, not a discovery of history's own list. *"Indiscernibility is not identity"* (canonical figures README). The stop is recorded here so a reader can locate the error rather than only feel it.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not produce a list of erased thinkers, and it does not claim one. It says that "suppressed", "appropriated" and "excluded" are three overlapping conditions needing separate checks; that an official history can look complete globally while single contributor slots stay live; that appropriation is a provenance failure, since a derived result and a taken result look identical in the artifact, so the missing thing is the record of the route; that exclusion happens when the archive has no contact with a frame that holds the knowledge, and importing it adds access, not new facts; and that methods and tacit routes do not survive when only their products are archived. It refuses to name which traditions were suppressed, because the framework returns a shape, not a name, and that naming has to be done from the ground. What it offers is the shape any such history must have, four tests that would settle a slot, and refuters written in advance.
