## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Why a union and not a partition

- Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- Fig 9 :56: *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*
- Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*
- A Franklin-type case is in A (data taken without permission) and E (the contribution not counted) at once. A single lever (add a footnote) clears A-only and leaves E standing. A second loop is owed for the intersection.
- **Falsifier written before the run:** if the intersections are empty, one lever suffices and the union claim fails.

### Q2 - What is the order of work

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* The chain for this question is:

1. **Perceive:** enumerate the live slots (Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*).
2. **Vocabulary:** describe the habitat in effect terms (Fig 11 :43 *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*). This is the middle link with no instrument (Fig 3 :27).
3. **Objective:** state what would count as progress, namely a recovered provenance and a settled slot.
4. **Planning:** choose the import from a differing closure (Fig 6 :23).
5. **Learn and carry:** settle against the ground, carry the method (Fig 4 :22).

Effort spent above a break arrives at a step that cannot use it (Fig 3 :20 to :21). Writing a roster before perceiving the slots is effort above the break.

- **Falsifier:** if a complete recovery can be produced by starting with a roster and no slot enumeration, the dependency order is wrong.

### Q3 - Are the three conditions isomers

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

- As written, S, A and E do not share an atom set: S is about entry into the archive, A about the record of a route, E about the frame's criterion of knowledge. So they are **not isomers** under Fig 12's definition.
- The stronger form: if each is rewritten over the full shared atom set of the history (artifact, route, provenance, closure, ground), then they **are** isomers, same atoms arranged differently, and the difference is decisive in the behavior (a footnote clears A but not E).
- Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."* A case in two conditions is a specific compound, not a harder case of either (Fig 9 :57 to :58).
- **Falsifier:** if each condition requires an atom the others do not, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading below stands instead.

### Q4 - The functional group, not the isomer, is the safe claim

Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

- All three conditions share the substructure "a live slot residual produced by a frame-to-ground contact failure." That fixes the kind (exclusion mechanism). The qualifier (entry, provenance, criterion) says which member.
- This is the claim used in `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md`: separate loops over a shared functional group, with the isomer claim marked `specified` and unmeasured.

### Q5 - The catalyst analogue

Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."*

- A practice or method that survives its own use in the archive (a method still reapplicable) is a catalyst. A token mention that is spent on one occasion is not. Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*
- The archive does not record which is which, so a citation count cannot discriminate (Fig 1 :24 to :27: predicates minted and credibility accrued are frame-internal and do not count).
- **Falsifier:** if the archive record distinguishes methods from one-off mentions reliably, the catalyst finding is unnecessary.
