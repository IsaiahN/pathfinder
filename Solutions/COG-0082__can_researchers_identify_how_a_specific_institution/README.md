## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not whether an institution and a cognitive or behavioral change occurred together. Both options accept the co-occurrence. What is being decided is the **bond between them**.

In effect terms: for person-slot s, the pre-institution library predicts a behavior; the institution I is introduced or varied; the observation o' returns a changed behavior. The residual R = |Γ(b,a) - o'| is indexed per person and per time, never averaged (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22).

- Option A, "the institution caused the change": the bond is **ordered and productive**. The institution precedes the change, and the change would not have occurred in its absence. In figure terms this is production or sequence (Fig 12 :77, "A produces B; B did not exist before"; Fig 12 :71, "A then B; B needs A to have happened").
- Option B, "the institution merely accompanied the change": the bond is **symmetric or common-caused**. Both hold at once (Fig 12 :56, "both hold at once"), or both are outputs of a hidden shared source (Fig 2 :19, "they never talk, and still agree"). People who enter the institution are already changing for other reasons; selection, not the institution, produces the association.
- What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a **shape** stating the conditions that must all hold before a cause can be attributed, plus an instrument that discriminates an ordered bond from a common-cause bond. The shape is: (C1) the association is real at the slot level, (C2) variation in the institution is pinned against a hidden common cause by an anchor that does not update on either, (C3) the mechanism lineage is traced through intermediate steps, (C4) the measurement discriminates the construct from its reading. Until this shape is bound it predicts nothing and no check in the loop can see it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is: yes in principle, but only by importing an anchor independent of the common cause and by tracing the lineage; association alone cannot do it. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged. Mode `specified`. The name of the cause in a specific historical case is a declared `gap`, not a hidden answer.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the question, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of causal identification.

Four reasons, three from the figures and one practical:

1. A room is not an anchor. "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool" (Fig 2 :40). A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
