# Expert verification packet - the Tether on cause versus accompaniment

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in institutional analysis or research methods can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No claim is made that this derivation identifies a cause in any particular institution. It is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

An institution that merely accompanies a cognitive or behavioral change and one that caused it are not different-sized effects. They are different bonds over the same measurements. Accompaniment is a symmetric conjunction, "both hold at once" (Fig 12 :56), or a hidden common cause, "they never talk, and still agree" (Fig 2 :19). Causation is ordered and productive, "A produces B; B did not exist before" (Fig 12 :77). Four conditions must hold together: slot-level co-occurrence, variation in the institution pinned against the common cause by an anchor that does not update, a traced mechanism lineage that discriminates rivals, and an instrument that distinguishes the construct from its reading. They form a union, so a single lever clears only the region where one condition binds. The framework returns this shape, not the name of the cause.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 12 and 2

- **Statement:** accompaniment and causation are different bond types, not different magnitudes, and the common-cause case is explicitly modelled.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :5 - "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Fig 12 :56 - "both hold at once". Fig 12 :77 - "A produces B; B did not exist before". Fig 2 :19 - "they never talk, and still agree". Fig 2 :21 - "not two frames confirming each other".
- **What to check:** are the symmetric and ordered bonds actually distinguished by the operators table, and does the common-cause panel actually model two unconnected frames agreeing through one evidence pool?
- **Refuter:** exhibit a case where the same co-occurrence is correctly describable as both a symmetric conjunction and an ordered production at the same level, or show that the common-cause panel requires communication between the correlated terms.

### C2 - identification-facing; checkable by a methods researcher

- **Statement:** the anchor condition is necessary, and no frame can certify its own completeness with respect to unmeasured confounders.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 - "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :25 - "pinned: it does not update on A or B". Fig 8 :35 - "A frame cannot certify its own limit." Fig 8 :39 - "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 9 :60 - "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."
- **What to check:** does an identification strategy supply variation independent of the common cause, and is its validity an assumption that the data cannot certify by themselves? Compare with Pearl 2010 (below), who states that causal assumptions cannot be verified even in principle without experimental control.
- **Refuter:** exhibit an ordered bond identified with variation fully determined by the common cause and no imported independence, or a procedure inside the observational frame that certifies no unmeasured confounder remains.

### C3 - mechanism and measurement; checkable by a process-tracing or measurement specialist

- **Statement:** "how" is answered by the recorded lineage, and the reading of a measure does not determine its source.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :129 - "It is recoverable from the lineage." Fig 13 :127 - "The array cannot be checked by inspection, so the record is what makes it verifiable." Fig 8 :51 - "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them". Fig 2 :38 - "instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure".
- **What to check:** can rival hypotheses be discriminated by traces (Bennett and Checkel 2015, via TASO: "Find evidence for rival hypotheses: The final step is to repeat step 6 for each alternative explanation."), and can the instrument distinguish a construct change from measurement drift?
- **Refuter:** show the mechanism route is fully recoverable from the final estimate alone, or exhibit a measurement whose reading uniquely determines its source for all possible sources.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that causal institutional effects are impossible. An anchor can settle a bond.
- Not that any particular method is sufficient, and not that this derivation is the only account of identification.
- Not that a significant association is an identification, or that a null effect is a refutation.
- Not that a measure's reading identifies the source; metamerism is the standing failure mode.
- Not that the framework can name which institution caused a specific change: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself" (Fig 9 :32).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by an experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating anchor, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source". Here:

1. **Anchor Test:** variation in the institution that does not move with the suspected common cause, and whether the change follows it.
2. **Trace Test:** an intermediate step predicted by the ordered bond and absent under each rival, with the lineage recorded.
3. **Discriminator Test:** an instrument that separates the construct change from measurement drift, or an honest statement that none is available at this resolution.
4. Any of the six pre-registered falsifiers in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 3.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 12 :5, :56, :77 and Fig 2 :19, :21, :29, :25. Seven short passages.
2. Check whether their own field's identification standard meets the anchor condition, or whether its assumptions are certified only inside the model (Fig 8 :35).
3. Name one rival that would predict a different intermediate trace, or say why none exists.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
