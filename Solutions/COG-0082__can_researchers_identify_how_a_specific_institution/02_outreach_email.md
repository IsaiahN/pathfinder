**Subject:** Check one output on cause versus accompaniment before you decide about the theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about identifying institutional effects. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with me. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a change that accompanies an institution and a change caused by an institution are not different-sized effects. They are different bonds. Accompaniment is a symmetric conjunction: both variables hold at once, or both are driven by a hidden shared source. Causation is ordered and productive: the institution's variation precedes the change, and the change would not have occurred without it. No increase in sample size or precision converts the first bond into the second.
>
> From that distinction four conditions follow, and they form a union rather than a list:
> 1. the co-occurrence is real at the person-slot level, not only in an average;
> 2. variation in the institution is pinned against a hidden common cause by an anchor that does not update on either variable (random assignment, a natural experiment, an instrument, a discontinuity);
> 3. the mechanism lineage is traced through intermediate steps, with rival traces checked;
> 4. the instrument discriminates the construct from its reading.
>
> What I am **not** claiming: that an association however precise is an identification; that controlling for every measured confounder certifies that none is unmeasured; that a null effect proves the institution did nothing; that an effect estimate alone tells you *how* the change happened; or that the framework can name which specific institution caused a specific change. It returns the shape an identification must have, not the name of the cause.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim.** I wrote these down before sending this:
> 1. the same co-occurrence is correctly describable as both a symmetric conjunction and an ordered production at the same level;
> 2. an ordered bond is identified with variation fully determined by the common cause and no imported independence;
> 3. a procedure inside the observational frame certifies that no unmeasured confounder remains;
> 4. the mechanism route is fully recoverable from the final estimate alone;
> 5. one intervention clears all four conditions in every case, or the intersections never bind.
>
> If any holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figures text with line numbers (local copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures and formula for direct download (branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Worked narrative and the shape derivation: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-intelligence-actually-works
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on an identification problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
