## 0. The claim under audit

> Can researchers identify how a specific institution caused a cognitive or behavioral change rather than merely accompanying it?

The question is not about the size of an association. Both options accept that the institution and the change were observed together. The question is about the **kind of bond** between them. "Accompanying" is a symmetric conjunction or a common cause. "Caused" is an ordered, productive bond. The figures distinguish these by bond type, not by effect size (Fig 12 :56, :71, :77).

**Bound answer-shaped term (Formula, "Direction").** Before the run I bind what an answer would look like, so the preference becomes a refusable ordinary term: a *shape* with four conditions. (C1) the co-occurrence is real at the person-slot level; (C2) variation in the institution is pinned against a hidden common cause by an anchor that does not update on either variable; (C3) the mechanism lineage is traced through intermediate steps; (C4) the instrument discriminates the construct from its reading. The claim is that these four are a **union**, not a list, so a single lever clears only the region where one condition binds (Fig 9 :55, :57).

### 0b. Residual in effect terms, and the domain census

**R.** For person-slot s at time t, the library predicts a behavior Γ(b,a); exposure to institution I is the action; the observation o' returns a changed behavior. R = |Γ(b,a) - o'| per slot, indexed by person and time.

- **Arity:** at least three-place (institution I, outcome change Δ, hidden common cause C), indexed by slot s and ordered by t. A fourth term, the measuring instrument M, is in contact with Δ and can fail on axes it does not measure.
- **Symmetry:** association is symmetric (conjunction, + bond); causation is ordered (sequence → or production ⇒). This is the crux of the question. The bond is not recoverable from the operands (Fig 12 :5).
- **Scale:** the individual person loop, the population estimate, and institutional-historical time. The transform between the individual mechanism and the population statistic is lossy (Fig 4 :30).

**One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor.** A line with no nameable anchor is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Observational association | researchers, datasets, measured I and Δ | association, estimated effect | none inside the frame: Δ moves with the common cause | admitted, anchor-deficient |
| D2 Identification by anchor | randomized assignment, natural experiment, instrument, discontinuity | variation in I independent of C | the assignment mechanism, which is pinned: "pinned: it does not update on A or B" (Fig 2 :25) | admitted, grounded only when the intervention exists |
| D3 Mechanism / process | intermediate steps, documents, traces, sequences | provenance and lineage | the historical record, which does not update on the account built from it | admitted |
| D4 Measurement / construct | the test, score, observer, rating | contact with the construct | a discriminating instrument, always provisional: "instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure" (Fig 2 :38) | admitted |
| X "the institution caused it" as a free-floating property | the institution alone | talent-like property | none nameable | not a domain yet; absorbed into D1 to D4 |

**Union, not partition.** C1 to C4 overlap and bind simultaneously: an unbiased estimate with a broken measure is still not an identification; a clean mechanism with no anchor is a plausible story. Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Perception of Δ (is the measure returning anything?) → vocabulary for what the institution affords (what would count as a change?) → objective (what is the estimand and the counterfactual?) → planning (which anchor breaks the common cause?) → learn and carry (name what worked and verify it). The chain is ordered by dependency, not importance: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." (Fig 3 :20). The middle links, vocabulary and objective, are where the instrument is usually missing: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." (Fig 3 :27).

## 2. Findings

### F1 -- The question is a bond-type question, not an effect-size question (decisive, `general`)

Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."

Fig 12 :56: "both hold at once" (the symmetric conjunction, + bond).

Fig 12 :71: "A then B; B needs A to have happened" (the ordered sequence, → bond).

Fig 12 :77: "A produces B; B did not exist before" (the ordered production, ⇒ bond).

Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance."

Accompaniment and causation are **different bonds over possibly identical ingredients**. A larger sample, a smaller p value, or a larger standardized coefficient changes the weight on the conjunction; it cannot convert a symmetric bond into an ordered one. This is why the question is answerable in principle: the thing being asked for is a structural relation, not a magnitude. Mode `general` for the bond distinction, `specified` for its application to institutions.

**Refuter:** exhibit a case where the same measured co-occurrence is correctly described as both a symmetric conjunction and an ordered production under the same description and the same level. If the two cannot be separated even in principle, the bond distinction does not govern this question and F1 falls.

### F2 -- "Merely accompanying" is the common-cause collapse (`general`)

Fig 2 :6: "collapse 2: common cause". Fig 2 :7: "(hidden shared source)".

Fig 2 :19: "they never talk, and still agree".

Fig 2 :20: "so the agreement is the pool talking".

Fig 2 :21: "not two frames confirming each other".

The institution and the change can co-occur without influencing each other because both are driven by selection into the institution, by background conditions, or by an earlier common cause. The figures' common-cause panel is exactly this: two frames that never communicate still agree because one evidence pool speaks through both. In institutional analysis this is selection bias, endogeneity, and omitted-variable bias under another name.

**Refuter:** show that the common-cause panel requires communication between the two correlated terms. It does not; the figure's point is that agreement without contact is the pool talking. If a reader can exhibit co-occurrence with no shared source and no ordered bond, F2 is incomplete and the census needs a fifth bond.

### F3 -- Identification requires an anchor that does not update (`specified`)

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."

Fig 2 :29: "A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."

Fig 2 :25: "pinned: it does not update on A or B".

Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update."

This is the governing line. To separate cause from common cause, the researcher needs variation in I that is **not produced by C**. Random assignment, a natural experiment such as a compulsory-schooling rule, an instrument, or a regression discontinuity is such a pin: the assignment mechanism does not consult the confounder. This is why the identification is not a property of the dataset but of the anchor imported into it. Mode `specified`: which anchor works in a given case is a ground question.

**Refuter:** exhibit a case where the institution's variation is fully determined by the common cause and the ordered bond is still identified without importing any independent variation. If so, the anchor requirement is too strong and must be narrowed to the cases where a hidden source is actually present.

### F4 -- Independence and triangulation, not agreement (`general` for the rule, `specified` for outreach)

Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."

Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"

Ten observational studies using the same dataset and the same covariate set are one witness wearing ten names (Fig 7 :12-13, "same closure, handed along;" / "agreement compounds, evidence does not"). A strong identification design and an independent process trace are two frames with low overlap, so their agreement means something. This is a rule about evidence, not about how many studies exist.

**Refuter:** show that two frames drawing on exactly the same evidence can still corroborate one another in the figure's sense. The figures deny this; if the denial fails, the independence requirement goes with it.

### F5 -- No frame can certify its own limit; absence is not proof of absence (`general`)

Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."

Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

Fig 8 :35: "A frame cannot certify its own limit."

Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"

Therefore "we controlled for every confounder" cannot be established from inside the observational frame. It is a convention about the model. When it decides the outcome, it has stopped being a convention and has become an authored constant carrying the researcher's authority. Likewise, a null effect is not a proof that the institution did nothing; the instrument may simply lack the resolution (Fig 6 :34-35). Formula step 7 keeps the three questions separate: SEARCHED (is the variation in here and unfound?), INWARD (is our representation adequate?), OUTWARD (which other frame already holds the anchor?). Importing against a genuine budget limit adds an atom that was not needed, so underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget must not be conflated.

**Refuter:** produce a decision procedure inside a frame that certifies its own completeness with respect to unmeasured confounders. The three metaframe results in Fig 8 are proofs against this; a refutation would have to break one of them.

### F6 -- "How" is answered by the lineage, not by the estimate (`specified`)

Fig 13 :127: "The array cannot be checked by inspection, so the record is what makes it verifiable."

Fig 13 :128: "Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available."

Fig 13 :129: "It is recoverable from the lineage."

Fig 13 :132: "The first is provenance: how did this come to be, and from what."

Fig 8 :51: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"

An unbiased estimate says *that* a change is attributable; it does not say *how* the institution produced it. The "how" is the chain of intermediate steps, decisions, and traces, and it is read off the lineage. This is the figure-grounded content of process tracing. It also supplies the check on F5: a rival explanation that predicts a different trace is a way to let the ground vote rather than letting the model certify itself.

**Refuter:** exhibit a case where the intermediate lineage is fully recoverable from the final effect estimate alone. If so, the provenance requirement adds nothing and D3 collapses into D1.

### F7 -- The measure is an instrument and can fail on unmeasured axes (`specified`)

Fig 2 :38: "instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure".

Fig 6 :32: "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."

Fig 6 :34: "the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."

A change in a score is not automatically a change in the construct. The source essay *The Hard Problem is About Source* names the metamerism error: a reading of green cannot say whether the source was green, or yellow plus blue, or RGB. The instrument that settled it was a spectrometer, not an argument. For institutions, a test score or a response rate is a reading; the causal claim is about a source. What is owed when the reading cannot discriminate is a better instrument, not a finer argument about the estimate.

**Refuter:** exhibit an instrument whose reading uniquely determines the source for all possible sources. The essay's metamerism case denies this; a genuine discriminator would refute the general form and would itself be the thing owed.

### F8 -- The specific institution is a slot-level question (`general`)

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."

Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

An average treatment effect over a population can be near zero while one person-slot carries a large live residual. Asking about "a specific institution" therefore forbids answering only with a population mean. The unit of attribution is the person-in-context at a time, which is also why the effect is a property of person-and-habitat rather than of the institution alone (Fig 11 :39, "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"; Fig 11 :40, "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves").

**Refuter:** show that the question is fully answerable at the population level with no slot-level residual and no loss. If the aggregate is sufficient, the per-slot indexing rule does not govern here and F8 is withdrawn.

### F9 -- A larger sample is a filter for the budget, never a witness for the verdict (`general`)

Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

Fig 9 :44: "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."

Sample size, covariate count, and precision are budget quantities. They decide how much of a fixed closure is actually visited. They cannot add the anchor atom that the closure lacks (Fig 6 :14, "the only operator that moves the wall"). A filter that hands you a verdict is the named failure genus.

**Refuter:** exhibit a statistical procedure that creates new identification from more of the same observations without any change in the variation source. If one exists, closure is not idempotent for this domain and F9 falls.

### F10 -- The cross-scale trip is lossy and must be bracketed (`general`)

Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."

Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."

A mechanism at the person level and an effect estimate at the population level are related by a lossy transform. What survives the trip up is a method, not a recording of one occasion. The round-trip gap R_T must be measured, not assumed zero; otherwise the claim is dead reckoning. `11_audit.md` runs this trip explicitly.

**Refuter:** exhibit a transform between the individual mechanism description and the population estimate that is lossless in both directions. If one exists, R_T is zero here and the bracketing requirement is decorative.

### F11 -- The four conditions form a union, so one lever is not enough (`specified`)

Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."

Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

Fig 9 :56: "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."

A better instrument fixes C4 but leaves C2 standing; a sharper identification design fixes C2 but leaves C3 standing. The case in two conditions is not a harder case of either; it is a specific compound. This is why the verdict must be kept per domain and never averaged into a single "strength of evidence" score.

**Refuter:** exhibit a single intervention that clears all four conditions in every case, or show that the intersections never actually bind. Then the union reading is unnecessary.

| domain | figure-grounded verdict | status | mode |

## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Required before any outreach: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device. I commit to withdraw or narrow the shape claim if any of the following holds:

1. **Bond collapse:** the same co-occurrence is correctly describable as both a symmetric conjunction and an ordered production at the same level (breaks F1).
2. **Anchor-free identification:** an ordered bond is identified with variation fully determined by the common cause and no imported independence (breaks F3).
3. **Self-certification:** a procedure inside the observational frame certifies that no unmeasured confounder remains (breaks F5).
4. **Lineage redundancy:** the mechanism route is fully recoverable from the final effect estimate alone (breaks F6).
5. **Single-lever union:** one intervention clears all four conditions in every case, or the intersections never bind (breaks F11).
6. **Slot irrelevance:** the population aggregate is sufficient with no per-slot residual and no loss (breaks F8).

If any fires, the shape is wrong and the packet is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Lead with the bond distinction (F1) and the common-cause collapse (F2) in any outreach. That is the question the asker actually asked.
2. Do not present an association, however precise, as an identification. Ask what pins the institution's variation (F3).
3. Report which of the four conditions is unmet rather than a single "strength of evidence" score (F11).
4. Treat the mechanism lineage as a separate deliverable, not as narrative garnish on the estimate (F6).
5. Name the instrument's failure axes before claiming the construct changed (F7).
6. Keep the specific-cause answer a declared gap. "Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence" (Fig 13 :134).
7. Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." Send the generator, the shape and its refuter, not a list of conclusions.

Consequence for this question: the figures license the **shape** of an identification claim (four conditions, a pinned anchor, a traceable lineage, a discriminating instrument). They do not license naming the institution that caused a particular change. That would be a name where the figures return a kind of thing (Fig 9 :32).
