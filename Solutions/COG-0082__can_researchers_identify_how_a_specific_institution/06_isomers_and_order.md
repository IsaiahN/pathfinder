# Symmetric versus ordered bonds, dependency order, and rival explanations

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks for the difference between an institution that *caused* a change and one that *merely accompanied* it. Three supporting derivations are owed: (1) can the two cases be written as different bonds over the same atoms, (2) what is the dependency order of the checks, and (3) what are the rival explanations that must be traced rather than assumed. Each has its own falsifier.

## DOMAIN CENSUS (supporting)

Atoms held: the measured institution I, the measured change Δ, the hidden common cause C, the intermediate steps z, and the instrument M. Bonds available: the seven (Fig 12) plus negation. No new primitive is admitted without an import. The critical distinction is that the observational frame holds + (conjunction) and ⇒ / → (production / sequence) as notation but cannot decide which one obtains without an anchor.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are accompaniment and causation isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance."
- Write accompaniment as `I + Δ` (both hold at once), with a dashed common source `C → I` and `C → Δ`.
- Write causation as `I ⇒ Δ` (production), with `C → Δ` but no `C → I` path after the anchor fires.
- Both molecules hold the same atoms `{I, Δ, C}`; the bond arrangement differs. Under Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**, not variants of one thing. This is the structural reason the question is well formed: asking which isomer you have is a legitimate question, whereas asking how much of one you have is a category error.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if the same observed co-occurrence cannot be written both ways under any admissible bond assignment, the isomer claim fails and the two cases are not even the same molecule. I would withdraw F1 in `01` and the outreach claim built on it.
- Triangulated: Fig 12 :117 plus the Operators table's separate tests for + and ⇒.

### Q2 - What is the dependency order of the checks?

- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."
- Formula step 7 orders SEARCHED before INWARD and OUTWARD, because the latter two assume the answer to the first.
- Mapped order for this question:
  1. **Perception (link 1):** is M returning a reading at all? If not, everything downstream is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :23, "A reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing.").
  2. **Vocabulary (link 2):** do we have terms for what the institution affords and for what would count as a change? This is the link with no instrument in most institutional research: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." (Fig 3 :27).
  3. **Objective (link 3):** state the estimand and the counterfactual. A goal is a comparison (Fig 12 :112, "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison."); without it the loop cannot say what would count as progress.
  4. **Planning (link 4):** choose the anchor that breaks the common-cause path.
  5. **Learn and carry (link 5):** name what worked, verify it, and record the lineage for the next case.
- **Falsifier:** if a researcher can identify an ordered bond while links 1 to 3 are unmeasured, the dependency order does not govern this domain and the 20-minute check list in `08` must be rewritten.
- Triangulated: Fig 3 dependency ordering plus Fig 9 :43 ("The verdict tells you to leave. The lookup tells you where to go. Elimination is what you do after you arrive.").

### Q3 - What are the rival explanations, and how are they discriminated?

From the TASO process-tracing guidance (Bennett and Checkel 2015 definition, read 2026-11-22): the method requires *"Identify counterfactual outcomes: Counterfactuals are vital to process tracing."* and *"Find evidence for rival hypotheses: The final step is to repeat step 6 for each alternative explanation."*

The rivals for a claimed institutional effect are:

| rival | bond it predicts | discriminating observation |
|---|---|---|
| Selection into the institution | `C → I` and `C → Δ` (common cause) | variation in I that is independent of C (the anchor) leaves Δ unchanged |
| Maturation or secular trend | Δ arrives on its own time axis | a discontinuity in I at a fixed date with no discontinuity in Δ |
| Reverse causation | `Δ → I` | the institution's introduction follows the change in the recorded sequence |
| Measurement drift | M changed, not the construct | a stable instrument with an independent source check removes the reading change |
| Institutional spillover | another concurrent institution produced Δ | rival traces name a different intermediate step |

- Fig 13 :129: "It is recoverable from the lineage." The rivals are discriminated by traces, not by a larger coefficient.
- Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."
- **Falsifier:** if a single rival survives every discriminating observation and cannot be refused, the identification is not unique and the claim must be narrowed to "one of several compounds". That is still a verdict, not a silence (Fig 13 :134).
- Triangulated for the method (Fig 13 lineage plus the TASO guidance); single-source for the specific rival list, which is field-specific and open.

### Q4 - Does the union need an eighth condition?

- Fig 13 :115: "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."
- The four conditions C1 to C4 are held terms. More of them, generated by recombining the same vocabulary, would add arrangements, not a new condition. Fig 6 :14: "the only operator that moves the wall".
- What would genuinely add a new condition is an import from a frame whose closure differs, for example a source-discriminating instrument from measurement science or an identification strategy from a field that does not use the same observational vocabulary. Such an import carries a provenance cost and must pass a shadow test (Fig 8 :51).
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a fifth condition that cannot be composed from the held terms and is not an import. If one exists, the four-condition census is incomplete.
- Single-source for the closure claim (Fig 13), corroborated by Fig 8 :32.

## CONCLUSION

Accompaniment and causation are **isomers** over the same atoms with different bonds (Fig 12 :117), so the question asks which arrangement obtains, not how large an effect is. The checks are **dependency ordered** (Fig 3 :20), with the estimand/counterfactual link the usual break. Rivals are discriminated by **lineage** (Fig 13 :129) rather than by precision. The four-condition set is closed but its arrangements are not, so new leverage must be imported with provenance.

No claim is `grounded`. The isomer reading, the dependency order and the rival list are `specified` shape claims with stated falsifiers. Which isomer actually holds in a specific institution is a `gap` the figures do not fill.
