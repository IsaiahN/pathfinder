## 1. The residual, stated in effect terms

Let Γ be a model that says: enforcement requires a formal authority holding a monopoly on sanction. Act a = withhold formal authority. Γ(b,a) predicts that defection pays, cooperation unravels, and a norm cannot bind. The observation o' is that many communities do sustain compliance with norms when formal authority is weak or absent (Ostrom 1990; Ellickson 1991; Henrich et al. 2006, all opened 2026-11-22).

R = |Γ(b,a) − o'| is therefore **the missing enforcement function**: what supplies a cost of defection when the state does not.

R is indexed per slot, never averaged. Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." The live slots are:

| slot | the question the slot asks |
|---|---|
| s1 detection | Is a violation actually registered by anyone? |
| s2 refusal | Can someone act to refuse the violation at a cost to themselves? |
| s3 transmission | Does the norm and the method of enforcing it persist and travel? |
| s4 scale | Does the same arrangement still work when the group grows and no higher authority backs it? |

A global statement such as "social capital holds the community together" aggregates the four slots and throws away the only signal that says where enforcement fails.

**Direction bound as an ordinary term.** Before running, write down what an answer would look like: an answer is a *shape* specifying (i) what registers a violation, (ii) what refuses it, (iii) what crosses between people and generations, and (iv) what is invariant across scale. This makes the sought-for shape refusable by the same test as everything else. The figures do not name a particular norm or a particular sanction.

## 2. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.** Arity: at least two actors, since enforcement is relational, and often three, since a third party punishes a transgression it did not suffer (Fehr and Fischbacher 2004, opened 2026-11-22). Symmetry: mixed. Peer monitoring is close to symmetric, while reputation and sanction capacity are ordered (a spectrum, "ordering, so more and less can be said", Fig 13 :20). Scale: local face-to-face up to nested multi-level arrangements (Ostrom's eighth design principle). The residual is thus a **union compound**, not one hard question. Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." The two conditions are absent formal authority and continued compliance.

### One line per domain

| domain | actors | currency | anchor (nameable or not) |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 detection and monitoring | fellow appropriators, accountable monitors, observers | observations, gossip, reputation, history of the other player | observed change in behavior after a violation is registered. If nothing changes, no anchor is nameable and this is not yet a domain. |
| D2 refusal and sanction | second-party victims, third-party punishers, norm entrepreneurs | graduated sanctions, shaming, ostracism, reputation loss | observed decline in defection when a sanction is applied, with the sanction not merely declared. |
| D3 transmission and origin | imitators, conformists, newcomers, neighbouring groups | w (private history versus inherited prior), conformist transmission, cultural lineage | persistence of the norm across a generation or a migration with contact intact. |
| D4 scale and membrane | local groups, nested enterprises, weak or absent higher authority | methods that cross scales, not recordings of particular sanctions | compliance still changing with ground pressure after the group grows. |

No domain is admitted on the strength of the word "norm" alone. Each has an anchor that can register a difference in the world.

### Partition or union

Union, not partition. D2 consumes what D1 produces (a sanction needs a detected violation), D3 supplies the norm that D1 and D2 enforce, and D4 modulates all three. Overlaps are compounds with their own loops. Fig 9 requires unbundling before searching: describe the residual, then find the frame; otherwise any field picked will seem to fit (Fig 9 :27-28). The four domains are kept as four verdicts and are never averaged.

### Dependency order

Per Fig 3 and `THE_FORMULA.md` step 1, the chain runs:

1. perception: can the community tell a violation from what was there before (D1);
2. vocabulary: does it have terms for what the situation affords, what can be objected to, and what would count as putting it right;
3. objective: can it say what progress toward compliance would be, in its own terms;
4. planning: choose a sanction under incomplete information (D2);
5. learn and carry: name what worked, verify it, carry it (D3, D4).

Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Link 2, vocabulary and objective, is the middle link that usually breaks. For informal norms, "what counts as a violation and what would count as making it right" is typically implicit and contested, and has the fewest instruments. Effort spent on link 4 (designing harsher sanctions) cannot rescue a break at link 2.

## 4. Findings F1 to F8

### F1. Enforcement is not the output of agreement; it is triangulation against a ground

Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."

A community whose members only check against one another drifts coherently and notices nothing. The framework author's own account of the safeguards people actually built matches this: independent replication, two-way apprenticeship correction, and courts that rule on evidence neither side can move (Decoding Tacit Knowledge, 2026-11-10, opened 2026-11-22). Ostrom's fourth design principle names the same structure in the field: monitors "are accountable to the appropriators or are the appropriators" (Ostrom 1990, as quoted on the Elinor Ostrom page, opened 2026-11-22). Accountability to the people monitored is what keeps the reading from becoming negotiation.

- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Verdict:** triangulated across Fig 2, Fig 7 and the Ostrom field record (low overlap: a checking theorem and a field study).
- **Refuter:** show a community sustaining compliance while every member's reading of a violation moves with the group's agreement and no independent ground registers anything. That would be coherent drift actually enforcing, which the figures deny.

### F2. A capacity to refuse is required, and it is not the same as adding rules

Fig 12 :140 "A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate."

Operators table :58 "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"

A sanction is a refusal of a claim ("this conduct is not acceptable here"), not a subtraction of an operand. With only conjunction-like accumulation, a group can record more and more norms without being able to carry the correction. The framework's society-of-agents sketch states the operational version: "Bad news travels in the same lane as good news", and failed patterns are marked for avoidance with decay so that innovation can still happen (How a Society of Agents Would Work, 2026-10-22, opened 2026-11-22).

**Status correction, recorded and not erased:** my first reading took Fig 12 :140 as a direct derivation of legal punishment. Re-census shows it governs the operator set of a *term vocabulary*, not the institutions of a community. Applied to norm enforcement it is a cross-level analogue with a shadow test (it explains why enforcement needs a refusal operation that mere rule-accumulation lacks), not a direct derivation. The claim survives as `specified`; its status was downgraded. This is a retraction of standing, not of the claim.

- **Mode:** `specified`, cross-level analogue.
- **Verdict:** triangulated between Fig 12 and the Operators table (different statements of the same operator fact, so low independence between those two), plus the society-of-agents design sketch.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a community that enforces norms while possessing no operation that refuses a claim, only an ever-growing list of affirmations.

### F3. The enforcing unit is the person-and-habitat, not the person alone

Fig 11 :39 "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."

Fig 11 :40 "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

Fig 11 :45 "actors: what acts on it, and what it acts on"

The habitat must be enumerated, never composed. For this residual that means listing monitors, punishers, victims, newcomers, the resource condition, and the relations among them, then following contact outward until the cascade stops mattering. A "community with strong norms" is not a property of the individuals; it is a property of the arrangement.

- **Mode:** `general` on the invariance, `specified` on the enumerated habitat.
- **Verdict:** triangulated across Fig 11 and Ostrom's field studies (different closures: a capability theorem and irrigation systems, forests and fisheries).
- **Refuter:** show an individual sustaining the same enforcement function after removal from the habitat that produced it, with contact unchanged.

### F4. Detection is a network effect and reputation is an ordering

Fig 13 :24 "that entities relate, and influence travels"

Fig 13 :20 "ordering, so more and less can be said"

Third-party punishment is punishment administered by a party not directly affected (Fehr and Fischbacher 2004). For that to work at all, information about a transgression must travel, and the traveler must be ranked: Axelrod's analysis gives two requirements for reciprocal cooperation, that players recognize other players to avoid cheaters, and that they track previous history with a given player (The Evolution of Cooperation, Axelrod 1984, as summarized on its Wikipedia page, opened 2026-11-22). Nowak's indirect reciprocity makes the same point: cooperation depends on "a reliable history being projected from past partners to future partners" (Nowak 2006, reported in the same page). The network (influence travels) and the spectrum (more and less can be said) are the two structures doing the work.

- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Verdict:** triangulated across Fig 13, Axelrod's game-theoretic requirements and Nowak's reputation mechanism (three distinct fields with low direct overlap).
- **Refuter:** show third-party sanctioning operating with no information path between the transgression and the punisher and no ranked history of the transgressor.

### F5. A declared norm is not a grounded norm, and a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one

Fig 10 :49 "Declaring a meaning does not ground it."

Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"

Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

This is the dependency-chain break. A norm is only enforced when a violation can be registered and the reading changes behavior; a written or spoken declaration does not do that. Ostrom's second principle requires congruence between appropriation and provision rules and local conditions, and her fifth requires graduated sanctions depending on the seriousness and context of the offense. Both are instruments on the middle link, not declarations.

- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Verdict:** triangulated (Fig 10 plus Fig 3 plus the Ostrom principles).
- **Refuter:** show a norm whose violation is never registered by any instrument or person and yet reliably alters behavior. If that happens, the declaration has become a ground, contrary to Fig 10 :49.

### F6. What crosses between people and generations is a method, not a recording

Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."

Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."

Peyton Young's description of norms as "patterns of behavior that are self-enforcing within a group" and his observation that "norms typically evolve without top-down direction" (Social norm page, opened 2026-11-22) are the world-side statement of the unmaintained habitat: nothing authored the norm, yet the ground kept it. Where a community does maintain the arrangement, what it hands to a newcomer is a head start (a useful default, a named violation category), not a replay of a particular sanction. The round trip still has to be measured; a head start that lands in a different habitat will not match what was sent (Fig 4 :30).

- **Mode:** `specified`.
- **Verdict:** triangulated across Fig 4, Fig 6 and the world literature on spontaneous norm emergence.
- **Refuter:** show a norm persisting across a generation by exact replay of recorded sanctions with no contact, no adaptation and no ground reading, and still changing behavior.

### F7. The four domains are a union and must not be averaged into one lever

Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."

The four verdicts stay separate. Detection can work while refusal fails; transmission can work while scale fails. Empirical support for keeping them apart: third-party punishment appears in all examined populations but varies greatly in magnitude (Henrich et al. 2006); people initially prefer a sanction-free group and only after several rounds join the sanctioning group (Gürerk, Irlenbusch and Rockenbach 2006, both reported on the Evolution of Cooperation and Third-party punishment pages, opened 2026-11-22). A single "norm strength" variable would hide both facts.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Verdict:** triangulated across Fig 1, Fig 9 and the two experimental findings.
- **Refuter:** exhibit one scalar that predicts detection, refusal, transmission and scale outcomes with equal sign and no residual left on any slot.

### F8. Scale preserves the kernel, not the magnitude of punishment

Fig 4 :20 "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."

Ostrom's eighth principle is nested enterprises, with "appropriation, provision, monitoring, enforcement, conflict resolution, and governance activities organized in multiple layers of nested enterprises." That is the same kernel at more than one size, not a different mechanism. However, the empirical picture strains the comfortable "small groups are enough" story: Marlowe and Berbesque (2007) report *more* altruistic punishment in larger societies, and Henrich et al. (2006) find large cross-society variance. Re-census: Fig 4 governs what is invariant across scale (the loop and the rule for naming what worked), not the magnitude of a sanction. No contradiction; the magnitude is an empirical question the figures do not settle. The strain is named in section 8.

- **Mode:** `general` on kernel invariance, `specified` on the empirical magnitude claim.
- **Verdict:** single-source for the figure invariance, corroborated but not settled by the nested-enterprises record; the magnitude claim is world-only and ungrounded here.
- **Refuter:** show enforcement surviving a scale change with a different loop and a different rule for naming what worked, while no ground reading changed.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the standing rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is:

> Informal norm enforcement works when there is (i) a ground that does not move with agreement, (ii) monitors and refusers who can act and are accountable rather than self-authorizing, (iii) a method that crosses between people and scales, and (iv) a vocabulary in which a violation can be named and refused.

It is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A community sustains measurable compliance with no detection function, no refusal function and no ground that can register a violation (breaks D1 and D2).
2. Monitors' readings move with the group's agreement, the anchor updates, and compliance still holds (breaks Fig 2 :35 and the triangulation requirement).
3. A norm survives a scale or generational change solely as a replayed recording, with no method, no contact and no ground reading, and still changes behavior (breaks Fig 4 :22).
4. Cross-cultural data show costly third-party punishment to be absent wherever formal authority is absent (breaks the world corroboration in F4).
5. A single scalar is found that closes all four slots with no residual on any of them (breaks Fig 1 :19).

If any fires, the shape claim is withdrawn or narrowed accordingly. Until one is run, no part of this is `grounded`.

## 8. Strains, honestly recorded

1. **¬ applied across levels.** The Operators table defines negation as a unary operator on a claim. Applying it to a community's refusal of conduct is an analogy. The unary/binary distinction does not survive the crossing. This is recorded as a strain, not hidden, and is why F2 is `specified` rather than `general`.
2. **Introduction versus exclusion.** Fig 11 :61 "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." Ostrom's first design principle is clearly defined boundaries, which includes excluding outsiders. Boundary exclusion in a commons is a subtraction (-), whereas the figure's asymmetry is about what a maintainer can add to a habitat. Re-census: the figure governs a seat's power over a habitat; the boundary rule governs membership in a resource. Different objects, but the analogy is imperfect and is flagged.
3. **Punishment magnitude across scale.** Named in F8 and in the retraction log. World-only, ungrounded here.
4. **Overlap among world sources.** Wikipedia's Social norm, Third-party punishment and Common-pool resource pages share references (Fehr, Henrich, Ostrom, Nowak). They are one literature pool, not four witnesses. The ledger records overlap per row.

**After re-census: three strains and one overlap declared, none hidden.**
