# Outreach draft: check one shape claim before deciding about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the four domain verdicts in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are read in full. If the stage-1 shape does not survive a check, there is nothing else to show.

---

**Subject:** Would these four conditions refute an account of informal norm enforcement?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one claim about how communities enforce norms without formal authority. I would like you to check it against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. A one-line failure is more useful to me than agreement.
>
> **The residual (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A model that says enforcement requires a formal authority predicts that defection pays once that authority is absent. Communities often sustain compliance anyway. The gap is the missing enforcement function. I split it into four slots and refuse to average them: (1) is a violation detected, (2) can someone refuse it at a cost to themselves, (3) does the method of enforcing it persist and travel, (4) does the arrangement still work when the group grows. Figure 1 of the framework says the residual is indexed per slot, not aggregated.
>
> **The shape claimed.** Enforcement is triangulation against a ground that does not move with agreement, not negotiation among peers. The enforcing unit is the person and the habitat together, not the person alone. What crosses between people and generations is a method and a head start, not a replay of a particular sanction. The kernel is invariant across scale, though the magnitude of a sanction is an empirical question. The framework returns a shape, not a name, so this claim does not say which norm or which sanction a community must use.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** That formal authority is never needed. That every informal norm is efficient or good. That Ostrom's eight design principles were derived from the figures; they are an import with a source gate, and I record them as such. That small groups alone are sufficient; the evidence on punishment magnitude across societies is mixed. That any of this is settled by measurement. It is not.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before this run:**
> 1. compliance sustained with no detection function, no refusal function, and no ground that registers a violation;
> 2. monitors' readings moving with the group's agreement, with the anchor updating and compliance still holding;
> 3. a norm surviving a generation or a scale change by exact replay of recorded sanctions, with no contact and no ground reading, and still changing behavior;
> 4. costly third-party punishment absent wherever formal authority is absent;
> 5. one scalar closing all four slots with no residual left on any of them.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw or narrow the claim. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Ostrom, *Governing the Commons* (1990) and the International Journal of the Commons: https://www.thecommonsjournal.org/
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own account says a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and that outputs could never be verified if they were contrived to fit the formula. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Residual and refuter before the shape and long before the framework | Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." A reader who holds the framework before judging has joined the frame being checked. |
| No request for agreement or trust | Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." A mirror cannot correct the claim. |
| Verdict recorded before theory is shown | Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration. |
| Four slots kept separate | Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." |
| Refuter written before the run | Standing rule: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. |
| Mode stated on every substantive claim | `THE_FORMULA.md` mode rule: general, specified, grounded. Nothing here is grounded. |
| Institutional list labelled as an import | Fig 8 :50 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them." |
| Shape not a name | The figures return what a missing thing would look like, a shape, not a name. The email never says which norm is right. |
| Offer the formula as a reusable method | Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." A generator can be given away without giving away an answer. |
| One-to-one, public opt-in venues only | A list selected for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that would disqualify the verdict. |
