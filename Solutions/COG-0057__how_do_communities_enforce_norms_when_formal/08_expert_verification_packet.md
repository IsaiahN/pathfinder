# Expert verification packet: informal norm enforcement

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher on informal institutions or norm enforcement can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape claims, `general` on the structural invariance claim. Not `grounded`.** No field measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

When formal authority is weak or absent, enforcement is not the product of peer agreement. It requires a ground that does not move with agreement, so that monitors are corrected by something they cannot talk into moving; it requires an operation that refuses a violation, not merely a longer list of rules; it is a property of the person and the habitat together; information about a transgression travels through a network and is ranked by history; and what crosses between people and generations is a method and a head start, not a replay of one sanction. Detection, refusal, transmission and scale are four separate verdicts, never one score of "norm strength". Ostrom's design principles and Axelrod's requirements are provenanced imports that corroborate this shape, not deductions from the figures.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1. Framework-internal and checkable by reading Figures 2 and 7

- **Statement:** peer agreement cannot be the enforcing anchor. A monitor whose reading moves with the group's agreement produces coherent drift, not enforcement.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Also Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
- **What to check:** is the field record consistent with this? Ostrom's fourth principle puts monitors inside the group but accountable to the appropriators, and the framework's own account of real safeguards names independent replication, two-way apprenticeship correction and courts that rule on evidence neither side can move.
- **Refuter:** show a community sustaining compliance while every monitor's reading of a violation moves with group agreement and no independent ground registers anything.

### C2. Cross-level analogue, checkable by a researcher on sanctions

- **Statement:** enforcement needs a refusal operation. Accumulating norms without the capacity to refuse produces a growing record and an unchanged reach.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :140 "A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate." Operators table :58 "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."
- **What to check:** the figures govern a *term vocabulary*, so applying ¬ to a community is an analogue, not a direct derivation. Does the analogue transfer? Third-party punishment, where an unaffected party sanctions a transgressor, is the empirical candidate. Does refusal without a named violation ever work?
- **Refuter:** exhibit a community that enforces norms while possessing only affirmations and no operation that refuses a claim; or show that a community reliably sanctions violations no member can name, which would break the dependency order in `06`.

### C3. Framework-internal with an empirical tail

- **Statement:** the kernel is invariant across scale, but the magnitude of a sanction is not settled by the figures. Nested enterprises are the same loop at more than one size.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :20 "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." Also Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
- **What to check:** Ostrom's eighth principle is nested enterprises. Henrich et al. (2006) find large cross-society variance in costly punishment, and Marlowe and Berbesque (2007) report more altruistic punishment in larger societies. Does either finding contradict the claim? Under re-census it should not: the figure governs what is invariant, not the measured size of a sanction.
- **Refuter:** show enforcement surviving a scale change with a different loop and a different rule for naming what worked while no ground reading changed.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that formal authority is never needed.
- Not that every informal norm is efficient or good; the relationalist case that norms need not serve the collective good is recorded.
- Not that Ostrom's eight principles are figure-derived. They are an import with a source gate (Ostrom 1990) and a shadow test.
- Not that small groups alone are sufficient.
- Not that the ¬ analogy is a direct derivation; the unary/binary distinction does not survive the crossing and the strain is recorded.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not that the second-seat audit in `11_audit.md` is independent corroboration. It shares this folder's evidence pool.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating observation, not a further argument. Observing compliance does not name its source. The five refuters in `01` §5 are the discriminator. The three most direct observations are:

1. compliance before and after altering the monitoring function, with monitors accountable to the monitored;
2. defection before and after a graduated sanction administered by an unaffected third party;
3. persistence after a generational or scale change with contact intact, versus a replayed recording with contact broken.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :35, Fig 7 :35, Fig 12 :140, Fig 4 :20 and Fig 4 :22, five short passages.
2. Check C1 against one field case you know: were monitors accountable to the group, and what did they range against?
3. Decide whether the ¬ analogy in C2 transfers, or whether it is a category mistake. Say which.
4. Say whether the cross-cultural magnitude findings strain C3 or merely constrain its empirical tail.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
