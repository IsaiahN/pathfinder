## Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual line alone, before relying on the primary's conclusions. R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The gap between "no formal sanction means defection pays" and "compliance persists". Arity: two or more, often three, because a third party can punish a transgression it did not suffer. Symmetry: mixed, since mutual monitoring is close to symmetric while reputation is ordered. Scale: local up to nested multi-level. Two conditions are conjoined, so this is a union compound, not one hard question.
- **Domain 1, detection.** Actors: monitors, observers, fellow users. Currency: observation and history. Anchor: behavior change after a violation is registered. If no anchor can be named, no domain exists yet.
- **Domain 2, refusal.** Actors: victims, third parties, norm entrepreneurs. Currency: graduated sanctions and reputation loss. Anchor: defection change after a sanction.
- **Domain 3, transmission.** Actors: newcomers, imitators, neighbouring groups. Currency: inherited prior versus private history. Anchor: persistence across a generation with contact intact.
- **Domain 4, scale.** Actors: local groups and nested enterprises. Currency: methods rather than recordings. Anchor: compliance after growth with ground pressure unchanged.
- **Order by dependency.** Register, name the violation, refuse, settle, carry. Refuse before mint. Settling is necessary but not sufficient before a method is composed over.

## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 2 :35, Fig 7 :35, Fig 11 :39-40, Fig 12 :140, Fig 4 :22 and Fig 9 :57.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07`, that enforcement is triangulation against a ground, needs a refusal, lives in the habitat, travels as a method, and keeps its kernel across scale.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the institutional list from Ostrom (accountable monitors, graduated sanctions, cheap conflict resolution, nested enterprises), Axelrod's requirements, and Nowak's reputation mechanism.

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down unchanged:

- **¬ crosses levels.** The Operators table defines negation as unary on a claim. In the concretisation it becomes a community's refusal of conduct. The unary/binary distinction is dropped. This is why F2 is `specified`, not `general`.
- **The membership boundary.** Fig 11 :61 says a seat can introduce but not exclude. Ostrom's first design principle, clearly defined boundaries, includes excluding outsiders. That is nearer to subtraction (-) than to the seat's asymmetry. Re-census shows different objects, but the mapping is imperfect.
- **Sanction magnitude.** Fig 4 :20 governs invariance, not size. The world literature supplies a magnitude claim the figures never made.
- **Provenance of the institutional list.** The figures do not name these eight principles. They enter as an import with a source gate and shadow test. Treating them as a deduction would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.

What survives cleanly: a ground that does not move with agreement, a refusal operation, contact as the thing that changes reach, and four slots that must not be aggregated. That residue is worth sending.
