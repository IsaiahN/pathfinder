## Three findings in the wider literature that bear directly on the question

1. **Independent convergence.** The framework author reports that a reader described his multi-agent systems paper as a governance system, after which he encountered Ostrom's work on communities managing shared resources without central authority. He writes: "I had arrived at the same conclusions from bacteria and biology and game agents. She had arrived at them from committees and fisheries and forests." (Recursive Self-Improvement Trap, opened 2026-11-22). Fig 8 :50 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them." The record is the evidence here, and the author read Ostrom afterwards, so this is convergent derivation with a declared provenance, not two unconnected witnesses. Use it as corroboration, never as a second ground reading.
2. **The second-order dilemma of enforcement.** Axelrod's summary states that where punishment is costly there is a second-order dilemma between those who pay the cost of enforcement and those who do not, and that people initially prefer the sanction-free group but after several rounds join the sanctioning group. This is the refusal operation at a higher level and matches Christine Horne's "metanorms", norms regulating how to enforce norms.
3. **Magnitude is not invariance.** Third-party punishment exists in all examined populations but varies greatly, and larger societies show more altruistic punishment in some studies. Do not present "small face-to-face groups are sufficient" as a result. Fig 4 governs the kernel, not the size of the sanction.

## The two-stage offer

- **Stage 1 before stage 2:** the residual and its refuter go first. The recipient checks the shape without holding the framework's interior.
- **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction:** once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. Their stage-1 verdict must be recorded first.
- **Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted:** Fig 4 :22 allows a generator to cross. The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer.
- **Stage 3 still needs a ground per problem:** `THE_FORMULA.md` states that a frame cannot check the direction of its own question, and the architecture article warns that outputs "could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula." Each user must write down what would refute their result before running it, and label the mode.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 shape survives. If any of the five refuters in `01` fires, stop and correct the shape before sending anything.
