# Isomers and order: four mechanisms, one union, and the sequence they run in

**Mode: `specified` on the analogues, `general` on the ordering rule.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §8).

## RESIDUAL

Are detection, refusal, transmission and scale four names for one lever, or are they different mechanisms in a union that must be searched separately? And in what order do they run? A wrong answer here would let a single "norm strength" variable stand in for four distinct failure modes.

## DERIVATION

### Q1. Are the four slots isomers or variants?

- Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Detection, refusal and transmission can be written over the same held atoms: a Network for information travel (Fig 13 :24), a Spectrum for reputation ordering (Fig 13 :20), and ¬ for refusal (Operators table :58). They are not isomers in the strict sense, because their atom sets differ: detection can run without a ranking; refusal cannot run without a detected violation; transmission can run without an active sanction.
- The stronger isomer claim would require all four over the identical atom set and differing only in bond. That is not the case here. **The correct reading is a functional group** (Fig 12 :124): each slot is `actor across a relation to a residual`, and what distinguishes them is the qualifier, which actor and which relation.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if one of the four slots can be shown to require an atom none of the others require while still producing the same enforcement outcome, they are different kinds, not a functional group, and the union reading is confirmed more strongly. If one scalar closes all four with no residual on any slot (Fig 1 :19), the union reading is refuted.

### Q2. Why the union cannot be searched as one room

- Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- A bundled problem is "usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones" (Fig 9 :55). A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. That is why "build trust" can improve transmission while leaving detection and refusal untouched.
- The residue after unbundling is "smaller and it is also categorically different" (Fig 9 :57). Each of the four slots has its own ground: a change in detected violations, a change in defection after sanction, persistence across a generation, and compliance after scale change.

### Q3. The order is not the order of importance

- Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."
- Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
- Order by dependency: detect before refuse (a sanction on an unregistered violation is noise); name the violation before choosing a sanction (link 2, the least instrumented); settle the method before carrying it up; measure the round trip before importing a new mechanism.
- `THE_FORMULA.md` step 2 adds the prior step: refuse before mint. A community that merely accumulates rules without refusing any claim has a library that grows and a reach that does not (Fig 5 :69 "a library that grows is not a reach that grows"). This is the operational content of F2 in `01`.
- **Falsifier:** if a community is found that reliably sanctions violations no member can name, link 2 is not a dependency and the prescribed order is wrong.

### Q4. The analogues, and where they stop

| analogue | source | what it contributes | where it stops |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ostrom's long-enduring CPR institutions | Ostrom 1990, opened 2026-11-22 | accountable monitors, graduated sanctions, cheap conflict resolution, nested enterprises | field cases, not a figure deduction; no claim that the principles are necessary everywhere |
| Axelrod's iterated prisoner's dilemma | Axelrod 1984, opened 2026-11-22 | recognition of cheaters, history tracking, "shadow of the future", clusters of nice strategies | game model, not a field measurement; players are symmetric, real communities are not |
| Nowak's indirect reciprocity | Nowak 2006, reported 2026-11-22 | reputation as "a reliable history being projected from past partners to future partners" | mechanism list, not a ground reading of any community |
| The framework's society of agents | Nwukor 2026-10-22, opened 2026-11-22 | outcomes-not-popularity votes, decay, bad news in the same lane, rooms not hand-picked | explicitly "a design sketch, and nothing in it has been built"; not a test of the Tether |
| Third-party punishment experiments | Fehr and Fischbacher 2004; Henrich et al. 2006; Marlowe and Berbesque 2007 | refusal by an unaffected party, cross-cultural variation | magnitude varies and is not settled by the figures |

The analogues are corroboration from low-overlap fields, not a second witness. Where they and the figures disagree, the figures govern. The most obvious disagreement is magnitude across scale, resolved in F8 of `01` by re-census: the figure governs kernel invariance, the studies govern measured quantity.

### Q5. Does a settled mechanism become an atom for the next level?

- Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."
- A monitoring arrangement that has been settled against the ground can serve as an atom for a refusal arrangement, and the pair can serve as an atom for a transmission arrangement. This is recursion, not import, and it needs no new primitive.
- The type caveat is still open. Fig 12 :104 permits the demotion to atom; whether an arrangement counts as a quantity at the next level is a question for an instrument, not an argument. This mirrors the open type question in the exemplar about ⋛'s operands and is recorded as a strain, not hidden.

## CONCLUSION

The four slots are a **functional group**, not strict isomers and not variants of one lever. They are ordered by dependency, not importance, and the first dependency is a vocabulary in which a violation can be named and refused. The analogues from commons research, game theory and multi-agent design corroborate the shape at low overlap but are imports with provenance, not deductions. Ordering and recursion need no new primitive; the operand type at the next level and the magnitude of cross-scale punishment remain open.

This framing rescues what is defensible in the intuitive claim, "communities enforce norms through reputation and sanction", without the part that failed: it refuses the single-lever reading, refuses the claim that the institutional list is figure-derived, and keeps the four verdicts separate.
