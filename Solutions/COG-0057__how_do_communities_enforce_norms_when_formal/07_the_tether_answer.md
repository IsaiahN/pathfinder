# The Tether's answer: informal norm enforcement

**Mode: `specified` on the shape claims, `general` on the structural invariance claim. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §8), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A model that credits only formal authority predicts that defection pays once that authority is weak or absent. Communities often sustain compliance anyway. R = |Γ(b,a) − o'| is the missing enforcement function. Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Four slots stay separate: detection, refusal, transmission, scale.

## 2. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. compliance sustained with no detection function, no refusal function, and no ground that registers a violation;
2. monitors' readings moving with the group's agreement, with the anchor updating and compliance still holding;
3. a norm surviving a generation or scale change by exact replay of recorded sanctions, with no contact and no ground reading, and still changing behavior;
4. costly third-party punishment absent wherever formal authority is absent;
5. one scalar closing all four slots with no residual left on any.

None has been run in this simulated derivation. Until one is, nothing here is `grounded`.

## 3. The shape

1. **Enforcement is triangulation against a ground, not negotiation among peers.** Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Accountability of monitors to the monitored is the practical form this takes in Ostrom's field cases. Agreement alone is variation, not a verdict.

2. **A capacity to refuse is required, and it is not the same as accumulating rules.** Fig 12 :140 "A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate." Operators table :58 "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate." A sanction refuses a claim; a rule book only adds claims. Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down" governs what is handed on.

3. **The enforcing unit is the person and the habitat together.** Fig 11 :39 "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40 "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." The habitat is enumerated, not composed: monitors, punishers, victims, newcomers, the resource condition, and the relations among them (Fig 11 :45 "actors: what acts on it, and what it acts on").

4. **Detection is a network effect and reputation is an ordering.** Fig 13 :24 "that entities relate, and influence travels"; Fig 13 :20 "ordering, so more and less can be said." Third-party punishment requires information to travel and the source to be ranked by history.

5. **A declared norm is not a grounded norm.** Fig 10 :49 "Declaring a meaning does not ground it." Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." The weakest link is usually the vocabulary of what counts as a violation and what would count as repair.

6. **The kernel is invariant across scale; the magnitude of a sanction is not.** Fig 4 :20 "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." Ostrom's nested enterprises are the same kernel at more than one size. The empirical finding that larger societies can show more altruistic punishment is a world result, not a figure result, and is deliberately left open.

7. **The framework does not name the norm.** It returns a shape, not a name. Ostrom's design principles, Axelrod's requirements and the reputation mechanisms are provenanced imports with a shadow test, not deductions from the figures. The convergent derivation the framework author reports from multi-agent systems to Ostrom's commons work is corroboration with a recorded provenance, not two isolated witnesses.

## 5. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that formal authority is never needed. The question is about weak or absent authority, not about the superiority of informal rule.
- Not that every informal norm is good or efficient. Horne and Mollborn's relationalist case is recorded: norms need not serve the collective good.
- Not that Ostrom's eight principles are figure-derived law. They are an import with a source gate (Ostrom 1990) and a shadow test.
- Not that small groups are enough. The cross-cultural magnitude evidence is mixed.
- Not that the ¬ analogy is a direct derivation. The Operators table defines ¬ on a claim; applying it to conduct is a cross-level analogue (strain recorded in `01` §8).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement settles `left(R,φ)` here.

## 6. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating observation, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information (it cannot define consciousness in full)." Compliance is the reading; it does not name the source. The discriminating observations are:

1. measure compliance before and after altering the monitoring function, with monitors accountable to the monitored;
2. measure defection before and after a graduated sanction, with a third party who did not suffer the transgression;
3. measure persistence after a generational or scale change with contact intact versus a replayed recording with contact broken;
4. check the cross-cultural prediction directly, since the magnitude claim is currently world-only.

Each is a ground that can register a difference and does not move because a frame agrees.

## 8. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that formal authority is unnecessary, and it does not declare which norm a community should enforce. It says that informal enforcement works only as triangulation against a ground that does not move with agreement, not as negotiation among peers; that a community needs an operation that refuses a violation, not just a longer list of rules; that the enforcing unit is the person and the habitat together; that information about a transgression travels through a network and is ranked by reputation; and that what crosses between people and scales is a method, not a replay of one sanction. Detection, refusal, transmission and scale are four separate verdicts, never one score of "norm strength". Ostrom's design principles and Axelrod's results are provenanced imports that corroborate the shape, not deductions from it. What is offered is the shape any candidate answer must have, five refuters written in advance, and the observations that would close it. The ground settles the rest.
