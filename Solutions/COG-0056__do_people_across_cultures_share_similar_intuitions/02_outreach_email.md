**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three strains in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 6 are recorded as resolved and the refuter below is checked as written. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** Check one output before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about moral judgment across cultures. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "do cultures share fairness intuitions or use different standards per relationship" is a union, not a choice. The framework says R, the gap between a prediction and what happens, is indexed by slot and not averaged (Fig 1 :19, :22). When the slot is the relationship, two things can hold at once:
>
> 1. a shared **comparison structure**, a threshold between two quantities, which the figure calls a functional group that *"says what kind"* (Fig 12 :87, :123);
> 2. a relationship-indexed **qualifier**, which says *"which member"*, so the same act changes valence when the relationship changes (Fig 11 :49-51).
>
> The derivation marks the particular standards as unknown. It claims a shape, not a name. It also notes that for a shared meaning, agreement is the product rather than a corruption (Fig 2 :41), while for a verdict about the world, agreement from one evidence pool is worthless (Fig 2 :40). Those are different question types and I keep the verdicts separate.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any culture has the universal standard, that there are no shared intuitions, that a particular list of moral foundations or relational models is the answer, or that this is `grounded`. Those are bindings from the world frame. Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents and only the record separates them, so until provenance is shown they carry zero evidentiary weight.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a population with no comparison structure in fairness judgment, or identical standards in every relationship despite different relationship atoms;
> 2. a person whose judgment is invariant across a documented change of relationship whose ground measurably changed;
> 3. an averaged fairness score per person that prospectively predicts which named relationship will be judged unfair;
> 4. one lever that restores both conditions with no categorically different residue left over.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - How Intelligence Actually Works: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-intelligence-actually-works
> - The Library of Babel: Locating The Book of Books: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/the-library-of-babel-locating-the
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
