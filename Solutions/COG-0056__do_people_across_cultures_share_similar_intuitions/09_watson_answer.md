## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about COG-0056. Option A: people share one fairness intuition across cultures and relationships. Option B: people apply different standards to different relationships with no shared core. Option C: the two are conditions of a union, a shared comparison structure plus a relationship-indexed qualifier. What each leads to: A averages across the live slot and discards the signal; B keeps the signal but cannot explain the cross-field evidence for a shared comparison; C keeps both, states a shape rather than a name, and leaves the particular standards open to the ground.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain giving actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between what a person's moral library predicts and what actually happens in a fairness judgment, indexed by slot. Arity: person × action × relationship type × cultural frame, and fairness itself is a threshold comparison of two quantities. Symmetry: the comparison is ordered, not symmetric; agreement between people is symmetric, which is a different object. Scale: individual, relational habitat, population. Fig 1 :19 indexes R by slot; Fig 1 :22 says aggregation destroys the signal.
- **Domain 1, individual moral cognition.** Actors: a person, their Γ of intuitions and learned norms, prior weighting w. Currency: residual reduction in judging. Anchor: the person's own judgment, legitimate because the question is about people (Fig 2 :37). Admitted.
- **Domain 2, the relational habitat.** Actors: kin, friends, strangers, authorities, exchange partners; conditions and relations. Currency: what the relationship pays and demands. Anchor: what actually sustains or breaks the relationship. Admitted.
- **Domain 3, the cultural / population library.** Actors: many people sharing a library, institutions, transmitted norms. Currency: conventions and agreement about shared meaning. Anchor: measured judgment distributions across populations. Admitted, but agreement here is the product of a convention, not corroboration of a verdict.
- **Domain 4, the comparison itself.** Actors: two quantities (payoff against payoff, effort against reward, need against contribution). Currency: the threshold. Anchor: the distribution compared. Admitted.
- **X1, fairness as a free-floating universal property.** No anchor independent of the judgments it purports to explain. Not a domain yet; excluded before any figure line is applied.
- **Partition or union.** A union of C1 (shared comparison structure) and C2 (relationship-indexed qualifier). D1/D2, D2/D3 and D4 all overlap, so the overlaps are compounds with their own loops. Fig 9 :54 says unbundle a union before searching.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** 1 perception of quantities, 2 vocabulary for what the relationship affords, 3 objective (what counts as fair here), 4 planning, 5 learn and carry. Settle the comparison, then relationship standards, then cultural transmission. Culture-level comparison made first is a reading below the break.

An early symmetric-conjunction reading of fairness failed the Fig 12 :89 test and was re-censed as a comparison. An early blanket application of "agreement disqualifies a verdict" strained against Fig 2 :37 and :41 and was split by question type.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

People share a comparison structure and apply different standards to different relationships. These are two conditions of a union, not competing answers. The residual is indexed by relationship slot, so averaging across relationships discards the live signal. What is shared is a threshold comparison, a functional group that says what kind of judgment is made. What varies is the qualifier, which quantities are compared and which relationship model governs; the same act changes valence when the relationship changes. Capacity is person-and-habitat. Agreement is the product for a shared meaning and worthless as a verdict when drawn from one evidence pool. Which particular standards a culture holds is a `gap`: the framework returns a shape, and the instrument that would name the qualifier is not held here. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, four refuters and the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a recording or a list of standards instead of a generator, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The world-frame bindings (four relational models, six moral foundations) are removable without touching the derivation; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already withhold them as answers. A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet, and no primitive has to be withdrawn.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A per-relationship judgment record with an independently measured relationship ground would name the qualifier; a standard that is invariant across a documented relationship change whose ground measurably changed would break Fig 11 :50; an averaged score that predicts named relationships would break Fig 1 :19; a population with no comparison structure would break Fig 12 :87; a culture-level statistic that predicts relationship-level judgments without relationship measurement would break Fig 3 :27. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
