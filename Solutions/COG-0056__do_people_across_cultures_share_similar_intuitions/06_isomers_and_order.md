## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are "shared intuition" and "different standards" isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :92: **"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."**
- The two horns do **not** share an atom set under the figure's definition. "Shared intuition" claims one invariant across relationships; "different standards" claims a family of invariants, one per relationship. Their atom sets differ (one standard versus many), so they are **not isomers** under Fig 12 :117. Calling them isomers was an early error and is retracted in section 6 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` (S3).
- What they share is a **functional group**: Fig 12 :123, **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** Both horns contain a threshold comparison; they differ in the qualifier. The comparison says *what kind* (fairness judgment), the qualifier says *which member* (universal or relationship-indexed).

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a case is found in which one horn lacks the comparison substructure entirely, the functional-group claim fails and the two horns are unrelated claims, not members of one kind.

### Q2 - Can the two horns be composed at all?

- Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
- Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**
- Under `+` (Fig 12 :55-57, *"both hold at once"*, symmetric) the two conditions are a conjunction of a shared kind and a variable qualifier. That composition is legal and is the one the Tether answer uses.
- Under `⋛` (Fig 12 :87, *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*) the composition would be a comparison of two standards. That is a different claim: it would say fairness is decided where two standards compare, which the cross-cultural literature does not assert. It is refused as written.

**Falsifier:** if a documented case shows a single standard that is neither shared nor relationship-indexed but genuinely thresholded between two standards, the `+` composition is wrong and the `⋛` reading must be re-examined.

### Q3 - What is the order of the union?

- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**
- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"**
- Order, upstream first: (1) the comparison substructure settles (C1); (2) the relationship vocabulary settles inside each habitat (C2 qualifier); (3) each relationship-specific standard becomes an atom at the next level; (4) a culture's transmitted norms compose over those atoms; (5) cross-cultural comparison compares cultures, not raw acts.
- A culture-level comparison made before the relationship-level standards are settled is a reading below the break (Fig 3 :22-23): *"a reading of nothing."* This is the structural reason a cross-cultural average of fairness scores is the wrong object.

**Falsifier:** if a culture-level fairness statistic prospectively predicts relationship-level judgments without any relationship-level measurement, the dependency order is wrong.

### Q4 - Do the competing explanations form a partition?

Three explanations appear in the world sources:

| explanation | claim | figure reading |
|---|---|---|
| universalism | one standard, all relationships | keeps C1, refuses C2 |
| relationalism | standards are relation-specific, no universal | keeps C2, refuses C1 |
| the Tether shape | shared comparison structure plus relational qualifier | keeps both as a union |

- Fig 9 :54-57 governs: this is a union, not a partition of three mutually exclusive options. Universalism and relationalism each keep one condition and mistake it for the whole.
- Fig 11 :49-51, **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries"**, supports relationalism at D2 without supporting its refusal of C1. Fig 12 :123 supports universalism at the level of kind without supporting its refusal of C2. Each is a partial reading, and the overlap is a compound with its own loop.
- The apparent literature conflict (Brosnan and de Waal's *"probably a human universal"* with *"substantial cultural variation in its particulars"*) resolves here: universal = C1, variation = C2.

**Falsifier:** if a single explanation predicts both the invariance of the comparison structure and the full pattern of relationship variation with no residual left over, the union collapses into a partition and this derivation is wrong.

### Q5 - What is transferred from person to culture?

- Fig 4 :19-20: **"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."** The kernel (per-slot loop, naming what worked) is invariant across size.
- Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** What transfers from person to culture is a method for setting a standard in a relationship, not a recording of any particular standard. A recorded standard replayed in a new culture produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one (Fig 4 :25-26).
- Fig 4 :30: **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."** The abstraction from many people to "a culture's fairness norm" loses information; R_T is nonzero and must be recorded, not assumed zero.

**Falsifier:** if a standard can be transferred from one culture to another with no loss and no re-anchoring, Fig 4 :27-32 is wrong for this case.

## CONCLUSION

The two horns are **not isomers** (no shared atom set) but **members of one functional group** (a shared comparison substructure with different qualifiers). Their composition is `+`, a union of two conditions, not `⋛`. The dependency order runs from the settled comparison, through relationship-level vocabulary and standards, to culture-level transmission; any culture-level average made before that order is respected is a reading below the break. Universalism and relationalism are partial readings that each keep one condition. This rescues what is defensible in both without naming any particular standard or importing a new primitive. The transfer from person to culture carries a method and a nonzero round-trip gap, never a recording of a standard.
