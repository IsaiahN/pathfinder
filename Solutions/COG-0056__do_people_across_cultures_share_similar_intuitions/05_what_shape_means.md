## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical README states the limit: the framework reports *"what a missing thing would have to look like"*, a shape rather than a name, and then stops where instruments can no longer distinguish and records the lineage. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 says it *"can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands"* and *"cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For COG-0056:

- Shape: a threshold comparison (a functional group) with a relationship-indexed qualifier, held as two conditions of a union.
- Name: "the fairness rule of culture X is equality matching", or "the moral foundations are care, fairness, loyalty, authority, sanctity, liberty". Those are bindings, not derivations.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. *"With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."*
- Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* This is why the domain census preceded any figure line and why the four relational models were not used to pick the frames.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: *"the finish line will also be the starting place"*, and the paradox that by then you have *"composed the answer through the process of locating it."*

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* Here the ground is a recorded judgment per relationship plus the survival or breakdown of the relationship it bears on (Fig 2 :37).
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test. The relational-model and moral-foundations lists are exactly this kind of import: they came from the world frame. Until provenanced, they are candidate Γ, not answers.

*The Library of Babel* gives the same stop from the search side: *"If the final book in your exhaustive search doesn't answer the question, then there are only two possibilities: the nature of your question was unanswerable, or malformed."* For COG-0056 the second possibility was real: the question was a bundle masquerading as a choice (Fig 9 :55).

## 4. Why the "one list per culture" reading is the forbidden error

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."* A list of standards produced by the same frame that is then asked to confirm the list is self-validation.

The essay's prestige rule bites here as well: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* A famous proponent of a moral-foundations or relational-model list gives that list a budget to be tried; it does not settle whether the list is the human standard. This is why `03_public_contact_points.csv` contains no personal emails and no receptiveness rank.

## 5. Q: does the framework settle "shared or different"? ANSWERED, as a union

**What licenses the shared part?** Only the functional-group claim (Fig 12 :123): the comparison structure is present wherever fairness is judged. This is `general`, structural, and does not name which comparison.

**What licenses the different part?** Only the qualifier claim (Fig 12 :123, Fig 11 :49-51): the same act changes valence when the relationship changes. This is `specified` and awaits ground measurement.

**Does the framework name the particular standards?** No. Fig 9 :32 returns a kind of thing, never the answer. The four relational models and the six moral foundations are world-frame bindings recorded as such in `10_source_ledger.csv`. The correct form remains a shape with an open qualifier.

## 6. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** Quoted above. A campaign gathering prestigious names to agree with the shape is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask is a refutation from a frame drawing on different evidence.

**This audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post"* but *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* So `01` is a simulated derivation, checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of the cross-cultural claim and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on.

## 7. Correction to my own reading

I initially read "shape, not a name" as a blanket ban on naming any standard. That overstates it. The canonical phrase governs a **missing** primitive, one not held in Γ. The relational models and moral foundations are not missing primitives of the framework; they are candidate bindings from an outside frame onto the qualifier slot. What is forbidden is presenting a binding as a derivation, or claiming it moves the wall (Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom"*). Both claims are now recorded so neither is quietly lost.
