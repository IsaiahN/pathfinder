## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not the *content* of any culture's fairness rule but **how the residual is indexed**. In effect terms (Formula step 1, Symbols table `R` and `s`):

- **b** is a person's belief about what fairness requires, formed from private history and inherited priors (Symbols table `w`, the weighting).
- **a** is the moral action: allocate, punish, cooperate, excuse, reciprocate.
- **Γ(b,a)** is what the person's moral library predicts they will judge or do.
- **o′** is what actually happens: the person's own judgment, and the judgment of the others in that relationship.
- **s** is the slot. The question asks whether s is *the human fairness intuition* (one slot shared by all cultures) or *the relationship* (kin, friend, stranger, authority, exchange partner).

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** (Fig 1 :19)
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** (Fig 1 :22)

A frame that averages across relationships to produce one "fairness intuition" per culture has thrown away the only signal that says where the standard changes.

- Option A, "one shared intuition": leads to the prediction that the residual attaches to a universal slot, so the standard is invariant across relationships.
- Option B, "different standards per relationship": leads to the prediction that the residual is indexed by the relationship slot, so the same act changes valence when the relationship changes.
- What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a **shape** with two conditions, (C1) a shared comparison structure (the kind) and (C2) a relationship-indexed qualifier (which quantity and which threshold), plus an instrument that discriminates a failure of C1 from a failure of C2. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no loop check can see it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is a **union of C1 and C2**, not a choice between A and B, in `specified` mode, per domain, never averaged.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the cross-cultural question, and no claim that the framework names which particular standards a culture holds.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
