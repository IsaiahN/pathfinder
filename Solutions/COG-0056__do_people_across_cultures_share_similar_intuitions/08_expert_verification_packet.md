# Expert verification packet - the Tether on fairness intuitions across cultures

**What this is:** a claim in a form a moral psychologist or cross-cultural researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No cross-cultural measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "do people across cultures share similar intuitions about fairness, or do they apply different standards to different relationships" is a union, not a choice. The residual, the gap between a moral prediction and what actually happens, is indexed by relationship slot, so averaging across relationships destroys the live signal. What is shared is a threshold comparison, a functional group that says *what kind* of judgment is being made. What varies is the qualifier, which says *which member*: which quantities are compared and which relationship model governs, so the same act changes valence across relationships. The framework does not name the particular standards; it returns a shape, falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 9 and 12
- **Statement:** the residual is per relationship slot and must not be averaged; the two horns form a union of a shared functional group and a variable qualifier.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." and Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 12 :123 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- **Refuter:** an averaged fairness score per person that prospectively predicts which named relationship will be judged unfair in a new sample; or one lever that restores both conditions with no categorically different residue left over.

### C2 - world-facing; checkable by a moral psychologist against their own data
- **Statement:** the same act changes fairness valence when the relationship changes, and fairness is a threshold comparison whose standard depends on which quantities are compared.
- **What to check:** do your data show relationship-conditioned judgments for the same act (kin versus stranger, compatriot versus foreigner, authority versus equal)? Does swapping the compared axis from effort to need change the standard? The opened sources report such effects: relationship regulation theory judges *"similar actions differently, depending on the relational context in which the act occurs"* (Wikipedia, Relational models theory, 2026, summarizing Rai and Fiske 2011); the ultimatum-game summary reports *"Malaysian players were more generous toward compatriots than toward British players"* (Wikipedia, Ultimatum game, 2026, summarizing Chuah et al. 2007). Fig 12 :87 "a threshold relation between two quantities" and Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."
- **Refuter:** an act whose measured fairness rating is invariant across a documented relationship change whose ground measurably changed; or identical rejection rates when the compared axis changes from effort to need.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation
- **Statement:** capacity is person-and-habitat, and the dependency order runs from the comparison, through relationship vocabulary and standards, to cultural transmission. A culture-level average made first is a reading below the break.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39 "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"
- **Refuter:** a person whose fairness judgments are invariant across radically different relational habitats independently measured to have changed contact; or a culture-level statistic that predicts relationship-level judgments without any relationship-level measurement.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that there are no shared intuitions. A shared comparison structure is claimed.
- Not that all cultures use the same standard. The qualifier is left open.
- Not that the four relational models or six moral foundations are the answer. They are world-frame bindings, provenanced as such, with zero evidentiary weight until a source gate and shadow test are supplied.
- Not that agreement among agreeable readers corroborates the verdict. Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool", while Fig 2 :41 "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it". The two are different question types.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by a cross-cultural measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

A per-relationship judgment record plus an independently measured relationship ground, not a further argument. Concretely:

1. Does the standard change when the compared axis changes from effort to need? That names the qualifier at the ground.
2. Does the judgment track what actually sustains or breaks the relationship, measured independently of the judgment?
3. Does a per-person average across relationships predict anything named at the relationship level? If yes, C1 is refuted.
4. Is there a population with no comparison structure at all, or identical standards across all relationships despite different relationship atoms? If yes, the shape is refuted.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, :22, Fig 12 :87, :104, :123 and Fig 11 :39, :50. Seven short passages.
2. Check C2 against your own data or the closest study you know: is the relationship-conditioned effect real, and does the compared-quantity swap move the standard?
3. Name one instrument that would record a fairness judgment per relationship without aggregating, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
