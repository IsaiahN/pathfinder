## What the wider literature bears on this question

1. **The universal half has a real witness.** Brosnan and de Waal report inequity aversion in capuchin monkeys and call the sense of fairness *"probably a human universal"* with *"substantial cultural variation in its particulars"*. This is a low-overlap field (animal behaviour) relative to the moral-psychology pages. It corroborates C1, the shared comparison structure.
2. **The relational half has a real witness.** Relational models theory describes similar acts judged differently by relational context and reports the four models as culturally universal with culture-specific implementations. This corroborates C1 plus C2 together, not either horn alone. The ultimatum-game page adds a relationship-slot result, compatriots versus British partners.
3. **Cross-cultural variation is real but is not the largest variance.** The moral-foundations page reports that moralizing varies less by culture than by social class and age. That is a variance partition, hence a budget filter (Fig 9 :60), never a verdict.
4. **Agreement is not automatically evidence.** The four Wikipedia pages share an editor pool and many of the same citations. Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* They are one witness for the moral-psychology pool, not four.
5. **The framework essays share an author with the figures.** Their agreement with the figures is expected and carries little independent weight. They are used here for the canonical reading of "shape", the simulation rule, and the prestige/action rule, not as corroboration of the verdict.
