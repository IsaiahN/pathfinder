## 0. The residual in effect terms

The loop defines R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| per slot s (Formula step 1; Symbols table, `R`, `s`). Here:

- **b** is a person's belief about what fairness requires, formed from private history and inherited priors. Symbols table `w`: *"How far the belief leans on private history versus inherited prior."*
- **a** is the moral action: allocate, punish, cooperate, excuse, reciprocate.
- **Γ(b,a)** is what the person's library of intuitions, learned norms and relationship models predicts they will judge or do.
- **o′** is what actually happens: the person's own judgment and the judgment of the others in that relationship.
- **s** is the slot. **The whole question is a question about how s is indexed.** Is it the human fairness intuition, one slot shared across cultures, or is it the relationship, a different slot for kin, friend, stranger, authority and exchange partner?

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** (Fig 1 :19)
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** (Fig 1 :22)

The tempting answer to "do people share similar intuitions" is a single fairness score per culture, averaged over relationships. That average is exactly what Fig 1 :22 says destroys the signal. The live signal sits at the relationship slot.

**Anchor test.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Crucially, this question is **constitutively about people**, so Fig 2 :37 applies directly: *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."* This differs from the physics exemplar, where human judgment is only an instrument. Here human judgment is legitimate as the anchor for the question asked, though it remains only an instrument for questions this one is not (Fig 2 :38). Viable anchors: recorded fairness judgments per relationship type, and the survival or breakdown of the relationship that the judgment bears on.

**Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term** (Formula, "Direction") before the run: the answer would be two conditions whose conjunction keeps the judgment stable and discriminating, (C1) a shared comparison structure and (C2) a relationship-indexed qualifier, plus an instrument that discriminates a failure of C1 from a failure of C2. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no loop check can see it. It is now bound, so the refuter in section 5 can fire on it.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### 1.1 Arity, symmetry, scale of R

- **Arity.** R is a multi-place relation: person × action × relationship type × cultural frame, indexed by slot s and ordered by social learning. Fairness itself is a **comparison**, `⋛`, *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* (Fig 12 :87): what one party gets against what another gets, effort against reward, need against contribution. So at least two quantities are compared and the arity is not one.
- **Symmetry.** The comparison `⋛` is ordered, not symmetric: *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."* (Fig 12 :89). But agreement between two people *is* symmetric (A agrees with B iff B agrees with A). These are two different objects. An early draft treated them as one and strained; see S2 in section 6.
- **Scale.** Three scales are in view: the single person's step-by-step loop, the relational habitat (dyad, kin group, exchange network), and the population level of a culture with *"many agents, a shared library, and something that ranks what they produce"* (Fig 4 :5). The membrane between scales is *"Not a wall: a filter with a direction"* (Fig 4 :21), and *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* (Fig 4 :22). The transform is lossy, so R_T must be measured (Fig 4 :30).

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

A domain is admitted only if an anchor can be named. Where none can be named, it is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor, nameable? | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 Individual moral cognition** | a person, their Γ of intuitions and learned norms, prior weighting w | residual reduction in judging; speed and confidence of intuition | yes: the person's own judgment, legitimately, because the question is about people (Fig 2 :37) | admitted |
| **D2 The relational habitat** | kin, friends, strangers, authorities, exchange partners; conditions (proximity, dependency, status); relations (sharing, authority, reciprocity, exchange) | the standing of the relationship, what it pays and demands | yes: what actually sustains or breaks the relationship; relations are habitat elements (Fig 11 :45-47) | admitted |
| **D3 The cultural / population library** | many people sharing a library, institutions, transmitted norms | transmitted conventions; agreement about shared meaning | yes: measured judgment distributions across populations (Fig 2 :29) | admitted |
| **D4 The comparison of fairness itself** | two quantities: own payoff vs other's, effort vs reward, need vs contribution | the threshold between them | yes: the distribution that is compared; a goal is a comparison (Fig 12 :112) | admitted |
| **X1 Fairness as a free-floating universal property** | nobody in particular | none | **no** anchor independent of the judgments it purports to explain | not a domain yet |

X1 fails the anchor test and is excluded before any figure line is applied to it. This is a structural exclusion, not a claim that universal fairness does not exist. It is a name in search of a slot, and the framework returns a shape, not a name.

### 1.3 Partition or union

D1 and D2 overlap: a person's intuition is always exercised inside a relationship, so their overlap is a compound with its own loop. D2 and D3 overlap: relationships are transmitted and named by culture, so that overlap is a second compound. D4 cuts across all three, because the comparison is made at every scale. The whole question is a **union**, not a partition.

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** (Fig 9 :54)
> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** (Fig 9 :55)
> **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."** (Fig 9 :57)

The two conditions are C1, a shared comparison structure (the kind), and C2, a relationship-indexed qualifier (which member). A culture that fails only C1 and a culture that fails only C2 are not different degrees of one failure; they are different compounds and need different instruments. The essay *The Library of Babel* states the same stop condition: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"* The "or" in the question is therefore a false partition.

### 1.4 Dependency order (Fig 3)

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."** (Fig 3 :20)
> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** (Fig 3 :27)
> **"Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."** (Fig 3 :29)

Order for this question: 1 perception (can the person read the relevant quantities, contribution, need, outcome?) → 2 vocabulary (does their Γ have terms for what the relationship affords: kin, authority, equality, exchange?) → 3 objective (what counts as a fair outcome in *this* relationship?) → 4 planning → 5 learn and carry. The middle link, having terms for what the situation affords and saying what would count as progress, is the one with no instrument. Fig 12 :112-113 says a vocabulary holding only ingredients can say what a situation contains and cannot say what would count as getting somewhere from it, and that *"having terms for what a situation affords was taken for a dictionary and it is a grammar."* Cross-cultural work usually measures outcome distributions, which sit above the break, not the vocabulary that sets the standard.

## 3. Findings

Mode tag on each: `general` if structural, `specified` if a falsifiable prediction about moral judgment, `grounded` only if settled by a measurement. None is `grounded`.

### F1: The residual lives at the relationship slot, not at a culture average

**Mode: `general` with a `specified` consequence for method. Domains D1, D2, D3. Status: triangulated (Fig 1 and Fig 9, low overlap: per-slot residual vs union unbundling).**

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** (Fig 1 :19)
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** (Fig 1 :22)
> **"The ground enters as the only metric."** (Fig 1 :23)

A cross-cultural average of "fairness intuition" scores is a frame-internal quantity in the sense of Fig 1 :24-27: it is produced by the measuring frame and cannot also score it. The specified consequence: any reported answer to COG-0056 that is a single mean per culture has already discarded the signal that says which relationship the standard changes in. World corroboration, secondary only: the ultimatum-game literature reports that *"Malaysian players were more generous toward compatriots than toward British players"* (Wikipedia, Ultimatum game, 2026, summarizing Chuah et al. 2007). That is a relationship-slot effect, not a culture mean.

**Refuter:** a per-person "fairness intuition" score, averaged across relationships, that prospectively predicts which *named* relationship will be judged unfair in a new sample. If it does, F1's methodological consequence is false.

### F2: Fairness is a comparison, and the standard is set by which quantities are compared

**Mode: `general`. Domains D4, D2. Status: triangulated (Fig 12 and the Operators table, two entries with distinct roles).**

> **"a threshold relation between two quantities"** (Fig 12 :87)
> **"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."** (Fig 12 :89)
> **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."** (Fig 12 :112)
> **"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"** (Operators table :51)

Fairness is not one quantity held up against a universal rule. It is a threshold between two quantities, and the standard is the pair and the direction of the comparison. Change the pair (contribution versus need, effort versus reward, in-group versus out-group) and the same allocation changes category. An early draft tried to make fairness a symmetric conjunction of considerations (Fig 12 :55-57, `+`, *"both hold at once"*). That failed the test at Fig 12 :89, so it was re-censed as a comparison; the correction is retained in section 6.

**Refuter:** a population whose fairness judgments are invariant across a controlled swap of the compared quantities, for example identical rejection rates when the compared axis changes from effort to need. That would break the claim that the standard is the comparison.

### F3: The shared part is a functional group; the varying part is the qualifier

**Mode: `specified`. Domains D4, D2, D3. Status: triangulated (Fig 12 and Fig 4, low overlap: composition structures vs kernel invariance across size).**

> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** (Fig 12 :123)
> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** (Fig 12 :117)
> **"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."** (Fig 4 :20)

C1 is the functional group: wherever people judge fairness, a comparison structure is present and fixes the kind of judgment. C2 is the qualifier: which quantities enter the comparison and which relationship model governs them. Under Fig 12 :123 the two claims are compatible rather than opposed. This is the figure-grounded resolution of the question's "or". Under `+` (Fig 12 :55) they are a conjunction of different levels, not a competition.

**Refuter:** a population with no comparison structure in fairness judgment at all, or one with strictly identical standards in every relationship type despite demonstrably different relationship atoms. Either breaks the functional-group reading.

### F4: A standard is relative to the relationship, not a property of the act

**Mode: `specified`. Domain D2. Status: triangulated (Fig 11 and Fig 12, low overlap: habitat relations vs bond structures).**

> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** (Fig 11 :49)
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."** (Fig 11 :50)
> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."** (Fig 11 :51)

An act is not fair or unfair in itself. Its valence is a property of act-in-relationship. The world corroborates from a different pool: relationship regulation theory is described as the view that *"people will judge and react to similar actions differently, depending on the relational context in which the act occurs"* (Wikipedia, Relational models theory, 2026, summarizing Rai and Fiske 2011). The same page reports the four relational models as *"culturally universal (though with culture-specific implementations)"*, which is Fig 12 :123 in the field's own vocabulary. One witness, since all four Wikipedia pages and the essays share an editor and citation pool (Fig 2 :31).

**Refuter:** an act whose measured fairness rating is invariant across a documented change of relationship where the relationship's ground (what sustains or breaks it) measurably changed. If found, F4 is false.

### F5: The capacity to judge is person-and-habitat, not person alone

**Mode: `specified`. Domains D1, D2. Status: triangulated (Fig 11 and Fig 4, low overlap: habitat vs membrane).**

> **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."** (Fig 11 :39)
> **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."** (Fig 11 :40)

Locating the universal in a free-floating "human nature" is an agent-only move. The animal-behaviour source reaches the same structure from a low-overlap field: capuchin monkeys *"respond negatively to unequal reward distribution in exchanges with a human experimenter"* and *"These reactions support an early evolutionary origin of inequity aversion"* (Brosnan and de Waal, Nature, 2003). The mechanism predates culture and is exercised in an exchange relationship, which is Fig 11 :39 at a different stratum. The moral-foundations source adds that *"moralizing indeed varies among cultures, but less than by social class (e.g. education) and age"* (Wikipedia, Moral foundations theory, 2026, summarizing Haidt's field work). That is a variance partition, a filter for where to look (Fig 9 :60), not a witness for the verdict.

**Refuter:** a person whose fairness judgments are invariant across radically different relational habitats that are independently measured to have changed contact. That would refute F5 at the ground.

### F6: Agreement has valence; the two halves of the question are different question types

**Mode: `general`. Domains D1, D3. Status: figure-governed, corroborated by the seat map (same framework, so one witness).**

> **"Correlation has valence."** (Fig 2 :39)
> **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"** (Fig 2 :40)
> **"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"** (Fig 2 :41)
> **"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."** (Seat Map :163)

This is the most important correction in the audit. "Shared intuitions" can be a **convention**, a shared meaning, in which case agreement is the product and Fig 2 :40 does not apply. It cannot be converted into a **verdict** about the world by adding more agreeing people from the same pool. So D3 is admitted with agreement as evidence for the convention, while D1 and D2 require independent frames ranged against a ground. *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict"* (Fig 7 :35) governs D1/D2; Fig 2 :41 governs D3.

**Refuter:** a case in which agreement about a shared meaning is shown to be disqualifying for a verdict about the same object, with frames that demonstrably draw on different evidence. That would collapse the valence distinction.

### F7: The break is usually at vocabulary and objective, not perception or planning

**Mode: `specified`. Domain D1. Status: single-source (Fig 3, with Fig 12 :112-115 as framework corroboration, so one witness).**

> **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** (Fig 3 :19)
> **"Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."** (Fig 3 :29)

In moral terms, the usual failure is not that a person cannot perceive a contribution or cannot reason about a split. It is that their Γ has no term for what *this* relationship affords and cannot say what would count as a fair outcome there. Most measurement lands above the break because that is where the instruments already exist.

**Refuter:** a documented person whose perception, vocabulary and objective links all measure as working and whose fairness failure is located at planning. That falsifies "usually at the middle", though not the dependency ordering.

### F8: Which particular standards are held is a gap, not a silence

**Mode: `general`. Status: figure-governed.**

> **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"** (Fig 9 :32)
> **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"** (Fig 13 :134)
> **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** (Fig 8 :39)

The four relational models and the six moral foundations are **bindings from the world frame** onto the shape, not derivations from the figures. Calling either one "the answer" would be an adopted import with no source gate or shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52). Fig 13 :115: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."* The honest stop is a shape (a comparison with a qualifier indexed by relationship), with the particular standards recorded as imported bindings and left open.

**Refuter:** derive one of the four relational models from the figures alone, with no world source. If that succeeds, re-census: the shape was narrower than this audit claims.

### F9: The apparent conflict in the literature resolves at the census, not against the figures

**Mode: `general`. Status: figure-governed.**

The animal source says both *"Although there exists substantial cultural variation in its particulars, this 'sense of fairness' is probably a human universal"* and reports inequity aversion in a nonhuman primate (Brosnan and de Waal, Nature, 2003). Read as a single claim that would be a contradiction. Re-census resolves it: the universal is C1, the comparison structure; the variation is C2, the qualifier. Likewise the ultimatum-game source reports both *"Some studies have found significant differences between cultures in the offers most likely to be accepted"* and that *"proposers in gift-giving cultures were more likely to make high offers and responders were more likely to reject high offers despite anonymity"* (Wikipedia, Ultimatum game, 2026). Those are relationship-conditioned C2 effects inside a shared C1 comparison. No conflict with the figures remains; the verdict was never flipped by a world source.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the design-instrument rule: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* (Seat Map :287). The shape claim is refuted if any of the following is observed:

1. A population is found with no comparison structure in fairness judgment at all, or with identical standards across every relationship despite different relationship atoms (breaks F2 and F3, Fig 12 :87, :123).
2. A person's fairness judgment is invariant across a documented change of relationship whose ground measurably changed (breaks F4 and F5, Fig 11 :49-51, :39-40).
3. A per-person average fairness score computed across relationships prospectively predicts which named relationship will be judged unfair in a new sample (breaks F1, Fig 1 :19, :22).
4. A single lever restores both C1 and C2 in both failure types and leaves no categorically different residue (breaks the union, Fig 9 :57).
5. Agreement about a shared meaning in D3 is shown to be disqualifying for a verdict about the same object with independent frames (breaks F6, Fig 2 :41).
6. Fig 9 :54 (unbundle unions) or Fig 1 :19 (index by slot) fails as a metalaw, that is, search across slots demonstrably preserves the live signal.

No part of this solution may be upgraded to `grounded` until at least one of these has been run and has failed to fire, and until a ground measurement has paid for the shape.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the residual and the refuter, not with a theory of moral universals (Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"*).
2. Keep C1 and C2 separate in every report. A blended "fairness score" is a pooled reading (Fig 10 :26) and destroys the diagnostic.
3. Ask, at each handoff, which link the chain stops at and whether that was measured (Fig 3 :29). Do not fund above the break.
4. Treat lists of foundations or relational models as Γ, that is, as candidate libraries whose reach must be tested against the ground, never as the answer (Fig 1 :27).
5. Use the cross-cultural literature as a budget filter and as one corroborating witness, not as the verdict. The four Wikipedia pages, the two Substack essays and the framework files share evidence pools (Fig 2 :31, :40); Brosnan and de Waal is the one genuinely low-overlap field source.
6. Offer the formula as a method after a verdict is recorded (Fig 4 :22). Each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.
7. Stop at the shape. Do not name which standard a culture holds; the framework returns *"a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (Fig 9 :32).

## 7. Recursive derivation summary

1. **Is this one question?** No. Fig 9 :54, :55, :57 plus the *Library of Babel* bundle passage: it is a union of a shared comparison structure and a relational qualifier, each with its own instrument.
2. **What is the anchor?** Human judgment, legitimately, because the question is about people (Fig 2 :37), plus the survival of the relationship the judgment bears on (Fig 11 :45-47). Not a culture average.
3. **How is R indexed?** By relationship slot, not by culture mean (Fig 1 :19, :22).
4. **What is shared?** A functional group: a threshold comparison, the kind (Fig 12 :87, :123; Fig 4 :20).
5. **What varies?** The qualifier: which quantities are compared and which relationship model governs, so the same act changes valence (Fig 11 :49-51; Fig 12 :89).
6. **How does a standard get transmitted?** A chain of handoffs with fidelity and ground pressure as two factors (Fig 7 :37-39); a repeated room stops travelling (Fig 7 :36).
7. **Where is the break?** At vocabulary and objective, having terms for what the relationship affords (Fig 3 :27; Fig 12 :112-115).
8. **What can be said at the stop?** A shape and two conditions with falsifiers. Not which standards, not a `grounded` answer. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"* and Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

## 8. Updated verdict

The shape survives: **people share a comparison structure (a threshold between quantities, the functional group), and they apply different standards to different relationships (the qualifier); these are two conditions of a union, not two competing answers.** The residual is indexed by relationship slot, so a culture average destroys the live signal. A standard's valence is relative to the relationship, and the capacity to judge is person-and-habitat. The two conditions are kept separate, never averaged. Which particular standards a culture holds is a `gap`, not a silence: the framework returns a shape, and the instrument that would name the source is not held here. Mode `specified`. No world source was allowed to flip a verdict; the apparent universal-versus-variation conflict was returned to the census and resolved as C1 plus C2. Three strains were named and dissolved by re-census. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures; it cannot settle the cross-cultural question it reports on.
