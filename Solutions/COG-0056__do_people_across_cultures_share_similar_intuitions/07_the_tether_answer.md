# The Tether's answer on shared fairness intuitions versus relational standards

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question is not about the content of a fairness rule but about how R is indexed. b is a person's belief about what fairness requires; a is the moral action; Γ(b,a) is what their library predicts; o′ is what actually happens; s is the slot. The question asks whether s is "the human fairness intuition" (one slot shared across cultures) or "the relationship" (kin, friend, stranger, authority, exchange partner).

Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." and Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A single fairness score per culture, averaged over relationships, has discarded the only signal that says where the standard changes.

## 2. The shape

1. **It is a union, not a choice.** Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." The question's "or" is a false partition. Two conditions hold together: C1, a shared comparison structure, and C2, a relationship-indexed qualifier.

2. **The shared part is a functional group.** Fig 12 :87 "a threshold relation between two quantities" and Fig 12 :123 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." Wherever fairness is judged, a threshold comparison is present and fixes the kind of judgment. The framework does not name which quantities are compared.

3. **The different part is the qualifier.** Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." and Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." The same act changes valence when the relationship changes, so a standard is not a property of the act.

4. **Capacity is person-and-habitat.** Fig 11 :39 "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." A "universal intuition" located in the person alone is an agent-only improvement that may not change contact (Fig 11 :40).

5. **Agreement has valence.** Fig 2 :39 "Correlation has valence.", Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool" and Fig 2 :41 "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it". For a shared meaning, agreement is the product; for a verdict from one evidence pool, it is worthless. The two halves of the question are different question types.

6. **The break is at vocabulary and objective.** Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." The usual failure is not that a person cannot perceive a contribution or plan a split; it is that their library has no term for what this relationship affords and cannot say what would count as fair here.

7. **The order runs upward.** Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world" - settle the comparison, then the relationship standards, then the cultural transmission (Fig 4 :19-20, "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."). A culture-level average made before relationship-level standards are settled is a reading below the break.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that there are no shared intuitions. There is a shared comparison structure, the functional group.
- Not that all cultures use the same standard. The qualifier is relationship-indexed and is left open.
- Not that the four relational models or six moral foundations are the framework's answer. They are bindings from the world frame, recorded with provenance, carrying zero evidentiary weight until a source gate and shadow test are supplied.
- Not a derivation of one horn from the other, and no new primitive.
- Not `grounded`. No cross-cultural measurement settles `left(R,phi)` in this run.
- Not that agreement among agreeable readers corroborates the verdict (Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes.").

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

Human judgment is the legitimate anchor here (Fig 2 :37 "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."), but only when recorded per relationship and never averaged into a culture score. The instruments owed are:

- **Per-slot judgment record:** fairness judgments indexed by named relationship, with the compared quantities stated, rather than a mean per culture.
- **Relationship-ground test:** does the judgment track what actually sustains or breaks that relationship, measured independently of the judgment? This is the ground pressure of Fig 7 :37-39, the two factors being bracket fidelity and the ground's pressure on the room.
- **Comparison-swap test:** does the standard change when the compared axis changes from effort to need? This tests F2 at the ground.
- **Standing refuters:** a population with no comparison structure; an invariant judgment across a documented relationship change; an averaged score that predicts named relationships; one lever that restores both conditions with no residue.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" and Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." The instrument that would name which qualifier a culture uses is not held in this run. The answer stops there and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Indiscernibility is not identity.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not choose between universal fairness intuitions and relationship-specific standards, because it finds those to be two conditions of one union rather than competing answers. It says that the gap between a moral prediction and what happens is indexed by relationship slot, so a culture average throws away the signal; that what people share is a comparison structure, a functional group that says what kind of judgment is being made; and that what varies is the qualifier, which says which quantities are compared and which relationship model governs, so the same act changes valence across relationships. Capacity is person-and-habitat, and agreement is the product for a shared meaning but worthless as a verdict when it comes from one evidence pool. The framework refuses to name the particular standards; it returns a shape, written refuters and the instruments that would close it. The qualifier stays open until the ground settles it.
