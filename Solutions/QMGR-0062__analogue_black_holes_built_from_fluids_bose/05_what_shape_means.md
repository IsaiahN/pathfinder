## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :133: *"what it returns is a shape rather than a name."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

For this question the shape is: an effective horizon plus a quantum field with an appropriate dispersion yields a robust, approximately thermal mode-conversion signature, with a temperature tied to surface gravity. The name that is withheld is the gravitational microphysics that would make an astrophysical black hole do it, and the outcome of the unitarity question. `Hawking-like spectrum with κ-set temperature` is a shape. `quantum gravity is a Bose fluid` is a name, and is not licensed.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary already held that is closest to the thing, then look for new terms that overlap or correlate; *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."* The effect here is mode conversion at a horizon and a correlation spectrum, not "gravity" in the abstract.
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* (listed, not re-opened; use the exemplar's reading): when contact is not possible, send a plain description of what one would expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: the finish line is also the starting place, because by then *"you have composed the answer through the process of locating it."*

## 3. What licenses a name

Two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays."* For R_lab the bench has paid for parts of the name (a measured correlation spectrum in a BEC or water channel). For R_grav it has not.
2. **A provenanced import from a closure that differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :51), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*They're Scanning Books When They Should Be Studying Nature* sharpens this from the other side: *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space."* And: *"A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six."* Also: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."*

The six substrate terms are a visible guess. Naming one as the gravitational mechanism, on the strength of an analogue measurement, turns a visible choice into an invisible one.

## 4. Why the target claim commits the source error

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. A colorimeter reads green and *"cannot comment on the source"*: one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or an undiscovered scheme. The reading does not determine the source. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* And *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."* It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved it. *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."*

Here the reading is a thermal correlation spectrum. Several source stories can produce it: a horizon-universal mechanism, or medium-specific dispersion and state preparation. Isoard and Pavloff report a departure from thermality with no general thermodynamic principle protecting analogue systems. Naming the gravitational source from the analogue reading is the metamerism error. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument (Fig 3 :27).

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."*

## 5. Correction to my own reading of "shape, not a name"

I initially treated "shape, not a name" as forbidding the naming of any structure that mediates a composition. That is too broad. The README's phrase is *"what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Its subject is a primitive not held in `Γ`. In this question the analogue's effective horizon and its field modes are **already held** in the bench frame: they are measured and derived, not missing. Naming them is a binding from the physical frame, not the invention of a new primitive.

What remains genuinely missing is the gravitational source: the microphysics that would make a real horizon thermal, and the unitarity outcome. For that, "shape, not a name" applies exactly. Both readings are recorded here so neither is quietly lost. The correction is triangulated (README wording plus Fig 13 :133-135).

## 6. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."* A list of famous analogue-gravity groups gathered to agree with the scope claim is the named collapse. The legitimate ask is to give the claim a budget to be tried against a ground.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post. It will often be better than the generic outputs that you will usually get otherwise too... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* So `01` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is not a test of the claim and cannot settle anything it reports on.
