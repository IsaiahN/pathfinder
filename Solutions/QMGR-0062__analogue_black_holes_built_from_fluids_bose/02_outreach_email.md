# Outreach draft: check one output before deciding what analogue black holes establish

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the scope distinction in F1 to F3 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is intact in your own words. If "the bench result" and "the gravitational claim" are not separated in the first paragraph, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about what analogue Hawking experiments do and do not establish

> Hello,
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If this output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a question about analogue black holes is a union of two different questions. One is a bench question: does a prepared medium with an effective horizon produce the predicted mode conversion and thermal correlation? That question has a ground, the instrument, and published experiments have paid for parts of it. The other is a gravitational question: does the same mechanism operate in an astrophysical black hole, and does the result settle unitarity? Its anchor is named but is not reachable from the bench. Treating one as the answer to the other is the error I want checked.
>
> The figure-grounded reading is that a tabletop analogue is a substituted habitat. It tests the wiring, meaning the horizon plus field mechanism, not the capability of the gravitational system. Different media that reach the same shape can corroborate that shape at high independence. What crosses to the gravitational target is a shape with a provenance record, not a verdict, and the specific gravitational microphysics stays open.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that an analogue has measured the Hawking temperature of a real black hole; that thermality in a medium fixes thermality in gravity; that the information paradox is resolved in the lab; or that any of this is `grounded`. Convergence and adopted import look the same in their contents, so provenance is what separates them. Until that record is shown, the transfer carries no evidentiary weight for real black holes.
>
> **What would refute the output:**
> 1. a laboratory measurement that directly settles a gravitational quantity, such as an astronomical Hawking temperature;
> 2. a demonstration that the thermal signal depends on a microphysical feature with no gravitational counterpart, so the shared-structure claim is false;
> 3. a realisation with a genuine horizon that fails to produce the predicted mode conversion once the effective description is applied correctly;
> 4. evidence that the supposed independent witnesses share one evidence pool, leaving one witness for the shape.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - analogue gravity review, open access: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5255570
> - the original sonic-horizon proposal: https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevLett.46.1351
> - the philosophy-of-science dispute on what analogues can confirm: https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.03859 and https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7422876
> - canonical figures and formula (branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
