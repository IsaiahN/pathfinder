## 4. Round-trip gap R_T

Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

- **T_A, going up:** carry the solution's verdict to a one-sentence abstraction. Result: *an accessible analogue can test the shape of an inaccessible mechanism, but cannot be its ground.*
- **T_E, coming back:** put that abstraction into a specific lab situation. Result: *build another realisation and check its spectrum against the thermal band.*

**R_T = gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x)).** The abstraction cannot hold four things:

1. It collapses the union into one lever. Coming back down, it tells a group to build another realisation even when what is missing is a source-discriminating instrument (Fig 3 :27). Effort lands above the break.
2. It loses the anchor distinction. "Accessible analogue" does not say which ground is being ranged against, so it permits the group to measure its own success with a quantity it produces (Fig 1 :27).
3. It loses the independence condition. "Test the shape" permits ten groups quoting one review to count as ten witnesses (Fig 2 :31).
4. It loses the reversibility clause. External validation would upgrade the analogue for a stated quantity; the abstraction has no place for a gap that can be reversed rather than merely reported (Evans and Thebault, read this session).

This gap is not an error to eliminate; it is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold (Fig 4 :31). The one-paragraph version in `07` is a deliberately lossy artefact and is labelled as such.
