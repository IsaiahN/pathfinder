## Three things in the wider literature that bear directly on this question

1. **The field itself distinguishes analogy from identity.** The standard review says *"analogy is not identity"* and that only a sufficient set of important features is captured (Barcelo, Liberati, Visser). This is the provenance gate of Fig 6 :12-13 stated in the physicists' own vocabulary.
2. **The epistemology literature is in genuine conflict, and the conflict is about the gate, not about the data.** Crowther, Linnemann and Wuthrich argue confirmation begs the question; Evans and Thebault argue inductive triangulation can establish well-supported knowledge when the inferential modes are independent; Dardashti and colleagues prove conditional confirmation with saturation. All three agree the bench data are real. They disagree on whether the source-target inference has been externally validated. That is exactly the Fig 8 :51 provenance distinction. Per the WATSON rule, the conflict sends us back to the census, it does not flip the verdict; the figures condition the transfer on independence, provenance and a shadow test, which predicts the shape of the disagreement without taking a side in it.
3. **Thermality is not a guaranteed export.** Isoard and Pavloff find a medium-specific departure from thermality, and the standard review notes that an inappropriate preparation plus modified dispersion can eliminate analogue Hawking radiation. This is the F7 source-discriminator limit: a thermal reading cannot by itself name its source.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the scoped output and its refuter; (2) ask what the recipient would count as a failure, including a failure of the scope split; (3) record that verdict before showing any framework material; (4) only then offer the links, with the note that the figures are the authority; (5) offer the formula as a method for the recipient's own ground.
