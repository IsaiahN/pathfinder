## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

A society's institutional library Γ holds rules, norms, decision procedures, and the vocabulary for naming outcomes. For each domain-slot `s` (a fishery, a labour market, a public-health regime, a credit system), Γ issues a prediction `Γ(b,a)` for action `a` under belief `b`. The environment returns `o'`. The residual is

```
R_s = | Γ(b,a) - o' |        per slot s, never aggregated
```

This is the Symbols table's definition applied to the institutional case: *"It is the aim, not only the error signal, and it is always a slice: what cannot be perceived yet is residual and simply cannot be read."* The question asks what "institutional flexibility" does to this per-slot residual and to the channel that carries `o'` back to Γ.

The first discipline is to refuse the question's ordinary noun as a single quantity. "Flexibility" names at least three distinct operators and one seat property:

| candidate object | figure operator | price | what it can and cannot do |
|---|---|---|---|
| rebind a rule inside the accepted frame | BROKEN · rebinding (Formula step 2) | cheap | re-fits a binding; cannot add an atom; Fig 6 :21 |
| mint a new rule | MINT (Formula step 3) | MDL bargain, three guards | composes inside closure(Γ); adds no atom |
| import a rule from a differing closure | IMPORT (Formula step 6/7) | provenance + shadow test, debited against independence | the only operator that moves the wall; Fig 6 :23 |
| keep the reference unmovable and refusable | Ground Maintainer seat (Fig 10) | requires an anchor outside the slice graded | not an operator on Γ at all; a checking arrangement |

A "flexible" society in the first sense may have no more adaptive reach than a "rigid" one: it can rearrange forever inside a closed atom set (Fig 8 :32, Fig 13 :115-116). A society with a high formal rate of rule change but a reference that updates on its own reports has *less* correction, not more (Fig 2 :33-34). So the effect cannot be indexed by a single flexibility number. The residual, not the label, selects the operator (Fig 9 :53).

What an answer would look like, bound before the run (Formula "Direction"): a **separator** that says, for each operator, which side of it keeps a non-updating ground reachable and which side turns the reference into another frame, plus an instrument that would fire if the separator were wrong. That separator, not a verdict on "flexibility", is what this audit derives.

## 1. Domain census

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor. Partition or union. Order by dependency (Fig 3).

**R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Arity: at least four, the three operators plus the seat property, and they overlap, so this is a **union compound**, not a partition (Fig 9 :4-5: *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*). Symmetry: "flexibility helps" is stated symmetrically while "causes capture" is ordered in time and in influence, so a single pooled estimate cannot carry the arrow (Operators table: `+` is symmetric, `→` and `⇒` are not). Scale: first order inside one rule-domain, second order at the interface between rule-domains where a rule's valence for another residual is decided (Fig 12 :104).

**Domain 1, the adaptation frame (rule library).** Actors: rule-makers, firms, citizens, the rules themselves. Currency: residual per slot and settled terms. Anchor: the outcome the rule predicts, which must not update on what the rule-makers think (Fig 2 :29). Question type: can Γ close R_s with what it holds, or is an import owed?

**Domain 2, the uncertainty / channel frame.** Actors: metrics, monitors, reports, the reference in use. Currency: contact with the ground. Anchor: the ground, which does not decay while the channel does (Fig 10 :24). Question type: is the reading at slot s still a reading of the world, or has it become a reading of the frame's own last report?

**Domain 3, the capture / verification frame.** Actors: regulators, concentrated interests, the outside checker. Currency: verdicts and conventions. Anchor: the ground, never agreement (Fig 2 :45). Question type: did a convention decide an outcome, and does the seat deciding it hold an interest in the reading it cannot declare?

**Domain 4, the comparison / isomer frame.** Actors: institutional configurations over a fixed set of atoms (multiple decision centers, redundancy, information access, conflict resolution). Currency: arrangement rather than ingredients. Anchor: behaviour at the ground, which decides whether two arrangements that look alike in the rulebook are the same substance. Question type: are "flexible" and "rigid" different amounts of one thing, or different bonds over the same atoms?

**Partition or union.** Union. The four domains overlap: an import (D1) can sever a channel (D2) and install a convention that decides an outcome (D3) at once. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* One lever, for example "add more local discretion", clears the region where only D1 binds and leaves the D2 and D3 intersections standing. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** The chain runs: (1) perceive R_s per slot; (2) have terms for what a rule affords and what would count as progress in the society's own vocabulary; (3) state the objective; (4) choose a route under incomplete information; (5) name what worked, settle it, carry it. The chain is *"ordered by dependency, not by importance"* (Fig 3 :20). Most institutional-flexibility argument lands above the break, on step 4 (which reform to pass), while step 2 to 3, what would count as adaptation, has no instrument (Fig 3 :27). A reading taken below a broken step 2 to 3 link is not a weak reading; it is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22-23).

**Strain test applied before choosing figure lines.** The first pass mapped "flexibility" onto MINT alone and read every figure as a warning about changing rules too fast. That strained Fig 6 :21 (mint adds no atom) against the plain fact that institutional change sometimes introduces genuinely new capacities. Re-census: those cases are IMPORT, a different operator with a different price. No strain remains after assigning each line to its own operator. See the retraction log, section 6.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The question is a union of operators, not one dial (decisive at the census level)

Fig 9 :4-5 and :54-57:

> **"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."**
> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**

Fig 1 :19 and :22:

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

An index of "institutional flexibility" that averages across slots and operators throws away the only signal that says where the residual lives. World corroboration, low overlap with the framework: Dietz, Ostrom and Stern 2003 write that *"Highly aggregated information may ignore or average out local information that is important in identifying future problems and developing solutions."* The same shape appears in civics as in the agent loop. **Verdict `specified`, triangulated** (Fig 9 plus Fig 1, two figures with low conceptual overlap: union structure versus per-slot indexing; world source corroborates the aggregation failure). **Refuter:** a single scalar is shown to order every slot under every operator with no residual remainder, or a study shows the per-slot residual is constant across slots for the intervention in question.

### F2 - Where flexibility helps: only a change of contact changes capability

Fig 11 :39-40:

> **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**
> **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

A rule change that alters the rulebook but leaves the society's contact with the outcome unchanged has not improved adaptation. Behnke 2024 (opened this session) gives the world-side separator: flexibility means *"actions must be tailored to a situation"* and that decision-makers *"must have the possibility to deviate from routines and do what they deem necessary"*, but her own claim is conditional, *"Flexibility is a condition for adaptation"*, not a guarantee. Baldwin et al. 2024 list "Flexibility to match local circumstances" as a positive feature of polycentric governance while immediately listing coordination cost and manipulation as negatives. That is the union again, in a second field. **Verdict `specified`, triangulated** (Fig 11 :40 plus the local-circumstances evidence; low overlap between an agent-loop law and public-administration review evidence). **Refuter:** a rule change is shown to raise capability while every measured contact (who reads which outcome, at what resolution) is held fixed.

### F3 - Re-arrangement inside the closure adds no adaptive atom

Fig 6 :21 and :23:

> **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."**
> **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**

Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."*

A society can rewrite its rules indefinitely and still not reach a capacity its primitives cannot express. Genuine new adaptive capacity is an import, and imports carry a price: Fig 6 :24, *"Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."* Ashby's law of requisite variety, opened this session via the cybernetics entry, is a low-overlap corroboration with an identical qualification: *"only variety can destroy variety"* and *"Requisite variety is necessary, but not sufficient to control the outcomes."* More response modes are required to match more disturbance modes, but they do not by themselves settle control. **Verdict `specified`, triangulated** (Fig 6 plus Fig 8, two distinct figures; Ashby from cybernetics is a genuinely separate evidence pool). **Refuter:** a case where composition inside a fixed atom set demonstrably introduces a new adaptive capacity with no import and no instrument extension, and the record shows where the new atom came from.

### F4 - Where flexibility creates uncertainty: the reference starts updating on the frame's own reports

Fig 2 :33-34:

> **"The anchor must not update."**
> **"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."**

Fig 10 :24-27:

> **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**
> **"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"**
> **"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"**

Institutional flexibility is dangerous exactly where the thing being loosened is the reference rather than the rule. When a regulator can revise the metric that measures its own performance, or when a reporting chain pools readings across a regime change, the anchor becomes another frame and the drift is coherent. The outcome looks like adaptation because everyone's numbers improve together. Regulatory capture theory is a low-overlap corroboration: Stigler 1971, quoted in the source opened this session, *"as a rule, regulation is acquired by the industry and is designed and operated primarily for its benefit... every industry or occupation that has enough political power to utilize the state will seek to control entry."* The capture is not a rule being bent once; it is the reference being recruited. **Verdict `specified`, triangulated** (Fig 2 plus Fig 10; Stigler from economics is a separate closure). **Refuter:** a flexible regime is shown whose reference demonstrably does not update on any frame's report, with a named instrument that would detect an update and a recorded check that it did not fire.

### F5 - Capture is a convention that decided an outcome, plus an undeclared interest in the reading

Fig 10 :47-48 and :59-60:

> **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**
> **"a convention nothing can check is a constant the seat authored, carrying the seat's authority"**
> **"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"**
> **"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."**

Fig 2 :43: *"Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."*

Capture is not captured by the word "discretion". It is the transition of a shared meaning into a decision. A flexible rule that lets an official interpret "reasonable" is a convention until that interpretation settles who gets the licence; at that instant it is a verdict, and no instrument flags the transition. Baldwin et al. 2024 list the negative side in a second field: *"Powerful actors may easily manipulate the system for personal/private interest"* and *"Weakens democratic accountability: 'who's in charge?'"* Gatto 2022 adds that adaptive governance requires *"peaceful contestation and enforcement of a shared system of rules"* and *"the attribute of continuous competition and conflict resolution"*, that is, a check that can refuse, not merely a rule that can bend. **Verdict `specified`, triangulated** (Fig 10 plus Fig 2; the governance sources corroborate at low overlap). **Refuter:** a case where flexibility produced a decision that everyone agrees was a convention and no seat had an interest in the reading, with the refusal path named and exercised.

### F6 - Settling and variety are necessary, not sufficient

Fig 5 :42-43:

> **"The acceptance test is a bargain, not a threshold."**
> **"A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before."**

`THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* Fig 5 :48: *"Its own output is never a measure of success. A system can be enthusiastically curious and produce nothing."*

A flexible institution can be busy minting rules and still return nothing new: the third guard (novelty relative to atoms) may be at zero. Fig 5 :41: *"Any one of them at zero stops the mint entirely. Which is why a system that is minting nothing may have nothing wrong with its minting."* The mirror image is a system minting hard whose output is a description of one occasion. Behnke 2024's warning about the pandemic is the world-side instance: flexibility was acclaimed in the abstract while authority in many countries migrated to executives, which is the capture side of the same bundle. **Verdict `specified`, triangulated** (Fig 5 plus Formula step 5, different objects: MDL bargain versus ground settlement). **Refuter:** a rule change is shown to pay the MDL bargain and settle at the ground while the three guards are not all positive, with the record showing which guard was bypassed.

### F7 - More centres only buy a surplus at low overlap and against a described residual

Fig 8 :45 and :47-48:

> **"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."**
> **"Independence is one quantity read twice."**
> **"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."**

Fig 8 :53: *"Residual first, frame second."* Fig 7 :35-36: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* / *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."*

Polycentric arrangements are often recommended as the institutional form of flexibility. The figure gives the condition: the centres must differ at the evidence level and the residual must be described before they are assembled. Without that, they are one frame wearing many names, and their agreement is the pool talking. Baldwin et al. 2024 record the empirical version: polycentric governance was adaptive *"conditional on the presence of effective systems for coordination and information exchange"* and *"without such mechanisms, dispersed authority was not adaptive"*, and *"there is nothing inherent in polycentricity that prevents DMCs from coalescing around a common policy or approach."* **Verdict `specified`, triangulated** (Fig 8 plus Fig 7; the governance review corroborates the condition). **Refuter:** a set of decision centres is shown to produce a surplus while drawing on a single shared evidence pool and with no residual stated before assembly.

### F8 - The broken link is the objective, not the permission

Fig 3 :24-27:

> **"Why the middle link is where chains usually break."**
> **"Perception has obvious failures and obvious tests. Planning has obvious failures and obvious tests. The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."**
> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

Most flexibility debate is about permission: may a regulator deviate, may a local body set its own rule. That is above the break. The missing instrument is the objective in the society's own terms: what would count as progress at this slot, and who is positioned to refuse a report that says progress occurred. Fig 3 :29: *"Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."* This is why the answer is a separator and an instrument, not a design catalogue. **Verdict `specified`, single-source on the figure side (Fig 3), with the governance literature's 'no one-size-fits-all' result as weak world corroboration.** **Refuter:** an institution is shown with a working, measured objective link at the slot in question, and the flexibility debate still fails for lack of permission.

### F9 - Flexible and rigid are isomers over a fixed atom set, not opposite amounts

Fig 12 :117 and :94:

> **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
> **"The arrangement is the substance."**

Fig 12 :119 and :121 name two further structures that matter here: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."* (an independent monitor or a court that survives the case) and *"cycle. The output re-enters as input."* (a feedback rule; the sign of the loop decides whether it closes). Two governance forms with the same atoms (multiple centres, redundancy, information access, a refusal seat) but different bonds, that is, different assignments of who ranges against the ground and who may refuse, are isomers: same rulebook ingredients, different behaviour. Over a fixed atom set, "more flexible" is not "more of a substance"; it is a different arrangement. The atom set is not fixed in reality, so where it changes the relation is composition plus import, a different claim. **Verdict `specified`, single-source for the figure definition (Fig 12), with the fixed-atom condition named as a live strain.** See `06_isomers_and_order.md`. **Refuter:** two arrangements sharing every atom are shown to differ only by a scalar amount with the same bond assignment and the same behaviour, which would collapse the isomer claim.

### F10 - The numerical amount is a gap, reported as unreached, not as silence

Fig 9 :32 and Fig 13 :134:

> **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**
> **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself, and narrowing gets you close while the ground decides."**

Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

No figure line returns an optimal number of decision centres, a permitted rate of rule change, or a universal flexibility quota. The framework returns a shape. Gatto 2022's source record makes the same negative move in the world literature: *"One-size-fits-all policies are not effective."* The honest report is `gap` of the shape-not-a-name kind, not underivable in principle: a ground reading would close it where a measurement exists. **Verdict `general` on the gap, `specified` on the separator that replaces the number.** **Refuter:** a figure line is exhibited that governs the numerical amount directly, or a measured objective function is shown that selects the amount at a named slot against a non-updating anchor.

### F11 - Valence is residual-relative, so there is no standing list of good flexible institutions

Fig 11 :49-51:

> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**
> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

A decision centre, a veto, a sunset clause, or an independent agency is a benefactor for a residual that needs a new atom and a malfactor for a residual that needs a stable reference. The figure refuses the categorical list. What survives is the relation to the residual and the seat arrangement around it. **Verdict `specified`, triangulated** (Fig 11 plus Fig 10 seat rules). **Refuter:** one institutional form is shown to be beneficial for every residual at every slot with the same bond assignment, which would reinstate the standing list.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The separators in this folder are refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Union refuter.** A single scalar is shown to order every slot under every operator with no residual remainder, or the per-slot residual is shown to be constant across slots for the intervention in question (breaks F1, Fig 1 :19/:22, Fig 9 :54).
2. **Contact refuter.** A rule change raises measured capability while every contact with the outcome is held fixed and a checkable record shows no contact changed (breaks F2, Fig 11 :40).
3. **Closure refuter.** Composition inside a fixed atom set is shown to introduce a genuinely new adaptive capacity with no import and no instrument extension, with the new atom's provenance recorded (breaks F3, Fig 6 :21/:23).
4. **Anchor refuter.** A flexible regime is shown whose reference does not update on any frame's report, where a named instrument would have detected an update and a recorded check shows it did not fire (breaks F4, Fig 2 :33-34).
5. **Convention refuter.** Flexibility is shown to have produced a decision that remains a convention, with no seat holding an interest in the reading and the refusal path exercised (breaks F5, Fig 10 :47/:59).
6. **Isomer refuter.** Two arrangements sharing every atom are shown to differ only by a scalar amount with identical bonds and identical behaviour (breaks F9, Fig 12 :117).
7. **Gap refuter.** A figure line is exhibited that governs the numerical amount of flexibility directly, or a measurement is shown that selects the amount at a named slot against a non-updating anchor (breaks F10).

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the census is redone. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Split before searching. Treat the question as a union of at least four overlapping conditions and keep verdicts per domain (F1, F8). Do not publish a single flexibility score.
2. Lead with the separator and the refuter, not with a design catalogue. The first contact asks what would count as a failure at a named slot (Fig 9 :27-28).
3. Require a provenance field for every claimed new capacity: which atom was imported, from which differing closure, with what shadow test, debited against whose independence (F3, Fig 6 :24, Fig 8 :51).
4. Make the reference auditable: name the instrument that would show the anchor updating, and keep it outside the slice it grades (F4, F5, Fig 10 :42).
5. State the mode on every sentence. *"an unmeasured quantity is a specification of what to measure and is worth having, it is not a verdict"* (Seat Map :281). The numerical amount is a gap (F10).
6. Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Send the separator and the instrument as a method, not a list of institutional designs that worked once.

### Updated verdict

The separators survive: flexibility helps adaptation where a rule change alters contact with a non-updating ground, where the residual stays indexed per slot, and where any genuinely new capacity arrives as a provenanced import with a shadow test. It creates uncertainty where the reference updates on the frame's own reports or the channel saturates, and opportunities for capture where a convention decides an outcome or a seat's interest in the reading goes undeclared. The question's ordinary noun is refused as a single dial; it is a union of operators whose valence is residual-relative. No figure line governs a numerical amount, so that is recorded as a gap.
