## 2. What the wider literature bears on the question

Four things, all secondary to the figures:

1. **The help side is real but conditional.** Behnke 2024 says flexibility is *"a condition for adaptation"* and defines it as the ability of decision-makers to deviate from routines and tailor action to a situation. Baldwin et al. 2024 list flexibility to match local circumstances as a positive feature of polycentric governance. Both are the world-side counterpart of Fig 11 :40: contact has to change.
2. **The capture side is in the same literature, not in an opposing camp.** Baldwin et al. list weakened accountability and manipulation by powerful actors as negative features of the same arrangements. Stigler 1971 gives the economics version: regulation is acquired by the industry. These are the world-side counterparts of Fig 10 :47 and :59. Agreement from a shared governance evidence pool is one witness, not two.
3. **Aggregation is a named diagnostic problem, not just a framework preference.** Dietz, Ostrom and Stern 2003 warn that highly aggregated information averages out local information needed to identify future problems. That is the same shape as Fig 1 :22, from a different field.
4. **Variety is necessary and not sufficient.** Ashby's law, as recorded in the cybernetics entry, gives a mathematical form of the Fig 6 and Fig 8 result: more regulator variety is required to absorb more disturbance variety, but it does not by itself guarantee control. This is the genuinely low-overlap corroborator; the institutional sources partly share one evidence pool.

No world source conflicts with the figure verdict. The conflict rule was not triggered. If one did, it would send the work back to the domain census, not flip the verdict.

## 3. Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22 -** *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the whole architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the separator and its refuter; offer links after a verdict is recorded.
- **Fig 10 :42 -** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28 -** *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see at a named slot before giving them the article.
- **Fig 2 :35 -** *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* A list selected for people likely to agree is a mirror chain.
- **Fig 10 :59 -** *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* State that this derivation is simulated and that the author's stake is in the outcome, not in a particular reading.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the separator and its refuter; (2) ask what they would count as a failure at a named slot; (3) only then the three links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (4) record their stage-1 verdict before any theory is shown, and never pool later agreement as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).

## 4. The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1, opt-in first contact:** residual, separator, and pre-registered refuter. Ask for a failure, not a belief.
2. **Stage 2, only if curious:** the three source links. The verdict on stage 1 must already be recorded.
3. **Stage 3, on request:** the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user write down what would refute their result and state the mode of what they get. Fig 4 :22 permits sending a generator; `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction" explains why a frame cannot check the direction of its own question.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. This folder is a simulated derivation; it is offered for checking, not as a settled result. If it fails a refuter, withdraw it rather than defend it.

## 5. Venue caveat, stated plainly

Several target venues restrict AI-assisted or unreviewed simulated work. JPART's author page warns that AI-generated DOI links can lead to rejection with no resubmission, and that a submission cap took effect in July 2026 partly because of AI-generated content. AOM and the journals each publish their own policies. Check the current policy of the receiving venue before submitting; if the venue requires a human-grounded empirical result, send the packet as a question to a public discussion venue instead of as a paper. This is a practical constraint on distribution, not a figure finding.
