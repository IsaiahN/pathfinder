# Expert verification packet - the Tether on institutional flexibility

**What this is:** a claim in a form an institutional scholar can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the separators, `general` on the union shape, `gap` on the numerical amount. Not `grounded`.** No society's residual was measured. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "does institutional flexibility help societies adapt" bundles four operators with different prices: rebind a rule inside the accepted frame, mint a rule under three guards, import a rule from a differing closure, and the seat property that keeps the reference unmovable. The residual is measured per institutional slot and never averaged. A rule change helps adaptation only where it changes contact with an outcome that does not update on what the rule-makers report; re-arrangement inside a closed atom set adds no genuinely new capacity. Flexibility creates uncertainty where the reference updates on the frame's own reports, because then everything drifts together coherently, and opportunities for capture where a convention, for example what counts as "reasonable", becomes the thing that decides an outcome. Over a fixed atom set, flexible and rigid forms are isomers, same atoms under a different bond. No figure line returns a numerical amount, so that is a declared gap. The whole thing is a simulated derivation, offered for checking, not a ground reading.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal plus institutional-facing; checkable at a named slot

- **Statement:** flexibility helps only where it changes contact with a ground that does not update on the frame's own reports. A rule change that leaves contact unchanged changes nothing.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :40 - *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Fig 11 :39 - *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 2 :29 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
- **What to check:** at a slot you know well, did the reform alter who reads which outcome, at what resolution, or did it only alter the rulebook? Behnke 2024 calls flexibility *"a condition for adaptation"*, not a guarantee; does that match your case?
- **Refuter:** a rule change is shown to raise measured capability while every contact with the outcome is held fixed and a checkable record shows none changed. Or a flexible regime is shown whose reference does not update on any frame's report, with an instrument that would have detected an update and a recorded check that it did not fire (breaks Fig 11 :40 and Fig 2 :33-34).

### C2 - capture; checkable against regulatory and administrative cases

- **Statement:** flexibility creates opportunities for capture where a convention decides an outcome, and uncertainty where the reference updates on the frame's own reports. The interest in the outcome is unavoidable; the interest in the reading is what must be declared.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :47 - *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*; Fig 10 :59 - *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"*; Fig 2 :33-34 - *"The anchor must not update."* / *"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."*
- **What to check:** in a case you know, did the discretionary rule stay a shared meaning, or did it become the decision? Was the metric revised by the people it measured? Baldwin et al. 2024 list weakened accountability and manipulation by powerful actors as negative features of the same arrangements that deliver flexibility; does your case support that pairing?
- **Refuter:** flexibility is shown to have produced a decision that remains a convention, with no seat holding an interest in the reading and the refusal path exercised (breaks Fig 10 :47 and :59).

### C3 - structural; checkable by reading the figures and testing the atom set

- **Statement:** over a fixed atom set, flexible and rigid institutional forms are isomers, same atoms under a different bond, not different amounts of one substance. The variable is which bond may change.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 - *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :94 - *"The arrangement is the substance."* Fig 8 :32 - *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."*
- **What to check:** name two institutional arrangements with the same atoms. Do they differ by which relation may be re-bound, or only by a numerical threshold? If a genuinely new capacity appeared, can you name the frame it was imported from and the gap it explained (Fig 6 :24)?
- **Refuter:** two arrangements sharing every atom are shown to differ only by a scalar amount with identical bonds and identical behaviour at the ground, which would collapse the isomer claim. Or a case where composition inside a fixed atom set demonstrably introduces a new capacity with no import and no instrument extension, with the atom's provenance recorded.

**Gap, not a sub-claim:** no figure line returns an optimal number of decision centres, a permitted rate of rule change, or a universal institutional design. Fig 9 :32 - *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* This is reported as a gap, not hidden and not filled with a number. Refuter for the gap: exhibit the figure line that governs the amount, or a measurement that selects it at a named slot against a non-updating anchor.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a flexibility index, a design catalogue, or a universal institutional form.
- Not that polycentric governance is the answer. More centres buy a surplus only at low overlap and against a described residual (Fig 8 :45, :47-48, :53), and without coordination and information exchange dispersed authority is not adaptive (Baldwin et al. 2024).
- Not that the anchor is a regulator, a constitution, or a committee. A regulator is a frame; the anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks (Fig 2 :29).
- Not that the framework selects a named institution. A binding is provenance, not derivation.
- Not grounded. No measurement of a society's residual is asserted. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, and it cannot settle the question it reports on.
- No personal emails, no receptiveness ranking, no bulk send, and no pooling of later agreement as corroboration.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. Per-slot residual before and after, with the slot named in advance, never a pooled index. Dietz, Ostrom and Stern 2003: *"Highly aggregated information may ignore or average out local information that is important in identifying future problems and developing solutions."*
2. An anchor test: name an instrument that would show the reference moving when pushed, and run it.
3. A provenance test for every claimed new capacity: source frame, gap explained, independence debited.
4. A refusal test: a seat able to say the report is wrong, with a declared interest in the outcome and none in the reading.
