## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not whether "flexibility" is good or bad in general. It is which operators a society's rule-set can perform, and what each one does to the residual `R = |Γ(b,a) - o'|` measured per domain-slot, never averaged (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22). A society's institutional library Γ predicts an outcome for each slot; the environment returns `o'`; the gap is the residual. "Flexibility" is the rate at which Γ may rebind an existing rule, mint a new one, or import one from a differing closure. Those are three different operators with three different price structures, not one dial.

- **Option A, "flexibility is one quantity, more is better"**: leads to the prediction that any loosening of rules reduces the residual and that any rigidity increases it. It also predicts that uncertainty and capture are side effects to be traded off against the benefit.
- **Option B, "flexibility is a union of operators whose valence is residual-relative"**: leads to the prediction that some operators lower R per slot while others sever the channel to the ground or let a convention decide an outcome. The same operator is a benefactor for one residual and a malfactor for another (Fig 11 :49-50). Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged into one flexibility score.

What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a **separator** naming, for each operator, the condition under which it keeps a non-updating ground reachable and the condition under which it turns the reference into another frame. That separator is the sought-for shape. Until it is bound it predicts nothing and no check in the loop can see it. This folder binds it and attaches an instrument and a pre-registered refuter.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B in `specified` mode, kept per domain and never averaged. No figure line returns a numerical amount of flexibility; that is a declared `gap`, not silence.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the institutional question, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of institutional change.

Four reasons, three from the figures and one practical:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
