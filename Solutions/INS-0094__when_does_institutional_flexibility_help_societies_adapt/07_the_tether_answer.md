# The Tether's answer on institutional flexibility

**Mode: `specified` on the separators, `general` on the union shape, `gap` on the numerical amount. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 5), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A society's rules predict outcomes per slot; the environment returns what actually happened; `R = |prediction - outcome|` measured per slot, never averaged (Fig 1 :19, :22). The question asks whether flexibility lowers that residual or raises uncertainty and capture. It asks about one thing, but the residual selects at least four: rebind, mint, import, and the seat property that keeps the reference unmovable. That is a **union compound**, not one hard question with two opposite answers (Fig 9 :4-5, :54-57). Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.

## 2. The shape, per domain

**Domain 1, the rule library. Flexibility helps adaptation where it changes contact.**

- Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*
- A rule change that only rearranges the rulebook leaves the reach where it was. Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."* Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."*
- Genuinely new adaptive capacity is an import from a differing closure, with a source gate and a shadow test, debited against the independence of the checkers (Fig 6 :23-24, Fig 8 :51). Ashby's law gives the same necessary-but-not-sufficient shape from cybernetics: *"only variety can destroy variety"* is required, but *"Requisite variety is necessary, but not sufficient to control the outcomes."*

**Domain 2, the channel. Flexibility creates uncertainty where the reference updates on the frame's own reports.**

- Fig 2 :33-34: *"The anchor must not update."* / *"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."*
- Fig 10 :24-27: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*; *"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"*.
- A regulator is a frame, not a ground. Calling the reference an "independent regulator" and then allowing it to revise the metric that measures its own performance is panel 1 arrived at by a different route (Fig 2 :34). The social-world counterpart is the regulatory-capture result: Stigler 1971, *"regulation is acquired by the industry and is designed and operated primarily for its benefit."*

**Domain 3, the seat. Flexibility creates opportunities for capture where a convention decides an outcome or an interest in the reading goes undeclared.**

- Fig 10 :47-48: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*; *"a convention nothing can check is a constant the seat authored, carrying the seat's authority"*.
- Fig 10 :59-60: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"*; *"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."*
- Baldwin et al. 2024 list the negative side in the same breath as the positive: flexible matching and experimentation, but high coordination cost, weakened accountability, and manipulation by powerful actors. Behnke 2024 warns that flexibility was acclaimed while authority migrated to executives. The two literatures agree from one evidence pool, so that agreement is one witness.

**Domain 4, the arrangement. Flexible and rigid are not opposite amounts.**

- Over a fixed atom set they are Fig 12 :117 isomers: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance."* The difference is which bond may change, not how many rules change (Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."*). The fixed-atom condition is a live strain, because real institutional change also changes stakeholders and infrastructure (Gatto 2022).
- No standing list of good flexible institutions survives a change of residual: Fig 11 :51, *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* A veto is a benefactor where a new atom is needed and a malfactor where a stable reference is needed (Fig 11 :49-50).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a flexibility index, a permitted rate of rule change, or an optimal number of decision centres. No figure line governs a number (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134). That is a declared `gap`, reported as unreached, not silence (Fig 8 :39).
- Not "polycentric governance is the answer". More centres buy a surplus only at low overlap and against a described residual (Fig 8 :45, :47-48, :53). Without coordination and information exchange, dispersed authority is not adaptive (Baldwin et al. 2024).
- Not that the anchor is a constitution, a regulator, or a committee. The anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks (Fig 2 :29).
- Not that the framework selects a named institution. It returns a shape, and a particular binding is provenance, not derivation (README, "shape, not a name").
- Not grounded. No society's residual was measured; this is a simulated derivation.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. The same failure here: "adaptation improved" is a reading with several possible sources, contact genuinely improved, the metric was changed, reports were pooled across a change, or the reference updated on the frame's own report. The discriminators owed are:

1. **Per-slot residual before and after**, with the slot named in advance, not a pooled national index (Fig 1 :19, :22). Dietz, Ostrom and Stern 2003: *"Highly aggregated information may ignore or average out local information that is important in identifying future problems and developing solutions."*
2. **Anchor test**: name an instrument that would show the reference moving when pushed, and run it. No move means the anchor held; a move means the regime is panel 1 (Fig 2 :33-34).
3. **Provenance test**: for every claimed new capacity, name the differing closure it came from, the gap it explained, and whose independence was debited (Fig 6 :24, Fig 8 :51).
4. **Refusal test**: exhibit a seat that can say the report is wrong, with a declared interest in the outcome and no interest in the reading (Fig 10 :42, :59).
5. **Standing falsifiers** from `01` section 4: a single scalar ordering every slot; capability rising with contact fixed; a reference that cannot be shown to update; a convention that remains a convention after deciding an outcome; an isomer claim collapsing to a scalar.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable in principle.** The nation-scale index and the universal design are shapes we cannot fill from the figures, not proofs that no answer exists (Fig 8 :39). The six-term vocabulary and its institutional bindings are a visible choice made by a human author: *"A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six"* (*They're Scanning Books*). Declaring the choice makes it auditable, not neutral. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Indiscernibility is not identity.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not return a flexibility score and does not name the best institution. It says the question is a union, not a dial: rebind, mint, import, and the seat that keeps the reference unmovable are four different objects with different prices. A rule change helps adaptation only where it changes contact with an outcome that does not update on what the rule-makers report; a change that alters the rulebook and nothing else changes nothing. Genuinely new adaptive capacity has to arrive from a differing closure with a stated source and a stated gap it explains. Flexibility creates uncertainty where the reference starts updating on the frame's own reports, so everyone drifts together and nobody notices, and opportunities for capture where a shared meaning such as "reasonable" becomes the thing that decides who gets the licence: at that instant it has stopped being a convention and become a verdict. Over a fixed atom set, flexible and rigid forms are isomers, same atoms arranged differently, so the variable is which bond may change, not how many rules change. No figure line returns a numerical amount; that is a gap. What is offered is the shape any answer must have, the refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The ground settles it, and nothing here claims to have done so.
