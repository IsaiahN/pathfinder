# Outreach draft - check the output first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** check the current policy of the receiving venue on AI-assisted or simulated work before sending. Several of the journals in `04_links_and_distribution.md` warn about AI-generated references and unreviewed simulated submissions. A rejection for that reason is avoidable.

**Subject:** Check one separator before you decide whether the flexibility claim is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> There is one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "does institutional flexibility help societies adapt" asks about one thing, but the framework returns a union of at least four, and it keeps the verdicts separate. Per institutional slot, the residual is `R = | prediction - outcome |`, measured for each slot and never averaged. A "flexible" rule change can be one of three different operators: rebind a rule inside the accepted frame, mint a new rule under three guards, or import a rule from a frame whose closure differs. There is also a fourth object, not an operator at all: whether the reference the rule is checked against can be moved by the people being checked.
>
> The separator I want checked is this:
>
> 1. A rule change helps adaptation only where it changes contact with an outcome that does not update on what the rule-makers report. A change to the rulebook that leaves contact untouched changes nothing, however much it improves.
> 2. Re-arranging rules inside a fixed set of atoms adds no genuinely new capacity. New adaptive capacity has to arrive from a differing closure, with a stated source and a stated gap it explains, and that import is debited against the independence of the people checking it.
> 3. Flexibility creates uncertainty where the reference starts updating on the frame's own reports, because then everything drifts together and nobody notices. It creates opportunities for capture where a shared meaning, for example what counts as "reasonable", becomes the thing that decides who gets the licence. At that moment it has stopped being a convention and become a verdict.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that flexibility is always good or always bad, that there is an optimal number of decision centres or a permitted rate of rule change, or that any named institution is a standing good actor. The framework returns a shape, not a name, and no figure line returns a numerical amount. That amount is a gap, recorded as unreached rather than hidden. I am also not claiming any measurement was taken: this is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency, not a ground reading.
>
> **What would refute the separator:**
> 1. a single scalar is shown to order every slot under every operator with no residual left over;
> 2. a rule change raises measured capability while every contact with the outcome is held fixed and a checkable record shows none changed;
> 3. a flexible regime is shown whose reference does not update on any frame's report, with an instrument that would have detected an update and a recorded check that it did not fire;
> 4. an arrangement is shown where a shared meaning decided an outcome and no seat held an interest in the reading, with the refusal path exercised.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the separator is wrong and I will withdraw it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula for direct download (SVG, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical usage rule (figures README): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Worked architecture and the six terms: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and its author warns that outputs "could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula." A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
