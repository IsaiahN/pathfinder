## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"narrowing gets you close while the ground decides."*

From *The Library of Babel*, opened this session: *"You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can measure it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question, "a set of conditions under which a rule change keeps a non-updating ground reachable" is a shape. "Adopt polycentric governance with seven design principles" is a name, and a name for something the framework does not select. The separator in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 3 stays a shape: it says what any admissible answer must satisfy, not which institution a society should pass.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. *"With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."* For this question the starting vocabulary was "flexibility", "adaptation", "uncertainty", "capture". The figure census supplied better terms: rebind, mint, import, anchor, channel, convention, seat. Those terms narrowed the search from one dial to a union of four operators.
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."* So the separator is stated in effect terms: what happens to contact, to the residual per slot, to the reference, not in the vocabulary of any one institutional theory.
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*, opened this session: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* That is why the outreach packet asks what would count as a failure at a named slot instead of sending a theory.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: the answer is reached when *"the finish line will also be the starting place"*, and the paradox is that by then you have *"composed the answer through the process of locating it."* Here the starting term "flexibility" is replaced by a union of operators; the search stops when no further operator changes the residual description.
- The same essay warns that a bundle masquerading as one question has to be split: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually."* This is Fig 9 :54 in a different vocabulary.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays."* The README: the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."* For this question that means a measured per-slot residual before and after a specified rule change, with an instrument that would detect whether the reference updated.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :51), with a source gate and a shadow test. Any claim that a new adaptive capacity entered a society has to name the source frame and the gap it explained.

*They're Scanning Books*, opened this session: *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space."* And: *"A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six."* Also: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."* So a list of institutional design principles is a visible choice drawn from a human evidence pool, not nature's own list. Naming one as *the* flexible institution turns a visible choice into an invisible one.

## 4. Source discrimination and the naming error

*The Hard Problem is About Source*, opened this session, gives the mechanism. Metamerism: an instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source"*; the source might be one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* And *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."* It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved it.

Applied here: a society's reported "adaptation" after a flexibility reform is a reading, not a source. Several sources could produce it: contact with the outcome genuinely improved; the metric was changed; reports were pooled across a regime shift; the reference updated on the frame's own report. Naming "flexibility" as the source of an observed improvement from the reading alone is the metamerism error. What is owed is a discriminator, for example an independent reading of the same slot that cannot be moved by the reform's authors, not a new name.

## 5. Prestige is not action, and this derivation is a simulation

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, opened this session: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly."* And *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."*

A campaign that gathers well-known institutional economists to agree with the separator is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask gives the claim a budget to be tried and lets a ground settle it. Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

Same essay, on the standing of this work: *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."* And: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* This audit is therefore a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of the institutional question and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on.

## 6. Correction: "shape, not a name" governs missing primitives

I initially overstated the rule in the first pass of `01`. The README's phrase is *"what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Its subject is a primitive not held in Γ. Rebind, mint, import, anchor, channel, convention, and the seat property are all already available in the figure vocabulary. Refusing to name them would be a category error. What the rule actually forbids here is:

- asserting a numerical amount of flexibility that no figure line governs (F10);
- inventing a seventh operator to absorb the union;
- treating a named institutional form as if the framework selected it.

The correction is visible and not erased. The gap in F10 is about the amount and the selection, not about naming the operators.

## 7. Consequences for this question

1. The deliverable is a **separator**, not a flexibility index and not a design catalogue.
2. It is stated in effect terms so a person outside the framework can check it at a named slot (Fig 6 :30, Fig 9 :30-31).
3. It carries a pre-registered refuter, because the sought-for shape is a preference until it is bound as an ordinary term (Formula "Direction"; Fig 5 :62-63: a frame holds its preferred answer *"as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term"* so *"nothing here can see it"*).
4. It does not claim a ground. No society's residual was measured.
5. It refuses the standing list (Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*) The valence of a decision centre, a veto, or a sunset clause is relative to the residual at that slot.
