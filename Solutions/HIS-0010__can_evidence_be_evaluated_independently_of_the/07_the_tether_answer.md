# The Tether's answer: can evidence be evaluated independently of its institutions?

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question is a **union compound**, not one hard question (Fig 9 :54-58: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*). It bundles four conditions that have different answers: whether an anchor exists that does not move with community opinion; whether the producer can evaluate its own evidence; whether a separate evaluator can be arranged with low overlap; and whether standards of proof arbitrate or merely coordinate. Each has its own loop and its own ground. Verdicts are kept separate and never averaged.

## 2. The shape

1. **There is an anchor, and this is the only sense in which "yes" is correct.** Fig 2 :29-30: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does."* A verdict can be independent of a community when it ranges against something that does not move when that community changes its mind. For a historical question the anchor is a record, a count, or a dated document, not necessarily a physical instrument (Fig 2 :36).
2. **A community evaluating its own evidence is a mirror.** Fig 7 :13: *"agreement compounds, evidence does not."* Fig 2 :19-20: *"they never talk, and still agree."* The verdict is the producer's standards re-described, and naming the institution as the anchor only hides the drift (Fig 2 :47).
3. **Independence between evaluator and producer is a measurable degree.** Fig 2 :31-32: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* The existing instrument is the effective-sample-size correction for correlated observations (Fig 2 :44; Seat Map :119 gives `n_eff = k/(1+(k−1)ρ)`).
4. **It is raised by non-access, an unauthored pool, and provenance.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Seat Map :39: *"independence is a form of not-knowing, and it is the one quantity the arrangement cannot manufacture."* Seat Map :213 requires the candidate pool to be unauthored first and examined second. Fig 8 :50-52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."*
5. **Standards of proof are conventions until they decide outcomes.** Fig 2 :41: *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."* Fig 2 :43: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* This is the mechanism by which evidential standards change under institutional pressure.
6. **There is no view from nowhere.** Fig 7 :6: *"the limit: no last room."* A standpoint outside all frames is a terminal frame, forbidden at both ends (Fig 12 :104, Fig 4 :33). *They're Scanning Books*: *"there is no view from nowhere to compress toward."*
7. **There is also no categorical "never."** Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The strong constructivist conclusion reports a failed search as a barrier theorem. The real barrier is about self-evaluation, not about evaluation by a frame whose closure differs (Fig 8 :45: *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."*).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any named institution *is* the independent evaluator. The framework returns a shape and an instrument, not a name (Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*).
- Not that credentials, prestige, or institutional reputation improve independence. Those bear on the maker, not on the residual (Fig 9 :51).
- Not that "objective" is a property of evidence. It is a property of an arrangement of evidence, evaluator, producer and ground.
- Not that there is a neutral standpoint, a final arbiter, or a last room.
- Not that the verdict is `grounded`. No measurement of pool overlap or anchor stability was taken here.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it, *"not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."* Here the corresponding questions are:

- **Anchor test:** does the final arbiter's answer move when the producing community changes its mind? If yes, it is not an anchor (Fig 2 :34).
- **Overlap test:** what is ρ between the evaluator's and the producer's evidence pools, and what is the resulting `n_eff`? Does raising independence by adding readers of the same report change `n_eff` at all? It should not.
- **Non-access test:** can the verifier reconstruct the claim from its own library? If yes, it is inside the frame (Fig 10 :42).
- **Convention test:** does the standard of proof determine which claims are admitted? If yes, it has stopped being a convention (Fig 2 :43).
- **Standing falsifiers from `01`:** an anchor that moves with opinion and still settles; a reconstructing verifier shown to be independent; disjoint pools whose agreement carries no information; a standard that decides outcomes and remains pure coordination; a frame with no outside that settles a live dispute.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The four conditions can be stated and in principle measured; none was measured here. *They're Scanning Books*: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."* The shape makes the choice of evaluator visible and auditable; it does not make it neutral. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether dissolves the yes/no rather than choosing a side. Evidence can be evaluated independently of the institution that produced it, but only in a specific sense: there is an anchor that does not move when a community changes its mind, and an evaluator that ranges against that anchor with a different evidence pool can be independent of the producer to a measurable degree. A community judging its own evidence by its own standards is a mirror, not a check; piling up evaluators who read the same report adds vocabulary, not evidence. There is no view from nowhere, because that would be a final room, and the framework forbids a last room. There is also no categorical "never," because a failure to find an independent frame is not a proof none can exist. What is offered is the shape any independent evaluation must satisfy, the instrument that would measure it, falsifiers written in advance, and an honest stop until a ground settles the rest.
