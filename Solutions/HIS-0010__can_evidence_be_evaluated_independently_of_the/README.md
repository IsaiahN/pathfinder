## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not whether evidence is "objective" in the abstract but whether the verdict an evaluation returns would stay the same if the institution and community that produced and interpreted the evidence were replaced. In effect terms: an evaluating frame E predicts a verdict Γ(b_E, a_E) from the evidence it is shown; the ground returns o′; the residual R = |Γ(b_E, a_E) − o′| is measured per claim-slot and never averaged across claims (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22). The threat named by the question is that o′ is not the ground at all but the producing community's own report, so E ranges against a reference that moves when that community changes its mind.

- Option A, "a neutral evaluation is possible": leads to the prediction that there is a standpoint outside all communities from which evidence can be judged, a view from nowhere.
- Option B, "all evaluation is captured": leads to the prediction that a verdict is nothing but the producing institution's standards writ large, so independence is impossible even in principle.
- What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a **shape** stating the conditions under which an evaluation's verdict is robust to the producer, plus an instrument that would measure the degree of that robustness. Until it is bound, the sought-for shape is a preference, predicts nothing, and no check in the loop can see it (Seat Map :190).

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` dissolves the binary. Both A and B fail. Independence is a relation, not a standpoint or a virtue: it is achievable as a degree by a specific arrangement, and it fails exactly where the producer also anchors or verifies the evaluation. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with this answer.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the historical question, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of evidence.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
