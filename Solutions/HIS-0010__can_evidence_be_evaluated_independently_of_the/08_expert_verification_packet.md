# Expert verification packet - the Tether on independence of evidence

**What this is:** a claim in a form a social epistemologist or historian of science can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No measurement of overlap, anchor stability or verifier access is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Independence of evidence" is a union of four questions with different answers. There is an anchor that does not move when a community changes its mind, so a verdict can be independent of that community. A community evaluating its own evidence against its own standards is a mirror, not an independent check. Independence between an evaluator and a producer is a directed, measurable degree, set by how little evidence the two draw from the same pool, and buyable in proportion to the cost of being wrong. Standards of proof are conventions for coordinating meaning and become corrupting when they decide which claims count as true. Therefore neither "a view from nowhere" nor "all evaluation is captured" survives: independence is a relation with conditions and a degree, and there is no final standpoint and no last room. The framework returns the shape and an instrument, not the name of an institution.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 2

- **Statement:** an anchor exists that does not update on any frame's opinion, and a verdict ranged against it can be independent of the producer. A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29-30, *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :33, *"The anchor must not update."* Fig 2 :47, *"A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame."* Fig 10 :24, *"the ground does not decay, the channel does."*
- **Mode:** `general` for the existence of an anchor in any checking arrangement, `specified` for a given historical case.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a putative anchor whose answer moves with community opinion and which still settles an evaluation declared independent; or show a case where naming the institution as arbiter leaves the verdict genuinely independent.

### C2 - the self-evaluation claim; checkable by a historian or philosopher of science

- **Statement:** a producer evaluating its own evidence with its own standards is a mirror chain. Adding evaluators who share the same evidence pool adds vocabulary, not evidence.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 7 :13, *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*; Fig 7 :35, *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 2 :31-32, *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."* Fig 2 :44, *"The effective-sample-size correction for correlated observations is standard in survey statistics and meta-analysis; the collapse is the same arithmetic read as a warning."*
- **What to check:** in a case you know, did the additional reviewers draw on a different evidence pool, or did they read the same report? If the latter, did the agreement carry any information the single report did not? Is the effective sample size in that case closer to 1 than to the headcount?
- **Refuter:** a case where the producer's own verdict, reached with no separate anchor and no second frame, tracks the ground better than any available independent check across several changes of community opinion; or a demonstration that adding readers of the same report increases independence.

### C3 - the arrangement claim; checkable by reading Figure 10 and the Seat Map

- **Statement:** independence is raised by verifier non-access, an unauthored candidate pool and recorded provenance; it cannot be manufactured from inside the frame and is bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :42, *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Fig 2 :42, *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Seat Map :39, *"independence is a form of not-knowing, and it is the one quantity the arrangement cannot manufacture."* Seat Map :213, *"a candidate set chosen by a participant is an authored room however well the selection is reasoned, so the pool must be unauthored first and examined second."* Fig 8 :50-52, *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."*
- **Refuter:** a verifier that can reconstruct the claim and is still demonstrated to be independent of it; a standard of proof that decides outcomes and remains a pure convention; a frame with no outside that settles a live dispute and cannot be revised by any lower frame.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that any named journal, society or panel *is* the independent evaluator. The framework returns a shape and an instrument, not a name (Fig 9 :32).
- Not that the verdict is `grounded`. Nothing here was measured.
- Not that prestige, credentials or institutional reputation carry information about the truth of a verdict (Fig 9 :51; Seat Map :163).
- Not that evidence is "objective" in itself. Objectivity is a property of an arrangement of evidence, evaluator, producer and ground.
- Not that there is a view from nowhere, a final arbiter or a last room (Fig 7 :6).
- Not that no evaluation can ever be independent. That conclusion mistakes a search for a proof of absence (Fig 8 :39).

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminator, not another argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument whose reading *"cannot comment on the source"* was resolved *"not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."*

1. **Anchor test:** does the final arbiter's answer move when the community changes its mind?
2. **Overlap test:** what are ρ and the resulting `n_eff` between evaluator and producer? Does adding reviewers of the same report change either?
3. **Non-access test:** can the verifier reconstruct the claim from its own library?
4. **Convention test:** does the standard determine which claims are admitted?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29-33, :42, :47 and Fig 7 :13, :35 - six short passages.
2. Check C2 against one case you know: were the reviewers independent in the evidence-pool sense or only in the headcount sense?
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish "the producer moved the verdict" from "the ground moved the verdict" - or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
