## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the two readings isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Reading 1 (`E independent of P`) is a **comparison**: Fig 12 :85-87 defines ⋛ as **"a threshold relation between two quantities"**, with the test *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."* Here the quantity is pool overlap ρ, and the threshold is "low enough to buy at this cost" (Fig 2 :42).
- Reading 2 (`E independent of every frame`) is a **negation**: Operators table :53-55, **"this does not hold"**, with the test *"is the point that it is false, rather than absent? Yes."* It refuses the standing of any frame to compose over the verdict.
- Same atoms (anchor, frames, evidence, pool), different bond (⋛ versus ¬), different substance (a measurable degree versus a refusal). By Fig 12's definition they are **isomers**. Treating them as synonyms is the flat-formula error Fig 12 :5 names: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if one bond can express both readings without remainder (so that "low overlap" and "no frame at all" are the same predicate), the isomer claim fails and the two readings collapse into a functional group.

### Q2 - A second isomer: same evidence, different standard, different verdict

- Take a fixed body of evidence X and two communities with different evidentiary standards S1 and S2. Atoms: X, S, E, G. Bond: the rule by which X is ranged against G. Fig 12 :117 again applies: same X, different bond, different substance, namely a different verdict.
- This is the historical residue of section 1 of the source file: standards of evidence change, and the same records come to support different conclusions. The difference is carried entirely by the bond, so it is invisible in a list that records only the evidence.
- Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."** A record that lists only the evidence has *"recorded half of itself"* (Fig 12 :5).
- **Falsifier:** if the same evidence under every pair of standards yields the same verdict in every tested case, standards are not the bond and the isomer claim fails. This is checkable against the historical record, so it is the strongest of the three sub-claims.

### Q3 - Dependency order

- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**
- Order for this question: (1) establish an anchor G that does not update (Fig 2 :33); (2) describe R in effect terms before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); (3) refuse P as its own anchor (Formula step 2, refusal before mint); (4) arrange E with low overlap and non-access (Fig 2 :31-32, Fig 10 :42); (5) only then discuss standards of proof, which are conventions (Fig 2 :41-43).
- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** Adding more reviewers (step 4 work) cannot rescue the case where the anchor is the producer itself (step 1 broken). This is why the four domain verdicts in `01` are never averaged.
- The middle link of Fig 3 is the one with no instrument, and it is the one this question lives at: having terms for what an evaluation affords and being able to say what would count as progress in independence. Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** The effective-sample-size correction (Fig 2 :44) is the existing instrument to start from.

### Q4 - The view from nowhere is a forbidden seventh term

- Fig 13 :116-118: **"Six members, and no amount of arranging produces a seventh: a new member would have to be a new kind, and composition only ever rearranges the kinds already held."** And: *"So a frame can search without end and still not arrive: infinite effort inside a finite reach."*
- A frame with no outside would be a new kind of frame: one with no closure boundary. Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."** Fig 7 :6: **"the limit: no last room."**
- *They're Scanning Books When They Should Be Studying Nature* states the world-side version directly: *"there is no view from nowhere to compress toward."* Same conclusion, different evidence pool.
- Fig 13 :43-44 on the continuum as the removed seventh member: *"A continuum was the seventh and was removed for failing exactly this: it is a spectrum without gaps, or smoothness over space and time. A generating set must not contain what its own members produce."* The view from nowhere fails the same test: it is an evaluator with the frame boundary removed, and an evaluator is what a frame already is.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a checking arrangement that settles a live evidential dispute with no possible outside frame, and show it is not a terminal room. If that succeeds, no-last-room does not forbid the view from nowhere and Q4 is withdrawn.

## CONCLUSION

The two readings are **isomers**, not synonyms: one is a comparison on overlap, the other a negation of all framing. A second isomer appears at the historical level: the same evidence under different evidentiary standards is a different substance, with a falsifier that the historical record can fire. The dependency order runs anchor, residual description, refusal, evaluator arrangement, convention. The view from nowhere is not reachable by rearranging what is held; it is a seventh kind and the set is closed. This rescues what is defensible in the "yes" answer (an anchor exists) and the "no" answer (self-evaluation is a mirror) without either's overreach.
