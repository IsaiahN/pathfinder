## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the claim that evidence can be evaluated independently of the institutions and communities that produce and interpret it. Option A: a neutral standpoint outside all communities exists and can settle the evaluation. Option B: every verdict is captured by the producer, so independence is impossible in principle. Option C: independence is a directed relation with conditions and a measurable degree. What each leads to: A claims a final room the figures forbid; B reports a failed search as a proof of absence; C yields a shape, an instrument, falsifiers written in advance and an honest stop. The verdict recorded here is C, kept per domain and never averaged.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain giving actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between a verdict E returns and what the ground pays, per claim-slot, when the evidence and the standard both come from the producer. Arity: a relation among evidence X, producer P, evaluator E and anchor G. Symmetry: directed and asymmetric, since E ranges against G and G does not update on E or P. Scale: individual, community, institution, cross-community, historical record. Fig 9 names this a union compound: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either.
- **Domain 1, the ground.** Actors: instruments, measurements, counts, records, dated documents. Currency: contact and residual. Anchor: the ground itself, which does not update. Question type: is there anything outside the community that settles the verdict? Verdict: yes, an anchor exists, so independence of a community is possible in this sense.
- **Domain 2, the producing frame.** Actors: researchers, institutions, shared standards, incentives. Currency: credibility, agreement, conventions. Anchor: none of its own, because it is a frame. Question type: can P evaluate its own evidence independently? Verdict: no, it is a mirror chain.
- **Domain 3, the evaluating frame.** Actors: outside reviewers, other communities, verifiers. Currency: residual versus agreement. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Question type: can E be arranged so its verdict is robust to P? Verdict: yes, as a degree, by low overlap, verifier non-access, unauthored pool and provenance.
- **Domain 4, standards of proof.** Actors: peer review, methodological rules, evidentiary norms. Currency: shared meaning and coordination. Anchor: the ground only where the question is constitutively about it. Question type: do standards arbitrate or coordinate? Verdict: coordinate, until they decide an outcome.
- **Order by dependency.** Establish the anchor (D1), describe R in effect terms before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28), refuse P as its own anchor (Formula step 2), arrange E (D3), discuss conventions last (D4).

Strain test applied: Fig 2 :36 says an anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it. A physical measurement is not the right anchor for a claim about what a community accepted; a record or a count is. Re-census resolves this because :36 governs legitimacy and the anchor definition governs existence. Different quantities, so no strain after re-census.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The binary dissolves. Evidence can be evaluated independently of the institutions that produce it only in the following sense: there is a non-updating ground, and an evaluator ranging against it with a low-overlap evidence pool, a verifier kept outside the producer's interior and a recorded provenance can be independent of the producer to a measurable degree. A community judging its own evidence by its own standards is a mirror. There is no view from nowhere and no last room, and there is also no categorical impossibility; the strong "never" conclusion mistakes a search for a barrier. Standards of proof are conventions until they decide outcomes. This is a `specified`, ungrounded result, kept per domain.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode, refuters and lineage before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending them the framework instead of the residual, which Fig 2 :42 prices as buying independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong. That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The four domain verdicts can be revised independently without touching the derivation, and any institution named as an evaluator is recorded as a binding and can be withdrawn. A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet, and no primitive must be withdrawn.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An anchor test showing the arbiter moves with community opinion, an overlap test showing `n_eff` near 1 for many reviewers, a non-access test showing a reconstructing verifier is still independent, a convention test showing an outcome-deciding standard remains pure coordination, or a frame with no outside that settles a live dispute, would each break the shape. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
