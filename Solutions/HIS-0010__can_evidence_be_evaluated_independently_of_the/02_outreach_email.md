# Outreach draft - check the shape before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` sections 4 and 5 are stated cleanly. The offer collapses on contact if the stage-1 output is not itself checkable.

---

**Subject:** One claim about independence of evidence, with its refuter attached

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a question in social epistemology: can a piece of evidence be evaluated independently of the institution and community that produced it? I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with me. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the common yes/no framing is a union of four different questions, and the four have different answers. (1) There is an anchor that does not move when a community changes its mind, so a verdict can be independent of that community. (2) A community evaluating its own evidence against its own standards is a mirror, not an independent check. (3) Independence between an evaluator and a producer is a measurable degree, determined by how much evidence they draw from the same pool, and it can be bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong. (4) Standards of proof are conventions for coordinating meaning, and they become corrupting exactly when they decide which claims count as true.
>
> So the honest answer is neither "yes, a view from nowhere" nor "no, all evaluation is captured." Independence is a directed relation with conditions and a degree. A view from nowhere would be a final standpoint, and the framework I am using forbids a final standpoint. The strong "never" conclusion mistakes a failure to find an independent frame for a proof that none can exist.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any named institution (a journal, a society, a review panel) *is* the independent evaluator. The framework returns a shape and an instrument, not a name. Selecting an institution is a binding from the social frame and must be recorded as such. I am also not claiming that prestige, credentials, or institutional reputation carry information about whether an evaluation is independent; those bear on the maker, not on the residual.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. an anchor whose answer moves with community opinion that still settles an evaluation declared independent;
> 2. a verifier that can reconstruct the claim and is still demonstrated to be independent of it;
> 3. two evaluators with genuinely disjoint evidence pools whose agreement is shown to carry no information about the ground;
> 4. a standard of proof that decides which claims are accepted and is still shown to be a pure coordination device.
>
> I wrote these down before drafting the conclusion. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Social Epistemology (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epistemology-social/
> - The Social Dimensions of Scientific Knowledge (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Helen Longino): https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/scientific-knowledge-social/
> - The figures and formula for direct download (canonical local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

**Two-stage discipline.** Stage 1 carries only the four verdicts and the refuters. Record the recipient's verdict *before* showing any framework material. Stage 2 is opt-in. Once a reader has absorbed the framework, their agreement stops being evidence (Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*). Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
