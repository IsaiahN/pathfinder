## 0. The question under audit

> Can evidence be evaluated independently of the institutions and communities that produce and interpret it?
>
> Source: `history of science and knowledge.txt` line 13, section 1 (Evidence and standards of proof).

Stated as an effect, not as a word dispute: an evaluating frame E is shown a body of evidence X together with a claim C. E returns a verdict V = Γ_E(b_E, a_E) and the ground returns o′. The residual R = |Γ_E(b_E, a_E) − o′| is indexed per claim-slot, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22). The question asks whether V would be unchanged if the institution and community that produced and interpreted X were replaced by a different one. The residual "moves with the producer" is the thing to detect.

Three candidate readings are kept distinct; none is chosen until the census is done:

- **Independence as a standpoint (Option A):** there exists a frame with no outside, a view from nowhere, from which X can be judged without any community's standards.
- **Independence as impossibility (Option B):** V is nothing but the producer's standards; no evaluation can be independent, so the question is answered "no" categorically.
- **Independence as a relation (Option C):** V is independent to the degree that E ranges against a ground that does not move with community opinion and that E draws on a different evidence pool from the producer.

What an answer would look like is written down and bound before the run (Formula, "Direction"; Seat Map :190): a shape of the form `INDEPENDENCE = non-updating anchor AND low pool-overlap AND verifier non-access AND provenance`, with an instrument capable of measuring each term. This is the sought-for term. Until it is bound it accrues no residual and no internal check can see it.

## 1. Residual: arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity.** Independence is a relation among at least four things: the evidence X, the producing frame P, the evaluating frame E, and the anchor G against which the verdict is ranged. It is not a property of X alone. A one-place predicate "X is independent evidence" is already a category error under Fig 2 :31, which defines independence between two frames.
- **Symmetry.** The relation is directed and asymmetric. E ranges against G; G does not update on what E or P thinks (Fig 2 :33). Treating the anchor as a fourth party to a negotiation destroys the relation (Fig 2 :47). The frame-frame part is symmetric in the sense that overlap is mutual (Fig 2 :31), but the frame-ground part is not.
- **Scale.** Individual assessor, local community, institution, cross-community comparison, and the long historical record of standards changing. The same loop applies at each scale; only actors, currency and anchor change (Fig 4 :19-20, Fig 10 :68).

Partition or union: a **union compound**, not a partition. The case "an institution evaluates evidence its own community produced" sits in the overlap of the producing frame and the evaluating frame. It is a specific compound with its own loop, not a harder case of either (Fig 9 :54-58). The verdicts are therefore kept separate and never averaged.

## 2. Domain census

One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor. A domain with no nameable anchor is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 The ground / anchor | instruments, measurements, experiments, counts, patient outcomes, the world | contact, residual R | the ground itself, which does not update on any frame's opinion | is there anything that settles the verdict outside the community? |
| D2 The producing frame P | researchers, institutions, shared standards, instruments, incentives | credibility, agreement, conventions | **none of its own**, because P is a frame | can P's own evaluation of its own evidence be independent? |
| D3 The evaluating frame E | outside reviewers, other communities, verifiers, auditors | residual versus agreement | the ground, never agreement | can E be arranged so its verdict is robust to P? |
| D4 Standards of proof as conventions | peer review, methodological rules, evidentiary norms | shared meaning, coordination | the ground, but only where the question is constitutively about it | do standards arbitrate evidence or only coordinate its use? |

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Establish whether an anchor exists (D1) before asking about frames; refuse P as its own anchor (D2) before arranging E (D3); treat conventions last (D4), because a convention that decides an outcome has already stopped being a convention (Fig 2 :43). Refusal precedes minting (Formula step 2). Describe the residual in effect terms before selecting any frame (Fig 9 :27-28).

**Strain test applied during the census.** Fig 2 :36 says *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* A physical measurement is not always the right anchor for a historical question about standards of proof. Re-census resolves this: the anchor for a claim about the world is a reading of the world; the anchor for a claim about what a community accepted is a record, a count, or a dated document. Both are things that do not move when a frame changes its mind. The line governs the *legitimacy* of the anchor, not its existence, so it does not strain D1. Named here rather than smoothed over.

## 4. Findings

### F1 - The anchor exists, and this is what makes independence possible (D1)

Fig 2 :29-30:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**

Fig 2 :33: **"The anchor must not update."**

Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**. Fig 1 :23: **"The ground enters as the only metric."**

The first half of the question therefore has a "yes" answer, but only about the ground, not about any community: there is something that settles a claim that is not the community's opinion. A measurement, a count, a patient outcome, a dated record. If the evaluating frame E ranges its verdict against that thing, the verdict can be independent of P by construction, because moving P's standards does not move it.

**Mode:** `general` for the existence of an anchor in any checking arrangement; `specified` for the claim that a given historical case has one. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 2, Fig 10 and Fig 1 (low overlap: the anchor definition, the channel/seat distinction, and the metric rule are three separate statements).

**Refuter:** exhibit a putative anchor whose answer changes when a community changes its mind, which is still used as the final arbiter of an evaluation declared independent. By Fig 2 :34 that is not an anchor but panel 1 drift: *"the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."*

### F2 - A community evaluating its own evidence is a mirror, not an independent check (D2)

Fig 7 :13: **"agreement compounds, evidence does not"**. Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**

Fig 2 :18-21 describes the collapse exactly: *"one evidence pool / they never talk, and still agree / so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other."* Fig 2 :19-20: *"they never talk, and still agree."*

When P produces the evidence, interprets it, and supplies the standard of proof, there is no second frame. The evaluating verdict is P's own report re-described. The historical analogue of this is the science wars material in the world sources: the Strong Programme's symmetry thesis and Longino's reply are both about what a community's own standards can and cannot settle.

Fig 2 :47 closes the escape route: **"A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame."** Naming the institution as the anchor does not make the evaluation independent; it makes the drift harder to see, because everyone can point at the institution they were supposedly grounded on.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 7 and Fig 2 (mirror chain and shared-pool collapse are different arguments).

**Refuter:** a case where the producing community's own verdict, reached with no separate anchor and no second frame, is shown to track the ground better than any available independent check, across several changes of community opinion. That would break the claim that self-evaluation is structurally a mirror.

### F3 - Independence is a degree bought by arrangement, not a virtue of a person (D3)

Fig 2 :31-32:

> **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."**

Fig 2 :42: **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."**

Fig 10 :42: **"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."** Seat Map :39: **"The general law: independence is a form of not-knowing, and it is the one quantity the arrangement cannot manufacture."**

So independence is measurable and purchasable, but never complete. It is raised by: lower evidence-pool overlap between E and P; a verifier kept outside P's interior; an unauthored candidate pool (Seat Map :213); and provenance that separates convergent derivation from adopted import. It is not raised by adding more evaluators who read the same report. Fig 2 :44 points at the existing instrument: *"The effective-sample-size correction for correlated observations is standard in survey statistics and meta-analysis; the collapse is the same arithmetic read as a warning."* Seat Map :119 gives the count: `n_eff = k/(1+(k−1)ρ)`.

Fig 11 :39: **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"**, and Fig 11 :40: **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."** An independent evaluator is therefore a property of the evaluator-and-habitat, not of the evaluator's character or credentials.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 2 (overlap as a quantity), Fig 10 (verifier non-access) and Fig 11 (contact), plus the Seat Map as framework primary.

**Refuter:** a verifier that can reconstruct the claim, draws on the same evidence pool as the maker, and is still demonstrated to be independent; or a demonstration that adding readers of the same report increases independence rather than only vocabulary.

### F4 - Standards of proof are conventions, and a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one (D4)

Fig 2 :41: **"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"**.

Fig 2 :43: **"Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."**

Fig 2 :36: **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."**

A standard of proof is a shared meaning. It coordinates what counts as an evidential relation inside a community and is legitimate for that. It becomes a verdict, and therefore corrupt as a convention, when it decides which claims are admitted as true rather than coordinating discussion of them. This is the mechanism behind the historical finding that standards change under institutional pressure: the convention has crossed the seam.

Seat Map :163 states the same rule for the checking arrangement: *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* Agreement about a shared meaning is the product; agreement about the world from the same pool is worth nothing.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 2 and Seat Map.

**Refuter:** a standard of proof that determines which claims count as true, shown to remain a pure coordination device with no verdict load; or a case where a shared meaning that settles no outcome still corrupts a verdict about the world.

### F5 - Option A fails: a view from nowhere is a last room

Fig 7 :6 names **"the limit: no last room"**; Fig 7 :31-32: *"no finite number of them ever arrives"* and *"one long room: motion, no progress."* Fig 13 :118: **"infinite effort inside a finite reach."**

A standpoint outside all communities from which every dispute can be settled is a terminal frame. The figures forbid both ends of such a frame: no bottom (Fig 12 :104) and no top (Fig 4 :33). Fig 8 :34-37 gives the proofs that a frame cannot reach its own metaframe or certify its own limit.

So "yes, evidence can be evaluated from nowhere" is not a modest claim; it is a claim for a final room. It fails on the framework's own structural rule, not on historical evidence.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 7, Fig 13 and Fig 8 (three separate arguments: no last room, no last route, metaframe underivable).

**Refuter:** exhibit a frame with no outside that settles a live dispute about evidence in a way no lower frame can revise, and show the framework's no-last-room rule does not apply to it.

### F6 - Option B fails: underivable is not the same as unreachable

Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

Fig 8 :40-43 separates *"underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget"* and issues different instructions: for underivable, go outside; for unreachable at budget, the remedy is more search, not another atom.

The strong constructivist conclusion (no evaluation can ever be independent) is the "no search found one, so none exists" move. It is a search result reported as a barrier theorem. The figures do establish a real barrier: a frame cannot range itself against itself (F2), and no frame escapes its closure (Fig 6 :23). But that barrier is about *self*-evaluation, not about evaluation by a frame whose closure differs. Fig 8 :45: **"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."**

So Option B is refused: the honest verdict is "independence under the stated arrangement, degree unspecified until measured," not "impossible."

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 8 and Fig 6.

**Refuter:** derive from the figures a categorical ban on E ranging against a ground when E and P have disjoint evidence pools, or show that the underivable/unreachable distinction does not apply to checking arrangements.

### F7 - Evidence about the maker is inadmissible at verification

Fig 9 :51: **"evidence bearing on the residual is admissible; evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel, and the verifier is denied it"**.

Fig 10 :59: **"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."**

Fig 10 :65: **"Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right"**.

For this question, the standing, prestige, institutional affiliation and reputation of the evaluating community are precisely the stake-inducing channel. They bear on the maker, not on the residual. They must be excluded from the verdict and declared where they cannot be removed. This is why the outreach artifacts in this folder carry no receptiveness ranking and no personal emails.

The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap makes the practical version of the same point: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."*

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 9, Fig 10 and the essay.

**Refuter:** show a case where the reputation of the evaluating institution carries information about the truth of the verdict that no reading of the ground carries, and where excluding it makes the evaluation worse.

### F8 - The question is a union compound and must be unbundled before it is searched

Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**

Fig 9 :55-58: a bundled problem is *"a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones"* and *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*

The compound here is "the producer is also the evaluator and also supplies the standard." A single lever that fixes only one condition (for example, independence of personnel) leaves the shared-evidence condition binding. The four domains in section 2 are therefore not four averaged scores; they are four loops with separate verdicts.

**Mode:** `general` for the structural claim, `specified` for its application here. **Status:** single-source for the figure definition (Fig 9), corroborated by the census itself.

**Refuter:** show that the four conditions collapse to one under a single measure, so that improving any one of them improves independence by the same amount in every case.

### F9 - What survives: the defensible residue

| claim | mode | figure |
|---|---|---|
| There is an anchor that does not move with community opinion | `general` | Fig 2 :29-30, Fig 10 :24 |
| A producing community's own evaluation of its own evidence is a mirror | `specified`, falsifiable | Fig 7 :13, Fig 2 :19-21 |
| Independence is a directed relation measured by evidence-pool overlap | `specified` | Fig 2 :31-32, :42 |

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Required before any outreach, per Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. An anchor is found whose answer moves with community opinion and which still settles an evaluation declared independent (breaks F1).
2. Two evaluators with demonstrably zero overlapping evidence are shown to agree in a way that carries no information about the ground, so overlap is not the operative quantity (breaks F3).
3. A verifier that can reconstruct the claim is demonstrated to be independent of it (breaks F3, Fig 10 :42).
4. A standard of proof that decides which claims are accepted is shown to remain a pure convention with no verdict load (breaks F4).
5. A standpoint with no outside is exhibited that settles an evidential dispute and cannot be revised by any lower frame (breaks F5, the no-last-room rule).
6. A categorical bar on cross-frame evaluation is derived from the figures, so that underivable really does mean impossible here (breaks F6).

If any fires, the shape is wrong and the claim is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`. These were written before the conclusion in section 7 was drafted.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Lead with the dissolved binary, not with "yes" or "no." The honest answer is that independence is a relation with conditions and a degree (F1 to F4).
2. State the mode on every sentence (F4 of the exemplar's discipline; Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*).
3. Describe the residual before naming a frame (Fig 9 :27-28), so that choosing peer review, a journal, or a community as "the independent evaluator" is recorded as a binding from the social frame, not presented as a derivation.
4. Ask outside readers what they would count as a failure of the shape, and record the answer before showing the framework (Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"*).
5. Send the generator, not the conclusion: Fig 4 :25, *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*

### Updated verdict

The shape survives: independence is a directed relation among evidence, producer, evaluator and a non-updating ground; it is measurable by evidence-pool overlap, buyable in proportion to the cost of being wrong, and destroyed when the producer also anchors or verifies the evaluation. The binary fails at both ends. Strained after re-census: **none**, one apparent strain (anchor legitimacy) dissolved by distinguishing two quantities, and one apparent strain (Fig 7 versus Fig 8) dissolved by the overlap parameter. Both are recorded above.
