## What is being decided, in effect terms

A merger is a composition that changes the actor set of a market from two independent decision-makers to one. The question asks whether that composition usually pays in one currency (productive efficiency, lower cost, more output) or usually costs in another (higher prices, fewer choices, weaker worker and supplier bargaining positions, less innovation). The two effects are not two readings of one quantity. They are indexed to different slots, ranged against different anchors, and measured with different instruments. What an answer would look like: a per-slot, anchor-invariant comparison with a pre-registered refuter, stated in effect terms before any frame is named. This is bound as an ordinary term in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 1 and revisited in `05_what_shape_means.md`.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the verdict.
- No bulk-send plan.

Reason, from the figures: Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." A list chosen for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict (Fig 2 :40). Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Legally, unsolicited bulk outreach to scraped personal addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions, a practical failure on top of the structural one.
