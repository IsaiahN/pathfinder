# Outreach draft: check the output before you decide whether the claim is worth reading

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send a verdict on mergers. Send the shape claim and its refuter. The moment the recipient is asked to agree with a position on mergers, the independence that makes their check worth having is spent (Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. **Subject:** Check one shape claim about merger efficiency before deciding whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific structural claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to agree with it, and I am not asking you to read the theory first. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the per-slot findings, not `grounded`):** "do mergers usually improve efficiency, or often reduce competition and bargaining power" is not one question with two opposite answers. It is a union of at least four slot-indexed comparisons: cost and output, price and variety, innovation and entry, and input-side terms including wages. Each slot needs its own anchor. A single "usually" averages across slots and throws away the signal (Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated.").
>
> The discriminator I want checked is the anchor test. A claimed efficiency saving is not a witness while it is scored by the merging parties who produced it. It becomes a witness only when it is verified against a benchmark the merged firm cannot move by virtue of its power. Where a merger gives the firm power over price or wages, the readings that were serving as the measure start reporting the firm's influence instead of its cost. That is an anchor that has become a frame, and the drift is coherent, which is why it is hard to notice.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that all mergers reduce competition; that efficiency savings are never real; that concentration metrics decide welfare; that one welfare standard is the true one; and that this derivation settles anything. Projected synergies, HHI thresholds and a chosen welfare standard are respectively a self-scored projection, a budget filter and a declared convention. None of them is a ground verdict.
>
> **What would refute the claim, written before this run:**
> 1. a figure line that fixes the sign of the efficiency-versus-power tradeoff for a named class of mergers;
> 2. a per-slot measurement with a valid control in which cost fell and price, wages, variety and innovation did not move against an anchor independent of the merging parties;
> 3. a demonstration that price and wage readings still discriminate cost after the firm acquires power;
> 4. a derivation of one welfare standard from the figures without declaring it.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (GitHub, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - The figure set and formula for direct download: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Two-stage offer, restated

1. **Stage 1.** Send the shape claim, the four slots and the pre-registered refuter. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict before any framework material is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the three links. The figures, not this summary, are the authority. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode. Sending a method is permitted; sending a recording is not (Fig 4 :25).
