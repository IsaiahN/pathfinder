# The Tether's answer on mergers, efficiency and bargaining power

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the four per-domain findings, `gap` on the empirical sign and frequency, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question puts two outcomes of one operation on opposite sides of an either/or. The operation changes the actor set of a market from two independent decision-makers to one. The two outcomes are indexed to different slots, ranged against different anchors and measured with different instruments. Fig 9 :4-5 "First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."

The residual is the gap between a projected saving and a registered outcome, per slot. It is not a single number. Symbols table :43 "R is measured per slot, never as a single global number."

## 2. The shape, domain by domain

**D1, the firm as composition.** A cost saving claimed by the merging parties is self-scoring until it is verified against a benchmark the parties cannot move. Fig 1 :27 "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." This is not a suspicion about motives; it is a structural disqualification of a frame scoring with a quantity it produces. The DOJ and FTC 2023 Guidelines express the same rule as a verifiability requirement: efficiencies "projected by the merging firms often are not realized". Verdict: the projection is not a witness; the sign of the cost effect is owed to measurement. `specified`, single-source for the figure rule plus an independent field rule, triangulated in form.

**D2, the market as measurement device.** Competition is the anchor that makes cost claims falsifiable. Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when" and Fig 2 :30 "you push on it, it is not an anchor." When a merger gives a firm the power to move price or wages, the readings that were serving as the measure begin to report the firm's influence rather than its cost. Fig 2 :34 "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices." Fig 11 :40 "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." A real efficiency gain changes contact; a market-power gain changes leverage over the measure. Verdict: the anchor test discriminates them. `specified`, triangulated (Fig 2, Fig 11, Fig 10).

**D3, input-side bargaining.** The same anchor failure on the buying side. Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." A large employer is not good or bad by kind; its sign flips with the residual. What does not flip is the question of whether the wage still reports labour's alternative value. The DOJ and FTC 2023 Guidelines apply the same frameworks to buyers including employers (Guideline 10); the BLS and JHR studies find lower wages associated with concentration and substantial potential wage effects from top-two mergers. Verdict: a merger can degrade the wage anchor exactly as it can degrade the price anchor. `specified`, triangulated for the structure, one labour evidence pool for the magnitudes.

**D4, the welfare evaluation.** Consumer welfare versus total welfare is a declared convention. Fig 10 :18 "a declared meaning is not a grounded one"; Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one". The figures require the convention to remain checkable and violable, and the person applying it to declare the stake (Fig 10 :59). They do not choose between the standards. Verdict: convention claim `general`; the particular choice is a `gap`.

**Across all domains: the aggregate.** "Usually" is an average over an uncharacterized population of mergers and over non-addable slots. Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." The recent large-sample evidence is itself the demonstration: average price effects near zero, with a standard deviation several times the mean and quartiles of opposite sign. Verdict: the scalar is refused as a form. `general`, triangulated (Fig 1, Fig 9).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that all mergers reduce competition. The figures give an anchor test, not a class verdict.
- Not that efficiency savings are never real. A change in contact is distinguishable from a change in leverage; only the former survives.
- Not that HHI thresholds decide anything. They are filters for the budget (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that consumer welfare is the correct standard, or total welfare. That choice is declared, not derived.
- Not a policy recommendation to block or permit any transaction.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement here settles `left(R,φ)` for any class of mergers.
- Not a claim that the empirical frequency is zero or unknowable. It is unreached at this budget, which is different from unreachable in principle (Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence.").

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A per-slot, before-and-after measurement with three properties: a control or counterfactual specified before the event, an anchor the merged firm cannot move by virtue of its power, and a pre-registered failure condition. Concretely:

- **S1 cost:** does average cost fall against a counterfactual that does not depend on the merging parties' own projections? The FTC retrospective method (difference-in-differences against unaffected markets) is the existing instrument, improved from a worse one (Fig 6 :32).
- **S2 price and variety:** do price, quantity and variety move against a benchmark that remains discriminating after the merger? A finding of near-zero average with large heterogeneity is a reason to report a distribution, not to average again.
- **S3 innovation and entry:** do entry rates and product introductions change over the longer horizon that retrospectives currently under-cover?
- **S4 wages and supplier terms:** do wages track marginal product after the employer gains concentration? The JHR and BLS instruments (other-market concentration as an instrument) are existing attempts.
- **Threshold test (F3):** after the merger, do price and wage readings still separate cost from influence? If not, the anchor has moved and the efficiency claim cannot be settled with those readings at all.
- **Standing structural refuters:** a figure line fixing the sign for a named class; a typed bond admitting the reduction of two firms to one; a lossless reduction of the four slots to one quantity; a derivation of one welfare standard from the figures.

Until one of these fires, the sign and the frequency are a declared `gap` with refuters, not silence.

## 5. The honest stop

The figures can say what an answer must look like and which readings are disqualified. They cannot say whether a given class of mergers usually pays. The empirical literature is large but selected on the enforcement margin and concentrated on short-run price, so its agreement is one evidence pool talking (Fig 7 :13 "agreement compounds, evidence does not"). Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence," and Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." The stop is recorded with its lineage so a later reader can locate the error rather than only feel it.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say mergers are good or bad. It says the question is the wrong shape. "Efficiency" and "competition and bargaining power" are four different slot-indexed comparisons, each needing an anchor the merged firm cannot move. A saving projected by the merging parties scores itself, so it is not a witness until a ground verifies it. Where a merger gives a firm power over price or wages, the readings that were serving as the measure start reporting the firm's influence instead of its cost, so the anchor has become a frame and the drift is hard to notice. Concentration thresholds are budget filters, and the choice between consumer and total welfare is a declared convention whose keeper must declare the stake. The answer offered is the shape any admissible result must have, plus six refuters written in advance. The empirical sign is left open until a per-slot retrospective with a valid control settles it. That is a `gap` reported as a verdict, not an evasion.
