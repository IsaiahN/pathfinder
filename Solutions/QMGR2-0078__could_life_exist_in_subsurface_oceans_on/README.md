## What is being decided, stated as an effect

R_s = |Gamma(b,a) - o'| on moon-slot s (Fig 1 :19). The slots are Europa, Enceladus, Ganymede, Callisto, Titan, Triton and the smaller icy bodies, and R is never averaged across them (Fig 1 :22). What fails to resolve is a plume mass spectrum, an induced magnetic field, an ice-shell thickness, a gravity reading and a surface composition against a prediction about whether the ocean below is inhabited and what would count as detecting it. Two options:

- **Option A:** treat the question as one problem with two halves, map Earth biochemistry onto the ocean, and name a single biosignature as the detection. This sends a name where the framework returns a shape, treats one instance as a template, and can spend the independence of the people asked to check it.
- **Option B:** treat the question as a union of two compounds with different anchors, keep the per-slot residual open, enumerate the habitat from the world, require a source-discriminating instrument above an abiotic baseline, and write the refuters before the run. This sends a checkable shape, an instrument instruction and an honest stop.

An answer, bound as an ordinary term before the run (formula step "Direction"), would look like: *a per-world habitability shape plus a detection route whose success condition is stated in advance and whose failure condition is a named instrument or a named absence of contact.* That binding is what accrues a residual like anything else.

## Headline verdict

The shape survives; a name does not. The existence half is a union of habitat contact and a compositional account of life, and the detection half is an ordered source-attribution route against an abiotic baseline, not a symmetric count of molecules. Life in an icy ocean is `candidate` under the figures: capability is agent-and-habitat (Fig 11 :39), the habitat is enumerated and never composed (Fig 11 :43), cross-kind composition requires a structure between states (Fig 13 :74), and whether anything actually lives there is owned by the ground (Fig 2 :29). Detection must be built from an instrument already returning something (Fig 6 :32, :34), must discriminate source and not only reading, and a null result is not an absence (Fig 8 :39, Fig 9 :44). The specific biochemistry, the specific biosignature and the occurrence number are declared `gap`s or bindings, not hidden answers.
