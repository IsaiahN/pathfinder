## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the two-part question: could life exist in subsurface oceans on icy worlds, and how could it be detected? Option A: treat it as one question, map Earth biochemistry onto an ocean, and name a single biosignature as the detection. Option B: treat it as a union of two compounds, keep the per-moon residual separate, enumerate the habitat from the world, require source discrimination against an abiotic baseline, and report a `gap` where the ground has not spoken. What each leads to: A sends a name where the framework returns a shape and can spend the independence of the people asked to check it; B sends a checkable shape, an instrument instruction, five pre-registered refuters and an honest stop.

- **X1 Earth-life template.** No anchor beyond the single instance already in D2. Not a domain; absorbed as a binding.

Union, not partition (Fig 9 :54). D1 and D2 are the existence compound; D3 and D4 are the detection compound. ## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the name does not. The question is a union of two compounds with different anchors, and verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged. D1: habitability belongs to ocean-and-candidate, the habitat is enumerated from the world, and a spendable difference must mediate energy into flow (`general`, triangulated). D2: the framework returns the shape of an inhabited world, not a biochemistry, and the actual occupancy of any named moon is a declared ground-owned `gap`, a verdict rather than a silence (`specified`). D3: detection must grow from instruments already returning unresolved material and must discriminate source against a quantified abiotic baseline; a reading alone does not determine its source (`specified`, triangulated). D4: independence is between instruments, null results are bounds, and filters never hand over a verdict (`general` to `specified`, triangulated). The stronger claim that two biosignature routes are isomers is only a functional-group claim as normally reported, and becomes an isomer claim over a full shared atom set with a stated falsifier (`06_isomers_and_order.md`). Nothing here is `grounded`.

- **Cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The main cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending a recording (a finished theory or a named biosignature) instead of a generator (the shape, the baseline requirement and the refuter). Fig 2 :42 prices that: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* A second cost is treating a null as an absence and thereby closing a slot that should stay open. Both are avoidable by leading with the refuter and reporting bounds with swept scopes.
- **Undoable:** yes. No term is deleted when a candidate signature is refuted; the refusal is added with provenance and the reach falls because *"something was added rather than because anything was deleted"* (Fig 6 :42-43). A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet, and no primitive has to be withdrawn.
- **Reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A source-discriminating in-situ or returned-sample measurement that establishes habitability conditions and separates biological from abiotic production for a named signature would move D2 from `candidate` toward `grounded`; a measurement of contact failure would narrow or withdraw D1; a single signature shown to determine source uniquely without context would break D3; a figure re-census that admits ⋛ over a measured excess and a baseline model as quantities at that level would clear the named strain. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`. Prestige or agreement is not such an instrument.
