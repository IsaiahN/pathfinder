# Structure, order and isomers in ocean-world life detection

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the verbatim figure text, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

Given two candidate biosignature routes for the same ocean (a carbon-isotope fractionation route and a chiral-excess route), and given the claim that habitability is a cross-kind composition, three questions are owed:

1. What is the **structure** of a habitability molecule, and is it legal under the six-term set?
2. What is the **order** of a detection route, and can a flat list of ingredients express it?
3. Are the two routes **isomers**, or only members of a functional group? What would falsify that?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: Space, Time, Energy (states) and Spectrum, Gradient, Network (structures). Bonds held: the seven, plus negation as a unary operator. No new primitive is available without an import, and no import is claimed here. The two routes are drawn from the world literature (Higgins et al. 2026; Patty et al. 2022), never from within the figure set, so they enter as bindings onto held atoms with provenance.

## DERIVATION

### Q1. What is the structure of habitability?

- Fig 13 :74: *"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."*
- Fig 13 :76: *"Energy and a gradient compose a flow."*
- Fig 13 :21-22: *"gradient"*, *"a difference that can be spent"*; Symbols table :96-98: *"A difference that can be spent. Not merely a distribution: what makes it a gradient is directedness."*
- Fig 13 :29: *"A state with no structure has no magnitude: three of what, ordered how, varying across what?"*

So an ocean with water and energy but no spendable difference is not a habitat in the figure sense. The legal first-order molecule is `state -> structure -> state`: for example water and rock (states) coupled through a spendable redox or pH difference (structure) to a persistent chemical flow over time (state). This is `general` for the rule and `specified` for the ocean application.

The H2 result of Oberg, Magnabosco and Tosca (2026) and the phosphate-derived pH of Glein and Truong (2025) are bindings of measured conditions onto that structure. They do not add a seventh member of the six. They are not imports that move the wall (Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."*).

**Falsifier Q1:** a self-maintaining ocean biosphere shown to operate with no spendable difference at all, or a seventh member derived from the other six. Either breaks the cross-kind rule. Written before the run.

### Q2. What order does a detection route have?

A vocabulary with only ingredients can name a state and cannot name a route to one. Fig 12 :111-112: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one. A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* Fig 12 :113: *"A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."*

The detection route therefore has four distinct ordered moves, plus one unary operator:

1. **Sequence (->).** Contact: plume grain or sample enters the instrument. `->` is defined as *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"* (Fig 12 :70-72), so acquisition precedes reading.
2. **Production (⇒).** A candidate signature is produced. The bond is ordered because *"the second operand is not waiting, it is absent until the first fires"* (Operators table :39-40). Critically, the production claim "life produced this signature" is **not** recoverable from the signature alone, because abiotic processes also produce it. That is the metamerism case in `05` section 4.
3. **Comparison (⋛).** The measured value is ranged against the abiotic baseline. Fig 12 :86: *"a threshold relation between two quantities."* Fig 12 :88: *"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."* The goal of detection is a comparison, so a vocabulary without ⋛ cannot state one.
4. **Subtraction (-).** The residual after the baseline is removed. Progress is a subtraction. What remains is the unexplained excess, and it may be zero.
5. **Negation (¬).** If an abiotic pathway is established for the candidate, the claim is refused, not deleted. Operators table :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."*

A flat list of molecules cannot carry steps 1 to 5. A report that says "we found methane, amino acids and phosphates, therefore life" is a flat formula: Fig 12 :14-17, *"the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not; nothing downstream can tell them apart, and nothing signals that anything was lost."*

**Falsifier Q2:** a detection claim in which the success condition is unchanged when acquisition order, production route, baseline comparison, subtraction and refusal are all varied. That would show the arrangement carries nothing. Written before the run.

### Q3. Are the two routes isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

As the two routes are normally reported, they do **not** share an atom set. The isotope route uses carbon isotopologues, CO2 context and a thermodynamic baseline; the chirality route uses amino-acid enantiomers, mineral catalysis and racemisation kinetics. Different atoms in the slots means they are two members of the same functional group (`measurement -> baseline comparison -> excess`), not isomers. Calling them isomers would be the flat-formula error in a new place.

The **stronger form** is worth testing. If both routes are rewritten over the full shared atom set held by the detection frame (sample, instrument, baseline model, geological context, transport history, construction history), and they differ only in which bond mediates which step, then they are isomers in Fig 12's sense: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance, decisive behavioural difference. Rutter et al. 2025 give a concrete version: molecular assembly and structure-based complexity measures can be applied to the same mass-spectrometry data and differ in whether construction history is recorded. Whether that is a genuine isomer relation needs a measurement, not an argument.

**Falsifier Q3:** if two routes share the full atom set and differ only in bond but produce identical source-attribution behaviour under varied abiotic baselines, the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim stands. Written before the run.

### Q4. Is there a cycle?

- Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*

An Enceladus plume is a physical cycle: ocean water is injected to space, grains fall back onto the surface, and surface material is reprocessed. The sign matters. A negative loop returns material to the ocean and can close ahabitable circulation; a positive loop loses it to space and the ocean's inventory drains. The figures supply the cycle form and the sign question; Tjoa, Mueller and van der Tak (2020) and Kimura and Kitadai (2015) supply the physical settings. A chain of plume detections cannot close the cycle by construction, so "we detected a plume" is not the same claim as "the ocean is sustained."

**Falsifier Q4:** a plume model in which the return branch is absent and the reported cycle still closes, or a detection chain that is shown to close without a return path. Written before the run.

### Q5. Analogues, used as analogues only

Two analogues are used in this folder, and neither is evidence:

1. **Metamerism.** The colour instrument reading green cannot comment on the source (Hard Problem is About Source). This is an analogy for a biosignature reading. It is not a measurement of an ocean.
2. **Isomerism in chemistry.** Fig 12 :97-98 cites silver fulminate and silver cyanate, *"same composition and different properties"* (Liebig and Wöhler, 1824 to 1826; Berzelius named isomerism in 1830). This is the figure's own analogy for bond information; it does not establish that any two biosignature routes are isomers. The atom-set test in Q3 must still be run.

Using an analogue as a witness would be echo without shadow: picking the frame first and then finding somewhere to put it (Fig 9 :27-28, *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*). Both analogues enter after the residual was described, and both are labelled as analogues.

## CONCLUSION

The habitability molecule is legal as a cross-kind composition with a spendable difference, and the specific difference is a measured binding, not a seventh primitive. A detection route is an ordered composition of sequence, production, comparison, subtraction and refusal; a flat ingredient list cannot carry it. As reported, the isotope and chirality routes are members of a functional group rather than isomers; over a full shared atom set the stronger isomer claim is testable and has a falsifier. The plume is a cycle whose sign the figures say decides closure, and neither analogue used here is a witness. One live strain is named: ⋛ is defined as a threshold relation between two quantities, while at the detection level the compared operands are a measured excess and a modelled baseline, so whether the baseline model counts as a quantity at that level needs an instrument, not an argument. It is recorded as a strain, not smoothed over.
