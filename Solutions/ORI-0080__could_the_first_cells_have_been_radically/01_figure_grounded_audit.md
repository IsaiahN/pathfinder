## 0. The residual, in effect terms

The ordinary question is ambiguous between a history question ("what were the first cells actually like?") and a reachability question ("could a first cell have been of a form no organism alive today instantiates?"). The figures only decide the second. So the residual is stated in effect terms before any figure is selected:

> **R:** the gap between (a) the set of cell-forms that can be inferred from the organisms and molecular machinery alive today, and (b) the set of cell-forms that could have composed and persisted at the origin. R is indexed per condition: informational substrate, code, energy coupling, compartment, chirality.

- **Arity:** multiple conditions. The question is not one variable with two settings; it is a bundle of at least five overlapping conditions, so it is a **union**, not a partition. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
- **Symmetry:** "radically unlike" is a **difference relation over a fixed functional target**. The target is a self-maintaining, evolving cell; the difference is in the atoms, bonds or arrangement that realize it. Difference is not itself a bond, and the comparison bond ⋛ is *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* (Operators :48), so the relation "unlike" must be stated as an arrangement comparison, not as a measured magnitude. This is a live type question, named in §6.
- **Scale:** the residual crosses the deep-time membrane from pre-cellular chemistry (about 4.3 to 3.8 Ga) to the first cells, and from the first cells to the present survivor set. The loop is the same at both sizes; the actors, currency and anchor change. Fig 4 :20: *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."*
- **What each option leads to:** A (yes, radically unlike) means the shared molecular core of modern life is a survivorship filter, and an instrument from the early Earth is owed. B (no) means the core is a necessary constraint and the origin is reachable by backward extrapolation from it.
- **The sought-for shape, bound as an ordinary term before searching:** any first cell must (i) hold a boundary that distinguishes inside from outside, (ii) couple a spendable difference to a copyable informational polymer in a loop, (iii) admit heritable variation and differential persistence. This is a shape; it names no chemistry. Formula step "Direction"; Fig 5 :65: *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM."*

## 2. Figure census and findings

Each finding quotes the verbatim figure text before concluding. Quotes are exact substrings of `03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`, cited as `Fig N :line`. A line that fits only with a strain governs another quantity and sends us back to the census (§7).

### F1: The survivor set cannot prove absence of other first-cell forms (decisive for D1)

Fig 8 :39:
> **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

Fig 9 :44:
> **"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."**

The set of organisms alive today is not a sealed room. It is the residue of roughly 3.8 billion years of extinction, substitution and horizontal transfer. The fact that no cell radically unlike the known biosphere is found in that residue therefore has no bearing on whether such cells existed at the origin. This is the strongest figure-grounded point in the answer, and it is **triangulated at low overlap**: Fig 8 derives the claim from closure idempotence (composition inside a closed set never leaves it), Fig 9 derives it from the impossibility of a sealed room and the metaframe limit. Different figures, different routes, same verdict. World corroboration (shadow biosphere, survivor bias, Budd and Mann) is secondary and recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` §WORLD.

**Refuter for F1:** produce a figure line that makes the survivor set a sealed room, or an instrument that reads the original cell-forms directly from living genomes. None exists in the extract. If one did, D1's `gap` would become a verdict.

### F2: "Radically unlike" splits into two dimensions with different governors

Re-census of the residual line, not a figure line, forces this split:

- **Arrangement dimension:** same chemical atoms, different bonds or arrangement (mirror chirality, alternative base pairs, a different membrane lipid set over the same elements). Governed by Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- **Substrate dimension:** different atoms or a different solvent (silicon for carbon, ammonia or methane for water). Governed by the import rules, not the isomer rule: Fig 6 :23 *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* and Fig 13 :115 *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."*

An earlier draft applied the isomer line to both dimensions. That was a strain. Re-census: isomerism requires a **fixed atom set**; a different chemistry is not a rearrangement of the held six and is not an isomer under the figure's definition. Both dimensions remain in the answer, with separate verdicts. See `06_isomers_and_order.md` for the full derivation and its falsifier.

**Strain status:** dissolved by re-census, preserved here. Not smoothed over.

### F3: The first cells were in the unmaintained mode; selection ran, nobody selected

Fig 11 :36:
> **"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran."**

Fig 11 :39:
> **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"**

At the origin there was no seat, no maintainer, no instrument and no live frame in the checking position. The habitat was pure contact. This matters because it rules out the most common quiet assumption in origin-of-life prose: that the first cells were "trying" alternatives or that a lineage was "led" toward the modern core. What survived is what the ground kept. Fig 11 :56: *"Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept."*

**Refuter:** a demonstrated maintainer at the origin (an external agent or a designed gradient-setting process that is not itself part of the habitat). This would move D1 from the unmaintained to the maintained mode and would require a seat in the census. The figures do not forbid a maintainer in general; they distinguish the two modes (Fig 11 :55: *"Two modes, and only one of them has a seat."*).

### F4: A self-maintaining cell requires a cycle; a chain cannot close

Fig 12 :121:
> **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**

Fig 12 :119:
> **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**

A cell is a closed loop with a boundary, not a linear reaction chain. The RNA-world, metabolism-first and hydrothermal-vent proposals differ in which part they make the catalyst and which part they make the copyable polymer, but all of them owe a cycle and a part that survives its own use. This is a **structural constraint on the shape**, independent of chemistry, so it survives across both dimensions of F2. It is `general` in mode: it says what any cell-shape requires, not what the first cell used.

**Refuter:** a self-maintaining, evolving compartment that can be written as an open chain with no re-entrant output. If one is demonstrated, the cycle constraint is wrong.

### F5: The figure frame returns a shape, not a name

Fig 9 :32:
> **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**

The canonical usage rule adds that the framework reports *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"* and stops at the limit of what instruments can distinguish. The six-term set does not contain a chemistry. Silicon, DNA, L-amino acids, a lipid bilayer and a genetic code are bindings from the chemistry and biology frames onto held atoms or imports from outside them; none is selected by Fig 13. The inference "the figures give the shape of a cell, therefore they tell us the first cell used RNA" is the named metamerism error: reading a source off a projection. See `05_what_shape_means.md` §3 and the verified essay quote there.

**Refuter:** a figure line that names a specific substrate or code for a cell at any level. None exists. If one were found, this `gap` would close.

### F6: The ground for this question is an instrument, not more comparison

Fig 6 :32:
> **"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."**

Fig 10 :24:
> **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**

Fig 3 :27:
> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

The broken link in D1 is not reasoning about LUCA; it is the absence of a reading of the original cell-forms. Comparing more modern genomes refines a link that already works while the link that matters (a ground reading from the early Earth or a discriminating laboratory synthesis) stays uninstrumented. This is Fig 3's dependency rule, not a complaint about data volume. Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* A second, independent instrument, not a second review of the same genomes, is what the cost of being wrong here demands.

**Refuter:** a demonstration that the existing comparative-genomic instrument already discriminates the original substrate. The NASA Astrobiology FAQ's admission of lost information counts against this, but the decision is figure-side: no figure line upgrades comparison into an anchor.

### F7: Deep time substitutes a habitat; two failure modes are silent

Fig 11 :66:
> **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."**

Fig 11 :68-69:
> **"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"**
> **"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"**

The survivor set is the result of a substituted habitat, not a cleaned sample of the original one. What the surviving lineages failed to reproduce (the alternative cell-forms, if any) is invisible because the goal of a lineage that went extinct no longer fails for anyone. What they unintentionally introduced (the near-universal core, horizontal transfer, the standard code) is visible only because it still acts. So the shared core is exactly the "brought with you" category, and it cannot be used as evidence that nothing else existed. This triangulates F1 from a different figure (Fig 11 isolation law, low overlap with Fig 8 closure).

**Refuter:** show that the original habitat was preserved intact into the present, so the survivor set is a sample rather than a substitute. Geologically untenable, and a figure-side refuter would be required to move the verdict.

### F8: Convergence and import look identical in the contents; only the record separates them

Fig 8 :50-52:
> **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."**

Fig 13 :121:
> **"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."**

Two first-cell proposals that arrive at a modern-like cell by different routes are convergent derivations, not one borrowing from the other. The figure's warning applies to how we *report* them: if we do not record which atoms were held, which were bound from another frame and which were imported, a convergent derivation and an adopted import are indistinguishable in the final description. In origin-of-life writing this is the difference between "RNA world was reconstructed from chemistry" and "RNA world was assumed because it resembles modern biology." Only the provenance separates them. See `10_source_ledger.csv` overlap column for the same discipline applied to world sources.

**Refuter:** a case where two closures arrive at the same cell-form but the route is recoverable from the artefact alone. Fig 13 :129 says it is not: *"The order is not recoverable from the artefact. It is recoverable from the lineage."*

## 3. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per Fig 5 :65 and the seat-map rule that *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument."* These are committed now, before §8, and are repeated in `07`, `08` and `09`.

The shape claim ("a radically-different first cell is reachable and the survivor set cannot refute it") is withdrawn if any of the following fires:

1. **A figure line names a chemistry.** Any line requiring cells at any level to use a specific substrate, code or solvent would close the `gap` in F5 against us. Searched: none found.
2. **A non-updating anchor appears in the survivor set.** A comparative reading that does not move with genome sample or method would refute D1's `gap` and F1. Observed behaviour moves (thermophile versus mesophile reconstructions), so it has not fired.
3. **A necessity demonstration.** A ground result showing that Darwinian evolution plus self-maintenance cannot be composed without one specific modern feature (for example, without a ribosome or without DNA) would refute D2's reachability in one or both dimensions.
4. **A direct ground reading.** A preserved, dated chemical or isotopic signature of the first cells showing the same molecular core as modern life would refute "radically unlike" in fact, even though it leaves the reachability claim intact.
5. **The cycle constraint fails.** A self-maintaining evolving compartment that is an open chain with no re-entrant output would refute F4.
6. **A sealed survivor set.** A figure line or instrument that makes the modern biosphere a sealed room would refute F1.

Until one of these is run, no part of this answer may be labelled `grounded`. The verdict remains `candidate`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Split the ordinary question into its five conditions before searching (F0/F9 union). Do not answer "radically unlike" with one global verdict.
2. Lead with F1, the non-sealed survivor set and the absence proof. It is the most triangulated figure result and the one an expert can check in two minutes.
3. Keep the two dimensions of F2 separate. "Same atoms, different arrangement" is an isomer claim; "different atoms or solvent" is an import claim. They have different governors and different refuters.
4. State `specified` on reachability and `general` on the shape. Do not state a probability, a date or a chemistry.
5. Ask a checker for a failure condition, never for agreement. Send the packet in `08`, hold the framework interior until stage 2, and never pool a later agreement as corroboration.
6. Record the lineage. Where the first-cell form is concerned, the record is the term (Fig 13 :130).
