Order by dependency: describe R in effect terms before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); fix the cell-shape before testing a chemistry; borrow an anchor from D2 because D1 has none; let D4 check without the interiors.

## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in the folder: the survivor set cannot settle the question; a cell needs a boundary, a cycle and a ground comparison; arrangement differences are isomers and inventory differences are imports; the original chemistry is a gap.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the concrete claim as an expert would hear it: the first cells could have been radically unlike any organism alive today.

**R_T is nonzero.** Three constraints carried by the full figure set do not survive the trip back down:

- the **fixed-atom condition** on the isomer claim, dropped if "radically unlike" is heard as one undifferentiated claim (Fig 12 :117 requires same atoms);
- the **unmaintained-habitat condition**, dropped if the origin is narrated as though something selected alternatives (Fig 11 :56);
- the **ground condition on settling**, dropped if the reachability verdict is heard as a verdict about what actually happened (Fig 6 :32, Fig 9 :32).

Conversely, two things survive cleanly: the non-sealed-set verdict and the requirement that any cell close a loop and hold a boundary. That residue is what `07` and `08` actually send. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the figures" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be exactly the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
