## The question restated as an effect

"What would it take for a cell to be radically unlike every organism alive today?" is a reachability question, not a history question. It asks whether the class of possible first cellular forms includes arrangements that share no living representative, i.e. forms whose organization was not a subset of the survivor set we can observe. Two options and what each leads to:

- **Option A, yes:** the molecular features all living organisms share are a survivorship filter, not a constraint on the origin. A first cell could satisfy the same functional shape (a boundary, a copyable informational polymer, a cycled energy gradient, Darwinian evolution) with different atoms, different bonds, or a different arrangement. This leads to the prediction that no amount of comparing modern genomes will enumerate the original cell forms, and that a ground signal from the early Earth is owed.
- **Option B, no:** the shared features are necessary to cellular life and therefore trace back to the first cells. This leads to the prediction that the origin is reachable by extrapolating the universal molecular core backwards, and that alternative biochemistries are not viable cells.

An answer would look like a **shape**, not a specimen: a set of conditions any first cell must meet plus a stated refuter for each. Bind that as an ordinary term before searching, per formula step "Direction" and Fig 5 :65.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that the figures name a first-cell chemistry. They return a shape, never a name (Fig 9 :32).
- No claim that a simulated derivation settles a 3.8-billion-year-old empirical question.

The reason for the first three is structural, not merely legal. Fig 2 :35 says *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* A list selected for receptiveness is a list selected to agree, and by Fig 2 :40 *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Fig 10 :42 says *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, never the framework interior.
