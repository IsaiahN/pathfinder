# Isomers, imports, cycles: the two dimensions of "radically unlike"

**Mode: `specified` on the per-dimension verdicts, `general` on the structural definitions.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05` §7, `01` header).

## RESIDUAL

The ordinary phrase "radically unlike any organism alive today" conflates two different relations. One is a difference of arrangement over the same chemical inventory. The other is a difference of inventory itself. They are governed by different figure lines and carry different refuters. A third question, about the order of composition, is owed because a cell is a loop, not a list.

## DOMAIN CENSUS (before figure lines)

- **Atoms and bonds available in this derivation:** the six substrate terms (space, time, energy; spectrum, gradient, network), the seven bonds plus negation, and the held chemistry bindings reported in the world sources (nucleic acids and their analogues, amino acid sets, lipid compartments, elemental inventories). No new chemical primitive is available without an import from the chemistry frame.
- **D1 arrangement domain:** actors = bonds and arrangements; currency = description cost and behavioural difference; anchor = an instrument that discriminates arrangements. This is a partition question over a fixed atom set.
- **D2 substrate domain:** actors = elemental inventories and solvents; currency = chemical plausibility and persistence; anchor = experiment. This is an import question, not an isomer question.
- **D3 order domain:** actors = cycles and catalysts; currency = closure of the loop; anchor = whether the system persists. This is a structure question.
- **Dependency:** settle whether an arrangement closes (D3) before asking which arrangement (D1); settle the atom set (D2) before asking whether two forms are isomers over it. Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*

## Q1: Which dimension is an isomer?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**
- Fig 12 :46: **"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."**

**Arrangement dimension.** A cell with mirror-image chirality, or one using a different base set over the same elements (the catalogue reports hachimoji DNA with four extra bases), is an isomer in the figure's sense: the ingredient list is largely unchanged, the bond or assignment differs, and the behaviour differs decisively (for mirror life, incompatibility with existing biomolecules). The isomer claim is **triangulated** for definition (Fig 12 :117 and :94 are low-overlap restatements of the same rule) and **single-source** for the specific chemical examples, which come from the chemistry frame with provenance.

**Substrate dimension.** A cell using silicon instead of carbon, or ammonia or methane instead of water, is **not** an isomer over the held atom set. The atom set changed, and Fig 12 :117 requires "same atoms." It falls under import: Fig 6 :23, *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 13 :115, *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."*

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a proposed alternative first cell changes the elemental inventory *and* is defended with the isomer line, the claim fails and must be restated as an import. If a proposed alternative keeps the inventory and is defended with the import line, it overstates what an import adds (Fig 6 :21: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*). This falsifier fired once in this run, against my own first draft, and is preserved in `01` §5.

## Q2: Are two different first-cell arrangements isomers or convergent routes?

- Fig 13 :121: **"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."**
- Fig 13 :123-124: **"Two chains built from different links may compose functionally identical terms, and the converse holds too: the same ingredients under a different bond are a different substance."**
- Fig 8 :50-52: **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."**

Two proposals that arrive at a cell-shape from different starting chemistries are **convergent derivations** if each was derived inside its own closure. They are **adopted imports** if one copied the arrangement from the other. The finished descriptions look the same; only the lineage separates them. This is why `10_source_ledger.csv` records overlap and why `01` F8 refuses to count agreement between sources sharing an evidence pool as a second witness.

**Falsifier:** if a route cannot be recovered from the record, the convergence claim is not checkable and must be downgraded to `single-source`. Fig 13 :129: *"The order is not recoverable from the artefact. It is recoverable from the lineage."*

## Q3: What order does the cell-shape have?

- Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**
- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**
- Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

A cell is not an ordered list of reactions. It is a cycle with a part that survives its own use. The RNA-world, metabolism-first and vent-first proposals disagree about which role is played by which molecule, but all three owe the same functional group: boundary plus copyable polymer plus regenerated catalyst plus a comparison against a ground. Under Fig 12 :123 that makes them members of one functional group with different qualifiers, not different kinds of thing.

**Strain, named:** the phrase "the sign of the loop decides whether it closes" invites reading the cycle as a sequence bond →, which would impose an order the figure does not require between the kinds. Re-census: Fig 12 :121 is about the sign (positive or negative feedback) of a re-entrant relation, not about the six-term substrate's rule that composition crosses kinds. Different objects. No contradiction; the apparent strain is dissolved and preserved here.

## Q4: Does the survivor set select a winner by convergence?

- Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**
- Fig 7 :36: **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."**
- Fig 11 :56: **"Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept."**

No. At the origin there was no maintainer and no seat. Ground contact kept what it kept. The modern core is the surviving room, and its agreement with itself is variation, not a verdict on the unoccupied forms. This is the figure-side reason the shared core cannot be used to exclude radically-different first cells, independent of the empirical point that the fossil and genomic records are incomplete.

**Falsifier:** a demonstrated maintainer at the origin (a seat acting on the habitat) would move this from the unmaintained to the maintained mode and would require a different verdict in `01` F3. No such maintainer is part of any standard origin model.

## CONCLUSION

The question splits cleanly. "Radically unlike" in **arrangement** is an isomer claim over a fixed atom set (Fig 12 :117), and the chemistry frame supplies real examples: mirror chirality, alternative base sets, alternative backbones. "Radically unlike" in **substrate** is an import claim (Fig 6 :23, Fig 13 :115), and the chemistry frame supplies candidates but no first-cell ground reading. Both are reachable as shapes. Neither is licensed as a history. The cell-shape itself is a cycle with a surviving catalyst (Fig 12 :119, :121), so any candidate, however strange, must close a loop and hold a boundary. That is the residue worth sending. The specific original chemistry remains a `gap` (Fig 9 :32).
