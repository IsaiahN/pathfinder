# Expert verification packet: the Tether on regions beyond the observable universe

**What this is:** a claim in a form a cosmologist or philosopher of science can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, `gap` on direct properties of a named beyond-horizon region. Not `grounded`.** No measurement beyond a horizon is claimed.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "are there regions beyond the observable universe and can their properties be tested" is a union of two conditions with different grounds, not one hard question (Fig 9 :54). For a named region beyond the particle horizon, no observation returns, so its direct properties are `gap`: existence is neither asserted nor denied. The horizon is a limit of a frame's reach, not a physical wall, and it is observer-relative. Some global properties of the whole, notably cosmic topology, leave traces in within-horizon data and are reachable in principle, with an anchor, though no positive detection exists. Confirmation is asymmetric: a finite signature can fire, an infinite whole cannot be confirmed by measurement. The framework returns this shape; it does not name what is out there.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 9

- **Statement:** existence and testability form a union and must be unbundled before searching; their verdicts are never averaged.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :59: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- **What to check:** is the overlap real? A global CMB signature can be evidence for a property of the whole without identifying a specific region, so the two conditions come apart exactly as claimed.
- **Refuter:** show a single anchor that settles existence of a specific beyond-horizon region and testability of its properties in one step. Then the union dissolves.

### C2 - instrument-facing; checkable by anyone who works with detectors or surveys

- **Statement:** direct properties of a beyond-horizon region are `gap`, because no proto-instrument exists to sharpen. Indirect global traces are reachable in principle.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :32: "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something." Fig 6 :34-35: "The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve. Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."
- **Physics anchor (secondary):** Luminet (2015) states that the infinite simply connected case "is not and will never be a testable hypothesis" while "a sufficiently small, finite space model could be testable"; Copi et al. (2026) report that topology searches have found no definitive evidence but that future surveys "could expand the detectable parameter space".
- **Refuter:** an instrument already returning something that fails to resolve for that region, from which a sharper instrument can be built; or a working instrument derived from a bare description with nothing previously registering. Either moves D3 from `gap` to reachable.

### C3 - physics-facing; checkable against cosmology data

- **Statement:** the two horizons are different quantities with different instructions, and a negative search is a constraint, not proof of absence.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :40-43: "Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions. Underivable: go outside. Unreachable-at-budget: the term is already in the closure and was not found, so the remedy is more search, not another atom." Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
- **What to check:** Ord (2021) distinguishes particle, event, eventually observable and ultimately observable regions; ScienceInsights (2026) notes the particle horizon expands while the event horizon overrides it in an accelerating universe. Is the underivable/unreachable split physically drawn as claimed?
- **Refuter:** a measurement of the dark-energy equation of state with `w >= −1/3` (no event horizon), or a demonstration that negative topology searches fully exclude a sealed finite space at the relevant scale. The first collapses the split; the second would make absence provable and break Fig 8 :39.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that any specific beyond-horizon region exists, and not that none does.
- Not that the horizon is a physical edge, or that conditions change there.
- Not that the observable universe is all there is, and not that "no last room" proves a physical beyond. The figure line governs closures, not spatial extent (`01` F8).
- Not that the universe is infinite, finite, or a bubble in a multiverse.
- Not that cosmic topology has been detected. It has not.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by observation.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A returned signal whose source is demonstrably beyond our particle horizon and not reproducible by in-horizon physics.
2. A matched CMB circle pair, topological lensing signal, or bubble-collision signature that settles a D4 property.
3. A measurement of `w` that decides whether an event horizon exists.
4. A measurement proving the universe infinite (which current sources say is impossible; if it happens, that claim is retracted).
