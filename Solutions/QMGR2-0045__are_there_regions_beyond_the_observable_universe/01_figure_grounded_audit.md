## 0. The question under audit, stated as an effect

> Are there regions beyond the observable universe, and can any of their properties be tested?

Translate into the loop's quantities. `Γ` is our cosmology: general relativity, FLRW models, ΛCDM, inflation. `b` is the assumption that the copy of `Γ` we hold applies beyond the patch we can see (the cosmological principle, an inherited prior). `a` is each act of observation. `o′` is what actually returns. `R = |Γ(b,a) − o′|` is per slot `s` (Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated.").

- Slots inside the horizon: photons, CMB, large-scale structure return. `o′` exists. `R` is computable and largely closes for local geometry and contents.
- Slots beyond the particle horizon: `Γ` predicts (inflation gives far more than our patch), but no `o′` returns. `R` is not zero; it is **unreadable**. There is a prediction and no observation to range it against.

The question therefore asks two different things. Condition A: do the unreadable slots carry content? Condition B: can any `o′` be made to bear on them? A and B do not share an anchor, so they are a **union compound**, not one hard question (Fig 9 :54). *The Library of Babel* states the same test in different words: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?" A region can exist without being testable; a signature can be testable without establishing that a specific region exists.

**What an answer would look like (Direction discipline).** `THE_FORMULA.md` warns that a frame cannot check the direction of its own question, because the sought-for shape is a preference, not a term. So bind the shape as an ordinary term before deriving: an acceptable answer must (i) say which slots have an anchor and which do not, (ii) keep existence and testability verdicts separate, (iii) state what would reverse each verdict at the ground, and (iv) refuse to name a specific beyond-horizon region unless a measurement returns from it. This binding is written before the findings below.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor.

- **R (the residual).** Arity: two conditions conjoined (existence, testability), so a union, not a partition. Symmetry: the horizon is one-way and observer-relative. Light cones are past-directed; an observer on a galaxy at "our" edge has their own overlapping sphere, so "beyond" is a relation between an observer and a light cone, not a property of the world. Scale: three distinct scales are in play, the particle horizon (roughly 46.5 Gly comoving), the event horizon (roughly 16 Gly), and global topology which may exceed both. Two horizons moving in opposite directions is itself a warning that one word, "observable", is covering two quantities.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 In-horizon cosmology | photons, CMB, galaxies, Ω_k | returned observations `o′` | CMB and large-scale structure, a measurement that does not move when we think about it | domain, anchored |
| D2 The horizon | past light cone, scale factor a(t), dark-energy w | causal access / information boundary | none that settles "what lies beyond"; the horizon is a feature of our situation, not an object in the world | boundary, not a domain with its own ground |
| D3 Named beyond-horizon regions | putative galaxies, bubbles, pocket universes | existence claims | none available, since by definition no signal returns | **not yet a domain**: no nameable anchor |
| D4 Global properties with in-horizon traces | multiply connected topology, curvature, bubble collisions, kSZ bulk flows | statistically testable signatures | CMB maps (WMAP, Planck) and galaxy surveys | domain, anchored |
| D5 Verification / outreach | expert readers, reviewers, instruments | agreement and residual | the ground (data), never agreement | domain, but in this run all seats share one evidence pool |

**Partition or union.** D1 and D4 are testable and anchored; D3 is unanchored. The question is a union of an anchored part and an unanchored part. Fig 9 :54 applies: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." A single lever cannot clear both: better instruments extend D1 and D4 and do nothing for D3.

**Dependency order (Fig 3).** The chain is perception (D1 returns something), vocabulary (D4 gives terms for global structure), objective (what would count as a trace), then inference to D3. A reading taken at D3 before D1 and D4 are settled is a reading below the break: Fig 3 :22 says it is "a reading of nothing." So order is D1, then D4, then D3.

**Strain test.** An early reading wanted Fig 7 :6 ("the limit: no last room") to prove that other regions exist. That strains: Fig 7 governs rooms, that is frames and closures, not physical spatial extent. Re-census required, recorded in §3 (F8) and in the retraction log. A second early reading used Fig 6 :34-35 to forbid all talk of anything beyond the horizon. That also strains: the rule needs "something already returning", and D4 has returning data. Narrowed in F3.

## 2. Findings, with verbatim figure lines and refuters

### F1 - The question is a union and must be unbundled

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :59: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
*The Library of Babel* (independent vocabulary, same shape): "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"

Existence (D3) and testability (D1, D4) have different grounds. Asking "are they there, and can we test them" as one question lets a positive topology result be reported as proof of a specific bubble universe, which it is not. **Mode: general.** **Verdict: union. Triangulated** across Fig 9 and the Babel essay (low overlap between a barrier-theorem figure and a search essay).
**Refuter:** show a single anchor that settles both existence of a specific beyond-horizon region and testability of its properties at the same stroke. None is named by any source read.

### F2 - The residual beyond the horizon is unreadable, not zero

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Symbols table: `R` is "the gap between prediction and observation" and "it is always a slice: what cannot be perceived yet is residual and simply cannot be read."
Toby Ord, *The Edges of Our Universe* (2021), opened this session: the paper "distinguishes four principal regions: the affectable universe, the observable universe, the eventually observable universe, and the ultimately observable universe." So the word "observable" already names more than one slot, and our slot is one of several.

Aggregating over all slots would give a global `R` near zero (cosmology fits its data) while the beyond-horizon slots stay live. That is the exact legal state Fig 1 :13 describes: "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state." **Mode: specified.** **Verdict: gap for D3 at the ground. Single-source for the figure discipline, triangulated with Ord for the multi-region census (low overlap: a metatheory figure and a causal-limits paper).**
**Refuter:** a returned measurement `o′` whose source is demonstrably a specific beyond-horizon slot and not any in-horizon process. If one arrives, `R` closes for that slot and this finding is retracted.

### F3 - Direct testability fails the proto-instrument rule

Fig 6 :32: "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something." Fig 6 :33: "Nobody specified a telescope from an account of Mars. A wandering point in the sky was already a reading, and every sharper instrument was built on the residual the last one produced." Fig 6 :34-35: "The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve. Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."

For a specific region beyond the particle horizon, nothing has ever registered at any resolution. There is no proto-instrument to sharpen. A proposal to "build a detector for what is out there" starts from a description, which the figure forbids as a route to a sensor. **Mode: specified** (a structural claim about instruments, not a claim that no future physics is possible). **Verdict: gap for direct test of D3. Triangulated** across Fig 6 and the cosmology literature's own admission (Scholarpedia, see F6) that an infinite whole "is not and will never be a testable hypothesis."
**Refuter:** an instrument already returning something that fails to resolve for that region, from which a sharper instrument could be built, or a demonstration that an instrument can be specified from a bare description and work. The first would move the verdict to reachable; the second would break Fig 6 :32 and require a re-census of what an instrument is.

### F4 - Absence of detection is not proof of absence

Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 9 :44: "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing." Fig 6 :27: "Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."

Negative searches for cosmic topology (Scholarpedia Luminet 2015; Copi et al. 2026) constrain which topologies remain, they do not show there is nothing beyond the horizon. From inside the frame, "there but unreachable" and "genuine absence" are indiscernible. Note the asymmetry: Luminet writes that "no physical measure can ever prove that space is infinite, but a sufficiently small, finite space model could be testable." So confirmation and refutation are not symmetric across the horizon. **Mode: general.** **Verdict: gap stays a verdict, not a silence. Triangulated** across Fig 8, Fig 9, Fig 6 and Luminet (physics/mathematics, low overlap with the figures).
**Refuter:** a sealed room, that is a frame that can be searched to completion. No candidate appears in any source read; the universe is not a sealed room because every observer has a different horizon.

### F5 - Underivable in principle and unreachable at this budget are different facts

Fig 8 :40-43: "Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions. Underivable: go outside. Unreachable-at-budget: the term is already in the closure and was not found, so the remedy is more search, not another atom."

The cosmology literature supplies the distinction physically. The particle horizon grows slowly; light from slightly more distant sources arrives over time, so some regions are **unreachable at this budget** (Ord's "eventually observable universe"). The event horizon, driven by dark energy with `w < −1/3`, shrinks the region from which new signals can ever reach us, so regions beyond it are **underivable in principle** by light. ScienceInsights (2026-09-27) states: "As the universe ages, light from slightly more distant sources has time to arrive, and the particle horizon technically expands. But in an accelerating universe, this expansion slows and is eventually overridden by the event horizon." The instruction differs: more search for the first, a different frame or a permanent stop for the second. Importing a new atom against a mere budget limit would add an unneeded primitive, which Fig 8 :43 prices above starting from primitives. **Mode: specified.** **Verdict: split. Triangulated** across Fig 8 and Ord / ScienceInsights (low overlap).
**Refuter:** a measurement of `w` showing no event horizon (which would collapse the underivable class into the unreachable class), or a demonstration that the particle horizon cannot grow. Both are ground questions, not arguments.

### F6 - Some global properties ARE reachable, with an anchor

Fig 2 :29-30: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."

D4 has anchors. Luminet (Scholarpedia, 2015): "the observable universe is an infinitesimal patch of the whole universe and, although it has long been the standard 'mantra' of many theoretical cosmologists, this is not and will never be a testable hypothesis" for the simply connected infinite case, while "a sufficiently small, finite space model could be testable" through matched circles and multiple images. Copi et al. (arXiv:2606.24886, 2026): "Whether cosmic topology is observable remains uncertain, but current and future data offer an unprecedented opportunity to probe the global structure of the Universe." Ryan (2024) argues the topological underdetermination is severe but not absolute. So *some* properties of the whole, notably topology and curvature, bear on in-horizon data and are testable in principle, even though no positive detection exists yet. Note the load-bearing asymmetry again: a finite signature can fire; an infinite whole cannot be confirmed by any measurement. **Mode: specified.** **Verdict: reachable in principle for D4, with no positive result yet. Triangulated** across Fig 2, Luminet (mathematics / cosmology) and Copi et al. (observational cosmology), low overlap between a figure about anchors and two survey-based reviews.
**Refuter:** a matched pair of CMB circles, a topological lensing signal, or a bubble-collision signature would confirm a specific global property (this refutes "no detection", not reachability). A complete survey with full sky coverage and a published likelihood that excludes all compact topologies at the relevant scale would refute the claim that this property is reachable for our universe. Either is a ground reading, not an argument.

### F7 - The horizon is a wall of the frame, not a wall in the world

Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom." Fig 6 :14 names IMPORT as "the only operator that moves the wall."

ScienceInsights (2026-09-27): "Our observational limit is not a wall or a boundary in space. It is a boundary in information, set by the finite speed of light and the finite age of the cosmos." And: "The key point is that the horizon is a feature of our observational situation, not a feature of the universe's structure. Nothing special happens at the horizon itself. There is no edge, no barrier, no change in physical conditions." The horizon is `closure(Γ)` in figure terms: search inside it adds no atom, and the bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs (another observer's sphere overlaps ours; Ord's "ultimately observable universe" is the union of spheres centred on reachable locations). **Mode: specified.** **Verdict: the "beyond" is observer-relative. Triangulated** across Fig 6, Fig 8 and Ord / ScienceInsights (independent fields).
**Refuter:** a physical discontinuity at the horizon: an anchor that returns a change in physical conditions exactly at the particle or event horizon. None is reported in any source read.

### F8 - "No last room" governs closures, not physical extent (strain named)

Fig 7 :6: "the limit: no last room." Fig 7 :30: "every room closes some of the gap; no finite number of them ever arrives." Fig 4 :33: "Which is why there is no top and no infinite regress." Fig 13 :90: "One chain is one route through a space that has no last route."

**Strain, not a conclusion.** These lines are about frames, rooms and levels. Reading them as "the universe must extend beyond our horizon" crosses from the epistemology of frames to a claim about physical space. The figures are domain-agnostic, so the mapping is permitted only as a *shape*, not as a measurement. After re-census: what they license is that the observable patch cannot be the **final closure of inquiry**, that there is always another frame to import from; they do not licence naming a physical region or asserting its existence at the ground. **Mode: general.** **Verdict: shape only. Single-source** for the figure lines themselves; the mapping to cosmology is a disciplined analogy, corroborated weakly by the canonical usage rule that the framework returns a shape, not a name.
**Refuter:** a derivation showing that "no last room" fixes physical spatial extent rather than the closure of a frame. That would require a re-census that breaks the figures' own domain-agnostic reading rule and is not available in any source read.

### F9 - Shape, not a name

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134-135: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" and "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

The framework can report that the observable patch is not a sealed final room (a shape). It cannot report *what* is out there: a bubble universe, a distant galaxy, a different constant of nature. Those are names imported from physics frames, and they carry zero evidentiary weight until a ground reading returns from them. *The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism: an instrument "cannot comment on the source", and "It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." We have no better instrument for a specific beyond-horizon region; we do have instruments (CMB, surveys) for global traces. **Mode: general.** **Verdict: shape survives, names refused. Triangulated** across Fig 9, Fig 13, the canonical README and the source essay.
**Refuter:** a measurement from a named beyond-horizon region that distinguishes its source. Then a name is licensed by the ground and this finding is narrowed, not erased.

### F10 - Verdicts are not pooled, and filters are not witnesses

Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

All sources in this run, including this simulated second seat, draw on one shared evidence pool. Agreement here is the pool talking, not corroboration. Tier, tractability and expert accessibility are budget filters, never verdicts. *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly." A famous cosmologist agreeing with the shape adds no anchor. **Mode: general.** **Verdict: no independent witness in this run. Triangulated** across Fig 2, Fig 9 and the essay.
**Refuter:** an independent frame whose closure differs and whose data were not used here: a survey with a different instrument and a pre-registered analysis, or an expert verdict recorded before seeing this folder.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the seat rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim of this solution is: *the observable patch is not a sealed final room; a specific beyond-horizon region has no anchor and is `gap`, while some global properties of the whole bear on in-horizon data and are reachable in principle.* It is refuted if any of the following fires:

1. A measurement returns with a source demonstrably inside a specific beyond-horizon region and not reproducible by any in-horizon process. This closes `R` for that slot and retracts the D3 gap.
2. A search of a sealed frame is completed. This contradicts Fig 9 :44 and would require the universe to be a sealed room.
3. An instrument works after being specified from a bare description with nothing previously registering at any resolution. This breaks Fig 6 :32-35.
4. A measurement proves space infinite. This contradicts Luminet's "no physical measure can ever prove that space is infinite" and supplies an anchor we declared impossible.
5. A global topology or bubble-collision signature is confirmed **and** shown to identify a particular beyond-horizon region's properties, which would merge D4 and D3 and dissolve the union.

Until one fires, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.
