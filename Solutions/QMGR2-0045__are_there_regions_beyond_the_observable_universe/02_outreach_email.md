**Subject:** One checkable claim about the edge of the observable universe

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (general on the shape, specified on the split, gap where there is no anchor):** the question "are there regions beyond the observable universe and can their properties be tested" is two questions with different grounds, not one. A region can exist without being testable; a signature can be testable without showing that a specific region exists. Under a figure-grounded reading of residuals and anchors:
>
> 1. Inside the horizon, observations return and the residual closes. This is where cosmology's ground lives.
> 2. For a named region beyond the particle horizon, our theories predict but nothing returns. The residual is unreadable, not zero. Direct properties are `gap`, and I am not claiming they exist or do not exist.
> 3. Some global properties of the whole, cosmic topology and curvature, do leave traces in the cosmic microwave background and galaxy surveys. Those are reachable in principle, with a real anchor. No positive detection exists yet.
> 4. A finite universe small enough to wrap around could be detected; an infinite whole cannot be confirmed by any measurement. Confirmation and refutation are asymmetric across the horizon.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I know what is out there, that the universe is infinite, that other bubble universes exist, or that the horizon is a physical wall. I am not claiming the framework settles any of those. The framework returns a shape, not a name. "Beyond" is observer-relative: every observer has their own horizon, so there is no privileged "our edge".
>
> **What would refute the output, written before this email:**
> 1. a measurement whose source is demonstrably a specific beyond-horizon region and not any in-horizon process (closes the gap);
> 2. a completed search of a sealed frame (there is no sealed room in this picture);
> 3. a working instrument specified from a bare description with nothing previously registering at any resolution;
> 4. a measurement proving space infinite;
> 5. a confirmed global signature that also identifies a specific beyond-horizon region, which would dissolve the split.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
