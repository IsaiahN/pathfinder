# Supporting derivation: partition, union, and the order of testing

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The single word "observable" is doing too much work. It covers the particle horizon (what has reached us), the event horizon (what ever can reach us), and global structure (topology) that may exceed both. Our derivation must decide whether these are one domain, a partition, or a union of overlapping conditions, and in what order they can be assessed.

## DOMAIN CENSUS (compact form, full census in `01` §1)

Atoms held: the six substrate terms (Fig 13), plus the cosmology terms already settled against the ground (FLRW, ΛCDM, curvature Ω_k, particle horizon, event horizon, CMB). Bonds held: the seven plus negation. No new primitive is available without import.

- D1 in-horizon: anchor = CMB and large-scale structure.
- D2 horizon: boundary, no anchor of its own.
- D3 named beyond-horizon region: no anchor, not yet a domain.
- D4 global properties with in-horizon traces: anchor = CMB maps and surveys.
- D5 verification: anchor = the ground, never agreement.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are "existence" and "testability" a partition or a union?

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

Test the overlap. Let E be "a specific beyond-horizon region exists", T be "a property of it can be tested". Four cases are possible: (E and T), (E and not T), (not E and T is vacuous), (not E and not T). T here means a returned observation bearing on that region. By the definition of the horizon, T requires a signal that has not had time to arrive, so T cannot be established for a specific region at all. But a global signature (matched CMB circles) can be T for a *property of the whole* without identifying any specific region. So E and T overlap in D4 and come apart in D3. That is a **union**, not a partition.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a single anchor settles both E and T for the same region, the union collapses and the two conditions must be searched as one.

### Q2 - Are the two horizons isomers?

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :117 also: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance."

The particle horizon and the event horizon are built from the same atoms: a state (space), a state (time), a structure (spectrum/ordering from the scale factor), and a structure (gradient from the expansion rate). They differ in *which limit is integrated*: past-directed from t = 0 for the particle horizon, future-directed to t = infinity for the event horizon. That is the ordered bond → applied to a different time orientation. Same atoms, different bond orientation, different behaviour: one grows slowly, the other shrinks under dark energy. They are **isomers** in the figure's sense. The flat formula "horizon" hides the difference, which is exactly the failure Fig 12 names: "the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not".

Consequence drawn in `01` F5: underivable (event horizon) and unreachable-at-budget (particle horizon) are different facts with different instructions, Fig 8 :40-43. Calling both "unobservable" is the flat-formula error.

**Falsifier:** if both horizons moved in the same direction under every equation of state, the isomer claim fails and the two are one quantity under two names.

### Q3 - What is the order of the testing chain?

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."

Dependency order for this question:

1. **Perception (D1):** something must return. CMB and galaxy surveys return. Without this link there is no residual at all.
2. **Vocabulary (D4):** from the returning data, terms for global structure (topology, curvature, bubble collision) become available. *Library of Babel* (opened): "With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."
3. **Objective:** state what would count as a trace: matched circles, topological lensing, a kSZ bulk flow, an azimuthally symmetric CMB feature.
4. **Inference to D3:** only now can one ask whether an unanchored region exists, and the honest answer at this ground is `gap`.

A reading taken at step 4 before steps 1 to 3 are settled is "a reading of nothing" (Fig 3 :22). This is why the derivation refuses to assert or deny existence, and why it reports the gap instead.

**Falsifier:** a program that obtains a return from a specific beyond-horizon region without first having any in-horizon instrument that fails to resolve. That would put step 4 before step 1 and break the chain.

### Q4 - Underivable versus unreachable: a two-track test

Fig 8 :41-42: "Underivable: go outside. Unreachable-at-budget: the term is already in the closure and was not found, so the remedy is more search, not another atom."

| | unreachable at this budget | underivable in principle |
|---|---|---|
| physical case | beyond current particle horizon, inside eventual horizon | beyond event horizon (dark energy) |
| figure instruction | distribute search, keep searchers independent (Fig 6 :49-50) | import, or record a permanent stop |
| anchor | CMB / surveys, deeper over time | none reachable by light |
| verdict | specified, open | gap for direct properties |

Fig 6 :53-54: "searchers drawing on the same evidence are one searcher wearing many names. Independence decides whether more of them buy more search or only more spelling." So a program of more surveys only helps the first column if the surveys use different instruments and different analyses. Reanalysing the same Planck maps twenty times is twenty spellings of one witness.

**Falsifier:** a measurement of the dark-energy equation of state showing `w >= −1/3` (no event horizon) would collapse column two into column one. That is a ground question, answered by data, not by further argument.

### Q5 - The bond that carries the derivation

The load-bearing operator here is the ordered sequence →, not the symmetric conjunction +. Writing the question as "existence + testability" hides the order: testability must be established before existence can be assessed, because the anchor lives on the in-horizon side. Operators table: "→ sequence, A then B; B needs A to have happened", with test "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes." Swapping the two conditions turns a grounded question into a vacuous one, so the bond is genuinely ordered.

This is the sense in which the derivation has an "isomers and order" structure: same vocabulary, two horizon isomers under a time-reversed bond, and an ordered chain from anchor to gap.

## CONCLUSION

The question is a union, not a partition (Q1). The two horizons are isomers: same atoms, opposite time orientation, opposite behaviour (Q2). The testing chain runs anchor first, vocabulary second, gap last, and a reading that skips the anchor is a reading of nothing (Q3). Underivable and unreachable-at-budget are two tracks with different instruments (Q4), and the ordered bond → rather than symmetric + is what carries the order (Q5). What survives re-census is a shape: split, ordered, observer-relative, with an asymmetric confirmation rule and a refused name for D3.
