## Wider literature that bears on the claim

The sources split cleanly by what they settle. That is the point worth carrying into outreach.

- **Causal limits (physics).** Ord distinguishes four regions, so "observable" is not one thing. The particle horizon is what has reached us; the event horizon is what ever can. They move in opposite directions in an accelerating universe.
- **Mathematics / topology.** Luminet gives the key asymmetry: the infinite simply connected case "is not and will never be a testable hypothesis", yet a finite compact topology can be tested by matched circles and multiple images. Ryan formalises the observational underdetermination even under the Cosmological Principle.
- **Observational cosmology.** Copi et al. report that searches have yielded no definitive evidence but that current constraints exclude only some topologies and parameter ranges, and that planned surveys could expand the detectable space. Negative results are constraints, not absence.
- **Philosophy of science.** Read and Le Bihan: a multiverse scenario is scientific when it leads or could lead to an empirical signature. Sourced from a different field from the figure text, so low overlap.
- **Human cognition / instruments.** *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a reading "cannot comment on the source", and the color case "was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." This is the figure's proto-instrument rule in a different vocabulary.

No source conflicts with the figure verdict. Where sources disagree with each other (Copi et al. are more optimistic than Ryan about future detectability), the disagreement is about budget and instruments, not about the figure-derived shape. By Fig 8 :40-43 that is the underivable versus unreachable-at-this-budget distinction, and it is recorded as a live parameter rather than smoothed over.
