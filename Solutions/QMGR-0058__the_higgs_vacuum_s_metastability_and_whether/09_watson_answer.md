## QUESTION

The Higgs vacuum's metastability and whether it could decay catastrophically. In effect terms: (R1) with the measured Higgs and top masses, is the electroweak vacuum the global minimum, or only a local one? (R2) if it is local, what is the nucleation rate of true-vacuum bubbles per unit spacetime volume, and would a nucleated bubble expand (catastrophe) or shrink (harmless)? Option A, treat this as one question and quote a lifetime. Option B, split the union, give each condition its own ground, and report the observable-catastrophe part as unreachable at this budget. A leads to a number with no anchor and a possible "safe" verdict that no instrument can support. B leads to a shape, four refuters, and an honest stop. Fig 9 :4-5: *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*

The figures decide: the question is a union, not one question. Classification is a threshold comparison with a measurement anchor (`specified`, triangulated). Calling the vacuum "safe" on the basis of a long lifetime spends the independence of readers on a claim no instrument can settle, and calling it "doomed" spends budget on an unobservable. Fig 2 :42 says *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* The cost is real but bounded, because the packet carries its own refuters and lineage.

- **Reversed by:** a stable-region mass measurement; an order-one bubble count in our past light cone; a potential with no radius where volume gain exceeds surface cost; new physics below the instability scale that flips the sign of λ; or any instrument at any resolution returning a failed resolution of vacuum decay.
