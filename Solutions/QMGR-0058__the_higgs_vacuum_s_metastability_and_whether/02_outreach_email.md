**Subject:** One checkable output on vacuum metastability, before any theory

> Hello,
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The question "is the Higgs vacuum metastable and could it decay catastrophically" is two conditions bound into one question, not one hard question. Fig 9 says to split rather than search when disagreements keep working well on different subsets. The two conditions have different grounds:
>
> 1. A classification: is the electroweak vacuum the global minimum? The figure-licensed shape is a threshold comparison, λ(μ) against zero, or equivalently the measured (m_h, m_t, α_s) against a critical surface, with the ground being the measured masses. The framework returns the shape of that comparison, not the number.
> 2. A rate and a sign: if it is a false vacuum, the decay rate is per unit spacetime volume, and a nucleated bubble is a cycle whose output re-enters as input. The sign of that loop decides whether the bubble expands (catastrophe) or shrinks (harmless).
>
> I am deliberately not quoting a lifetime. A number from a calculation is not a figure result, and the observable-catastrophe part of the question has no accessible ground: no instrument at any resolution is currently returning a failed resolution of a decay. The honest label for that part is `unreachable at this budget`, not "safe" and not "imminent".
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the figures give the instability scale, that they give the lifetime, that a long lifetime makes decay impossible, or that the true vacuum is a new primitive of the theory. It is a different arrangement of the same field. I am also not claiming that agreement among papers settles anything; most of them draw on the same tunnelling machinery.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a measurement placing the Standard Model in the absolutely stable region (λ positive up to the Planck scale);
> 2. a rate large enough that the expected bubble count in our past light cone is of order one while we still observe the electroweak vacuum;
> 3. no radius at which volume-energy gain exceeds wall surface cost, for the actual potential;
> 4. any instrument at any resolution returning something that fails to resolve vacuum decay.
>
> I wrote these before writing the answer. If any holds, I retract the relevant claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Full architecture (Medium, 19 min): https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
> - Public figures folder, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - The Higgs vacuum stability literature I actually read is listed with quotes in `10_source_ledger.csv` of the packet, including Coleman 1977 and the 2012 NNLO stability analysis.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** split your own residual into conditions before choosing a figure, write down what would refute the result before you run it, and state its mode (`general`, `specified`, or `grounded`). A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
