# Outreach draft: check the shape claim before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the two strains in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 6 are stated as open in the recipient's copy. The first contact must carry the residual, the shape claim and the refuter, never the framework's interiors.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about how variability and forced change combine

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read any theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question in effect terms.** Observed climate trends come from two asserted generators: internal variability (the coupled atmosphere-ocean system running without forced change) and the long-term human-driven forced response. Each alone leaves a residual. Variability does not carry a sustained centennial rise; a smooth forced trend does not produce the observed interannual and decadal structure, including episodes such as 1998-2012 when the surface trend departed from the forced trend while ocean heat content kept rising. What is owed is the composition rule that joins them, the scale at which it holds, and an instrument that fixes the weights.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The join is a cross-scale union compound. At global scale it behaves as a symmetric conjunction: a sustained forced gradient-over-time plus a chaotic residual, where the residual is the chaos term that re-enters each turn rather than a second trend generator. At regional and decadal scale it is an ordered compound, because the forced response projects onto internal modes and back. The global mean is a lossy compression of the regional field, so the round trip from global to regional and back has a gap that has to be measured, not assumed zero. The observational record is the ground; it settles the weights by triangulation across low-overlap measurement lines, not by the number of models that agree.
>
> **What I deliberately am not claiming.** I am not claiming that natural variability produced the observed centennial warming. I am not claiming that a single global mean temperature series settles regional attribution. I am not claiming that a large model ensemble is independent evidence just because its members agree; they share code and tuning, which is one evidence pool. I am not claiming that the interaction term is measured. This is a shape and a route, not a result.
>
> **What would refute it, written before this message:**
> 1. an unforced control integration, reproduced across independent models, producing a persistent centennial global trend of the observed magnitude;
> 2. the interaction term demonstrably zero at all scales, so the two sources form a strict partition with one linear model and independent errors;
> 3. the observational series shown to move with the expectations of the attribution frame, so the anchor updates;
> 4. a single global mean series at one depth and coverage shown to settle regional attribution and depth-resolved heat uptake;
> 5. swapping forced and internal terms shown to change the large-scale fit.
>
> If any holds, I will narrow or withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - IPCC AR6 WG1, Chapter 3 (Human Influence on the Climate System): https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/chapter/chapter-3
> - Deser, Phillips, Bourdette and Teng, Climate Dynamics, 2012 (internal variability and large ensembles): https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00382-010-0977-x
> - Hasselmann, Climate Dynamics, 1997 (optimal fingerprinting): https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s003820050185
> - Wills, Battisti, Armour, Schneider and Deser, Journal of Climate, 2020 (pattern recognition to separate forced response from internal variability): https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/mqh7j-b8e38
> - Gou, Liu and Wang, Tellus A, 2022 (mechanisms of the hiatus): https://tellusjournal.org/articles/10.16993/tellusa.38
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the figure set and the eight-step formula are public. Run them on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and an LLM simulation is not a ground reading. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
