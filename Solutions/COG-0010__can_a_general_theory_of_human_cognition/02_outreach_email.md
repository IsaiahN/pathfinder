**Subject:** One checkable claim about cultural variation in a general theory of cognition

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question I started from:** can a general theory of human cognition accommodate deep cultural variation without reducing it to individual differences? Stated as an effect, a theory built on one population fits its own data well, yet a live group-level residual remains: differences in attention, categorisation and reasoning that do not shrink with more within-group study and that are not always the sum of within-group individual traits. The two options are (A) hold an invariant kernel and let the variation live in the arrangement, or (B) dissolve the group-level signal into individual differences.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the figures say the kernel is the part that does not change with size: "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change" (Fig 4 :20). That licenses option A. They also say a set can be closed while its arrangements are not: "The set is closed. The arrangements are not" (Fig 13 :115). So the shape of an accommodating theory is: a closed set of shared cognitive primitives, with cultural variation living in the bonds between them, not in extra primitives and not in aggregated individual traits. Under that reading, two cultures built from the same primitives but arranged differently stand to each other as isomers: "same atoms, different bond, different substance" (Fig 12 :117). The framework also says capability is "a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone" (Fig 11 :39), which is why removing the cultural habitat and explaining the residual from individuals alone is not a simplification but a level error.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I can name the universal set. The framework returns a shape, not a name: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself" (Fig 9 :32). Any particular list of universals is a binding from the cognitive-science frame, provenanced as such, and ungrounded until cross-cultural data settle it. I am also not claiming that all cultural differences are irreducible; I am claiming that a general theory has the resources to accommodate them without reduction.
>
> **What would refute the output:**
> 1. No loop and naming rule can be written that works at both the one-person and the population scale.
> 2. A group-level construct is shown to be fully determined by individual-level traits, with zero group-level surplus.
> 3. A cultural form requires a primitive that cannot be composed from the shared set.
> 4. Two cultures differ in their atoms rather than in their arrangement.
> 5. An agent-only change moves the ground of cross-cultural cognition.
>
> I wrote these down before running the derivation. If any holds, that part of the shape is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
