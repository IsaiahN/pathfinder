## 0. The residual in effect terms

A "general theory of human cognition" claims a kernel shared by all humans. Cross-cultural work finds group-level differences in attention, categorisation, reasoning and social orientation that (a) do not shrink with more within-group study, and (b) are not always the sum of within-group individual differences. The residual is the **live group-level signal left over after a theory built on one population has explained all it can**.

The two options in the question are:

- **A (accommodate):** hold an invariant across scales and let the variation live in the arrangement of that invariant.
- **B (reduce):** explain the group-level signal as the aggregate of individual-level traits.

Effect consequence: A keeps both a general theory and a real cultural level. B keeps generality only by declaring the cultural level to be nothing over and above individuals. Fig 1 :13 says "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state", so a theory's good average fit is compatible with a live cultural residual. What an answer would look like has been written down and bound above (Formula, "Direction"), so it can accrue a residual like any other term.

## 1. R by arity, symmetry and scale, then the domain census

**R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Arity: at least two conditions (a universal kernel and a cultural variation) plus a third claim (that the second reduces to individual differences). Symmetry: the reduction move is **ordered**, from culture down to individuals, so it is a sequence or a comparison, not an identity; treating it as identity (≡) is an error to be tested, not an assumption. Scale: within one agent, across a population, and the membrane between (Fig 4). Test from Fig 9 :4-5: "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." The residual passes this test, so it is a **union compound** until split.

One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor. No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1, framework-internal composition | atoms, bonds, molecules, Γ, closure(Γ), levels | composition then settlement | the figures as metalaws, plus the ground for any measurement | partition: which compositions cross kinds, which level is irreducible |
| D2, the cognitive-science frame | candidate universals, mechanisms, the researcher's Γ | prediction and explanation of behaviour | cross-cultural behavioural data (Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it"; the question is about people) | partition or union, depending on which pairing of "general" and "variation" is meant |
| D3, the cultural / habitat frame | practices, languages, institutions, transmitted knowledge, environments | contact and transmission, valence relative to the residual | what survives, the ground | union: culture and individual compose at a boundary and the compound has its own loop |
| D4, the verification / outreach frame | expert readers | agreement and residual | the ground, never agreement | checking; in this simulated run all seats draw on one evidence pool, so independence is approximately zero |

**Partition or union.** D1 and D2 are partitions (legal composition, what counts as a universal). D3 is a union: a culture-and-individual compound, not a harder individual (Fig 9 :54: "when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again"). D4 is the checking seat, not a witness.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** The chain is ordered by dependency, not importance: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces" (Fig 3 :20). Descriptively: (1) describe R in effect terms before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); (2) identify which level carries the residual (Fig 4) before asking whether a theory is general; (3) split the union (Fig 9 :54) before composing; (4) settle first-order molecules before a second-order comparison (Fig 12 :104); (5) refuse the reduction term before minting an accommodation (Formula step 2).

## 3. Findings F1 to F10

### F1 : The question is a union compound, not one question (decisive for framing)

Fig 9 :4-5: "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." Fig 9 :54: "when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."

At least three pairings are bundled: (i) universal kernel versus variation in learned categories, (ii) universal kernel versus variation in capacity, (iii) universal kernel versus variation in habitat. These have different answers. The Library of Babel essay names the same failure: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"

**Refuter:** if the disagreement shrinks with effort and one rule covers every subset, it is one question and the split is unnecessary.

### F2 : The kernel is invariant across size (the accommodation is licensed)

Fig 4 :19-20: "The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."

A general theory can be general by keeping the loop and the naming rule fixed while letting the actors, currency and anchor of a culture differ from those of an individual. This is exactly what "accommodate without reducing" requires. Note the constraint: what crosses up is a **method**, not a recording (Fig 4 :23). A general theory built by aggregating individual recordings is the forbidden direction.

**Refuter:** if no loop and naming rule can be found that operate at both the one-agent and the population level, the kernel claim fails.

### F3 : Reduction to individual differences is a cross-level composition error

Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom".

To reduce a cultural construct to individual traits is to carry the individual-level recording upward and call it the cultural level. Fig 12 says irreducibility is a fact about a level, so a settled cultural molecule is an atom for what composes over it; refusing that standing is a level error, not a simplification. The world literature makes the same point in its own terms. Na et al. (2010, PNAS), read 2026-11-22: "We conclude that behavioral constructs that distinguish among groups need not be valid as measures of individual differences." And: "correlations at one level pose no constraint on correlations at another level".

**Refuter:** if a group-level construct is monotone in and fully determined by individual-level traits, with zero surplus at the group level, reduction succeeds and F3 is withdrawn. Na et al. is the test, not the authority.

### F4 : Universals as a closed set, culture as open arrangements (core)

Fig 13 :115: "The set is closed. The arrangements are not." Fig 13 :116-118: "Six members, and no amount of arranging produces a seventh: a new member would have to be a new kind, and composition only ever rearranges the kinds already held." Fig 13 :118: "infinite effort inside a finite reach."

Transfer of **form**, not of the specific six physical terms (see strain S1 in section 5): a general theory supplies a closed set of cognitive primitives; deep cultural variation is the unbounded arrangement of those primitives. This answers the question without reducing culture to individuals. The variation lives in the bonds, which are a different kind of thing from the atoms (Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording.").

**Refuter:** demonstrate a cultural cognitive form that requires a new primitive not composable from the universal set (a "seventh member" in the transferred sense). Then the set is not closed and F4 fails.

### F5 : Cultures are isomers over the shared set (core)

Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Two cultures built from the same universal atoms but with different bonds are isomers: the difference is decisive in behaviour yet invisible in an ingredient list. This is the precise sense in which a general theory can hold universality and deep variation at once. Isomers are a real, independently documented phenomenon: Wikipedia's "Isomer" entry (read 2026-11-22) states "Isomers do not necessarily share similar chemical or physical properties." That is a low-overlap corroboration of the figure's definition, not the source of it.

**Refuter:** if two cultures require different atoms rather than different arrangements of shared atoms, the isomer claim fails and only the weaker functional-group claim survives.

### F6 : Capability lives in agent-and-habitat, not the agent alone

Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone". Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."

Culture is part of the habitat in which cognition operates. Option B removes the habitat and tries to explain the residual by improving the agent in isolation, which Fig 11 forbids. Cultural evolution research agrees from a different closure: Kendal, Tehrani and Odling-Smee (2011, Phil Trans R Soc B), read 2026-11-22: "Human ecological inheritance is exceptionally potent because it includes the social transmission and inheritance of cultural knowledge, and material culture."

**Refuter:** an agent-only improvement that moves the ground of cross-cultural cognition (changes the cultural residual without changing contact) would refute F6. Fig 11 :71 itself records this as the live refuter: "an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim."

### F7 : Per-slot residual, never an aggregate

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 1 :13: "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state".

Averaging over individuals is how a cultural signal vanishes into a mean. This is a method carried across scales, not a new psychological claim: the slot discipline generalises from one object slot to one cultural slot. The general theory's near-zero average fit does not license the claim that the cultural residual is absent.

**Refuter:** if averaging preserves the live residual rather than destroying it, the slot discipline is unnecessary here and F7 is withdrawn.

### F8 : Levels are not copies; they have different grounds

Fig 10 :68: "Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between." Fig 10 :70: "If levels were copies there would be one lineage drifting and no branches. The tree of life is what a non-invertible transform looks like from outside."

If levels were copies, a single ground and a single set of individual traits would suffice and cultural variation would be individual variation writ large. They are not copies. The cultural level has its own ground (what survives in a habitat) and the transform between levels is lossy. This is why the group-level correlation in Na et al. can be r = 0.73 while the individual-level correlation is r = 0.04 in the same data: two levels, two readings.

**Refuter:** if the transform between the individual and cultural levels were invertible, one ground would settle both and F8 would fail.

### F9 : Independence buys the verdict; shared pools are one witness

Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."

A dozen studies of undergraduates at one university are one frame, not twelve witnesses. Henrich, Heine and Norenzayan (2010, Behavioral and Brain Sciences), read 2026-11-22: "there are no obvious a priori grounds for claiming that a particular behavioral phenomenon is universal based on sampling from a single subpopulation." Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." A convenience sample is a filter for the budget, not a witness for universality.

**Refuter:** if two cultural frames draw on genuinely independent evidence and converge on the same claim, that is corroboration at maximum independence (Fig 8 :50). If they share a pool, it is one witness and F9 stands.

### F10 : The framework returns a shape, not the specific universals

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence," (full line: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"). The framework can say what form an accommodating theory must have: invariant kernel, cross-kind composition, closed set with open arrangements, habitat-relative capability. It cannot from these lines name which cognitive primitives fill the set. That is a binding from the cognitive-science frame, provenanced as such, and it awaits ground settlement.

**Refuter:** a figure line that enumerates specific cognitive universals would overturn F10. None was found after the census.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required by the discipline recorded in the exemplar and by Formula step 2 (refuse before mint). A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument.

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. No loop and naming rule can be written that operates at both the individual and the population scale (breaks F2 and Fig 4).
2. A cultural-level construct is shown to be fully determined by individual-level traits, with zero group-level surplus (breaks F3 and the Na et al. test).
3. A cultural cognitive form requires a new primitive not composable from the universal set (breaks F4 and Fig 13 :116).
4. Two cultures differ only in atoms, not in arrangement (breaks F5 and Fig 12 :117).
5. An agent-only improvement moves the ground of cross-cultural cognition (breaks F6 and Fig 11 :40).
6. The group-level residual survives averaging unchanged (breaks F7 and Fig 1 :22).

If any fires, the corresponding part of the shape is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this answer is labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Strains and re-census

**S1 : Fig 13's six physical terms do not enumerate cognitive universals.** My first pass read Fig 13's six (space, time, energy, spectrum, gradient, network) as a candidate list of cognitive primitives. That is a **strain**: Fig 13 governs the substrate of any composed reality, not a psychological inventory, and forcing it into cognitive science would mint a seventh meaning for a closed set. Re-census: what transfers from Fig 13 is the **form**, "The set is closed. The arrangements are not" (Fig 13 :115), not the specific members. The physical six stay in physics. Retraction recorded below.

**S2 : "Culture" as one level versus many.** A single "cultural level" is an idealisation. Fig 10 :53: "A prohibition nothing can satisfy is a prohibition stated wrong" and its note that a rule no real occupant could meet describes an idealisation. Cultural levels are multiple and overlapping; the derivation holds for any bounded frame whose closure differs, and the census above treats D3 as a class of frames, not one monolith.

**S3 : Fig 2 :36 and the anchor for this field.** Fig 2 :36-37 says human judgment is the right anchor "because the question is about people", but also that instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure. Here the ground is cross-cultural behavioral data, and the reading instrument (the task, the translation, the sample) can be wrong. This is why F9 and the Na et al./Henrich sources are load-bearing. No strain after re-census: the line governs which anchor is legitimate, not whether a given measurement is adequate.

**S4 : Na et al. versus the question's premise.** Na et al. show that some group-level constructs are not reducible to individual differences; they do not show that no general theory is possible. A conflict was checked for and not found: the paper is evidence for the residual, not a theory that closes it. The verdict remains with the figures.

After re-census: three strains named (S1 to S3), one retracted reading, none hidden.

## 7. Recommendation

1. Answer **yes, conditionally**. A general theory accommodates deep cultural variation by holding the kernel invariant across size (Fig 4), locating the variation in bonds over a closed universal set (Fig 13 :115, Fig 12 :117), and treating capability as agent-and-habitat (Fig 11 :39).
2. **Refuse reduction** as a level error, not as a political preference (F3, F6, F7).
3. **Split the question** before answering it: universality of kernel, of atoms, and of mechanism are different questions (F1).
4. **Do not name the universals** from the figures. Record any candidate set as a binding from the cognitive-science frame, with provenance, and send it to the ground (F10).
5. Keep the pre-registered refuter in front of any outreach (section 4), and never pool later agreement as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
