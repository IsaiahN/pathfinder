# The Tether's answer on COG-0010

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the shape, not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading. No cross-cultural measurement here settles which cognitive primitives are universal.

## 1. The residual it answers

A general theory fits the population it was built on, while a live group-level residual remains: cultural differences in attention, categorisation and reasoning that do not shrink with more within-group study and that are not always the sum of within-group individual traits. Fig 1 :13 "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state" says a good average fit does not cancel a live slot. The question asks whether that slot can be accommodated without being dissolved into individuals.

## 2. The shape

1. **Split the question first.** It is a union compound, not one question (Fig 9 :4-5 "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."). Universality of kernel, of primitives and of mechanism have different answers.

2. **The kernel can be invariant across size.** Fig 4 :20 "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." A general theory stays general by holding the loop and the rule for naming what worked fixed, while the actors, currency and anchor of a culture differ from those of one person. What crosses up is a method, not a recording (Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down.").

3. **Universals are a closed set; culture is in the arrangements.** Fig 13 :115 "The set is closed. The arrangements are not." Deep variation is unbounded arrangement over a finite shared set. It is not a new primitive, and it is not an average of individual traits. Fig 12 :5 "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."

4. **Cultures stand as isomers.** Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Two cultural forms built from the same universal atoms with different bonds differ decisively in behaviour while the ingredient list cannot tell them apart. The weaker claim, where only a common substructure is shared, is a functional group (Fig 12 :124).

5. **Capability is agent-and-habitat.** Fig 11 :39 "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone". Culture is part of the habitat. Reducing cultural variation to individual differences removes the habitat and tries to improve the agent in isolation, which Fig 11 :40 forbids: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

6. **The signal is per slot, not the average.** Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Averaging over individuals is exactly how the cultural signal vanishes.

7. **The framework does not name the universals.** Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Any candidate universal set is a binding from the cognitive-science frame, provenanced as such, and ungrounded until the ground settles it.

**Answer to the question:** Yes, conditionally. A general theory can accommodate deep cultural variation without reducing it to individual differences, provided the theory is a kernel-plus-arrangement theory rather than an ingredient-list theory, and provided the universal set is treated as a closed generative set whose members remain a ground question. The accommodation is a shape, not a discovered list.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any particular cognitive universal has been identified. The set is open as a ground question.
- Not that all cultural differences are irreducible. Some are learned categories over a shared capacity; the claim is that a general theory has the resources for deep variation, not that every observed difference is deep.
- Not that Fig 13's six physical terms are cognitive primitives (retracted strain S1 in `01`).
- Not that the group-level construct is a second "mind" over and above people. It is a settled molecule that can serve as an atom for what composes over it (Fig 12 :104), nothing more.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement here settles `left(R,φ)`.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

Cross-cultural instruments that discriminate sources, not further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (carried reading), an instrument that reads a colour "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

- **Level test:** does the group-level construct have a stable correlation structure at the cultural level while its individual-level correlations are negligible, with translation and sampling controlled? Na et al. (2010) is the existing instance, not the final word.
- **Kernel test:** can the same loop and naming rule be written at the individual and population scales, with actors, currency and anchor specified at each?
- **Isomer test:** do two cultures share the candidate atoms but differ in bonds, or do they require different atoms? The latter kills the isomer claim.
- **Habitat test:** does changing the cultural habitat, with the individual unchanged, move the observed cognition? Fig 11 :70 "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."
- Standing falsifiers from `01` section 4, all pre-registered.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The universal set has not been enumerated by a source-discriminating instrument. Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence," applies to the missing discriminating instrument, not to a missing primitive. The derivation stops at the limit of what the available instruments distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Indiscernibility is not identity.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether's answer to whether a general theory of cognition can accommodate deep cultural variation is yes, conditionally, and the condition is structural. A general theory should be a kernel plus an arrangement, not an ingredient list. The kernel is the part that does not change with size; what changes between one person and a culture is the actors, the currency and the anchor. A universal set can be closed while the arrangements over it are unbounded, so cultural variation lives in the bonds between shared primitives rather than in extra primitives or in an average of individual traits. Two cultures related that way are isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, decisive behavioural difference. Capability sits in the agent-and-habitat, not the agent alone, so reducing culture to individuals removes the habitat and calls the remainder an explanation. The framework does not name the universals; it returns the shape any answer must have, together with falsifiers written in advance. The universal set stays open until cross-cultural instruments settle it.
