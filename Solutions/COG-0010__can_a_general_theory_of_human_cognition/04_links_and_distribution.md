## Wider literature that bears on the question

1. **The direct result.** Na et al. (2010) is the nearest published result to COG-0010. It shows that social orientation and analytic versus holistic cognitive style are coherent at the group level while their individual-level correlations are negligible (mean r = 0.008 for social orientation, r = 0.038 for cognitive style). This is the empirical form of "deep cultural variation without reduction." It does not by itself supply a general theory, and it is used here as corroboration of the residual, not as a verdict source.
2. **Universals are a hierarchy, not a yes/no.** Norenzayan and Heine (2005) distinguish accessibility, functional and existential universals. That maps cleanly onto the figure-derived distinction between an invariant kernel and an arrangement: a universal can be a capacity that is used differently rather than an identical output everywhere.
3. **Levels are local.** The Marr source and the OECS entry (opened via search integration; host returned 403 to plain-text fetch) agree that levels answer different questions and that a single function can be realised in multiple ways. This is the independent structural analogue of Fig 4 and Fig 12, and it is the strongest non-framework corroboration of the shape.
4. **Culture is habitat.** Laland et al. (2016) and Kendal et al. (2011) give the evolutionary version of Fig 11: cultural knowledge and material culture are inherited resources that modify selection. This makes it concrete why removing culture and appealing to individuals alone changes contact.

Overlap warning: Na, Henrich and Norenzayan draw on overlapping cross-cultural evidence pools and share collaborators (Nisbett, Kitayama). Their agreement is one witness, not three (Fig 2 :40). The Marr/niche-construction/chemistry sources are from different pools and are the independent corroboration.
