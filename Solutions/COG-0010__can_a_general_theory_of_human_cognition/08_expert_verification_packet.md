# Expert verification packet: COG-0010

**What this is:** a claim in a form a cross-cultural psychologist or theoretical psychologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so the reader knows what to look for and what would settle it. One-to-one use only.

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the shape, not `grounded`.** No measurement here is claimed to settle which cognitive primitives are universal.

## 1. The claim in one paragraph

A general theory of human cognition can accommodate deep cultural variation without reducing it to individual differences, if it is a kernel-plus-arrangement theory. The kernel (the loop and the rule for naming what worked) is invariant across size; what changes between one person and a culture is the actors, the currency and the anchor. Universal primitives form a closed set, while cultural forms are the unbounded arrangements over that set. Two cultures related that way are isomers: same atoms, different bonds, decisive behavioural difference. Capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone, so removing the cultural habitat and explaining the residual from individuals alone is a level error. The framework does not name the universals. It returns the required shape and falsifiers written in advance.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 : framework-internal; checkable by reading Fig 4 and Fig 13

- **Statement:** a general theory can hold an invariant kernel across individual and cultural scales, and the variation can be located in arrangements over a closed set rather than in individual traits.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :20 "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." Fig 13 :115 "The set is closed. The arrangements are not." Supporting: Fig 13 :74 "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."
- **Refuter:** show that no loop and naming rule can be written that operates at both scales, or demonstrate a cultural form requiring a primitive not composable from the shared set. The second is the "seventh member" test in Fig 13 :116.

### C2 : cross-cultural psychology facing; checkable against Na et al. 2010 and later replications

- **Statement:** the group-level construct is not the sum of the individual-level traits. A live cultural residual survives a theory that fits individuals well. The level-specific correlations can differ without contradiction.
- **What to check:** Na et al. (2010, PNAS) report that social orientation and cognitive style are coherent at the group level while individual-level correlations are negligible (mean r = 0.008 for social orientation, r = 0.038 for cognitive style); in five cultures the analytic versus holistic correlation was r = 0.04 at the individual level and r = 0.73 at the cultural level. Is that contrast robust to translation, sampling and task? Do later studies replicate or overturn it?
- **Figure context:** Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 10 :68 "Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between."
- **Refuter:** show a cultural construct fully determined by individual traits with zero group-level surplus, or show that the level contrast is a measurement artefact that disappears under adequate instruments.

### C3 : structural analogue; checkable by a theoretical psychologist or philosopher of science

- **Statement:** cultures sharing cognitive atoms but differing in bonds stand as isomers under Fig 12's definition, and the composition is ordered: settle individual-level atoms, refuse the reduction term, then compose cultural forms at second order.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom". For the order, Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces".
- **What to check:** is the isomer analogy a structural claim about bonds rather than a metaphor? Does the chemistry definition support it (Wikipedia, "Isomer", read 2026-11-22: "Isomers do not necessarily share similar chemical or physical properties")? Is the levels claim supported independently (Marr levels source, read 2026-11-22: "Marr's levels are local levels, which do not aspire to individuate a general structure of cognition")?
- **Refuter:** two cultures require different atoms rather than different arrangements, or a cultural form can be composed and ground-checked before the individual-level atoms are settled.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a universal list. The framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32).
- Not that every cultural difference is deep or irreducible.
- Not that Fig 13's six physical terms are cognitive primitives (retracted strain S1 in `01`).
- Not that the cultural level is a super-mind. It is a settled molecule usable as an atom at the next level.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here has been settled by a measurement performed in this derivation.
- Not corroborated by agreement among the cross-cultural sources: Na, Henrich and Norenzayan share an evidence pool and several collaborators (Fig 2 :40).

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Level test:** a cross-cultural instrument that discriminates a group-level effect from an individual-level one under controlled translation and sampling, with a pre-registered criterion for "not reducible".
2. **Kernel test:** an explicit statement of the invariant loop and naming rule at both scales, with actors, currency and anchor named at each. If either level cannot name an anchor, it is not yet a domain.
3. **Isomer test:** a case where two cultures demonstrably share the candidate atoms and differ only in arrangement. A case requiring different atoms refutes the claim.
4. **Habitat test:** change the cultural habitat with the individual held fixed and observe whether cognition moves. Fig 11 :70 "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."
5. Any of the six pre-registered refuters in `01` section 4 firing.
