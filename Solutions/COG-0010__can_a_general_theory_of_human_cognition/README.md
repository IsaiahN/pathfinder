## Question restated as an effect

A general theory claims a kernel of cognitive machinery shared by all humans. Cross-cultural data show systematic group-level differences that do not shrink with further within-group study and that are not always the sum of within-group individual traits. The residual is the live group-level signal that remains after a theory built on one population has explained everything it can. Two options are being decided. Option A: accommodate the variation by holding the kernel invariant and letting the arrangement vary. Option B: dissolve the group-level signal into individual differences. Each leads somewhere different. A keeps a general theory and a real cultural level. B keeps a general theory only by denying the thing the residual names. An answer would look like a composition that preserves an invariant across scales while admitting a variation that is not the aggregate of within-scale variation. This folder binds that shape as an ordinary term and tests it (Formula, "Direction").
