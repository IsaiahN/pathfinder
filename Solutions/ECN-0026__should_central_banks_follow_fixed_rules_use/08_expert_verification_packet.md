**What this is:** a claim in a form a monetary economist or central-bank researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so the reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A central bank's error is a vector over mandate slots (inflation, activity, financial stability), not a scalar. A single headline target is a projection that hides a live slot residual. The outcome data the bank ranges against must be anchor-like and must not update on the bank's beliefs. A rule is a convention whose coordinating value depends on it remaining violable and checkable; a rule that decides the outcome regardless of the data stops being a convention and becomes an authored constant. Discretion is the narrow office of keeping the channel to the ground live and importing new information under a source gate. Coordination and adaptation are two loops, not one averaged hybrid, and neither is senior. The framework does not name a numerical rule, target or escape clause.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1 and 2

- **Statement:** R must be read per mandate slot, and the outcome data must not update on the bank's beliefs. A bank cannot score itself by rule-compliance.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 1 :27 "A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." Fig 2 :33 "The anchor must not update."
- **What to check:** is the slot-wise indexing a correct reading of the mandate, and is there an institutional arrangement in which the target did move because the bank wanted it to move while the outcome series failed to register it?
- **Refuter:** a single aggregate target that preserves every live slot residual, or a bank that updates its own anchor by its own beliefs and still stays anchored, or a bank that scores itself by rule-compliance without losing contact.

### C2 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 6, 8, 9 and 10

- **Statement:** the rule is a violable convention and the averaged hybrid is a collapse. Imports need provenance and a shadow test.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"; Fig 10 :19 "install what can be violated"; Fig 8 :4 "collapse 4: the blend"; Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."; Fig 6 :24 "Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."
- **What to check:** does the institutional record support the claim that escape and review clauses are conditions of a rule's coordinating value, and is there a case where a single averaged hybrid tracked every slot residual under novelty?
- **Refuter:** a rule that cannot be violated, refused or checked that nevertheless remains a convention; or a single averaged hybrid that tracks every slot residual as well as the unbundled pair under a shock absent from its model.

### C3 - structural derivation; checkable by reading Figure 12 and the Operators table

- **Statement:** a rule is a sequence, the target is a comparison, progress is a subtraction, and a rule-only vocabulary cannot refuse one of its own rules without the unary negation operator, which is not one of the seven bonds.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :112 "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction." Fig 12 amendment: "A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate." Also: "Without a unary operator a system can write what a situation contains, what follows what, and how far along it is" (the line continues with the refusal claim). Operators table :77-78 "What a unary operator costs is open: an atom slot, nothing, or the choice of what to negate."
- **What to check:** is the bond typing correct, and can a rule-only framework refuse a held rule using only binary bonds?
- **Refuter:** a rule vocabulary that states its own goal and progress using sequence alone, or a rule-only framework that refuses a held rule without negation or any external operator.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that rules win, or that discretion wins, or that the two should be averaged.
- Not a Taylor rule, an inflation target, a coefficient set, or a specific escape clause. The framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself").
- Not that a rule is bad. Its defect is unviolability or being treated as the verdict, not its existence.
- Not that discretion is freedom from the anchor. It is channel maintenance plus gated import.
- Not `grounded`. No regime comparison was measured here.
- Not that the framework's figure lines settle an empirical question about which regime performs better. They constrain the shape of an answer; the ground decides the answer.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. The most checkable three:

1. **Slot-resolved record:** a case where a live residual on one slot coexisted with a met headline target, and which arrangement detected it first.
2. **Novelty case:** a shock absent from the committee's model at the time; which actions were reachable, and was the import source-gated.
