# Outreach draft: check one shape before deciding about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the stage-1 output below survives the reader's check. The offer collapses on contact if the shape does not hold up without the theory.

---

**Subject:** One claim about policy rules, and what would refute it

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one structural claim about the rules-versus-discretion question that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, stated in effect terms.** A central bank's error is not one number. It is a gap measured per mandate slot: inflation, real activity or employment, and financial stability. A single headline target is a projection of those slots. The figures' indexing rule is that a global residual near zero with one live slot is a legal state, and that aggregating across slots is how the live signal disappears into an average. So the first claim is: read the error per slot, never as one scalar.
>
> **The shape (`specified`, not `grounded`).** Four clauses:
>
> 1. The outcome data the bank ranges against must be anchor-like: they must not change their answer because the bank changed its belief. If the target moves when the bank pushes on it, it has stopped being an anchor.
> 2. A rule is a convention, not an anchor. Its value is a shared meaning that coordinates expectations. But a convention that decides the outcome regardless of the data has stopped being a convention and has become a constant carrying authority. So a rule must be violable and checkable: published exceptions and review clauses are conditions of it remaining a convention.
> 3. Discretion has a narrow structural job: it keeps the channel to the ground live when readings go stale or get pooled, and it imports new information under a source gate. It is not freedom from the anchor.
> 4. Coordination and adaptation are two loops, not one averaged hybrid. A blended rule that swallows novelty to the mean is a collapse in the framework's own vocabulary.
>
> **What would refute it (written down before this run):**
>
> 1. A single aggregate target that preserves every live slot residual.
> 2. A fixed rule that reaches the correct action for a shock whose description is absent from its model, with no import from another frame.
> 3. A rule that cannot be violated, refused or checked that nevertheless remains a convention rather than an authored constant.
> 4. A bank that updates its own anchor by its own beliefs and still stays anchored.
> 5. A single averaged hybrid that tracks every slot residual as well as the two unbundled loops, including under novelty.
>
> If any of those holds, I retract the corresponding clause. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not deriving a Taylor rule, an inflation target number, a specific escape clause, or any institutional design. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Those parameters are bindings from the economics frame, and until they carry provenance and a shadow test they have zero evidentiary weight here. I am also not claiming the rule is bad or that discretion is good. The question is misposed if it demands one winner.
>
> **If the shape survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Public figures repository: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Monetary policy rules literature actually read this session: Taylor rule overview https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_rule ; time inconsistency and commitment devices https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamic_inconsistency ; Goodhart's law https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodhart%27s_law ; Svensson on targeting rules https://www.nber.org/papers/w9421
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is at `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`. Run it on a policy problem you know well and can check against data. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so outputs can be contrived to fit. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks for reading,
> [name / role]

## Conduct rules

- One recipient at a time. No distribution lists, no automation, no misleading subject lines.
- Record the stage-1 verdict **before** any theory is shown. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
- An opt-out line in every message. Public institutional contact forms are public, not confidential; do not send anything you need protected.
- A refutation is a successful outcome. A conversation is not a witness.
