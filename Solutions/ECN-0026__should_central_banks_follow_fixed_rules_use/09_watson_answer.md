## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the choice among a fixed policy rule, discretion, and a combination. Option A (fixed rule) buys a stable shared meaning for expectations and blocks drift in the reaction function, but has bounded reach when a shock lies outside the bank's model. Option B (discretion) keeps the channel to the ground live and permits gated imports of new information, but drifts coherently if the target itself becomes movable. Option C (combine) is ambiguous: it can mean an unbundled pair of loops, or a single averaged hybrid. What each leads to: A risks an authored constant that decides outcomes without the ground; B risks a movable anchor; C as an average is a blend collapse, while C as two unbundled loops is the figure-licensed shape.

- **Domain 1, the bank's controller.** Actors: committee, model Γ, belief b, instrument a. Currency: prediction Γ(b,a), slot residual R_s. Anchor: measured outcomes, which must not move with the bank's belief. Question type: control-loop boundary.
- **Domain 2, the economy as ground.** Actors: households, firms, banks, prices. Currency: observed inflation, output, financial conditions. Anchor: the data series themselves. Question type: ground, not frame.
- **Domain 3, the expectations convention.** Actors: households, firms and markets forming expectations of the rule. Currency: shared meaning and coordination. Anchor: the convention's checkability, not a verdict about the world. Question type: convention.
- **Domain 4, novelty and regime change.** Actors: shocks, new instruments, balance sheets. Currency: residuals the current Γ cannot compose. Anchor: a frame whose closure differs, reached by import. Question type: import boundary.
- **Order by dependency.** Perception, then vocabulary, then objective, then planning. Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." The rule sits at planning. Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the three-way menu does not. The figures return a settlement with four clauses: read R per slot; keep the outcome data anchor-like and non-updating; keep the rule a violable, checkable convention; and treat discretion as channel maintenance plus gated import. Coordination and adaptation are separate loops, not an average, and neither is senior. No numerical rule, target or escape clause is figure-derived. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, seven written refuters and the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is sending a recording rather than a generator and spending the independence of outside readers, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The four clauses can be refuted one at a time without touching the others, and no primitive has to be withdrawn. A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet. The unbundled loops can be recombined if a ground reading shows the average performs as well.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, never agreement. A slot-resolved outcome record showing a blended rule detecting a live residual before the unbundled loops would reverse F6. A documented case of a fixed rule reaching a shock absent from its model with no import would reverse F2. A rule that cannot be violated yet retains its coordinating value would reverse F4. A bank that updates its own anchor and stays anchored would reverse F5. A derivation of the numerical parameters from the figures, which the canonical rule forbids, would be needed to move any name from binding to result. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as candidate.
