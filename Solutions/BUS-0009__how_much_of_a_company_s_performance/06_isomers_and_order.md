# Union, effect classes as isomers, and dependency order

Three sub-questions are owed: (1) is this a partition or a union, and what follows for the arithmetic of percentages; (2) are the competing "effect classes" isomers under Fig 12, so that their disagreement is about bonds rather than ingredients; (3) in what dependency order should an attribution be attempted, and which link currently has no instrument.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Partition or union?

- Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- A firm-slot is always in at least two of the three conditions at once: a choice is made inside a habitat and at a time. By Fig 9 :57, *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*; it is a specific compound.
- Therefore percentages over the three sources are not additive in the ordinary sense. There is a shared fraction. Ecology names it directly: the shared variation *"cannot be decided to which of them should be attributed, and is a result of correlation between both variables"* (Zelený 2026).
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** a decomposition of a named performance measure into the three sources with no unassignable shared term, stable under the addition of a fourth overlapping condition. If it is produced, the union reading fails for that measure.

Result: **union**, triangulated across Fig 9 and a genuinely low-overlap field (community ecology). `specified` for firms, `general` for the arithmetic.

### Q2 - Are the competing effect classes isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."*
- Rumelt (1991) and Vedula et al (2026) hold the same ingredients: firm/business-unit, industry, year, and a corporate-parent or neighborhood term. They differ in the bonds: stable-versus-transitory splits, nested ANOVA versus covariance models, with or without an interaction term.
- Under Fig 12 they are therefore **isomers**, not variants of one estimate: same atoms, different bond, different substance. That is why the numbers disagree without either study being wrong about its own object.
- The weaker reading is also available: they are members of one **functional group**, *"A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* (Fig 12 :124). The group is "variance decomposition"; the qualifier is the bond set.
- **Falsifier:** two models with the same effect classes and different bonds producing the same per-slot attribution, so that the bond carries no information. If shown, the isomer reading fails and the functional-group reading stands alone.

Result: **isomer claim `specified`**, single-source for the figure definition with a non-independent business illustration. The illustration is not a second witness; it shares the business evidence pool.

### Q3 - What is the dependency order of an attribution?

The chain of Fig 3 is ordered by dependency, not importance:

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."** (Fig 3 :20)
> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** (Fig 3 :27)

1. **Perception:** can the firm read the market response at all? Instrument: realized return per slot.
2. **Vocabulary:** does Γ have terms for what the market affords? Instrument: not available. This is the middle link.
3. **Objective:** can the firm say what would count as progress here? Instrument: not available. Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."*
4. **Planning:** a route chosen under incomplete information.
5. **Learn and carry:** name what worked, verify it, carry it.

The "how much" question is asked at link 3, where there is no instrument. Most attribution effort lands above the break, on links 4 and 5, because that is where the measurements already exist (Fig 3 :29-30). Adding a fourth effect class to an ANOVA is effort above the break; it cannot rescue a missing objective term.

**Falsifier:** a validated instrument for link 3 in a named firm, such that the per-slot attribution changes when the instrument is applied. If none can be built after a genuine search, the attribution stays unreachable at this budget; that is different from underivable in principle (Formula step 7).

### Q4 - What order are the three sources in?

They are not a sequence (`→`) and not a production (`⇒`). They are co-present conditions in a habitat, so the correct bond is conjunction (`+`, *"both hold at once"*, Fig 12 :57) among conditions, with a comparison (`⋛`) between performance and its competitor threshold. Writing them as a chain *"management → industry → economy"* would import an ordering the census does not license (Fig 13 distinguishes ordered and symmetric bonds by their tests).

The time channel adds an ordered relation: conditions at t affect the reading at t+1, and *"each turn leaves a remainder (the residual) that re-enters the next"* (Fig 13 :31). So the attribution is a sequence **across time** but a conjunction **across sources**. Mixing the two is a type error.

**Falsifier:** a demonstrated case in which the three sources are ordered rather than conjoined for a named firm-slot, with the order recoverable from the market record rather than imposed by the model. If shown, Q4 fails.

### Q5 - Competing explanations and the source gate

Three explanations compete for the same reading: (i) a management-choice change, (ii) an industry-condition change, (iii) a macro-condition change. Under Fig 8 :50-52, *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*, so the record must state which explanation was derived from the residual and which was imported from a frame.

The empirical literature does not do this. It reports effect shares and leaves the source names attached by convention. That is an import with no source gate. The Vanneste meta-analysis states the consequence from inside the field: *"the observed effects are not causal effects."*

## CONCLUSION

The three sources form a **union** with an unassignable shared fraction. The competing effect classes are **isomers**, same atoms with different bonds, which is why their percentages disagree without any one of them being a measurement of a cause. The attribution question is posed at the middle link of Fig 3's chain, the one with no instrument, so effort spent refining variance decompositions is effort above the break. The correct bond among sources is conjunction, with an ordered relation only across time. Each of these carries a written falsifier.

This framing rescues what is defensible in the attribution literature - a reproducible filter that tells researchers where to look - without the part that fails: reading a variance share as a cause, or treating a frame-dependent percentage as a universal answer. No new primitive, no import that moves the wall, and no naming of a seventh source is required.
