## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation the folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :19/:22 (per-slot indexing), Fig 9 :54/:57/:60 (union, compound, filter), Fig 11 :39/:40/:46/:51 (capability as relation, habitat conditions, residual-relative valence), Fig 2 :29 (market anchor).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07_the_tether_answer.md`: the question asks for the wrong object; index per slot; the sources are a union; percentages are filters; the universal number is a gap.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the concrete business answer the question asks for: "management X percent, industry Y percent, economy Z percent".

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- **per-slot indexing**, which the concrete percentage replaces with an average across objects, exactly the loss named at Fig 1 :22;
- **the unassignable shared fraction**, which the concrete percentage forces into one of the three boxes;
- **the filter-versus-witness distinction**, which the concrete percentage erases by reading a variance share as a cause.

Conversely, two things survive cleanly: the union reading of the three sources, and the claim that management contributes only through contact. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the figures say the split is a filter" as equivalent to "the answer is no number" would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a measured gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` states.
