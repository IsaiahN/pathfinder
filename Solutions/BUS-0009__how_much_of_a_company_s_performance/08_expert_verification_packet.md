# Expert verification packet - the Tether on performance attribution

**What this is:** a claim in a form a strategy scholar or methodologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the firm claims, `general` on the structural shape - not `grounded`.** No source-discriminating measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "how much of performance comes from management versus industry versus the economy" asks for an aggregate over a union, so the requested object is not the one that steers a firm. The residual is indexed per firm-slot and per time, and averaging across slots removes the signal that says where to look. The three named sources are overlapping conditions, not additive parts, so a shared fraction cannot be assigned; adding a fourth condition to a standard decomposition moves the reported firm effect from 43.9% to 11.5%. A variance share is a filter for the budget, not a witness for the cause, which is why its practitioners disclaim causation. Management choices contribute only where they change contact, and their valence flips with the residual. The framework returns a shape, the falsifiers written in advance, and the instrument that would close the attribution; it does not return a universal percentage.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal and method-facing; checkable by reading Figures 1 and 9

- **Statement:** the residual must stay indexed per firm-slot; a global share discards the steering signal, and the three sources form a union with an unassignable shared fraction.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 - *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 9 :55 - *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57 - *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
- **Independent corroboration from another field:** Zelený (2026, community ecology) defines the shared fraction as variation that *"cannot be decided to which of them should be attributed, and is a result of correlation between both variables"*.
- **Refuter:** a percentage split with no unassignable shared term, stable under the addition of a fourth overlapping condition; or a demonstration that the global percentage predicts a named firm's response to an intervention better than the per-firm reading does.

### C2 - method-facing; checkable against the variance-decomposition literature

- **Statement:** a variance decomposition is a filter for the budget, not a witness for the cause. Effect shares from different bond sets are isomers, not competing estimates of one quantity.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :60 - *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- **What to check against the literature:** Vedula, Fitza, Purkayastha and Ellsaesser (2026, *Asia Pacific Journal of Management*) write that *"variance decomposition as a methodology does not pinpoint which underlying aspect of an effect class influences firm performance"* and report the 43.9% to 11.5% drop when neighborhood and interaction effects are added. Vanneste (2017, *Strategic Management Science*) states *"the observed effects are not causal effects."*
- **Refuter:** show that a variance share, with no additional instrument, identifies a causally active source and predicts the outcome of intervening on it; or show that two models with the same effect classes and different bonds produce the same per-slot attribution, so the bond carries no information.

### C3 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 11 and 2

- **Statement:** management choices are a lever changing contact, not a free-floating property, and their valence flips with the residual. Industry and macro conditions are habitat and time channel around one market anchor, not competing anchors.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :40 - *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Fig 11 :51 - *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* Fig 11 :46 - *"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"* Fig 2 :29 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
- **Refuter:** a management contribution invariant across a documented change of residual and habitat; or a demonstration that an industry or macro variable settles performance on its own while the market anchor and habitat remain unchanged.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a universal percentage, not "management matters X percent".
- Not that industry or the economy are irrelevant. They are habitat conditions in a union.
- Not that variance decomposition is useless. It is a legitimate filter (Fig 9 :60).
- Not a causal identification. The literature itself disclaims it.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by measurement.
- Not that the framework names the sources as new primitives. They are bindings onto habitat elements already in Fig 11, provenanced as such (see `05` section 5b).

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further aggregate. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it.

1. For a named firm-slot, which condition changed the market's return: a choice, a habitat condition, or a time-channel state?
2. Do two independent frames, with genuinely different closures, agree on that attribution while ranging separately against the market anchor (Fig 2 :35 - *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*)?
3. Does the attribution predict the outcome of intervening on the source it names? If not, it remains a filter.
