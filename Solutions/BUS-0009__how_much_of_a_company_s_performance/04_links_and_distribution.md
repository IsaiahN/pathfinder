## What the wider literature bears on the question

1. **The aggregate number moves with the frame.** Vedula et al (2026) add a neighborhood effect and a neighborhood-industry interaction to a standard variance decomposition and watch the "firm effect" fall from 43.9% to 11.5%. This is the strongest available empirical illustration of the figure claim that the three sources form a union, not a partition: add an overlapping condition and the share changes. It is not evidence that management does not matter. It is evidence that the share is frame-dependent.
2. **The method explicitly disclaims causation.** The same authors write that variance decomposition does not *"pinpoint which underlying aspect of an effect class influences firm performance"* and that they are *"not examining causal relationships of specific drivers ... but instead look at the relative importance of aggregate effect classes"*. Fig 9 :60 says the same thing as a metalaw: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
3. **The founding result already names its own limit.** Rumelt (1991) describes partitioning variance *"into industry factors (whatever their nature)"*. The parenthetical concedes that the effect classes are shapes, not named mechanisms. The result (*"negligible corporate effects, small stable industry effects, and very large stable business-unit effects"*) is a filter output, not an attribution of cause.
4. **The reviews say the answer depends on the frame.** Wang and Coff's review of the Rumelt tradition reports *"It seems unlikely that there is a single correct answer to the effect size question"* and that the answer will be *"it depends"*. The Vanneste meta-analysis states *"the observed effects are not causal effects."*
5. **A different field reaches the same structural limit.** Ecology's variation partitioning flags a shared fraction that *"cannot be decided to which of them should be attributed"*. That is the union clause of Fig 9 in a field that never read the figures, so it is a low-overlap corroborator rather than a member of the strategy evidence pool.
6. **A different field reaches the same salience failure.** Social psychology's attribution literature reports that people over-weight the visible actor and under-weight the ground, and that *"Causal attribution tracks what is visually prominent, not what is actually true"*. "Management choice" is visually prominent; industry and macro conditions are the ground. This corroborates the warning in Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."*
7. **The AI-policy constraint is real.** AOM requires submitted work to be created by the authors and not be a product of AI tools unless appropriate to the research question and properly cited. This package is a simulated derivation (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), so it is offered as a method note and a refuter, not as an empirical article. That constraint is recorded, not hidden.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22-25** - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down. A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and its refuter; offer the links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the entire framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** - *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the literature.
- **Fig 2 :40** - *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (with its leading clause *"disqualifying for a verdict:"*). A send selected for people who already agree produces a convention, not corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its five refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure, and record the answer; (3) only then the three links, with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

- **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52 - *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"* - and Fig 2 :45 - *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* The recipient checks the shape without holding the framework's interior.
- **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. Their verdict on stage 1 must be recorded *before* any theory is shown, and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
- **Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted.** Fig 4 :22 - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer.
- **Stage 3 still needs a ground per problem.** A frame *"cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it holds."* Each user must write down what would refute their result before running it, and label the mode of what they get.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. If a reader supplies one of the five refutations, that failure is copied into `11_audit.md` with provenance and re-evaluated by the auditor seat as if it came from any other source.
