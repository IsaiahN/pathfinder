## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical limit, from `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From the Library of Babel essay (quote carried from the exemplar mapping): *"You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

Applied here, the shape is:

> An apparent social reading is born in a corpus as an inherited weighting and a bond arrangement, is read through an evaluator's frame, is settled only at deployment contact, and cannot name its own source without a discriminator that is currently missing.

That is a set of constraints. It does not say "English Wikipedia," "individualism," "WEIRD annotators," or any other name. Those are bindings from world frames and must be provenanced as such.

A shape is not an answer-shaped object that can be filled in. Fig 13 :143: *"Indivisibility and minimality are claims and both are refutable"*; a claimed source is a claim and is refutable by the refuters in `01` section 4.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- Library of Babel (carried): use the vocabulary closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap with it, recurse. *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."*
- Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."*
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* (opened): *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."*
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"Ultra-compression is 'terms only we can read' as heard by someone without that history. The history itself can't be sent, but a description of effects can."*

So the first email in `02_outreach_email.md` describes an effect (a per-slot reading that flips when the cultural ground changes) and asks what a checker would expect to see, before showing any of this folder.

## 3. What would license a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* For this question, a source-discriminating instrument would settle which source produced a reading.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test. Measurement invariance and cultural-bias research are such frames; they must record where they came from.

From *They're Scanning Books When They Should* (carried): *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space."* And: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."* Naming a specific culture as *the* source would turn a visible choice into an invisible one.

### Correction to my own §1 and §3 (kept visible)

I first wrote the "shape, not a name" rule as if it forbade naming any cultural valence. It does not. The canonical phrase governs a **missing** primitive, one not held in Γ. The inherited prior `w`, the bond set and the per-slot residual are already in the figure set; naming them is using held terms. What is forbidden is inventing a seventh kind or declaring that the framework returns the name of a source. The correction does not weaken F12: the source of a particular reading is still missing, so the gap stands. Both statements are recorded so neither is quietly lost. This matches the exemplar's own `05` section 5b correction.

## 4. The decisive essay for this question: source, not behaviour

*The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened) gives the instrument argument that governs D4:

- *"The Problem: You can have several underlying causes that create identical readings but the definition of the output would be read as the same."*
- *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"*
- *"it cannot comment on the source"*
- *"The full set of ways to produce a reading is not known to be closed or determined."*
- *"the answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."*
- *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it."*

This is exactly the metamerism of an "apparent social intelligence" score: memorised cultural regularities, genuine perspective-taking, and a well-tuned evaluation pool can yield the same score. The score cannot comment on which. The remedy is an instrument, not a further argument.

## 5. Pretext is the shape of culture in a model

From *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* (opened): every message is understood through **context** (what is going on), **subtext** (what the words are for) and **pretext** (the history and assumptions the listener had before the words arrived). *"Culture is a shared backdrop, Collins's collective tacit knowledge. Its members can hold very different mindsets; what they share isn't a set of beliefs but the backdrop they read each other against."*

A language model trained on text has context and subtext in abundance and almost no pretext. It has the trace of a culture's readings, not the lineage that produced them. Fig 12 :107-110 says where the record is not carried, *"the result carries forward and the deriving does not, so every lineage pays again."* The model carries the result; the deriving stayed with the culture. That is the structural reason the reading is apparent.

The same essay supplies the drift rule: *"Two people who only check against each other drift, and they drift together, so neither notices."* Fig 2 :34: *"everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."* A benchmark and a corpus from the same pool drift together coherently.

## 6. Prestige is not action, and agreement is not a validator

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (opened):

- *"The catch is that the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."*
- *"You may all agree and be wrong. You may set your clock to someone else's, who was setting your clock to yours, and be off by many deviations over time. You need ground truth to define where you stand."*
- *"Humans in the 1800s had different values, customs, and cultures than humans of today. What does it mean to align an AI to human values when human values are a moving target? You cannot align to something that changes."*
- *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."*

The moving-target line is directly on point: "align to human values" inherits a cultural pretext, and the ground for "a good social answer" moves with the culture. Fig 10 :72: *"When the question changes, the room should change. A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom."*
