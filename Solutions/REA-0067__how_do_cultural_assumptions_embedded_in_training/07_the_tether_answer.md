# The Tether's answer: cultural assumptions and apparent social intelligence

**Mode: `specified` on the four domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on which named assumption occupies which slot. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading. Every verdict below is `candidate` until a ground pays for it.

## 1. The residual it answers

A system's social behaviour reads as intelligent to observers from one cultural pool and differently to observers from another. The question asks how the training corpus's cultural assumptions produce that reading. The four-condition union is: corpus bonds, evaluator pool, deployment habitat, and a source-discriminating instrument that is missing. Fig 9 :55: *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."* Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.

## 2. The shape, per domain

### D1, corpus and model: the assumptions are the prior and the bond, not a bias list

Fig 11 :36: *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."* The real object is not a standing property but contact. The Symbols table says the belief is *"Formed from its own history and from what it inherited, mixed by w. Not the observation."* Culture enters there, as the inherited component of `w` and as the bond arrangement of the corpus.

Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* A corpus report that lists percentages records the ingredients and omits the arrangement. Over a fixed ingredient multiset, differently bonded corpora are Fig 12 :117 isomers: *"same atoms, different bond, different substance."*

The model cannot see this from inside. Fig 5 :62-64: a frame holds its sought shape *"as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term. It is not bound, it predicts nothing, and it accrues no residual, so nothing here can see it."* Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*

**Verdict:** `specified`, `triangulated`. The assumptions change what the system takes to be the case before it acts, bound its reach, and are invisible to a self-audit.

### D2, evaluator: a shared pool is a convention, not a verdict

Fig 2 :18: *"one evidence pool"*, and Fig 2 :20-21: *"so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other."* Fig 2 :39-41 gives the valence: *"disqualifying for a verdict"* and *"efficient for a convention."* If annotators, benchmark designers and the corpus share a cultural evidence pool, their agreement fixes what a polite answer means in that pool; it does not measure capability. Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."*

Fig 2 :33-34: *"the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."* A benchmark that updates toward the model's score is panel 1, not panel 3.

**Verdict:** `specified`, `triangulated`. Independent evaluator frames, each ranged against a non-updating ground, are required before agreement means anything.

### D3, deployment habitat: capability is not in the model alone

Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Fig 11 :70: *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."* The two silent failures are what the habitat failed to reproduce and what was unintentionally introduced (Fig 11 :68-69). Gender Shades is the deployment-side demonstration: error rates up to *"34.7%"* for darker-skinned females against *"0.8%"* for lighter-skinned males, in systems that scored well on the substituted benchmarks of their day.

**Verdict:** `specified`, `triangulated`. A social benchmark is a substituted habitat; capability must be read at contact, per slot, with valence relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49, :51).

### D4, source instrument: `gap`, unreached not silence

The reading does not determine its source. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"* and the instrument *"cannot comment on the source."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."*

No figure line returns which cultural assumption produces which reading, or how much. The missing thing has a shape: a probe, improved from a worse probe already returning something (Fig 6 :32), that returns different values for different sources of the same social reading, without being authored by the frame it checks. The three formula step 7 questions stay distinct: **SEARCHED** (no such instrument in anything opened), **INWARD** (better per-slot logging; fails by refining an axis that cannot carry the source), **OUTWARD** (find a frame whose closure predicts the shape; cross-cultural psychometrics and animal behaviour are candidates). INWARD and OUTWARD are never read against each other.

**Verdict:** `general` structural, `gap` on the name, `unreached`. A source-discriminating instrument is owed.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that a system has no social behaviour. The behaviour exists; its source is underdetermined by the reading.
- Not that any named culture, corpus or annotator group is *the* cause. That is a binding requiring provenance and a shadow test, not a figure result.
- Not that "diverse data" fixes the problem. Diversity is a filter for the budget, never a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that a benchmark score is a ground. It is a substituted habitat and possibly a convention.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a ground reading.

## 4. What would settle it, and the pre-registered falsifiers

A source-discriminating instrument, not another argument. The spectrometer resolved the green-light case; the corresponding instruments here are:

1. A controlled corpus intervention changing the inherited prior at fixed bonds, with per-slot readings against a non-updating ground (falsifies D1 if no reading moves).
2. Two evaluator frames from demonstrably different cultural evidence, each ranged against a separate ground (falsifies D2 if they converge with no shared source).
3. A bond swap at fixed ingredient multiset (falsifies the isomer claim if readings do not change).
4. A benchmark whose outcomes transfer to two low-overlap deployment habitats without residual (falsifies D3's substitution claim for that benchmark).
5. A probe that returns the source from the reading alone (falsifies the metamer gap).

These were written in `01` section 4 before this conclusion. If any fires, the corresponding claim is withdrawn. Standing falsifier of the whole shape: an agent-only improvement that moves the deployment ground would break Fig 11 :40.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name a culture as the cause of an apparent social-intelligence score, and it does not claim a benchmark measures a property of a model alone. It says that "social intelligence" is a reading whose source is underdetermined by the reading: a green-like score that several different sources can produce. Cultural assumptions enter as the inherited weighting of the corpus and as the arrangement of its ingredients, which is why two corpora with the same percentages can behave like isomers, same atoms, different bond, different substance. The model cannot audit its own prior from inside its closure. A benchmark scored by evaluators who share the corpus's cultural evidence pool is efficient at fixing a convention and worthless as a verdict about capability, and a test harness proves wiring rather than capability because it substitutes one habitat for another. What the framework returns is the per-slot shape any answer must have, four separate verdicts, falsifiers written in advance, and a named gap where a source-discriminating instrument is owed. The source stays open until an instrument, not an argument, settles it.
