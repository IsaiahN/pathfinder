## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, the difference between what was sent up and what came back down. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 11 :36/:39/:40/:70 (capability and habitat), Fig 12 :5/:117 (bond and isomer), Fig 2 :40-41 (pool valence), Fig 1 :19 (per slot), Symbols `b`/`w`.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07` and `08`: a reading whose source is underdetermined; culture enters as prior and bond; a shared pool makes a convention; a harness substitutes a habitat; the source is a gap.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete audit request, for example: "measure the cultural bias of model M on benchmark B by adding more representative data and a broader evaluator pool."

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **per-slot indexing** collapses into a single benchmark score (Fig 1 :19, :22);
- the **pool test** is lost if evaluators are selected for receptiveness or drawn from the corpus culture (Fig 2 :40, :41);
- the **source gap** is silently filled by a named culture or a correlation, because a request for "cultural bias" invites a cause (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134).

Conversely, two things survive cleanly: the distinction between a reading and its source, and the requirement that capability be read at contact in a habitat. Those are the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the formula as identical to the full figure set would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
