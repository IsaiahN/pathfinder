## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, how cultural assumptions embedded in training data shape a system's apparent social intelligence. Option A: the benchmark score is a direct measurement of a property the system has, and more representative data plus broader evaluation will improve it. Option B: the score is a projection whose source is underdetermined by the reading, so the assumptions enter as the inherited weighting and bond arrangement of the corpus, are certified or hidden by the evaluator's evidence pool, and are settled only at deployment contact. What each leads to: A mistakes a convention for a ground and spends the independence of the checkers; B returns a per-slot shape, four separate verdicts, falsifiers written in advance, and a declared gap where a source-discriminating instrument is owed.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the standing noun does not. "Apparent social intelligence" is a reading whose source is underdetermined by the reading. Cultural assumptions enter as the inherited weighting `w` and the bond arrangement of the corpus, so two corpora with the same ingredient percentages can behave as Fig 12 isomers. The model cannot audit its own prior from inside its closure. An evaluator pool drawn from the same cultural evidence fixes a convention and cannot decide a verdict. A benchmark is a substituted habitat, so it proves wiring and not capability. The framework returns per-slot verdicts, an unbundled union, falsifiers written in advance, and a declared gap where a source-discriminating instrument is owed. It does not name which culture or assumption is responsible. Mode: `specified` on D1 to D3, `general` on the shape, `gap` on D4, not `grounded`, candidate throughout.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet is mode-labelled, per-slot and refuter-first, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is treating a shared-pool benchmark as a ground and thereby buying a convention while believing we bought a verdict. Fig 2 :42 prices the remedy: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the residual, the refuter and the pool declaration, and by recording evaluator verdicts before showing any theory.
- **undoable:** yes. The corpus weighting, the bond documentation, the evaluator pool and the habitat enumeration can all be revised without withdrawing a primitive. A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet. No figure line has to be retracted to undo this solution.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A controlled corpus intervention that leaves every per-slot reading unchanged would refute D1; two low-overlap evaluator grounds that converge without shared evidence would refute D2; a benchmark whose outcomes transfer to two substituted habitats without residual would refute D3; a probe that returns the source from the reading alone would close the D4 gap; and an agent-only improvement that moves the deployment ground would break Fig 11 :40 and the whole shape. Until one fires, every verdict stands as `candidate`.
