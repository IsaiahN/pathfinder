**Subject:** One checkable claim about cultural bias and social-intelligence scores

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about cultural bias and AI evaluation. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a social-intelligence score is a reading whose source is not determined by the reading. Two different sources, memorised cultural regularities and genuine perspective-taking, can produce the same green-like score, and the score cannot comment on which produced it. Cultural assumptions enter a model through the inherited weighting of its corpus and through the arrangement of the corpus's ingredients, not through a checklist of biases. Two corpora with identical demographic percentages but different arrangements of who addresses whom and what a silence means should behave, in the framework's terms, like isomers: same atoms, different bond, different substance. The model cannot audit that weighting from inside its own closure, and a self-report listing its biases is a check on the mechanism, not on the source.
>
> **The evaluation half of the claim:** if the annotators, the benchmark authors and the training corpus draw on the same cultural evidence, their agreement is efficient at fixing what a polite answer means in that pool and worthless as a verdict about capability. The same agreement does both things at once. A benchmark is also a substituted habitat: it proves the wiring works, not that the system can meet a real interlocutor who may be offended, may repair a misunderstanding, or may simply leave.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the behaviour is fake, that a named culture or dataset is the cause, that a diversity percentage fixes anything, or that a benchmark score is a ground. The framework returns a per-slot shape, not a name and not a number. I am also not claiming this derivation is a measurement. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency, and it cannot settle the question it reports on.
>
> **What would refute it (written down before this message):**
> 1. Change the corpus prior while holding arrangement and evaluator fixed, and every per-slot reading stays the same against a ground that does not update.
> 2. Swap the arrangement at fixed ingredient percentages, and nothing changes in behaviour.
> 3. Two evaluator groups from genuinely different cultural evidence, each checked against its own non-updating ground, converge with no shared source.
> 4. A benchmark score transfers to two different deployment habitats with no residual.
>
> A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources I actually opened. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Gender Shades, Buolamwini and Gebru 2018: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v81/buolamwini18a.html
> - Debiasing Word Embeddings, Bolukbasi et al. 2016: https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2016/hash/a486cd07e4ac3d270571622f4f316ec5-Abstract.html
> - The Hard Problem is About Source: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/the-hard-problem-is-about-source
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula and figures are in the figures link below. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

> — [name]
