# Expert verification packet: cultural assumptions and apparent social intelligence

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher on cultural bias in AI, cross-cultural measurement or AI evaluation can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the three sub-claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the named source. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. No model, corpus or evaluator was measured.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

An apparent social-intelligence score is a projection whose source is not determined by the reading. Cultural assumptions enter the system as the inherited weighting `w` of the belief and as the bond arrangement of the corpus, not as a checklist of biases. A self-audit cannot see those assumptions from inside the model's closure. If the evaluator pool shares the corpus's cultural evidence, its agreement fixes a convention rather than a verdict; a benchmark harness substitutes a habitat and proves wiring rather than capability. The framework returns a per-slot shape, separate verdicts for corpus, evaluator and habitat, pre-registered falsifiers, and a declared gap where a source-discriminating instrument is owed. It does not name which culture or which assumption is responsible.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1. The prior and the bond, not a bias list, carry the cultural assumption

- **Statement:** culture enters a model through the inherited component of the belief and through the arrangement of corpus ingredients. A two-frame divergence with identical architecture is expected.
- **Source, verbatim:** Symbols table, entry `b`: *"What the system takes to be the case before it acts. Formed from its own history and from what it inherited, mixed by w. Not the observation."* Entry `w`: *"Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge."* Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- **What to check:** is the distinction between ingredient multiset and bond arrangement real for the corpora you know? Does a demographic-percentage report omit information that changes per-slot behaviour?
- **Refuter:** a controlled prior change at fixed bonds that leaves every per-slot reading unchanged against a non-updating ground; or a bond swap at fixed ingredients that changes nothing. Either breaks C1.

### C2. Shared-pool agreement is a convention, not a verdict

- **Statement:** an evaluator pool drawn from the same cultural evidence as the corpus agrees efficiently about meaning and cannot corroborate a claim about capability.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :39: *"Correlation has valence."* Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* Fig 2 :41: *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"* Fig 2 :33-34: *"the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."*
- **What to check:** do the annotators, benchmark authors and corpus share sources? Is the benchmark updated toward model scores? If yes, it is a drift system, not an anchor.
- **Refuter:** two evaluator frames from demonstrably different cultural evidence, each ranged against a separate non-updating ground, converging on the same per-slot verdict with no common source. That breaks the shared-pool diagnosis for that case.

### C3. Capability is agent-and-habitat; the source of a reading is a gap

- **Statement:** a social benchmark is a substituted habitat, so it proves wiring and not capability; the source of a given reading cannot be recovered from the reading alone.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Fig 11 :70: *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- **What to check:** which actors in the deployment habitat are missing from the benchmark, and which were unintentionally introduced? What would a probe have to return to distinguish memorised regularity from perspective-taking at the same score?
- **Refuter:** a harness whose outcomes transfer to two low-overlap habitats without residual breaks the substitution claim for that harness; a probe that returns the source from the reading alone breaks the gap.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that a system has no social behaviour, or that the behaviour is fake. The source is underdetermined, not absent.
- Not that any named culture, dataset or group is the cause. Bindings need provenance and a shadow test.
- Not that "more diverse data" is a fix. It is a filter for the budget, never a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that a benchmark score is a ground, and not that agreement among friendly evaluators is evidence.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Corpus intervention:** change the inherited prior at fixed bonds; measure per-slot readings against a ground that does not update.
2. **Independent evaluators:** assemble frames with different cultural evidence and separate grounds; record their verdicts before any theory is shown.
3. **Bond swap:** hold the ingredient multiset fixed, vary the arrangement, watch the per-slot readings.
4. **Habitat transfer:** test whether a benchmark's outcomes survive substitution of the deployment habitat.
5. **Source probe:** build the instrument that returns different values for different sources of the same reading, from a worse probe already returning something.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :39-41, Fig 11 :39-40 and :70, Fig 12 :5 and :117, and the two Symbols entries. Six short passages.
2. Say whether C1's ingredient/bond distinction survives your knowledge of a corpus you have actually inspected.
3. Name one evaluation you know where the annotator pool and the training data share cultural evidence, and say whether its score was treated as a verdict.
4. Name one instrument that would distinguish two sources of the same social reading, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. No reply is needed if you would rather not engage.
