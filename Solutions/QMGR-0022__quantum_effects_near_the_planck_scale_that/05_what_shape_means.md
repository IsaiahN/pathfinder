## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question:

- `causal order is a scale-dependent composition residue` is a shape.
- `causal set sprinkling at density 1 in Planck units`, `linear Lorentz violation with E_QG near the Planck energy`, and `spacetime foam` are names.

The distinction is not pedantry. A name selects one closure and excludes the others before the ground has voted.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is *"closest"* to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"*.
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."*
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: *"the finish line will also be the starting place"*, with the paradox that by then you have *"composed the answer through the process of locating it."* For this question the stop is not a theory; it is the point where the residue is describable and the instruments run out.

## 3. What licenses a name

Two things, both outside the shape itself.

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* No Planck-scale ground reading exists.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs**, with source gate and shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52). Surya 2019, Mattingly 2005, Desai 2023 and Vasileiou et al. 2010 were opened and attributed in `10_source_ledger.csv`. They supply candidate shapes, not a settlement.

*They're Scanning Books* sharpens this: *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space."* And: *"A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six."* Also: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."* The six are a visible guess, not nature's own list.

## 4. Why "change of causal structure" is a projection question

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. An instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source"*: one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* What was missing was not an argument but an instrument: *"It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."*

Applied here: the low-energy readings (arrival-time lags, birefringence, dispersion, discrete area spectra) are projections of a source we cannot yet discriminate. Several different closures can give the same low-energy reading. Naming the source from the reading is the metamerism error. What is owed is a discriminating instrument, not a name.

## 5. Q: Does "shape, not a name" forbid naming causal sets or Lorentz violation?

**Answer: no, and the blanket form of that rule has been overstated.** The canonical README says the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* The subject is a primitive not held in Γ. Causal order (as sequence and comparison) is held in the bond set; discreteness and smoothness are residues the figure explicitly names. Causal set theory and Lorentz-violating operators are atoms already available in the physics frame. Naming them is a **binding** from that frame onto held vocabulary, not the invention of a new primitive.

What remains forbidden is claiming the framework derived which binding. That claim would confuse convergent derivation with adopted import (Fig 8 :50-52). Both claims are now recorded: naming is allowed with provenance, and derivation of the name is not.

## 6. Prestige is not action

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."*

A campaign that gathers well known names to agree with the shape claim is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask gives the claim a budget to be tried and lets an instrument, an experiment or a dataset settle it. This is why `03_public_contact_points.csv` lists venues and not people, and why no receptiveness column exists anywhere in this folder.

## 7. My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output"* and *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* *Arrival of the Fittest* adds that outputs *"could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula."* So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, this file and `09_watson_answer.md` are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are **not** tests of the claim, cannot settle it, and must be labelled as such wherever they are quoted.
