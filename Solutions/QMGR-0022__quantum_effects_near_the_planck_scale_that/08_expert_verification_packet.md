# Expert verification packet: the Tether on Planck-scale causal structure

**What this is:** a claim in a form a physicist or philosopher of physics can check. Modes, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the composition rule, `specified` on the shape, not `grounded`.** No Planck-scale measurement is claimed. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Smooth causal order is not a primitive of the six-term substrate; smoothness and discreteness are both residues a composition leaves behind. So a "quantum effect that changes causal structure near the Planck scale" is, in effect terms, a change in a scale-dependent composition residue. A structure must sit between space and time because two states cannot bond directly, but the six do not select which structure, so the slot stays open. Substrate discreteness and propagation symmetry are separate conditions in a union and must be unbundled before search. Their union is second order, built from already-settled first-order pictures; a closed causal loop is licensed by a cycle bond, with its sign left to the ground. The framework returns this shape, not the name of an effect, and the state is `unreached`, not `unreachable in principle`.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a theory of everything; no final room (Fig 7 :6, *"the limit: no last room"*).
- Not a derivation of causal sets, Lorentz violation, doubly special relativity, spacetime foam or any discrete spectrum.
- Not a claim that Lorentz invariance is violated at the Planck scale. The world sources disagree, and the figures do not adjudicate.
- Not a measurement at the Planck scale, not a seventh primitive, and not a unified field.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not a claim that the six are nature's own list. They are a visible guess, and declaring them makes the choice visible (They're Scanning Books, 2026).
