## Q1 - Are two workers with the same résumé isomers?

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** (Fig 12 :117)
> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** (Fig 12 :124)
> **"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."** (Fig 12 :92)

Two workers with identical listed atoms (same qualifications, same years of experience, same wage history) have the same atoms. If the bonds differ, that is, if one worker's skills are composed as a narrow sequence specific to one employer and the other's as a reconfigurable network usable across employers, they are **isomers** under Fig 12 :117: same atoms, different bond, different behaviour, with the difference invisible in the résumé. This is the figure-grounded version of "it is not what you know, it is how you can use it", and it is stronger, because the arrangement is itself a substance and carries information the ingredient list does not.

If the two workers share only a pattern (both do `skill → application → job`) while differing in which atoms occupy the slots, the weaker claim holds: they are members of one **functional group** (Fig 12 :124), the qualifier saying which member. The presence of the pattern fixes the kind, not the outcome.

The distinction is testable. A résumé comparison that finds no difference between two workers cannot distinguish isomer from functional-group; the bond has to be recorded. Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*

The Gale-Shapley result from the AMS Feature Column corroborates the form at a different stratum: a matching is stable when there is no pair who would prefer each other to their current matches, and *"there is always a stable matching"*. Stability is a property of the arrangement of preferences, not of the headcount. That is Fig 12 :5 in mathematical language, but it is a mathematics witness, not a labor measurement.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two workers with identical atoms and identical bonds nevertheless diverge in unemployment duration with no difference in ground contact or imported atoms, the isomer framing is insufficient and the difference must be habitat (D3/D5), which would move the explanation out of D3.

**Status:** `specified`, triangulated for the legality of the reading (Fig 12 and Fig 11 :50, different figures), single-source for the labor application. The world corroboration is the AMS stability result plus the Tidy Ecology finding that a single matching slope is *"a good summary and a poor diagnosis"*, both low-overlap mechanism witnesses, not labor facts.

## Q2 - What order do the conditions bind in?

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."** (Fig 3 :20-21)
> **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** (Fig 3 :19)

Order for a worker facing a changed labor market:

1. **Perception.** Can the worker read which vacancies exist and what they pay? Failure here is visible and has obvious tests.
2. **Vocabulary.** Does Γ have terms for what the changed occupation affords, and can the worker say what would count as an acceptable match? This is the middle link. Fig 3 :26: *"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."*
3. **Objective.** Can the worker state a comparison that would count as getting somewhere? Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* A vocabulary with only qualifications cannot state a goal.
4. **Planning.** A route to that objective under incomplete information: apply, train, relocate.
5. **Learn and carry.** Name what worked, verify it, carry it to the next search.

The four conditions map onto this chain:

- **C1 demand** is mostly a failure of the ground's pressure at the handoff (Fig 7 :39), not of the worker's links. It can break the chain even when links 1 to 5 all work.
- **C2 mismatch** is mostly a failure of links 2 and 3, the vocabulary and objective.
- **C3 wage adjustment** is mostly a failure of the comparison in link 3: the threshold no longer tracks the residual (Fig 10 :26, Fig 12 :112).
- **C4 institutions** is mostly a failure of link 5, because the worker names what worked but cannot import a new atom across a closed boundary (Fig 6 :23).

That mapping is why a single lever cannot clear all four (Fig 9 :55). The queueing source gives the same ordering in engineering terms: delay focuses on *"demand ... exceeds its capacity"*, and when arrival rate exceeds service rate *"the queue has a never ending growth"*. Effort spent telling arrivals to search harder does not fix a service-rate failure, which is Fig 3 :19 in operations research clothing.

**Falsifier:** a worker whose chain demonstrably works at links 1, 2 and 3 and fails only at planning, across many cases, would falsify the "usually middle" claim of F8 in the primary audit (though not the dependency ordering).

## Q3 - Competing explanations, and what each can and cannot reach

| candidate explanation | what it names | figure-line bearing | what it cannot reach |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Demand-deficient unemployment** | too few vacancies relative to workers | ground pressure goes to zero (Fig 7 :39); C1 | Why vacancy and unemployment can rise together; the Nobel 2010 page assigns that pattern to matching efficiency or structural change |
| **Structural mismatch** | skills do not fit jobs, possibly for long periods | bond and vocabulary failure (Fig 12 :5, Fig 3 :26); C2 | Cowen 2010: one overall negative shock can make good matches harder, so *"unemployment can look like it is structural when it is not"*. The name can be an effect, not a cause |
| **Sticky / efficiency wages** | wage slow to fall or held above clearing | comparison bond whose bracket stopped tracking (Fig 12 :112, Fig 10 :26); C3 | Cowen 2010: the search results *"do not require sticky wages or AD shocks"*. It is one condition, not the whole mechanism |
| **Employment protection / benefits** | firing costs, wage floors, benefit generosity | walls that only import moves (Fig 6 :14, :23); C4 | Wikipedia EPL: net effects on unemployment *"not identifiable a priori"*; the same rule raises search time and can improve matching |
| **Search and matching frictions** | imperfect information, heterogeneous partners, costly search | Fig 3 perception and vocabulary; Fig 12 isomer | It is the economics-pool name for the union's mechanism, not a derivation. It does not say which condition binds for a named worker |
| **Cognitive entrenchment of the worker** | expert schemas becoming stable | Fig 7 :36, Fig 10 :26, Fig 11 :49-51 at a different scale | Trinh 2019 is about individual experts, not unemployed workers. Fig 4 :20 requires the anchor to be re-named at the new scale |
| **Ideal free distribution / matching slope** | workers distribute across jobs by payoff | Fig 3 :27, Fig 9 :30: describe the effect before the frame | Tidy Ecology: the slope is *"a good summary and a poor diagnosis"*; 44 of 52 slopes fell below one, from several mechanisms |
| **Luck / unobserved shock** | an unearned early reading that persists while unfuted | Fig 8 :40: *"Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts"* | Cannot be excluded from inside a frame; Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Honest `gap`, not a rival verdict |

No candidate is discarded by prestige or familiarity. Each is ranged against the same residual in the order Fig 8 :53 requires. The point is not to pick one; it is to notice that the first five are all in one evidence pool (Fig 2 :31) and that the three low-overlap fields corroborate mechanism, not the labor-level ranking.

## Q4 - Does the shape generate the literature's answers?

Partly. The shape can generate:

- the demand emphasis, as C1 and the ground-pressure factor of Fig 7 :39;
- the structural and search-friction emphasis, as C2, the vocabulary/bond failure of Fig 3 :26 and Fig 12 :5;
- the sticky-wage emphasis, as C3, the comparison bond of Fig 12 :112 whose bracket has gone stale (Fig 10 :26);
- the institutional emphasis, as C4, the wall that only import moves (Fig 6 :23);
- the insider/outsider and policy-complementarity findings, as the valence flip of Fig 11 :49-51 and the union of Fig 9 :57.

It cannot generate the *rank* of the four for a named worker, or the name of the specific condition that binds next. That is the `gap` recorded in F10 of `01`. It is a gap at the instrument boundary, not a silence: Fig 13 :134 (*"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*) and Fig 8 :39 apply. The source essay states why: an instrument that reads a result *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"the answer is unreached until a better instrument exists"*.

## CONCLUSION

Two workers with the same atoms but different bonds are isomers under Fig 12 :117; two workers sharing only a `skill → application → job` pattern are members of one functional group under Fig 12 :124. The four conditions bind in dependency order: demand as ground pressure, mismatch at vocabulary and objective, wage rigidity at the comparison, institutions at the carry link, which is why C1 to C4 need different instruments. The standard economics explanations are mostly bindings of one evidence pool and are generated by the shape only up to the point where a rank or a name would be required. The isomer claim has a written falsifier; the order claim has one; the ranking gap is declared rather than filled.
