## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not the *level* of unemployment but *which condition, failing, leaves a worker-slot with a live residual*. In effect terms:

- **b** is what workers and firms believe about available jobs, required skills and acceptable wages, formed from private history and inherited priors.
- **a** is what they do: firms post vacancies, set wages, hire and fire; workers search, train and accept or refuse offers; policymakers set benefits, wage floors and employment protection.
- **Γ(b,a)** is what the economy's library of skills, contracts, institutions and expectations predicts: matches, vacancies, durations and wage bargains.
- **o′** is what actually happens: whether the vacancy is filled and the worker employed, and at what wage and duration.
- **s** is the slot: a worker, a vacancy, an occupation, a region or a demographic group. R is indexed per slot, never averaged into one national rate (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22).

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** (Fig 1 :19)
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** (Fig 1 :22)

The four candidate answers are four different ways a slot's R stays positive: no vacancy to fill (demand), a vacancy the worker does not fit (mismatch), a wage comparison that will not clear (wage adjustment), or a closure that cannot be crossed (institutions).

**Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term** before the run (Formula, "Direction"): an answer would be a set of conditions with one instrument each, a dependency order among them, and a per-slot, per-phase statement of which condition binds, plus a falsifier for each. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no loop check can see it. It is now bound, so the refuter in `01` section 4 can fire on it.

**Anchor test.** Fig 2 :29 names *"a market"* explicitly as an anchor. The anchor here is realized labor-market outcomes: a filled vacancy, a worker employed, the wage actually paid, the duration actually experienced. It is not unemployment consensus, not a single averaged rate, and not the theory's own vocabulary. Those instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure.

**The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md`:** the four candidates are a **union** of overlapping conditions, not a partition and not a ranking; each keeps a separate verdict, never averaged. The claim that any one is "mainly" responsible is a `gap` at the instrument boundary, not a hidden answer. Mode `specified`. No source was allowed to flip a figure verdict.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles ECN-0061, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of unemployment.

Four reasons, three from the figures and one practical:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35).
2. Correlation has valence. *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
