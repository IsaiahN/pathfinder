**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter are checked by the sender against the figure lines in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`. If the shape does not survive, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** One checkable output on GW quantum signatures, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "do black hole mergers emit detectable quantum signatures?" is four conditions with different anchors, not one yes/no question. Emission, propagation, detection and source attribution. The detector anchor returns a classical strain. It has never returned a quantum-gravity signature at any resolution. By the instrument rule that says a sensor is improved from a worse instrument already returning something, and that a sensor for what has never registered anywhere is unavailable, the detection condition is **unreachable at this budget**. That is a budget verdict, not a claim that such signatures do not exist. A search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence.
>
> **The shape:** any candidate answer must be a per-event, per-detector residual that sits above a non-updating noise anchor, survives glitch and template-systematic checks, survives the lossy transform from the source scale to the detector band, and is attributed to a quantum source by an instrument that discriminates among causes. A deviation alone is a filter for where to spend search effort, not a witness for a quantum source. The framework returns that shape, not a name for the mechanism.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that quantum black holes exist or do not exist; that Hawking radiation from a stellar-mass remnant is detectable with current instruments (it is about 10^-7 K against a 2.7 K background); that any particular echo model is the right one; that the null echo searches prove absence. Those are bindings from the physics frame or claims about mechanisms, and I am not asserting them.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. A per-event residual in released data that survives all systematic checks and is attributed by an independent instrument to a quantum mechanism at high confidence.
> 2. A quantum-gravity theory with its own settled anchor predicting a specific in-band signature shape in advance, with provenance and a shadow test, and that shape being found.
> 3. Hawking-like radiation from a stellar-mass merger remnant detected above the background and above detector noise.
> 4. A demonstration that the source-to-detector scale transform is not lossy for this quantity, so a Planck-scale signature could arrive unchanged.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the relevant part of the answer is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.

## Legal and practical note

The contact venues in `03` are public institutional submission routes and opt-in channels. No personal emails are used, no list is purchased, and there is no bulk send. Unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions, which is a practical failure on top of the structural one: a list selected for likely agreement manufactures exactly the correlation that disqualifies a verdict (Fig 2 :40).
