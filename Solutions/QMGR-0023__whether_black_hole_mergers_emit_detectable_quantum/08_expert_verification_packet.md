## 1. The claim in one paragraph

The question bundles four conditions, emission, propagation, detection and source attribution, with different anchors, so one yes or no is not an answer. The detector anchor returns classical strains and has never returned a quantum-gravity signature at any resolution, so detection is unreachable at this budget, not proven impossible. Which mechanism could emit a signature is a gap: the framework returns the shape any such signature must have, a per-slot residual above a non-updating noise anchor, surviving a measured lossy transform from source scale to detector band, and attributed by a discriminator, and it refuses to name the mechanism. Propagation is separately bounded by an independent electromagnetic counterpart. A residual by itself is a filter for where to search, not a witness for a quantum source.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

- **Statement:** no quantum-gravity signature has registered in gravitational-wave data at any resolution, so "detectable at this budget" fails at the instrument. The LVK GWTC-4.0 remnant analysis reports *"We find no evidence for post-merger echoes in the events that were analyzed"* and concludes *"there is no strong evidence for a GR deviation"*; the GW150914 tests report *"no evidence for violations of general relativity in the genuinely strong-field regime of gravity"*.
- **Figure line, verbatim:** Fig 6 :40 - **"What is unavailable is a sensor for something that has never registered anywhere, at any resolution. That is the whole of it."** Also Fig 6 :32 - **"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."** And the limit on what the null may be used for, Fig 8 :39 - **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**
- **What to check:** is the published null result a statement about the events searched and the models used, rather than a proof of absence? Does any released event carry a per-slot residual that survives the vetoes and is attributed to a quantum mechanism?
- **Refuter:** a per-event residual in released data that survives glitch, calibration and template-systematic checks and is attributed by an independent instrument to a quantum mechanism at high confidence. If that fires, C1 is withdrawn.

### C2 - Framework-internal and physics-facing; checkable by reading Figure 9 and the detector characterization literature

- **Statement:** a residual is a filter, not a witness. Attribution requires a source-discriminating instrument because several causes produce the same reading.
- **Figure line, verbatim:** Fig 9 :30-31 - **"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."** And Fig 9 :60 - **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**
- **What to check:** in O4a, 29 percent of GW candidates had overlapping or nearby glitches in one or more detectors (world source W5). Does the candidate signature shape discriminate among glitches, template mismatch, environment, detector quantum noise and a quantum source, or does it merely fit a residual?
- **Refuter:** a demonstrated one-to-one map from a signature shape to a specific quantum mechanism, with all classical and instrumental alternatives excluded by an instrument rather than by assumption. If that exists, the discrimination condition is satisfied and C2 is withdrawn.

### C3 - Framework-internal derivation

- **Statement:** the mechanism cannot be named by the framework, and the quantum-corrected waveform is an unsettled isomer of the classical one over the held atom set.
- **Figure lines, verbatim:** Fig 13 :137 - **"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."** Fig 9 :32 - **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."** Fig 12 :117 - **"isomer. If yes, a sourced import is available and the gap can be closed. If a corrected waveform needs an atom the classical one does not, the isomer claim fails and the relation becomes composition plus import.
- **Refuter:** a quantum-gravity theory with its own settled anchor predicting a specific in-band signature shape in advance, with provenance and a shadow test, and that shape being found. Or a re-census showing the corrected waveform requires a new atom. Either firing withdraws C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that black holes do not emit quantum signatures. The emission domain is `gap`, not `no`.

- Not that detector quantum noise is the same object as a source quantum signature. The first registers as a habitat condition; the second has never registered.

- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
