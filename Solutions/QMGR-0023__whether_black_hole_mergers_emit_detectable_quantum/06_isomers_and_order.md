## Q1 - The dependency order and the location of the break

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*

The chain for this question, read from the residual backwards:

```
1 perception      detector returns a classical strain, per slot            WORKS
2 vocabulary      terms for a candidate deviation (echo, dispersion, QNM shift)  HELD but ungrounded
3 objective       a positive live residual attributable to a quantum source  NOT REACHED
4 planning        search strategies: matched filters, echo templates, ringdown tests  RUN, NULL
```

The mapping is not a metaphor. Fig 3 :5 defines perception as being able to *"tell what is in front of it, and tell it apart from what was there before"*; the interferometer does this for classical strains. Fig 3 :7 defines vocabulary as *"terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked"*; the phenomenological parameters are such terms. Fig 3 :10 defines the objective as being able to *"say what would count as progress here, in its own terms"*; this is the link that fails, because no measurement has ever assigned positive mass to the quantum slot.

Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** More strain sensitivity optimizes link 1, which works. It does not create the objective at link 3. Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** This link has actually been looked at: echo searches and parameterized tests were run (world sources W1, W2, W4) and returned nothing. So the state is not "nobody looked"; it is "looked, nothing registered". Formula step 7 keeps those apart: SEARCHED has been asked and answered, and INWARD is permitted only on an axis already returning something (Fig 6 :32-33).

**Falsifier Q1:** if a search on the existing instruments returns a per-slot residual that survives discrimination, then the objective link is reached and the break is somewhere else. If a new instrument returns a quantity never before registered, then link 1 was the break and the chain must be re-read. If neither, the break stays between vocabulary and objective, and F3 of `01` stands.

## Q2 - Union unbundling, not a single lever

Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** Fig 9 :55-57 makes the consequence explicit: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."* A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either; it becomes a specific compound.

The four conditions and the lever that would clear each alone:

| condition | single lever | why the intersection remains |
|---|---|---|
| D2 emission | propose a quantum-gravity mechanism | an unregistered mechanism is still unregistered at the detector |
| D4 propagation | tighten a dispersion bound | a bound on propagation says nothing about a source-local emission signature |
| D1 detection | build a more sensitive interferometer | more sensitivity produces more residuals, not more attributions |
| D3 discrimination | model the glitch population better | better glitch models do not create a quantum source |

Fig 11 :40: **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."** A better glitch model changes contact with the classical habitat. It does not change contact with a quantum source, because there is no such contact to change.

**Falsifier Q2:** a single measurement or instrument that changes contact with all four conditions at once, so the union collapses to a partition. Until that is shown, averaging the four into one "probability that the answer is yes" is a category error.

## Q3 - Are the classical and quantum-corrected waveforms isomers?

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Consider two waveforms written over the same held atom set, the six-term substrate plus the detector bindings (masses, spins, distance, strain, noise floor):

- **Classical waveform:** the numerical-relativity template, with the inspiral, merger and ringdown composed by `→` sequence and the detector comparison `⋛` against the noise floor.
- **Quantum-corrected waveform:** the same atoms, with one bond changed (for example, a modified ringdown frequency or a late-time reflection replacing the pure absorption boundary).

As written over this fixed atom set they satisfy the figure definition: same atoms, different bond, different substance, with the difference invisible in the ingredient list and decisive in the observable behaviour. So the **isomer claim is licensed as a `specified` structural statement about waveform space**, provided the bond is specified. This is the analogue of the exemplar's finding that GR and QM are isomers over the full shared atom set.

But there is a crucial asymmetry. The classical waveform is settled against the ground (decades of experiment, anchored by GW150914 and following events). The quantum-corrected waveform is not settled. Fig 12 :106: *"Nothing composes over what was never admitted as a part."* A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it (Fig 12 :104), but an unsettled candidate cannot yet serve as a settled atom. So the quantum-corrected waveform is a candidate molecule, not a settled one, and the isomer relation holds in the space of candidate descriptions rather than in the library of settled terms.

Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."** If a catalog reports only the fitted parameters of a waveform and not the bond (classical ringdown versus reflected ringdown), it has recorded the ingredients and lost the substance. Nothing downstream can tell the two apart, which is exactly why a deviation alone cannot settle the source in F4.

**Falsifier Q3:** if either waveform requires an atom the other does not (for example, the corrected waveform needs a new quantum-gravity primitive with no counterpart in the classical atom set), the isomer claim fails and the relation becomes composition plus import, with the import owing a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 8). This is the live fixed-atom condition, named rather than hidden.

## Q4 - The two currencies again

Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."** Fig 12 :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**

A phenomenological echo model is cheap in bits: a few parameters over an existing waveform. It is expensive in actions: it needs a detector that has never registered the effect and a discriminator that does not exist. Fig 12 :128-129: *"A long chain of cheap parts is expensive to describe and may cost one action to refute. A short arrangement over a costly operand is cheap to state and may take a whole budget to settle."* The quantum-corrected waveform is the second case: cheap to state, a whole budget to settle, and the budget has been spent (W1) without settlement.

Summing the two into one figure is the named error (Fig 12 :130: *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."*). A catalog that reports "tight constraints on echo parameters" is reporting the bits accounting. It has not bought the actions.

## CONCLUSION

The dependency chain breaks between vocabulary and objective, not at the detector (Q1). The question is a union of four conditions and cannot be cleared by one lever (Q2). Over a fixed atom set the classical and quantum-corrected waveforms are isomers, but the corrected one is an unsettled candidate, so the relation holds in candidate description space and not in the library of settled terms (Q3). The two currencies of description and action do not add, so a tight parameter bound is not a settled quantum signature (Q4).

What survives is a checkable structural claim: **the quantum-corrected waveform is an isomer of the classical one over the held atom set, the observable difference is carried entirely by the changed bond, and the isomer is unsettled because no atom in the corrected molecule has passed a source gate.** This rescues the part of the quantum-signature idea that is figure-licensed without naming a mechanism or asserting absence.
