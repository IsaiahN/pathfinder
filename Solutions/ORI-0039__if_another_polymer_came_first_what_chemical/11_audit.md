## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed zero. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 12 :5/:101/:119/:121/:123, Fig 13 :115, Fig 6 :23, Fig 11 :39/:49/:70 and Fig 5 :69.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07` and `08`: a pre-RNA polymer needs storage, copying, cross-pairing, a habitat, settlement and a readout; no name is returned.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the concrete claim a chemist would want, namely that PNA, TNA or a specific sugar-backbone polymer was the actual first genetic polymer and passed information to RNA on the early Earth.

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- **per-slot indexing** (Fig 1 :19, :22) is dropped when transfer is treated as one global event rather than a set of sequence positions with their own residuals;
- **import provenance and the shadow test** (Fig 8 :50-52) are dropped when a laboratory transfer is read as a historical source;
- **habitat valence** (Fig 11 :39, :49) is dropped when a condition set is treated as intrinsic properties of a molecule;
- **the settlement gate** (Fig 5 :69) is dropped when a product band is counted as an initiated lineage.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: the arrangement must carry the difference and a closing cycle must regenerate it. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
