# Outreach draft: check the condition set before deciding about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** the condition set below must survive stage 1 on its own. If a reader needs the framework to see why a condition is necessary, the offer has failed and the reader's verdict must be recorded as a failure.

---

**Subject:** One checkable condition set on a pre-RNA to RNA handoff

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read any theory first, and I am not asking you to agree that a pre-RNA polymer existed. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** I am asking what a hypothetical pre-RNA genetic polymer would have to be like for a sequence difference in it to become a sequence difference in RNA that changes RNA behaviour. Treating this as a union of overlapping conditions, the necessary properties are:
>
> 1. a typed, reproducible linkage with at least two distinguishable states per position, so the arrangement, not just the monomer inventory, carries the sequence;
> 2. a finite monomer alphabet with uniform coupling, giving unbounded arrangements from a closed set;
> 3. a copying loop whose template survives its own use and whose pairing site discriminates partners with sufficient accuracy;
> 4. a physical complementarity to RNA that lets a sequence cross from one chemistry into the other, with a traceable lineage so a handoff is distinguishable from independent origin;
> 5. an RNA-side observable that can be measured independently of the original polymer, so "it was passed on" can actually fire or fail;
> 6. a habitat in which the polymer persists and is copied, with the valence of each of the above re-read per habitat;
> 7. settlement, meaning the RNA product is copied and persists, not merely produced once in a flask.
>
> This is a shape, not a name. It does not say PNA, TNA, GNA or anything else was first. Which polymer, if any, actually came first is a separate question for which I know of no chronology instrument, and I am reporting it as a gap rather than filling it.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** That any named polymer satisfies all seven. That a laboratory transcription of one polymer into another is the historical route. That base-pairing strength alone selects a polymer. That selection chose anything in advance. That this derivation is a chemistry measurement: it is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against a framework, and it cannot settle the question it reports on.
>
> **What would refute the condition set.** I wrote these down before running the search:
> 1. a single indistinguishable linkage at every position demonstrably passes a sequence difference to RNA and changes RNA behaviour;
> 2. copying capacity demonstrably requires new monomer kinds rather than new arrangements over a closed set;
> 3. a one-shot non-regenerating event passes information with no closing loop and still leaves a heritable RNA difference;
> 4. a polymer-controlled RNA behaviour with no complementarity, no pairing geometry and no lineage, after solvent, ionic and concentration effects are excluded;
> 5. the same condition set predicting equal persistence in habitats that differ in cycling and concentration;
> 6. a one-off transfer with no copied descendant counted as a successful start of genetic information flow.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract that condition. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources I actually read. They are secondary; the figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - The XNA alphabet, Chaput, Egli and Herdewijn 2025, Nucleic Acids Research: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12255307/
> - Information transfer from peptide nucleic acids to RNA by template-directed syntheses, Schmidt, Nielsen and Orgel 1997, Nucleic Acids Research: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC147122/
> - Synthetic life with alternative nucleic acids as genetic materials, Nie, Bai and Mei 2020, Molecules: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7435384/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link below. Run it on a problem you can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
