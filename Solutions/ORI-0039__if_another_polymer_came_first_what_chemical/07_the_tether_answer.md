# The Tether's answer: what a pre-RNA polymer would need

**Mode: `specified` on the condition set and on each per-domain verdict, `general` on the structural composition rule, `gap` on which polymer came first. Not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading and not a chemistry measurement (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## 1. The residual it answers

The unexplained handoff: a sequence distinction held in a hypothetical pre-RNA polymer P becomes a sequence distinction in RNA, and RNA behaviour tracks it. The question is compound, not one hard question (Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*). The seven conditions below and the separate priority question are different conditions with different anchors. One lever clears the region where one holds and leaves the intersections standing. Verdicts below are kept per domain and never averaged.

## 2. The shape: seven necessary conditions

1. **A typed, distinguishable linkage (C1).** Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* A flat monomer inventory cannot carry a sequence. Fig 12 :101: *"A bond that costs nothing is a bond that carries nothing, which is the flat case."* Necessary property: at least two distinguishable linkage states per position, reproducibly made.
2. **A closed alphabet with open arrangements (C2).** Fig 13 :115: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."* Capacity comes from unbounded arrangements over a finite monomer set, not from increasingly exotic monomers. Accuracy and uniformity of pairing are separate requirements from raw stability (world corroboration, XNA review 2025).
3. **A closing copying cycle with a surviving template (C3).** Fig 12 :121: *"The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Fig 12 :119: *"A part that survives its own use."* A template is a catalyst role; a one-shot event is not a genetic system.
4. **Cross-readout between two closures (C4).** Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* The handoff is an import in the figure sense. It needs geometric complementarity and a lineage that distinguishes transfer from independent convergence (Fig 8 :50-52).
5. **A receiver-side observable (C5).** Symbols table :40: *"what cannot be perceived yet is residual and simply cannot be read"*. The transferred difference must be registered in RNA behaviour measured independently of the P sequence, or the claim cannot fire or fail. Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
6. **A habitat that gives the conditions their valence (C6).** Fig 11 :39: *"And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* "Stable enough" and "useful" are relations, not intrinsic properties. The habitat is enumerated, not composed (Fig 11 :43).
7. **Settlement before composition (C7).** Fig 5 :69: *"settling is necessary for a term to be composed over, and it is not sufficient."* Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."* A single laboratory transfer proves wiring, not capability (Fig 11 :70). The RNA product must be copied and persist before the handoff can be composed over.

**The handoff is ordered production, not symmetric exchange.** Fig 12 :72: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*. Operators table :37: *"A produces B; B did not exist before"*. "P and RNA exchanged information" hides the arrow.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that PNA, TNA, GNA or any other named polymer was first. Names enter only as provenanced candidate bindings in the ledger.
- Not that "strongest base pairing" is the selection criterion. The 2025 XNA review explicitly says maximum stability alone was unlikely to be decisive.
- Not that a laboratory transfer is the historical route, nor that in vitro evolution proves Hadean capability (Fig 11 :70).
- Not that selection chose a polymer in advance. Fig 11 :36: *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran."*
- Not a derivation of RNA from chemistry, and no new figure primitive.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by a chemistry measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not another argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened this session): an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the discriminators are:

1. **Transfer discriminator.** An RNA-side observable measured independently of the P sequence, with solvent, ionic and concentration effects excluded, that separates transfer from convergence. Shannon 1948 supplies the low-overlap statement of the requirement: *"reproducing at one point either exactly or approximately a message selected at another point."*
2. **Fidelity threshold.** The copying accuracy at which the sequence difference survives, measured per slot (Fig 1 :19). Below threshold, C3 fails as a matter of chemistry, not argument.
3. **Settlement test.** A copied RNA descendant that persists and can be composed over, not just a product band on a gel.
4. **Chronology instrument.** For D5 only: anything returning the temporal order of a candidate polymer and the first RNA replicator. Importing one is the correct move if it exists; importing a favourite polymer now against a budget limit would add an atom that was not needed (Fig 9 :60).

Standing refuters from `01` section 5 are repeated here: an indistinguishable single linkage that still transfers; capacity requiring new monomer kinds; one-shot transfer with no closing loop; transfer with no complementarity or lineage after confounds are excluded; equal persistence across differing habitats; a one-off product counted as a start of genetic flow. If any fires, the condition it governs is withdrawn.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable in principle.** The condition set is a shape; the molecule and the date are not. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The derivation stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. A result saying RNA preceded all candidates and no handoff occurred would retire the counterfactual's premise while leaving the conditional shape intact.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name a first genetic polymer. It says what any candidate would have to be able to do for a sequence in it to become a sequence in RNA that changes RNA behaviour. The arrangement, not the ingredient list, must carry the difference; a closed alphabet must supply open arrangements; a template must survive its own use inside a closing copying loop; the two chemistries must be geometrically complementary with a traceable lineage; RNA must have a readout that can be measured independently of the source; all of this is relative to an enumerated habitat; and the RNA product must be copied and persist before the handoff can be composed over. The handoff is production, not a symmetric exchange. Laboratory transfers prove the wiring of these conditions, not the history. Which polymer, if any, actually came first is a gap until a chronology instrument returns a reading. What is offered is the shape, the refuters written in advance, and the discriminators that would close it.
