## 0. The claim under audit

> If another polymer came first, it would need to be able to hold a sequence difference, copy that difference, expose it to RNA in a form RNA can read, and have the resulting RNA persist and be composable over. It would not need to be any particular named molecule. The figures return a condition set, not a name, and they cannot date the handoff.

This is the shape claim. It is offered before the world sources and before the conclusion, so that the verdict is not built to fit a favourite polymer. The thing being decided is *what would count as a successful handoff*, not *which polymer won*.

## 1. Residual stated in effect terms

Let R be the unexplained handoff. In a hypothetical pre-RNA world, a sequence distinction held in polymer P becomes a sequence distinction in RNA, and RNA behaviour tracks it. Nothing in the held framework says what properties make that possible or sufficient.

- **Arity:** seven overlapping conditions, linkage, alphabet, copying, cross-readout, receiver-side readout, habitat and settlement (C1 to C7 below), plus a separate historical-priority question. R is therefore a **union compound** (Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*). One lever clears the region where one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.
- **Symmetry:** the handoff is **ordered**. It is production, not a symmetric join. Operators table :37: *"A produces B; B did not exist before"*. Fig 12 :72: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*. A language that says "P and RNA exchange information" has hidden the arrow.
- **Scale:** first order inside the chemical composition of one polymer; second order at the handoff between two genetic systems; third order at the evolutionary settlement that makes the receiver composable over. Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."*
- **Per slot:** the residual is indexed per sequence position and per readout, never pooled into one global "information transfer" number (Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*; Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*).

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term (formula step Direction).** An answer is a set of necessary conditions C1 to C5 such that (a) each is stated in effect terms, (b) each has a discriminator that could fail, and (c) none names a molecule. If a reader can substitute "PNA" or "TNA" for the whole set and feel the question answered, the answer has been mis-bound. This sentence is written down before the run so it can accrue a residual like any other term.

## 4. Findings

### F1. The bond, not the ingredient list, is the first necessary property

Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."** Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."** Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

A polymer whose monomers can be joined in more than one distinguishable way can carry a sequence; a polymer whose linkage is fixed and indistinguishable at each position is a flat formula. Fig 12 :101: **"A bond that costs nothing is a bond that carries nothing, which is the flat case."**

**C1, necessary property (effect form).** A typed, reproducible linkage with at least two distinguishable states per position, so that changing the arrangement changes the substance and not only the inventory. The sugar, backbone and anomer are bindings onto this role, not the role itself.

**World check (secondary).** Chaput, Egli and Herdewijn 2025 (*The XNA alphabet*) split synthetic genetic polymers into those that cross-pair with natural nucleic acids and those that are orthogonal to them, and say the sugar defines conformational space which in turn influences hybridisation. Nie, Bai and Mei 2020 report that homo-DNA forms a very stable duplex with itself but does not cross-pair with DNA or RNA because its ladder-like geometry differs from the A/B form, and state that it therefore cannot transfer genetic information to DNA. That is the world-side statement of F1: a stable arrangement is not automatically a readable one. `single-source` for the figure claim about typed bonds, `triangulated` for the consequence that geometry governs cross-readout.

**Refuter for F1.** A polymer with a single indistinguishable linkage at every position demonstrably passes a sequence difference to RNA and changes RNA behaviour. If that happens, the bond requirement is wrong.

### F2 : Capacity comes from a closed set with unbounded arrangements, not from chemical exoticism

Fig 13 :115: **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."** Fig 6 :21: **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."** Fig 8 :32: **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."**

A small, uniform alphabet of monomers is enough in principle, because the arrangements over a closed set do not run out. Searching a larger inventory of exotic monomers does not add a new kind of information capacity; it changes which arrangements are physically reachable and how accurately they copy.

**C2, necessary property.** A finite monomer alphabet with uniform, repeatable coupling rules, giving unbounded distinguishable arrangements from a closed set. "More letters" is a budget filter, not a witness for capacity (Fig 9 :60).

**World check.** The 2025 XNA review tabulates a very large alphabet of sugar-modified polymers and notes that pairing stability alone was unlikely to be what selected RNA, with accuracy and uniformity of base pairing likely more critical. The 2020 review reports that chemical replication suffers insufficient efficiency, low fidelity and limited length. Both are world statements about C2, and both share a chemistry evidence pool with each other. `triangulated` at the level of shape (closed-set combinatorics plus the accuracy constraint), `single-source` for the specific chemistry numbers.

**Refuter for F2.** A demonstrable case where capacity requires the monomer inventory itself to grow, that is, where no enlargement of the arrangement space over a fixed alphabet can encode the transferred difference.

### F3 : Copying must close a cycle and be paid for by a part that survives its own use

Fig 12 :121: **"The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."** Fig 12 :119: **"A part that survives its own use."** Fig 12 :123: **"A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

A template is a catalyst role: it survives the copying act. A copying cycle is a closed loop whose output re-enters as input. A pairing site is a functional group: its presence fixes which partner is incorporated, while the base identity says which member of the pair. These are three different figure lines and three different conditions; collapsing them into "it can base pair" is the flat-formula error of F1 one level up.

**C3, necessary property.** A template mechanism that (a) re-enters as input, (b) is regenerated rather than consumed, and (c) has a local pairing geometry that discriminates among partners with accuracy above the threshold at which the sequence information survives.

**World check.** Schmidt, Nielsen and Orgel 1997 (Nucleic Acids Research) report directly: *"we show that information can be transferred from PNA to RNA"*, with adenylic, cytidylic and guanylic acids incorporated opposite their complement on PNA, *"although less efficiently than on DNA templates"*. This is a demonstrated C3-shaped mechanism in a laboratory, with a stated efficiency shortfall. Yu, Zhang and Chaput 2012 (*Nature Chemistry*) show a TNA receptor evolved in vitro and say TNA has *"the ability to fold into tertiary structures with sophisticated chemical functions"*. The 2020 review adds that engineered polymerases were needed to transfer information back and forth between XNA and DNA with reasonable fidelity and efficiency. `triangulated` for the cycle-and-catalyst shape; `single-source` for the claim that any specific prebiotic polymerase existed.

**Refuter for F3.** A one-shot, non-regenerating chemical event that passes sequence information to RNA and still leaves a heritable RNA difference. A single transcription without a closing loop would break the cycle condition.

### F4 : The handoff is an import between closures, and imports need a source gate and a shadow test

Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."** Fig 8 :50-52: **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."** Fig 8 :53: **"Residual first, frame second."**

P's chemistry and RNA's chemistry are two closures. Information crossing from one to the other is an import in the figure sense. An import adds no atom to the world; it gives a frame access to an atom another frame holds. Here the "atom" is a sequence distinction. The source gate in chemistry is provenance: does this RNA sequence actually descend from the P sequence, or did it arise independently? The shadow test is: does the transferred sequence explain a gap in RNA behaviour that the RNA-only account leaves open?

**C4, necessary property.** A physical complementarity between the two chemistries that lets a sequence in P specify a sequence in RNA, plus an observable lineage that distinguishes a genuine handoff from independent convergence. Stronger pairing is not the same as a better handoff; the XNA review explicitly warns that maximum pairing strength alone was unlikely to be the selection factor.

**World check.** Schmidt et al. 1997 is the direct demonstration of a P to RNA handoff; Nie et al. 2020 supply the negative case (stable but non-cross-pairing homo-DNA). Yu et al. 2012 say TNA's *"ability to exchange genetic information with itself and RNA"* is a reason it is considered a possible RNA progenitor. These are three reports in one chemistry evidence pool, not three independent witnesses. `triangulated` for the two-closures shape, `single-source` for the specific direction of any historical handoff.

**Refuter for F4.** A case where a sequence in P controls RNA behaviour with no complementarity, no pairing geometry and no traceable lineage, and the control is not a solvent, ionic or concentration effect. That would break the import condition.

### F5 : Readability is a property of the receiver, and the receiver's limit is a slice

Symbols table :40: **"what cannot be perceived yet is residual and simply cannot be read"**. Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."** Fig 10 :42: **"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."**

Information is not transmitted just because a physical correlation exists. It is transmitted when the receiver's readout registers the difference. Here the receiver is RNA, and its readout is its own pairing/folding behaviour. The ground is a measurement of RNA behaviour that does not move when we decide a handoff occurred. If we only read the P sequence and infer RNA behaviour, we have one frame reporting on its own output.

**C5, necessary property.** The transferred distinction must be registered in an RNA observable that can be measured independently of the P sequence, and a discriminator must exist that separates "RNA changed because of P" from "RNA changed anyway". This is the instrument condition, and Fig 3 :27 applies: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

**World check (low overlap).** Shannon 1948 (*A Mathematical Theory of Communication*): *"The fundamental problem of communication is that of reproducing at one point either exactly or approximately a message selected at another point."* This is a mathematics and information-theory frame, not an origins-of-life frame, so it is the genuinely low-overlap corroborator. It predicts that the requirement is reproduction at the receiving point, not mere existence of a source pattern. It corroborates the figure verdict about readability without supplying any chemistry. `single-source` as a world witness, corroborating a figure verdict that is itself `triangulated` (Symbols table plus Fig 2).

**Refuter for F5.** An experiment in which RNA behaviour changes in a way that tracks P, but no independent RNA-side observable can be found that distinguishes the tracking from a pooled environmental cause. If no discriminator can be built even in principle, the claim is not refuted but becomes `unreachable`; if a discriminator exists and returns no tracking, the claim is refuted.

### F6 : "Stability", "useful" and "first" are habitat-relative, not intrinsic properties

Fig 11 :39: **"And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."** Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** Fig 11 :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."** Fig 11 :36: **"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran."**

The question asks for "chemical properties", which invites an intrinsic list. The figures refuse that form. A polymer is "stable enough" only relative to a habitat that supplies cycling, concentration and protection. A transferred difference is "useful" only relative to an RNA behaviour the ground pays for. Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."** We must enumerate wet-dry cycling, UV, mineral contact, ionic strength and feedstock rather than invent an ideal polymer in isolation.

This is also the answer to the standing-list objection. The figures do not forbid a list of conditions; they forbid a standing list of *winners*. C1 to C7 are conditions whose valence is relative to the handoff residual. They become a standing list of good molecules the moment someone turns them into "PNA has all five, therefore PNA was first".

**C6, habitat condition.** The polymer must persist, be copied and undergo the handoff in the same enumerated habitat, and the valence of each of C1 to C5 must be re-read per habitat. Selection is not a planner that chose the polymer for these properties; it is what survived contact.

**World check.** Nie et al. 2020 report that uncharged PNA self-aggregates and precipitates in water and cite the argument that a repeating charge may be a universal feature of genetic molecules in water. That is a habitat-relative valence result, not an intrinsic verdict on PNA. `single-source` for the specific chemistry, `triangulated` for the figure-level valence claim.

**Refuter for F6.** A property set that predicts persistence and handoff equally well in two habitats that differ in cycling, concentration and ionic environment, with no change in valence. That would dissolve the habitat condition.

### F7 : Settlement, not occurrence, is the gate to composing over the transferred sequence

Fig 5 :69: **"settling is necessary for a term to be composed over, and it is not sufficient."** Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."** Fig 11 :70: **"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."**

A single laboratory transcription proves that the wiring can be made to work. It does not prove that the product settled against a ground, nor that a lineage could compose over it. The next level of evolution needs the RNA product to persist and be copied; until that happens the handoff is an instance, not a generator. Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** What crosses upward from the lab result is the method (the discriminating experiment), not the recording of one successful run.

**C7, settlement condition.** The RNA product must be settled against an independent ground (persistence, copied descendant, changed behaviour) before the pre-RNA-to-RNA step can be composed over. Occurrence is not settlement.

**Refuter for F7.** A one-off transfer that is never copied and never persists, yet is claimed to have initiated genetic information flow. If the field counts that as success, the settlement condition must be withdrawn or re-derived.

### F8 : Historical priority is a gap, reported as a verdict and not as silence

Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"**. Fig 13 :135: **"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."** Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**. Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

No available instrument dates a pre-RNA handoff. More searching among candidate polymers will not supply one, because search inside a closure adds no atom (Fig 8 :32). This is `unreachable-at-this-budget` rather than underivable in principle: a chronology or isotopic/instrumental discriminator is conceivable, and importing one is the correct move if and when one exists. Importing a favourite polymer now, against a budget limit, would add an atom that was not needed (Fig 9 :60, Fig 6 :24).

**Reversal condition for F8.** An instrument that returns a temporal order between a candidate pre-RNA polymer and the first RNA replicator, with a source gate and a shadow test, would move D5 from `gap` to `specified`. A result showing RNA preceded all candidates and no handoff occurred would retire the counterfactual's premise while leaving the conditional property shape intact.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

The shape claim C1 to C7 is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A single, indistinguishable linkage at every position demonstrably passes a sequence difference to RNA and changes RNA behaviour (breaks F1).
2. Capacity demonstrably requires a growing monomer inventory rather than more arrangements over a closed set (breaks F2).
3. A one-shot, non-regenerating event passes information without a closed copying loop or a surviving template (breaks F3).
4. A sequence in P controls RNA behaviour with no complementarity, no pairing geometry and no traceable lineage, and the effect is not a solvent, ionic or concentration confound (breaks F4).
5. No RNA-side observable can be measured independently of the P sequence, while a discriminator is demonstrably possible and returns no tracking (breaks F5).
6. The same condition set predicts equal persistence in habitats differing in cycling, concentration and ionic environment, with no valence change (breaks F6).
7. A one-off transfer with no copied descendant is counted as a successful initiation of genetic information flow (breaks F7).

These are written before the world sources were used to support the verdict and before the conclusion. They are a design instrument, not an honesty device added afterwards. If any fires, the condition set is withdrawn or re-derived. A failure is more useful here than an endorsement.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Lead with the condition set and its refuter. Do not lead with PNA, TNA or any candidate name (F1, Fig 9 :32).
2. Keep the verdicts per domain. Do not average storage, copying, cross-readout, habitat and settlement into one "viability score" (Fig 1 :19, Fig 9 :60).
3. Enumerate the habitat before ranking candidate polymers (Fig 11 :43). A polymer ranked outside a habitat is a ranking of an idealisation.
4. Ask for a discriminator, not for agreement. Fig 10 :52: **"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."** Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**
5. Report D5 as `gap`. Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42: **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."**), and do not let a filter hand you a verdict (Fig 9 :60).
