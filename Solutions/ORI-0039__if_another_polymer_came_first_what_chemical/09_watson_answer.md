## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what a hypothetical pre-RNA genetic polymer P would have to be like for a sequence difference in P to become a sequence difference in RNA that changes RNA behaviour. Option A names a candidate polymer (PNA, TNA, GNA) and treats that as the answer. Option B derives the necessary condition set, keeps verdicts per domain, refuses to crown a molecule and reports priority as a gap. What each leads to: A produces a name no available instrument settles and a standing list of winners the figures refuse; B produces a checkable shape, six refuters written in advance, and a discriminator that would close the gap.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The unexplained handoff from P sequence to RNA sequence and RNA behaviour. Arity: seven overlapping conditions (linkage, alphabet, copying, cross-readout, receiver-side readout, habitat, settlement) plus a separate priority question, so a **union compound**, not one hard question. Symmetry: the handoff is ordered production, not a symmetric join; operators table :37 defines production as *"A produces B; B did not exist before"*. Scale: first order inside one polymer, second order at the two closures, third order at evolutionary settlement. Indexed per sequence slot and per readout (Fig 1 :19, :22), never pooled into one transfer score.
- **Domain 1, chemical composition.** Actors: monomers, linkage bonds, anomers, backbones, bases. Currency: bonded arrangements and their distinguishable states. Anchor: experiment, pairing and structure. Union with D2 and D3; the ingredient list is a collapse, not a domain.
- **Domain 2, information channel.** Actors: sequences, alphabet, template, copied complement, error. Currency: description length versus actions spent copying. Anchor: a readout measurement that can fire. Union: capacity and fidelity have different anchors.
- **Domain 3, cross-readout.** Actors: the two closures, pairing geometry, the handoff bond. Currency: a sequence crossing between chemistries. Anchor: a transliteration experiment. Union with D1 and D2; the overlap is a compound with its own loop.
- **Domain 4, habitat and selection.** Actors: wet-dry cycles, concentration, ions, mineral surfaces, competitors. Currency: persistence and differential copying. Anchor: the ground of what was retained. Union; valence is relative to the residual.
- **Domain 5, historical priority.** Actors: candidate polymers, chronology, geological record. Currency: temporal order. Anchor: none held. This is a **`gap`**, not a domain with an anchor we currently hold.
- **Domain 6, verification and outreach.** Actors: outside readers, the residual, the refuter. Currency: agreement versus residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all domains draw on one evidence pool, so independence is zero.
- **Order by dependency.** Vocabulary before objective, objective before route, route before settlement (Fig 3 :20). Describe the residual before naming a candidate frame (Fig 9 :27-28). Refuse the standing-list reading before minting (Formula step 2).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The condition set survives; the name and the date do not. A pre-RNA polymer would need a typed linkage whose arrangement carries the sequence, a closed alphabet with open arrangements, a closing copying loop with a regenerating template and a discriminating pairing site, geometric complementarity plus a traceable lineage into RNA, an RNA-side readout independent of the source, habitat-relative valence for every condition, and settlement of the RNA product before the step can be composed over. The handoff is production, not symmetric exchange. Laboratory transfers prove wiring, not history. Which polymer, if any, came first is a `gap`, reported as a verdict rather than silence. This is a `specified`, ungrounded result, and the whole folder is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency, not a ground reading.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode, lists six refuters before the conclusion and records provenance, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending a candidate polymer instead of a condition set and a discriminator. Fig 2 :42 says "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the condition set and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. A false condition is removable without touching the others, and no figure primitive has to be withdrawn. A sent claim can be superseded by the corrected packet. A polymer named as a candidate can be withdrawn when a discriminator returns against it, and the record keeps both the name and the withdrawal.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An independent RNA-side readout separating transfer from convergence would name the mechanism; a measured fidelity threshold would confirm or break the copying condition; a copied and persisting RNA descendant would settle C7; a chronology instrument would move priority out of `gap` or retire the counterfactual's premise. A result showing RNA preceded every candidate and no handoff occurred leaves the conditional shape intact while ending its historical relevance. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
