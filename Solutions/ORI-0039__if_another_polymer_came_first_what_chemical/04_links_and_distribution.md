## Wider literature that bears on the condition set

1. **Chaput et al. 2025** is the governing review. Its distinction between cross-pairing and orthogonal XNAs is the world-side form of the figure's two-closures claim. It explicitly refuses "strongest pairing" as the selection criterion. Use it when explaining why C4 is a geometry and lineage requirement, not a stability ranking.
2. **Nie et al. 2020** supplies the negative case. A polymer can be chemically stable and still unable to hand information to DNA or RNA because its duplex geometry is wrong. It also reports the PNA aggregation problem, which is the habitat-relative form of C6.
3. **Schmidt et al. 1997** is the direct demonstration of a P to RNA handoff and reports an efficiency shortfall relative to DNA templates. Use it as an instance of C3 and C4, not as evidence of what happened on the early Earth.
4. **Yu et al. 2012** shows that a candidate polymer can fold and be evolved in vitro, which is a capability demonstration in a substituted habitat. It does not date anything.
5. **Shannon 1948** is the genuinely low-overlap frame. It says the problem is reproduction of a message at another point, which is exactly the receiver-side condition C5. It supplies no chemistry and is not allowed to pick a polymer.

All these sources share a chemistry or origins evidence pool except Shannon. The chemistry papers are one witness with several names, not four independent confirmations (Fig 2 :40).
