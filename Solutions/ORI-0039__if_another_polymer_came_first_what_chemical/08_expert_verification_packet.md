# Expert verification packet: the Tether on a pre-RNA to RNA handoff

**What this is:** a claim in a form a prebiotic chemist or origins researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the condition set and per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural rule, `gap` on historical priority. Not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading and not a chemistry measurement. ## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

If a pre-RNA genetic polymer passed useful sequence information to RNA, it would have to satisfy seven conditions: a typed linkage with distinguishable states per position; a closed monomer alphabet with unbounded arrangements; a closing copying cycle whose template survives its use and whose pairing site discriminates partners; geometric complementarity and a traceable lineage across the two chemistries; an RNA-side observable measurable independently of the source; a habitat in which all of the above have their valence; and settlement of the RNA product before the step can be composed over. The handoff is ordered production, not a symmetric exchange. No figure line names a polymer, and the question of which polymer, if any, came first is a `gap` until a chronology instrument returns a reading.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 : framework-internal plus chemistry-facing; checkable by reading Figure 12 and the XNA literature

- **Statement:** the bond, not the monomer inventory, carries the transferred sequence; a flat linkage cannot carry it, and a stable arrangement is not automatically a readable one.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :5 *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Fig 12 :94 *"The arrangement is the substance."* Fig 12 :101 *"A bond that costs nothing is a bond that carries nothing, which is the flat case."* Fig 12 :117 *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- **What to check:** is it true in your chemistry that the linkage position, anomer and backbone identity, not only the base composition, determine whether a strand can be read by RNA? Does the XNA literature's distinction between cross-pairing and orthogonal polymers support the geometry condition?
- **Refuter:** a polymer with a single indistinguishable linkage at every position demonstrably transfers a sequence difference to RNA and changes RNA behaviour. Also: a stable but geometrically mismatched polymer transfers information just as well as a matched one.

### C2 : information-channel claim; checkable by a chemist against copying and pairing data

- **Statement:** the necessary copying form is a cycle whose output re-enters as input, with a template that survives its own use and a local pairing site that fixes the incorporated partner. Capacity comes from arrangements over a closed monomer set, not from a growing exotic inventory.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :121 *"The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Fig 12 :119 *"A part that survives its own use."* Fig 12 :123 *"A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Fig 13 :115 *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."*
- **What to check:** do the template-directed synthesis data (Schmidt et al. 1997: PNA to RNA transfer, less efficient than DNA templates) and the XNA review's fidelity and length constraints match this shape? Is there any demonstrated case where new monomer kinds, rather than new arrangements, were required for capacity?
- **Refuter:** a one-shot, non-regenerating event leaves a heritable RNA difference; or capacity demonstrably requires the monomer inventory itself to grow; or a pairing site that does not discriminate among partners still copies the sequence accurately.

### C3 : frame-crossing and settlement claim; checkable by an origins researcher

- **Statement:** the handoff is an import between two closures, needs source provenance and a shadow test, is ordered production, and must be settled before it can be composed over. A laboratory transfer is a substituted habitat, not a history.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :23 *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 8 :50-52 *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."* Operators table :37 *"A produces B; B did not exist before"*. Fig 5 :69 *"settling is necessary for a term to be composed over, and it is not sufficient."* Fig 11 :70 *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."*
- **What to check:** is there an RNA-side observable independent of the P sequence that separates transfer from independent origin in the 1997 experiment? Does the 2012 TNA result license folding capability in a substituted habitat only? Is any claim of historical priority supported by a chronology instrument rather than by the capability result?
- **Refuter:** a polymer-controlled RNA behaviour with no complementarity, no pairing geometry and no lineage after solvent, ionic and concentration confounds are excluded; or a one-off product with no copied descendant counted as a start of genetic information flow.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that PNA, TNA, GNA, homo-DNA or 4-prime-thioDNA was the first genetic polymer. These appear only as candidate bindings with a habitat and a source in the ledger.
- Not that stronger base pairing is better. The 2025 XNA review says maximum stability alone was unlikely to be the selection factor.
- Not that a harness result proves a Hadean capability, and not that in vitro evolution dates anything.
- Not that selection planned the polymer. Fig 11 :36: *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran."*
- Not a derivation of RNA from chemistry, no new figure primitive, and nothing `grounded`.
- Not that a standing list of winning molecules is being offered. Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*

## 5. What would settle it

1. An independent RNA-side readout that separates transfer from convergence, with solvent, ionic and concentration effects excluded.
2. A copying-fidelity threshold measured per sequence position at which the transferred difference survives.
3. A settlement test: a copied RNA descendant that persists and can be composed over.
4. For historical priority only, a chronology instrument returning the order of a candidate polymer and the first RNA replicator. Without one, priority stays a `gap` and is reported as a verdict, not as silence.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 12 :5, :101, :119, :121, :123 and Fig 13 :115. Six short passages.
2. Say whether the seven conditions are necessary in your own chemistry, and name one that is not. A named failure is the useful output.
3. Look at the 1997 PNA to RNA result and ask whether an independent RNA-side readout exists in that experiment. If not, name the discriminator you would build.
4. Say whether any capability result in the XNA literature has been given a chronology that would move historical priority out of `gap`.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
