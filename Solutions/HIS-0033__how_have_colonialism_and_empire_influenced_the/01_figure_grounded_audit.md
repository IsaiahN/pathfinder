## 0. The question under audit, stated as an effect

The question asks how colonialism and empire influenced the collection and classification of knowledge. Stated in effect terms:

> Under colonial conditions, (a) what is gathered and carried from the periphery, (b) how what is gathered is named and arranged, and (c) what record survives of where it came from, all change in a systematic way. What is the shape of that change, what would count as a failure of the shape, and what ground would settle a claim about it?

Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term before running, per Formula step "Direction" and Fig 5 :65-66 ("make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else"):

> **SHAPE (bound before the run):** empire inserts a directional membrane between a knowing periphery and a classifying centre; recordings travel up instead of methods; the receiving frame imports atoms from a closure that differs but strips the source gate; the arrangement (bond) information is not recorded with the ingredients; the resulting agreement among receiving frames is a convention, not corroboration; and the artefact cannot disclose its own route, so the route lives only in a provenance record that empire also damaged.

Options: Option A asserts that the influence is adequately described as "extraction plus imposing categories" and treats that phrase as a settled result. Option B returns the structural shape above as `specified`, states what would refute it, and leaves the specific historical instantiations to ground-level evidence. This audit runs B.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** three linked operations, not one. Collection, classification, and the provenance record that connects them. The question names two; the third is forced by Fig 13 :130 ("A term carries the atoms it was built from and the bonds that joined them") and by the historical literature, which treats the archive as a distinct production site.
- **Symmetry:** collection is contact and enumeration (ordered outward from the residual, Fig 11 :44); classification is composition under ordered bonds, where the arrangement is not recoverable from the operands (Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance"). The two are not interchangeable, so the residual is not symmetric under swapping them.
- **Scale:** runs from a single object slot (one specimen, one census entry) to an empire-wide room chain (Fig 4 membrane; Fig 7 :13). Per slot measurement is required: Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Aggregating all colonial knowledge into one effect would throw away the signal that says where to look.

**No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.** D1 and D2 both have the ground as anchor. D3 has the archive as a working anchor for historical claims, with the explicit caveat that it was produced by the process under study. That caveat is a declared stake, not a reason to drop the domain (Fig 10 :60 "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it").

### Partition or union

Collection leads to classification leads to record: that is a dependency chain (Fig 3 :20-21 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces"). But the provenance question cuts across all three, so the residual also behaves as a union of overlapping conditions. Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." The literature does unbundle this way: studies of collection/extraction, studies of classification/naming, and studies of archival silence are three literatures with different grounds. We keep them separate and do not average their verdicts.

### Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. Collection before classification: atoms before bonds; a link whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed (Fig 3 :22-23).
2. Provenance before trust: a classification cannot be credited until its route is recorded (Fig 13 :132).
3. Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).
4. Refuse before mint (Formula step 2; Fig 5 amendment). An unexamined "extraction" term is refused before a new term is offered.

## 3. Findings F1 to F7

### F1 - Collection is a provenance-less import of recordings (triangulated)

Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."
Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."
Fig 6 :12: "source gate: provenance"
Fig 6 :13: "shadow test: explains a gap"

The periphery is a frame whose closure differs from the metropole. Contact with it is the only way the metropolitan wall moves, so local knowledge enters as IMPORT, not as MINT. The figures require an import to carry a source gate (provenance) and a shadow test (it must explain a gap the receiver had). What the colonial record shows crossing the membrane is specimens, counts, and observations: recordings of particular occasions. By Fig 4 :25, these look like knowledge while remaining descriptions of one occasion. So the influence on collection is structural: empire substituted recordings for the re-applicable method that the membrane is supposed to pass, and the source gate was weakened.

**Mode:** `specified` on the claim that colonial collection instantiates this; `general` on the membrane rule itself.

**Status:** triangulated. Fig 4 (membrane direction across scales) and Fig 6 (closure expansion by import) have low overlap: one governs what crosses a size boundary, the other governs what expands a library. Agreement across them is not one evidence pool.

**Refuter:** show a case where what crossed up was a verified, re-applicable method carried with intact provenance and a shadow test, and where the receiving frame's reach grew by that import. Then F1 fails for that case.

### F2 - Classification recorded the ingredients and dropped the bond (triangulated)

Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
Fig 12 :14: "the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not"
Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance."
Fig 12 :95: "Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."

A census or a specimen catalogue records ingredients: a name, a count, a set of traits. It does not record the local relations that made those ingredients mean something in the source frame. By Fig 12 this is the flat-formula collapse: the items are recorded, the arrangement is not, nothing downstream can tell the difference, and nothing signals that anything was lost. So the influence on classification is not simply "wrong categories were imposed"; it is that the bond information was systematically omitted, producing entries that look complete while being structurally silent.

Fig 1 :27 adds the validity failure: "A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." Metropolitan classifiers scored the classified populations using categories the classifiers themselves produced, which the figure disqualifies as a frame-internal metric.

**Mode:** `specified` on the colonial instantiation; `general` on the bond rule.

**Status:** triangulated. Fig 12 (composition notation) and Fig 1 :27 (metric validity) are low-overlap: one is about what a record contains, the other about what may count as evidence.

**Refuter:** produce a classification from the period in which the relational information (the bond) was recorded alongside the items, and show that downstream users could distinguish arrangements that share ingredients. Then F2 fails for that case.

### F3 - Metropolitan agreement is a convention, not corroboration (triangulated)

Fig 2 :40: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"
Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
Fig 7 :13: "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"
Fig 2 :41: "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"

Colonial classification schemes were validated by agreement among metropolitan institutions that drew on the same surveys, the same census returns, and the same advisory networks. By Fig 2 :40 that agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other. By Fig 7 :13, handing the scheme along a chain of agreeing rooms compounds agreement without adding evidence. The correct reading is Fig 2 :41: such agreement is efficient for a convention, and a convention is not a verdict. This is why a claim like "the classification was objectively correct because every authority used it" is structurally void under the figures.

**Mode:** `general` (the alignment rule), applied `specified` to metropolitan institutions.

**Status:** triangulated. Fig 2 (anchor and correlation) and Fig 7 (handoff dynamics) are low-overlap.

**Refuter:** show two metropolitan frames with genuinely low evidence overlap that independently arrived at the same classification and then had it settled by a ground that neither could move. Then F3 fails for that case.

### F4 - The artefact cannot disclose its route; the record carries it (triangulated)

Fig 13 :128: "Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available."
Fig 13 :130: "A term carries the atoms it was built from and the bonds that joined them. That record is not documentation of the term. It is the term."
Fig 13 :132: "The first is provenance: how did this come to be, and from what."
Fig 8 :51: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"

A classified artefact (a table, a catalogue, a census category) does not reveal, on inspection, whether it was derived locally or imported from a colonised frame. What separates convergent derivation from adopted import is the record of provenance. Empire damaged that record, so the route is often unrecoverable from the artefact itself. This is the figure-grounded content of the archival-silence literature: a silence is a missing route, not a missing object.

Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". Therefore an empty place in the colonial archive is a verdict about reachability, not evidence that the knowledge never existed. This is a governing constraint on how the historical question may be answered.

**Mode:** `general` on the record and search rules; `specified` on the colonial case.

**Status:** triangulated. Fig 13 (lineage) and Fig 8 (search and absence) are low-overlap: one is about what a term carries, the other about what a search can prove.

**Refuter:** exhibit an artefact from which the colonial route is directly readable without any external provenance record, and show that the archive's silence was settled by a ground rather than assumed. Then F4 fails for that case.

### F5 - Categories are relative to the residual, not neutral properties (single-source with world corroboration)

Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."
Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"

Colonial categories (caste, tribe, race, "traditional" versus "modern") were treated as natural properties of the classified people. The figures say valence and standing are relative to the residual the classifying frame is trying to reduce, not properties carried by the actor. A category is a fixed valence produced by a frame with a stake in a question. This is a structural reason to expect such categories to shift when the administrative residual shifts, which is what the historical scholarship reports.

**Mode:** `general`.

**Status:** single-source for the figure claim (Fig 11 only). World sources (Bowker and Star; the PolSci Institute survey of Cohn, Dirks and Appadurai) corroborate independently, but per the rule they do not raise the figure verdict to triangulated.

**Refuter:** exhibit a category whose valence survived a change of residual and whose standing was settled by the ground, not by the classifying frame. Then F5 fails for that case.

### F6 - The verification arrangement must keep the archive outside the claim it checks (general)

Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."
Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source."
Fig 10 :59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"
Fig 10 :60: "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."

Any historian checking a claim about colonial influence is working with archives the colonial process produced, so the archive is not a neutral seat. The figure's remedy is not to pretend the stake is absent but to declare it and to send a characterised residual rather than a theory. This governs the outreach artifacts in this folder: the first contact is the shape and its refuter, not the framework.

**Mode:** `general`.

**Status:** triangulated with Fig 2's anchor rule (Fig 2 :33 "The anchor must not update"), low overlap between them.

**Refuter:** show a checking arrangement in which the verifier authors the ground it grades against and the resulting reading still settles the residual. Then F6 fails.

### F7 - The framework returns a shape, not a historical name (decisive limit)

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"
Fig 13 :135: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."
Fig 13 :133: "what it returns is a shape rather than a name"
Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

No figure line licenses a claim such as "the census invented caste" or " empire produced orientalism". Those are ground-level historical claims to be settled by archives, not by the figures. The Tether's contribution is the constrained shape above, the refuters, and the statement of what would close it. The ranking or importance of a historical case is a filter for the budget, never a witness for the verdict.

**Mode:** `general`.

**Status:** triangulated across Fig 9 and Fig 13, and confirmed by the canonical figures README, which says the framework returns "a shape, not a name".

**Refuter:** exhibit a figure line that names a specific historical category or institution as the answer to this question. Then F7 fails. None was found in a full read of the figure text.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach: Seat Map :287 says "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."

The bound SHAPE claim (§0) is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A documented case in which what crossed the colonial membrane was a verified, re-applicable method, carried with intact provenance and a passing shadow test, and the receiving frame's reach grew by that import. (Breaks F1.)
2. A classification from the period in which the bond or arrangement information was recorded with the ingredients and downstream users could distinguish same-ingredient arrangements. (Breaks F2.)
3. Two metropolitan frames with genuinely low evidence overlap independently reaching the same classification, settled by a ground neither could move. (Breaks F3.)
4. An artefact whose colonial route is readable from the artefact alone, with no provenance record, plus an archival silence settled by a ground. (Breaks F4.)
5. A category whose valence survived a change of residual and was grounded rather than frame-produced. (Breaks F5.)
6. A figure line in the verbatim text that names a specific historical category or institution as the answer. (Breaks F7.)

Also a standing design refuter: if any figure line above fits only with a strain, that line governs another quantity, so the domain census is re-run rather than the line softened. Strains are recorded in §7 and in `11_audit.md`.

Until one of 1 to 6 is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.
