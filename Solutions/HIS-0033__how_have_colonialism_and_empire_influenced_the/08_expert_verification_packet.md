## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Under empire, knowledge movement has a direction. Recordings of particular occasions (specimens, counts, observations) travel from the periphery to the centre instead of re-applicable methods, so what arrives looks like knowledge while remaining a description of one occasion. The importing frame takes in atoms from a closure that differs but runs the import with a weakened source gate. At the centre, the items are named and arranged, but the local relations that made them meaningful are not recorded with them, so same-item arrangements become indistinguishable downstream. Agreement among central institutions compounds without adding evidence because those institutions share an evidence pool. The finished artefact cannot disclose its route; the route lives only in a provenance record that the same process damaged. An empty place in the archive is therefore a reachability verdict, not proof of absence. The framework returns this as a shape, not as the name of any census, category, or empire.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 4 and 6
- **Statement:** Empire's effect on collection is structurally a directional crossing in which recordings replace methods and the import's source gate is weakened.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :25 "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 6 :12 "source gate: provenance"
- **Mode:** `general` structure, `specified` instantiation.
- **Refuter:** show a case where a verified, re-applicable method crossed with intact provenance and a passing shadow test, and the receiving frame's reach grew by that import. Then C1 fails for that case.

### C2 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 12
- **Statement:** Classifications of this kind record the items and drop the bond, so same-item arrangements become indistinguishable downstream. Fig 11 adds that the categories' valence is relative to the classifier's residual, not a property carried by the classified.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :5 "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance." Fig 12 :95 "Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond." Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
- **What to check:** find a catalogue or schedule from the period and ask whether the relational information was recorded with the items. Ask whether downstream users could distinguish two arrangements with the same items.
- **Mode:** `general` structure, `specified` instantiation.
- **Refuter:** exhibit such a classification in which the bond information was present and the distinction survived downstream. Then C2 fails for that case.

### C3 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 13 and 8
- **Statement:** The artefact cannot disclose its route; only the provenance record can, and an archival silence is a reachability verdict rather than proof of absence.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :128 "Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available." Fig 13 :130 "A term carries the atoms it was built from and the bonds that joined them. That record is not documentation of the term. It is the term." Fig 8 :51 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them" Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
- **Refuter:** exhibit an artefact whose route is readable from the artefact alone, with no external provenance record, plus a silence settled by a ground. Then C3 fails for that case.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that knowledge under empire was only extraction. Local collaboration, adaptation, and resistance are real and are recorded in the world sources.
- Not that any specific category, census, or empire is named by the framework as the answer. That is a ground-level claim and a name, which the framework forbids (Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself").
- Not that the archive is neutral, and not that silence equals absence. The archive is a product of the process under study; this stake is declared (Fig 10 :60 "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it").
- Not a moral verdict. The framework describes structure, not culpability.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by an archive.
