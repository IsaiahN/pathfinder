# Order, union, and the analogue test for colonial knowledge practices

**Mode: `specified` on the instantiation, `general` on the structural rules.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question names two operations, collection and classification, and the audit forces a third, the provenance record. Three questions are owed: (1) what is the order of these operations, (2) is the residual a chain or a union, and (3) does the chemistry analogue of isomerism govern same-ingredient classifications, and with what price?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - What is the order of collection, classification, and record?

- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
- Fig 3 :22-23: "A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can. Which is why a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing."
- Collection produces the atoms; classification bonds them; the record documents the route. So the order is **collection → classification → record**, and this is a dependency order.
- A practical consequence, and the reason the exemplar's dependency figure matters here: a historian cannot assess a classification scheme in a region where the collection never reached. Effort spent refining the classification arrives at a step that cannot use it.
- Fig 3 :18: "Where a given chain stops is a reading, never a property of the chain." So where a colonial knowledge chain actually stopped (survey only, census only, archive only) is an empirical finding, not a structural property.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** exhibit a case where the record was authored before the classification it documents and that classification was still settled by the ground. Then the dependency order fails for that case.

### Q2 - Chain or union?

- Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."
- The three operations form a chain (Q1), but the *evidence* for each comes from a different literature with a different ground: collection from exploration and survey history, classification from taxonomy and census studies, record from archival and silence studies. That makes the **evidentiary** residual a union of overlapping conditions, even though the **process** is a chain.
- So: unbundle for the search (three literatures), then re-assemble by dependency for the answer. The two readings are not in conflict because they govern different objects, the process and the evidence about it.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit one literature and one ground that settles all three operations together without residue. Then the union reading fails.

### Q3 - Are same-item classifications isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :118-119: the exemplar notes "catalyst. A part that survives its own use" and the silver fulminate / silver cyanate case from chemistry, which forced structure into the notation.
- A census table and a botanical catalogue may contain the same items arranged under different bonds (one by region, one by descent). By Fig 12 they are **not variants of one thing**; they are different substances whose ingredient lists cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond.
- Fig 12 :42 gives the price: "(k+1)·log₂(|atoms|+1)  +  (k−1)·log₂(|bonds|)" and Fig 12 :43 "one bond type costs nothing; seven cost 2.8 bits each". A classification that records only the flat `+` of ingredients pays no bond cost and therefore carries no arrangement information. That is precisely the failure F2 names.
- Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Two classifications over the same population can assign opposite valence to the same group because the residuals differ. That is a structural reason not to treat competing classifications as noisy versions of one true list.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit two classifications with identical items and different arrangements whose downstream behaviour is identical and whose bond cost was paid. Then the isomer reading fails for that case.

### Q4 - What is the order of the *union* at the verificatory level?

- Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
- Fig 7 :36: "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."
- The rooms here are the three literatures. Progress requires low overlap between them, and each must be picked against the residual the last one surfaced. A chain of colonial-history surveys that all cite the same census is one long room: motion, no progress (Fig 7 :32).
- Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The cost here is a false structural claim about a painful history, so independence is worth paying for. This is a contact-cost and evidence rule, not a ranking of people.

## CONCLUSION

The process is a **dependency chain** (collection → classification → record). The evidence about it is a **union** of three literatures that must be unbundled before search and re-ordered by dependency for the answer. Classifications sharing items but differing in arrangement are **isomers** under Fig 12, and a flat ingredient list pays no bond price and therefore records no arrangement. This rescues the structural content of the "imposed categories" claim without naming a specific category or polity: the defect is the unrecorded bond and the stripped provenance, not a nameable invention.
