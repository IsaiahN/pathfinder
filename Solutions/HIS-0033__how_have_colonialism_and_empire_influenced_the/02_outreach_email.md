# Outreach draft - check the shape before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** the six refuters below must be stated before any theory is shown. They are stated first in this draft. If a recipient's verdict on stage 1 is not recorded before stage 2, the arrangement is void (Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it").

---

**Subject:** One checkable shape about colonial knowledge collection, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific structural claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read anything first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`general` on the structure, `specified` on its colonial instantiation, not `grounded`):**
>
> Under empire, the movement of knowledge has a direction. Things gathered at the periphery travel to the centre as recordings of particular occasions, specimens, counts, observations, rather than as re-applicable methods. What arrives is treated as knowledge even though it is a description of one occasion. At the centre, the items are named and arranged, but the local relations that made them mean something are not recorded with them, so two arrangements that share the same items become indistinguishable downstream. Agreement among central institutions then compounds without adding evidence, because those institutions draw on the same reports and surveys. Finally, the finished artefact does not disclose the route by which it was made; that route lives only in a provenance record, which the same process damaged. An empty place in the archive is therefore a verdict about reachability, not proof that the knowledge was absent.
>
> This is a shape, not a historical name. It does not say that any particular census or any particular category was invented by empire. That is a ground-level historical claim and I am not making it here.
>
> **What would refute it.** I wrote these down before sending this:
> 1. A case where what crossed the membrane was a verified, re-applicable method, with intact provenance and a clear account of the gap it explained.
> 2. A classification in which the relational information was recorded with the items, so downstream users could tell same-item arrangements apart.
> 3. Two central institutions with genuinely separate evidence that independently reached the same classification and had it settled by something neither could move.
> 4. An artefact whose route is readable from the artefact alone, with no external provenance record.
> 5. A category whose standing survived a change of the question and was settled by the ground rather than produced by the classifier.
> 6. A structural rule in the source framework that actually names a specific historical institution or category as the answer.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract the corresponding part. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am explicitly not claiming:** that knowledge under empire was only extraction; that every colonial category was false; that the archive contains nothing of value; or that a framework can settle a historical question. None of those follow from the shape, and I am not asking you to grant any of them.
>
> **Stage 2, only if you are curious and only after your stage-1 verdict is recorded:** the source links are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local framework copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Public figures repository, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **Stage 3, if you want to reuse the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so a reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - [name]
