## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is the shape of the route by which a collective regularity, a social norm, changes three different operations on an emotion: putting it on display, keeping it off display, and reading it in another person. The options lead to different obligations. If norms write the display, then agreement inside a group is the product of a convention and cannot be used as evidence about what people actually feel. If norms only filter an already fixed display, then the same agreement is a measurement problem in disguise. If norms tune recognition, then a perceiver's criterion is part of the mechanism and must be ranged against an anchor that does not move when the perceiver moves.

What an answer would look like has to be written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, Direction). Here it is: a shape with three slots, a norm-to-person transmission that carries a head start rather than a recording, a convention verdict for display, an anchor verdict for recognition, and a residual-relative valence for any single norm. That shape is the term the refuter in `01` tests.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the shape claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

The reason is structural, not courtesy. Fig 2 :41 says agreement is *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*, but Fig 2 :40 says it is *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. A list selected for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation. Fig 10 :42 adds: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, never the framework's interior.
