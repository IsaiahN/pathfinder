## 1. A shape is a constraint you can state before you can name the occupant

The canonical README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

For this question the shape is: three slots, a norm-to-person membrane that carries a head start, a convention verdict for display, an anchor verdict for recognition, a vocabulary link where the norm acts, and residual-relative valence. It constrains any answer to the question without telling us which norm or which emotion fills a slot. `SPACE(???)TIME` in the exemplar is the analogue; here the analogue is `FELT EMOTION → (???) → DISPLAY` and `DISPLAY → (???) → READING`.

A shape is not a filler. It does not say "culture matters" and stop. It says where the mattering must occur and what would count as a failure of the account.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: bootstrap vocabulary by proximity. Use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."* For this question the bootstrap terms were "display rule", "convention", "in-group advantage" and "source discriminator".
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."* The effect here is what changes in the display and the reading, not a story about what culture "is".
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* This is why the outreach email leads with expected effects and failure conditions.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."* The paradox: *"By the time you learn the EXACT search terms to find the CORRECT book, you won't need to read it anymore, because you have already composed the answer through the process of locating it."*
- *Library of Babel* on bundles: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"* That is the three-slot finding in `01` F1.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* For recognition that means an independently measured target state, not perceiver agreement.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test. Naming "display rule" as the mechanism is an import from cross-cultural psychology into the framework. It must carry its provenance and its shadow test, and it adds no atom to the framework (Fig 6 :25: *"An import adds no atom to the world."*).

*They're Scanning Books* (carried from exemplar) sharpens this: *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space."* And: *"A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six."* Also: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."*

## 4. Why agreement has opposite valence in the two slots

*Decoding Tacit Knowledge* gives the sharpest statement in the corpus:

> *"Shared pretext is good for one thing and ruins another. Agreement is the whole point when you're settling what words mean. It's worthless for settling what's true when both sides learned from the same sources (Fig 2 :40–41)."*

This is Fig 2's correlation-valence rule restated in terms of shared history. It is the reason the solution refuses to pool cross-cultural agreement as corroboration of a claim about what people feel, while treating the same agreement as the product when the question is what a display means inside a group.

The same essay adds the drift mechanism: *"Two people who only check against each other drift, and they drift together, so neither notices."* And: *"A shared mindset lets two people drift together without either one noticing: each correction lands easily, each confirms the other, and the pair moves away from the world together."* That is the recognition-slot failure in `01` F4 at the scale of a community.

## 5. Why concealment cannot be read as the absence of a fact

*Decoding Tacit Knowledge* on substitution: *"Deception runs on substitution: a false context, a false pretext, a false subtext."* A concealed emotion is not a refuted one. The display is replaced or staged; the felt state is not withdrawn from the world. This is the essay-side support for `01` F5, where subtraction (`−`) and replacement differ from negation (`¬`).

The same essay's collective tacit knowledge case is relevant: Collins's third kind is *"knowledge we draw that is the property of society, such as the rules for language"*, acquired *"only by living among them."* A display rule is exactly this kind of pretext. It travels as a head start (Fig 4 :22) and the lived history behind it does not travel (Fig 12 :107-110).

## 6. The source problem: an accuracy score cannot name its source

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. Metamerism: an instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source"*; it might be one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* The fix was not an argument: *"It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."* And: *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."*

Applied here: an emotion recognition score reads a display and names its source as a felt emotion. Several paths (genuine feeling, masking, qualifying, simulating) produce the same display. The score cannot discriminate among them. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument, not a better argument and not more agreement.

## 7. Prestige is not action, and this derivation cannot validate itself

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."*

A campaign that gathers respected names to agree with the three-slot shape is the named collapse. The legitimate ask is the opposite: give the claim a budget to be tried against a ground and let the ground settle it.

The same essay supplies the honesty label for this whole folder: *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself. An external validator, or ground, is always needed."* And on simulation: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* So `01` is a simulated derivation, checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of the claim and cannot settle anything it reports on.

## 8. Correction to my own §1 to §3: what "shape, not a name" governs

I initially wrote that the framework forbids naming a display rule as the mechanism. That was an overstatement, and it is corrected here rather than erased.

- The README's phrase is *"what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* The subject is a primitive not held in Γ.
- A display rule is not a missing primitive of the six-term set. "Network" is already held (Fig 13 :24: *"that entities relate, and influence travels"*), and a norm is a network relation over it. Naming a display rule is a **binding** from the cross-cultural psychology frame onto held atoms.
- What remains forbidden is inventing a seventh primitive, or claiming the binding moves the wall (Fig 6 :25: *"An import adds no atom to the world."*).
- What remains owed is provenance for the binding and a source-discriminating instrument for the recognition slot. Those are separate from the shape limit.

My §4 metamerism point is **not** retracted. An accuracy score still names a source from a reading where several sources could give the same reading. The reason is the missing discriminator, not a ban on naming held atoms. Both claims are recorded so neither is quietly lost.

## 9. Consequences for the verification packet

1. Lead with the shape and the five refuters; offer the sources only after a verdict (Fig 10 :52).
2. Do not ask a recipient to trust the framework or to agree with it (Fig 2 :35; the prestige rule).
3. State the mode on every substantive claim (Formula mode rule).
4. Ask for a failure, not an endorsement. A reported failure is more useful than a reported success.
5. Tell the recipient to write their own refuter and name their own ground if they reuse the loop, because a frame cannot check the direction of its own question.
