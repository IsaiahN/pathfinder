# Outreach draft: check the shape before you decide whether the framing is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** send only after the three slots in `01` F1 are kept separate in the text you send. If the email collapses expression, concealment and recognition into one verdict, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Three separate claims about norms and emotion, with the failure conditions written first

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the mechanism, `general` on the shape, not `grounded`):** the question "how do social norms influence which emotions people express, conceal, or recognize?" is a union of three slots, not one question with one answer.
>
> 1. **Expression and concealment are convention slots.** A display rule is a shared meaning about who may show what to whom. Within a convention, agreement is the product: *"agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"* (Fig 2 :41). That agreement says nothing, by itself, about what the person actually feels.
> 2. **Recognition is a ground slot.** A claim about what another person feels is a claim about the world. For that slot, agreement among perceivers who learned the same display grammar is *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). The anchor has to be an independently measured target state; *"The anchor must not update"* (Fig 2 :33). If the perceiver's criterion moves with the perceiver, everyone drifts together and nobody notices (Fig 2 :34).
> 3. **The norm reaches the person as a head start, not a recording.** The same perceive-choose-observe loop runs at group and person scale; only actors, currency and anchor change (Fig 4 :19-20). What crosses down is *"a head start"* that makes the next display choice cheaper, *"never a replay of what worked once"* (Fig 4 :22, :24).
>
> **What I am not claiming.** That a norm determines which emotion is felt. That suppression is bad in every context; its valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49), so the same norm can help cohesion and cost well-being. That an emotion category names a fixed internal state; recognition treats an expression and a label as one referent, and under display rules that is a bond reading, not an identity. That any specific norm occupies a slot. The framework returns a shape, not a name; naming a display rule is a binding from your field with its own provenance, and I carry zero evidentiary weight on it until that provenance is shown.
>
> **What would refute it.** I wrote these before sending this.
> 1. One measure predicts expression, concealment and recognition with no slot-specific residual left over.
> 2. Perceivers from genuinely different evidence pools, each ranged against an independently measured target state, show criterion-equivalent recognition across norm groups.
> 3. Display behavior is invariant across audiences and norm contexts once the felt emotion is held fixed.
> 4. Concealment is shown to negate the felt emotion with no subtraction or replacement step, or masking and expressing become indistinguishable under one behavioural measure.
> 5. A norm is shown to be harmful or beneficial for every residual regardless of the goal.
>
> If any one holds, the corresponding claim is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Local canonical figure text: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` (cite as `Fig N :line`)
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop and the mode rule: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** take the loop, write down what an answer to your question would look like before you run it, bind it as an ordinary term, state its mode, and write the failure condition before you start. The framework's own rule is that a frame cannot check the direction of its own question. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thank you for your time either way.

## What is deliberately absent

No statement that a named researcher endorses this, no prestige list, no receptiveness ranking, no personal email, no bulk-send plan, and no request to forward. A reply is a conversation. If it contains a refutation, it is copied into `11_audit.md` with provenance and re-evaluated as if it came from any other source.
