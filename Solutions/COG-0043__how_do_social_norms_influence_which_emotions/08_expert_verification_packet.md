# Expert verification packet: the Tether on social norms and emotion

**What this is:** a claim in a form an emotion researcher, cross-cultural psychologist or social-norms scholar can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the separators and mechanism claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on which norm and which emotion, not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question is a union of three slots, not one question with one answer. Expression and concealment are convention slots: a display rule is a shared meaning about who may show what to whom, and agreement inside a group is the product of that convention rather than evidence about felt emotion. Recognition is a ground slot: a claim about what another person feels must be ranged against an independently measured target state, because agreement among perceivers who learned the same display grammar is one evidence pool and cannot corroborate a verdict about the world. The norm crosses from group to person as a head start at the vocabulary link, concealment is missing or replaced display rather than a refused claim, and the valence of any norm is relative to the residual. The framework returns that shape, five pre-registered failure conditions and the instruments that would close it. It does not name which norm or which emotion fills a slot.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 2 and 3
- **Statement:** the residual is indexed per slot; agreement has opposite valence in the display slots and the recognition slot; the norm acts at the least instrumented link.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 1 :19 **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
  - Fig 2 :41 **"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"**
  - Fig 2 :40 **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**
  - Fig 3 :24 **"Why the middle link is where chains usually break."** and Fig 3 :27 **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**
- **Refuter:** one measure predicts all three slots with no slot-specific residual left; or the vocabulary link is shown to be well instrumented and explains none of the disputed variance; or perceivers from genuinely independent evidence pools, each ranged against an independently labelled target set, show criterion-equivalent recognition across norm groups.

### C2: psychology-facing; checkable against the display-rule and recognition literature
- **Statement:** display modes are isomers over a fixed atom set; concealment is subtraction or replacement and never negation; the in-group advantage is the expected fingerprint of a perceiver-side criterion entering recognition.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 12 :117 **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
  - Operators table on subtraction: **"A with B removed or absent"**; on negation: **"this does not hold"**; and :73 **"Negation is the refusal of a claim."**
  - Fig 12 :67 identity: **"two names, one referent"**
- **What to check:** do masking, neutralizing, amplifying and simulating behave as different substances over the same emotional atoms in observers' responses (Elfenbein and Ambady 2002 report culturally specific non-verbal accents; Gallant et al. 2026 report 104 unique accents in posed expressions)? Does any concealment case genuinely negate the felt emotion rather than remove or replace its display? Is the in-group advantage better read as perceiver criterion than as target biology?
- **Refuter:** two display modes become behaviourally indistinguishable in the observer's response, or a concealment case shows genuine negation with no subtraction or replacement step, or expression and felt state are one referent for a perceiver in all norm contexts.

### C3: framework-internal derivation with a world-side check
- **Statement:** the norm reaches the person as a head start down the scale membrane, and its valence flips with the residual; a sanction-backed norm that fixes an outcome is not a convention.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 4 :19-20 **"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."**
  - Fig 4 :22 **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**
  - Fig 11 :49 **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
  - Fig 10 :47 **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**
- **What to check:** Ford and Mauss 2015 report fewer well-being costs of suppression in interdependent contexts. That is the sign flip the figure predicts. The SEP entry on Social Norms distinguishes conventions from sanction-backed norms by empirical and normative expectations. If a display rule is really a norm that decides an outcome, it falls under Fig 10 :47 and the convention reading is wrong for that case.
- **Refuter:** display behaviour is invariant across audiences once felt emotion is held fixed; or a norm is harmful or beneficial for every residual regardless of the goal; or a sanction-backed norm can decide an outcome and still be a pure convention.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that a norm determines which emotion is felt. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Naming a display rule is a binding from the world frame and carries provenance, not framework authority.
- Not that suppression is bad everywhere. Valence is residual-relative (Fig 11 :49).
- Not that recognition accuracy is the ground. It is a reading against a criterion, and several sources can give the same display (source essay on metamerism).
- Not that all norms are conventions. Fig 10 :47 governs the sanction-backed, outcome-fixing case.
- Not that concealment is negation. It is missing or replaced display (Operators :42/:73).
- Not `grounded`. No independent measurement was taken by this agent.
- Not a result of the eight essays alone. They share the framework author's evidence pool with the figures and carry no independent witness weight.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved the case. Here:

1. **Source discriminator:** independently measured target state paired with coded display, so felt state and shown state are not the same reading.
2. **Criterion test:** recognition accuracy and criterion on the same labelled target set for perceivers from different norm groups, with independence bought in proportion to the cost of error.
3. **Audience-invariance test:** felt emotion held fixed, display measured across norm contexts.
4. **Vocabulary-link instrument:** a measure of the display and reading terms actually available in a setting.
5. **Standing refuters:** the seven conditions in `01` §4.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, Fig 2 :40-41, Fig 2 :33-35 and Fig 3 :24, :27. Five short passages.
2. Check C2 against your own knowledge: are masking, neutralizing and simulating actually different substances in observers' responses, or one thing with three names?
3. Look at Ford and Mauss 2015. Does the reported cultural sign flip match Fig 11 :49, or is it a confound?
4. Name one instrument that would separate a felt state from its display, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
