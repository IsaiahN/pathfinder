# The Tether's answer: how social norms influence which emotions are expressed, concealed, or recognized

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain separators and the mechanism claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on which norm and which emotion occupy a slot. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question bundles three operations under one verb. The residual is the gap between a prediction of display and concealment and the observed display, plus the gap between a prediction of what an observer will read and the target's independently measured state. These are three slots (Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*), and the full question is a **union** of display control and recognition with an unassignable shared fraction (Fig 9 :54). It has no single verdict and no single ground.

## 2. The shape

1. **Expression and concealment are convention slots.** A display rule is a shared meaning about who may show what to whom. Fig 2 :41: *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."* Agreement inside the group is the product of the convention. It is not evidence about what any member feels.
2. **Recognition is a ground slot.** Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* The anchor must be independently measured. Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."* A perceiver who uses the display convention as their anchor drifts coherently with their group and does not notice (Fig 2 :34).
3. **The norm crosses the scale membrane as a head start.** Fig 4 :19-20: the kernel is the same loop at both sizes; *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* The norm makes the next display choice cheaper. It is not a replay of one occasion.
4. **The norm acts at the vocabulary link.** Fig 3 :24-27: *"Why the middle link is where chains usually break."* The link that supplies terms for what a situation affords has *"neither"* an obvious failure nor an obvious test, and *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Better measurement of recognition accuracy (link 1) cannot rescue a missing link 2 (Fig 3 :19).
5. **Concealment is missing or replaced display, not a refused claim.** Subtraction removes an operand; negation refuses a claim (Operators table :42/:73). The display modes are isomers over a fixed atom set (Fig 12 :117): same atoms, different bond, different substance.
6. **Recognition applies an identity bond over a produced display.** Fig 12 :67: identity is *"two names, one referent"*; Fig 12 :77: production is *"A produces B; B did not exist before."* Under display rules the expression is norm-shaped, so the identity bond is misapplied even when the judgment is accurate. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument.
7. **The valence of any norm is relative to the residual.** Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* A norm that suppresses anger can help cohesion and cost well-being; the sign is not carried by the norm.
8. **Two kinds of norm, not one.** Coordination display rules are conventions (Fig 2 :41). Sanction-backed norms that decide outcomes have *"stopped being one"* (Fig 10 :47). They differ in bond and anchor and must not be averaged.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that a norm determines which emotion a person feels. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that suppression is bad everywhere. Its valence is residual-relative, and the world literature reports fewer well-being costs in interdependent contexts.
- Not that recognition accuracy is the ground for recognition. Accuracy is a reading against a criterion; several sources can produce the same display.
- Not that all norms are conventions. A sanction-backed norm that decides an outcome has stopped being one (Fig 10 :47).
- Not that a figure line names a norm, an emotion, or a magnitude. That is a `gap`.
- Not `grounded`. No independent measurement of a target state was taken by this agent.

## 5. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved the case. Here:

- **Source discriminator:** a reading that separates felt state from displayed state (independent physiology or self-report paired with FACS-coded display, not the same measure twice). This would test F5 and F6.
- **Criterion test:** recognition accuracy and response criterion measured against the same independently labelled target set for perceivers from different norm groups, with independence bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42). This would test F4.
- **Audience-invariance test:** felt emotion held fixed, display measured across audiences who differ in the applicable norm. This would test F3.
- **Vocabulary-link instrument:** a measure of which display and reading terms are actually available to a person in a setting. This would test F10.
- **Standing falsifiers:** the seven conditions in `01` §4, written before the conclusion.

## 6. The honest stop

The answer stops at the shape. Which norm, which emotion and what fraction are `gap`: not underivable in principle, but unreachable at this budget without a source-discriminating instrument and a non-updating anchor for the recognition slot. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The figures README: *"Indiscernibility is not identity."* The record of the route, the refuters and the strains is what makes the stop checkable.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that social norms dictate which emotions people feel. It says the question splits into three slots that must not be averaged. Expression and concealment are convention slots: a display rule is a shared meaning, so agreement inside the group is the product of the convention and says nothing by itself about what anyone feels. Recognition is a ground slot: a claim about what another person feels must be ranged against an independently measured target state, because a perceiver who uses the group's display grammar as the anchor drifts together with everyone else and does not notice. The norm reaches the person as a head start at the vocabulary link, the least instrumented link in the chain, and concealment is a missing or replaced display rather than a refused emotion. Any single norm's valence flips with the residual, so no standing list of good or bad display rules survives. The framework returns that shape, its five failure conditions and the instruments that would close it. Which norm and which emotion fill the slots stays open until the ground settles it.
