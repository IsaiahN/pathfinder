## DERIVATION

### Q1: Are the display modes isomers over a fixed atom set?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Hold the atom set fixed: one felt emotion, one display channel, one audience. Gallant et al. 2026 list six display-rule modes: express as felt, amplify, deamplify, neutralize, mask or simulate.
- Under Fig 12 :117 those are **isomers**. The atoms do not change; the bond does. Showing anger, masking anger with a smile and neutralizing anger are different substances with the same ingredients, and the difference is decisive in behaviour (an observer reacts differently).
- The practical consequence: an instrument that records only "anger was displayed" has recorded the ingredients and not the arrangement. Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a single behavioural measure makes two display modes indistinguishable in the observer's response, the isomer claim fails for that pair and they collapse into one substance. Gallant et al.'s FACS-level differences in action units are the discriminating reading; if those differences do not track the modes, the claim is withdrawn.
- Status: `specified`, triangulated for the definition (Fig 12 plus the world list of modes), single-source for the figure's chemistry analogue.

### Q2: Which bond is concealment: subtraction, replacement, or negation?

- Operators table on `−`: **"A with B removed or absent"** (subtraction), with the test *"is the second operand's absence the point? Yes."*
- Operators table on `¬`: **"this does not hold"**, with the test *"is the point that it is false, rather than absent? Yes."*
- Operators table :73-74: *"Subtraction is the nearest bond and is not it. − is A with B removed or absent, which is the absence of an operand. Negation is the refusal of a claim. A vocabulary with − and without ¬ can say what is missing from an arrangement and cannot say that an arrangement is wrong, so it can only ever accumulate."*
- **Neutralization** is subtraction in the strict sense: the felt emotion is absent from the display, and its absence is the point.
- **Masking** is not bare subtraction. A different emotion is shown in its place. That is either a production bond (`⇒`, Fig 12 :77: *"A produces B; B did not exist before"*) over the same atoms, or a different arrangement of held atoms. Either way it is a bond change, not a new atom.
- **Concealment is not negation.** A person who hides anger has not refused the claim that they are angry. The emotion is missing from the display, not declared false.
- **Strain, kept visible:** the figure's `⇒` requires B not to exist before A fires. In masking, the smile already exists in the person's repertoire; it is selected, not produced from nothing. So `⇒` is a strained label. Re-census: masking is an isomer over the fixed atom set (Q1) with a selection relation, and the figure's bond vocabulary has no dedicated "select from held repertoire" bond. That is a claim about what has not been made yet, not a flaw in the figures (Fig 12 :90: *"A relation that fails all six is not a bond you are missing. It is a claim that has not been made yet."*). We record the strain rather than force a bond.
- **Falsifier:** if a concealment case is found where the person comes to hold that the emotion is false and the display change follows from that refusal, with no subtraction or replacement step, then negation governs and this answer is wrong.

### Q3: What bond does recognition apply?

- Identity `≡`: Fig 12 :67 **"two names, one referent"**, with the test *"substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No."*
- Production `⇒`: Fig 12 :77 **"A produces B; B did not exist before"**.
- A perceiver who reads "this face means anger" is applying the identity bond: the expression and the category are treated as one referent. That works only where display and feeling are not decoupled.
- Under a display rule the expression is produced by a norm-shaped process, so the identity bond is misapplied even when the resulting judgment is accurate. Accuracy and bond correctness are different things. Fig 12 :5 again: the bond carries information the operands do not, and a read of the face alone records half the molecule.
- **Dialect reading.** Elfenbein and Ambady 2002 describe culturally specific *"non-verbal accents"*, and Gallant et al. 2026 report 104 unique cultural accents in posed expressions. An accent is a different bond over the same core atoms. The in-group advantage then follows: a perceiver whose library contains the accent applies the identity bond correctly more often. That is structure, not mystery.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a perceiver for whom expression and felt state are one referent in every norm context, so the identity bond is always correct and no source discriminator is owed. Absent that, the source problem in `05` §6 stands.

### Q4: Where does the norm sit in the dependency chain, and does that give an order?

- Fig 3 :4-16 lists the five links: perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry.
- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**
- A norm supplies terms for what a situation affords: which emotion may be shown to this audience, which reading is licensed for this expression. That is link 2, vocabulary. It is not the percept (link 1) and not the choice (link 4).
- Fig 3 :24-27: **"Why the middle link is where chains usually break."** *"Perception has obvious failures and obvious tests. Planning has obvious failures and obvious tests. The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."* And: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
- **Order.** perception of emotion and audience (1) gives terms including the norm (2) gives a social objective (3) gives a chosen display (4) gives learning (5). The same chain runs for the perceiver with the expression as percept. De Leersnyder et al. 2013 corroborate that much regulation happens at elicitation, upstream of the response, which is link 2 and link 3 rather than link 4.
- **Fig 3 :19:** *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Better measurement of recognition accuracy (link 1) cannot rescue a missing vocabulary link. Gallant et al.'s finding that the disputed variance sits in production method, not in the count of recognition studies, is the same claim in world terms.
- **Falsifier:** if the vocabulary link is well instrumented and the disputed variance is fully explained at perception or planning, link 2 is not the break and this order is wrong.

### Q5: Are the three slots a functional group?

- Fig 12 :123-124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- All three slots share the substructure `agent → norm-term → response-to-emotion`. That fixes the kind: norm-mediated emotion handling. The qualifiers are the slot: display out, display in, reading of another's display.
- Over the full shared atom set the three are isomers (Q1). As usually reported in the literature they share only a family resemblance, so the weaker functional-group claim is the one that is safe without a controlled atom set.
- **Falsifier:** if a slot can be performed with no norm term at all, and still be the same kind of operation, the functional-group claim fails for that slot.

## CONCLUSION

The display modes are isomers over a fixed atom set (Fig 12 :117), concealment is subtraction or replacement and never negation (Operators :42/:73), recognition applies an identity bond where a norm-shaped production process actually holds (Fig 12 :67/:77), and the norm acts at the vocabulary link, the middle link Fig 3 says is least instrumented. The three slots form a functional group with qualifiers. The figure lines supply the structure; nothing here names which norm or which emotion, and nothing here is a ground reading. Two strains are carried forward and named: the bond label for masking (no dedicated "select from repertoire" bond in the figure's operator set), and the operand reading of the identity bond at recognition.
