## 0. The question in effect terms

The ordinary wording hides three different questions behind one verb, "influence". Stated as an effect, the residual R is:

> Given a felt emotion in a social setting, what is the gap between a prediction of what will be shown and hidden and what is actually shown and hidden, and what is the gap between a prediction of what an observer will read and what the target actually feels?

R is not one number. It splits at least three ways:

1. **Expression.** The person puts the felt emotion on display, possibly amplified or qualified.
2. **Concealment.** The person keeps the felt emotion off display, by neutralizing, masking, or simulating something else.
3. **Recognition.** A second person assigns an emotion to the first person's display.

The three are not symmetric. The first two are first-person actions on one's own emotion; the third is a third-person reading of another's display. The first two are about a convention (what may be shown to whom); the third is about a fact (what this person actually feels). That asymmetry is the whole derivation, and it is why they must never be averaged.

**Dependency order.** Following Fig 3, the chain runs perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry. Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."* A display rule is a term at the **vocabulary** link, link 2. It says what a situation affords: which emotion may be shown to whom. Fig 3 :24-27 says this is exactly the link that usually breaks, because it has *"neither"* an obvious failure nor an obvious test, and *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* So the norm does not act at the point where most measurement effort lands. This is a figure-derived prediction about where the residual lives.

**Partition or union.** Expression and concealment are a partition at the display layer (show or do not show). Recognition is a separate operation with a different anchor. The full question is a **union** of two overlapping conditions, display control and recognition, not one hard question. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :55 adds that *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."* The intersection, a norm that both suppresses a display and mis-shapes its reading, is a specific compound with its own loop.

## 1. Domain census (written before any figure line was chosen)

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor. A domain without a nameable anchor is not yet a domain.

**R itself.** Arity: three slots (express, conceal, recognize), so per-slot indexing is mandatory. Symmetry: broken between first-person action and third-person perception. Scale: the norm is a population-level regularity (many agents, a shared library); the operation is single-agent. This is a cross-scale membrane problem. Anchor: observable display for the first two slots; the target's actual felt state for the third.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1: display control (express, conceal) | the person, the felt emotion, the audience | displayed behavior and audience reaction | the display as measured behavior, plus the felt state measured independently | convention about shared meaning; agreement is the product |
| D2: the norm frame, population scale | the group, norm holders, sanctioners | shared expectations, display rules, social meaning | the convention itself, which coordinates but does not settle a fact about the world | two kinds, see F9: coordination convention versus sanction-backed norm |
| D3: recognition, third-person | perceiver, target, target's expression | recognition judgment and its accuracy | the target's actual emotion, an anchor that must not move when the perceiver moves | verdict about the world; agreement from one pool is disqualifying |

**No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.** A claim about "the emotion itself, independent of any measure" has no anchor at this budget. It is not treated as a domain and is recorded as a `gap` in §7.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Perceive the emotion and the audience (link 1); have terms for what the situation affords, which is where the norm sits (link 2); say what would count as a successful interaction (link 3); choose the display (link 4); learn and carry (link 5). For the perceiver the same chain runs with the expression as the percept. The norm acts at link 2 for both, which is the middle link Fig 3 says nobody has instrumented.

**Union, not partition.** D1 and D3 overlap where a display becomes a percept. D2 is the shared frame that supplies link-2 terms to both. One lever cannot clear the compound.

## 3. Findings

### F1: The residual is indexed by slot, not by a single "influence" score (decisive for the framing)

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

The three operations are three slots. A study that reports one number for "emotional expression" has already committed the error the figure names: the live residual on the concealment slot or the recognition slot vanishes into the average. The tacit-knowledge essay makes the same point in world terms: shared pretext is *"good for one thing and ruins another. Agreement is the whole point when you're settling what words mean. It's worthless for settling what's true when both sides learned from the same sources."* That is a one-slot reading dressed as a verdict.

**Mode:** `general`, structural. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 1 slot indexing plus Fig 9 union/partition, which are low-overlap: one governs measurement, the other governs problem splitting). **Refuter:** show one measure that predicts expression, concealment and recognition with no slot-specific residual left over. If it does, the three-slot framing is unnecessary and is withdrawn.

**Strain test.** Does "slot" mean the person, the emotion, or the operation? The Fig 1 definition says *"One object, region or entity being tracked"* (Symbols table :43). Here the tracked objects are the three operations on one emotion. That is a consistent reading, not a strain. If a reader insists the slot is the person, the framing fails and the re-census in §1 must be redone.

### F2: Two slots are a convention; the third is an anchor question

Fig 2 :39: **"Correlation has valence."**
Fig 2 :40: **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**
Fig 2 :41: **"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"**

Expression and concealment are governed by display rules, which are conventions about shared meaning. When members of a group agree that anger is shown to in-group members and hidden from out-group members, that agreement is the product, not a corrupted measurement. Recognition is different: it is a claim that this target feels anger, which is a fact about the world. Agreement among perceivers who learned the same display grammar is one evidence pool talking.

The world literature supplies the exact vocabulary. Chiao 2015 writes: *"Cultural display rules provide a set of social norms concerning when it is appropriate to express emotion, to whom and to what extent."* Gallant et al. 2026 write that *"display rules are culturally learned guidelines that dictate how and when emotions should be shown, concealed, or exaggerated."* Both are descriptions of conventions. Elfenbein and Ambady 2002 supply the recognition side: *"Accuracy was higher when emotions were both expressed and recognized by members of the same national, ethnic, or regional group, suggesting an in-group advantage."* That advantage is the perceiver's grammar entering the measurement.

**Mode:** `specified` (the mapping of the three slots onto the two valence cases). **Status:** triangulated (Fig 2 correlation valence plus Fig 10 :47, low overlap: one governs what agreement means, the other governs when a convention stops being one). **Refuter:** exhibit a recognition case where perceivers from genuinely different evidence pools, each ranged against the target's independently measured state, converge and that convergence is the product of shared meaning rather than a shared pool; or a display case where agreement is a verdict about the world rather than a convention.

### F3: The norm crosses the scale membrane as a head start, not a recording

Fig 4 :19-20: **"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."**
Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**
Fig 4 :24: **"downward: something that makes the next search cheaper. Never a replay of what worked once."**

The loop is the same at both sizes: perceive the emotion and the audience, choose a display, observe the reaction, carry what worked. What changes is the cast. At group scale the actors are many people with a shared library, the currency is shared meaning and sanction, the anchor is the coordination problem. At person scale the actors are one person and an audience, the currency is displayed behavior, the anchor is the outcome of the interaction. A display rule handed down makes the person's next choice cheaper: they need not recompute which emotion is safe with this audience. It is a head start. A verbatim replay of one ancestor's expression would be a recording, and Fig 4 :25 says a recording *"looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*

**Mode:** `specified` on the application, `general` on the membrane rule. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 4 scale filter plus Fig 11 :39, *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"*, low overlap: one governs what crosses a size boundary, the other governs where capability lives). **Refuter:** show a norm that determines display as a replay of one occasion with no reapplicable method, or show a person's display capability that is unchanged when their norm habitat changes. Either breaks the head-start reading.

### F4: Recognition must range against an anchor that does not move with the perceiver

Fig 2 :33: **"The anchor must not update."**
Fig 2 :34: **"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."**
Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

If the perceiver uses the group's display convention as their ground, that is, assumes that what is shown is what is felt, then the supposed anchor is made of the same frames it is supposed to correct. The result is coherent drift: everyone reads everyone correctly according to the convention and the error against the target's actual state is invisible. This is the named collapse, not a rhetorical objection. The in-group advantage is its empirical fingerprint: recognition is tuned by who the expresser is relative to the perceiver, so the criterion has moved with the frame.

The tacit-knowledge essay states the safeguard: *"Two people who only check against each other drift, and they drift together, so neither notices."* And: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it"* (Fig 2 :45, quoted in that essay). The ground for recognition has to be an independently measured target state, not the agreement of the room.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 2 :33-35 anchor invariance plus Fig 10 :18, *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"*, low overlap: ground invariance versus the status of declared meanings). **Refuter:** exhibit a perceiver whose recognition criterion is demonstrably independent of their norm group, with equivalent accuracy and criterion across groups against an independent ground. That would show display conventions do not enter the recognition channel.

### F5: Concealment is subtraction or replacement, not negation

Operators table :42: **"A with B removed or absent"** (subtraction).
Operators table :73: **"Negation is the refusal of a claim."**
Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Masking, neutralizing and simulating are ways of removing the felt emotion from the display or putting a different one in its place. That is subtraction (`−`) or a different bond over the same atoms. It is not negation (`¬`), which refuses a claim. A person who hides anger has not thereby asserted that anger is false or absent in the world; they have changed what is shown. A vocabulary with `−` and without `¬`, the Operators table says at :74, *"can say what is missing from an arrangement and cannot say that an arrangement is wrong, so it can only ever accumulate."* For this question the consequence is that concealment can be accumulated as a display practice without the underlying emotion being refused or changed.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** single-source on the figure side (Operators and Fig 12 are one bond vocabulary), triangulated with the world literature on display-rule modes (Gallant et al. 2026 list deamplification, amplification, neutralization, masking, qualification and simulation as distinct operations). **Refuter:** show a concealment operation that genuinely negates the felt emotion, with the person coming to hold that the emotion is not the case, and with no subtraction or replacement step. Also refuted if a single measure makes masking and expressing indistinguishable, since Fig 12 :117 says the difference is decisive in behaviour.

**Strain test, kept visible.** Pure subtraction is "A without B", but masking is "A with C shown instead". That is not the bare subtraction bond. Re-census: the atoms are the felt emotion and the available display, and the bond differs. This is the isomer reading in F5, not a failed fit. The strain is whether "replacement with another emotion" is a production bond (`⇒`, *"A produces B; B did not exist before"*, Fig 12 :77) or a different arrangement of held atoms. It is named in `06` Q3 and is not smoothed over.

### F6: Recognition applies an identity bond where a produced display is read

Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**
Fig 12 :67: **"two names, one referent"** (identity, `≡`).
Fig 12 :77: **"A produces B; B did not exist before"** (production, `⇒`).

To recognize anger is to treat the expression and the category "anger" as one referent. That is the identity bond. But under display rules the expression is produced by a norm-shaped process; it is not identical to the felt emotion. The bond carries information the operands do not, and a perceiver who reads only the face has recorded half of the molecule. Fig 12 :94 then governs the diagnosis: *"The arrangement is the substance."* The same face under a different display rule is a different substance even though the ingredient list is unchanged.

This is also why "recognition accuracy" is a thin instrument on its own. An accuracy score treats the identity bond as if it were correct by stipulation. The instrument owed is a source discriminator, one that distinguishes a felt state from the display that purports to show it. *The Hard Problem is About Source* makes the general case: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"*, and the fix was not an argument but a better instrument.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** single-source for the figure reading (Fig 12), with world corroboration from the in-group advantage and the metamerism essay. **Refuter:** exhibit a perceiver for whom expression and emotion label are genuinely one referent across all norm contexts, so the identity bond is correct and no source discriminator is owed.

### F7: The valence of a norm is relative to the residual, not carried by the norm

Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
Fig 11 :50: **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**
Fig 11 :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

A norm that suppresses anger is beneficial for a cohesion residual and costly for an authentic-expression or well-being residual. Ford and Mauss 2015 report exactly this sign flip: *"empirical research largely supports the hypothesis that suppression carries fewer well-being costs for individuals from interdependent (vs. independent) cultures."* De Leersnyder, Boiger and Mesquita 2013 add that *"The combined evidence suggests that cultural differences in emotion regulation go well beyond the effortful regulation based on display rules"*, so the norm acts upstream at elicitation as well as at display. A standing verdict such as "suppression is bad" is the error Fig 11 :51 names: it treats a relation as a kind.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 11 residual-relative valence plus Fig 2 :39-41 correlation valence, low overlap: ecological valence versus the valence of agreement). **Refuter:** show a norm that is beneficial, or harmful, for every residual regardless of the goal; then valence is intrinsic and Fig 11 :49 does not govern this case.

### F8: The framework returns a shape: three slots, a head start, two verdict kinds, no named emotion

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**
Fig 9 :27-28: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**

What survives the census is the shape: influence runs through a norm-to-person membrane, acts at the vocabulary link, is indexed per slot, carries a convention verdict for display and an anchor verdict for recognition, and has residual-relative valence. The figures do not name which norm or which emotion occupies a slot, and they do not return a magnitude. That is a `gap`, recorded in §7 and in `07` and `09`, not a silence.

The Library of Babel essay gives the stopping rule: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."* And the warning about premature framing: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"*

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 9 :32 plus the canonical README's *"a shape, not a name"* limit). **Refuter:** derive a specific norm and emotion for a slot from the figures alone, with no world import. If that is possible, the gap closes and this finding is withdrawn.

### F9: Two kinds of norm, resolved by re-census, not by strain

The first pass treated every norm as a display convention. That forces a contradiction. Fig 2 :41 says agreement about shared meaning is the product, but sanction-backed norms are not merely shared meanings; the SEP entry by Bicchieri, Muldoon and Sontuoso 2023 distinguishes them by empirical and normative expectations and by sanctions. Fig 10 :47 says: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**, and Fig 10 :48 adds *"a convention nothing can check is a constant the seat authored, carrying the seat's authority."*

**Strain test:** if all norms are forced into "head start" (Fig 4 :22), the sanctioned, outcome-deciding case cannot be represented, and a figure line only fits by straining. Per the canonical usage rule, a strained line governs another quantity, so re-census.

**Re-census.** Two objects:
- Coordination display rules: shared meaning, agreement is the product, head start down the membrane (Fig 2 :41, Fig 4 :22).
- Sanction-backed norms: normative expectations and punishment, a convention that has begun to decide an outcome (Fig 10 :47-48, SEP 2023).

They share the network atom (Fig 13 :24, *"that entities relate, and influence travels"*) but differ in bond and anchor. No figure contradicts another after the split. The first-pass reading is retracted and preserved in §7.

### F10: Where the chain breaks: the vocabulary link

Fig 3 :24-27: **"Why the middle link is where chains usually break."** *"Perception has obvious failures and obvious tests. Planning has obvious failures and obvious tests. The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."* And Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

The norm supplies link-2 terms: which emotion is available for display to this audience, which reading is available for this expression. That is precisely the link Fig 3 says is least instrumented. The literature corroborates the asymmetry: Gallant et al. 2026 note that *"most cross-cultural studies focus on recognition, while the production of expressions has received comparatively little attention"*, and their review shows the disputed effect sits in how expressions are produced (posed versus spontaneous), which is a link-2 question.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** single-source on the figure side (Fig 3), world corroboration from the production review. **Refuter:** show a well-instrumented vocabulary link for display rules in which the disputed variance is fully explained at perception or at planning; then link 2 is not the break.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the standing instruction that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. One measure predicts expression, concealment and recognition with no slot-specific residual left (breaks F1, Fig 1 :19 and :22).
2. Perceivers from genuinely different evidence pools, each ranged against an independently measured target state, show criterion-equivalent recognition across norm groups (breaks F4 and the in-group-advantage prediction).
3. Display behavior is invariant across audiences and norm contexts once the felt emotion is held fixed (breaks F3, the head-start crossing).
4. A concealment operation is demonstrated that negates the felt emotion with no subtraction or replacement step, or masking and expressing become indistinguishable under a single behavioural measure (breaks F5).
5. A norm is shown to be harmful or beneficial for all residuals regardless of the goal (breaks F7, Fig 11 :49).
6. A specific norm and emotion are derivable for a slot from the figures alone, with no world import (closes F8's gap and disproves the shape-only limit).
7. The vocabulary link is shown to be well instrumented and the disputed variance is fully explained upstream or downstream (breaks F10).

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn, not softened. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Unbundle before answering. Report three verdicts, one per slot, and keep the display-versus-recognition intersection as its own compound (F1, Fig 9 :54).
2. Treat display rules as conventions (Fig 2 :41) and recognition as a ground question (Fig 2 :33-35). Do not let agreement on a display grammar count as accuracy about a felt state.
3. Send the generator, not a recording. Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* The first contact carries the residual, the shape and the refuter.
4. Range recognition against an independently measured target state, not the agreement of the room. Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*).
5. State the mode on every claim, including the world literature. An observed cultural difference is a measurement; the three-slot shape is `general`; the mechanism mapping is `specified`.
6. Do not name a norm or an emotion as the answer to a slot. That is a binding from the world frame and must carry its provenance (F8).
7. Keep the two norm kinds separate (F9). A sanction-backed norm that fixes an outcome is not a display convention, and treating it as one hides the seat's authority.

## 7. The gap, stated plainly

No figure line names a norm, an emotion, or a magnitude. After a genuine census of all thirteen figures plus both tables, the specific occupancy of a slot remains `gap`. This is a verdict, not a silence: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"* (figures README quoting Fig 13's honest-stop rule). What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument that separates a felt state from its display and a non-updating ground for the recognition slot, not more agreement.
