# What kind of structure a network is, and what order the cascade runs

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question names a network but does not say what kind of thing a network is, nor in what order transmission should be read. Two questions are owed: (1) is the ecological network a new primitive, an isomer of a structure already in the six-term set, or a functional group; and (2) what is the dependency order of a route, and at which order does the consequential residual sit.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held in the framework: Space, Time, Energy (states) and Spectrum, Gradient, Network (structures). Bonds held: the seven, including `+` conjunction, `→` sequence, `⇒` production and `⋛` comparison; plus negation. No new primitive is available without import.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 : Is "network" a seventh term?

- Fig 13 :6 "THE SIX, IN TWO KINDS"; :17 "structures"; :23 "network"; :24 "that entities relate, and influence travels".
- Fig 13 :41-44: each of the six is indivisible and "A continuum was the seventh and was removed for failing exactly this: it is a spectrum without gaps, or smoothness over space and time. A generating set must not contain what its own members produce."
- Network is **already one of the six**. An ecological contact network is not a seventh primitive; it is an instance of the held structure Network with a particular cast of actors. This is a binding, not an import that moves the wall (Fig 6 :26 "The world does not gain primitives; a frame gains access.").
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if ecological contact networks required a primitive not reducible to related entities with travelling influence, the six-term set would be incomplete. I would withdraw F2 in `01` and mark the network claim `gap`.

### Q2 : Is a network an isomer, a chain, or a functional group?

- Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Fig 12 :48 "A chain is the degenerate case: one bond type, no branching."
- A pure host-to-host chain is the degenerate case: one bond type, no branching, and no way to represent a vector, an environmental stage or a dead end. It is the form Option A would impose in `01` §0.
- A network over the same cast of actors with different contact bonds (direct, vector-borne, environmental, trophic) is Fig 12's isomer reading: same atoms, different arrangement, different behaviour. Two landscapes with the same species can behave differently because the bonds differ.
- The weaker reading: all ecological disease routes share the functional group `network-over-actors-with-travelling-influence`, and the qualifier (which pathogen) says which member. This survives even if the stronger isomer claim is refused.
- **Falsifier:** if two landscapes with identical actors and different contact bonds produced identical transmission outcomes in every measured case, the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim stands.

### Q3 : What is the order of a route?

Dependency order from Fig 3, applied to a route (Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces"):

1. **perception:** is the infection registered at all? Surveillance quality decides this. Roberts et al. 2021: "Many spillover events are undetected, misdiagnosed or unreported."
2. **vocabulary:** is there a term for what a host affords the pathogen (reservoir, bridge, competent, dead end)? Craft 2015 Box 1 begins "Define pathogen of interest" for exactly this reason.
3. **objective:** what would count as progress, in effect terms? Here: a route whose residual stops registering at a stated order.
4. **enumeration:** list contacts outward until the cascade stops mattering (Fig 11 :43-44).
5. **carry:** record which contact types and which roles paid, so the next landscape starts from a head start rather than a replay (Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down.").

A reading taken below the break is not a weak reading, it is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22-23). A control plan written before steps 1-4 is such a reading.

### Q4 : At which order does the consequential residual sit?

- Fig 11 :52-53: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."
- Order 1: direct host-to-host contact inside the affected class.
- Order 2: a bridge host or vector connecting classes.
- Order 3: environmental persistence, land-use change, or a trophic side effect.
- The framework does not fix the order in advance. It requires the enumeration to continue until the residual stops. Declaring the route complete at order 1 is the specific failure the figure names.
- **Falsifier (refuter 1 of the audit):** a named disease whose residual is zero after order-1 enumeration, measured, with no order-2 or order-3 contributor. The third-order claim is then withdrawn for that case.

### Q5 : How is a host jump like a non-invertible transform?

- Fig 10 :70 "If levels were copies there would be one lineage drifting and no branches. The tree of life is what a non-invertible transform looks like from outside."
- Fig 10 :68 "Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between."
- A pathogen entering a new host species is not copied into an identical role. The new habitat remakes what it does; the branching phylogeny is the visible remainder. Roberts et al. 2021 distinguish "spillover" (infection of a novel host population) from "host shifting" (infection of a novel host species) and note variation in competence, which is the world-facing shape of the same asymmetry.
- **Falsifier:** a documented cross-species transfer with no remainder, no changed competence and no branching. That would break the non-invertibility reading.

## CONCLUSION

An ecological network is an instance of a held structure, not a seventh primitive. Relative to a fixed cast it reads as an isomer: same actors, different contact bonds, different behaviour; the weaker and safer claim is a shared functional group. A route is ordered by dependency from perception to enumeration to carry, and the consequential residual is expected at order two or three, not assumed at order one. A host jump is a non-invertible transform whose branching is visible from outside. Three tempting analogues were refused after the strain test. The isomer and order claims are `specified`, ungrounded, each with a stated falsifier.
