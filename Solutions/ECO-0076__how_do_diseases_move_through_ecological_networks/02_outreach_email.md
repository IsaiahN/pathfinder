# Outreach draft : check the output before deciding about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. There are no personal emails in this folder.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until a checker has been asked for their own enumeration and their verdict on stage 1 is recorded. If the shape claim does not survive that, there is nothing else to offer.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about how a disease route should be described

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a question in disease ecology: how a pathogen moves through a network that connects wildlife, livestock and people. I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read any framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with me. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` as applied to this host system, `general` as a structural rule):**
>
> 1. The route is not read off a list of host classes. The habitat is enumerated from the pathogen outward: everything in contact with the infection, then everything in contact with those, until the cascade stops mattering. Contacts not yet observed are not absent; they are the residual.
> 2. The network is a structural term in its own right. It is the structure in which entities relate and influence travels, not a sum of hosts.
> 3. Roles are assigned by the pathogen, not carried by the species. The same actor can be a reservoir for one pathogen, a bridge or amplifying host for another, and a dead end for a third. There is no standing list of good or bad hosts.
> 4. The consequential step is often not the direct one. In the figure's own ecological case, first-order enumeration missed an effect that sat three steps out. So a first-order contact list is a hypothesis, not a completed route.
> 5. Management adds actors to a habitat; it does not subtract the habitat. Contact is a fact about the world and does not consult the manager.
>
> I am not claiming to have identified the route of any particular disease. I do not know which host carried which infection in your system, and I am not asking you to accept a named path.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim** (written down before I wrote any of this):
> 1. a named disease whose transmission is fully accounted for by first-order direct contacts, with no bridge, vector, environmental stage or third-order effect, and with residual zero after enumeration;
> 2. a host class whose role as source, bridge or sink is fixed across pathogens, so the same actor cannot be re-assigned when the pathogen changes;
> 3. an intervention that deletes a contact from the habitat and keeps it deleted without maintenance;
> 4. a lossless, non-branching cross-species transfer.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw that claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would like from you in twenty minutes:** name the contacts you would enumerate for one pathogen you know well, and say at which order you would expect to find the consequential transmission. That is all. Your answer on this is recorded before I show you anything else.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`. The relevant passages are Fig 11 :43-44, :49-53 and :62, Fig 13 :23-24, and Fig 6 :23.
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`.
> - The eight-step loop and habitat/mode rule: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`.
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Output first, framework second | Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Fig 2 :45 "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." |
| No request for agreement | Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." A reply selected for likely assent is a convention, not a check. |
| Pre-registered refuter | Seat Map :287: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. It was written in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` before this email. |
| Mode stated on every claim | `THE_FORMULA.md`: "Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which. Accepted means the ground settled it; otherwise candidate." |
| Roles not named as fixed kinds | Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." |
| First-order list is a hypothesis | Fig 11 :52 "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." |
| No named route asserted | The framework's limit is a shape, not a name: what is owed is the form of the route and the refuter, not a specific transmission chain. |
| No prestige ask | *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it." |
| Offer the formula as a method, after stage 1 | Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." A generator can be given away without giving away an answer. |
| Effect descriptions travel | *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." Hence the ask for an expected enumeration. |
