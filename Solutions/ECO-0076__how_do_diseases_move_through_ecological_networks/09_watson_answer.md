## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether licenses us to say about the route a pathogen takes through a network that connects wildlife, livestock and people. Option A: describe movement as a chain of direct host-to-host contacts and treat a named path as the answer. Option B: describe movement as a contact cascade over an enumerated habitat, with roles assigned by the pathogen, and return a shape plus refuters while the named path stays open. What each leads to: A omits bridge hosts, vectors, environmental stages and third-order effects, and presents a complete-looking route that is not complete; B requires an enumeration up front but states exactly which links must be measured and what would reverse it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** An infection in one host class not accounted for by a direct-contact list inside that class. Arity: multi-condition, at least donor, recipient and pathogen plus vector, environment and non-host species, so a **union compound** with its own loop (Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."). Symmetry: physical contact may be symmetric, but transmission along the edge is directed; the network is that in which influence travels. Scale: order 1 direct contact, order 2 bridge or vector, order 3 environmental persistence, land use or trophic cascade.
- **Domain E, ecological habitat and contact frame.** Actors: wildlife, livestock, people, vectors, soil and water, predators, competitors. Currency: contact and the transmission travelling along it. Anchor: observed infection pattern in a named host population, which does not move when the modeller changes their prior. Union with P and M; a bridge host sits in all three.
- **Domain P, pathogen and host-shift frame.** Actors: strains, mutations, receptor compatibility, competence. Currency: reproduction and host shifting. Anchor: sequence and phylogenetic data and observed host range, which do not move when a virologist changes a hypothesis. Shares a boundary with E; a host jump is contact between closures.
- **Domain M, management and intervention frame.** Actors: managers, surveillance, vaccination, movement and land-use controls. Currency: modification of contact rates. Anchor: post-intervention outcome, which does not move when a manager prefers a cheaper policy. Depends on E and P.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Describe the residual before naming a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); enumerate contacts outward (Fig 11 :43-44); assign valence relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49); trace the cascade to its stopping order (Fig 11 :52); intervene last, with addition available and subtraction not (Fig 11 :62).

Only head starts come down." was tried as a cross-species line and declined (it governs what passes between research scales); Fig 6 :22 "NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world." was declined (library novelty, not epidemiological novelty); Fig 12's sequence bond was declined as a transmission chain (it would collapse the branching to the degenerate case). ## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives and the named path does not. A disease route through wildlife, livestock and people has the form of a contact cascade over an enumerated habitat, with network as the structure in which influence travels, roles borrowed from the pathogen, the consequential step typically at order two or three, and management adding actors to a habitat it cannot subtract. Domain E is `specified` and triangulated; domains P and M are `general` and single-source in the figures, with world corroboration that is real but pooled. Nothing is `grounded`. The named route of any named pathogen is a `gap` awaiting paired contact and genetic or serological data in one landscape, not a silence. Refuters 1 to 5 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are pre-registered and un-fired.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, three checkable sub-claims, verbatim lines and refuters before the conclusion, so an error is findable. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a recording instead of a generator, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and the refuter. A second cost is presenting a first-order contact list as complete, which would misdirect surveillance toward the wrong host class.
- **undoable:** yes. The shape claim can be narrowed without withdrawing the derivation. If refuter 1 fires for one disease, the third-order expectation is withdrawn for that case; if refuter 2 fires, role relativity is replaced by a fixed-role claim for that pathogen; if refuter 5 fires, the network structure claim returns to census. No primitive has to be withdrawn and no sent claim is irrecoverable, because a corrected packet supersedes it.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. Paired contact and genetic or serological data showing that a named disease's residual is zero after order-1 enumeration (refuter 1), that a host class keeps fixed valence across pathogens (refuter 2), that a contact can be deleted from a habitat without maintenance (refuter 3), that a cross-species transfer is lossless and non-branching (refuter 4), or a re-census deriving Network from the other five substrate terms (refuter 5). A review agreeing with the shape is not such an instrument. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
