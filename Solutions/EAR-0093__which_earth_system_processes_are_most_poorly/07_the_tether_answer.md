# The Tether's answer: which Earth-system processes are most poorly represented in global models?

**Mode: `general` on the ranking rule, `specified` on the candidate set. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a verdict. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

---

## 1. The residual it answers

A global model predicts, per process slot, what its parameterizations say should happen. Observations return what happened. The gap `R_s = |Γ(b,a) - o'|` is indexed by slot. The question asks which slot is worst.

The question is a **union compound**, not one hard ranking (Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*). "Poorly represented" bundles four conditions with different remedies: a process absent from the model's representable set, a process present but mis-attached, a process present but unsettled because no process-level instrument returns a reading, and a process whose residual is hidden by compensating errors elsewhere. It has its own loop and its own ground.

## 2. The shape

1. **Rank per slot, never by a global score.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A near-zero global residual with one live slot is a legal state, and compensation between slots is the ordinary case. W2 (Wang et al., 2026) found six models simulating cloud optical depth reasonably well *"although through compensating errors"* while all six underestimated cloud cover response.

2. **The target is the persistent, non-trending residual.** Fig 5 :59: *"a term whose residual PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN is the one to point the refusal at"*. "Most poorly represented" does not mean largest one-off error. It means the slot that keeps being wrong while other slots improve under sustained effort.

3. **Only an unmoving ground may rank.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Multimodel spread is a filter for where to aim an instrument, never a witness. Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."*

4. **The worst slot is at an unmeasured middle link.** Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Fig 3 :26 says this link *"attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."* W1 (Morrison et al., 2020) documents the case: individual microphysical process rates are hard to obtain from natural observations, and even in the laboratory what can be measured differs from what schemes need. Global mean temperature and top-of-atmosphere flux, by contrast, have obvious instruments.

5. **Absent and unfound issue different instructions.** Fig 6 :34: *"Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."* Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* For an absent term, OUTWARD import with a source gate; for a mis-attached term, rebinding; for an unsettled term, INWARD instrument work from something already returning a reading; for hidden compensation, a return to per-slot indexing.

6. **The answer is a kind of thing, not a name.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. The figures describe a slot shape: persistent residual, at a no-instrument link, with a specified failing bond. They do not and cannot say "clouds". World sources supply three `specified` candidates, with no ground order among them:

| candidate | witness | mode |
|---|---|---|
| cloud microphysics and aerosol-cloud interaction | W2: all six models miss cloud cover response despite tuning five schemes; W6: cloud feedback remains the largest contribution to overall uncertainty in climate sensitivity | `specified`, ungrounded |
| marine ice-sheet dynamics and ice-cliff failure | W5: adding hydrofracture and marine ice-cliff failure to a coupled model changes high-end sea-level projections | `specified`, ungrounded |
| permafrost carbon feedback | W4: magnitude and timing of greenhouse gas release *"remain uncertain"* | `specified`, ungrounded |

7. **The ranking is second-order.** Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."* Each process residual must be settled before it enters a cross-process comparison. The comparison is a `⋛` relation at a threshold, not a new process or a unified theory.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "clouds are the answer". W6 ranks uncertainty contributions to climate sensitivity, which is one objective, not a cross-process verdict about representation quality in general.
- Not that every global model lacks ice-sheet processes. W5 describes a process addition in one model family. Absence must be established for a named model before import.
- Not that permafrost carbon is already in every model's objective. W4 says magnitude and timing remain uncertain, which is an unsettled term, not proof of absence.
- Not a single global complexity score. Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*
- Not grounded. No process-level instrument comparison was run in this derivation.
- Not a derivation of one process from another, and no new primitive.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument per slot, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

- **Process-rate discriminator:** a measurement that returns individual ice-phase or autoconversion rates, rather than the net cloud property that is their projection (W1 names laboratory work and new probes as existing edges).
- **Cloud susceptibility constraint:** the Holuhraun 2014 eruption used as an anchor case in W2. It is already returning a process-level reading against which six models fail. This is INWARD, extending an instrument that works.
- **Paleo constraint on ice-sheet processes:** W5's calibration against Last Interglacial and Pliocene sea level is a source gate for importing hydrofracture and ice-cliff failure.
- **Long soil-carbon record:** W4's research strategy for permafrost carbon, including abrupt thaw features, is the instrument programme for that slot.
- **Standing falsifiers from `01` section 3:** a per-process ranking whose worst slot lies above the broken link; a consensus ranking that changes what an instrument returns; a candidate residual that trends down while unlisted slots persist; a single protocol collapsing all four conditions; a figure re-census that ranks processes by name.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** No opened source supplies a cross-process, per-slot instrument comparison. W1, W2, W4 and W5 each address one process; W6 addresses one objective. Reporting this is required by Fig 8 :39: a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence. The six-term substrate lesson transfers in kind: the choice of which processes count as slots is a visible choice, and declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral (*They're Scanning Books*: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."*). The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *How Intelligence Actually Works*: *"indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name a worst-represented Earth-system process, and it does not claim a final ranking. It says the question has to be asked per process slot, because a global score hides a live slot residual through compensating error. It says the target is the residual that persists and does not trend down, that only an instrument that does not move when models push on it may rank the slots, and that the worst slot is likely at a link nobody has measured rather than at the one everybody discusses. It says "absent from the model", "wrongly attached", "unsettled" and "hidden by compensation" are four different conditions with four different remedies, and that a cross-process ranking is second-order and must wait for each process to be settled. Published work makes cloud microphysics, marine ice-sheet dynamics and permafrost carbon the leading candidates, but it records them as `specified` witnesses with no ground order. What the framework offers is the shape any answer must have, falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The top slot stays open until the ground settles it.
