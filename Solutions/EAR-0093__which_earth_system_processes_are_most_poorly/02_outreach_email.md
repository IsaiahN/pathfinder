# Outreach draft: check one output before you decide whether the framing is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the candidate ranking in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 5 is read as `specified` and not as a verdict. If the recipient hears a name rather than a rule, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Which model process is worst: one check on the ranking rule before the framing

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on the question of which Earth-system processes are most poorly represented in global models. I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read any framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the rule, `specified` on the candidates, not `grounded`):** a global skill score cannot answer this question, because the quantity is indexed by process slot. A near-zero global residual with one live slot residual is a legal state. Compensating errors between slots are the ordinary case, not a caveat. So "most poorly represented" has to be unbundled before it is ranked: a process can be absent from a model's representable set, present but mis-attached, present but unsettled because no process-level instrument returns a reading, or hidden by compensation elsewhere. Those four conditions issue different instructions and do not share one loop.
>
> The slot to point at is the one whose residual persists and does not trend down while other slots improve. That is a ranking rule, not a name. The framework I am using returns a shape, not a name, so it does not tell me whether the leading slot is cloud microphysics, marine ice-sheet dynamics, or permafrost carbon. Published work makes all three plausible witnesses: cloud cover response is underestimated across six models despite tuning five schemes; ice-shelf hydrofracture and ice-cliff failure change high-end sea-level projections when added; the magnitude and timing of permafrost carbon release remain uncertain. I record those as `specified` candidates with no ground order among them.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that clouds are the answer because an assessment calls cloud feedback the largest contribution to uncertainty in climate sensitivity. That is one objective in one assessment, not a cross-process verdict. I am also not claiming that every global model lacks ice-sheet processes. Absence has to be established for a named model before anything is imported.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before this message:**
> 1. a per-process instrument comparison, independent of the model families it ranks, in which the worst slot lies at a step that already has a clean process-level instrument;
> 2. a consensus ranking of processes, with no independent instrument behind it, that changes what an instrument returns;
> 3. one of the three candidate residuals that trends monotonically down under sustained scheme improvement while slots I did not list persist;
> 4. a single measurement protocol in which absent, mis-attached, unsettled and hidden collapse into one instruction.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, the framing is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the working note and the public source links are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Audit with the verbatim figure lines and the retraction log: local file `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` in this packet
> - Morrison et al., 2020, on the challenge of representing cloud and precipitation microphysics: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7507216/
> - Wang et al., 2026, on structural uncertainty in aerosol-cloud response across six models: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-67359-3
> - IPCC AR6 WG1 Chapter 7, on cloud feedback and overall uncertainty: https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/chapter/chapter-7/
>
> **If you want to use the method:** the derivation rule is in the audit. Run it on a process you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Best,
> [name]
