## RESIDUAL

The audit unbundled "most poorly represented" into four conditions: absent from `closure(Γ)`, present but mis-attached, present but unsettled, and hidden by compensation. Two questions are now owed:

1. Are those four **isomers** under Fig 12, that is the same atoms arranged differently, or are they different kinds with different atoms?
2. What is the **order** of the searches owed to each, and can the cross-process ranking be composed before the individual process residuals are settled?

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Are the four conditions isomers?

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

All four conditions can be written over the same atom set `{p, Γ_s, o'_s, R_s}`. What differs is the bond that fails:

| condition | failing relation | bond reading |
|---|---|---|
| absent from closure | `p` not reachable from `atoms(Γ)` | REACHABILITY fails; outward import owed |
| mis-attached | `p` present but the prediction does not track `o'_s` | rebinding; refit, do not mint |
| unsettled | `p` present, prediction and observation agree only at aggregate level | no discriminating instrument; inward owed |
| hidden by compensation | `R_s` positive while the global score is near zero | per-slot indexing restored; re-census |

So the four are **isomers under Fig 12**: same atoms, different bond, different substance. This is not a metaphor. Fig 12 :94 says *"The arrangement is the substance."* It explains why a single lever clears only the region where one condition holds alone and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55-57).

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if one measurement protocol settles all four conditions at once, so that a single instrument distinguishes absence, mis-attachment, unsettledness and compensation for a given process, the isomer claim fails and the four collapse to one condition. I did not find such a protocol in the eight sources opened.

### Q2: What is the order of the three searches?

`THE_FORMULA.md` step 7 separates three questions: SEARCHED, INWARD, OUTWARD, asked in that order, with INWARD and OUTWARD *"opposed; never read one against the other."*

- **absent** -> OUTWARD. Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* The frame here is the process literature holding a term the model does not. W5 (DeConto and Pollard) introduced hydrofracture and ice-cliff failure into a coupled model and changed the projection, but whether a given model's closure lacks them is a separate question to be checked before import.
- **mis-attached** -> SEARCHED, then rebinding. W2 tried five cloud schemes in one model and still missed the observed cloud-cover response. No new atom was owed; the attachment was wrong.
- **unsettled** -> INWARD. Fig 6 :32-35: an instrument is improved from a worse instrument already returning something, and *"Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from."* W1 names the existing edges: laboratory process-rate work, new probes, next-generation space instruments.
- **hidden** -> re-census at slot level (Fig 1 :19). W2's compensating errors are the case.

Order by dependency: determine the condition first (Fig 3 links 1 and 2), then choose the search (Fig 9 :43: *"The verdict tells you to leave. The lookup tells you where to go. Elimination is what you do after you arrive."*). Asking "which process is worst" before classifying the condition is the inversion Fig 9 :27 forbids.

**Falsifier:** show that INWARD and OUTWARD can be run as one combined operation for a process without changing the atom budget. If they can, step 7's opposition is wrong for this domain and the order above collapses.

### Q3: Are the three candidate processes isomers, or a functional group?

Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

As the candidates are normally written, cloud microphysics `{grid, condensate, droplet activation, radiation}`, ice-sheet dynamics `{grounding line, ice shelf, hydrofracture, sea level}` and permafrost carbon `{soil carbon, thaw, microbial respiration, CH4/CO2}` share almost no atoms. Under Fig 12 they are **not isomers**. They belong to the same functional group: "subgrid process with a persistent, non-trending residual at an unmeasured middle link." The group fixes the kind; the process is the qualifier.

The stronger form is worth testing: if all Earth system models are written over the same full process-atom set and differ only in which process mediates which state transition, then the candidates **are** isomers (same atoms, different arrangement, different behaviour). That is a checkable structural claim, not a naming of the worst one.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if one candidate can be removed from a model while leaving the other two candidates' residuals and rankings unchanged, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. This is the same test form the exemplar used for GR and QM, applied to a different domain.

**Status:** single-source for the figure definition (Fig 12); the physics-facing check is unmeasured.

### Q4: What order is the cross-process comparison?

Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."**

Each process residual `R_s` must be settled against the ground before it can serve as an atom in a cross-process ranking `R_1 ⋛ R_2 ⋛ ...`. The comparison is **second-order**, and it is legal only because the first-order residuals were settled. This is the recursion Fig 12 names, not an import of a new primitive.

Type caveat, still open: the Operators table defines `⋛` as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*. At second order the operands are settled residuals now serving as atoms. Whether they count as quantities at that level needs an instrument, not an argument. Named, not hidden.

Fig 9 :40-41 adds the safety rule for the ranking cuts: *"a wrong cut removes the answer and speeds up so keep every cut ranked and reversible"*. A process dropped from the candidate set because it looked small in a global score must remain recoverable. Fig 6 :42-46 explains why: what is recorded only grows, what is reachable can fall, and a refused term cannot cycle in without new growth.

**Falsifier:** exhibit a valid cross-process ranking computed before any individual process residual is settled against an independent instrument. That would make the comparison first-order and break the recursion claim.

### Q5: What does the ranking cost, in two currencies?

Fig 12 :125-126: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."** *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*

- A process term is cheap to state. Hydrofracture and ice-cliff failure are a short addition to an ice-sheet model (low bits), but confirming their role needed palaeo sea-level constraints and scenario ensembles (high actions).
- A cloud microphysics scheme is expensive to state and expensive to settle: W2 reports that five distinct schemes, tested with perturbed key processes, still failed to reproduce the observed cloud-cover response.

Summing the two into a single "model complexity" score is the named error, not the fix. Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

**Falsifier:** give a figure-derived conversion between description bits and settlement actions for these slots. If one exists, this sub-derivation is retracted.

## CONCLUSION

The four conditions are **isomers** under Fig 12: same atoms, different failing bond, different remedy. That is why "which process is worst" cannot be answered until the condition is classified. The searches owed to the conditions are ordered: SEARCHED first, then INWARD for unsettled residuals and OUTWARD for absent terms, never read against each other. The three candidate processes are **not** isomers as usually written; they share a **functional group**, with a stronger isomer claim available if a full shared process-atom set is demonstrated. The cross-process ranking is **second-order** and must wait for first-order settlement. A live strain remains on the operand type of `⋛` at second order.

Nothing here names a process. The derivation rescues what is defensible in the question (a structured, ordered ranking problem) without supplying the verdict the figures withhold.
