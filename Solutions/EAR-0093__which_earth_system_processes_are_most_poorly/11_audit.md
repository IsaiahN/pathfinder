## 4. Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :19/:22 (per-slot residual), Fig 3 :27 (unmeasured link), Fig 5 :59 (persistent residual), Fig 9 :32 (shape, not a name).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07`: rank per slot, ground-anchored, four conditions separate, cross-process ranking second-order.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the concrete claim a reader is likely to take away: "clouds are the worst represented process, add a better cloud scheme."

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **per-slot indexing**, which becomes a global "model quality" statement in the concrete reading;
- the **four-condition split**, which collapses into a single "add a scheme" prescription;
- the **shape-not-a-name limit**, which the concrete reading breaches by selecting a process;
- the **second-order requirement**, which disappears when the cross-process rank is treated as directly measurable.

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a process at an unmeasured middle link, with a persistent residual, is the kind of slot to point at. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a recorded gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` states.
