## What in the wider literature bears on this question

1. **The four conditions are visible in the sources, not invented here.** W1 documents a term whose process rates are unsettled; W2 documents mis-attached schemes and compensating error; W5 documents processes added that were not in the model's representable set; W4 documents a feedback whose magnitude and timing remain uncertain. That is the unbundling in `01` F4, arrived at independently from the figures.
2. **"Complexity" is not one cost.** W3 separates parameter uncertainty from structural uncertainty. W2 shows that five different cloud schemes, each a more elaborate description, still miss the same observed response. This is Fig 12 :126: *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*
3. **A reading does not determine the source.** W7's model equifinality says distinct parameter configurations can yield equally acceptable simulations. Fig 9 :32 gives the figure-side limit: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* The world corroborates, it does not decide.
4. **Aggregation hides the signal.** W8's reversal under pooling is the statistical form of Fig 1 :22. Both say the pooled number is the wrong object. Agreement between a statistics source and the figures is one corroboration on a shared logical point, not two independent witnesses for a claim about clouds.
5. **No source found gives a cross-process, per-slot rank.** This is the honest gap. W6 ranks uncertainty contributions to climate sensitivity, which is one objective. W1, W2, W4 and W5 each address one process. None compares process representation quality on a common ground. Reporting this is required by Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

## Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 4 :22** *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full framework before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the per-slot rule and the pre-registered refuter.
- **Fig 10 :42** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27** *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before offering the links.
- **Fig 2 :40** selecting recipients for likely agreement manufactures the disqualifying correlation. Use public opt-in venues only.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the per-slot claim and its refuter; (2) the question, what would they count as a failure; (3) only then the three source links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

**Stage 1:** send the residual, the shape claim and the four refuters. Record the verdict before showing any framework. The recipient checks the output without holding its interior. This is Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*

**Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the source links and the audit. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because the agreement of frames sharing an evidence pool is worthless as a verdict (Fig 2 :40).

**Stage 3:** offer the derivation rule as a method to reuse. Fig 4 :22 permits sending generators. Each user must state their own ground and write their own refuter before running it, because a loop cannot check the direction of its own question.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if stage 1 survives. As drafted, the claim is a shape and a `specified` candidate set, not a name. If a reader cannot tell the difference after one paragraph, repair the wording before sending.
