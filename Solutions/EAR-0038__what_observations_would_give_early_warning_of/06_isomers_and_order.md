# Structure, order and analogues: what makes an observation a warning

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The primary audit says a warning is a persistent non-shrinking residual at a named slot. Three questions are still owed: (1) what makes a set of readings one observation rather than a list, (2) are the several early-warning statistics the same thing or different things, and (3) what is the order of the union between a warning and its cascade?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: states (elevation, mass, velocity, fracture state of a slot), structures (spectrum as ordering, gradient as directedness, network as influence travel), bonds (the seven plus negation), slots (basins, glaciers, ice shelves), and instruments already returning data. No new primitive is available without import.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - What makes the readings one observation?

Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."

A state reading (elevation) and a statistic (AC1) are not a warning by themselves. The warning is the **bond** between them: the fact that the deviation from the expected recovery is failing to decay. Fig 13 :75: "Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state." By the same rule, a state (elevation) cannot compose directly with another state (mass) without a structure in between; the recovery rate is that structure.

- A **state** answers: what is the ice doing now?
- A **structure** answers: is the deviation recovered, and in which direction?
- A **network** answers: which neighbouring slot is affected?

A warning is therefore a molecule: `slot(state) - recovery structure - time`, measured by an instrument already returning the state. **Falsifier:** if a state reading alone discriminates a coming change better than its recovery structure, across validated cases, the bond is not load bearing and this composition fails.

### Q2 - Are AC1, variance, skewness and DFA isomers or a functional group?

Fig 12 :117: "same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

All the standard indicators share the same atoms: a detrended residual and a recovery rate. They differ in what they emphasise. AC1 and spectral reddening measure short-term memory; variance measures the spread of the same memory; DFA measures long-range memory; skewness and flickering measure directional asymmetry between alternative states (Fang et al. 2025; Dakos et al. 2012).

- As the record writes them, they share every atom and differ in the statistic applied, so they are **isomers** in the figure's sense: same atoms, different bond, different substance. The behaviour differs decisively: AC1 can plateau or decline after a transition while variance keeps rising, and skewness can move in opposite directions depending on which state lies on the other side (Dakos et al. 2012).
- The weaker and safer reading is that they form a **functional group**, "the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." The substructure that fixes the outcome is "computed on the detrended residual of a named slot"; the qualifier is AC1, variance, skewness or DFA.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two indicators built on the same residual never disagree across a set of validated transitions, the isomer claim fails and the cheaper functional-group reading stands. If they disagree but only one ever fires before a change, the stronger claim fails too and the indicator set should be narrowed to the one that pays.

### Q3 - What order is the union of warning and cascade?

Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."

A first-order warning is settled at a slot when a low-overlap instrument confirms it and the inherited-forcing null is rejected. That settled molecule then becomes an atom for the second-order claim: the neighbouring basin inherits a warning (Fig 11 :52-53: "first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out"). The cascade is **not** a bigger version of the first-order warning. It is a different thing composed over it.

This is also why the two must not be averaged. A second-order warning can be strong while a first-order one is weak (transmitted CSD, Fang et al. 2025), and that is the false-warning case, not evidence of robustness.

**Falsifier:** show a second-order cascade signal that is fully explained by each slot's own first-order residual, with no additional cross-slot term. Then the cascade is a replay, not a composition, and the second-order claim is withdrawn.

### Q4 - What refuses a warning?

Operators table: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate." The Operators table also notes that subtraction is not negation: subtraction is the absence of an operand, while negation is the refusal of a claim. A dataset with no warning is not a refusal; a validated null of inherited forcing is.

So the monitoring vocabulary needs three operators beyond the indicators:

1. **¬ warning** when the forcing null is not rejected (a refusal of the source claim);
2. **¬ slot** when the purported warning is only a global aggregate (a refusal of the unit);
3. **¬ independence** when the two confirming instruments share a processing chain (a refusal of the witness).

Without these, the system can only accumulate alarms. With them, a false warning removes the standing of a claim without deleting any data (Formula step 3: nothing is ever deleted, the reach falls because something was added).

## CONCLUSION

An observation becomes a warning by its bond (a failing recovery), not by its ingredients. The standard CSD statistics are isomers over the same residual atoms, with a functional-group reading as the safer fallback. The union of warning and cascade is second order and is licensed by Fig 12's settled-molecule rule, not by a new primitive. Refusal is a first-class part of the recipe: a vocabulary that can only add alarms cannot correct itself.
