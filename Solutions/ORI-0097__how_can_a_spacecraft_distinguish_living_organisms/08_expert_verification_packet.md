# Expert verification packet: the Tether on life detection

**What this is:** a claim in a form a life detection researcher or instrument scientist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the discrimination constraints, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed here. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A spacecraft reading does not determine its source: living chemistry, abiotic chemistry and contamination can produce the same observed composition. Therefore the discrimination cannot be one perfect biosignature measurement. It requires a procedure with a specific shape: per-slot residuals rather than bulk averages; a union of four conditions (contamination, abiotic mimic, environmental context, positive structure) unbundled before search; instruments sharpened from ones already returning an unresolved reading; several low-overlap measurements ranged against a fixed ground; candidate structural discriminators (a regenerating catalyst, a closing cycle, a functional group) held as shapes rather than certificates; and an honest `unreachable at this budget` report when nothing closes the gap. The framework does not derive which molecule or instrument is the discriminator. It states the form any answer must have and the refuters that would break it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal, checkable by reading the figure text

**Statement.** The reading cannot comment on the source; the framework puts the burden on provenance and independent instruments, not on a single value.

**Source, verbatim.**
- Fig 13 :121 "Different routes can arrive at the same substance."
- Fig 8 :50 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."
- Fig 2 :31 *"Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."*
- Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*

**Refuter.** Exhibit a reading with a one-to-one map to living versus nonliving across all known abiotic mimics and contamination states, or show that two supposedly independent instruments share enough of a calibration, preparation or analysis pool that their agreement is the pool talking.

### C2: instrumentation-facing, checkable against real life detection practice

**Statement.** The route to a discriminator is to sharpen an instrument already returning something that fails to resolve, and to track the residual per object rather than averaging it away.

**Source, verbatim.**
- Fig 6 :32 *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."*
- Fig 6 :34 *"The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve."*
- Fig 1 :28 *"The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."*
- Fig 3 :27 *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

**What to check.** In a real payload (PIXL and SHERLOC on Perseverance are the documented instance), did the useful signal come from per-spot mineral and organic patterns plus higher-resolution imaging and context, rather than from a bulk average? Is there any case where a life-discriminating sensor was built from a clean description with no prior instrument returning an unresolved reading?

**Refuter.** A bulk average that preserves a discrimination no per-slot reading can make; or a documented sensor built from a pure description with no proto-instrument.

### C3: framework-internal derivation with a ground-facing consequence

**Statement.** The union must be unbundled, the structural discriminators are shapes not certificates, and an unresolved case must be reported as `unreachable at this budget` with underivable-in-principle kept distinct.

**Source, verbatim.**
- Fig 9 :54 *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*
- Fig 8 :39 *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
- Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"
- Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

**Refuter.** Show that contamination, abiotic mimic, context and positive structure form a partition for a target class, so one lever clears all four; or derive a unique biosignature from the figure vocabulary with no chemistry or biology import; or demonstrate that absence of a discriminator entails absence of the source.

## 4. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a theory of everything, and not a final answer. There is no last room.
- Not a list of biosignatures, a flight instrument design, or a mission recommendation.
- Not a claim that any mission found life, or that a reported potential biosignature is biological.
- Not "life is the only explanation" for any observation. The framework's rule is the opposite: rule out alternatives first.
- Not a claim that more agreeing teams add evidence. Agreement compounds; evidence does not (Fig 7 :12).
- Not a claim that a confidence scale or threshold settles a case. It is a filter, not a witness.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by measurement.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read five short passages: Fig 13 :121, Fig 8 :50, Fig 6 :32 and :34, Fig 9 :54 and :32. Ten minutes.
2. Check C2 against a payload you know: is the signal per-slot and multi-instrument, or a bulk average? Three minutes.
3. Name one known abiotic mimic for the signature you work with, and say whether a single reading can exclude it. Three minutes.
4. Say which of R1 to R4 you would try first, and what result would change your mind. Two minutes.
5. Send the refutation, or one line saying which sub-claim survives. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
