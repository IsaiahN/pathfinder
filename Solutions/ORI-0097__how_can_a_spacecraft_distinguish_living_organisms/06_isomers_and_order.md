# Isomers, order and analogues in the life detection question

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the substrate rule.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §5).

## RESIDUAL

The life/nonlife problem is often framed as: "find the molecule that only life makes." Two questions are owed: (1) is that an isomer question in the figure's sense, and (2) what order does the discrimination procedure have, and which bonds carry it? A strain was found in the first framing and is re-censused rather than smoothed over.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Is the life/nonlife case an isomer case? NO, with a re-census

Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

The strict isomer definition requires the same atoms arranged by a different bond. A living sample and an abiotic sample can have **identical molecular composition and identical bonds** while differing in history. So the figure line does not govern the life/nonlife difference directly. Forcing it would be a strain.

**Re-census:** Fig 12 :117 governs composition-level difference. The governing lines for source difference are:

- Fig 13 :121 "Different routes can arrive at the same substance."
- Fig 13 :122 "In chemistry this has been established since Liebig and Wöhler found two substances with the same composition and different properties, 1824 to 1826, and Berzelius named it isomerism in 1830."
- Fig 8 :50 "Only the record of where each came from separates them"

Note the productive tension inside Fig 13 itself: "different routes can arrive at the same substance" (many to one) and "same composition and different properties" (one to many). Both are true at different levels. The first governs the **source to reading** map; the second governs the **composition to property** map. Re-census puts each on its own quantity, so there is no contradiction. The correct statement for this question: **equal composition does not fix identity of process.** That is weaker than isomerism and is what the figures actually license.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a known biological and a known abiotic sample can be separated by composition and bond arrangement alone, with no provenance and no history, then the isomer framing would have been the right one and the re-census is wrong.

### Q2: Which structures are licensed as candidate discriminators?

Fig 12 :119 *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."*
Fig 12 :121 *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*
Fig 12 :123 *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

These are the three structures in the figure vocabulary that could, in principle, carry a source discrimination:

1. **catalyst that survives its own use:** self-regeneration is a property of living metabolism, but abiotic catalysis exists. The discriminator is not the presence of a catalyst; it is whether the same part is regenerated across uses in the same slot, which is a measurement over time.
2. **cycle whose output re-enters as input:** a closing production loop is structural. Geochemical cycles also close. The figure itself warns that "the sign of the loop decides whether it closes", so direction must be measured, not assumed. A flat inventory cannot tell a cycle from a chain (Fig 12 :94 *"The arrangement is the substance."*).
3. **functional group:** fixes *kind*, not *member*. Finding a group says what class of process could be present; it does not say whether the process is biological.

Conclusion: all three are **candidate shapes**, none is a certificate. They must be combined with per-slot residuals and low-overlap independent lines (F1, F4). Narrowed claim, strain named.

**Falsifier:** a single one of these structures, observed once, that separates living from abiotic across all known mimics. Then the combination requirement fails.

### Q3: What order does the procedure have?

Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
Fig 3 :19 *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*

Dependency order for this question:

```
perception of slots
  -> vocabulary of what a sample affords (and what would count as progress)
    -> objective: rule out one named abiotic alternative for this slot
      -> plan the next probe or sample
        -> name, verify and carry what worked to the next slot and mission
```

The middle link is vocabulary and objective, and it is the one most likely to be broken. Fig 3 :25: "Perception has obvious failures and obvious tests. Planning has obvious failures and obvious tests. The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither". The CoLD scale and the Standards of Evidence framework are instruments for that middle link. They do not improve the spectrometer; they make the objective statable.

### Q4: What bonds carry the procedure?

- **→ sequence** (Fig 12 :30, "A then B; B needs A to have happened"): the order of the mission, and the order within a single slot (observe, perturb, re-observe). Ordered, so swapping is a different procedure.
- **⋛ comparison** (Operators table :48, "a threshold relation between two quantities"): used to compare two independent lines of evidence. It is ordered and is *not* a symmetric join. A threshold on one line is a filter (Fig 9 :60), not a witness.
- **+ conjunction** (Fig 12 :56, "both hold at once"): the union of conditions holds simultaneously; unbundling splits it before search.
- **¬ negation** (Operators table :53, "this does not hold"): the refuters. A vocabulary without negation can only accumulate candidate biosignatures; it cannot carry a correction. This is why R1 to R5 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are written down.

A vocabulary with only molecule names can say what a sample contains. It cannot say what would count as getting somewhere (Fig 12 :111 to :113). That is the exact gap the standards-of-evidence work fills.

### Q5: How does composition recur across scale?

Fig 12 :103 *"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."*
Fig 12 :104 *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"*
Fig 13 :107 *"Every cell is a composition, and each becomes an operand for the next, alongside the six and every link already made. The array does not close."*

A settled biosignature argument at one level (an isotopic ratio agreed to be biological in a specific environment) becomes an atom at the mission level. It is then composable with a second line of evidence. It is not *irreducible in the world*, only at that level. This is why the LDKB stores arguments with their provenance and environmental context: the term must carry what it was settled against, or the next level composes over an ungrounded atom.

## CONCLUSION

The life/nonlife case is **not** an isomer case in Fig 12's strict sense; it is a source-ambiguity case governed by Fig 13 :121 and Fig 8 :50. Equal composition does not fix process. The licensed candidate discriminators are a regenerating catalyst, a closing cycle and a functional group, each a **shape** requiring combination with per-slot, low-overlap evidence, none a certificate. The procedure's dependency order puts vocabulary and objective before planning, and its bonds are sequence for the mission, comparison for independent lines, conjunction for the union, and negation for the refuters. A seventh primitive is not minted, and no binding is allowed to masquerade as an import that moves the wall.

## FALSIFIER (standing, written before the run)

The supporting derivation fails if any of the following is demonstrated:

1. a biological and an abiotic sample separated by composition and bond arrangement alone, with no provenance or history (kills the re-census in Q1);
2. a single structural discriminator from Q2 that works across all known mimics on one observation (kills the combination requirement);
3. a procedure whose planning stage works while its vocabulary of "what would count as progress" is absent (kills the Fig 3 dependency order);
4. a threshold on one reading that settles the source (kills the filter/witness distinction).
