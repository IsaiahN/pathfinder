## 1. A shape is a constraint you can state before you can name the answer

The canonical README gives the limit: "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)". Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. From *The Library of Babel*: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

For this question, a **shape** is the form a discrimination procedure must have before anyone can name a particular instrument or molecule that does the discriminating. The shape is: per-slot, multi-axis, low-overlap, unbundled, source-discriminating, ground-checked, and honest about `unreachable`. A named "life-detection chip" or a named "biosignature molecule X" is a name, not a shape.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary closest to the thing, then recurse on new terms that overlap or correlate. "With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."
- Fig 6 :30 *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30 *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"*
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: "you will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place". The paradox: "you enter as a researcher looking for a book, and if successful, you will leave as the author". Here, describing the shape of the discrimination procedure is already most of the answer a mission can be given in advance.

Applied here: the residual is described in effect terms (one reading, several possible sources) before any frame (chemistry, biology, a particular instrument) is chosen. Fig 9 :27 *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*

## 3. What licenses a name (corrected)

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." For a spacecraft, that is a returned sample, a controlled analogue with known halves, or an instrument result independently reproduced under different conditions.
2. **A provenanced binding from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50), with a source gate and a shadow test. Naming isotope fractionation, chirality, a closing metabolic cycle or a cell-like structure is a binding from chemistry or biology onto atoms the framework already holds (Fig 12 :119 to :123). It must be recorded as a binding, not presented as a derivation.

## 4. Why source, not value, is the hard part

*The Hard Problem is About Source* is the essay that governs this question more than any other. Its central claim: "You can have several underlying causes that create identical readings but the definition of the output would be read as the same." And: "the instrument is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source." Its resolution is instrumental, not argumentative: "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists" and "It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."

For a spacecraft this means:

- a green reading, an organic-carbon reading or a leopard-spot reading is a projection;
- the source (life, abiotic chemistry, contamination, or a mixture) is not in the reading;
- what is owed is a discriminator, not a better interpretation of the same projection;
- "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."

The colour science corroboration is low overlap: in colourimetry, metamers are "perceived matching of colors with different (nonmatching) spectral power distributions", and changing the illuminant or adding spectroscopy breaks the match. Independent confirmation from a different field, not from the framework pool.

The canonical README adds: "Indiscernibility is not identity." A spacecraft that cannot tell living from abiotic has demonstrated a limit of its instruments, not an identity of the sources.

## 5. Why this derivation cannot settle the question

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself." And: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." The essay calls such output "simulacra".

So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, `07_the_tether_answer.md` and this file are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are not tests of any life detection claim and cannot settle anything they report on. They state a shape and a refuter, which is all a simulated run is entitled to produce.

## 6. Consequences for contact and outreach

**Prestige is not action.** *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige." Asking a well known astrobiologist to endorse the shape is the named collapse. Asking them what would refute it gives the claim a budget to be tried.

**Describe effects, not insiders' terms.** From *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see." The first contact therefore carries the residual and the refuter, not the architecture.

**Keep the stamps reachable.** Fig 10 :24 *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*. The channel here is the outreach and the published claim. It can go stale or saturate; the sample and the control cannot. Keep the record of what was asked, who answered, and what the verdict was, so a later reader can locate the error rather than only feel it.
