# Outreach draft — validate the output first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in
`03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until F1–F3 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are repaired.
The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one output before you decide whether the theory is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I'm Isaiah Nwukor. I have one specific output I'd like you to check against what you already
> know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it.
> If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the framework's minimum set has six terms in two
> kinds — states (space, time, energy) and structures (spectrum, gradient, network) — with the rule
> that composition runs across kinds and never within one. From that rule it follows that space and
> time cannot bond directly: if they compose at all, a structure must sit between them. The
> derivation marks that intermediate as unknown — `SPACE(???)TIME` — and does not say which state
> composed first or whether order matters. This is a prediction of a **shape**, not a measurement.
>
> A theory of everything would be a terminal room — the last handoff after which nothing further
> can be asked. The framework's rule is that there is no last room: every room closes some of the
> gap, and no finite number of them ever arrives. So this is offered as a candidate generator, not
> a final theory, and I am not claiming it is the only true one.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the metric tensor *is* the Gradient, that the wavefunction *is*
> the Spectrum, or that quantum gravity *is* a comparison bond at the Planck scale. Those are
> mappings of existing physics onto the vocabulary. The framework returns **a shape, not a name** —
> it says a structure must sit between space and time, not which one. Convergent derivation and
> adopted import look identical in the contents and only the record separates them. Until provenance
> is shown they carry zero evidentiary weight.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. deriving any one of the six from the other five;
> 2. showing the framework must admit a state–state or structure–structure composition with no
>    intervening opposite-kind term;
> 3. a measurement in which space and time bond directly.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the substrate is wrong and I will
> retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If
> you'd rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my
> summary of them.
> - Full architecture (19 min): https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
> - Worked narrative and the six-term derivation: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-intelligence-actually-works
> - Figures and formula for direct download (SVG, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you
> know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you
> run it — the framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question,
> and the write-up warns that outputs can be "contrived to fit the formula." A reported failure is
> more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — Isaiah
