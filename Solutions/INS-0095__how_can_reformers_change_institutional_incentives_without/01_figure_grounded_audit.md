## 0. The residual in effect terms

R is the gap, per affected slot s, between the incentive the reformer intends to install and the incentive that actually pays after the reform is announced. It is indexed by slot: what changes for frontline staff is not what changes for middle management, and not what changes for the incumbent who set the old rule. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A single "culture change score" is the named failure.

Arity: at least two conditions. A = the formal rule is changed. B = the informal response that restores the old equilibrium. A lever that clears A alone leaves B standing (Fig 9 :54-57). Symmetry: the resistance is ordered, not symmetric. It arrives after the reform, and it is a negative feedback that returns the system to a prior attractor, so swapping the order produces a different thing. Scale: individual behaviour, institution rules and field-wide norms are three scales with a membrane between them, and only methods and head starts cross (Fig 4 :22).

The question asks for a strategy. The sought-for shape is bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula "Direction"): a strategy is a reform strategy in this sense only if it is anchored to a non-moving ground, changes contact rather than declaration, introduces rather than subtracts, declares the reformer's stake, and reports per-slot residuals. That binding is what the refuter tests.

## 3. Findings F1 to F10

### F1 — Status-quo preservation is coherent drift, not a resisting force (triangulated)

Fig 2 :13-15: "they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence". Fig 2 :34: "everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices." Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."

When members have no anchor, they update on one another. The outcome is convergence on whoever already had the most influence, which in an institution is the incumbent who authored the old incentive. Because the drift is coherent, no one inside sees a defect. The "resistance" is this convergence, not a separate conservative instinct.

**Refuter.** Show a reform in which members corrected only against a non-moving anchor, the effective incentive changed, and it still reverted to the prior equilibrium without any change in influence weights. That would break this finding.

**Verdict:** triangulated across Fig 2 (anchor rule) and Fig 10 :24 ("the ground does not decay, the channel does"), two figures with low overlap in subject matter.

### F2 — A declared rule is not a grounded incentive (triangulated)

Fig 10 :16-17: "the convention does not fix the bug" and "it makes the bug statable". Fig 10 :18: "a declared meaning is not a grounded one". Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one". Fig 10 :49: "Declaring a meaning does not ground it." Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

Changing the written incentive rule changes a convention. If the rule merely makes the failure statable, it is doing legitimate work. If the new convention itself decides the outcome, it has stopped being a convention and has become an authored constant. If it only declares a meaning, it is not grounded. The test is contact: did what actually pays change?

**Refuter.** Measure that the effective payoff changed while no anchor moved and no new actor or instrument was introduced. If that is possible, this finding is false.

**Verdict:** triangulated across Fig 10 (convention/ground) and Fig 11 (contact/capability).

### F3 — A new incentive logic cannot be composed inside the old closure (single-source on the barrier, corroborated by Fig 8)

Fig 6 :14: "the only operator that moves the wall". Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 6 :22: "IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure." Fig 8 :32: "no search, however long, adds an atom". Fig 8 :53: "Residual first, frame second."

A reform phrased entirely in the institution's existing vocabulary rearranges arrangements without adding a primitive. The wall moves only when a primitive arrives from a frame whose closure differs. That is why the residual must be described in effect terms before a frame is chosen: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit" (Fig 9 :27-28).

**Refuter.** Derive a new incentive primitive entirely from the old vocabulary and show it settles against the ground. Under Fig 8 :32 this cannot happen, so a positive result would be a real refutation of the barrier reading.

**Verdict:** single-source for the barrier claim (Fig 6), with Fig 8 as a corroborating figure of the same metalaw family. Low overlap on mechanism but shared evidence pool, so treat as one witness with two statements.

### F4 — Introduce, never subtract; enumerate cascades (triangulated)

Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :44: "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." Fig 11 :61: "Introduction is available; exclusion is not." Fig 11 :32: "introduce, never subtract". Fig 11 :52-53: "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."

Resistance that restores the status quo is often a second- or third-order effect. A reform that only deletes an old incentive leaves the habitat intact, and contact reinstates what was removed. The seat's power is asymmetric: you can add an actor, monitor or instrument; you cannot subtract the habitat. "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose" (Fig 11 :67).

**Refuter.** Exhibit a reform that removed an incentive, enumerated no replacement cascade, and produced no reinstatement at two or more steps out.

**Verdict:** triangulated across Fig 11 (habitat asymmetry) and Fig 4 (a head start makes the next search cheaper), with the ecology source in `10_source_ledger.csv` as a low-overlap world analogue, not a figure.

### F5 — The reformer's stake must be declared (single-source, strong)

Fig 10 :58: "A seat with no stake at all does not exist." Fig 10 :59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying". Fig 10 :60: "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it." Fig 10 :61: "standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on". Fig 9 :51: "evidence bearing on the residual is admissible; evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel, and the verifier is denied it".

The reformer cannot be neutral about whether the reform works. That is unavoidable and legitimate. What disqualifies a reformer is an interest in the reading: a budget, reputation or standing that depends on the reform being judged successful. Such an interest is modelled anyway, and if it is unnamed it runs unchallenged.

**Refuter.** Show a reform design in which the reformer's standing or budget depends on a favourable verdict and no declaration was recorded. Under Fig 10 :60 that is a live defect, not a stylistic one.

**Verdict:** single-source (Fig 10, plus Fig 9 :51 on admissible evidence). The seat rules are a framework-imposed rule, not a measured law.

### F6 — Iterate by selection between differing rooms, not by agreement (triangulated)

Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Fig 7 :36: "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced." Fig 7 :13: "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not". Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

A reform programme whose evidence is stakeholder agreement is one long room. The next pilot must differ from the last and match the residual the last one surfaced. Each affected group is a slot with its own residual; averaging them throws away the signal that says where to look. Independent comparison sites are bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong, not selected for likely agreement.

**Refuter.** Exhibit a programme whose only evidence is agreement or an averaged satisfaction score, with no per-slot residual and no genuinely differing comparison site, and which nonetheless distinguishes changes the ground pays for from changes it does not.

**Verdict:** triangulated across Fig 7 (selection) and Fig 1/Fig 2 (per-slot residual and independence).

### F7 — Find the broken link first (single-source, high consequence)

Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Fig 12 :112: "A system holding only ingredients can say what a situation contains and cannot say what would count as getting somewhere from it."

The middle links of the dependency chain, having terms for what a situation affords and being able to say what counts as progress, have neither obvious failures nor obvious tests, so they attract the least attention. Reform effort usually lands above the break where measurements already exist: communications campaigns, incentive schemes, training. If the objective link has no instrument, that effort cannot arrive.

**Refuter.** Show that the objective link had a working instrument and the reform still failed at the same point with the same residual. Then the break is elsewhere and this finding is mislocated.

**Verdict:** single-source for the dependency claim (Fig 3), with Fig 12 :112 as a structural corroboration about vocabulary without bonds.

### F8 — The answer is a shape, not a named policy (triangulated)

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."

What the figures license is the shape: anchored, contact-changing, introductive, declared-stake, per-slot. They do not license "adopt policy X", because naming a policy is a binding from a world frame onto held atoms and must be provenanced as such. "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" means a figure-grounded answer may stop without embarrassment.

**Refuter.** Produce a figure-grounded derivation that names a specific institutional policy as the unique answer and shows why the framework can name it. Under Fig 9 :32 that would falsify the shape limit.

**Verdict:** triangulated across Fig 9, Fig 13 and the canonical figures README.

### F9 — The compact strategy carries a round-trip gap R_T (single-source, structural)

Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." Fig 4 :27: "The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."

Condensing the derivation into a five-point slogan loses the anchor requirement's force, the import requirement, the declaration condition and the per-slot discipline. Treating the slogan as identical to the derivation sets R_T to zero and is the dead-reckoning error.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The strategy in this folder is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A reform with no non-moving external anchor changes the effective incentive and does not revert, with influence weights unchanged (breaks F1).
2. An improvement in the reformer alone, with no change in contact, changes what actually pays (breaks Fig 11 :40 and F2/F4).
3. Agreement among frames selected for expected agreement is shown to be a verdict about the world (breaks Fig 2 :40 and F6).
4. A re-census admits composition inside the institution's own closure as a way of moving the wall (breaks F3).
5. Per-slot residuals aggregate without loss into a single reform score that still tells you where to look (breaks Fig 1 :22).

If any fires, the strategy is withdrawn or narrowed. No ground reading has been run, so no part of it is `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Anchor the new incentive to something that does not move when incumbents push on it: a counter, a published measure, a market outcome, a patient outcome (Fig 2 :29-30). The anchor must not be one of the parties (Fig 2 :46).
2. Range members against the anchor separately; do not negotiate a compromise with them (Fig 2 :35, :45).
3. Tell the difference between a convention that makes the bug statable and a declared rule that decides the outcome. Only the first is legitimate work (Fig 10 :16-18, :47).
4. Introduce monitors, instruments and comparison sites; enumerate the habitat to two or three steps out (Fig 11 :43-44, :52-54, :61).
5. Find the broken link before spending effort; ask whether the objective link has an instrument (Fig 3 :19, :27).
6. Declare the reformer's stake; deny the verifier evidence bearing only on the maker (Fig 10 :59-60; Fig 9 :51).
7. Report per-group residuals and pick each next pilot to differ from the last and match the residual surfaced (Fig 1 :19; Fig 7 :36).
8. Send the generator, not the recording: the refuter and the shape claim go first, the links only after (Fig 4 :25).

### SEARCHED / INWARD / OUTWARD, kept distinct

- **SEARCHED** (asked first): is an anchorable ground already present in the institution and unfound? Look for counters, audits, outcome measures and complaints already returning something. The closure does not change (Fig 6 :49-50).
- **INWARD**: sharpen our own instrument. Can the existing per-group measures resolve what pays? Fig 6 :29: "inward: is our representation adequate to hold it? The instrument extends access."
- **OUTWARD**: import a primitive from a frame whose closure differs, cast in effect terms. Fig 6 :30: "outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels." Source gate and shadow test are mandatory (Fig 8 :9-13). The world sources in `10_source_ledger.csv` are candidates, not authorities.

INWARD and OUTWARD were not read against each other. Importing against a budget limit would add an atom that was not needed, and a wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives (Fig 6 :51-52).
