# Expert verification packet — the Tether on institutional incentive reform

**What this is:** a claim in a form an institutional scholar or reform practitioner can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` — not `grounded`.** No institution was measured and no reform was run. This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Resistance that preserves an institutional status quo is the convergence of unanchored frames: members update on one another and converge on whoever already has the most influence, and because the drift is coherent no one inside notices. The failure is a union compound, a rule change plus a restoration, not one hard problem. The shape of a reform that does not preserve the status quo is: anchor the new incentive to a measure incumbents cannot move; range each affected group against that measure separately rather than negotiating; change what actually pays rather than only what is written; add monitors, instruments and comparison sites instead of relying on removal; declare the reformer's stake; find the broken link before spending effort; and report residuals per group, choosing each next pilot to differ from the last. The framework names no specific policy. What it offers is the shape any candidate must have, refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 2

- **Statement:** with no anchor, frames converge on influence, and the drift is invisible from inside. A reform conducted as negotiation is a mirror chain, not a triangulation.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :13-15 "they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence"; Fig 2 :34 "everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices"; Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."; Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."
- **Refuter:** show a reform with no non-moving external anchor that changes what pays and does not revert, while influence weights are unchanged. Or show agreement among frames selected for expected agreement functioning as a verdict about the world (breaks Fig 2 :40).

### C2 — institution-facing; checkable against a real reform

- **Statement:** a declared rule is not a grounded incentive, and the restoration is often a second- or third-order cascade. The seat can introduce monitors and instruments; it cannot subtract the habitat.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :49 "Declaring a meaning does not ground it."; Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"; Fig 11 :40 "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."; Fig 11 :61 "Introduction is available; exclusion is not."; Fig 11 :52-53 "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."
- **What to check:** in an institution you know, did the formal change alter what actually pays per affected group, or only what is written? Was the restoration first-order (a direct refusal) or third-order (a shift in who answers, who remains, or what gets measured)?
- **Refuter:** measure that effective payoff changed while no anchor moved and no new actor or instrument was introduced; or exhibit a reform that removed an incentive, enumerated no replacement cascade, and produced no reinstatement two or more steps out.

### C3 — framework-internal derivation with a live strain

- **Statement:** the failure is a union compound with two loops; new incentive logic requires an import from a closure that differs; the reformer's stake must be declared; and the programme is ordered by dependency.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."; Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."; Fig 10 :59 "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"; Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
- **Refuter:** a single lever that reliably clears both conditions across independent institutions; a new incentive primitive derived inside the old vocabulary and settled against the ground; a validator whose standing depends on a favourable verdict with no declaration; or a working instrument at the objective link with the failure unchanged.
- **Live strain, disclosed:** whether a world frame's outcome measure can serve as the institutional anchor without itself moving when pushed. Fig 2 :30 says "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." No figure settles whether a market, counter or published metric is movable by an incumbent coalition in a particular field. This needs an instrument, not an argument.

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminator, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*, opened directly: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source", and "the answer is unreached until a better instrument exists". For this claim:

1. **Anchor Test:** name a measure of the institution that does not move when incumbents push on it. If every candidate moves, the reform owes an anchor.
2. **Contact Test:** a per-group measure of what actually pays before and after, not of stated policy.
3. **Restoration Test:** a two- and three-step-out check for reinstatement of the old payoff.
4. **Stake Test:** identify who gains standing from a favourable verdict and declare it.
5. **Difference Test:** compare two pilots that differ by at least one atom and match the residual the first surfaced.
