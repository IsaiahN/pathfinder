**Subject:** Check one output about institutional reform before you decide whether the frame is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about institutional change. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** when a reform changes only the written incentive rule, members have no common anchor, so they update on one another and converge on whoever already had the most influence. That convergence is what "resistance to change" looks like from inside: coherent drift, invisible to the people in it. The framework's anchor rule says an anchor is "whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks", a measurement, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. So the shape of a reform that does not preserve the status quo is: anchor the new incentive to something incumbents cannot move; let each group range against that anchor separately rather than negotiating a compromise; change what actually pays and not only what is declared; introduce monitors and instruments rather than relying on removal; declare the reformer's own stake; and report the residual per affected group instead of one averaged satisfaction score.
>
> What I am explicitly **not** claiming: that any particular policy is the answer, that the framework can name the right incentive, that stakeholder agreement shows the reform worked, or that this is settled by anything I have measured. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
>
> **What would refute the output:**
> 1. a reform with no non-moving external anchor changes what pays and does not revert, while influence weights stay the same;
> 2. an improvement in the reformer alone, with no change in contact, changes what actually pays;
> 3. a reform whose only evidence is agreement among people selected for being likely to agree still tells you which changes the ground paid for.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, I will narrow or withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures, verbatim text with line numbers (local canonical extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: `https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md`
> - The framework's validation essay: `https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/the-recursive-self-improvement-trap`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** run it on an institution you know and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and its author warns that outputs can be shaped to fit. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
