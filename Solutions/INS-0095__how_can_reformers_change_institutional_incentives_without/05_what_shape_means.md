**Sources and how they were used.** Opened and read directly on 2026-11-23: (1) *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, (2) *The Hard Problem is About Source*, (3) *The Library of Babel: Locating the Book of Books*, (4) *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*. ## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical figures README states the limit: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)". Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence", and it "cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it".

From *The Library of Babel* (opened directly): "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of a solution you can state before you can name it. For INS-0095 it is the five-part constraint set: anchored to a non-moving ground, changing contact rather than declaration, introductive, declared-stake, per-slot. "Adopt policy X" is a name, and the framework cannot issue it.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. "With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."
- Fig 6 :30: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* (opened directly): "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: "you will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place" and "you have already composed the answer through the process of locating it."

This question's vocabulary bootstrapping is visible in the domain census: "institutional inertia", "resistance to change", and "buy-in" are borrowed words. The figure lines convert them into effect terms: unanchored convergence, drift toward influence, and agreement as variation rather than verdict.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." The canonical README: the framework "stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*They're Scanning Books When They Should* (via the local exemplar file) sharpens this: "The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space." And: "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible." For institutional reform this matters: Ostrom's design principles or North's informal constraints are imported primitives from other closures, not derivations from the figures, and must be stamped as such. `10_source_ledger.csv` records their provenance and overlap.

## 4. Why naming a specific reform is the specific forbidden error

*The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened directly) gives the mechanism. Metamerism: an instrument reads green and "the instrument is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source". Several paths can create the same reading, so "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information". It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved it: "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists" and it was "proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."

An institution that reports high satisfaction after a reform has a reading, not a source. Several underlying arrangements (a real change in what pays, a change in who answers the survey, a change in who remains to answer) can produce the same reading. Naming "the reform worked" from that reading is the metamerism error. What is owed is a discriminator, not a verdict.

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (opened directly): "the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself." And: "You may all agree and be wrong. You may set your clock to someone else's, who was setting your clock to yours, and be off by many deviations over time. You need ground truth to define where you stand."

## 5. What this question's shape excludes

Reading *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* directly makes the exclusion precise. Two people or two groups who only check against each other "drift, and they drift together, so neither notices". The safeguard it names is exactly the framework's: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it" (Fig 2 :45), "Each frame is corrected by it separately, and what makes the correction sound is that neither frame can move it" (Fig 2 :46), and "institutions and communities that only check against themselves drift together and don't notice". The essay's own institutional examples are "Science's independent replication", "Apprenticeship is two-way contact with correction", and "Courts of law rule on evidence, which neither side can move". Those are shape instances, not policy names.

So the shape excludes: a reform whose success measure is internal consensus; a reform that changes only written rules; a reform that selects its own checkers; a reform whose validator is the reformer's standing; and a reform reported as a single averaged score.

## 6. Correction to my own §3

I initially wrote that "a name is only licensed by the ground or a provenanced import". That is too strong in one direction. Fig 13 :134 and the README's "shape, not a name" govern a **missing** primitive, one not held in Γ. Most institutional atoms here (monitor, rule, sanction, comparison site, outcome measure) are already held in the world's institutional vocabulary. Selecting one is a **binding** from a world frame onto an available atom, not the invention of a new primitive and not necessarily an import that moves the wall (Fig 6 :22: "IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure"). What remains forbidden is claiming the framework itself names the binding, or importing a primitive without a source gate and shadow test. Both readings are kept visible: the shape rule is correct for missing primitives; the binding rule governs held ones.

## 7. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (opened directly): "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly." And: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige." A list of distinguished institutional reformers assembled to endorse the strategy is the named collapse. The legitimate ask is a budget to try it and an independent ground to check it.

**This audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post" but "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself", and LLM output is "simulacra". So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation, checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is not a test of any reform and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle the question it reports on.
