# The Tether's answer: changing institutional incentives without status-quo-preserving resistance

**Mode: `specified` — not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A reformer changes a formal incentive rule. Observation: members resist and the effective incentive returns to its prior setting. Per affected group, R is the gap between the intended incentive and what actually pays. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." It is a **union compound**, not one hard problem (Fig 9 :54-57): condition A is the rule change, condition B is the restoration. They need separate loops.

## 2. The shape

1. **Without an anchor, convergence goes to influence, not to truth.** Fig 2 :13-15: "they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence"; :34: "everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices." Resistance is this drift, not a conservative force. The anchor is "whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks" (Fig 2 :29), a measurement, counter, market or patient outcome. It must not be a party to the disagreement (Fig 2 :46).
2. **Triangulate, do not negotiate.** Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Each group ranges against the anchor separately; the reform is not a compromise among members.
3. **Change contact, not declaration.** Fig 10 :49: "Declaring a meaning does not ground it." A convention that makes the bug statable is legitimate (Fig 10 :16-17); a convention that decides the outcome "has stopped being one" (Fig 10 :47). Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
4. **Introduce; do not rely on subtraction.** Fig 11 :61: "Introduction is available; exclusion is not." Add monitors, instruments, comparison sites and head starts (Fig 4 :22). Enumerate the habitat to two or three steps out, because "the residual that mattered was three steps out" (Fig 11 :52-53).
5. **Declare the stake.** Fig 10 :58: "A seat with no stake at all does not exist." Fig 10 :59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying." A reformer whose standing depends on a favourable verdict has an interest in the reading and must declare it; the verifier is denied evidence bearing only on the maker (Fig 9 :51).
6. **Keep the link order.** Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." The middle links, vocabulary and objective, usually have no instrument (Fig 3 :27), so spend there first.
7. **Select between differing pilots, not by agreement.** Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Each next pilot "must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced" (Fig 7 :36). Report per-group residuals; an average throws away the signal (Fig 1 :22).
8. **Import with provenance.** A new incentive primitive comes from a frame whose closure differs (Fig 6 :23), described in effect terms first (Fig 9 :27-28), with a source gate and shadow test. Ostrom's design principles, mechanism design and principal-agent theory are such imports, stamped as such in `10_source_ledger.csv`, not derivations from the figures.
9. **The framework returns a shape, not a policy.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." No specific reform is derived and none is claimed unique.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any named policy is the answer, and not a ranking of reform designs by popularity or prestige. "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action." (validation essay, opened directly)
- Not that resistance is irrational or malicious. It is the ordinary result of unanchored mutual update.
- Not that formal rules are useless. They are useful when they make the bug statable, not when they decide the outcome.
- Not that agreement is worthless. A convention's product is shared meaning. It is worthless only as a verdict about the world (Fig 2 :40-41).
- Not that the reformer must be neutral. Neutral is impossible; declare the stake instead.
- Not grounded. No institution was measured, and no reform was run.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

A discriminator, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source", and "the answer is unreached until a better instrument exists". Here the corresponding questions are:

- **Anchor Test:** is there a measure in the institution that does not move when incumbents push on it? If every candidate measure moves with influence, no anchor exists and the reform owes one.
- **Contact Test:** did what actually pays change, or only what is written? A per-group measure of effective payoff, not of stated policy.
- **Restoration Test:** after a rule change, does the old payoff reappear at two or three steps out? This is the DDT-to-eagles cascade applied to incentives (Fig 11 :52-54).
- **Stake Test:** does the validator's standing or budget depend on a favourable verdict? If so, declare it or replace the validator.
- **Difference Test:** does the next pilot differ from the last and match the residual it surfaced, or is it another room like the last one?
- **Standing pre-registered refuters** from `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4: an unanchored reform that does not revert; an agent-only improvement that changes contact; selected agreement that functions as a verdict; composition inside the closure that moves the wall; an aggregate that preserves the per-slot signal.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The five-part shape is all the figures license. Which measure, which monitor and which comparison site are bindings from world frames, and are settled by the ground of a particular institution, not by this derivation. Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". One live strain remains: whether a world frame's outcome measure can serve as the institutional anchor without itself moving when pushed. It is named in `09_watson_answer.md` and re-censused in `11_audit.md`. "Indiscernibility is not identity": an agreed satisfaction score is not thereby a changed incentive.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> Resistance to institutional reform is not a conservative force; it is what happens when members have no shared anchor, so they update on one another and converge on whoever already had the most influence. The drift is coherent, which is why nobody inside notices. So the Tether does not name a reform, it gives the shape one must have: tie the new incentive to a measure incumbents cannot move, let each group range against that measure separately instead of negotiating, change what actually pays rather than only what is written, add monitors and comparison sites instead of relying on repeal, declare the reformer's own stake, and report the residual for each affected group rather than one averaged score. Pick each next pilot to differ from the last and to match the residual it surfaced. Three refuters were written in advance, and the framework stops where the instruments stop: which measure to use is settled by the institution's ground, not by the diagram.
