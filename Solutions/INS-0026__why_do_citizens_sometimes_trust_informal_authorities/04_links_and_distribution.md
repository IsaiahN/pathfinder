## 2. What the wider literature bears on the question

**One broad evidence pool, fewer witnesses than pages.** Legal pluralism, procedural justice, the Weber pages, traditional and charismatic authority, polycentric law and everyday resistance all circulate in the same law-and-society / social-theory neighbourhood. Per Fig 2 :31, *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Their agreement that informal forums matter is one witness, not seven. Polycentric law in particular is a normative literature with a declared stake in non-monopoly, so its agreement with the reach condition is weak corroboration and is recorded as such.

- Legal pluralism reports that *"individuals would choose to bring their legal claims under the system that they thought would offer them the best advantage"* and that *"the formal ideology of law as it is written exists alongside the informal ideology of law as it is used"*. This corroborates C2 (a differing closure already holds a cheaper term) at the world level, but it is a frame reading of forum choice, not a measurement of resolution.
- The SEP entry on authority states that *"the duty of the subject is grounded not in the content of the directive itself but in the nature of the source issuing it"*. This corroborates the source-not-content rule and therefore the warning that a legitimacy belief is a claim about a source, not about outcomes.
- Procedural justice supplies the low-overlap warning that *"giving disgruntled group members a voice regardless of whether it is instrumental or non-instrumental is sometimes enough for a process to be viewed as fair"*. A fairness reading can rise while resolution does not. That is a saturated metric (Fig 10 :26), not maintained contact.
- The Weber pages supply names, not derivations. *"In traditional authority, the legitimacy of the authority comes from tradition"* is a binding from the sociology frame. The charisma page adds that Weber saw authority *"as a relationship between the leader and his followers"*, which corroborates the valence relation (Fig 11 :50) rather than any list of types.
- Everyday resistance supplies the metamerism warning: *"Everyday resistance is a dispersed, quiet, seemingly invisible and disguised form of resistance"*. The same observed routing away from the state can be a legitimacy judgment, a resistance practice, or simple convenience. This corroborates F6's source rule, not a specific cause.
- **Trust (sentiment)** is the lowest-overlap substantive source, coming from social psychology and behavioural economics. It defines trust as *"the belief that another person will do what is expected and is built through repeated consistency"* and as *"a heuristic decision rule, allowing a person to deal with complexities that would require unrealistic effort in rational reasoning"*. It also notes *"There is asymmetry in the building versus destruction of trust"*. These corroborate the contact-and-channel shape: repeated consistency is contact, the heuristic is the per-slot loop, and the asymmetry matches channel fragility.

**Framework-internal essays** are used for the canonical reading of "shape" in `05_what_shape_means.md` only. Their author wrote the figures, so they carry no independent witness weight; that overlap is recorded in the ledger. The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap essay mentions Ostrom's *"Beyond Markets and States: Polycentric Governance of Complex Economic Systems"* as a field that rhymed with the author's multi-agent work. This is framework-internal corroboration of the reach shape, not an independent world source, and is recorded that way.

## 3. Distribution rules

Figure-grounded reasons for how material is offered:

- **Fig 4 :22:** *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Send the generator, the residual and the refuter, not the conclusion. A recording carried upward *"looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion"* (Fig 4 :25).
- **Fig 10 :42:** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28:** *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"* if the residual is not described first. Ask what they would expect to see if the two-condition shape were true before showing the source.
- **Fig 2 :40:** agreement from a shared pool is worthless as a verdict. Later agreement about the framework is never pooled as corroboration.
- **Fig 10 :59:** *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* Declare the stake; do not ask for belief. For this question the author's stake is structural: a framework that claims generality gains from a successful application to institutional trust. That interest is in the outcome and must be declared; an interest in the reading would be disqualifying.

**Hard practical constraint.** IPSA links a *Journal Guidelines on Generative AI* resource from its fetched homepage, and the venue pages for law and political science generally require submitted work to be authored by a person who can defend it. This whole solution is an LLM-assisted simulated derivation and is labelled as such everywhere. It must **not** be submitted as an authored empirical or theoretical article. The legitimate use is as a private checking instrument: post the residual and the refuter, invite a human to run the check, and let that human author whatever they conclude, disclosing the AI contribution under the applicable policy. The IPSA guideline page itself was not opened, so this is recorded as a constraint signalled by a fetched page, not as a quoted rule.

**Order in any reply:** (1) the shape claim and the pre-registered refuter; (2) the question "what would you count as a failure here?"; (3) only after a verdict is recorded, the three source links with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (4) the formula as a reusable method, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

## 4. The two-stage offer, compressed

**Stage 1.** Send `08_expert_verification_packet.md` sections 1 to 3 only: the one-paragraph claim, the three checkable sub-claims with their verbatim figure lines and refuters, and the non-claims. No provenance bundle, no essay links.

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If the recipient is curious and has already recorded a verdict on stage 1, offer the three public links: the figure text, the formula and usage rule, and the essays. Their later agreement is a conversation, not corroboration (Fig 2 :40).

**Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method, never as an answer (Fig 4 :22). Each user writes a refuter before running it and states the mode of what they get.

**Stop rule.** If stage 1 fails, the theory is not offered. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.

**Hosting.** Nothing here is hosted or sent by this agent. The human operator hosts the packet on an opt-in page and sends one to one. Prefer a download page that lets a recipient decline contact over an attachment pushed to a list.
