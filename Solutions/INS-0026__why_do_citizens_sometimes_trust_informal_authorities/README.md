## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not the *level* of trust in an authority but *which slot keeps a smaller residual*. In effect terms: a citizen's library Γ predicts how an authority will treat them; the citizen acts by relying on one forum rather than another; o′ is what that forum actually delivers; R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| is measured per citizen-slot and per domain (dispute, service, safety, land, family), never averaged into a national trust score (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22).

- Option A, "informal authorities possess a legitimacy that the state lacks": leads to the prediction that the smaller residual is a property of the informal authority considered alone.
- Option B, "the smaller residual is a relation between citizen, habitat and forum that must be maintained": leads to the prediction that routing changes when contact with the citizen's ground changes or when a differing closure becomes available (Fig 11 :39, Fig 11 :40).

What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a shape naming the two conditions whose conjunction keeps a citizen's reliance residual small in a given domain, plus an instrument that discriminates a failure of one condition from a failure of the other, and an honest `gap` where no instrument can name the specific authority. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no loop check can see it. It is now bound, so the refuter in `01` section 4 can fire on it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B in `specified` mode, kept per domain and never averaged.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the institutional question, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of authority.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
