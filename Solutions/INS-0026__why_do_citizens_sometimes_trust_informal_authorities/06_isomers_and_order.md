## Q1 - Are a state court and a customary forum isomers?

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** (Fig 12 :117)
> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** (Fig 12 :124)
> **"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."** (Fig 12 :46)

Both forums hold the atom "resolves a dispute". If a state court composes it as `rule → evidence → judgment` and a customary forum composes it as `relationship → acknowledgement → restitution`, they are **isomers** under Fig 12 :117: same atom, different bond, different behaviour, with the difference invisible in any list that records only "dispute resolution available". This is the figure-grounded version of the intuition that it is not what a forum has but how it composes what it has, and it is stronger, because the arrangement is itself a substance that carries information the ingredient list does not.

If the two forums share only a pattern (both do `matter → hearing → decision`) while differing in which atoms occupy the slots, the weaker claim holds: they are members of the same **functional group** (Fig 12 :124), the qualifier saying which member. The presence of the pattern fixes the kind, not which citizen's residual goes down.

The distinction is testable. A forum inventory that records only "courts: 2, customary forums: 3" cannot distinguish isomer from functional-group; the bond has to be recorded. Fig 12 :47: *"A recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself."* The legal-pluralism page's observation that *"the formal ideology of law as it is written exists alongside the informal ideology of law as it is used"* is the world-pool name for this: same law atom, different bond.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two forums with identical atoms and identical recorded bonds nevertheless diverge in which citizen-slot they serve with no difference in ground contact or imported atoms, the isomer framing is insufficient and the difference must be habitat (D2), which would move the explanation out of D4.

**Status:** `specified`, triangulated for the legality of the reading (Fig 12 and Fig 11 :50, different figures), single-source for the institutional application. The world corroboration is the legal-pluralism and polycentric-law pages, both in the same broad law pool.

## Q2 - What order do the conditions bind in?

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."** (Fig 3 :20-21)
> **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** (Fig 3 :19)

Order for a citizen facing a matter to resolve:

1. **Perception.** Can the citizen read how each forum actually treats people in this domain? Failure here is visible and has obvious tests: published resolution rates, observed treatment of neighbours.
2. **Vocabulary.** Does their Γ have terms for what each forum affords, and can they say what a resolved matter would count as? This is the middle link. Fig 3 :26: *"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."*
3. **Objective.** Can they state a comparison that would count as getting somewhere? Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* A vocabulary with only institution names cannot state a resolution criterion.
4. **Planning.** A route to that forum under incomplete information and possible pressure.
5. **Learn and carry.** Name what worked, verify it against the resolved matter, carry it to the next one.

The two conditions map onto this chain: C1 (contact) is mostly a failure of links 1 and 2; C2 (reach) is mostly a failure of link 5, because the state names what worked but never imports a new atom from a differing closure. That mapping is why a single lever cannot clear both (Fig 9 :55).

The order also decides which instrument to buy first. Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* The perception link has surveys and administrative data; link 5 has almost none. So budget piles up at links 1 and 4 while the break sits at 2 and 5. The SEP authority entry's source-based rule and the procedural-justice voice finding both operate at link 1 or 2 and can look like a whole theory of trust; they are instruments, not the chain.

**Falsifier:** a citizen whose chain demonstrably works at 1, 2 and 3 and fails only at 4. That would falsify the "usually middle" claim of F8 in the primary audit.

## Q3 - Competing explanations, and what each can and cannot reach

| candidate explanation | what it names | figure-line bearing | what it cannot reach |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Weber's traditional authority** (Wikipedia, via Bendix) | legitimacy tied to tradition or custom | a binding onto Γ, not a derivation; Fig 1 :27 bars scoring the frame with its own name | Why the same traditional forum loses valence when the matter changes (Fig 11 :49-51) |
| **Charismatic authority** (Wikipedia) | personality-based legitimacy, founded on followers' recognition | matches the relation reading of Fig 11 :50 | It is a type name; no instrument for which matter will be taken to whom |
| **Legal pluralism** (Wikipedia) | multiple legal orders; forum shopping for advantage | close to C2 and to Fig 6 :23 | Whether the chosen forum actually resolved the matter; it records choice, not the ground |
| **Procedural justice** (Wikipedia, Tyler and Blader) | voice, fairness perceptions, internalised obligation | close to C1's channel readings and to Fig 10 :26 | A fairness reading can rise while resolution does not; non-instrumental voice is a frame, not a ground |
| **Everyday resistance** (Wikipedia, Scott) | disguised, dispersed resistance to power | corroborates the metamerism rule, Fig 8 :50 and Fig 9 :27-28 | Cannot distinguish resistance from legitimacy from convenience using routing alone |
| **Polycentric law** (Wikipedia) | competing or overlapping legal providers | close to C2, but a normative literature with a declared stake | Its preference for non-monopoly is an interest in the reading; zero verdict weight |
| **Trust as heuristic** (Wikipedia, Trust sentiment) | repeated consistency, in-group preference, asymmetry | close to the per-slot loop and channel fragility | The heuristic is a mechanism inside Γ, not a discriminator of which forum won |
| **State illegitimacy as sole cause** | a deficit in the state | absorbed into D3 (Fig 9 :55) | A citizen with a failing state channel and no alternative forum does not switch; one lever cannot clear the union |
| **Coercion or dependency** | an unchosen reliance | Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence"* | Cannot be excluded from inside a frame; honest `gap`, not a rival verdict |

No candidate is discarded by prestige or familiarity. Each is ranged against the same residual, in the order Fig 8 :53 requires. The point is not to pick one; it is to notice that the first seven are all in one broad law-and-social-theory pool (Fig 2 :31) and that none of them supplies the source-discriminating instrument F9 asks for.

## Q4 - Does the shape generate the literature's answers?

Partly. The shape can generate:

- the Weberian emphasis on the source of authority, as the source-not-content rule under a convention that has not yet decided an outcome;
- the legal-pluralist emphasis on competing forums, as C2 and Fig 6 :23;
- the procedural-justice emphasis on voice and fairness, as C1's channel readings, with the saturation warning attached;
- the resistance emphasis on hidden refusal, as the metamerism warning and Fig 8 :50;
- the trust literature's repeated consistency and in-group preference, as the per-slot loop and Γ.

It cannot generate the *name* of the forum that will work next, nor the source of a given reliance reading. That is the `gap` recorded in F9 of `01`. It is a gap at the instrument boundary, not a silence: Fig 13 :134 (*"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*) and Fig 8 :39 apply. The source essay states why: an instrument that reads a result *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"the answer is unreached until a better instrument exists"*.

## CONCLUSION

Two forums with the same atom but different bonds are isomers under Fig 12 :117; two forums sharing only a `matter → hearing → decision` pattern are members of one functional group under Fig 12 :124. The conditions bind in dependency order, with the middle link (vocabulary and objective) and the carry link the usual breaks, which is why C1 and C2 need different instruments. The standard institutional explanations are mostly bindings of one evidence pool and are generated by the shape only up to the point where a name or a source would be required. The isomer claim has a written falsifier; the order claim has one; the naming and source gaps are declared rather than filled.
