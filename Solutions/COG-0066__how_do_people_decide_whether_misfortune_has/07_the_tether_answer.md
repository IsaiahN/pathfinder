# The Tether's answer: deciding the cause of a misfortune

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A person holds a prediction about how things will go. An event arrives that misses it at one tracked slot. R is positive. Something is owed: a cause term that makes the event expected enough that R stops arriving. The question asks which of four labels the person chooses. The derivation answers the upstream question: what was missing or wrong in the person's library that made one binding rather than another payable. The four labels are a *union* of overlapping conditions, not a partition (Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*), so each intersection has its own loop.

## 2. The shape, one route at a time

1. **Expected event, nothing owed.** Fig 5 :11 *"held: the model was right, nothing owed"*. "Just bad luck" is a stopped search, not a cause term. Do not count it as a fourth or fifth cause.
2. **Anomalous event, extend perception.** Fig 5 :12 *"new: extend perception, aim the probe here"*. No cause is bound yet. Observations are gathered.
3. **Known cause mis-attached, re-fit.** Fig 5 :13 *"mis-attached: refit what you have, do not invent"*. A familiar actor (much of the **social** case) or a familiar mechanism (much of the naive **natural** case) is re-fitted. Nothing new is minted.
4. **Mechanism broken, mint inside the closure.** Fig 5 :14 *"mechanism broken: a new term is owed"*. This is where a genuinely new **natural** cause term is licensed, subject to support, reachability and novelty.
5. **Held term false, refusal owed.** Fig 5 :53 *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"*. This is the shape of the **moral** case. What people usually bind is a character term (they deserved it, they were careless), which is a documented mis-route: Fig 12 :140 *"A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate."* The refusal is available in principle (Operators table :58 *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*) but is not what gets used.
6. **Closure differs, import.** Fig 6 :23 *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* The **supernatural** case is not a sixth routing bin. It imports an agent term from a closure that differs, moving the explanatory wall, and needs a source gate and a shadow test. Agent-detection research corroborates the shape: agents are ascribed when the cost of a miss is high, a budget asymmetry rather than a verdict (see `09_watson_answer.md`).

**The labels themselves are relations, not kinds.** Fig 11 :49 *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Whether a cause is read as social or supernatural depends on the observer's residual and habitat. A declared category is not a grounded one (Fig 10 :18 *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"*). The four words are a working convention, which Fig 2 :41 calls *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*, and never a verdict.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any of the four labels is false, or that religious attribution is irrational.
- Not that the four form a partition, or that picking one excludes the others. The union claim is the opposite.
- Not that "bad luck" is a cause term. It is a stopped search.
- Not that moral attribution is a mistake deserving correction. It is a structural mis-route: a refusal owed, a character term minted.
- Not that supernatural agents exist or do not exist. The framework speaks about the wall move and the provenance debt, not about the entity.
- Not that a cross-cultural average settles an individual case. Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
- Not `grounded`. No measurement discriminates the bins here.

## 5. What would settle it, the ground

Because the question is constitutively about people, human judgment is the legitimate anchor (Fig 2 :37), and it must be measured, not negotiated (Fig 2 :45 *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*).

1. **An inward instrument.** A probe already returning something that fails to resolve which route a person took at the moment of attribution, for example a time-course measure distinguishing probe-aiming (bin 2) from re-fitting (bin 3) and from term minting (bin 4).
2. **A source-discriminating instrument.** *The Hard Problem is About Source*: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"*. An instrument that reads "witchcraft" cannot by itself say whether the source was an import, a re-fitting or a moral refusal. The essay's conclusion applies: *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."*
3. **An outward frame.** A frame whose closure predicts the union shape and whose terms can be cast in effect terms, with provenance and a shadow test (Fig 6 :30 *"Cast it in effect terms."*).
4. **The standing falsifiers** in `01` §4. Any one firing narrows or withdraws the re-description.

Search alone will not do it: Fig 8 :32 *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."* And a null result is not an absence: Fig 8 :39 *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The four categories are a visible convention, not nature's own list. The answer stops where the instruments cannot distinguish a mint from a re-fit from an import, and the lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. Fig 13 :134 *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*, and the figures README: *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not tell you which of four labels is correct. It says that a person's decision about a misfortune is a routing of a prediction failure at one slot: if the event was expected, no cause is owed; if it was anomalous, a probe is aimed first; if a familiar cause was mis-attached, it is re-fitted rather than invented; if a mechanism genuinely failed, a new natural term is minted; and if a held expectation was refuted, what is owed is a refusal, though people usually mint a character term instead. The supernatural case is different in kind: it imports an agent term from a frame whose closure differs, which moves the explanatory wall and incurs a provenance debt. The four everyday labels are overlapping bindings onto these moves, not a partition, and their valence depends on the observer's habitat, not on the event. What the framework offers is that shape, six refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The slot stays open until the ground settles it.
