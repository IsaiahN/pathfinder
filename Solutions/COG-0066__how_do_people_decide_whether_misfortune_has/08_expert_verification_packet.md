# Expert verification packet: how people decide the cause of a misfortune

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in causal attribution or belief studies can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** No measurement discriminates the routes here. This is a simulation, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The four labels (social, natural, moral, supernatural) are not a partition that people choose from. They are overlapping bindings onto a routing of a slot-indexed prediction failure. When the event was expected, nothing is owed and "bad luck" is a stopped search. When it was anomalous, perception is extended and a probe is aimed before any cause is bound. When a familiar cause was mis-attached, it is re-fitted rather than invented. When a mechanism genuinely failed, a new natural term is minted inside the current closure. When a held expectation was refuted, a refusal is owed, though the usual result is a minted character term. When the observer's closure lacks a paying term, a non-natural agent is imported from a frame whose closure differs, moving the explanatory wall and incurring a provenance debt. Labels are relative to the observer's residual and habitat, so the same cause can be read differently in different slots.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1  -  The routing claim (framework-internal and ground-facing)

- **Statement:** the decision routes a positive residual by what is missing or wrong before any of the four labels is bound. Expected events owe nothing; novel events trigger probing; mis-attached causes are re-fitted; broken mechanisms owe a mint; refuted expectations owe a refusal.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :11 *"held: the model was right, nothing owed"*; Fig 5 :12 *"new: extend perception, aim the probe here"*; Fig 5 :13 *"mis-attached: refit what you have, do not invent"*; Fig 5 :14 *"mechanism broken: a new term is owed"*; Fig 5 :53 *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"*. Slot indexing: Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
- **What to check:** does the observed sequence show a probe stage before binding for anomalous events, and a re-fit rather than a mint for familiar causes? Is the effect present per event rather than only in a cultural average?
- **Refuter:** the same observer with the same vocabulary routes a predicted event and a novel mechanism failure identically, with no difference in probing or term minting; or a group-level rate predicts individual binding without loss of live slots.

### C2  -  The union and habitat-relative claim (cross-cultural and social)

- **Statement:** the four conditions overlap, so an event can be natural and supernatural at once; whether a cause is read as social or supernatural is relative to the observer's residual and habitat, not intrinsic to the cause.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 11 :49 *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :50 *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."*
- **What to check:** find a single event for which the same observer affirms a physical mechanism and an agentic cause. The Azande granary case is the recorded example (Wikipedia, *Azande witchcraft*, quoting Evans-Pritchard): the poles were eaten by termites and the timing was attributed to witchcraft. Also check whether the same agent is classified differently when the residual changes.
- **Refuter:** the four conditions are mutually exclusive by construction, so affirming one denies the others, with no observer able to hold two; or a cause's classification is invariant across residuals and independent habitats.

### C3  -  The refusal and import claim (moral and supernatural cases)

- **Statement:** the moral case has the shape of a refuted term, where refusal is owed but a character term is minted. The supernatural case is not a routing bin: it is an import from a closure that differs, requiring provenance and a shadow test.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :140 *"A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate."* Fig 6 :14 *"the only operator that moves the wall"*. Fig 6 :23 *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 8 :50-51 *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*.
- **What to check:** in a violated-expectation paradigm, is the norm refused, or is a disposition attributed? In an agentic-attribution case, is there a record of where the agent term came from, or is it treated as self-evident?
- **Refuter:** a population that refuses the norm and mints no character term; or a supernatural attribution requiring no import, no provenance gap and no wall move; or an imported term that fails the shadow test while the attribution persists with no residual.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that any of the four labels is false, or that supernatural belief is irrational.
- Not that the four form a partition. The claim is the opposite.
- Not that "bad luck" is a cause term. It is a stopped search.
- Not that moral attribution is a moral error. It is a structural mis-route.
- Not that supernatural agents exist or do not exist. Only the wall move and provenance debt are claimed.
- Not that a cross-cultural average decides an individual case.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"* and *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* Concretely: a time-course measure that separates probe-aiming from re-fitting from minting; a paradigm that separates refusal of a norm from attribution of character; a record of where an agent term entered an explanatory closure. Search inside one closure will not do it (Fig 8 :32 *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."*).
