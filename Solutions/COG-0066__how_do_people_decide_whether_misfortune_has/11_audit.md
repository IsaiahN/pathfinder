## Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual line alone, before using the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** An observation misses a prediction at one tracked slot. Arity: one event at one slot, so per-slot (Fig 1 :19). Symmetry: broken, because agentic and mechanism causes differ in available theory of mind, and actor and observer differ in the term they bind. Scale: first order inside the observer's frame, second order at frame contact.
- **Domain 1, the perceiver's loop.** Actors: observer, belief, library, observation, slot. Currency: residual and description cost. Anchor: what pays against the ground. Question type: a partition of the residual into bins by what is missing or wrong.
- **Domain 2, the habitat.** Actors: social others, mechanisms, norms, non-natural agents, conditions, relations. Currency: contact with the residual. Anchor: the ground. Question type: enumeration. Verdict: labels are relations to the residual, not kinds.
- **Domain 3, frame contact.** Actors: the observer's closure and closures that differ. Currency: atoms crossing a wall. Anchor: provenance and shadow test. Question type: import. This domain exists only when a closure differs.
- **Domain 4, verification.** Actors: outside frames. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Because the question is about people, human judgment is the legitimate anchor; internal seat agreement here is zero-independence.
- **Union or partition.** Union. A single misfortune can satisfy natural, social, moral and supernatural conditions together, so an overlap is a compound with its own loop (Fig 9 :55, :57).
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, carry. Label-first protocols read below the break.

My census matches the primary's four domains, its union reading and its dependency order. I reached it from a different starting point: I began with Fig 9's union/partition split and Fig 1's per-slot rule, whereas the primary began with Fig 5's bin sorting and Fig 11's valence rule. Different starting figures, same boundaries: weak non-circular agreement inside the framework, still one evidence pool.

## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :19 (per-slot), Fig 5 :11-14 and :53 (bins plus refusal), Fig 9 :55 (union), Fig 11 :49 (valence), Fig 6 :23 (import), Fig 2 :37 (human judgment as anchor).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07_the_tether_answer.md`: four labels are overlapping bindings onto routes, with one import case and one refusal-deficit case.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete claim about one person's attribution of one event.

**R_T is nonzero.** Four constraints held by the shorthand do not survive the trip back down:

- **which slot** carried the residual (dropped by the cultural-average phrasing);
- **which conditions overlapped** in the compound (dropped by saying the four "overlap" without naming the intersection);
- **whether refusal or minting occurred** in the moral case (the shorthand says "refusal deficit", which hides the actual bound term);
- **whether the agent term was imported with a source gate** (the shorthand says "import case" without the provenance record).

One thing survives cleanly: a positive residual is routed by what is missing or wrong before a label is bound. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists. It is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the four-label shorthand as identical to the figure set would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
