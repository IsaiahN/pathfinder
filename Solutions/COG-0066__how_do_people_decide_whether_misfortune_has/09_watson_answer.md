## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what people are doing when they assign a misfortune to a social, natural, moral or supernatural cause. Option A: treat the four labels as a partition and find the rule that selects the right one. Option B: re-describe the decision as a routing of a slot-indexed residual by what is missing or wrong, with overlapping labels whose valence is relative to the observer's habitat and one case that requires an import. A sends a `general`-mode verdict about four borrowed words and invites a debate that does not shrink with effort. B sends a checkable shape, refuters written before the run, and a ground that could settle it. The figures decide B.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** An observation misses the prediction at one tracked slot. Arity: one event at one slot, so per-slot, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19). Symmetry: not interchangeable. Agentic causes carry a theory of mind available to the observer; mechanism causes do not; moral attribution is asymmetric between actor and observer. Scale: first order inside the observer's frame, second order at frame contact when a term crosses a closure boundary.
- **Domain 1, the perceiver's loop.** Actors: observer, belief b, library Γ, observation o', object slot. Currency: residual R per slot and the description cost of a binding. Anchor: the ground, meaning what happened and what pays. Question type: a partition of the residual into routing bins.
- **Domain 2, the habitat of candidate causes.** Actors: social others, physical mechanisms, a normative order, non-natural agents, conditions and relations. Currency: contact with the residual, read off the world. Anchor: the ground. Question type: enumeration, never composition. Its verdict is that labels are relations, not kinds.
- **Domain 3, frame contact.** Actors: the observer's closure and closures that differ. Currency: atoms crossing a wall. Anchor: provenance and shadow test. Question type: import, not routing. Without a differing closure this domain does not exist.
- **Domain 4, verification.** Actors: outside frames and expert readers. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Because the question is constitutively about people, measured human judgment is the legitimate anchor. In this simulated run all seats share one evidence pool, so their independence is zero.
- **Partition or union.** Union. Fig 9 :55 says a bundled problem is a union of overlapping conditions. A single event can be termite damage, a maintenance failure, a punishment and a hostile agent at once, and each intersection is a compound with its own loop.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Perception, then vocabulary, then objective, then planning, then learn and carry. A label-first protocol reads below the break.

Strain test applied: the first reading mapped all four labels onto Fig 5's bins. Supernatural strained and was re-censused into domain 3; moral was re-censused into the fifth bin, which owes a refusal. See `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §7.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The question's own four-way framing is the wrong shape. People route a slot-indexed prediction failure by what is missing or wrong in their library: expected events owe nothing, novel events trigger a probe, familiar causes are re-fitted, broken mechanisms owe a mint, refuted expectations owe a refusal that is usually replaced by a character term, and a missing term in a differing closure is imported with a provenance debt. The four labels are overlapping bindings onto these moves, not a partition, and their valence is relative to the observer's residual and habitat. This is a `specified`, ungrounded result. It does not say any label is false, does not settle whether supernatural agents exist, and does not promote a cultural average to an individual decision. The strongest single reading is Fig 9 :55 plus the Azande granary case, because the figure line and the ethnography come from different evidence pools.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, sub-claims and refuters before the conclusion, and keeps verdicts per domain, so an error has an address. The real cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending the theory before the refuter (Fig 10 :42), which Fig 2 :42 prices as *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* That cost is avoidable by leading with the residual.
- **undoable:** yes. The re-description can be narrowed domain by domain without withdrawing the whole shape. A failed union claim leaves the routing claim intact; a failed import claim leaves the habitat-relativity claim intact. No primitive has to be withdrawn, and the six refuters make each cut reversible and ranked.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A time-course instrument that separates probe-aiming from re-fitting from minting would settle the routing claim; a paradigm separating refusal of a norm from attribution of character would settle the moral claim; a demonstrated mutually exclusive partition would break the union claim; a supernatural attribution with no import, provenance gap or wall move would break domain 3. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
