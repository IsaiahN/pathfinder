# Outreach draft: check the re-description before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until you have checked that the refuter in §"What would refute this" is still the one recorded in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4. If it has changed, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One output to check on how people attribute a misfortune

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question as usually posed:** how do people decide whether a misfortune has social, natural, moral, or supernatural causes?
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** that question is not one decision among four labels. It is a union of overlapping conditions, and the actual decision is a routing of a prediction failure. Concretely:
>
> 1. When the event was already expected in outline, nothing is owed. "Just bad luck" is a *stopped* search, not a cause term.
> 2. When the event is anomalous, the person extends perception and aims a probe before binding anything.
> 3. When a known cause has been mis-attached, the person re-fits a term they already hold, a neighbour, a faulty brake, a familiar mechanism.
> 4. When a mechanism has genuinely failed, a new natural cause term is owed.
> 5. When a held expectation is refuted, what is owed is a **refusal** of that expectation. What people usually bind instead is a character term: they deserved it, they were careless, the world is just. That is a documented mis-route in the derivation, not a verdict about anyone's morality.
> 6. A supernatural attribution is not a fifth kind of routing. It imports an agent term from a frame whose closure differs, which moves the explanatory wall. It should carry provenance and a shadow test, or be marked as carrying neither.
>
> The four everyday labels are therefore **bindings onto these moves**, and they overlap. The same event can be termite damage, a maintenance failure, a punishment and a hostile agent at once. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
>
> **What this is not claiming:** that a particular culture is right or wrong, that religious attribution is irrational, or that the four labels are useless. As categorical words they are a convention, and conventions are useful. The claim is only about what the decision routes on, and it is a shape, not a measurement.
>
> **What would refute it** (written down before this message was sent):
> 1. The same observer, with the same vocabulary, routes a predicted event and a novel mechanism failure identically, with no difference in probing or term minting.
> 2. The four conditions are mutually exclusive by construction, so affirming the supernatural denies the natural, with no observer able to hold both.
> 3. A population facing a refuted normative expectation refuses the expectation and mints no character term.
> 4. A supernatural attribution needs no import, has no provenance gap, and moves no wall.
> 5. A group-level attribution rate predicts an individual's per-event binding without losing the live slot.
>
> If any one holds, I will narrow or withdraw the re-description. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures, verbatim with line numbers (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - The loop and the mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - Cognitive Science Society submissions and journals: https://cognitivesciencesociety.org/submissions/
> - IACESR, the association for cognitive and evolutionary sciences of religion: https://iacesr.com/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
