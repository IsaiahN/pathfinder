# Are the four causes isomers? Structure, order and analogues

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks for a four-way choice. Before answering it we must know what kind of set those four words form. Two structural questions are owed: are they **isomers** under Fig 12, and is the decision **symmetric** under exchange of the labels? A third is owed on order: in what dependency sequence does the decision actually run?

## DERIVATION

### Q1  -  Are social, natural, moral and supernatural causes isomers?

Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

An isomer claim requires a fixed atom set shared by all four, with only the bond differing. This set fails that test:

- A **natural** binding lives in domain 1 as a mint inside the current closure.
- A **social** binding lives in domain 1 as a re-fitting of a known actor from the habitat, domain 2.
- A **moral** binding lives in the refusal domain: a term held is false, refusal is owed, a character term is minted instead.
- A **supernatural** binding lives in domain 3: an atom crosses from a closure that differs.

They do not share one atom set with different bonds. They are bindings at **different loop levels**. So they are **not isomers** under Fig 12.

The closest Fig 12 structure is the functional group. Fig 12 :124 *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* The shared substructure across all four is a positive residual at a slot. The qualifier, which route was taken, says which member of the group.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** exhibit a fixed atom set shared by all four causes such that they differ only in which bond joins the atoms, with no difference in loop level, closure or provenance. If that succeeds, the isomer claim is true and this finding fails.

### Q2  -  Is the choice symmetric under exchange of labels?

Fig 12 :92 *"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."* If the four were related by a symmetric bond, exchanging any two would leave one term and one meaning.

They do not. Exchanging natural and supernatural changes the closure and the provenance owed (import versus mint). Exchanging moral and natural changes what is owed (refusal versus a new mechanism term). Exchanging the perspective of actor and observer is the documented actor/observer asymmetry in the attribution literature: the same event gets a dispositional term for another person and a situational one for oneself. The choice is **ordered**, not symmetric, and the ordering is set by the habitat and the slot, not by any rank among the four words.

**Falsifier:** show an observer for whom swapping any two of the four labels leaves both the decision and the evidence required unchanged, across at least two different residuals.

### Q3  -  What is the dependency order of the decision?

Fig 3 :20 *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* Fig 3 :19 *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*

The order derived in `01` §2d is: **perception** (is there a residual, on which slot) to **vocabulary** (which cause terms are reachable in Γ) to **objective** (what would count as left(R,φ) falling) to **planning** (which probe to aim) to **learn and carry**. A protocol that asks "which of the four is it?" at the start is acting at the planning stage while perception and vocabulary are unsettled, which Fig 3 :22 calls a reading of nothing.

This is a `general` structural claim. Its world check is a task for an instrument: a protocol that records probe-aiming before term-binding should show a different time course from one that records binding first. No such measurement was taken here.

### Q4  -  Is the four-label frame a shadow or an echo?

Fig 8 :50-51 *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*. The four labels look like a frame that predicts the residual's shape. That could be convergent derivation (the labels were reached independently by attribution research) or an adopted import (the labels were borrowed from everyday vocabulary and given somewhere to live). The contents cannot tell us. Only the record can. `01` §2 was written before the world search, and the labels were treated as borrowed words from the outset (Fig 9 :31), which is the provenance discipline this figure requires.

**Falsifier:** show that the four labels were selected because they fit the derivation after the fact, with no independent record of their prior use, and the shadow claim fails.

### Q5  -  Round trip gap R_T

Fig 4 :30 *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure lines, chiefly Fig 1 :19 (per-slot residual), Fig 5 :11-14 and :53 (routing bins and refusal), Fig 9 :55 (union), Fig 11 :49 (valence relative to residual), Fig 6 :23 (import).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07_the_tether_answer.md`, "four labels are overlapping bindings onto routes, with one import and one refusal case".
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete claim about one person's attribution of one event.

**R_T is nonzero.** Four constraints are dropped by the shorthand and reappear in the concrete case: which slot carried the residual; which conditions overlapped in the compound; whether a refusal was owed or a term was minted; and whether the agent term was imported with a source gate. The shorthand is a generator, not a recording. Treating "four labels map to four bins" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest report is a lossy condensation with a recorded gap. R_T is not measured numerically, because no ground reading exists.

## CONCLUSION

The four causes are **not isomers**. They share a functional group (a positive residual at a slot) and differ by loop level, closure and provenance. The choice is **ordered**, not symmetric, with the order supplied by the habitat and the slot. The dependency chain runs perception to vocabulary to objective to planning to carry, so a label-first protocol reads below the break. The four-label frame is treated as borrowed vocabulary until provenance is shown. R_T is nonzero and recorded qualitatively.
