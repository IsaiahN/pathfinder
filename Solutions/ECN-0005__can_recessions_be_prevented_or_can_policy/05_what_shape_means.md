## 1. A shape is a constraint you can state before you can name the filler

The canonical figures README returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)". Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."

From *The Library of Babel* (opened): "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

So a **shape** narrows the class of admissible answers without filling the slot. For this question the shape is:

> a maintained seat, with a per-slot ground that does not update on policy announcements, a refusal operator for held fragilities, continuous comparison of amplitude against that ground, and no terminal handoff.

That shape rules out a single lever and a final state. It does **not** rule in a named central-bank tool, a multiplier, or a percentage. Those are fillers, and the framework says it cannot name them.

## 2. How the shape is obtained: describe the effect, split the bundle, then bootstrap vocabulary

*The Library of Babel* (opened) gives the search rule: use the vocabulary already held that is closest to the unknown, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, and recurse. "With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary." It also gives the stopping rule: "the finish line will also be the starting place, as there is nowhere else to search and nothing else that can describe the answer better." And it gives the diagnostic that governs this question directly: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."

That is Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones," reached from a different field. The exemplar's `05` records the same convergence for the GR/QM question; here the bundle is the set of shock and propagation conditions named in the source file itself.

Fig 9 :30-31 "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause". So the residual was described in effect terms in `01` section 0 (refusal versus comparison, per slot, multiple conditions) before any frame was chosen. *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* [carried]: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." That is the outreach order in `02_outreach_email.md`.

## 3. What licenses a name: the ground, or a provenanced import

Only two things license a particular term, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground pays for it.** Formula step 5 says the ground settles a term, verified only by what pays, and that a gate is not the ground. The README: the framework "stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest".
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs**, with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52).

*They're Scanning Books When They Should* [carried]: "The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space." Also: "A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six." Also: "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."

For this question, the tempting fillers are named macro instruments. A claim such as "the countercyclical capital buffer prevents recessions" is a binding from the policy frame onto a held atom, not a derivation from the figures. It needs a ground reading and provenance. Naming it inside the shape claim would convert a visible choice into an invisible one.

### Correction to my own first reading (kept visible)

I initially wrote that the framework's "shape, not a name" rule forbids naming any policy instrument at all. That overstates it. The canonical phrase governs a **missing** primitive, one not held in `Γ`. Naming a binding onto an available atom is provenance, not an invented primitive; what is forbidden is claiming that the binding moves the wall (Fig 6 :25 "An import adds no atom to the world."). What remains forbidden in this record is presenting the shape as if it selected the instrument. Both claims are now recorded; the earlier, stronger one is withdrawn. This mirrors the correction preserved in the exemplar's `05` section 5b.

## 4. Why the target series cannot be the anchor: the source/projection error

*The Hard Problem is About Source* [carried] gives the mechanism. Metamerism: an instrument reads green and "cannot comment on the source" - one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." And: "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy." The spectrometer, not an argument, resolved it.

Here the reading is a headline aggregate or an inflation target. Several sources can produce the same reading: a genuine demand recovery, a statistical revision, a composition shift, a target-induced report change. Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does". Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." Naming the target as the anchor is the metamerism error: a source named from a projection. What is owed is a discriminator, not a name. The world source Hakkio 2013 (quoting Bean 2009) states the same limit from inside macroeconomics: "shocks are not measured directly, only their consequences."

## 5. What a shape cannot do, and why that is a feature

Recursive Self-Improvement Trap (opened): "the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself." And: "You may all agree and be wrong. You may set your clock to someone else's, who was setting your clock to yours, and be off by many deviations over time. You need ground truth to define where you stand."

So the shape returned here cannot certify itself. It cannot settle whether a given country prevented a given recession, because that needs a counterfactual the ground never supplies. It can say what any answer must satisfy, what would refute it, and which instruments are owed. That is the honest stop.

## 6. Prestige is not action, and my own audit is a simulation

Recursive Self-Improvement Trap (opened): "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly." "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige." A campaign that gathers famous economists to agree with a claim is the named collapse, not corroboration. A famous name can give the claim a budget to be tried; it cannot make the claim true.

Same essay: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post. It will often be better than the generic outputs that you will usually get otherwise too (intelligence is essentially arrangement). However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."

So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, `06_isomers_and_order.md`, `07_the_tether_answer.md` and this file are **simulated derivations** checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are not tests of the policy question and must be labelled as such. They cannot settle anything they report on.
