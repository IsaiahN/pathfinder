**Subject:** A boundary is a filter, not a wall. Would you count this as a failure?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one shape claim about community boundaries and contagion that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a community boundary does not simply stop spread. It discriminates by what crosses. A live infectious case that seeds a new local outbreak is a head start; a signal, warning or model update is a recording. The same cut therefore reduces spread when bridges pass few live cases and the local susceptible/infectious difference is spent before a bridge is used, and sustains spread when bridges pass live cases, or when a dense group holds a critical mass that a sparse bridge protects from dilution. The first case is a closure effect; the second is a catalyst and cycle effect. These are two conditions, not two points on one scale, so no single modularity value decides the sign.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** I am not claiming a critical modularity threshold, not claiming that modularity always slows epidemics, not claiming that weak ties always help, and not claiming to have settled any of this against outbreak data. Modularity Q is a filter for choosing what to study, not a witness for a verdict. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against a figure set, not a ground reading.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before this message:**
> 1. A first-order contagion reaching global prevalence with zero cross-boundary transmission events.
> 2. A controlled experiment showing dense internal contact does not sustain prevalence below the pairwise threshold with bridge rate held fixed.
> 3. A pre-registered cross-dataset test showing the simple/complex distinction does not predict the sign of the boundary effect.
> 4. A single scalar predicting the sign monotonically with no interaction term across all four regime cells.
> 5. A signal with no live case crossing reliably starting a new transmission chain.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so an unwritten preference is invisible to the check.
