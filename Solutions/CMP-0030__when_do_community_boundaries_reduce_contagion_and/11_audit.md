## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 4 :21-22 (directional filter, head start versus recording), Fig 6 :23 and Fig 8 :32 (closure without contact), Fig 12 :119 and :121 (catalyst, cycle), Fig 11 :52 and :66 (cascade, isolation).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07_the_tether_answer.md`: boundaries reduce when they deny contact with a differing closure, sustain when live cases cross and overlapping members catalyse, with a union of two conditions crossed with two orders.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete outbreak claim, for example "this school closure reduced R by 30 percent" or "modularity above Q star always protects."

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **identity of what crosses** (Fig 4 :22 separates a head start from a recording; a policy that changes one and not the other is invisible in the shorthand);
- the **order of the composition** (Fig 12 :104: first order inside a group, second order between groups; a single reproduction number cannot carry both);
- the **per-community residual** (Fig 1 :19: the global aggregate drops the one live slot that decides the sign).

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: a boundary is not a wall, and the sign depends on contact with a differing closure. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists. It is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the condensed answer as identical to the figures would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a recorded gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` states.
