## QUESTION

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry, scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor, partition or union, and dependency order.

- **R (the residual).** The same cut is reported as barrier and as reservoir. Arity: a partition relation over nodes plus a transmission relation of order 1 or order k greater than 1. Symmetry: crossing is directed from source group to target group; one-exposure transmission is effectively reversible, threshold transmission is not. Scale: first order inside a group, second order between settled groups. Fig 13 :36: "adjacency is not duration, an ordering is not a pathway, a pathway is not a direction." Counting paths and measuring directed crossing are different instruments.
- **Domain 1, structure.** Actors: nodes, internal edges, bridges, overlapping members, groups as meta-nodes. Currency: edges, modularity, membership overlap. Anchor: the measured adjacency or contact matrix. Question type: union, because communities may overlap.
- **Domain 2, dynamics.** Actors: infectious and susceptible nodes, exposures, threshold. Currency: infection events, reproduction number, persistence or extinction. Anchor: an outbreak curve or experiment. Question type: union of simple and complex contagion, crossed with two orders.
- **Domain 3, the boundary.** Actors: bridges, boundary nodes, high-betweenness and overlapping nodes. Currency: what crosses (live case versus signal), direction and crossing rate. Anchor: measured cross-boundary transmission events. Question type: a directional filter, not a wall.
- **Domain 4, verification.** Actors: researchers, models, panels. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all seats draw on one evidence pool, so independence is zero.
- **Order by dependency.** Measure structure (D1), characterise what crosses (D3), specify contagion order (D2), then verdict (D4). Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

Strain test: the first draft read Fig 4 as a literal claim about pathogens. Re-census moved the boundary into D3 and the figure now governs the type of thing that crosses. Canonical rule applied: a reading that makes one figure contradict another has misidentified its domain.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

A community boundary reduces contagion when it denies contact with a differing closure: bridges pass few live cases, one exposure would suffice, and the local susceptible/infectious difference is spent before a bridge is used. It sustains contagion when the same cut passes live cases and overlapping members act as catalysts within a cycle of local reinfection, or when a dense group holds a critical mass that the cut protects from dilution and later re-exports. The two are conditions of a union crossed with two orders, so a single modularity value cannot decide the sign. Modularity is a budget filter, not a witness. This is a `specified`, ungrounded result; the numeric crossover is a `gap` owed to an instrument. The shape survives, the number does not exist yet, and no domain is `grounded`.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The regime table and five refuters are written before the conclusion, so an error is findable. The real cost is spending a checker's independence by sending a finished verdict instead of a characterised residual. Fig 2 :42 says "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. No primitive has to be withdrawn. A wrong regime assignment is corrected by adding a sixth refuter or by splitting a cell; the record keeps the earlier reading visible. A sent verdict can be superseded by a corrected packet.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A crossing-type experiment distinguishing signals from live cases, a reservoir test at fixed bridge rate, or a pre-registered regime test across datasets would move a cell to `grounded` or kill it. A derivation of a seventh primitive, or a measurement in which one scalar predicts the sign across all four cells, would break the shape. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
