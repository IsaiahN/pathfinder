# The Tether's answer: when do community boundaries reduce contagion, and when do they sustain it?

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §5), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The same structural object, a dense-in/sparse-between cut, is reported both to stop spread and to keep it alive. Stated as an effect: what selects the sign? The figures answer with a shape, not a number: the sign depends on **what crosses**, **at what order**, and **how much the closures on either side overlap**. No figure line supplies the modularity value at which the sign flips, so that number is a declared gap (F8), not an omitted detail.

## 2. The shape

1. **A boundary is a filter with a direction, not a wall.** Fig 4 :21: "The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction." A live case that seeds a new local outbreak is a head start; a signal, warning or model update is a recording. Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." The same cut passes or blocks these differently.

2. **Structure and process are two substrates.** Fig 13 :24 says a network is "that entities relate, and influence travels"; Fig 13 :22 says a gradient is "a difference that can be spent." The bridge is structure; the susceptible/infectious contrast with directedness is the spendable difference. A crossing is a composition of the two (Fig 13 :74). Counting bridges without measuring the local difference composes within one kind and cannot predict the effect.

3. **The reduce regime.** When bridges are too sparse to constitute contact with a differing closure, the process exhausts what the local closure reaches and stops. Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom." This requires one-exposure contagion and a local difference spent faster than a bridge is used.

4. **The sustain regime.** When bridges pass live cases, boundary nodes act as catalysts and within-group reinfection as a cycle. Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use." Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes." A dense group can hold an endemic state below the pairwise threshold if a critical mass is seeded; the cut protects it from dilution and re-exports it later. Contact cascades three steps out, so a direct-contact reading misses the effect (Fig 11 :52-53). Isolation substitutes a habitat; it does not delete contact (Fig 11 :66).

5. **Two regimes, not one scale.** Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Four cells: one-exposure with rare bridges reduces; one-exposure with live-case bridges sustains; multi-exposure with signal-only bridges reduces; multi-exposure with a dense critical mass sustains locally. A single modularity value cannot decide across them. Modularity is a filter for the budget (Fig 9 :60), never a witness for the verdict.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a critical modularity value, a bridge density, or a reproduction-number threshold. Those are owed measurements (F8).
- Not that modularity always slows contagion, and not that weak ties always help. Those are single-cell claims promoted to verdicts.
- Not that closing a boundary removes contact. It substitutes a habitat (Fig 11 :66).
- Not that a chain model can represent a reservoir. A chain cannot close by construction (Fig 12 :121).
- Not `grounded`. No outbreak measurement was taken here.
- Not a claim that the framework named any of the world mechanisms. The mesoscale and household results are imports with provenance, not derived primitives (Fig 6 :23).

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

An instrument, not another argument. Fig 6 :32: "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something." The worse instruments already returning something are the household within/between transmission decomposition and the mesoscale metapopulation fit in `10_source_ledger.csv`.

Concretely:

- **Crossing-type test:** does a boundary that passes signals but no live cases ever start a new chain? If yes, the head-start/recording distinction fails (refuter 5).
- **Reservoir test:** with bridge rate fixed, does dense internal contact sustain prevalence below the pairwise threshold? Refuter 2.
- **Regime test:** does the simple/complex distinction predict the sign across a pre-registered set of datasets? Refuter 3.
- **Detection precondition:** if within- and between-group tie probabilities are too close, community labels cannot be recovered better than chance. An undetectable boundary cannot have a measured crossover.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a modularity threshold at which community boundaries flip from bad to good for contagion. It gives the shape any answer must have. A boundary is a filter with a direction, not a wall: what matters is whether a live case crosses, not whether a signal crosses. When bridges pass few live cases and a local susceptible/infectious difference is spent before a bridge is used, the boundary reduces spread by denying contact with a different closure. When bridges pass live cases, boundary members act as catalysts and within-group reinfection as a cycle, so the same cut protects a reservoir and re-seeds it later. Which regime you are in also depends on the order of contagion: one exposure and several exposures are different conditions, so this is a union of cases, not one curve. What is offered is the regime table, five refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would supply the missing number. The crossover stays open until the ground settles it.
