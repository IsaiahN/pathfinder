**Subject:** Check one output on recessions before you decide whether the framing is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output about the causes of recessions that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "do recessions occur because wages and prices adjust too slowly, or because the economy is prone to deeper instability?" does not pass the framework's first test for a single question. The two mechanisms keep working well on different subsets of episodes, and the disagreement does not shrink with more effort. That is the signature of several well-formed questions bundled into one, so the framework says split it before searching it. Fig 9 says *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* It then names the overlap: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Coordination failure and staggering appear in both stories, so this is a union compound, not a clean A-or-B choice.
>
> The shape that follows is a checkable partition, not a verdict. For each episode and each sector slot, measure (1) the adjustment speed of the relevant price or wage, (2) the amplification coming from balance sheets, credit and incomplete markets, and (3) the round-trip gap between the micro price-setting statistic and the observed aggregate response. Keep the two loops separate and never average them into a single "rigidity" or "instability" score.
>
> **What would refute this shape claim, written before sending:**
> 1. One mechanism alone, adjustment speed OR internal instability, predicts the cross-episode pattern with a residual that trends to zero and no interaction term.
> 2. The sector-level residual differences vanish once a single aggregate rigidity index is used in their place.
> 3. A micro price-change statistic predicts the aggregate output response with a measured round-trip gap of zero across regimes.
>
> If any one holds, the unbundling is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** I am not claiming that recessions are "just" sticky prices, or "just" financial instability. I am not claiming the framework names which mechanism dominates in any actual episode. No data was opened for this derivation, so its status is `specified` and `candidate`, never `grounded`. Convergence of opinion among economists would not move it: Fig 2 says *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.

> - The eight-step loop and mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
