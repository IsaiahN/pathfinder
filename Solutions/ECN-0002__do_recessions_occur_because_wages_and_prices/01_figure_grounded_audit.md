## 0. The question under audit, restated as an effect

> Do recessions occur because wages and prices adjust too slowly, or because the economy is prone to deeper instability?

Stated in effect terms: we observe a persistent shortfall of output and employment relative to trend. We must decide whether the mechanism that keeps the shortfall alive is the **speed of a nominal adjustment channel** (option A) or the **gain of an amplifying structure inside the economy** (option B). Option A predicts that faster price and wage response removes the residual. Option B predicts that faster response need not remove it and can increase it, because the feedback is in quantities, balance sheets and credit rather than in the price quote.

**Binding the sought-for shape as an ordinary term before the run** (formula step "Direction"): the answer will be called satisfactory only if it (i) splits the question into per-episode and per-slot conditions rather than choosing A or B globally, (ii) names a measurable adjustment speed and a measurable amplification for each slot, and (iii) measures the round-trip gap between the micro price-setting statistic and the aggregate output response. This prediction was written before the figure census below. It is refutable in section 6.

## 1. The residual by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity: two conditions, not one.** A recession requires a disturbance that moves the economy off full employment and something that makes the deviation persist. Option B mainly addresses the first and its propagation; option A mainly addresses the second. Two conditions combined are a union compound until shown otherwise.
- **Symmetry: not symmetric.** Adjustment speed is a **rate** over a channel (how quickly a signal decays). Instability is a **structure** (feedback, network, directedness, sign of loop). A rate and a structure are different kinds of quantity. Averaging them into one "cause of recessions" score is a type error, not a synthesis.
- **Scale: micro to macro.** Option A lives at the level of individual price setters and wage bargainers; option B lives at the level of aggregate demand, credit and balance sheets. The bridge between these levels is a lossy transform, so a number read at one level cannot be copied to the other without measuring the gap.

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
Fig 1 :23: *"The ground enters as the only metric."*

The first consequence is already decided: a single global verdict on "recessions" throws away the signal that would say where to look. Sector and episode slots are not an optional refinement here, they are the unit of measurement. The empirical result of Ho (2025), discussed in F3, is a direct instance: price stickiness transmits monetary shocks while wage stickiness does not, a difference an aggregated rigidity index would erase.

## 5. Findings

### F1 — The A-or-B question fails the "is there a fact here?" test (decisive for the shape)

**Mode: `general` (structural).**
**Status: triangulated** (Fig 9 :4-5 and Fig 1 :19/:22 are two figure lines with different offices and low overlap; the Library of Babel essay corroborates from a different domain but shares an author with the figures, so it is a second voice, not an independent instrument).

Fig 9 :4-5: *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*
Fig 9 :5 is the instruction, and Fig 9 :4 names its trigger: disagreements that do not shrink with effort and where each rule works on a different subset. That is exactly the observed state of the macro debate. Romer (Econlib) reports that the empirical record favours spending shocks and that monetary policy has played a crucial role; Minsky (1992) argues that the economy generates cycles from internal financial dynamics without exogenous shocks; Nakamura and Steinsson (2013) show sluggish price adjustment is a leading explanation for large demand-shock effects. Each rule keeps working on its own subset and the dispute does not shrink. By Fig 9 the remedy is a split, not a search.

The Library of Babel essay (2026-10-02) says the same in other words: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."*

**Refuter:** demonstrate that one mechanism alone predicts the cross-episode and cross-sector pattern of output and price response with a residual that trends to zero and no interaction term. If it does, there was one fact here and the split was unnecessary.

### F2 — The compound is a union, not a partition

**Mode: `specified`.**
**Status: triangulated** (Fig 9 :54-57 gives the union rule; Fig 8 :45 gives the surplus rule; the world sources supply the concrete overlap).

Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
Fig 9 :56: *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*
Fig 8 :45: *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."*

The overlap is concrete, not assumed. Greenwald and Stiglitz (1993) state that flexibility can amplify shocks while also regarding rigidity as a symptom of deeper failures. Tobin (1993) argues that markets not clearing instantaneously, rather than nominal rigidity, is the required Keynesian premise. OpenStax (2022) uses the coordination argument to explain sticky wages, and coordination failure is also a canonical amplification mechanism. So the two conditions share atoms and the union has an intersection. A policy that only speeds price adjustment clears the single-condition region and leaves the intersection standing, exactly as Fig 9 :56 predicts.

**Refuter:** show that the two conditions are disjoint in the data, with no episode and no sector in which slow adjustment and amplification both bind. If the intersection is empty, the compound is a partition and the union rule does not apply.

### F3 — The residual must be measured per slot, never aggregated

**Mode: `specified`.**
**Status: triangulated** (Fig 1 figure rule plus the independent empirical result of Ho 2025, which is not a figure line and comes from a different evidence pool).

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

Ho (Economics Letters, 2025) finds that relative to sectors without nominal stickiness, output in the sticky-price sector falls by about two percent at the trough after a contractionary monetary shock, while the effects for the sticky-wage sector are smaller and statistically insignificant. A single "wages and prices are sticky" variable would have hidden this difference. The slot here is the price setting sector versus the wage setting sector, and the episode is a monetary contraction.

**Refuter:** construct one aggregate rigidity index using published price and wage statistics and show that it predicts the cross-sector output response at least as well as the separate per-sector measures, with no loss of residual signal. If it does, Fig 1's per-slot requirement is not load-bearing for this question.

**Mode: `general` (structural).**
**Status: triangulated** (Fig 2 :29 and Fig 2 :35/:40 are distinct figure clauses; Seat Map :163 corroborates from the checking arrangement).

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*

Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*

The anchor here is nameable: published price-change micro data, wage data, output and employment series, credit spreads, and the recession counter. None of these changes its answer when a school changes its mind. A census of economists' beliefs is not the anchor; it is a frame that votes. ### F5 — The micro-to-macro bridge is lossy and carries a nonzero R_T

**Mode: `specified`.**
**Status: triangulated** (Fig 4 figure rule plus Nakamura and Steinsson 2013, an independent empirical review).

Fig 4 :27: *"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."*
Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

The Symbols table defines `R_T` as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, the gap between what was sent up and what came back down. Here `x` is the micro price-setting process, `T_A` is the aggregate statistic (frequency and size of price changes), and `T_E` is the prediction of the aggregate price level's response to a shock. Nakamura and Steinsson warn that *"Simple statistics such as the frequency of price change may be misleading guides to the flexibility of the aggregate price level in a setting where temporary sales, product-churning, cross-sectional heterogeneity, and large idiosyncratic price movements play an important role."* The gap is therefore expected to be nonzero and must be measured rather than assumed. Treating "micro frequency" as "aggregate flexibility" is the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.

**Refuter:** exhibit a micro statistic that predicts the aggregate output response to a demand shock with a measured round-trip gap statistically indistinguishable from zero across at least two distinct regimes. That would collapse the membrane for this quantity.

### F6 — "Deeper instability" is a structure, not a faster or slower rate

**Mode: `specified`.**
**Status: triangulated** (Fig 12 :121 and Fig 13 :24 are separate figure lines with different offices; Minsky 1992 and Scheffer et al. 2009 corroborate from economics and from ecology respectively).

Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*
Fig 13 :24: *"that entities relate, and influence travels"*

The "deeper instability" option is not a complaint that adjustment is slow. It is a claim about the sign of a feedback loop: output falls, which tightens balance sheets, which cuts demand further, which lowers output. That is a cycle with a positive feedback sign, and a chain cannot close by construction. Minsky (1992) states the same structure in financial terms: *"the economy has financing regimes under which it is stable, and financing regimes in which it is unstable."* Scheffer et al. (Nature, 2009) find the same generic structure across ecosystems, financial markets and climate: *"Complex dynamical systems, ranging from ecosystems to financial markets and the climate, can have tipping points at which a sudden shift to a contrasting dynamical regime may occur."* The low-overlap analogue corroborates the structural reading; it does not decide the economics.

**Re-census note.** The first reading attempted to use Fig 13's gradient here and was strained (see section 4). The correct figure lines are Fig 12 :121 for the closing loop and Fig 13 :24 for influence travelling through a network. No verdict was flipped; the quantity was reassigned.

**Refuter:** show that the amplification can be written entirely as a shorter adjustment lag with no feedback term, and that it predicts the observed persistence and the cross-episode variation as well as the loop model. If so, the two options are one rate after all and the structural distinction collapses.

### F7 — If "flexible prices restore employment" is false, a refusal is owed, not a hybrid explanation

**Mode: `specified`.**
**Status: single-source for the figure mechanism** (Fig 5 :53 and :55); the world side (Tobin 1993, Greenwald and Stiglitz 1993) supplies the content of the refusal but is not a figure line.

Fig 5 :53: *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"*
Fig 5 :55: *"What it removes is not a term but a term's standing to be composed over."*

A common response to the debate is to mint a hybrid: "recessions happen because prices are somewhat sticky and the economy is somewhat unstable." Under Fig 5 that is the wrong move if the real finding is that a held regularity is false. The held regularity is the classical claim that falling wages and prices restore full employment. Greenwald and Stiglitz say directly that they *"would regard price and wage rigidity more as a symptom of underlying financial and labor market failures and not as a fundamental cause of business cycles."* That is a refusal of the term's standing as a fundamental cause, not a new hybrid cause. Nothing is deleted from the record; the reach falls because the term can no longer be composed over.

**Open item, named not hidden.** Fig 5 :56 notes that a refusal requires an operator for negation, which the seven bonds do not contain. The Operators table lists ¬ as the eighth operator, unary, with its price open. This derivation uses ¬ as the figure's amendment licenses; it does not claim the price is settled.

**Refuter:** show that the classical restoration claim survives in the relevant episodes under a correctly specified test, in which case the refusal is unwarranted and the hybrid is the better bargain.

## 6. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. One mechanism alone, adjustment speed OR internal instability, predicts the cross-episode and cross-sector pattern of output and price response with a residual that trends to zero and no interaction term. (Breaks F1 and F2.)
2. The per-slot residual differences vanish when a single aggregate rigidity index replaces the separate price and wage measures. (Breaks F3.)
3. A micro price-change statistic predicts the aggregate output response with a measured round-trip gap statistically indistinguishable from zero across two distinct regimes. (Breaks F5.)
4. The amplification is fully expressible as a shorter adjustment lag with no feedback term and equal predictive power. (Breaks F6.)

If any fires, the unbundling is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of the economic verdict may be labelled `grounded`.

## 7. Recommendation

1. Stop asking the either/or as one question. Present the split first and the mechanisms second (F1, F2).
2. Measure per slot and per episode; refuse to average a price slot and a wage slot into one score (F3).
3. Measure the round-trip gap explicitly before claiming that micro price flexibility explains aggregate output stability (F5).
4. State the amplification as a signed loop, not as an adjustment speed, and keep the two quantities in different columns (F6).
5. Treat the classical restoration regularity with Fig 5's refusal test before minting a hybrid (F7).
6. Use filters only to decide where to look; use the data to decide what is true (F8).
7. Send the generator, not the recording. Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*

### Three questions when nothing closes R, kept distinct
