### Q1 — Are they isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

D2 as written uses {balance sheet, credit flow, borrower type, feedback sign, disturbance, incomplete markets}. They share almost no atoms. By Fig 12 :117 they are **not isomers**; they are two different substances built from different atoms.
- Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* The shared substructure is the **disturbance plus a propagation channel**. Both stories have a shock and a channel that carries it; what differs is the channel. So at this level they are members of the same functional group, with different qualifiers (nominal versus financial), not isomers.

That is the claim worth testing, and it is not established by the figure alone.

### Q2 — What order do the measurements take?

- Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*
- Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*
- The dependency order for ECN-0002 is: **perception** (the recession is dated by an independent counter, D3); **vocabulary** (there are terms for adjustment speed and for feedback sign, so the search can reach them); **objective** (what would count as progress is a shrinking per-slot residual, not a win for a school); **planning** (measure speed and sign separately in each episode); **learn and carry** (name which condition settled which episode, verify against the data, carry the method to the next recession).

### Q3 — Does the union have its own loop?

- Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
- The intersection of D1 and D2 is a compound, not a harder case of D1 or of D2. It needs its own loop with its own ground. Concretely: when prices are sticky *and* balance sheets are fragile, falling prices raise real debt burdens, which cuts demand further, which makes the price fall larger. That is a new mechanism created by the overlap, not the sum of two mechanisms.
- Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* The compound is a closing loop; either single condition is only a chain segment.
- **Falsifier:** show that in every overlapping episode the joint effect is exactly additive in the two separately measured effects. If it is, the intersection has no new loop and Fig 9 :57 is doing no work here.

### Q4 — Analogy check: critical slowing down in ecology

This is an import, so it gets a source gate and a shadow test, per Fig 8.

- **Source gate.** Marten Scheffer et al., *"Early-warning signals for critical transitions,"* Nature 461, 53 to 59, 2009: *"Complex dynamical systems, ranging from ecosystems to financial markets and the climate, can have tipping points at which a sudden shift to a contrasting dynamical regime may occur."* The framework paper explicitly includes financial markets, so this is a near neighbour, not a distant field. S. Kéfi et al., *"Early warning signals also precede non-catastrophic transitions,"* Oikos 122(5), 2013, 641 to 648: *"a decrease in recovery rate cannot be considered as specific to approaching catastrophic shifts."*
- **Shadow test.** The pair explains a gap in the economics framing. A long recovery from a recession looks like slow adjustment, but the ecology result says slow recovery does not diagnose an approaching instability. So the observable "slow" cannot be used as a proxy for either condition; the two must be measured separately. That is exactly F5 plus F6 in the audit.
- **What the analogue does not do.** It does not decide which mechanism operates in a given economy. Analogies supply structure, not verdicts. Fig 8 :53: *"Residual first, frame second."* The residual was described before this frame was named.
- **Falsifier for the analogue:** produce a case in which slow recovery is shown to be a sufficient statistic for an approaching critical transition across systems, so that no separate measurement of feedback sign is needed. That would break the analogy's usefulness here, though not the figure verdict.

## CONCLUSION

At the atom sets the two explanations actually use, they are **not isomers**: they are different functional groups that share a disturbance-plus-channel substructure. The stronger isomer claim requires rewriting both over the full shared substrate and is a real, falsifiable structural hypothesis, not a result of this run. The dependency order is perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, carry, with the unmeasured middle link being the micro-to-macro gap. The intersection of the two conditions is a compound with its own loop and its own ground, and the ecology analogue corroborates the need to keep slow recovery and feedback sign as separate observables. All claims here are `specified`. The one thing not settled is which condition binds in any actual recession; that is `gap` at the ground.
