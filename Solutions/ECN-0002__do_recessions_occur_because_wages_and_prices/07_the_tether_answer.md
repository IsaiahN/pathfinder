# The Tether's answer on recessions

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the split. Not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §8), not a ground reading and not a test of the economic claim.

## 1. The residual it answers

A recession is a persistent shortfall of output and employment relative to trend, dated by a counter that does not update on either theory. The question asks whether the persistence is caused by too-slow adjustment of wages and prices or by an economy prone to deeper instability. Fig 9 :4-5 says *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* The two candidates each work well on a different subset of episodes, and the disagreement does not shrink with more effort. So the residual is a union compound, not one hard question (Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*).

## 2. The shape

1. **Split before you search.** The either/or is refused. The two mechanisms are two conditions that overlap (coordination failure and staggering appear in both), so this is a union with an intersection, not a partition. A lever that clears single-condition episodes leaves the intersection standing (Fig 9 :56).
2. **Measure per slot and per episode.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A price slot and a wage slot are not one score. Ho (2025) finds that price stickiness, not wage stickiness, transmits monetary shocks, a difference an aggregate index would hide.
3. **Keep the two quantities in different columns.** Adjustment speed is a rate over a channel; instability is the sign of a feedback loop. Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* A faster price response is not the same operation as reversing a loop.
4. **Measure the round-trip gap.** Fig 4 :27: *"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."* Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* A micro price-change frequency is not an aggregate price flexibility until the gap is measured.
5. **Refuse a held regularity where it fails.** The classical claim that falling wages and prices restore full employment is not a hybrid cause to be blended with instability. Fig 5 :53: *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so."* Greenwald and Stiglitz (1993) go further and call rigidity a symptom of deeper failures. What is owed is a refusal of its standing as a fundamental cause, not a new composite mechanism.
6. **The anchor is data, not agreement.** Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* School, prestige and headcount are filters for where to look, never witnesses for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "sticky prices are the cause" and not "instability is the cause." Both are `specified` bindings from the economics frame, not figure verdicts.
- Not that the framework can name which mechanism dominates in the 1981 recession or in 2008. No figure line governs empirical magnitudes. That part is `gap` in this run.
- Not that a single aggregate "rigidity" or "instability" index is adequate. Fig 1 forbids the aggregation.
- Not that the classical restoration claim is true or false in all regimes. The refusal in point 5 is conditional on the data and is itself refutable.
- Not `grounded`. No dataset was opened for this derivation.
- Not a ToE of macroeconomics. Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* says an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"* and that *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* Here the equivalent questions are:

- **Speed Test.** In a given episode and sector, what is the measured hazard rate of price and wage changes after the shock? This is the adjustment-speed slot. The existing micro price datasets are the worse instrument already returning something (Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."*).
- **Sign Test.** Does output feed back into credit and demand in a way that closes the loop, and what is the sign? Minsky's hedge, speculative and Ponzi regimes supply the vocabulary; the data must supply the sign.
- **Round-Trip Test.** Feed the measured micro adjustment statistic up to an aggregate prediction and back down to the observed output response; measure `R_T = gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))` rather than assuming it is zero.
- **Intersection Test.** In episodes where both conditions bind, is the joint effect exactly additive in the two separately measured effects? If not, the intersection has its own loop and its own ground (Fig 9 :57).
- **Standing falsifiers from section 6 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`:** one mechanism alone with a residual trending to zero; per-slot differences vanishing under an aggregate index; zero round-trip gap across regimes; amplification expressible as a shorter lag with equal power.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** *They're Scanning Books* says *"A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six."* The vocabulary of "rigidity" and "instability" is likewise a human guess at the axes of this problem, and declaring it makes the choice visible rather than neutral. The answer stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and the lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not choose between sticky prices and deeper instability, because the question fails its first test for a single question: the two mechanisms keep working on different subsets of episodes and the disagreement does not shrink. Fig 9 says split it rather than search, and the split is a union with an overlap, not a clean either/or. What the framework returns is a shape. Date the recession on an independent counter; measure the price and wage adjustment speed per sector slot and never average them; measure the sign of the credit and demand feedback separately; and measure the round-trip gap between the micro price statistic and the aggregate output response instead of assuming they are the same thing. Where the two conditions overlap, treat the intersection as its own compound with its own ground. A single observable such as slow recovery cannot name the source, because slow recovery is not diagnostic of an approaching tipping point. The framework refuses to say which mechanism dominated in any actual recession. That is `gap` here, and it is owed to measurement, not to agreement between schools.
