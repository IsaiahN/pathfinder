## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the claim that recessions occur either because wages and prices adjust too slowly (option A) or because the economy is prone to deeper instability (option B). What each option leads to: option A makes the cure faster nominal adjustment and treats persistence as a channel-speed problem; option B makes the cure structural and treats faster adjustment as possibly destabilising. A third possibility, kept open before the run, is that the question is a bundle of two conditions and must be split rather than answered. That possibility was bound as an ordinary term before the census, per formula step "Direction".

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** A recession: a persistent shortfall of output and employment relative to an independently dated trend. Arity: two conditions, a disturbance and a persistence mechanism. Symmetry: not symmetric, because adjustment speed is a rate while instability is a feedback structure with a sign. Scale: micro price setting versus aggregate quantities, so a lossy transform sits between them. Fig 9 names this a union compound, not one hard question.
- **Domain 1, nominal adjustment.** Actors: firms, workers, unions, price setters. Currency: nominal quotes and adjustment speeds, money. Anchor: published micro price and wage data. Question type: one condition of a union.
- **Domain 2, amplification and instability.** Actors: banks, borrowers, households, firms, shocks. Currency: credit, balance sheets, aggregate demand, real shocks. Anchor: output and employment series, credit spreads, recession dates. Question type: the other condition of the union, with concrete overlap through coordination failure and staggering.
- **Domain 3, observation and dating.** Actors: statistical agencies and the business cycle dating committee. Currency: GDP, unemployment, price indices. Anchor: the published series and the peak and trough counter, which do not update on either frame. This is the ground under both other domains.
- **Domain 4, verification in this run.** Actors: expert readers and this simulated seat. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this run all seats draw on one evidence pool, so independence is zero and agreement is internal consistency only.
- **Order by dependency.** Date the recession first, then measure the adjustment speed, then measure the feedback sign, then compare at the intersection. Fig 3 :19 says "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

Strain test applied: an initial attempt to price adjustment speed with Fig 13's gradient strained the line, because gradient is a directed structure and a speed is a rate. Re-census assigned the rate to Fig 4 and the directed amplification to Fig 12 :121 and Fig 13 :24. No verdict was flipped; the quantity was reassigned. ## WORLD (secondary sources read)

World sources corroborate; they never overrule the figures. Overlap with each other is declared.

- **Macroeconomics and economic history.** Christina D. Romer, "Business Cycles," Concise Encyclopedia of Economics, Econlib, original 1993, online 2026. Quote: "Business cycles do occur, however, because disturbances to the economy of one sort or another push the economy above or below full employment." Prediction: disturbances are necessary; recessions are not explained by adjustment speed alone. Corroborates the disturbance condition and the union reading. Overlap: independent of the framework author.
- **Price rigidity, empirical micro.** Emi Nakamura and Jón Steinsson, "Price Rigidity: Microeconomic Evidence and Macroeconomic Implications," NBER Working Paper 18705, 2013, also Annual Review of Economics 5(1), 133 to 163. Quote: "Sluggish price adjustment is a leading explanation for large effects of demand shocks on output and, in particular, the effects of monetary policy on output." Prediction: the adjustment condition is real and measurable. Also warns that "Simple statistics such as the frequency of price change may be misleading guides to the flexibility of the aggregate price level." Corroborates Domain 1 and the nonzero R_T.
- **Empirical macro, price versus wage.** Cheuk Yin Ho, "Do sticky prices or sticky wages matter for monetary non-neutrality?," Economics Letters, 2025. Quote: "Price stickiness, but not wage stickiness, empirically transmits monetary shocks on output." Prediction: a per-slot difference that an aggregate index would erase. Corroborates Fig 1.
- **Heterodox macro, financial instability.** Hyman P. Minsky, "The Financial Instability Hypothesis," Levy Economics Institute Working Paper 74, 1992. Quote: "the economy has financing regimes under which it is stable, and financing regimes in which it is unstable." Prediction: instability is structural and can arise without exogenous shocks. Corroborates Domain 2 and Fig 12 :121.
- **Macro theory, information imperfections.** Bruce Greenwald and Joseph E. Stiglitz, "New and Old Keynesians," Journal of Economic Perspectives 7(1), 1993, 23 to 44. Quote: "even if wages and prices were perfectly flexible, output and employment would be highly volatile. It sees the economy as amplifying the shocks that it experiences, and making their effects persist." Also: they "would regard price and wage rigidity more as a symptom of underlying financial and labor market failures and not as a fundamental cause of business cycles." Prediction: the rigidity-as-fundamental-cause term should be refused. Corroborates Fig 5 :53.
- **Macroeconomic thought.** James Tobin, "Price Flexibility and Output Stability: An Old Keynesian View," Journal of Economic Perspectives 7(1), 1993, 45 to 65. Quote: "Keynesian macroeconomics neither asserts nor requires nominal wage and/or price rigidity. It does assert and require that markets not be instantaneously and continuously cleared by prices." Prediction: the real contrast is instant clearing versus not, not fast versus slow adjustment. Corroborates the re-census in F6.
- **Economics education.** OpenStax, Principles of Macroeconomics 3e, section 12.2, 2022. Quote: "the macroeconomy may adjust only slowly to shifts in aggregate demand because of sticky wages and prices, which are wages and prices that do not respond to decreases or increases in demand." Prediction: the textbook statement of option A, including the coordination argument that also appears in option B. Corroborates the union overlap.
- **Complex systems and ecology, low overlap.** Marten Scheffer et al., "Early-warning signals for critical transitions," Nature 461, 2009, 53 to 59. Quote: "Complex dynamical systems, ranging from ecosystems to financial markets and the climate, can have tipping points at which a sudden shift to a contrasting dynamical regime may occur." Prediction: feedback instability is a generic structural possibility. Corroborates Domain 2 by analogue. Overlap: the paper explicitly includes financial markets, so this is a near neighbour rather than a distant witness.
- **Ecology, low overlap.** S. Kéfi et al., "Early warning signals also precede non-catastrophic transitions," Oikos 122(5), 2013, 641 to 648. Quote: "a decrease in recovery rate cannot be considered as specific to approaching catastrophic shifts." Prediction: slow recovery does not diagnose a tipping point, so the two observables must be measured separately. Corroborates the split and Fig 4 plus Fig 12. This is the least overlapping witness in the set.

One apparent conflict was encountered: the textbook treats rigidity as a fundamental cause, while Greenwald and Stiglitz treat it as a symptom and Tobin denies that rigidity is required at all. Per the WATSON rule, the conflict was sent back to the census, not used to flip a verdict. The re-census produced the union reading: both are real conditions that overlap. Agreement among the economics sources is partly one evidence pool, because they cite and respond to each other, so it counts as one witness with several names. The two ecology sources are lower overlap but are analogues, not decisions about the target domain.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The either/or is refused as a union compound. The figure-decided shape is: split before search; measure the adjustment speed per episode and per sector slot, never aggregated; measure the feedback sign separately; measure the round-trip gap between micro price statistics and aggregate response; treat the intersection as its own compound with its own ground; and refuse the restoration regularity where the data falsifies it. The specific choice between A and B in any actual recession is gap in this run and is owed to measurement. Everything here is specified, candidate, not grounded.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode, refuters and provenance before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a recording or a school-aligned list instead of a generator, which Fig 2 prices elsewhere as buying independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong. That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter and using opt-in institutional venues only.
- **undoable:** yes. The split shape is removable without touching the world sources, and a falsifier in section 6 of the audit withdraws it. No economic primitive has to be abandoned. A claim already sent can be superseded by a corrected packet, and the retraction log keeps the earlier reading visible.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A speed instrument and a sign instrument that predict the cross-episode pattern with one mechanism and a residual trending to zero would break the split; an aggregate rigidity index that preserves the per-slot signal would break Fig 1; a micro statistic with a measured round-trip gap of zero across two regimes would break Fig 4; a demonstration that amplification is just a shorter lag with equal power would break Fig 12 :121; and a demonstration that slow recovery is sufficient to diagnose a tipping point would weaken the low-overlap corroboration. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as candidate.
