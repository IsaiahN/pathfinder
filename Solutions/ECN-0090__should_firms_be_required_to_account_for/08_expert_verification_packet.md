# Expert verification packet — the Tether on corporate harm accounting

**What this is:** a claim in a form a corporate accountability researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` — not `grounded`.** No measurement of a firm's harm was taken. This is a shape and a route, not a policy verdict.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

An environmental or social harm that is not reflected in a market price is a dropped channel reading, not an absent fact. The price instrument is legitimate as an anchor for an allocation question but can be wrong on the harm axis, because an anchor is legitimate only when the question is constitutively about it. Any answer, mandatory or voluntary, must satisfy four separate conditions: an external ground for the harm, a per-slot enumeration of the contact cascade, a convention that makes the harm statable and can be violated, and two readings held apart rather than summed. A disclosure mandate is a convention, not a ground; an industry voluntary standard is a room, not a verdict. Adding a harm term to a firm's library is an import that needs provenance and must pay the acceptance bargain. The framework returns this shape with written falsifiers; it does not name a standard, threshold or jurisdiction, and it does not return a yes or no.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 2 and Figure 10

- **Statement:** the market is not the anchor for the harm question. It is an instrument that can be wrong on an axis it does not measure, and the harm is a channel failure rather than ground decay.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :36 "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Fig 2 :37-38 "It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure." Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does". Fig 10 :26 "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings".
- **Refuter:** a prospective measurement showing the market price already tracks the physical harm on the relevant axis while the reporting frame is held fixed, so price is a valid anchor for that harm. Or an aggregate statistic that identifies per slot which exposure will breach a threshold next with no live signal lost.

### C2 — world-facing; checkable by a corporate accountability researcher

- **Statement:** voluntary and mandatory accounts are isomers over the same atom set, so a reporting template that lists only ingredients cannot distinguish them; they share a functional group but differ in the bond. Both poles have characteristic failure modes.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :124 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." Fig 12 :77 "A produces B; B did not exist before" for the production bond under an external rule.
- **What to check:** is the characterization faithful? Gatti, Seele and Rademacher, 2019, find that a wholly voluntary CSR approach "facilitates the diffusion of greenwashing" while an exclusively mandatory approach "may favor the establishment of grey zones where companies look for ways around the rules", and propose a combination. The AAAJ review of CSR reporting regulations, 2022, reports that reporting "continues to be ceremonial rather than substantive after the regulations". Do these support the two failure poles, or is one pole actually harmless?
- **Refuter:** a regime that is demonstrably neither (third failure class), or evidence that one pole has no characteristic failure at all. Also: a voluntary and a mandatory regime written over the same atoms with the same bond, which would collapse the isomer claim into two spellings.

### C3 — framework-internal derivation with a stated strain

- **Statement:** the harm term must be enumerated through the contact cascade per slot, and the two readings must be held apart. A mandate makes the bug statable but cannot ground it.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :52 "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three". Fig 10 :19 "→ install what can be violated". Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one". Fig 12 :130 "Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."
- **Strain, named not hidden:** Fig 12's two currencies are bits and actions. Mapping them to money and physical harm is a cross-level analogue under Fig 4 :20, not a direct figure line about externalities. C3's summing claim is therefore the weakest of the three and is marked single-source.
- **Refuter:** a domain in which all material harm is first-order and the cascade contribution measures zero; a mandated metric that cannot be violated or is set entirely by the reporting firm; or a single monetized aggregate that prospectively predicts both the firm's decision and the physical trajectory on both axes.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not yes and not no on mandatory accounting. The framework returns four conditions, not a policy verdict (Fig 9 :32, "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself").
- Not that markets never work. They are the anchor for questions about allocation, an instrument on the harm axis.
- Not that voluntary regimes are worthless or mandatory regimes sufficient. Each fails a different condition.
- Not that a corporate total, a social cost number or a single money figure is an account of the residual.
- Not that a particular standard, carbon price, threshold or jurisdiction fills a slot. Those are bindings from world frames with no source gate here.
- Not `grounded`. No harm measurement pays for these bindings in this packet.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. Does price track physical harm per named slot with the reporting frame fixed?
2. Does the harm register at zero beyond direct contact in a given domain?
3. Can the mandated metric be violated, and does the checker use evidence outside the firm's pool?
4. Does a single monetized aggregate predict both trajectories with no residual on either axis?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :36-38 and Fig 10 :24, :16-19, :47. Five short passages. Decide whether "instrument, not anchor" and "convention, not ground" are faithful readings.
2. Read Fig 12 :117, :124. Decide whether the voluntary/mandatory isomer claim is a real structural claim or a metaphor. Name the bond you would use instead if it is wrong.
3. Check C2 against your own field: is there a third failure class beyond the voluntary grey zone and the mandatory workaround? One counterexample is enough.
4. Name one instrument that would distinguish a clean production path from a damaging one at the same price. If none exists, say why.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement, and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
