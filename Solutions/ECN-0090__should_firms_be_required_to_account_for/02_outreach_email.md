# Outreach draft — check the shape before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. No personal emails are used.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are stated cleanly and the four conditions are separated. The offer collapses on contact if the first stage carries a policy verdict instead of a residual.

---

**Subject:** One checkable shape about corporate harm accounting, before any theory

> Hello,
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, I am not asking you to agree with it, and I am not asking you to trust it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question in effect terms.** A firm's price signal carries what the market will pay. It does not carry all the harm the firm's activity causes. Call the gap between what the price predicts and what actually happens `R = |Γ(b,a) − o′|`, measured per exposed party, never as a corporate total (Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."). The claim about "harms not reflected in market prices" is a claim about the instrument's coverage, not about the harm's reality.
>
> **The shape claim (`specified`, not `grounded`).** Any answer, mandatory or voluntary, must satisfy four separate conditions:
> 1. an external ground for the harm that does not move when the firm reports it (Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it.");
> 2. a per-slot enumeration of the contact cascade, because the material harm is often three steps out (Fig 11 :52, the DDT and eagles case from Carson, 1962);
> 3. a convention that makes the harm statable and can actually be violated (Fig 10 :19: "→ install what can be violated"), checked from a frame that does not draw on the firm's own data;
> 4. the money reading and the physical reading held apart rather than summed (Fig 12 :130: "Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it.").
>
> The reason the market cannot settle this on its own is Fig 2 :38: "do not measure." Fig 2 :37 says an instrument "can be wrong on axes they" do not measure. The price is the right anchor for an allocation question; it is an instrument, potentially wrong, on the harm axis.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** That a yes or a no follows from the figures. That a particular standard, tax rate or jurisdiction is the answer. That a corporate total impact score is an account of the residual (Fig 10 :26 names "pooled readings" as a way contact is lost). That industry agreement is evidence: Fig 7 :13 says "agreement compounds, evidence does not." Which standard fills the slot is a declared gap, not a name I can supply from the framework.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim. I wrote these before sending this.**
> 1. A prospective measurement showing the market price already tracks the physical harm on the relevant axis while the reporting frame is held fixed.
> 2. An aggregate statistic that identifies per slot which exposure will breach a threshold next, with no live signal lost.
> 3. A domain in which all material harm is first-order and the cascade contribution measures zero.
> 4. A mandated metric that cannot be violated, or whose value is set entirely by the reporting firm.
> 5. A single monetized aggregate that predicts both the decision and the physical trajectory on both axes.
>
> If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework file,Pathfinder/FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md)
> - Figures README, canonical usage rule (local framework file, Pathfinder/FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md)
> - Journal of Business Ethics submission guidelines, for the corporate accountability research venue: https://link.springer.com/journal/10551/submission-guidelines
>
> **If you want to use the method:** the eight-step loop is in the framework's `THE_FORMULA.md`. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> With respect,

## Why this order

| move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Residual and refuter first, theory second | Fig 10 :52 — "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Frames range against the ground separately (Fig 2 :45). |
| Do not ask for agreement or trust | Fig 2 :35 — "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." A verifier who has absorbed the interiors cannot verify (Fig 10 :42). |
| Pre-registered refuter | A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device. |
| Mode stated on the substantive claim | `THE_FORMULA.md`: "Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which." |
| Four conditions kept separate | Fig 9 :57 — a case in two conditions "is not a harder case of either"; a single lever leaves intersections standing. |
| No yes or no policy verdict | Fig 9 :32 — "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." |
| No claim that a standard fills the slot | Figs README: "a shape, not a name." |
| No ranking of recipients by receptiveness | Fig 2 :40 — "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." |
| Links only after stage 1 | Fig 4 :22 — "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." A recording sent before a residual is checked is a description of one occasion. |
| Offer the formula as a method, not a belief | Fig 4 :22; ask each user to state their own ground, refuter and mode. |
