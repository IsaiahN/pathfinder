# Are voluntary and mandatory accounts isomers? Order and analogues for ECN-0090

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

`01` leaves four conditions unbundled (C1 external ground, C2 cascade enumeration, C3 violable convention, C4 two readings held apart). Two supporting questions are owed: (1) are a voluntary regime and a mandatory regime different substances or two spellings of one? (2) in what order must the four conditions be satisfied? A third question is whether the ecology analogue is a real isomorphism or only a rhyme.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Voluntary versus mandatory: isomers or spellings?

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** The test is symmetric: under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term (Fig 12 :92).

Write the two regimes over the same atom set `{firm Γ, harm term, metric, external ground, report, checker}`:

- Voluntary: a firm **adopts** the harm term and invites a checker. The bond between firm and report is a conjunction of internal decisions.
- Mandatory: an external seat **requires** the harm term and a checker verifies. The bond between firm and report is a production under an external rule (`⇒`: "A produces B; B did not exist before", Fig 12 :77).

The atoms are the same. The bond is not. Under Fig 12 :96, *"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."* So they are **isomers**, and a reporting template that lists only ingredients cannot distinguish them.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a voluntary regime and a mandatory regime can be written over the same atoms with the same bond (both merely `+`), the isomer claim fails and they are two spellings of one term. That would also predict identical behaviour, which the greenwashing literature disputes (Gatti et al. 2019: voluntary CSR "facilitates the diffusion of greenwashing"; exclusive mandatory CSR creates other grey zones). The literature is corroboration only, not the figure verdict.

### Q2 — Do they share a functional group?

Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

Both regimes share `firm → metric → report → checker`, a `state → structure → state` style chain. That is the functional group: it says *what kind* of thing an account is. The qualifier, voluntary or mandatory, says *which member*. This matters because a critic can grant the functional group while denying the isomer difference; the figure allows both readings, and they are answers to different questions.

**Falsifier:** a regime in which the checker is absent, so the chain stops at `report`. Then the functional group is not present and the regime is not an account in the figure's sense at all. Under Fig 3 :27, *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

### Q3 — What order do the four conditions take?

Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."** Dependency order for ECN-0090:

1. **C1 external ground** corresponds to perception. Can anything register the harm at all? Without this, the other three conditions operate on a reading that does not exist.
2. **C2 cascade enumeration** corresponds to vocabulary. Does the frame have terms for what the harm affords, three steps out? Fig 12 :115: *"having terms for what a situation affords was taken for a dictionary and it is a grammar."*
3. **C3 violable convention** corresponds to objective. Can the regime say what would count as progress, in its own terms, such that a violation is stateable?
4. **C4 two readings held apart** corresponds to planning and learn and carry. A plan that sums them cannot detect which condition failed, so the loop cannot carry a usable correction.

The middle link is C2, and it is the one with no instrument. Fig 3 :24: *"Why the middle link is where chains usually break."* Most regulatory effort lands at C3, where instruments already exist: disclosure templates, assurance standards, reporting deadlines. That is effort above the break.

**Falsifier:** a documented regime in which perception, vocabulary and objective all measure as working and the failure is located at planning, that is, in the summing of the two readings. That would falsify the "usually at C2" claim, though not the dependency ordering itself.

### Q4 — Union or partition?

Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** The four conditions overlap: C3's convention becomes part of the firm's Γ, which is the object C1 ranges against; C2's enumeration determines which slots C4 holds apart. This is a union. A single lever (a disclosure mandate) clears where only C3 is missing and leaves the intersections standing, because C1 or C2 still binds (Fig 9 :55-57).

**Falsifier:** one instrument that resolves all four conditions in both failure types and leaves no categorically different residue. That collapses the union into a partition and would make the unbundling unnecessary.

### Q5 — Is the DDT case an isomorphism or a rhyme?

Fig 11 :52-54 gives the contact cascade: *"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles"*, a *"second-order effect"* with the residual *"three steps out"*, established through biomagnification and eggshell thinning (Carson, *Silent Spring*, 1962). The mapping to a supply chain is **structural at the contact level only**. The actors, currency and anchor differ: the habitat in Fig 11 is ecological, the firm's is economic. Fig 4 :20 licenses the transfer of the kernel while requiring the anchor to be re-named at the new level.

So the analogue is an isomorphism of the **enumeration rule**, not of the parties. Claiming more, for example that a firm is literally an ecosystem, would be a strain and is refused. Marked `general` for the rule, `specified` for the firm-level application, single-source for the DDT example.

**Falsifier:** an agent-only harm reduction that changes the physical ground without changing any habitat contact. Under Fig 11 :40 (*"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing"*) that should be impossible for this class of case; if observed, the analogue is wrong.

## CONCLUSION

Voluntary and mandatory accounts are **isomers** over a shared atom set: same ingredients, different bond, and the difference is invisible in a reporting template that lists only ingredients. They share a **functional group** (`firm → metric → report → checker`) that fixes what kind of thing an account is. The four conditions are **ordered by dependency** as C1 to C4, with the break usually at C2 (vocabulary), not at the disclosure template where most effort lands. The question is a **union**, so the conditions are never averaged into one score. The DDT case is a real isomorphism of the enumeration rule, not of the actors.

This rescues what is defensible in the public debate without the part that fails: both poles of the voluntary/mandatory argument are needed, but neither is a verdict the figures can hand down, and the order of the conditions is not the order in which policies are usually written.
