# Expert verification packet: the Tether on what counts as conservation success

**What this is:** a claim in a form a conservation scientist, ecosystem ecologist or evaluation practitioner can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape and per-domain verdicts, `general` on the barrier rules, `gap` on the universal winner, not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The four candidate criteria are not four answers to one question. Species persistence, ecosystem function, evolutionary potential and human well-being are four domains with different actors, currencies and anchors, so the question is a union that must be split before it is searched. Success has to be reported as a vector of per-slot residuals against declared baselines, never as a composite score, because an average hides the one slot still failing. Each criterion is a legitimate anchor only when the constitutive question is about continued existence, a process, adaptive capacity, or a person's life respectively. There is no figure line naming a universal winner, so that slot is reported as `gap`, a verdict and not a silence. The shape any defensible report must have is fully statable: split the union, declare the anchor, enumerate the habitat to the cascade horizon, keep the convention revisable, keep indicators independent with provenance, and carry a refuter written before the run.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - Question form: the four criteria form a union and must be split

- **Statement:** the disagreement does not shrink with effort because each criterion works well on a different subset. It is several well-formed questions, not one hard one.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :4-5, "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." Also Fig 9 :54, "when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." And Fig 4 :20, "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."
- **What to check:** is there a real conservation case where one criterion improved and another declined, with independent instruments? If so, a single scalar cannot represent the outcome and the union reading is supported. Tobias et al. 2025 and Brichieri-Colombi et al. 2018 supply published examples, but the check is against your own cases.
- **Refuter:** a validated study showing that all four criteria load on one latent variable measured by disjoint instruments, so that each rule works on the same subset, would collapse the union to a partition and refute C1.

### C2 - Measurement form: per-slot residuals, not a composite

- **Statement:** the residual is indexed by object slot. Aggregation deletes the live signal. A success report must list each slot with its own baseline, threshold and provenance.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19, "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22, "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 2 :40, "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Fig 2 :42, "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."
- **What to check:** take a real report that publishes a composite success score. Does the composite reverse the decision in any case where one slot is failing? If it does, the figure prediction is borne out. Also check whether the component indicators share a monitoring program, sites and funders; if they do, they are one witness.
- **Refuter:** an instrument that returns the same decision from the composite as from the full slot vector in all cases, including a case with one live failing slot, refutes C2. So does a demonstrated case where four indicators were derived from genuinely disjoint evidence with quantified low overlap, which upgrades them from one witness to four.

### C3 - Anchor form: each criterion wins only for its constitutive question, and the universal winner is a gap

- **Statement:** an anchor is legitimate only when the question is constitutively about it. Human judgment is legitimate for a question about a person and for no other question by default. No figure line names a universal anchor for conservation, so that slot is `gap`.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29, "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." Fig 2 :36, "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Fig 2 :37, "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people." Fig 13 :134, "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence." Fig 9 :32, "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."
- **What to check:** for a published claim that one criterion is the correct measure of conservation success, ask which constitutive question the study actually answered. If the question was about continued existence, the persistence anchor was legitimate locally; if the study then generalised it to all conservation, it overreached. Check also whether the anchor moved when a stakeholder pushed on it. If it did, it was not an anchor.
- **Refuter:** a verbatim figure line naming one criterion as the general anchor, or a constitutive restatement of the field's question that makes one anchor legitimate everywhere, refutes C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that any one criterion is the correct one. The winner slot is `gap`.
- Not a new index, weighting scheme or universal threshold.
- Not that species persistence is primary because it is countable, or that human well-being wins because people are involved.
- Not that the four criteria are interchangeable or that their currencies add.
- Not that agreement among indicators is corroboration without measured low overlap and a provenance record.
- Not that a captive or fenced population is simply "persisted." Fig 11 :67: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."
- Not that a declared success criterion may decide outcomes. Fig 2 :43: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by experiment or dataset.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A measurement for each slot with a declared baseline, an instrument outside the producing frame and a threshold: a census for persistence, a process rate for function, a cross-generational genetic reading for evolutionary potential, and the person's own judgment for well-being.
2. For the universal question: one of the five firing conditions listed in `07` section 5, of which the strongest is a verbatim figure line naming a general anchor.
3. A direct refutation of C1, C2 or C3 under the refuters above. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :4-5 and :54, Fig 1 :19 and :22, and Fig 2 :36-37. Five short passages.
2. Name one case from your own work where one criterion improved and another declined. If you can, C1 is supported; if you cannot after an honest search, say so.
3. Look at one real composite success score you know. Ask whether it could hide a failing slot. If it could, C2 is supported.
4. For one published claim that a criterion is "the" measure of success, identify the constitutive question the study actually answered. Then say whether that answer was generalised beyond its question.
5. Send the refutation, or the case. A failure is more useful to me than agreement.
