## 1. Independent domain census

I redid the census before relying on the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the gap between a predicted conservation outcome under a chosen success term and the observed world state. Arity: four conditions, so a **union compound**, not one hard question. Symmetry: not symmetric, since a population count, a process rate, a heritable variance and a well-being judgment are different currencies and cannot be swapped without loss. Scale: first order within a site or lineage, second order at the union, third order at the cascade horizon.
- **Domain 1, persistence.** Actors: populations and individuals. Currency: abundance and extinction risk. Anchor: a counter. Constitutive question: did the lineage continue?
- **Domain 2, function.** Actors: processes and interactions. Currency: rates and fluxes. Anchor: a process measurement. Constitutive question: does the system still do what it did?
- **Domain 3, evolutionary potential.** Actors: genomes, alleles, lineages. Currency: heritable variance across generations. Anchor: a cross-generational genetic reading. Constitutive question: can the lineage still adapt?
- **Domain 4, well-being.** Actors: people and communities. Currency: health, livelihood, security, subjective and objective well-being. Anchor: human judgment, conditional on the question being about a person.
- **Domain 5, verification.** Actors: indicators and auditors. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all domains share one evidence pool, so independence is zero.

**Order by dependency.** Perception, vocabulary and objective come before planning and carrying. The objective link is where "what counts as progress" is decided (Fig 3 :9-10). Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).

**My census matches the primary's five domains and its union reading.** Where the primary derived the split from Fig 9 :4-5 and Fig 4 :20, I derived it from Fig 9 :54 (union unbundling) and Fig 11 :43 (habitat enumeration). Different starting lines, same domain boundaries. The routes have low conceptual overlap inside the framework, but they still share the figure set and the same evidence pool. Agreement here is internal consistency, not corroboration.

## 4. Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :19/:22, Fig 2 :36/:43, Fig 9 :4-5/:54, Fig 11 :43/:52/:63 and Fig 13 :134.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07` and `09`: "split the union, report a per-slot vector, declare the conditional anchor, keep the winner slot as gap."
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** what a practitioner would actually do with it: choose four indicators, set their baselines, decide who measures them, and publish a report.

**R_T is nonzero.** Five things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down.

- The **cascade horizon** from Fig 11 :52. The shorthand says "enumerate the habitat," which does not tell a practitioner how far out to stop or how to detect a three-step effect.
- The **convention guard** from Fig 2 :43. The shorthand says "declare the anchor," which does not by itself prevent the declared anchor from becoming the thing that decides funding.
- The **independence parameter** from Fig 2 :31-32 and :40. The shorthand says "count agreement at low overlap," which does not tell a practitioner how to measure overlap or what threshold to use.
- The **permanent-work condition** from Fig 11 :63. The shorthand says "report a vector," which does not carry the maintenance requirement into a report that is published once and forgotten.
- The **isomer strength distinction** from Fig 12 :117 versus :124. The shorthand says the criteria are "different," which collapses the strong shared-atom claim and the weak functional-group claim into one.

Conversely, three things survive cleanly: the ban on aggregation, the conditional anchor, and the `gap` on a universal winner. That residue is worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists. It is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the figures" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
