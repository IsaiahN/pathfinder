# Are the four criteria isomers? Structure, order and analogue

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading or a measurement of any conservation project.

## RESIDUAL

The question names four criteria as if they were four options in one list. That is a flat formula: it records ingredients and not arrangement. Fig 12 :14 states the failure:

> **"the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not"**

Two questions are owed before the answer in `07` can be used. (1) If the four criteria are built over the same atoms, are they **isomers** under Fig 12, meaning same atoms, different bond, different substance? Or are they members of one **functional group**, sharing a substructure but differing in fillers? (2) What **order** relates them: a symmetric conjunction in the report, a dependency chain in their production, a sequence, or a comparison? Getting this wrong would turn the per-slot vector into either a single molecule with the wrong bond or a list with no route.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms available for the derivation: `actor`, `currency`, `anchor`, `instrument`, `timescale`, `cascade horizon`, `baseline`. Bonds available: the seven in Fig 12 plus negation. No new primitive is available without import.

- D1 species persistence bonds `actor → counter` over a short to medium timescale.
- D2 ecosystem function bonds `actor → process rate` with an experiment as anchor.
- D3 evolutionary potential bonds `actor → heritable variance` across a long, generational timescale.
- D4 human well-being bonds `actor → judgment` with a person as the legitimate anchor under the constitutive condition.

These are not four names for one reading. The currency differs, the instrument differs and the timescale differs. "The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording" (Fig 12 :5). A flat list called "success" drops exactly that half.

## DERIVATION

### Q2 - Are the four criteria isomers, or a functional group?

Fig 12 :117:

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fig 12 :124:

> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

There are two defensible readings, with different strengths.

**Weak reading, the one the literature actually supports.** As the four criteria are normally written, D1 uses population actors and a counter, D2 process actors and a rate, D3 genetic actors and a variance, D4 people and a judgment. They do not share a full atom set; they share only the abstract substructure `actor → anchor`. Under Fig 12 :124 that is a **functional group**: the group says what kind (a residual against an anchor), the qualifier says which member (counter, rate, variance, judgment). This is the safe claim and it is `triangulated` across Fig 12 :124 and the four published protocols.

**Strong reading, not yet demonstrated.** If both criteria are written over the *full shared atom set* `{actor, currency, anchor, instrument, timescale, cascade horizon, baseline}` and differ only in which bond connects which atoms, then they are isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. For example D2 and D3 over the same set, with a rate bond in one and a variance-across-generations bond in the other, would be isomers. The difference would be invisible in a formula that lists only the ingredients and decisive in the behaviour, exactly as Fig 12 :117 says. This claim is `specified` and unmeasured: whether a given pair of real monitoring programs really uses the same atom set is an empirical question about their protocols, not a figure fact.

**Falsifier, written before the run.** If two criteria can be written with identical atoms and identical bonds, meaning the same actor, the same currency, the same anchor, the same instrument, the same timescale and the same baseline, the isomer claim fails and the pair collapses to an identity bond (`≡`, two names for one referent). In that case they are one slot and the vector has one fewer dimension. Conversely, if no two criteria share a full atom set under any honest restatement, the strong isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim stands. Either outcome is a refutation of part of this derivation and is recorded rather than smoothed.

### Q3 - What order relates the four slots?

Three different orders are in play and must not be conflated.

1. **At report time: conjunction (`+`), symmetric.** The four slots are all held at once. Swap their order in the report and nothing changes. Fig 12 :59 gives the test: "swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No." Under a symmetric bond, "k operands have k! spellings and one term" (Fig 12 :92). Reporting D1, D2, D3, D4 or D4, D3, D2, D1 is one vector. A code that charges for the sequence overcharges by log(k!).
2. **At production time: dependency chain (Fig 3), ordered.** Each slot's report has to pass through perception, vocabulary and objective before planning and carrying. "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not" (Fig 3 :19). This is a sequence in the sense of Fig 12 :70, "A then B; B needs A to have happened." It is a sequence *within* a slot's production, not *between* the four slots. A claim that evolutionary potential must always be measured before ecosystem function is an ordering the figures do not license.
3. **At target setting: comparison (`⋛`), ordered and thresholded.** Operators table: "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one." Each criterion carries its own threshold relation between a measured quantity and a target. A single universal threshold applied across four currencies is a type error, because `⋛` is "a threshold relation between two quantities" (Fig 12 :90), not a way of adding heterogeneous quantities.

**Falsifier for the order claim.** If a report's decision changes when the four slots are permuted, the conjunction reading is refuted and an ordered bond must be named. If a production chain is demonstrated in which the objective link can be skipped without loss, the dependency reading is refuted.

### Q4 - Progress, baseline and refusal

Two further operators matter and are often omitted from success reports.

- **Subtraction (`−`).** Fig 12 :85: "A with B removed or absent." Operators table: "progress is a subtraction, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot say how far along it is." A residual is always `current − baseline`. The IUCN Green Status operationalises exactly this with counterfactual scenarios: viability, functionality and representation are scored against what the state would have been without conservation. Without a declared baseline, a criterion is a state description, not a measure of success. This is a figure-grounded requirement, corroborated by the world source but not derived from it.
- **Negation (`¬`).** Fig 12 :139-140: without a unary operator "a system can write what a situation contains, what follows what, and how far along it is" and "cannot write that any of those is false." A success vocabulary that contains only indicators can accumulate good news and cannot carry a correction. The pre-registered refuter in `01` section 4 is a negation, and it is not a bond: it joins nothing and does not enter the bond price. A report without one is a report that can be corrected only by being replaced, not by being refused.

### Q5 - Settlement and the second-order report

Fig 12 :104:

> **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."**

When a slot's reading has been settled against the ground, it can serve as an atom in a second-order report. But Fig 5's amendment must be carried with it: settling is necessary for composition and not sufficient. A term can be purchased and still be unusable if it does not compose with the others. A four-slot vector whose slots were each validated by different instruments in different closures is not automatically composable. That is the work F8 of `01` names: agreement counts only at measured low overlap, with provenance.

## CONCLUSION

As written in the conservation literature, the four criteria form a **functional group** over the substructure `actor → anchor`, not a demonstrated isomer set. The stronger isomer claim is available if two criteria are restated over the full shared atom set and differ only in their bonds; it is `specified`, unmeasured and carries a stated falsifier. In the report they join under a symmetric conjunction, so their order carries no information. In their production they obey a dependency chain, and at target setting each carries its own threshold comparison. Progress is a subtraction against a declared baseline, and a refusal is a negation, not a bond. Nothing here names a universal winner. The derivation supports the shape in `07` and is checkable by the three sub-claims in `08`.
