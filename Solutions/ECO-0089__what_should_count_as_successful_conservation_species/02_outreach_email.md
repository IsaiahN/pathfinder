**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` survives the reader's own check. The output must stand on the residual and its refuter before any theory is offered.

**Subject:** One check on how conservation success gets reported

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** A conservation intervention predicts an outcome under some chosen success term. The world returns an observation. The gap between them has to be read per object slot: per population, per process, per lineage, per community. The framework's own rule is that the residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated, and that aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** Four things follow, and I would like you to test them, not believe them:
>
> 1. The four candidate criteria (species persistence, ecosystem function, evolutionary potential, human well-being) are not four answers to one question. They are four domains with different actors, currencies and anchors, so the question has to be split before it is searched.
> 2. Success has to be reported as a vector of per-slot residuals, never as one composite score. A composite hides the one slot that is still failing.
> 3. No criterion is the universal anchor. Each is the right anchor only for a question that is constitutively about it: continued existence, process delivery, adaptive capacity, or a person's life. Choosing one as a permanent winner turns a useful convention into a thing that decides outcomes.
> 4. The question "which one criterion is the answer" has no answer in the framework. I am reporting that as `gap`, a verdict and not a silence. What the framework does return is the shape any defensible success report must have.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not saying one of the four criteria is the correct one. I am not offering a new composite index, a weighting scheme, or a threshold. I am not saying human well-being wins because people are involved, or that species persistence wins because it is measurable. I am not claiming a ground reading: no dataset was analysed to produce this.
>
> **What would refute the output.** I wrote these down before sending this:
>
> 1. A single verbatim figure line naming one of the four as the general anchor for conservation, with no constitutive restriction.
> 2. A validated instrument that returns the same decision from a composite score as from the full slot vector, including cases with one live failing slot.
> 3. Evidence that all four criteria reduce to one latent variable measured by disjoint instruments, which would collapse the union into a partition.
> 4. A direct-contact species count shown to track cascade effects two or more steps out with no lost signal across independent habitats.
> 5. A conserved outcome that stays stable with no continuing maintenance of the monitoring or funding channel.
>
> If any holds, the shape claim is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would actually like from you.** Tell me what *you* would count as a failure here. If you have a case where one of the four criteria genuinely settled a conservation decision, tell me which question was being asked. That answer is more useful than a verdict on the framework, and I will record it before showing you anything else.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
>
> - The verbatim figure text, with line numbers: local framework file `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - The eight-step loop and the mode rule: local framework file `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - The canonical usage rule: local framework file `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the local file. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and its literature warns that outputs can be contrived to fit the formula. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Stage gates

- **Stage 1:** residual, shape claim and pre-registered refuter. Record the reader's verdict before any theory is shown.
- **Stage 2, opt in only:** the three local source links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
- **Stage 3, opt in only:** the formula as a reusable method, with the instruction that each user state their own ground and write their own refuter.

Nothing in this folder sends anything. The host does the sending.
