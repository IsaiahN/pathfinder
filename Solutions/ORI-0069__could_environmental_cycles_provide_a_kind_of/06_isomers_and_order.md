## DERIVATION

### Q1: Which of the four structures is "external memory"?

Fig 12 names four structures:

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."
- Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

The question's phrase splits cleanly:

1. "Environmental cycles" is the **cycle** structure (Fig 12 :121). It closes a chain from outside. It does not persist as a record; it recurs.
2. "Memory" points to the **catalyst** structure (Fig 12 :119). A part that survives its own use is the only named structure with the survive-the-occasion property.
3. It is **not isomerism**. Isomerism needs the same atom set under different bonds. A habitat recurrence and a molecular product do not share an atom set, so "cycles are an isomer of a polymer" is a category error. This is the analogue the question does not need.
4. It is weakly a **functional group** claim: "something that makes the next search cheaper" (Fig 4 :24) says what kind of contribution is made, not which condition makes it. The qualifier is left open, exactly as Fig 9 :32 requires.

So the honest structural reading is a **pair in lockstep**, not one structure: cycle from the environment, catalyst or settled molecule as the surviving record. Fig 13 :28: "They alternate, and the alternation is where both the recursion and the randomness come from."

**Falsifier for Q1:** show that a single one of the four named structures carries both the closing and the surviving property, with no partner. That would collapse the pair and break Fig 12 :119/:121, which distinguish them.

### Q2: What order must the four slots be settled in?

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces."

Ordered derivation:

1. **Recurring condition.** Is there a returning difference at all? Fig 13 :12 "the ordered succession of differences." Without this, there is no cycle to speak of.
2. **Carried state.** What is handed forward? Fig 13 :88 "The residual of the turn just closed is what the next turn composes against, so no two passes begin from the same place." This is the cross-turn state, and it is a remainder, not a snapshot.
3. **Cross-turn mechanism.** How does the state change the next turn's reactions? Fig 12 :111 "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." The mechanism must be a bond, not an ingredient list.
4. **Settling ground.** What decides which structures persist? Fig 11 :73 "The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."

Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Slots 1 to 3 have figure-level instruments; slot 4 is the one owed to experiment. Slot 3 is where the vocabulary problem lives: "memory" is an unbought term pretending to be a bond. Refuse it before minting (Formula step 2): a refusing term removes standing, not content.

**Falsifier for Q2:** reverse the order successfully, that is, settle which structures persisted before establishing whether any condition recurred. If that produces a non-circular account, the dependency order is wrong.

### Q3: Cross-kind composition, not within-kind

Fig 13 :74: "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."
Fig 13 :75: "Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."

The environmental recurrence is a state-side condition (time as ordered succession). The persisting record is a structure-side property (a part surviving its use). Their composition crosses kinds, so it is legal; a "memory" made only of states, or only of structures, would be the within-kind error Fig 13 forbids. This is the structural reason the pair in Q1 cannot be collapsed to one member.

### Q4: The low-overlap analogue, and its limit

Clark and Chalmers (1998), *The Extended Mind*: "active externalism, based on the active role of the environment in driving cognitive processes." Michaelian and Sutton (2013) survey distributed memory research in which remembering is "distributed across heterogeneous systems." These are analogues from human cognition, a genuinely low-overlap field, and they corroborate that "external memory" is a coherent phrase in *that* frame.

They do not corroborate the chemistry. Michaelian's own paper ("Is external memory memory?", 2012, cited in the 2013 review) argues that external memory performs a function different from biological memory. By Fig 2 :40, agreement drawn from a shared pool is one witness, and here the pool is only a metaphor. The analogue is recorded, not promoted to evidence. The verdict in `07` and `09` comes from Figs 4, 11, 12 and 13.

## CONCLUSION

"External memory" is not one of Fig 12's four structures. It is a loose name for a **pair**: the environmental cycle (which closes a chain) and a surviving structure (which carries the record). Isomerism does not apply; functional-group language applies only to the contribution, not to a named condition. The four slots settle in dependency order, with the naming question refused before any mint. The analogue from cognition is welcome as a kind of thing and worthless as a witness for prebiotic chemistry.
