## Relevant wider literature, read this session

1. **Environmental selection rather than storage.** Mamajanov (2019) found that wet-dry cycling delayed gelation and enriched branched polyesters, while continuous drying gave crosslinked products. Her phrase, "environmental conditions have the ability to exert a rudimentary pressure to selectively enrich the polyesterification products," matches the figure-level conclusion that cycling is a head start and a selection pressure, not a recording. This is the closest experimental analogue to ORI-0069 that was actually read.
2. **The skeptical check.** Orgel (2000) argued that complex cycles on mineral surfaces make "unreasonable assumptions about the catalytic properties of minerals and the ability of minerals to organize sequences of disparate reactions." This is a real conflict with an optimistic reading of the cycle claim. It is recorded as such in `01` F7 and `09` WORLD. It did not flip the verdict, because it governs the chemistry domain while the figure verdict governs the shape.
3. **Closure from the environment.** Lehman and Kauffman (2021) describe work cycles that construct their own boundary conditions: "work itself can construct the constraints needed for closure." That corroborates the cycle-as-closing-bond reading and the Fig 12 settled-molecule rule.
4. **Cycles versus memory.** Dill and Agozzino (2021) place daily cycles on the energy side and genomes on the memory side. That independently matches the audit's central separation between head start (Fig 4) and persisting record (Fig 12).
5. **Low-overlap analogue, cognition.** Clark and Chalmers (1998) argue for "active externalism, based on the active role of the environment in driving cognitive processes." Michaelian and Sutton (2013) survey the resulting literature on distributed memory. These are analogues only: they show that "external memory" is a live concept in a different field, but they carry no weight for prebiotic chemistry. They are marked as low overlap in the ledger.

## Distribution rules: offer, do not push

- **Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source."** Lead with the four slots and the refuter. The framework interior comes later, and only if asked for.
- **Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."** A recipient who has absorbed the whole framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked. Record the stage-1 verdict first.
- **Fig 9 :28 "any frame you pick will seem to fit"** if the residual is described only after choosing a favourite geochemical setting. Describe the slots before naming a setting.
- **Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."** Do not ask for agreement. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
- **Fig 4 :24 "downward: something that makes the next search cheaper. Never a replay of what worked once."** Send the generator, not a conclusion.
- **Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."** Use the public submission and contact routes in `03`, not personal lists. No bulk send.

**Recommended order in any reply:** (1) the four slots and the shape claim; (2) ask what would count as a failure and record it; (3) only then the three links, with the note that the figures, not this summary, are the authority; (4) on request, the formula as a method the recipient can test against their own ground.
