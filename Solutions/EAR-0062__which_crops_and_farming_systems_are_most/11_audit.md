## Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual line alone, before using the primary's conclusions, in the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

Arity: at least three dimensions plus intersections, so a **union compound** (Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."), not a single hard question. Scale: crop, farm seat, regional supply, with the kernel fixed and the actors, currency and anchor changing (Fig 4 :20).
- **Domain 1, crop genotype.** Actors: genotypes, traits, seed systems. Currency: yield and persistence under sampled environment-types. Anchor: multi-environment trials. Question type: a binding from this frame onto held slot terms, not a new member of the figure set.
- **Domain 2, farming system.** Actors: the manager and practices. Currency: introduced actors and changed contact. Anchor: multi-year on-farm outcome plus a refusal seat. Question type: maintained seat, introduction available and exclusion not (Fig 11 :61).
- **Domain 3, climate and habitat.** Actors: conditions and relations. Currency: selection pressure and cascades. Anchor: observations that update, so this is habitat and not the ground (Fig 11 :73). Question type: enumeration, not composition (Fig 11 :43).
- **Domain 4, verification frame.** Actors: researchers, reviewers, farmers. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: a measurement that does not update. In this simulated run all domains draw on one evidence pool, so domain 4's independence is near zero.

Order by dependency: perceive conditions, acquire affordance terms, bind an objective with an instrument, then plan, then carry (Fig 3 :20). The break is at the objective, not at planning. Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28). Settle a first-order candidate before composing it into a supply-scale claim (Fig 12 :104).

## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :19 (per-slot residual), Fig 9 :55 (union), Fig 11 :49/:51 (relative valence, no standing list), Fig 11 :39/:61/:63 (capability and the maintained seat), Fig 3 :27 (objective link).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07` and `09`: success is per-slot maintained contact; the residual is a union; the farming system is a maintained seat; the list is a `gap`.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete agronomic recommendation, for example "heat-tolerant maize with legume intercropping and supplementary irrigation is the system most likely to adapt".

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **per-slot indexing** (Fig 1 :19), which a global "most likely" erases;
- the **relative valence and no-standing-list rule** (Fig 11 :49/:51), which a recommendation worded as a winner breaches;
- the **objective instrument** (Fig 3 :27), which a recommendation worded as settled skips, since mean yield is a proxy;
- the **provenance and shadow test** on the imported G x E x M and ecology frames (Fig 8 :50), which a recommendation with no source record drops.

Conversely, two things survive cleanly: a structure or environment-type must sit between a crop state and a climate state, and the system is a maintained seat whose power is introduction rather than exclusion. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
