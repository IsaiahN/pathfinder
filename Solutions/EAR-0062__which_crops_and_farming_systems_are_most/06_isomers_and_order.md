# Structure, order and analogues for the crop adaptation question

**Mode: `specified` on the composition claims, `general` on the substrate analogue. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## RESIDUAL

Two supporting questions are owed by the main audit:

1. **Structure.** Is a crop-and-system pair a list of ingredients (a genotype plus a practice plus a climate) or a bonded arrangement? If it is an arrangement, what follows about two systems that share the same ingredients?
2. **Order.** Does "genotype by environment by management" name a sequence, a symmetric conjunction, or a typed composition? What is the dependency order for investigating a slot, and is that the same as the composition order inside the system?

The answers matter because the question's superlative implicitly treats systems as points on one scale. If the arrangement is the substance, two systems with identical ingredients can be different substances, and no amount of averaging over them returns a winner.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held for this derivation: a genotype `G`, an environment-type `E`, a management act `M`, and the six substrate terms used as a cross-scale analogue (space, time, energy states; spectrum, gradient, network structures). Bonds held: the seven typed relations plus negation. No new primitive is available without import, and none is claimed.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 :Is the pair an ingredient list or a molecule?

`Fig 12 :5`: "The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."

`Fig 12 :46`: "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."

A recommendation that records "drought-tolerant maize, intercropping, dryland" has recorded its ingredients and not its arrangement. Which crop is planted first, which one is the overstorey, when the second is sown relative to the first, what happens to the water balance at flowering, and what would count as failure are all carried by the bond, not by the ingredient list. `Fig 12 :94`: "The arrangement is the substance." Two recommendations with the same three words can be inverses of one another in the behaviour.

**Falsifier:** show a pair of recommendations with identical ingredients and different bonds that behave identically at the slot under a declared shock. If they do, the bond carries nothing here and the flat reading stands for this case.

### Q2 :Are two farming systems isomers?

`Fig 12 :117`: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

`Fig 12 :123`: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome."

Write two systems over the same atom set `{maize, legume, water, season, soil}`.

- System A: maize and legume grown simultaneously in alternating strips, with the legume fixing nitrogen during the maize's vegetative phase.
- System B: maize and legume grown in sequence, the legume after maize harvest, with residue retained.

Same atoms, different bond (conjunction `+` versus sequence `→`), different substance. The difference is invisible in an ingredient inventory and decisive in water use, pest cycles and labour timing. These are **isomers** under Fig 12 :117.

If instead the two systems share only a general pattern (a cereal, a legume, a retained residue) but use different species and different environments, they are not isomers. They are members of the same **functional group** (Fig 12 :123), where "the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member". The world literature supplies the matching distinction: Tamburini et al. 2020 find that diversification practices targeting aboveground biodiversity boost pest control and water regulation, while those targeting belowground biodiversity enhance nutrient cycling and soil fertility. Same functional group, different member, different outcome bundle.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two systems over a fixed atom set with different bonds produce the same outcome bundle under the same declared shock at the same slot, the isomer claim fails for that pair and the functional-group reading is all that remains.

### Q3 :What is the bond in G x E x M?

The multiplication in "G x E x M" is not the figure's `→` sequence. `Fig 12 :112`: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction." Writing the factorial as `G → E → M` would impose an order the figure disclaims for the composition, and would turn an interaction into a plan. Operators table :34 warns that "the only bond a flat chain can express, so every term today is a sequence whether or not it should be". That is the exact notation error to avoid.

At the composition level the appropriate reading is a typed conjunction with interaction: `G + E + M` where the bond carries which genotype is planted into which environment-type under which management. `Fig 12 :92`: "Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing." Swapping G and M changes the substance (a trait is not a practice), so the operands are not interchangeable even though the notation `+` is symmetric; the typed bond is doing the work the glyph alone cannot show. This is the "flat formula" failure Fig 12 names: a formula that records ingredients and not arrangement lets a downstream reader treat the operands as interchangeable when they are not.

**Order is a separate question from composition.** Dependency order for *investigating* a slot follows Fig 3 :20, "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance": perceive the conditions, acquire terms for what they afford, bind an objective with an instrument, then plan, then carry what worked. That is an epistemic sequence over the researcher's actions, not an assertion that nature composes genotype before environment before management. Mixing the two is a category error, and it is why Cooper et al.'s "sequential tag team" of breeding followed by agronomy is a research-organisation fact, not a law of the system. They explicitly argue for "co-design new G–M technologies for a non-stationary TPE", which is a different arrangement of the same atoms.

**Falsifier:** exhibit a case where the investigation order is forced by the composition itself, so that environment cannot be characterised until a genotype has been chosen and the two are not separable even in principle. Then the distinction between epistemic and compositional order collapses here.

### Q4 :What survives across seasons, and what closes the loop?

`Fig 12 :119`: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use."

`Fig 12 :121`: "cycle. The output re-enters as input."

A seed system, a soil organic matter stock, a pollinator population and a farmer's recorded knowledge are catalysts in this system: parts that survive their own use and behave differently across uses from spent and regenerating parts. A harvested annual crop is a spent part. `Fig 12 :119` adds that "nothing records which a term is", so a recommendation that counts only the current harvest treats catalysts and spent parts as the same kind of thing and prices the system wrong. Handayani et al. 2026 record that enabling institutions and adaptive feedback are necessary links between diversification and resilience, which is the world-side version of the catalyst and cycle claim.

The cycle closes when the residual of one season becomes the input to the next: the observed rank flip at a slot re-enters as the new environment-type definition, which is Cooper et al.'s "iterative empirical-modelling framework" and `Fig 13 :88`: "The residual of the turn just closed is what the next turn composes against, so no two passes begin from the same place."

**Falsifier:** show a farming system whose season-to-season loop has no residual re-entry, where next season's environment-types are defined independently of what last season surfaced. Then the cycle reading does not apply to it.

### Q5 :Are the domains at different levels, and what composes over what?

`Fig 12 :104`: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."

`Fig 13 :74`: "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."

`Fig 13 :75`: "Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."

A genotype that has been settled in multi-environment trials becomes an atom for the farming system; a farming system settled across years becomes an atom for a regional food-supply assessment. That is recursion (`Fig 12 :103`: "Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible"), and it is what permits the same loop to run at crop, farm and supply scale with "only the actors, the currency and the anchor change" (Fig 4 :20).

The substrate analogue must not be forced. At the agronomic scale, space is the field and region, time is season and phenology, energy is the conserved finite radiation and water budget, spectrum is the ordering of environment-types by severity, gradient is the spendable difference across a heat or water cline, and network is the pollination, pest, market and seed relations. A crop state cannot bond directly with a climate state; an environment-type structure must sit between. That is why the TPE vocabulary in Cooper et al. is the right imported term, and why Fig 13 :75 governs the analogue rather than contradicting it. This remains `general` mode and an analogue, as recorded in F13 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`.

**Falsifier:** derive one of the six from the other five at any scale, or show an agronomic composition that requires a seventh kind rather than a binding of the six. Either breaks the analogue.

## CONCLUSION

A crop-and-system pair is a molecule, not a list. Two systems over the same atom set with different bonds are isomers (Fig 12 :117); two systems that share only a pattern are members of a functional group (Fig 12 :123). The factorial's `x` is a typed interaction, not a sequence, and the dependency order for investigating a slot is a separate, epistemic order from the composition order inside the system. Catalysts and cycles mean that a season's output re-enters as the next season's input, which is why adaptation is permanent work (Fig 11 :63) rather than a solved state. The substrate analogue is a recursion across scales at `general` mode, not a literal identification.

All four claims are checkable with the falsifiers written above. None is a ground reading. The superlative "most likely" still has no object until an objective link with an instrument exists, which is F4 of the main audit.
