# Expert verification packet: when protecting components increases system fragility

**What this is:** a claim in a form a systemic-risk researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the conditions, not `grounded`.** No measurement of a particular system is claimed. This is a set of conditions and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A protective intervention has no standing valence: its sign is a relation to a residual. It changes system fragility only by changing contact. From the figures it follows that protection raises system fragility under seven structural conditions: harm appears beyond first-order contact; suppressing small failures cuts the channel that carries the correction signal; isolation substitutes a habitat without reproducing the goal; a global aggregate hides a live per-slot residual; "component safe" and "system safe" form a union whose intersection a single lever leaves standing; a regenerating protection flips a loop sign; and the introduced contact outlives the horizon over which safety was claimed. The figures return this shape and a per-slot test, and withhold the name of a system-specific threshold until the ground settles it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal and habitat-facing; checkable by reading Figures 11 and 1

- **Statement:** protection has no standing valence; it changes system fragility only by changing contact; first-order scoping misses third-order residuals; the residual must be measured per slot.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 11 :52: "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
- **What to check:** in a system you know, can you name the contact the protection changes? If not, the claimed system-level effect is unexplained. Does any harm from the protection appear beyond first-order contact? Does a system-level indicator read safe while a downstream slot is live?
- **Refuter:** a protection with invariant valence across residuals in two unrelated habitats; or a protection that changes no contact yet changes system failure at the ground; or a measured system in which all harm is first-order where a third-order residual was predicted; or a global metric that provably preserves every per-slot residual at all horizons.

### C2 - channel and union; checkable against monitoring data and the decision procedure

- **Statement:** suppressing small failures cuts the signal that drives correction, so load accumulates off-channel; and the two conditions "component safe" and "system safe" are a union, so one lever leaves their intersection standing.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does"; Fig 10 :25: "stale percepts, dropped transients,". Fig 13 :31: "a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos". Fig 9 :56: "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."
- **What to check:** after the protection, are the small failures still arriving as signals, or were they reclassified as transients? Is "safe" being tested as one condition rather than two? Is there a case in the intersection (component safe, system not safe) that the lever leaves standing?
- **Refuter:** a protection that suppresses small failures yet preserves every per-slot residual and improves system outcomes at all stated horizons; or a single lever whose intersection is provably empty across the enumerated habitat.

### C3 - bond structure and habitat substitution; framework-internal with a physics-facing analogue

- **Statement:** a regenerating protection differs from a spent one across uses even with the same atoms; isolation substitutes a habitat rather than removing one; and the framework cannot name a system-specific threshold.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is." Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence".
- **What to check:** does the system record which protecting parts are spent and which regenerate? Did isolation omit a goal-relevant condition or inherit an unchosen actor? Is anyone claiming a numeric threshold derived from the framework rather than measured?
- **Refuter:** a system that records spent versus regenerating status and shows identical per-slot residuals across uses; an isolation that substitutes no habitat and inherits no unchosen actor with the goal preserved; or a named system-specific threshold derived with no import and no ground reading that survives independent measurement.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that protection is always harmful. Valence is relative to the residual.
- Not a numeric fragility threshold, a universal cascade horizon or a universal stress-test interval.
- Not that any named component is the culprit. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that system-level indicators are forbidden. They are filters for the budget, not witnesses for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that withdrawing a protection restores the old system. Introduction is available, exclusion is not (Fig 11 :61-62).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

A per-slot ground reading with independent instruments, not a further argument. Carlson and Doyle (PNAS 2002) write that designed systems are "robust, yet fragile," that is "robust to what is common or anticipated but potentially fragile to what is rare or unanticipated and also to flaws in design, manufacturing, or maintenance." The corresponding tests here:

1. Does the protection change contact, and where does the residual move per slot?
2. Are the small failures still arriving as signals, or were they dropped as transients?
3. Does the substitute habitat reproduce the goal, and what did it inherit?
4. Does a global indicator read safe while a per-slot residual is live?
5. Is the protecting part spent or regenerating across uses, and does the loop sign flip?
6. At what measured threshold does a named protection flip sign in a named system?
