**Subject:** When does protecting a component make the system more fragile? One checkable output

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the conditions, not `grounded`):** protecting an individual component has no standing valence. An intervention's valence is a relation to a specific residual, not a property of the intervention. So there is no universal answer to "when does protection increase fragility." There are structural conditions. From the framework's habitat and channel rules, protection raises system fragility when:
>
> 1. the harm appears beyond first-order contact and the protection was scoped to direct contact;
> 2. the protection suppresses the small failures that would have carried the signal for correction, so the load accumulates off-channel;
> 3. isolation substitutes a habitat and the substitute omits a goal-relevant condition or inherits an actor that was not enumerated;
> 4. safety is assessed as a global average while a per-slot residual grows;
> 5. "component safe" and "system safe" are treated as one condition, so one lever leaves their intersection standing;
> 6. the protection is a regenerating part that changes the sign of a loop, or two protections share atoms but differ in bond;
> 7. the introduced contact persists beyond the horizon over which safety was claimed.
>
> And a protection that changes no contact at any slot cannot change system fragility at all, however much it improves the component. That is the load-bearing line: this is a contact question, not a component-strength question.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the framework can name a numeric threshold for your system. It returns a shape and a verification procedure, not a name. A system-specific threshold is an import from a measurement with provenance, not something derivable from the figures. I am also not claiming protection is always harmful. Under other residuals the same protection is a benefactor.
>
> **What would refute the output.** I wrote these down before writing this note:
> 1. a protection whose valence is invariant across residuals in two unrelated habitats;
> 2. a protection that changes no contact yet changes system failure probability at the ground;
> 3. a protection that suppresses small failures, preserves every per-slot residual and improves system outcomes at all stated horizons;
> 4. a global safety metric proven to preserve every per-slot residual at all horizons;
> 5. an isolation that substitutes no habitat and inherits no unchosen actor, with the goal preserved;
> 6. one lever that provably empties the intersection of "component safe" and "system safe" across the enumerated habitat;
> 7. a named system-specific threshold derived with no import and no ground reading, surviving independent measurement.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in the Project Pathfinder workspace
> - Canonical usage rule (GitHub): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Highly optimized tolerance, Carlson and Doyle, PNAS 2002: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC128573/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
