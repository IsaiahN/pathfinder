# The Tether's answer: when does protecting components increase system fragility?

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the per-domain conditions, not `grounded`.** This is a set of conditions and a verification route, not a measured result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A system's components are in contact. An intervention protects one component. We ask when that protection raises system-level fragility. Option A assumes failure rates compose additively, so protecting components is always good. Option B treats protection as an actor introduced into a habitat whose valence is relative to the residual, and treats "component safe" and "system safe" as a union compound with its own loop and ground. Option A cannot see the harm that the question is about, because that harm lives in another slot, at a later time, or in a channel the protection cut.

## 2. The shape

1. **Protection has no standing valence.** Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." The same guard is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another. So there is no unconditional list of safe components.

2. **The effect must be a contact effect.** Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." A protection that changes no contact cannot change system fragility. The first check is to name the contact it changes.

3. **First-order scoping misses the harm.** Fig 11 :52: "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." Enumerate contact outward to a stated cascade horizon (Fig 11 :43-44). Condition 1.

4. **Suppressed small failures cut the channel.** Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does"; Fig 10 :25: "stale percepts, dropped transients,". Fig 13 :31: "a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos." The small failure is both damage and the signal that drives correction. Remove the signal and the stress accumulates off-channel. Condition 2.

5. **Isolation substitutes a habitat.** Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Fig 11 :68: "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"; Fig 11 :69: "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts". Condition 3.

6. **The aggregate hides the live slot.** Fig 1 :13: "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"; Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Measure per slot; use system-level indicators only as budget filters (Fig 9 :60). Condition 4.

7. **One lever leaves the intersection.** Fig 9 :56: "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds." Unbundle "component safe" and "system safe" before choosing a lever (Fig 9 :54-55). Condition 5.

8. **Bonds carry the difference.** Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is." Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." A regenerating protection in a flipped loop is a different substance from a spent one, even with the same atoms (Fig 12 :117). Condition 6. Derived with written falsifiers in `06`.

9. **Introduction cannot be recalled.** Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." Fig 11 :63: "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state." The contact created by a protection outlives the horizon over which safety was declared. Condition 7.

10. **The ground settles; independence must be bought.** Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Fig 7 :13: "agreement compounds, evidence does not." High-cost, irreversible failures require separate instruments ranged against the same unmoving ground.

11. **The threshold name is owed.** Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." The figures return the conditions and a test, never a system-specific number. That number is an import from a measurement with provenance.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that protection always increases fragility. Valence is relative to the residual; under other residuals the same protection helps.
- Not that any named component is the culprit. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not a numeric fragility threshold, a universal cascade depth, or a universal stress-test interval. Those require the ground.
- Not that system-level indicators are forbidden. They are admissible as budget filters, not as the verdict.
- Not that removing a protection restores the old system. Contact was introduced, not subtracted (Fig 11 :61-62).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement at any slot was taken here.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A per-slot measurement with independent instruments, not a further argument. The questions a ground must answer:

- Which contact does the protection change, and at which slot does the residual move?
- Does the cascade stop at first order, or is there a live residual three steps out?
- Are the small failures still arriving as signals, or has the protection dropped them as transients?
- Does the substitute habitat reproduce everything the goal required, and what did it inherit?
- Does a global indicator read safe while a per-slot residual is live?
- Is the protecting part spent or regenerating, and does the loop sign change across uses?
- Does the introduced contact persist past the horizon over which safety was claimed?

Standing falsifiers from `01` section 3: invariant valence across residuals; a contact-free protection that changes system failure; all harm at first order where a third-order residual was predicted; a protection that suppresses small failures yet preserves every per-slot residual and improves outcomes; a global metric that preserves every per-slot residual; a single lever that empties the union intersection; sign-invariant recorded spent/regenerating status; removable introduced contact; one-pool agreement surviving an independent instrument; a figure-derived named threshold surviving measurement.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The conditions are derived; the threshold is not. Fig 6 :27: "Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same." Fig 4 :30 requires the round trip and the measured gap `R_T`; `11_audit.md` records it qualitatively, since no ground reading exists. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity."

## 6. Cost if wrong, undoable, reversed by

- **Cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The answer is a set of conditions with written refuters, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a framework instead of a residual, which Fig 2 :42 prices ("Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."). That cost is avoidable by leading with the conditions and the refuter.
- **Undoable:** yes for the claim, not for the action it describes. The conditions can be withdrawn or narrowed without touching the figure set. An introduced contact, by contrast, is not recalled by withdrawing the claim (Fig 11 :61-62). Do not confuse the two undoabilities.
- **Reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A per-slot experiment or stress test showing that a named protection flips sign at a measured threshold; a demonstration that suppressing small failures preserves every downstream residual; a global metric proven to preserve every slot-level residual; or a derivation of a system-specific threshold from the figures alone with no import. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that protecting components is always harmful, and it does not name a threshold. It says protection has no standing valence: it increases system fragility only by changing contact, and specifically when the harm appears beyond first-order enumeration, when suppressing small failures cuts the signal that drives correction, when isolation substitutes a habitat without reproducing the goal, when an aggregate hides a live per-slot residual, when one lever leaves the intersection of "component safe" and "system safe" standing, when a regenerating protection flips a loop sign, or when the introduced contact outlives the horizon over which safety was claimed. Protect nothing on a component-by-component average; enumerate the habitat per slot, keep the channel reachable, buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong, and withhold the threshold name until the ground pays for it. What is offered is the shape any answer must have, the refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it.
