# The Tether's answer: how local institutions mediate before escalation

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the four domain mechanisms, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a measured result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A claim or grievance between parties fails to close locally. If it is handed among venues that read the same evidence, or folded into an aggregate "community conflict", it compounds through reciprocal response and crosses into a broader arena. The question asks what a local institution must do to close it before that crossing. The residual is a **union compound** (Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*), not one problem scaled up. Legal, relational, structural, and data conditions intersect, and a lever that clears one leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*).

## 2. The shape: a seat with four properties

1. **It ranges the parties against something neither can move.** Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* The anchor can be evidence, a rule, a precedent, or an impartial procedure. The test is Fig 2 :30: *"you push on it, it is not an anchor."* If a party can move it, the process is negotiation in disguise.
2. **It keeps R indexed per dispute.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Each grievance is a slot. Counts and conflict indices are budget filters, not witnesses (Fig 9 :60).
3. **It is a low-overlap third frame.** Fig 8 :45: *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."* Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Independence is bought in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42), not maximised. Convergence from separate experience counts at maximum independence (Fig 8 :50: *"Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."*).
4. **It filters at the membrane and leaves a record.** Fig 4 :21: *"The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction."* What crosses up is the method, not a recording (Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*). The seat survives its own use (Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."*), and *"A ruling carries provenance like an atom."* (Fig 10 :62): date, reason, and the residual it answered.

These are four properties of a seat, not four names of institutions.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that mediation beats arbitration, or that customary councils beat courts. That selection is the F6 `gap`. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*; Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*.
- Not that any particular local institution actually reduced escalation in any measured setting. No recurrence or escalation data were opened.
- Not that a respected mediator's prestige settles a dispute. Prestige funds a trial of the process; only the ground settles it.
- Not that a dispute pyramid or any conflict index proves local mediation works. Those are filters (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that one mechanism fits every union of conditions. Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
- Not `grounded`. No measurement settled `left(R,φ)`.

## 5. What would settle it, the ground

The corresponding move to the spectrometer in *The Hard Problem is About Source* is a **source-discriminating instrument**, not another argument. "No escalation" is a reading; it does not reveal whether the institution caused it or the dispute would have cooled on its own.

- **Recurrence test:** matched disputes with and without local institutional handling, with recurrence measured at the same interval, across at least two frames that do not share an evidence pool.
- **Anchor capture test:** can one party move the rule or the record by pushing? If yes, it is not an anchor (Fig 2 :30), and a realisation of Fig 10 :47 (*"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*) is confirmed.
- **Per-slot test:** does handling a dispute individually improve its settlement relative to handling the same grievance through an aggregate conflict index? This fires refuter 2.
- **Membrane test:** does a ruling exported upward and brought back down retain fidelity, or does the round trip lose constraints (Fig 4 :28: *"the trip is not reversible"*)?
- **Standing refuters:** the five in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` §2 and the seven in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The four-property shape is what was in reach from the figure text and the opened world sources. The named institution, the empirical effect size, and the winning mechanism for a given union of conditions were not. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 13 :115: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not."* Indiscernibility is not identity; the route is recorded so the stop is checkable. Any claim to have selected a winner now would be adding an atom that was not earned (Fig 9 :60).

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not tell you which local institution is best, and it has not measured whether any of them prevents escalation. It says that a dispute cannot close against a reference the parties can move, so an effective seat must range them against something neither controls; that each grievance has to stay its own slot, because aggregation hides the live signal; that the seat must draw on evidence that does not largely overlap with the disputants', or it is one frame wearing two names; and that it must settle before the dispute crosses into a broader arena, exporting a method rather than a recording when a crossing is necessary. It leaves the choice among mediation, arbitration, customary councils, and restorative circles open and declared as a gap, because the framework returns a shape, not a name. What it offers is the shape any answer must have, five refuters written in advance, and the instrument that would close it: a source-discriminating comparison that can tell mediation from cooling on its own. The center stays open until the ground settles it.
