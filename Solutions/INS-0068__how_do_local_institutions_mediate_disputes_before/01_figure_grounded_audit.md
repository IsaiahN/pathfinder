## 0. The residual in effect terms

The question asks how local institutions mediate disputes *before* they escalate into broader conflict. Stated as an effect, not as a topic:

> **R:** a claim or grievance between parties that does not close at the local level. The unwanted outcome is a **membrane crossing**: the dispute is carried from one local arena into a broader one (more parties, higher stakes, coercion, group mobilisation), where it compounds through reciprocal response.

What is being decided, and what each option leads to:

| option | what it leads to |
|---|---|
| A local institution can settle the dispute against something the parties cannot move | the residual closes at the local scale; what crosses upward, if anything, is a method |
| The parties only negotiate against each other, with no external reference | each updates on the other, the readings converge on whoever has more influence, and the drift is coherent and unnoticed (Fig 2 panels 1 and 2) |
| The dispute is aggregated into a single "conflict" statistic | the per-dispute signal of where to look disappears into an average (Fig 1 :22); a filter is promoted to a witness (Fig 9 :60) |

**Binding the sought-for shape as an ordinary term** (Formula, "Direction"): by *effective local mediation* I mean a seat that (i) holds a non-updating anchor, (ii) keeps R indexed per dispute, (iii) has low overlap with the disputants' evidence, and (iv) settles before the membrane crossing while leaving a recorded ruling. That term is now bound and can accrue a residual like anything else. It is not assumed to be true.

## 1. Domain census before choosing any figure line

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.**

- **Arity:** at least two disputants plus a third seat. A grievance is not a monadic property; it is a relation. If the parties are the only actors, the configuration has no anchor and cannot close (Fig 2 panels 1 and 2).
- **Symmetry:** the *form* of the relation between parties is symmetric (each can update on the other), but its *power* is asymmetric, and what is contested is a threshold (who gets how much), which is the comparison bond ⋛ (Fig 12 :87, *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*). The seat-to-party relation is asymmetric: the seat does not have a stake in the outcome.
- **Scale:** local (one dispute, one venue) to broader (many disputes, many venues, coercion). The transition is a membrane crossing (Fig 4 :21) and, in the world literature, a positive-feedback escalation trap.

**One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor.** A domain with no nameable anchor is not a settling domain; it is recorded as such rather than promoted.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | settles R? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 the dispute frame | two or more parties | claims, threats, concessions, retaliation | none of its own; each party's "anchor" is its own position and moves under pressure | no, it is a mirror configuration |
| D2 the local institution | mediator, judge, council, elders, ADR or customary body | procedure, evidence, precedent, ruling, record | the rule or fact that does not move when a party pushes (Fig 2 :29-30) | yes, if the anchor stays reachable and the seat is blind to prestige |
| D3 the broader conflict | wider community, higher courts, outside actors, media | alignment, mobilisation, aggregated grievance | none; reciprocal response is the engine | no, it is the thing to avoid crossing into |
| D4 the habitat around the dispute | kinship, markets, schools, police, overlapping identities | contact and cascade (Fig 11 :44) | the ground that settles the underlying resource question | indirectly, by changing contact |

**Partition or union.** This is a **union**, not a partition. A single dispute can be legal, relational, and structural at once. In the world literature, Moore's "Circle of Conflict" lists data, relationship, value, structure, and interests as sources and says conflicts may have multiple sources. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57 gives the consequence: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* A single lever (a court ruling) clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. So escalation is not one hard problem scaled up; it is a compound with its own loop.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* The mediation chain runs:

1. perception: what happened, and is it distinguishable from what was there before;
2. vocabulary: what claims and remedies are nameable, what the situation affords;
3. objective: what would count as a settled dispute, in the institution's own terms;
4. planning: a route to that objective under incomplete information;
5. learn and carry: name what worked, verify it, carry it to the next dispute.

The chain usually breaks between 2 and 3: having terms for what a situation offers is easily mistaken for a dictionary when it is a grammar (Fig 3 :24-27; Fig 12 :111). Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* In dispute resolution this link is "what counts as resolution", and it is the one most often assumed rather than measured.

## 3. Findings, one loop per domain, verdicts kept separate

### F1. The dispute frame cannot settle itself (Domain 1). `triangulated`.

Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."* Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*

Two parties who only check against each other converge on whoever had more influence; the agreement is the pool talking. The world literature converges from three separate fields:

- The *Conflict resolution* page describes "conflict resolution mechanisms: independent procedures in which the conflicting parties can have confidence," and Wallensteen's sixth mechanism adds that the procedure is *"outside of the parties' immediate control"*.
- The interest-based relational approach (Fisher and Ury, via the same page) includes *"insist on using objective criteria"*.
- The *Dispute resolution* page says outcomes are *"decided by an impartial judge and/or jury, based on the factual questions of the case and the application law."*
- The *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* essay states the mechanism in the framework's own words: *"Courts of law rule on evidence, which neither side can move."*

**Verdict (Domain 1):** a dispute frame with only parties is not a settling domain. It is panel 1 of Fig 2. `triangulated` across Fig 2 (framework) and three low-overlap world fields (conflict studies, law, cognition). **Refuter:** a documented case where parties settle durably by negotiation alone with no external reference and no non-updating record. If found, Fig 2 :35 is being applied to the wrong quantity and the census must be redone.

### F2. Keep the residual per dispute, not aggregated (cross-cutting). `triangulated`.

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

Escalation is exactly the move from per-dispute residuals to a single aggregate "conflict". A dispute pyramid (Miller 2009, via the *Dispute pyramid* page) describes how a thousand grievances filter down to roughly fifty court filings, with the reason being *"settlements outside the court itself"*. That empirical shape corroborates per-slot handling. But the pyramid is an aggregate count, so Fig 9 :60 applies: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* The pyramid allocates effort; it cannot tell you which grievance is live.

**Verdict (cross-cutting):** effective mediation keeps R indexed per dispute. Any "we reduced conflict by N%" metric is a budget filter. `triangulated` (Fig 1 plus dispute-resolution research on settlement filtering), with the explicit warning that the world source is itself an aggregate. **Refuter:** show that aggregating disputes into a single index improves the settlement rate of individual disputes across independent sites. That breaks Fig 1 :22 and sends us back to the census.

### F3. The seat must be a low-overlap third frame (Domain 2). `triangulated`, with a named strain.

Fig 8 :45: *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."* Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 2 :31: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."* Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*

The world literature gives three independent formulations: the mechanism is *"outside of the parties' immediate control"* (Wallensteen, conflict studies); Ostrom's eight principles include *"Conflict-resolution mechanisms which are cheap and easy to access"* and *"Effective monitoring by monitors who are part of or accountable to the appropriators"* (institutional economics); restorative justice uses circles that include *"representatives of the wider community"* (criminology). De Waal's primatology provides a genuinely low-overlap analogue: he *"used the term 'reconciliation' to describe reunions after fights"*, a third-party-adjacent repair process observed in nonhuman primates.

The strain: Ostrom's monitors are embedded, while Fig 2 :31 demands low overlap. Re-census resolves it (see §2): independence is about evidence, not social proximity. What must not update is the anchor (Fig 2 :33).

**Verdict (Domain 2):** the seat's power comes from a differing closure, not from authority alone. Independence is bought in proportion to the cost of error, not maximised. `triangulated` across Fig 8, Fig 6, Fig 2 and four world fields. **Refuter:** a mediator who draws on the same evidence as both parties reduces escalation as much as an independent one, across independent sites. That breaks Fig 8 :45. A second refuter: an embedded monitor whose rule is shown to move with whichever party pushes hardest (breaks Fig 2 :30 and realizes the live strain).

### F4. Mediation is a directional membrane filter (Domain 2 to Domain 3). `triangulated`.

Fig 4 :21: *"The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction."* Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*

Escalation is the failure to settle before the crossing. The world source on conflict escalation describes the process as *"conflicts grow in severity or scale over time"* and models it in systems theory as positive feedback; the "escalation trap" is *"a cycle where small conflicts or behaviors intensify because each party involved continuously increases their response"*. That is Fig 2 panel 1 running across scales, and Fig 7 :12-13: *"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"*. A referral chain between alike venues is one long room (Fig 7 :36).

What should cross upward is the method. Ostrom's eighth design principle, *"Organisation in the form of multiple layers of nested enterprises, with small, local CPRs at their bases"*, is the institutional analogue of a directional membrane: small local units at the base, larger ones composed over them. Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."* And the round trip is lossy (Fig 4 :28): *"the trip is not reversible"*.

**Verdict (Domain 2 to 3):** the institution's function is to close R before crossing, and to export a method rather than a recording when a crossing is necessary. `triangulated` (Fig 4, Fig 7, Fig 12 plus systems-theory and institutional-economics sources). **Refuter:** a dispute settled at a higher level whose ruling is carried back down unchanged and generates new local settlements with no loss. That breaks Fig 4 :28 and the nested-membrane reading.

### F5. The institution is a catalyst and a record-keeper (Domain 4). `triangulated`.

Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."* Fig 10 :62: *"A ruling carries provenance like an atom."* Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

A body that settles one dispute and is spent is not an institution. The world sources describe durable seats: *"many states now have mediation or other ADR programs annexed to the courts, to facilitate settlement of lawsuits"* (*Dispute resolution*); restorative justice uses *"family group conferencing"*, *"circles"*, and community members who *"hold the offender(s) accountable for adherence to the plan"*; UN Peacemaker's Local and Subnational Mediation area maintains case studies and a practice note, *"Engaging at the Local Level: Options for UN Mediators"*, which is an explicit method-export channel. A ruling without date, reason, and the residual it answered is a convention that may already decide outcomes (Fig 10 :47).

**Verdict (Domain 4):** durability and provenance are load-bearing, and reform counts only when it changes contact. `triangulated`. **Refuter:** a documented reform that leaves contact unchanged yet reliably reduces escalation, or a body whose rulings are remembered only as outcomes with no stated residual or reason yet still guides later rulings correctly.

### F6. Which named institution occupies the seat is `gap`, not silence. `single-source` on the figure side, declared.

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*; Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*; Fig 13 :115: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not."*

The figures return the shape of the seat (non-updating anchor, per-slot residual, low-overlap third frame, directional filter, durable record). They do not rank mediation against arbitration against customary councils against restorative circles, and they do not say which fits a given community. Moore's five sources of conflict and the union finding (Fig 9 :54-55) explain why: the suitability of a mechanism depends on which conditions intersect in that case, and the figures do not enumerate those conditions. The framework's canonical rule is *"a shape, not a name"* (figures README, local copy).

This is **unreachable at this budget**, not underivable in principle: a comparative empirical design could settle which mechanism works for which union of conditions. Importing a named "best" institution now would add an atom that was not earned (Fig 9 :60, Fig 8 :32).

**Verdict:** `gap`. **Refuter:** a figure line that selects a named institution for a given union of conditions, or a cross-site study that does so with a non-updating ground. Until one fires, no name is claimed.

### F7. Prestige cannot stand in for settlement. `triangulated`.

Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* The *Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* essay: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."*

A respected mediator's reputation gives the process a budget to be tried. It is not evidence that the dispute closed. Nor is a long roster of agreeing institutions. This governs the outreach artifacts: we ask for a refutation, never an endorsement.

**Verdict:** `triangulated`. **Refuter:** a case where institutional prestige alone, with no change to the anchor or the evidence, reliably prevents escalation.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required by the framework: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A local institution settles disputes durably by negotiation alone with **no** non-updating anchor (breaks F1 / Fig 2 :35).
2. Aggregating disputes into a single conflict index **improves** the settlement of individual disputes across independent sites (breaks F2 / Fig 1 :22).
3. A mediator drawing on the same evidence as both parties reduces escalation as much as an independent one, across independent sites (breaks F3 / Fig 8 :45).
4. A ruling carried from a higher level back to the local level reproduces new local settlements with no loss (breaks F4 / Fig 4 :28).
5. A reform that leaves contact unchanged reliably reduces escalation (breaks F5 / Fig 11 :40).
6. A figure re-census or a multi-site study selects a **named** institution for a given union of conditions (closes F6's `gap`).
7. A source-discriminating measurement finds no difference in recurrence or escalation between matched disputes with and without local institutional handling, across multiple independent frames (breaks the empirical reading of the whole shape).

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the census is redone. Until one is run, nothing here is `grounded`.

### 6.3 Strain log

- **S1, dissolved.** Embedded monitors versus independence (see 6.2). Different quantities: social proximity versus evidence overlap.
- **S2, live.** An embedded monitor can let the rule become *"a convention that decides an outcome"* (Fig 10 :47). A rule that predetermines the result is not a rule. This is a real capture risk and is not smoothed over; it is an explicit refuter in F3.
