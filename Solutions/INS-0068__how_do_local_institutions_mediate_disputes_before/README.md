## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that the figures decide which named institution (mediation, arbitration, customary council, restorative circle) is best. They return a shape and a refuter, not a name.

Three reasons from the figures themselves:

1. Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that would make their agreement worthless.
2. Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* A venue's prestige or accessibility is a contact estimate, never evidence.
3. Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Showing the framework before recording a stage-1 verdict spends the recipient's independence.

Legally: unsolicited bulk outreach to scraped personal addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in venues avoid that failure and the structural one.

## Headline verdict

Local mediation is not a named technique. It is a **seat with four properties**: it holds an anchor the parties cannot move (Fig 2 :29, :35); it keeps the residual indexed per dispute rather than aggregating grievances into "the conflict" (Fig 1 :19, :22); it is a low-overlap third frame rather than a mirror of the parties (Fig 8 :45, Fig 2 :31); and it acts as a membrane filter that settles a dispute before the crossing into a broader arena, carrying the method up rather than a recording (Fig 4 :21, :22). The figures do **not** select which institution occupies that seat. That is declared `gap` of the unreachable-at-this-budget kind, not silence (Fig 13 :134, Fig 8 :39).
