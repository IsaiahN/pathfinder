# Expert verification packet: the Tether on local dispute mediation

**What this is:** a claim in a form a dispute-resolution researcher or practitioner can check. Mode, derivation, and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the mechanisms, not `grounded`.** No measurement of recurrence or escalation is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status:** simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

An effective local institution has four properties: it ranges the parties against a reference neither can move; it keeps each dispute as its own slot rather than aggregating grievances; it draws on evidence that does not largely overlap with the disputants' evidence; and it filters at the boundary between local and broader scales, carrying a method upward and leaving a provenanced ruling. The framework does not name a winning institution. Mediation, arbitration, customary councils, and restorative circles are candidate bindings onto held terms, not derivations. What is offered is a checkable shape, refuters written in advance, and the instrument that would close it: a source-discriminating comparison that distinguishes mediation from disputes cooling on their own.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1. Framework-internal and world-facing; checkable by reading Fig 2 and by inspecting a real process

- **Statement:** a dispute frame with only parties cannot close against a movable reference. Triangulation against an anchor is required.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Also Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* And Fig 2 :30: *"you push on it, it is not an anchor."*
- **World corroboration:** the *Conflict resolution* page describes mechanisms as *"independent procedures in which the conflicting parties can have confidence"*, and reports Wallensteen's sixth mechanism as done through *"a process outside of the parties' immediate control"*. The interest-based relational approach says to *"insist on using objective criteria"*.
- **Refuter:** a documented case of durable settlement by negotiation alone with no external reference the parties cannot move, across independent sites. If found, Fig 2 :35 is being applied to the wrong quantity and the census must be redone.

### C2. Cross-cutting; checkable against any dispute-resolution dataset

- **Statement:** effective mediation keeps the residual indexed per dispute. Aggregating disputes into a single conflict index is a filter for the budget, never a witness for a verdict.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Also Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* And Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
- **World corroboration and its limit:** the dispute pyramid describes grievances filtering down to filings, with *"settlements outside the court itself"* as the reason. That corroborates per-slot handling, but the pyramid is itself an aggregate. It allocates effort; it cannot decide which grievance is live.
- **Refuter:** show that aggregating disputes into one index improves the settlement of individual disputes across independent sites. That breaks Fig 1 :22 and sends us back to the census.

### C3. Institutional; checkable against the design of a mediation body

- **Statement:** the seat's surplus comes from a differing closure and a directional filter. Independence is sufficient, not maximal; what must not update is the anchor. The institution must be a catalyst that survives its own use and leave a provenanced ruling.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :45: *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."* Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 4 :21: *"The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction."* Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."* Fig 10 :62: *"A ruling carries provenance like an atom."*
- **Live strain, declared:** Ostrom's design principles require monitors *"part of or accountable to the appropriators"*, which is high embeddedness. Re-census: independence governs evidence, embeddedness governs social proximity. The live risk is capture, Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."*
- **Refuter:** a mediator drawing on the same evidence as both parties reduces escalation as much as an independent one across independent sites; or a reform that leaves contact unchanged reliably reduces escalation (Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*); or an embedded monitor whose rule moves with whichever party pushes hardest.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a ranking of mediation, arbitration, customary councils, or restorative circles. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*; Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*. The named-institution question is a declared `gap`.
- Not that any institution has been measured to reduce escalation.
- Not that prestige, reputation, or a roster of endorsing bodies validates the shape. Prestige funds a trial; it does not settle.
- Not that a dispute pyramid or conflict index is evidence for the verdict. It is a budget filter.
- Not that one mechanism fits every union of conditions. Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not an argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. **Recurrence test:** matched disputes with and without local institutional handling, recurrence measured at the same interval, across at least two frames with no shared evidence pool.
2. **Anchor capture test:** can a party move the rule or record by pushing? If yes, it is not an anchor.
3. **Per-slot test:** individual handling versus an aggregate conflict index, on the same grievances.
4. **Membrane test:** does an exported ruling carried back down retain its constraints, or is the round trip lossy (Fig 4 :28: *"the trip is not reversible"*)?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29-30 and :35, Fig 1 :19 and :22, Fig 8 :45, Fig 4 :21, and Fig 10 :47. Eight short passages.
2. Check C1 and C3 against your own knowledge of a mediation or customary process: is there an actual reference neither side can move, or is the "neutral" seat a mirror of the parties?
3. Check C2 against any dataset you know: does the field's own reporting aggregate grievances into a conflict index, and what does that index hide?
4. Name one instrument that would discriminate mediation from cooling on its own, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
