## 1. A shape is a constraint on an answer, not the answer

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 :133: "could this have been reached at all. A system with a closure can answer that before it searches, and what it returns is a shape rather than a name."

From *The Library of Babel*, opened this session: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question." The same essay: "With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."

So for this question a **shape** is the narrowed form of a discriminator that can be stated before we can name a specific test. It constrains any admissible test; it does not supply one. The four conditions in `01` are a shape. "Use LiveBench with a 10-point threshold" would be a name, and no figure line licenses it.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary already held that is closest to the thing, "connect and correlate combinations of the existence of your known words and their relative proximity to commonly recurring new terms", and recurse until the set returned is small and descriptive.
- Fig 6 :30: "outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."
- Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect" / "rather than the cause".
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*, opened this session: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."

Applied here: describe the effect we are trying to discriminate (a behaviour that was not reachable before, against a ground that does not move), not the cause we suspect (scaling, extra data, a new architecture). Then choose a frame. Fig 8 :53: "Residual first, frame second."

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape:

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." Here, a non-updating task in a reproduced habitat pays or does not.
2. **A provenanced import from a differing frame** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :51), with a source gate and a shadow test. Here, an atom or method entering the model's closure from a frame whose closure differs, recorded as such.

*The Hard Problem is About Source*, opened this session, gives the instrument rule: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information" and "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it." The essay's example is resolved "not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument", and "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists." Our separator is owed as an instrument, not as a verdict.

## 4. Training on traces is not the same as contact

*Decoding Tacit Knowledge* states the point that bears most directly on this question: "Training on it reproduces 'the traces the thought left, not the thought', with enough structure to rebuild the appearance of reasoning." And: "Sounding right isn't the same as being checked against the world, which is why I call the Turing test a test of fluency, not of accuracy."

Fig 11 :40 names the structural version: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 11 :70: "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability." A model trained on the written record of human reasoning has context and some subtext from that record and almost none of the pretext, to use the essay's terms. Fluency on a benchmark made from the same record is not a discriminating reading of capability.

This is a mechanism-shape corroboration from the framework's own author. It shares the figure evidence pool completely and is not an independent witness.

## 5. Q: does "shape, not a name" forbid naming a candidate capability? CORRECTION TO MY OWN FIRST PASS

**First pass.** I read the rule as forbidding any named capability, so that even saying "this looks like tool use" would be a framework violation.

**Correction, kept visible.** The README's phrase is *"what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Its subject is a primitive **not held in Γ**. This matches the exemplar's §5b correction. "Tool use", "recursion", and "planning" are not new primitives being invented; they are bindings onto atoms and bonds the library already holds, or imports with provenance. Naming one is allowed as a `specified` binding, with its source recorded. What is forbidden is claiming the framework derived a name it cannot derive, or reporting a binding as a new atom.

**Not retracted.** The limit still governs this question, because no figure line names the discriminating test. The gap in `01` F7 stands. The correction narrows the rule; it does not remove it. Fig 13 :135: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

## 6. Why a benchmark average is a projection, not a source reading

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the metamerism case: an instrument reads green and "cannot comment on the source". Several underlying causes create identical readings, so "behavior is a projection with lossy information". The spectrometer, not an argument, resolved it.

Here the reading is a benchmark score and the sources are at least two: more search inside the closure, or a changed closure through import, or a changed contact in the habitat. These produce the same score. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument, per condition 3 and 4 of `01`, not a better argument about what the score "really" means.

## 7. Prestige is not action, and my own audit is a simulation

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, opened this session: "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly." And: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."

A claim backed by famous labs or a large agreement pool is funded, not settled. Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

Same essay on simulation: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post. It will often be better than the generic outputs that you will usually get otherwise too. However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." *This folder is that simulated output.* `01`, `06`, `07`, `08`, `09`, and `11` are checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are not a ground reading and they cannot settle whether any system has a new capability.

## 8. What shape means here, in one paragraph

A shape is a checkable constraint on any acceptable discriminator, stated before a test is chosen. For this question the constraint has four parts: read the residual per task slot against a ground that was not in training; decide whether the new behaviour is composable from the old atom set and record any import; require a change of contact in a reproduced habitat rather than a harness solve; and require an anchor that does not update plus a low-overlap evaluator. The framework does not name the benchmark, the threshold, or the model. Where it cannot name, it records the gap and stops, so the lineage stays checkable.
