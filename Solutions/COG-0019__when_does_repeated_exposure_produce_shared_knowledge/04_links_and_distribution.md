## What in the wider literature bears on this question

1. **Convergence without accuracy is a measured phenomenon.** Lorenz et al. (2011) is the closest empirical match to the figure's Fig 7 law: exposure to other people's estimates narrows diversity *"without improving its collective error"* and increases confidence. That is the mirror chain, quantified. It corroborates F3 and it refuses the common-sense claim in the question.
2. **Convergence under ambiguity, not under a fixed ground.** Sherif's autokinetic study (reported in the Conformity page) produced convergence precisely because the stimulus was ambiguous and there was no anchor to range against. Conformity in a variable environment impaired performance, in a stable one it did not. That is the two-condition shape in experimental clothing.
3. **Divergence from identical machinery is expected.** Dual inheritance theory lists conformist bias, rarity bias and cultural drift from errors in recall; animal culture shows 39 of 64 measured behaviour categories differing between chimpanzee communities. Same learning capacity, different local histories, different traditions. That corroborates F4.
4. **Repeated retelling reconstructs rather than copies.** Bartlett's transmission chain method shows material transformed to fit the receiver's schema. That is F2's warning about recordings at the level of a single mind.
5. **Public versus private transmission changes the epistemic status.** Common knowledge research shows that a public announcement gives common knowledge while private transmission gives only mutual knowledge. This bears directly on what "shared" means and why a shared convention is not automatically a world verdict (F5).
6. **Group polarization is the divergence counterpart to the mirror chain.** The same evidence, read through different prior commitments, widens disagreement. That is Fig 11's valence-relativity at the group scale.

None of these sources overrides the figures. They are secondary corroboration, and the field overlap is declared in `10_source_ledger.csv`. Several of the framework essays share an author with the figures, so their agreement is expected and carries little independent weight.
