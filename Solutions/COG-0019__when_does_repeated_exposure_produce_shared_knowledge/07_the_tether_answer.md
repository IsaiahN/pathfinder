# The Tether's answer: When does repeated exposure produce shared knowledge, and when does it produce divergent interpretations?

**Mode: `specified` on the conditions, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading. No experiment in this package was run.

## 1. The residual it answers

Repeated exposure presents the same observation o′ to two or more frames over n exposures. The common claim is that more exposure produces more agreement and that agreement is knowledge. The residual is what makes a small gap between frames count as knowledge rather than drift, a shared source, or a convention, and what makes a large gap count as honest ranging rather than a broken channel.

The residual is a **union**, not one hard question. Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Its two conditions are independent frames and a non-updating ground, with a third ingredient, transmission in effect terms, and a carve-out for conventions. Single-lever answers clear only the region where one condition holds.

## 2. The shape

1. **Agreement is not the metric.** Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."** A mirror cannot correct because it returns what it was given. Convergence produced by frames updating on each other is coherent drift (Fig 2 :34), and convergence produced by a hidden common source is *"the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other"* (Fig 2 :21).

2. **Shared knowledge requires a non-updating anchor plus independence.** Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."** Fig 2 :31: **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."** Both are needed. Without the anchor, independence just preserves different errors. Without independence, the anchor is only one frame wearing two names. Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**

3. **What crosses must be a method, not a recording.** Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** Fig 4 :25: **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."** A description travels only if cast in effect terms (Fig 6 :30: *"Cast it in effect terms"*). Repeated exposure to a prior frame's conclusion propagates an occasion, not a method.

4. **Divergence is admissible while the residual is live.** Symbols table :25: **"Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge."** Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** The same exposure can help one residual and hurt another. Divergence is a failure only when the channel, not the ground, broke: Fig 10 :24, *"the ground does not decay, the channel does."*

5. **A shared convention is real but carries no world verdict.** Fig 2 :41: **"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."** Fig 10 :47: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."** Repeated exposure is exactly what produces shared meanings. The error is promoting that product to a claim about the world.

6. **Exposure count is a budget filter, never a witness.** Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."** Fig 8 :32: **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."** More repetitions inside the same closure is the SEARCHED question, asked first and cheapest, but unable to add a primitive.

7. **The framework returns a shape, not a number.** Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."** No critical exposure count is derivable here. Any such number would require a measurement this package does not contain.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that agreement proves knowledge. Agreement is a reading that cannot comment on its source (Fig 2 :40).
- Not that divergence is always a failed exposure. It can be the correct output of two different histories against a live residual.
- Not that there is a threshold number of repetitions. That would be a name where the framework returns a shape.
- Not that a shared convention is a world verdict. It is a product of a different kind.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by an experiment. The refuters are written; none has been fired.
- Not that the figures decide the empirical question about any particular group. They decide the shape an answer must have. The ground decides any instance.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating design, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. The corresponding questions here are:

- **Independence test.** Collect estimates privately before any feedback, then introduce only the estimates of others. Does diversity fall while accuracy stays flat? Lorenz et al. (2011) report exactly that: *"social influence triggers the convergence of individual estimates and substantially reduces the diversity of the group without improving its accuracy."*
- **Anchor test.** Add a stable external check that does not change with the group's opinions. Does convergence now track the check rather than the group mean? Sherif's autokinetic study converged only where the stimulus was ambiguous; the Conformity page reports that conformity impaired performance in a variable environment but not a stable one.
- **Transmission test.** Give one group a recording of a successful occasion and another a reapplicable method in effect terms. Which group adapts when the habitat changes? Fig 4 :22-25 predicts the method group.
- **Valence test.** Change the residual while holding the exposure fixed. Does the same item flip from benefactor to malfactor? Fig 11 :50 predicts it does.
- **Standing refuters from `01` §3:** mutual updaters whose agreement survives an independent instrument; identical-architecture frames with identical history and w diverging on the same residual; an exposure that adds a primitive inside a fixed closure; a convention that settles a world question without ground contact.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The paper *How a Society of Agents Would Work* states the empirical limit plainly: *"Nothing here has been measured for a society of Tether agents. Three tests against the ground would settle it."* The same limit applies here. The two-condition shape is stated, the discriminator is characterized, the refuters are written, and the stop is recorded. *"Indiscernibility is not identity"* (figures README): agreement between this simulated audit and the figures is internal consistency, not corroboration. A real outside frame with a different evidence pool is what the verification packet is for.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that repeated exposure produces shared knowledge. It says agreement is not the metric. Convergence counts as knowledge only when the people making the estimates stay independent and range against something that does not change with their opinions, and when what passes between them is a reapplicable method rather than a recording of one occasion. If they update on each other, the result is coherent drift; if they share one hidden source, the agreement is the pool talking. Where those conditions hold and the question is still open, divergence is the right output, not a defect, because the same exposure is read against different histories and different residuals. Repeated exposure is genuinely good at producing a shared convention, but a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one. Exposure count is a budget, never a witness. What this offers is the shape any candidate answer must have, four refuters written in advance, and the tests that would close it. That is a deliberately lossy summary; the full packet is the thing to check.
