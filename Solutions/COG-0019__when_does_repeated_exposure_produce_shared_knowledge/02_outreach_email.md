# Outreach draft: check the shape claim before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the two-condition claim and its refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are stated plainly. If the stage-1 output cannot survive a twenty-minute check, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one prediction about repeated exposure and agreement

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the common claim is that repeated exposure produces shared knowledge, and that more exposure means more agreement. The figures I work from split that claim into conditions. They say agreement is not the metric. The relevant lines are: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* and *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*
>
> The prediction is therefore a conjunction, not a lever:
>
> 1. Convergent estimates count as knowledge only if the people making them range independently against something that does not update on their opinions. If they update on each other, the result is coherent drift. If they share one hidden source, the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other.
> 2. What crosses between people has to be a reapplicable method, not a recording of one occasion. *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*
> 3. Where those conditions hold and the question is still unresolved, divergence is the correct output. Two systems with the same architecture can diverge because their histories and their weightings diverge. That is a prediction, not a defect.
> 4. Repeated exposure is genuinely good at producing a shared convention, which is agreement about meaning. That is a real product, but it carries no verdict weight about the world. A convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention.
>
> Exposure count itself is a budget, never a witness. More repetitions inside the same set cannot add a new element.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that there is a critical number of exposures, that agreement is proof, that divergence always means a failed intervention, or that any of this is measured here. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the source figures, not a ground reading.
>
> **What would refute it (written before this message):**
> 1. A group with no stable non-updating anchor, whose members only updated on each other, whose agreement later survives an independent instrument.
> 2. Two systems with identical architecture, identical histories, identical weighting and the same ground contact, diverging on the same question.
> 3. A case where more exposure inside a fixed set added a genuinely new primitive rather than visiting more of the same space.
> 4. A convention that settles a world question without ground contact, where the settlement survives an independent test.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - The verbatim figure text (local file, line numbered): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - The canonical usage rule (local file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step formula (local file): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - [name]

## Two-stage discipline

**Stage 1.** Send the four predictions and the refuter. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict **before** showing the framework.

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, send the three local figure links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40, *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*).

**Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode. A method crossing up is permitted; a recording is not (Fig 4 :22-25).
