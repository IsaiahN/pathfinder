# Expert verification packet: repeated exposure, shared knowledge, divergent interpretations

**What this is:** a claim in a form a specialist in social cognition or cultural transmission can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the conditions, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No experiment in this package was run. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Repeated exposure does not by itself produce shared knowledge. Convergence counts as knowledge only under two conditions: the frames making the estimates stay independent, and they range against a ground that does not update on their opinions. What crosses between them must be a reapplicable method in effect terms, not a recording of one occasion. Without those conditions, repeated exposure produces one of two counterfeits, coherent drift from mutual updating or agreement from a hidden shared source. Where the conditions hold and the residual is still live, divergence is the correct output, because identical architectures with different histories and weightings diverge. Repeated exposure also produces something real, a shared convention, but that is agreement about meaning and carries zero verdict weight about the world. Exposure count is a budget filter and never a witness.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 2 and Figure 7

- **Statement:** convergence produced by mutual updating or by a shared hidden source is not knowledge about the world; only independent ranging against a non-updating anchor is.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 - **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."** Fig 2 :31 - **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."** Fig 2 :35 - **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."** Fig 7 :35 - **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."** Fig 7 :13 - **"agreement compounds, evidence does not."**
- **Refuter:** find a group whose members only updated on each other, with no common ground contact and no shared external source, whose agreement later survives an independent instrument.

### C2 - world-facing; checkable against the experimental literature

- **Statement:** exposure to other people's estimates narrows the spread of answers without improving accuracy, and raises confidence. This is the empirical signature the figure calls coherent drift.
- **Source (world, secondary):** Jan Lorenz, Heiko Rauhut, Frank Schweitzer and Dirk Helbing, "How social influence can undermine the wisdom of crowd effect", PNAS 108(22):9020-9025, 2011, opened 2026-11-22: **"social influence triggers the convergence of individual estimates and substantially reduces the diversity of the group without improving its accuracy."** The same paper reports a confidence effect: convergence boosts confidence *"despite a lack of improvements in accuracy."* Secondary corroboration only; it does not decide the framework claim.
- **What to check:** is the experimental design (private first estimates, then aggregated or full feedback, with monetary incentives for accuracy) a fair test of the predicted distinction between independent ranging and mutual update? What would count against it?
- **Refuter:** show a controlled study in which exposure to others' estimates increases accuracy while holding the ground fixed, with independence measured rather than assumed. If that is the robust result, C2 is wrong.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation, with a declared analogue

- **Statement:** repeated exposures form an ordered sequence, not a symmetric set; the presence of a non-updating anchor fixes the kind of outcome, as a functional group fixes kind; and what crosses between frames must be a method rather than a recording.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :72 - **"A then B; B needs A to have happened."** Fig 12 :111 - **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."** Fig 12 :123 - **"A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** Fig 4 :22 - **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** Fig 4 :25 - **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."**
- **Analogue, not a derivation:** Fig 12 :117 - **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** This governs composition inside a library, not transmission between frames. It is used in `06_isomers_and_order.md` only as a declared analogue with its own falsifier. Do not treat it as the framework's answer to this question.
- **Refuter:** permute the order of exposures with no change in the resulting shared shape and no change in the library (kills the sequence claim); or remove the non-updating anchor and show the outcome kind is unchanged (kills the functional-group claim); or show that a verbatim recording adapts as well as a method in a changed habitat (kills the transmission claim).

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that there is a critical or sufficient number of exposures. No such number is derived.
- Not that agreement is proof, or that a large group is more reliable than a small independent one.
- Not that divergence is always failure. Divergence from different histories and weightings is predicted.
- Not that a shared convention is a world verdict.
- Not a theory of everything, and no claim that this framework is the only true one. A frame cannot certify its own limit (Fig 8 :37).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement in this packet settles any instance.
- Not an outside verification. This packet was written by the same simulated seat that wrote the audit. Its agreement with the figures is internal consistency.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating design, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. **Independence test.** Private first estimates, then feedback of others' estimates only. Does diversity fall while accuracy stays flat?
2. **Anchor test.** Add a stable external check that does not move with group opinion. Does convergence track the check rather than the group?
3. **Transmission test.** Recording versus reapplicable method, then change the habitat. Which group adapts?
4. **Standing refuters:** mutual updaters whose agreement survives an independent instrument; identical-history identical-w divergence on the same residual; an exposure that adds a primitive inside a fixed closure; a convention that settles a world question without ground contact.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29, :31, :35 and Fig 7 :13, :35. Five short passages.
2. Check C2 against your own knowledge of the conformity and social-influence literature. Is the distinction between convergence and accuracy fair?
3. Name one design that would discriminate triangulation from coherent drift in your own field, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
