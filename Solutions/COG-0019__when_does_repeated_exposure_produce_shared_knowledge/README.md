# Solution COG-0019

**Question:** When does repeated exposure produce shared knowledge, and when does it produce divergent interpretations?

**Source:** cognitive science.txt line 24 (question 19 in that file).

**Section:** 2. Perception, attention, and learning.

**Mode:** `specified` on the substantive conditions, `general` on the structural shape. Nothing here is `grounded`: no experiment in this package was run, and no measurement settles which condition held in any actual case.

**Status:** done.

**Registry status:** pending.

## The question restated as an effect

Repeated exposure presents the same observation o′ to two or more frames over successive exposures n. Each frame carries a library Γ, a belief b with a weighting w, and ranges against o′. The residual is per slot: R = |Γ(b,a) − o′|, and the comparison between frames is whether their updated predictions converge or separate. What is being decided is not whether people end up saying the same thing. It is what has to be present for sameness to be knowledge rather than drift, a shared source, or a convention, and what has to be present for difference to be honest ranging rather than a broken channel.

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction").** An answer is a list of conditions, each named in effect terms, each with a refuter, such that (i) if the condition holds, convergence is triangulation against a ground, and (ii) if it fails, convergence is agreement among frames and divergence may be the correct reading of an unresolved residual. The sought-for shape is therefore a two-condition conjunction with a convention carve-out, not a single lever and not a number of repetitions. Binding it this way makes the preference refusable by the same test as everything else.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the answer.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this package settles the question. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **Agreement is not the metric.** *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Selecting recipients for likely agreement manufactures the correlation that makes agreement worthless as a verdict.
2. **A room cannot anchor.** *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35). More people who already agree add vocabulary, not an anchor.
3. **The source must stay hidden from the verifier.** *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42); Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*

Legally: unsolicited bulk email to scraped personal addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in venues avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
