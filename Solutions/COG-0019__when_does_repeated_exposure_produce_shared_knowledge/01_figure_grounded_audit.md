## 0. The claim under audit

> Repeated exposure is sufficient for shared knowledge. More exposure means more agreement, and agreement is the mark of knowledge. Where interpretations diverge, the exposure failed.

This is the common-sense reading of the question, and it is the claim under audit. Its defect is that it treats agreement as a verdict. The figures forbid that move. They separate three things the claim conflates: agreement about the world, agreement about a shared meaning, and mere correlated update. The audit asks which of these repeated exposure can actually produce, and what else is owed for each.

The residual is stated in effect terms as required by the method.

> **R:** after n exposures of the same o′ to frames i and j, what drives |Γ_i(b_i,a) − o′| and |Γ_j(b_j,a) − o′| toward the same value, and what drives them apart? The object of explanation is not the size of the gap between people. It is what makes a small gap knowledge rather than drift.

Do not answer this with a number of repetitions. Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* Exposure count n is a budget. It never enters the verdict.

**Anchor check.** Does this residual have a nameable anchor? Yes, conditionally. The ground is listed as the anchor in Fig 2 :29, and the question is constitutively about what settles a shared claim. So this is a well-formed question, not yet a `gap`. But the anchor must be shown to be present in any given exposure design. If the repeated item is itself a frame's output, there is no anchor and the honest answer is that exposure cannot produce knowledge at all.

## 1. Domain census

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity.** A relation among at least two frames, one observation o′, and an exposure index n, read per slot s. A two-place reading (person, belief) loses the observation and the other frame and is rejected. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
- **Symmetry.** Exposure is symmetric across frames (the same o′ is presented), but the update is not symmetric under exchanging frames, because histories and w differ. Symbols table :25: *"Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge."* So the symmetry is in the input, not in the result.
- **Scale.** At n = 1 the loop is inside one frame. At n > 1 across people it crosses the membrane (Fig 4). "Shared" is a population-scale word. The residual is intrinsically cross scale, so a single-scale answer is already the wrong shape.

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | admitted? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1, contact inside one frame | one frame, percepts, Γ | residual R per slot | the observation o′, if it is stable | yes |
| D2, transmission across the membrane | sender, receiver, the artifact or utterance | method versus recording | none by itself | yes, as a filter with direction |
| D3, social alignment in the room | the frames, their estimates, influence | agreement, confidence | absent in collapses 1 and 2; present in Fig 2 panel 3 | yes, with two opposite verdicts |
| D4, ground and habitat | the world, conditions, relations, other actors | settlement, what pays | the ground, which does not update | yes |
| D5, convention and shared meaning | the group, a symbol or practice | coordination value | a declared meaning | yes, but zero world-verdict weight |
| X1, "the influential person" | a person | prestige | none independent of influence | no, not a domain |
| X2, "culture" as a superorganic thing | a category | structural constraint | none; it is a name for the pattern | no, absorbed into D3 and D4 |

X1 is excluded because influence is not an anchor: it moves when frames push on it (Fig 2 :29). X2 is excluded because capability is relational: Fig 11 :39, *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*

### 1.3 Partition or union

**Union, not partition.** There are at least two independent conditions: contact with a non-updating ground, and independence between frames. Exposure is a third ingredient that is necessary but not sufficient. Their overlap is a compound with its own loop. Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Therefore any answer of the form "exposure does X" is a single lever applied to a union, and single levers clear only the region where one condition holds. This is the structural heart of the answer.

### 1.4 Dependency order (Fig 3)

Perception (the exposure is registered and told apart from what was there) → vocabulary (frames can describe the residual in effect terms) → objective (what would count as shared knowledge, and what would count as progress) → planning (who is exposed to what, and in what order) → learn and carry (name what worked, verify it, carry it).

Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Almost all practical "repeated exposure" effort lands above the break: more impressions, more repetitions, longer campaigns. The link that usually breaks is the middle one, having terms for what the exposure affords and being able to say what would count as progress (Fig 3 :25-27). A group that cannot say what would count as a failure cannot be helped by more exposure.

## 2. Findings

### F1 - Repeated exposure does not make an anchor (D1)

Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**

An exposure supplies o′. It becomes an anchor only if it settles the slot and does not move when frames push on it. If the repeated item is a recording, a prior frame's conclusion replayed, it has no anchoring power. Fig 4 :25: **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."**

**Verdict per domain (D1): `specified`, single-source on the membrane claim, triangulated for the anchor rule across Fig 2 :29 and Fig 10 :24 (*"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*).** Verdict: exposure to a stable ground-bearing observation can anchor; exposure to a recording propagates an occasion.

**Refuter:** show a case where repeating a pure recording, with no ground contact at either end, produces a frame-stable naming of the slot that survives an independent instrument.

### F2 - What crosses between people is a method or a head start, not a recording (D2)

Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** Fig 4 :23: *"upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."*

A description travels only if it is cast in effect terms. Fig 6 :30: **"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."** And a frame cannot be entered by another frame's closure without contact: Fig 6 :23, *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*

**Verdict per domain (D2): `specified`, triangulated across Fig 4 and Fig 6 (two low-overlap mechanisms: the across-size membrane versus the within-size closure).** Verdict: repeated exposure transmits what was exposed. If what was exposed is a recording, the receiver repeats an occasion; if it is a reapplicable method in effect terms, the receiver can test it in its own habitat.

**Refuter:** demonstrate a case where a verbatim recording, carrying no reapplicable method and no effect-term description, crosses to a new frame in a new habitat and produces convergent ground-contacting behaviour there.

### F3 - Convergence by mutual update or common source is not knowledge (D3, decisive)

This is the central finding. The figures name two collapse modes and one legitimate mode.

- **Collapse 1, mutual update / coherent drift.** Frames update on each other. Fig 2 :14-15: *"they converge, and what they converge on is whoever had the most influence."* Fig 2 :34: *"the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."*
- **Collapse 2, common cause.** Frames never talk but share one evidence pool. Fig 2 :20-21: *"so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other."*
- **Legitimate, triangulation against a fixed anchor.** Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."** Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*

The room chain adds a second, independent statement of the same law. Fig 7 :13: **"agreement compounds, evidence does not"**; Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."** And Fig 7 :36: *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."*

**Verdict per domain (D3): `specified`, triangulated across Fig 2 (anchor geometry) and Fig 7 (handoff across rooms), two figures with low conceptual overlap.** Verdict: repeated exposure produces shared knowledge only when the frames range independently against the same non-updating ground. When they range against each other or against a hidden common source, it produces shared error with high confidence.

**Refuter:** find a group whose members only updated on each other, with no common ground contact and no shared external source, whose agreement later survives an independent ground instrument. Under the figures this cannot happen; if it does, the verdict fails.

### F4 - Divergent interpretations are not automatically a defect (D3, D4)

Divergence has three distinct sources, and only one is a failure.

1. **Different histories and weightings at equal architecture.** Symbols table :25: **"Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge."** The weighting is defined at Symbols :24: *"How far the belief leans on private history versus inherited prior."* This is a structural prediction of divergence, not a pathology.
2. **Valence is relative to the residual.** Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :50: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* The same exposure can be helpful to one frame and harmful to another because their residuals differ.
3. **A broken channel.** Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; Fig 10 :28: *"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch."* This is the genuine failure case.

**Verdict per domain (D3 and D4): `general` on the structural claim, `specified` on any particular attribution, triangulated across the Symbols table (weighting) and Fig 11 (habitat valence), two low-overlap sources.** Verdict: divergence is the honest sound of an unresolved residual unless the channel, not the ground, is what broke.

**Refuter:** show two frames with identical architecture, identical histories and identical w, with the same ground contact, diverging on the same residual. That would remove the mechanism the figures give and falsify this reading.

### F5 - Repeated exposure can produce a convention, and that is legitimate, but it is not a world verdict (D5)

Fig 2 :41: **"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."** So shared meaning is a real product of repeated exposure. The figures do not forbid it; they price it correctly.

But a convention that decides an outcome stops being one. Fig 10 :47: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."** And Fig 10 :18: *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one."* So if a community repeats a label until it counts as settled knowledge about the world, it has made a convention into a verdict, which is the named failure genus.

**Verdict per domain (D5): `specified`, triangulated across Fig 2 :41 and Fig 10 :18/:47 (valuation of agreement versus the seat's prohibition).** Verdict: shared knowledge in the weak sense, a shared convention, is exactly what repeated exposure is good at; it carries zero verdict weight about the world.

**Refuter:** produce a case where a convention, by being repeated, settles a world question without any ground contact and the settlement survives a later independent test.

### F6 - More exposure is a SEARCHED answer (cross cutting)

The three routes in Formula step 7 are distinct and must not be merged. Fig 8 :32: **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."** More exposures inside one closure is the SEARCHED question: is it in here and unfound? It visits more of the same space. It cannot add a primitive.

If the residual persists, the remedy is INWARD (sharpen an instrument already returning something, Fig 6 :32) or OUTWARD (import from a frame whose closure differs, Fig 6 :23), never simply more of the same. And underivable in principle and unreachable at this budget are different facts with different instructions (Fig 8 :40-43).

**Verdict: `general`, triangulated across Fig 8 :32 and Fig 9 :60.** Verdict: exposure count is a filter for the budget. It never becomes a witness for the verdict.

**Refuter:** show a case where increasing exposure count inside a fixed closure added a genuinely new primitive rather than visiting more of the same space.

### F7 - The framework returns a shape, and this answer is that shape (cross cutting)

Fig 9 :27-28: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."** Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."** So the correct output here is not a threshold number of exposures. It is the narrowed form of the design that could produce knowledge: independent frames, a non-updating ground, effect-term transmission, and a declared convention carve-out.

Formula step 2, "Direction": *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."* The shape was bound in the README before the figure lines were read. It is refusable by the refuter in §3.

**Verdict: `general`, single-source for the shape limit from Fig 9, confirmed by the canonical figures README.** Verdict: any answer that names a critical exposure count is a name where the framework returns a shape.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per Fig 7 and the Formula's Direction discipline: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The whole derivation is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A group with no stable non-updating anchor and only mutual updating produces agreement that survives contact with an independent ground instrument. (Breaks F3.)
2. Two frames with identical architecture, identical histories, identical w and the same ground contact diverge on the same residual. (Breaks F4.)
3. Increasing exposure count inside a fixed closure adds a new primitive rather than visiting more of the same space. (Breaks F6 and the idempotence law.)
4. A convention repeated enough settles a world question without ground contact, and that settlement survives an independent test. (Breaks F5.)

If any fires, the two-condition shape collapses and the answer is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

Note the discipline: these are written against the mechanism, not against the direction of the question. The Formula warns that *"Every check here runs on the MECHANISM and none on the DIRECTION OF THE QUESTION."* §3 is an attempt to bind the direction as an ordinary term so it can be checked too.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Describe the residual before designing the exposure. Ask what is supposed to become shared: a verdict, a convention, or a method (F7, Fig 9 :27-28).
2. Stop treating exposure count as evidence. It is a budget filter (F6, Fig 9 :60).
3. Build the design around two conditions, not one: independent frames and a non-updating ground. Their overlap is a compound that needs its own loop (Fig 9 :57).
4. Transmit methods in effect terms, never recordings (F2, Fig 4 :22, Fig 6 :30).
5. Keep the convention carve-out explicit so that shared meaning is not silently promoted to a world verdict (F5, Fig 10 :47).
6. When the residual persists, ask SEARCHED first, then INWARD, then OUTWARD, and keep the three distinct (Formula step 7).
7. Carry methods across scales, not occasions. Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*

## 6. Final verdict

The common-sense claim is refused. Repeated exposure produces shared knowledge only under a two-condition union: frames must range independently against a ground that does not update on their opinions, and what crosses between them must be a reapplicable method in effect terms rather than a recording. Where those conditions fail, repeated exposure reliably produces one of two counterfeits: coherent drift (frames updating on each other) or the pool talking (frames sharing one source). Where they hold but the residual is still live, divergence is the correct output, not a defect. Repeated exposure also produces something real and legitimate, a shared convention, but that is agreement about meaning and carries zero verdict weight about the world. Exposure count is a budget filter, never a witness. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, with a pre-registered refuter and an open instrument gap.
