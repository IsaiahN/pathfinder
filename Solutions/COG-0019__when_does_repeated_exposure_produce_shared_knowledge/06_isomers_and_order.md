# Supporting derivation: structure, order, and analogues

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question invites an analogy: the same exposure plus a different interpretation looks like the same ingredients arranged differently. Two supporting questions are owed.

1. Can "same exposure, different interpretation" be stated as an isomer claim under Fig 12, and what would falsify it?
2. What order do repeated exposures have, and does the order carry information the list of exposures does not?

A third question is forced by the strain log in `01`: the isomer line only fits transmission if exposure is treated as an atom and interpretation as a bond. The figures do not say that. So the first task is to re-census, not to force the fit.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

- **Atoms available.** The observation o′ presented on each exposure, the frame's prior library Γ, its history, and its weighting w. Symbols table :25: *"Two frames with identical architecture diverge because their histories and their w diverge."*
- **Bonds available.** The seven typed relations plus negation, Fig 12 :49: *"The seven bonds, and whether the order of the operands matters. Negation is the eighth operator and is not a bond."*
- **Transmission objects.** A method or a recording (Fig 4 :22-25). These are not atoms in Γ; they are what crosses between frames.
- **Excluded.** "Exposure" is not an atom. "Interpretation" is not a bond in the figure's sense. Assigning those roles was the S1 strain and is recorded as a re-census, not adopted as a derivation.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Is "same exposure, different interpretation" an isomer claim? STRAIN, RESOLVED BY RE-CENSUS

- Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :95-96: *"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart."*
- Fig 12 :114: *"having terms for what a situation affords was taken for a dictionary and it is a grammar."*

**Re-census.** Fig 12 governs composition *inside a library*: atoms joined by typed bonds. Transmission between frames is governed by Fig 4 and Fig 2. Therefore the isomer line does not, by itself, describe two people hearing the same story. What it can describe, at one remove, is the composition each frame builds out of the shared observation.

**The analogue, declared as such.** If two frames receive the same set of observations (same atoms) but compose them under different relations (different bond), their resulting libraries are different substances. This is a legitimate structural analogue, but it is a binding from the figure to a new domain, not a figure-derived result. Its status is `specified`, single-source (Fig 12 only), with no ground reading.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two frames exposed to the same atom set build the same bond arrangement and the same resulting substance, the isomer analogue fails; or if they build different substances from *different* atom sets, the case is not isomerism at all and the analogue must be withdrawn. A third failure: if the analogue predicts convergence where the atoms differ but the ground is shared, Fig 12 is being used outside its domain.

**Outcome of the strain:** kept only as an analogue, never as a primary finding. This is the S1 handling recorded in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §5.

### Q2 - What order do exposures have?

- Fig 12 :72: **"A then B; B needs A to have happened"** (sequence, ordered).
- Fig 12 :77: **"A produces B; B did not exist before"** (production, ordered).
- Fig 12 :59: *"swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No."* (conjunction, symmetric).

A list of exposures under conjunction has no order and carries no route. A list under sequence carries a route: earlier exposures change the library that later exposures are read into. That is a real structural difference, not a notation choice. Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**

Bartlett's transmission chain work (opened this session: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederic_Bartlett) shows the ordered effect directly. Material that does not fit a listener's schema is *"omitted from the recollection, or transformed into more familiar forms."* The order of retellings changes the substance even though the nominal content is the same story. The sequence carries information the ingredient list does not.

The repeated exposure sequence n is therefore a `→` chain, not a `+` set. Where someone reports only "the group saw the same thing N times," they have recorded ingredients and not arrangement. Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*

**Falsifier:** if the order of exposures can be permuted with no change in the resulting shared shape and no change in the frame's library, then the sequence reading is wrong and repetition is genuinely a symmetric conjunction.

### Q3 - Functional group: what fixes whether convergence is knowledge?

- Fig 12 :123: **"A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**

The presence of a non-updating ground plays the role of a functional group: it fixes the *kind* of outcome. A process with such a group is triangulation; the same process without one is drift. The qualifier, which ground and which instrument, says which member of the kind. This is why the question cannot be answered by looking at convergence alone: the group is invisible in the convergence reading, exactly as a functional group is invisible in an ingredient list.

**Falsifier:** remove the non-updating ground from a case of triangulation and show that the outcome kind does not change. If knowledge and drift are the same kind, the functional-group reading fails.

### Q4 - Cycle: why mutual exposure amplifies

- Fig 12 :121: **"The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**
- Fig 7 :13: **"agreement compounds, evidence does not"**

When frames update on each other's estimates, each frame's output becomes another's input. That is a cycle. Its sign is positive with respect to agreement (more agreement produces more agreement) and neutral with respect to ground contact. The loop closes on itself. Lorenz et al. (2011) measured the consequence: *"social influence triggers the convergence of individual estimates and substantially reduces the diversity of the group without improving its accuracy."* Group polarization is the divergent sibling: repetition and validation of statements *"increased by settings in which people repeat and validate each other's statements."*

**Falsifier:** introduce a term that actually reduces agreement when agreement exceeds ground contact (a negative feedback to the ground) and show that the two-condition shape does not predict the result. The figures give the sign test; they do not give the coefficient.

### Q5 - Round trip between one frame and the group

- Fig 4 :30: **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."**

Carry a claim from an individual frame to the group (T_A) and bring it back to a new individual (T_E). The two are not inverses. What the group can hold is a compressed method; what an individual needs is a head start plus its own test. Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* The gap R_T = gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x)) is what the compression dropped. In `11_audit.md` this gap is measured on this solution's own one-paragraph version.

**Falsifier:** show a round trip in which the method carried to the group and brought back reproduces the original situation exactly, with no loss. The figures assert the transform is lossy (Fig 4 :27-30); an exact round trip would break that claim.

## CONCLUSION

The isomorphism reading is a genuine strain and is not used as a primary finding: Fig 12 governs composition inside a library, not transmission between frames. It survives only as a declared analogue with a stated falsifier. What does hold structurally is this: repeated exposures are an ordered sequence, not a symmetric set; the presence of a non-updating anchor fixes the kind of outcome the convergence has; mutual updating is a positive-feedback cycle that compounds agreement without evidence; and the across-scale round trip is lossy, so what reaches a new frame is a head start, never a recording. Every one of these claims is `specified` and each has a falsifier above. None is grounded.
