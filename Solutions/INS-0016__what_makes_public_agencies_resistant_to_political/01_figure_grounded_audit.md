## 0. The residual, in effect terms

The question asks for a system with two properties at once:

- **R1, resistance:** political pressure P does not change the agency's decision D.
- **R2, responsiveness:** legitimate elected direction E does change D.

Stated as causes, R1 and R2 fight each other. Stated as effects, the contradiction is conditional: it holds only if P and E reach D on the **same channel**. The residual is therefore not "how much independence" but:

> **What separates the channel that must be closed from the channel that must stay open?**

This is an effect description, not a cause description, so it can travel to a frame without importing that frame's vocabulary (Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"*).

**Binding the sought-for shape as an ordinary term (formula, "Direction").** The shape I am searching for is: *a specification of an anchor, a correction channel that ranges against it, and a seat that maintains that channel, such that each part is separately refutable.* Before any figure line is chosen I bind that as a term `S`, so it can accrue a residual like anything else. If the derivation returns something that is not `S`, the preference has been falsified rather than silently preserved.

**What R is not.** It is not "should agencies be independent." That is a question in cause terms with a preferred answer. It is not "how do we make bureaucrats obey ministers." That already chooses the channel. Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."*

## 1. DOMAIN CENSUS (before any figure line is chosen)

### 1a. R by arity, symmetry and scale (Fig 9 :22, :46-48)

- **Arity:** two conditions, joined. R relates a decider, two would-be influencers (P and E), and an outcome. Two influences on one decision is a seam, not two independent questions.
- **Symmetry:** **asymmetric**. P is a pressure to be refused; E is a direction to be compared against. A refusal is a negation (¬), a direction is a comparison (⋛). They are not interchangeable, and a design that treats them symmetrically (for example "balance independence and accountability" as if they were weights on one scale) has already lost the distinction that matters. Fig 12 :112: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one. A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."*
- **Scale:** **first order** inside a single decision (does this case get decided on the law or on a phone call?), and **second order** in the design of the checking arrangement (who maintains the channel, and who checks them?). Conflating the two is the error Fig 3 names: effort at one level cannot rescue a break at another.

### 1b. One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor (nameable?) | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1, the decision frame** | caseworker, citizen or firm, the statute and the facts of the case | correctness of the decision, i.e. whether it follows law and fact | **the public outcome the law is constitutively about**, plus the case facts. Yes, nameable. | a domain |
| **D2, the political frame** | elected leaders, political appointees, party and interest networks | votes, appointments, influence, short-run electoral gain | **the same public outcome**, to which elected leaders are also answerable; and separately, elections. Yes, nameable. | a domain |
| **D3, the checking arrangement / ground maintainer** | legislature, courts, auditors, inspectors general, professional peers, the public | conventions, verdicts, provenance, reason giving | **the reachable ground**, kept reachable by a seat that does not author it. Yes, nameable (Fig 10 :29). | a domain |
| **D4, the compound, D1 ∩ D2** | the case where P and E arrive together through one person or one channel | ambiguous: the same input is simultaneously pressure and direction | the anchor is the same as D1, but the channel is shared, so the anchor cannot correct both separately. Nameable as a failure class. | a compound with its own loop |

No anchor, no domain. D4 qualifies because its anchor is nameable and its failure is specific: a shared channel makes the anchor unable to correct the two frames separately.

### 1c. Partition or union?

This is a **union**, not a partition. R1 and R2 overlap in D4. Fig 9 :54-56:

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**

Consequence: a single lever such as "grant more independence" clears the region where only resistance is missing and leaves D4 standing. In D4 the second condition still binds. The intersection is not a harder case of either condition. It is a specific compound (Fig 9 :57-58: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*).

### 1d. Order by dependency (Fig 3)

> **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** (Fig 3 :19)
> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."** (Fig 3 :20-21)

Order:

1. **State what counts as a correct outcome** (the anchor). Without this, the agency has no terms for what the situation affords, and no way to say what would count as progress. This is the middle link of Fig 3 that has "neither" an obvious failure nor an obvious test (Fig 3 :25-27).
2. **Separate the two channels** (which frame the agency ranges against in which decision). This presumes step 1.
3. **Maintain the channel** (D3). This presumes step 2, because there is nothing to maintain until there is a channel with a direction.

Most real reform lands at steps 2 and 3 because those have instruments. Step 1 is where the break usually is, and "a link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at" (Fig 3 :27).

## 3. Findings F1 to F10 (verbatim lines before conclusions)

### F1 - The anchor must be something that does not move when a politician pushes (governs D1 and D2)

Fig 2 :29-30:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."**

Fig 2 :33-34:

> **"The anchor must not update."**
> **"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."**

An agency whose standard of correctness is "what the current political lead wants" has no anchor. Its decisions update on the political frame, and the two drift together coherently. Coherent drift is exactly why capture is hard to see from inside: every participant can give a locally sensible account. The counterfactual that separates capture from correctness is whether the answer changes when the same push is applied. Fig 2 :45-46:

> **"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."**
> **"The anchor is not a third party to the disagreement and it is not the tie-breaker in a vote. Each frame is corrected by it separately, and what makes the correction sound is that neither frame can move it."**

So resistance and responsiveness can share an anchor without sharing a channel: the agency and the elected leaders are each corrected by the same public outcome, separately, and neither can move it. The legitimate elected will enters as the standing law and the mandate (which define what the anchor is for this question), not as an instruction in a particular case.

**Legitimacy constraint.** Fig 2 :36: **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."** A private outcome, an agency's own budget, or its reputation cannot be the anchor for a question about public administration. This is what disqualifies an agency that has become a ground with a reputation to protect (Fig 10 :93: *"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."*).

**Verdict for D1 and D2:** `triangulated` across Fig 2 (anchor and triangulation) and Fig 11 (contact, below) with low overlap: one figure governs the reference, the other governs what makes a system capable. Refuter: show a standard that settles the question yet changes its answer when a frame pushes on it, and still yields correct outcomes.

### F2 - Resistance is a refusal, responsiveness is a comparison (governs R's asymmetry)

Fig 12 :112 (quoted in §1a) and Operators table :52: *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one."*

R1 is a ¬ on influence: *this does not get to decide the case*. R2 is a ⋛: *does the agency's action meet the standard the elected legislature set?* A vocabulary that has only "more independence" or "more responsiveness" as quantities on one line cannot write either operator correctly. It can name a state and cannot name a route to one.

**Verdict:** `single-source` for the operator distinction (Fig 12 plus Operators table are one framework document, not two low-overlap witnesses). Refuter: exhibit a design in which refusing pressure and meeting a goal are the same operation and no information is lost.

### F3 - The compound D4 must be unbundled, not balanced (governs the union)

Fig 9 :54-58 quoted in §1c. The practical consequence: "more independence" and "more oversight" are both filters. Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."** A governance index or an independence score is a budget filter: it says what gets tried first. It cannot say that the compound is resolved.

**Verdict:** `triangulated` across Fig 9 (union) and Fig 3 (order by dependency), which have low overlap. Refuter: show that a single lever clears the intersection where the same person is both the legitimate direction and the illegitimate pressure.

### F4 - Interference usually degrades the channel, not the ground (governs D3)

Fig 10 :24-28:

> **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**
> **"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"**
> **"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"**
> **"→ keep the anchor reachable"**

An agency can keep a perfect statute and still lose contact with the outcome it exists to produce. Political interference often works by degrading the channel: a metric becomes saturated, readings are pooled across cases, a report is dropped, and the agency's output stops being a verdict about the ground. The repair is to keep the anchor reachable, not to change the law again.

Fig 10 :50-51: **"Looking inside the frame it grades is how the seat stops being outside. A seat that reads that interior acquires the frame's model, and thereafter grades reports against its own picture rather than against the ground."**

**Verdict:** `single-source` (Fig 10). Refuter: show a case where contact with the ground is unchanged but capability changes, which would break Fig 11 :40 as well.

### F5 - A formal rule that decides an outcome is not a neutral convention (governs D3)

Fig 10 :46-49:

> **"A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do. The seat may author what has no truth value and nothing that does."**
> **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**
> **"a convention nothing can check is a constant the seat authored, carrying the seat's authority"**
> **"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."**

A tenure rule, a merit rule, or a consultative procedure is a convention only while it carries no claim about who wins a particular case. The moment it settles the outcome, it is an authored constant wearing a convention's clothes. This is the failure mode of "we followed the procedure" when the procedure was itself the interference.

Fig 10 :53: **"And a prohibition nothing can satisfy is a prohibition stated wrong."** A rule that would require an agency to have no stake at all describes no real occupant. Fig 10 :58-60:

> **"A seat with no stake at all does not exist."**
> **"The condition is that nothing about which value the ground returns today serves the occupant: an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."**
> **"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."**

**Verdict:** `single-source` (Fig 10). Refuter: show a formal rule that decides outcomes, cannot be checked by anyone outside, and still functions as a checkable convention.

### F6 - Independence is a property of the channel and habitat, not a virtue of the agent (governs D3 and the whole design)

Fig 11 :39-40:

> **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**
> **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

Training, pay, or exhortation aimed at the agency alone cannot produce resistance or responsiveness unless it changes what the agency is in contact with and who can move its readings. This is why the answer is a design of channels and seats, not a prescription of character.

Fig 11 :49-51: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries. The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another."* A political appointee is not a fixed hazard; whether they are a benefactor depends on which channel they are on for this residual.

**Verdict:** `triangulated` across Fig 11 (capability as contact) and Fig 10 (channel decay), low overlap: one states the positive condition, the other the failure mode. Refuter: an agent-only improvement that demonstrably moved the ground.

### F7 - Agreement with elected leaders is not the mechanism (governs D2)

Fig 2 :35 and :39-41:

> **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**
> **"Correlation has valence."**
> **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**
> **"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"**

An agency that mirrors the elected leadership has not become responsive; it has joined the frame it was supposed to be checked against. But agreement is not always a defect: when the product is a shared meaning, for example a commonly understood statutory term, agreement is the product. The valence depends on whether the agreement is being used as a verdict about the world or as a convention. This is the precise place where "responsiveness" is often smuggled in as "agreement," which is why the distinction must be stated on every substantive claim.

**Verdict:** `triangulated` across Fig 2 (mirror and correlation) and Fig 7 :35 (*"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*). Refuter: show a mirror that corrects, or a verdict whose agreeing frames share no evidence pool yet whose agreement carries no information.

### F8 - Oversight only helps when its closure differs (governs D3)

Fig 7 :36: **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."** Fig 2 :42: **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."**

Adding oversight bodies that read the same reports and share the same evidence pool does not buy a second witness. It buys more spelling (Fig 6 :53: *"searchers drawing on the same evidence are one searcher wearing many names"*). The second seat must see something the first cannot see, which is why the seat is defined by what it cannot see (Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*).

**Verdict:** `triangulated` across Fig 7 and Fig 2, low overlap. Refuter: a chain of identical rooms that produces a ground reading.

### F9 - Multiple grounds are a union, and the framework does not name the institution (meta finding)

The political frame has two grounds: elections answer "who should govern", and public outcomes answer "did government work". The agency is pinned to the latter. If those two grounds diverge, "responsiveness to elected leaders" can fail even though the agency is perfectly pinned to the public outcome. The figures do not resolve which ground governs when they conflict. This is not a figure conflict. It is the union of §1c arriving again at a higher scale, and it is recorded as a strain in §7 rather than solved by preference.

Also: the framework returns a shape, not a name. Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."** So the derivation below does **not** name merit recruitment, fixed terms, an independent budget, a statutory mandate, or central-bank instrument independence as *the* answer. Those are bindings from world frames, with their own provenance. They are candidate imports, and Fig 6 :23 applies: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*

**Verdict:** `triangulated` for the shape limit (Fig 9 plus canonical figures README); the two-grounds divergence is a `gap` at this budget.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required by seat reasoning: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device. The shape claim `S` is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Anchor test fails.** A standard of correctness is exhibited that settles the question, changes its answer when a frame pushes on it, and still produces correct outcomes. This breaks Fig 2 :29-30 and :33.
2. **Separate correction fails.** A case is exhibited where the agency's channel to the ground and the political channel are the same channel, yet both R1 and R2 hold. This breaks Fig 2 :45-46 and Fig 10 :24.
3. **Contact test fails.** An improvement to the agent alone, with no change in contact or habitat, demonstrably moves the ground. This breaks Fig 11 :40.
4. **Convention test fails.** A formal rule that decides outcomes and cannot be externally checked still functions as a checkable convention. This breaks Fig 10 :47-48.
5. **Union test fails.** A single lever is shown to clear the intersection where the same person is both legitimate direction and illegitimate pressure, with the second condition no longer binding. This breaks Fig 9 :55-58.

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn and the question is re-censused. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

**A note on cost and reversibility.** The cost if wrong is bounded: the packet is mode-labelled and states refuters before the conclusion, so an error is findable. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a recipe instead of a shape. Fig 2 :42 prices that: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* The claim is undoable, because the specific bindings are separable from the shape in `07_the_tether_answer.md`.

## 7. Strains, re-census and the honest stop

- **Fig 2 :33 (anchor must not update) versus law changes.** Re-census: the law is a binding of the anchor for a question; a change of law is a change of question, not the anchor updating on a frame. The distinction is real but fine. It needs an instrument that separates "the outcome changed" from "the preference changed" before it can be settled. Named as a strain, not hidden.
- **Two grounds in the political frame.** Elections and public outcomes are both grounds for elected leaders. If they diverge, "responsiveness" is underdetermined by the figures. This is a `gap` at this budget: "unreachable is a verdict, not a silence" (Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*). A source-discriminating instrument would be required, not another argument.
- **World-source conflicts.** Eijffinger and de Haan (CEPR, 2026) report that legal independence is necessary but not sufficient to prevent political pressure. Walters and Bolger (2019) find the procedural-justice effect on compliance weak and not reliably established in longitudinal data. Both conflicts send the derivation back to the census, not to a softer reading, and both support the union finding: a single lever cannot clear the intersection. They are recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` WORLD and in `10_source_ledger.csv`.
- **Round trip.** The condensation "pin the anchor, keep the channel, maintain it" drops at least three constraints: the union/unbundle requirement, the ¬ versus ⋛ operator distinction, and the convention rule. Treating the condensed formula as identical to the figure set would set `R_T` to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* The gap is enumerated qualitatively in `11_audit.md`.

**Verdict.** The shape survives: a union of two conditions that can share one anchor if the channels are separated; an anchor that does not move when pushed; a channel that decays even when the ground does not; and a checking seat that is outside what it grades. The specific institutions do not follow from the figures and are not claimed. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, candidate. This is internal consistency, not truth.
