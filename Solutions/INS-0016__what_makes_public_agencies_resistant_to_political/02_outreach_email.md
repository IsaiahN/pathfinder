**Subject:** One output on agency independence and responsiveness, with its refuter

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question in public administration: what makes a public agency resistant to political interference without making it unresponsive to elected leaders? I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output fails, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The two wanted properties look like a trade-off, but as effects they are a union of two conditions that can share one substrate. The condition for resistance is that the agency's standard of correctness does not change when a political actor pushes on it. The condition for responsiveness is that the elected will still sets that standard through law and mandate. The two can share an anchor: the public outcome the law is constitutively about, which the elected leaders are themselves answerable to. The agency and the elected leaders are each corrected by that anchor separately, and neither may move it. Resistance is a refusal of a particular channel; responsiveness is a comparison against a goal. They are not the same operation and they are not weights on one scale.
>
> The three design moves, in dependency order, are: state what counts as a correct outcome before adding any insulation; keep the correction channel to that outcome separate and intact; put the maintenance of that channel in seats that are outside what they grade, with declared stakes and a checkable record. The important negative finding is that independence is a property of the channel and habitat, not a virtue of the agency. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not naming merit recruitment, fixed terms, an independent budget, a statutory mandate, or central bank instrument independence as *the* answer. Those are bindings from other fields, each with its own provenance. The framework returns a shape, not a name. I am also not claiming that an agency should mirror elected leaders: agreement with a leader is not responsiveness, because a mirror cannot correct you. And I am not claiming a formal rule is neutral merely because it is written down: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim.** I wrote these down before sending this:
> 1. A standard of correctness that settles the question, changes its answer when a frame pushes on it, and still produces correct outcomes.
> 2. A case where the agency's channel to the ground and the political channel are the same channel, yet both resistance and responsiveness hold.
> 3. An improvement to the agency alone, with no change in contact or habitat, that demonstrably moves the ground.
> 4. A formal rule that decides outcomes, cannot be externally checked, and still functions as a checkable convention.
> 5. A single lever that clears the intersection where the same person is both legitimate direction and illegitimate pressure.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract the shape claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in this project.
> - Figures and formula, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures

>

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - [name]

## Stage order

1. **Stage 1:** send the shape claim and the five refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict **before** showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt in only:** if they are curious, offer the three links. The figures, not the summary, are the authority.
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

A stage 2 agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40). A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
