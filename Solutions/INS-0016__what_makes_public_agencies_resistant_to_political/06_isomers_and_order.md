# Unbundling the union, dependency order, and institutional isomers

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question bundles two conditions, resistance and responsiveness, that overlap in one compound: the case where the same person is both the legitimate direction and the illegitimate pressure. Two supporting questions are owed: (1) where does the dependency chain actually break when an agency fails at both properties, and (2) are different institutional arrangements variants of one kind (a functional group) or genuinely different substances (isomers)?

## DOMAIN CENSUS (reused from `01` §1, not replaced)

R: arity two conditions joined; symmetry asymmetric (¬ versus ⋛); scale first order in a decision, second order in the design of the checking seat.

- **D1 decision frame:** caseworker, case, statute / currency of correctness / anchor the public outcome the law is about.
- **D2 political frame:** elected leaders, appointees, networks / currency of influence / anchor the same public outcome, plus elections.
- **D3 checking arrangement:** oversight seats / currency of conventions and verdicts / anchor kept reachable.
- **D4 compound:** D1 ∩ D2, shared channel / currency ambiguous / anchor present but unable to correct both frames separately.

Order by dependency (Fig 3): anchor first, channel second, seat third. Union: D1 ∪ D2 with D4 as a compound with its own loop.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Partition or union? (decides whether one lever can work)

- Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
- Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**
- Here the region where only resistance is missing is cleared by insulation; the region where only responsiveness is missing is cleared by oversight. The intersection, D4, is cleared by neither, because the second condition still binds in each case.
- Fig 9 :57-58: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."**
- ⇒ The compound needs its own loop: separate the channels. This is a `general` structural result. Triangulated with Fig 3 (order) and with the *Library of Babel* line about a bundle masquerading as one problem.

### Q2 - Where does the chain break? (dependency order)

- Fig 3 :20-21: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**
- Fig 3 :25-27: perception and planning have obvious failures and obvious tests; the middle link, *"having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress"*, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments. *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
- In this question the middle link is: can the agency say what would count as a correct decision in this case, independently of who asked? That is the anchor link. Most reform lands above it: training, pay, transparency rules, more oversight. Those steps consume an objective that has not yet been stated, so their effort arrives at a step that cannot use it.
- Order therefore: (1) state the outcome the law is constitutively about; (2) decide which frame the agency ranges against in which decision; (3) put the maintenance of that channel in a seat that cannot author the verdict.
- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** A perfect inspector general cannot rescue an agency that has no criterion for a correct decision.
- **Falsifier for Q2:** show a reform at step 3 that restores both properties in an agency that has no stated criterion at step 1. If that happens, the dependency order is wrong.

### Q3 - Are institutional arrangements isomers? (structure)

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Bersch and Fukuyama (Annual Review of Political Science, 2023) observe that other democracies *"use different mixtures of these mechanisms, for example by substituting stronger ex post review for ex ante procedures or using appointment and removal power in place of either."*
- Read through Fig 12: the atoms are the same in both designs (an insulated decision space, a source of expertise, a review capability). The **bond differs**: ex ante procedure puts the review before the decision; ex post review puts it after; appointment and removal power puts it in the career channel. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. These are **isomers**, not different quantities of one thing.
- A governance index that counts "how many accountability mechanisms exist" reads the formula and misses the bond. That is exactly Fig 12's flat-formula collapse: *"the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not"* (Fig 12 :14), so *"nothing downstream can tell them apart, and nothing signals that anything was lost"* (Fig 12 :15-16).
- The weaker claim, if a design lacks one of the atoms, is a **functional group** reading: it is the same kind of arrangement with a different member in a slot. Both readings are checkable. The isomer claim is `single-source` for the figure definition (Fig 12) and needs the world check on whether the atoms really match; Bersch and Fukuyama supply that check as a secondary source.
- **Falsifier for Q3:** show that ex ante procedure and ex post review require different atoms, not merely a different bond, so they cannot be isomers; or show that the observed behaviours are identical, so the difference is not decisive.

### Q4 - The analogue of a pinned goal (mapping, not resemblance)

Three world frames supply the same shape. Map relations, not looks (Fig 4 :19-20).

| analogue | actors | currency | anchor | what it decides here |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Central bank with an elected mandate (Meyer, Federal Reserve, 2000) | elected government, central bank, markets | policy instruments and credibility | the publicly legislated price-stability target | goal set outside, instrument chosen inside; insulation is a channel property, not freedom from government |
| High-reliability organisation (PSNet/AHRQ, 2024) | frontline staff, senior hierarchy, the operating system | safety and situational awareness | the actual state of operations | *"the person with greatest knowledge of the situation might not be the person with the highest status and seniority"*; authority follows contact, not rank |
| Separated departments (Madison, Federalist 51, 1788) | legislature, executive, judiciary | ambition and constitutional means | the constitutional rights of each place | *"Ambition must be made to counteract ambition"*; independence of appointment and emoluments, with *"auxiliary precautions"* |

All three share the same relational shape: a standard or reference that the operator does not author, a channel that reaches it, and a separation that keeps the operator from moving it. Their overlap with the figures is **low** (economics, safety science, 18th-century political philosophy), so their agreement is weak corroboration of the shape, not a second derivation. They are witnesses for the binding question, never for the framework's authority.

### Q5 - Refuse before mint (order of operators)

- Formula step 2 routes a REFUTED residual to a refusal, not an explanation: *"a term held is false; what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation."*
- Fig 5 :53: **"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"**
- The term to refuse here is *"responsiveness means agreement with the current political lead"*. It is held widely and it predicts nothing that discriminates R1 from R2. Refusing it comes before designing any new oversight body. A vocabulary that cannot refuse (Fig 12 amendment: *"A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate"*) will add oversight after oversight and never remove the wrong premise.
- Fig 10 :42: **"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."** So the refusal must be stated before the checker sees the framework's interior.

## FALSIFIER (written before the run)

The supporting derivation fails if any of the following holds:

1. A reform at the seat level (step 3) restores both properties in an agency with no stated criterion at the anchor level (step 1). This breaks the dependency order (Q2).
2. Ex ante review and ex post review are shown to require different atoms, or to produce identical behaviour, so the isomer claim collapses (Q3).
3. A system is found in which authority follows rank rather than contact and reliability is unchanged, breaking the HRO analogue's relevance (Q4).
4. Adding oversight to a system that still holds "responsiveness equals agreement" resolves the compound D4. This breaks Q5 and the formula's refusal-before-mint order.

## CONCLUSION

The question is a **union with a specific compound**, not a trade-off; the chain breaks at the anchor link, which has the fewest instruments; institutional designs are best read as **isomers** over a shared atom set (same atoms, different bond, different behaviour) with a functional-group reading as the fallback; and the three low-overlap analogues all instantiate one relational shape, a reference the operator does not author, reached through a channel that is kept separate. None of this names an institution. The names remain candidate bindings with provenance in `10_source_ledger.csv`.
