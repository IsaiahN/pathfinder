# Expert verification packet - agency independence and responsiveness

**What this is:** a claim in a form a public administration scholar, administrative lawyer or institutional economist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No measurement in this packet is claimed to settle the question. This is a shape and a design order, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

An agency resists political interference when its standard of correctness does not change when a political actor pushes on it, and it remains responsive when the elected will still sets that standard through law and mandate. The two conditions can share one anchor, the public outcome the law is constitutively about, provided the agency and the elected leaders are corrected by it separately and neither can move it. The conditions form a union with a specific compound, so no single lever clears both. Resistance is a refusal of a channel; responsiveness is a comparison against a goal. The channel, not the ground, is what decays under interference, so the design task is to keep the anchor reachable and put the maintenance of that channel in a seat outside what it grades, with stakes declared. Independence is a property of channel and habitat, not of the agent alone. The framework returns this shape and its refuters, not an institution.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 2 and 10

- **Statement:** the wanted properties require a pinned anchor and separate correction channels. An agency whose correctness standard updates on political pressure has no anchor, and the resulting drift is coherent and hard to see from inside.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29-30 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* Fig 2 :33 - *"The anchor must not update."* Fig 2 :46 - *"Each frame is corrected by it separately, and what makes the correction sound is that neither frame can move it."* Fig 10 :24 - *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a standard of correctness that settles the question, changes its answer when a frame pushes on it, and still produces correct outcomes; or a case where the agency's channel and the political channel are the same channel yet both properties hold.

### C2 - domain-facing; checkable against public administration evidence

- **Statement:** the two properties are a union with a compound, not a trade-off, and institutional designs that share atoms but put the review at a different point are isomers (same atoms, different bond, different behaviour), not quantities on one scale.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 - *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."* Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- **What to check:** is the union reading faithful to the cases you know? Does a single lever (more insulation, or more oversight) actually leave the intersection standing? Do ex ante procedure and ex post review behave as different substances over the same atoms, or do they require different atoms?
- **Refuter:** a lever that clears the intersection; or a demonstration that the competing designs require different atoms, or produce identical behaviour, so the isomer claim fails.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation with a domain consequence

- **Statement:** independence is a property of channel and habitat, not of the agent, so person-focused reform cannot produce either property unless it changes contact. The checking seat must be outside what it grades, and undeclared stakes are modelled anyway.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :40 - *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Fig 10 :86 - *"Outside the slice being graded. Whoever is measured by a reading cannot own it."* Fig 10 :60 - *"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."*
- **Refuter:** an agent-only improvement with no change in contact that demonstrably moves the ground; or an oversight body that grades a reading it also owns and still functions as a check.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a trade-off to be balanced, and not a governance index.
- Not that an agency should agree with elected leaders. *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you"* (Fig 2 :35).
- Not a name. Merit recruitment, fixed terms, independent budgets, statutory mandates and central-bank instrument independence are candidate bindings from other fields with their own provenance. *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (Fig 9 :32).
- Not that written rules are neutral. *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"* (Fig 10 :47).
- Not that the two grounds of the political frame, elections and public outcomes, are reconciled by the figures. That is an open gap.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not another argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a colour instrument "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. Can a measurement in the same decision distinguish "followed the statute" from "followed the preference"? If not, the instrument is inadequate, not the question unanswerable.
2. Is there exogenous variation in insulation that measures both resistance and responsiveness against a ground neither side controls?
3. Do the standing falsifiers in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 fire? Any one of them retracts the shape.

Two world findings are already live conflicts and are offered for checking, not hidden: Eijffinger and de Haan find legal independence necessary but not sufficient; Walters and Bolger find the procedural justice effect weak and not reliably established in longitudinal data.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29-30, :33, :46, Fig 9 :55, Fig 10 :24, :86, Fig 11 :40 and Fig 12 :117 (eight short passages).
2. Check C2 against a case you know: does a single lever leave the intersection standing?
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish law-following from preference-following in the same decision, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
