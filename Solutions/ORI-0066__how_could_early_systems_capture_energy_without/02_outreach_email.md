**Subject:** One checkable claim about prebiotic energy capture, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output about early energy capture that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the shape, `general` on the structure, not `grounded`):** early energy capture without modern enzymes and membranes still owes four things. Energy is conserved, finite and directionless on its own, so "capture" requires a spendable difference, not just a source of energy. That difference must be bonded to a state, either space (a barrier or pore that holds inside and outside apart) or time (a cycle whose output re-enters as input). A lipid bilayer is one way to supply the spatial state; a mineral wall or a thermal cycle is another. A gene-encoded enzyme is one way to have a part that survives its own use; an Fe(Ni)S cluster or green rust is another. And the difference has to be kept far from equilibrium by a habitat, because capability belongs to the system and its habitat together, not to the isolated system. What makes this an answer rather than a list is the coupling: the energy-harnessing device has to make a copy of itself.
>
> This is a shape, not a historical claim. I am not saying the answer was alkaline vents, or hot springs, or a particular mineral. The framework returns a kind of thing, never the answer itself, and which pathway actually ran is left open.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that a pH gradient was necessary in every case (catalyst-driven CO2 reduction in free solution is reported without one), that any named mineral has been shown to be the historical catalyst, or that any of this is settled by experiment. The reactor and mineral results are provenanced bindings from the geochemistry frame, not derivations.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
>
> 1. a directed, copy-making capture with no spendable difference anywhere in the system;
> 2. a spendable gradient with no state, spatial or temporal, bonded to it;
> 3. capture that persists while the putative catalyst is consumed one-for-one and never regenerated;
> 4. an improvement to the isolated system, with no change of contact, that moves the ground.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
>

> - An Origin-of-Life Reactor to Simulate Alkaline Hydrothermal Vents, Herschy et al. 2014, J Mol Evol: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4247476
