## Two findings in the literature that bear directly on this question

1. **The literature contains a live disagreement about the gradient itself.** Jackson 2016 says natural pH gradients were *"unlikely to have played a part in life's origin"*; Herschy et al. 2014 and Lane 2017 defend the vent gradient; Schwander et al. 2023 report CO2 reduction with catalysts and *"no gradient or compartmentation"*. Do not present any one of these as settled. Present the disagreement as a union of two questions at different scales, as in `01` §5. This is the single most useful thing to tell a reader, because it stops them from assuming the shape claim requires a pH gradient.
2. **The vent story is a generator, not a result.** Herschy et al. 2014 report *"low yields"* and *"variable and inconsistent"* formaldehyde and call the work *"a preliminary proof of concept"*. The Herschy paper's own concluding sentence says the reactor provides *"empirical evidence that simple organics can be generated and concentrated"* under simulated conditions. That is a checkable sub-claim, not a derivation of life's origin. Present it as provenance for a binding, never as the answer (Fig 8 :50-52: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."*).

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 10 :52**: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Sending the full framework before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the four conditions and the refuter; offer links after a verdict is recorded.
- **Fig 10 :42**: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28**: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*. Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the papers.
- **Fig 2 :40**: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. The bioenergetics papers cite one another heavily; agreement among them is one pool talking. The physics source (Wolchover/England) is the only genuinely low-overlap witness here.
- **Fig 7 :35-36**: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* A conference session is useful only if the next room differs from the last and matches the residual it surfaced.
- **Fig 2 :42**: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Prepare to lose independence if the claim is pushed as a belief.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the four-condition shape and its four refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) record that verdict; (4) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer (and the optional third stage)

1. **Stage 1, before anything else.** Send the shape claim and the pre-registered refuter. No theory, no links, no request for agreement. The recipient checks the output without holding the framework's interior (Fig 10 :52; Fig 2 :45).
2. **Stage 2, opt-in by construction.** If they are curious, offer the three source links above. Their agreement with the theory later is never pooled as corroboration, because by then they hold the interiors (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3, offer a method.** The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer (Fig 4 :22). Each user must write their own refuter and state their own mode before running it, because a frame cannot check the direction of its own question (THE_FORMULA.md, "Direction").

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 work only if the stage-1 shape survives. If a recipient shows that a copy-making capture can occur with no spendable difference, the shape is refused and the offer stops there.
