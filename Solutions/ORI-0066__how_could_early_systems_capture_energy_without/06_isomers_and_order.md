# Isomers and order: catalyst, cycle, and the two bindings of a gradient

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the bond-level distinction. This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).**

## RESIDUAL

The main audit established that early energy capture owes a spendable difference bonded to a state and a part that survives its own use. Two supporting questions remain: (1) are the two ways of bonding the difference, spatial and temporal, **isomers** under Fig 12's definition; and (2) what is the order of the four conditions, and what kind of bond joins them? A wrong answer to (2) would turn a union into a recipe, which Fig 12 :111 warns a flat vocabulary cannot express.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Spatial gradient versus temporal cycle: are they isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 13 :76: **"A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."** The figure already distinguishes them at the level of terms; the question is whether the distinction is an isomerism.
- Fig 13 :74: **"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."** Both candidates are legal first-order molecules: `space → gradient → space` (a difference held across a barrier or pore) and `time → gradient → time` (a difference sustained through a cycle). Each crosses state to structure to state.
- Over the shared atom set `{space, time, energy, gradient, network, spectrum}`, the two candidates have the same atoms and differ only in which state the gradient is bonded to and which bond closes the loop. By Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. Their behaviour differs decisively: a spatial barrier can be breached by proton leak; a temporal cycle can be broken by a phase shift.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if the spatial and temporal candidates can be written with different atoms (for example, the temporal case requiring a spectrum the spatial case does not), the isomer claim fails and they are merely two members of a functional group, Fig 12 :123: **"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** As written, both use the gradient, so the claim stands as `specified`, unmeasured.
- **Triangulation status:** single-source on the figure side (Fig 12 :117 plus Fig 13 :76, same framework), with world corroboration from two different fields. Herschy et al. 2014 demonstrate the spatial case in a reactor with thin inorganic barriers. England's dissipation-driven adaptation, reported by Wolchover 2014, describes driven systems whose structures re-form through cycles. Two different fields, but the second is a shape analogue, not a measurement of the prebiotic case.

### Q2 — Catalyst versus spent part: same atoms, different standing across uses

- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**
- The distinction is not chemical composition. An Fe(Ni)S cluster inside a modern enzyme and an Fe(Ni)S precipitate on a vent wall can share the same atoms. What differs is whether the arrangement is regenerated after use and whether the record says so.
- Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."** A list that says "iron, nickel, sulfur" has recorded the ingredients and not the arrangement, so it cannot tell a catalyst from a spent reagent.
- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"** means that once re-use is settled, the catalyst becomes an atom for the next level of the composition. Until then it is just a reactant.
- **Falsifier:** if a candidate actor is consumed one-for-one and capture nevertheless persists, the actor was a reagent and not a catalyst, and F3 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` fails. I recorded this as refuter 3 there before writing this section.
- **Strain, named:** Fig 12 :119 says *"nothing records which a term is"*. The whole point of the critic's requirement is to record it. The framework describes the failure (no record) but does not supply an instrument for regeneration at the prebiotic scale. Reactor re-use runs are the instrument; they are world sources, not figure lines.

### Q3 — Chain versus cycle: a chain cannot close by construction

- Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**
- A one-shot energy source is a chain. It cannot sustain capture; it delivers an occasion. A cycle returns its output as input, and the sign of the loop decides whether it closes. This is why the copy-making objective in Sousa et al. 2013 (*"the energy-harnessing device makes a copy of itself"*) is structural: the device must be regenerated with the copy.
- Sousa et al. 2013 call acetyl phosphate a good candidate for *"the first universal currency of high-energy bonds"* precisely because a thioester can be cleaved by phosphorolysis to regenerate it. That is a cycle at the currency level, not a claim about which currency actually won.
- Fig 12 :121's sign condition matters: a positive feedback loop that amplifies a difference is not the same as a cycle that restores it. A thermal gradient that concentrates organics by thermophoresis (Herschy et al. 2014 report up to about 5,000 fold) is a maintained difference only while the drive is on. Remove the drive and it relaxes. This is the habitat condition of `01` F4, not a new term.
- **Falsifier:** a demonstration that copy-making capture can be sustained by a strictly open chain with no re-entry of output, which breaks Fig 12 :121.

### Q4 — What bond joins the four conditions? Production, not mere sequence

- Fig 12 :72: **"A then B; B needs A to have happened"** defines → sequence (the bond line is at :71-74; the test at :74 is **"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."**).
- Fig 12 :77: **"A produces B; B did not exist before"** defines ⇒ production, with the test at :79: **"is there a B before A fires? No."**
- "Capture" is production. Before the difference is made, there is no spendable gradient; after it is made, there is one. Writing the four conditions with → would assert an ordering of the conditions that the framework does not give, the same mistake the exemplar found in the GR/QM chain. Fig 13 :75 writes cross-kind composition with commas, not arrows.
- The Operators table adds the needed caution at :34: *"the only bond a flat chain can express, so every term today is a sequence whether or not it should be."* A vocabulary with only → cannot say that a catalyst is regenerated or that a difference is produced; it can only say that one thing followed another. This is the middle-link problem of Fig 3 in a notation form.
- **Falsifier:** a derivation showing that the difference exists before the coupling fires, so the relation is sequence or conjunction rather than production. That would demote F1 from a structural requirement to a temporal description.

### Q5 — Order by dependency, not by importance

- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**
- The order used in this folder is: spendable difference (D1) → bonded state (D2) → surviving catalyst (D3) → maintaining habitat (D4) → check against ground (D5). Each consumes the previous one's output. A paper that measures a mineral surface without saying what difference it holds has worked above the break.
- Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** The copy-making objective is the link most often assumed rather than measured here; the Sousa et al. 2013 definition is the closest thing to an instrument in the literature read.
- **Falsifier:** a candidate mechanism that satisfies D4 without D1, or D3 without D2, would show the order is a list rather than a dependency chain. None of the sources read does this; all four conditions are present in every candidate, which is further evidence for the union reading.

## CONCLUSION

The spatial and temporal bindings of a spendable difference are **isomers over the fixed six-term atom set**: same atoms, different arrangement, different behaviour, with a stated falsifier. A catalyst is not a different ingredient from a spent reagent; it is the same arrangement with a different standing across uses, and the record is what is missing (Fig 12 :119, :5). A cycle, not a chain, is what closes energy capture into copy-making, and the sign of the loop decides whether it closes (Fig 12 :121). The bond between the conditions is production (⇒), not sequence (→), and the dependency order is D1 through D5 rather than a ranking of interestingness (Fig 3 :20). This framing rescues what is defensible in a one-mechanism answer without naming a mechanism: it needs no seventh primitive, no import that moves the wall, and no arrow that the figures do not license.
