# Solution EAR-0067 — figure-grounded package

**Question:** Would diversified crops and farming systems improve resilience enough to offset possible yield or cost trade-offs?

**Source:** earth systems.txt line 89 (question 67 in the source file).

**Section:** 7. Food, agriculture, and land use.

**Mode:** `general` on the bare structural shape, `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, `gap` on the literal net yes/no. Not `grounded`. Every substantive file is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading, so it cannot settle the question it reports on.

**Status:** done.

**Registry status:** pending.

**Contact field:** agricultural diversity research.

## Restatement as an effect

The decision maker has two packages: a diversified crop and farming system (more species, varieties, rotations, non-crop habitat, or livestock integration) and a simplified one. The question asks whether the change in one quantity, resilience to a shock, is large enough to pay for changes in two other quantities, mean yield and cost. In effect terms this is a threshold comparison across different currencies, indexed by crop, field, season, region, and shock. Option A: assert a single net yes or no. Option B: refuse the net figure as written, return the shape any such answer must have, keep verdicts per domain, and name the instrument and the import that would close the comparison.

## What an answer would look like (bound as an ordinary term)

A `specified` shape claim: diversification changes contact between a crop package and an enumerated habitat, the change is indexed per slot and relative to a residual, and a net "enough to offset" claim requires a declared currency and horizon; plus a pre-registered refuter that would fire if a direct shock instrument and a rank-invariance test settle the comparison. The sought-for shape is written down and bound, per the formula's "Direction" rule, so it can accrue a residual like any other term.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named researchers.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree that diversification works.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. A room does not upgrade the label. *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"* (Fig 7 :13). A list of people selected for receptiveness produces a convention, not corroboration.
2. Correlation disqualifies a verdict. *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). Selecting recipients for likely agreement manufactures the correlation that would make their agreement worthless.
3. Asking for trust before a check spends independence. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The outside frame sees the residual and the refuter, not the framework's interiors.

Legally, scraping personal addresses for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one.

## Suggested next step

Take `01` and `08` to the public venues in `03`. Ask what would count as a failure of the shape claim. Record that verdict before offering any framework material. A refutation is more useful than an endorsement.
