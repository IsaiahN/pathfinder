# Outreach draft: check the shape claim before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the type question in `01` §7 is stated in the first paragraph and the refuter is written down. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about diversification and resilience, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against work you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (in effect terms):** a grower compares a diversified package (more species, longer rotations, intercropping, non-crop habitat, reduced tillage, livestock) with a simplified one. Three gaps are involved, not one: the shock-response gap, the yield gap, and the money-and-labour gap. Each is indexed by crop, field, season, region, and shock type. The question asks whether the first gap shrinks by an amount large enough to pay for the other two.
>
> **The shape claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the net "enough to offset" figure is not composable as written. The comparison operator is a threshold relation between two quantities, and a recovery trajectory, a mass of grain, and a sum of money are different currencies that do not add. A comparison is legitimate only after each side is bound to a declared currency and horizon at the same slots. Diversification itself is a maintained change of contact: the farming seat can introduce actors and cannot subtract the habitat, so adaptation is permanent work with a refusal condition rather than a one-off purchase. Success is per slot and relative to the residual; there is no standing list of diversified practices that wins across all shocks.
>
> **What the figure set does not decide:** which practice to recommend, a universal diversification percentage, and the sign of the net for a named farm. Those are bindings from the agriculture frame onto held slot terms. They need provenance and a ground reading, and I am not asserting them.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim. I wrote these before sending this:**
> 1. A verbatim figure line that licenses adding the three currencies, or a measurement convention that binds them in one currency and holds across a second environment-type distribution.
> 2. A direct shock-response instrument, independent of yield proxies, showing that the resilience gain exceeds the yield and cost loss at the same slots and horizon.
> 3. Rank-invariant response across crops, shocks, and years, with no sign flip.
> 4. A diversification practice that removes a habitat actor and stays removed without maintenance.
>
> A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you'd rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the three sources below are the ones that bear on the claim. The figure text, not my summary, is the authority.

## Two-stage discipline

1. **Stage 1:** send the residual, the shape, and the refuter. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record that verdict **before** any theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if curious, give the three links. The figures are the authority, not the summary. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because a reader who has absorbed the framework has joined the frame being checked (Fig 10 :42).

Nothing in this file authorises a bulk send. Public institutional and opt-in venues only, one to one, with an opt-out line in every message.
