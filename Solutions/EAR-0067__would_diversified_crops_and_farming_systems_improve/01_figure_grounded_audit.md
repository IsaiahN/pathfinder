## 0. The residual in effect terms

A grower or policy maker holds two packages. Package S is simplified: few crops, short rotation, conventional tillage. Package D is diversified: more species or varieties, longer rotations, intercropping, non-crop habitat, organic amendment, reduced tillage, or livestock integration. The question asks whether D raises *resilience* by an amount *enough to offset* whatever it costs in *yield* and *money or labour*.

Stated as a residual, there are three per-slot gaps, not one:

- `R_res(s)`: the gap between predicted and observed performance of the package when a defined shock arrives at slot s, where s indexes crop, field, season, region, and shock type.
- `R_yield(s)`: the gap between expected and realised yield under the package.
- `R_cost(s)`: the gap between expected and realised monetary and labour cost.

The question asks for a comparison: is the reduction in `R_res` larger than the increase in `R_yield` and `R_cost`? That is a threshold relation. The word *offset* carries the weight of the whole question.

Two preliminary consequences follow before any figure line is chosen:

1. The three gaps live in different currencies. A recovery trajectory, a mass of grain, and a sum of money are not the same kind of quantity.
2. Each gap is indexed by slot. There is no reason the comparison comes out the same for corn and soybean, for a hot dry year and a cold wet year, or for year 3 and year 40.

Both consequences are tested against the figures below. They are not assumed.

## 1. Domain census (before any figure line is chosen)

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity:** at least three conditions (diversity change, shock response, yield and cost) plus their intersections. The intersections are the question: a package that raises biodiversity and lowers profit is not a harder case of either condition, it is a specific compound.
- **Symmetry:** the "offset" relation is ordered. "Resilience gain offsets yield loss" is not the same claim as "yield loss offsets resilience gain". Comparison is an ordered bond, not a symmetric join.
- **Scale:** plant and root zone; field and farm seat; region and food supply; and a temporal axis from one season to several decades. The temporal axis is where several literature estimates disagree, so it is a parameter, not a decoration.

### 1.2 One line per domain (actors / currency / anchor)

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 crop diversity | genotypes, species, varieties, seed systems | yield and persistence under sampled environment types | multi-environment trials, measured yield | binding onto held slot terms |
| D2 farming-system diversity | the manager and practices (rotation, intercropping, tillage, organic amendment, habitat, livestock) | introduced actors and changed contact | multi-year on-farm outcomes, a seat able to refuse a practice | maintained seat |
| D3 resilience | the enumerated habitat: light, water, temperature, season, terrain, and relations of pollination, predation, competition, dependency | shock absorption, loss magnitude, recovery trajectory | a measured shock response that does not update on the grower's own report | objective link, currently broken |
| D4 yield and cost trade-offs | markets, input prices, labour, machinery, processing | tonnes, money, labour hours | measured yield and cost | different currencies |
| D5 verification and research frame | agronomists, ecologists, economists, crop modellers, reviewers, farmers | agreement and residual | measured shock response and yield, non-updating | frames with shared evidence pools |

A domain needs a nameable anchor. D1 to D5 each have one. "Resilience" by itself does not, which is why D3 is defined by its instrument rather than by its label.

### 1.3 Partition or union

Union, not partition. The three conditions overlap: diversification can raise a service and lower yield in the same field in the same year. A lever that clears the single-condition regions leaves the intersections standing. The object the question asks about is the intersection, so unbundle before searching.

### 1.4 Dependency order (Fig 3)

Perceive the conditions; acquire terms for what diversity affords; bind an objective with an instrument; plan; carry what worked. The chain currently breaks between the second and third links: there are many terms for diversification practices and few instruments that separate a shock response from a yield proxy. Effort spent on more practice vocabulary lands above the break.

### 1.5 Strain test applied before conclusion

A first pass mapped "diversity" onto the network term of the six-term substrate. Pressed literally, that strains: the substrate governs physical states and structures, and a crop species list is not a physical primitive. Re-census assigns it to `general` mode as a cross-scale recursion analogue under Fig 4 :20, *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* The identity reading is retracted in §8 and kept visible. No other strain survived re-census; the one live type question is named in F1 and §7.

## 2. Findings

### F1 — The net "enough to offset" figure is not composable as written (decisive)

Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**

Fig 12 :130: **"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."**

Operators table :49: **"a threshold relation between two quantities"**

Operators table :52: **"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"**

Fig 12 :87, the test that identifies comparison: **"is it about which is larger, rather than both being present?"**

The comparison bond ⋛ is ordered and takes two quantities. It can compare, at a stated level and in a stated currency, whether one quantity is larger than another. It cannot add resilience, tonnes, and money into one net score. The question as ordinary language asks for exactly that addition: "improve resilience enough to offset yield or cost trade-offs." The figures name that summing as the notation error, not the fix.

What survives is a shape: a threshold comparison is legitimate once each side has been bound to a declared currency and horizon at the same slots. Until then the superlative has no operand of the right type. This is a `specified` structural finding, not a measurement.

**Refuter:** a figure re-census yielding a verbatim line that licenses adding the three currencies, or a measurement convention that binds them with a declared price and holds across a second environment-type distribution. Absent that, the net yes/no is refused as written.

### F2 — Success is per-slot maintained contact, not a property carried by diversity

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**

Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

Symbols table :43: **"R is measured per slot, never as a single global number."**

Fig 11 :39: **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**

Fig 11 :40: **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**

Fig 11 :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

A crop or a practice is not a resilience carrier. Its contribution is a relation to a residual at a slot. Gaudin et al. 2015, opened this session, supply the low-overlap world check: in a 31-year rotation and tillage trial, increased rotation diversity improved soybean yield stability under hot and dry conditions while *"tillage and rotation diversity had no effects on corn yield variation in abnormal hot/dry or cool/wet conditions"*, and *"soybean and corn had opposite responses to tillage in hot and dry years."* The same package, two slots, different signs. Tamburini et al. 2020 report *"Variability in responses and occurrence of trade-offs highlight the context dependency of outcomes."*

A global "yes, diversification improves resilience" is an aggregate that throws away the live slot signal. Verdict `specified`, triangulated across Fig 1, Fig 11, and the Gaudin trial (low overlap between per-slot indexing and a long-term field experiment).

**Refuter:** a multi-environment trial showing rank-invariant response across all sampled crops, shocks, and years, with no slot where the sign flips.

### F3 — Diversification is introduction into a habitat, not subtraction, and it is permanent work

Fig 11 :61: **"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."**

Fig 11 :63: **"a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state"**

Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**

Fig 11 :46: **"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"**

Fig 11 :47: **"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"**

The farming seat can add a crop, a habitat strip, a cover crop, or livestock. It cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world. The added actors bring cascades the seat did not author. So diversification is not a one-off purchase of resilience; it is the installation and maintenance of a practice that can be refused when it fails. Handayani et al. 2026, opened this session, find that *"competition, added labor, weak demand, and insecure access to resources could offset benefits"*: the introduced set includes costs that were not in the original slot, which is exactly the introduction asymmetry. IPCC AR6 WGII Ch 5 lists diversification as a feasible option while noting that *"trade-offs and benefits vary with socio-ecological context (high confidence)."*

Verdict `specified`, triangulated across Fig 11, Handayani 2026, and the IPCC assessment, which are different evidence pools. This is a reason to expect trade-offs, not a reason to expect net failure.

**Refuter:** a figure line or a measured case where a diversification practice removes a habitat actor and the removal persists without continued maintenance.

### F4 — The objective link is broken: yield variability is used as a proxy for resilience

Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**

Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**

*The Hard Problem is About Source*, opened this session: *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it."*

Handayani et al. 2026: *"improvements in soil health, biodiversity, yield, income, or diet do not demonstrate the capacity to absorb, recover from, or adapt to climatic shocks."* They classify direct resilience evidence separately from indirect proxy evidence and report that *"heterogeneous evidence precluded a pooled effect estimate."*

Gaudin et al. 2015 write that in field studies *"stability and resilience are often used interchangeably to describe fluctuations in final crop yields after perturbance"* and state plainly: *"Our study does not investigate systems response to stress during crop development and fluctuations in crop physiological and yield potential status during stress would have to be studied to clearly establish how crop diversity and tillage alter the dynamic of crop stability (ie: resilience, tolerance, resistance)."*

So the measurement that most diversification studies report, yield variance, does not settle the resilience claim. It is a reading of an outcome whose source (absorption, recovery, avoidance, or simple portfolio averaging) is not discriminated. The channel, not the ground, is the problem: the ground keeps producing shocks, the measurement stops resolving them.

Verdict `specified` on the broken link; `gap` on the direct-shock instrument. Triangulated across Fig 3, Handayani 2026, and Gaudin 2015 (low overlap).

**Refuter:** a measurement that separates absorption, recovery, and adaptation for a defined shock, applied to diversified and simplified packages at the same slots, and shown to move with the package rather than with yield level alone.

### F5 — The question is a union of overlapping conditions; unbundle before searching

Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**

Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**

Fig 9 :57: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"**

Fig 9 :4, on disagreements that do not shrink with effort: **"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."**

Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**

The world sources show the union directly. Tamburini et al. 2020 find a neutral mean yield effect, *"lnRR = 0.01; 95% confidence interval (CI) = −0.12 to 0.14"*, alongside positive effects on biodiversity, pollination, pest control, nutrient cycling, water regulation, and soil fertility, and report that climate regulation *"presented instead the highest number of trade-offs, with 50% representing situations where an increase in yield corresponded to a decrease in climate regulation provision."* Sridhar et al. 2026 report *"average yields that are 20-38% better than monocropping"* while adding that *"the effectiveness of the diversification is moderated by regional climatic conditions, policy frameworks and access to markets."* Raveloaritiana and Wanger 2026 report that diversification *"increases financial profitability, biodiversity, pollination, soil quality, and carbon sequestration from 37 to 189% over 20 years, and we find no significant effects on crop yield, pest control, and climate regulation"* and that the yield-service trade-off *"shows win-win outcomes during the first 40 years"* before lose-yield outcomes become most likely.

These are not three readings of one number. They are different estimands, horizons, and service sets. The honest move is to unbundle: (i) shock response at a slot, (ii) yield level and variance, (iii) money and labour, each with its own instrument, then examine their intersections. A single lever, for example adding a legume, can clear the biodiversity condition and leave the labour condition standing.

Verdict `specified`, triangulated across Fig 9 and three sources with partially distinct evidence pools.

**Refuter:** a figure re-census showing the three conditions form a partition with one binding instrument, or a single lowest-overlap source that measures all three in one currency at the same slots and horizon.

### F6 — Effect sizes and rankings are filters for the budget, not witnesses for the verdict

Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

Fig 2 :40: **"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**

Fig 7 :13: **"agreement compounds, evidence does not"**

Fig 10 :42: **"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."**

Fig 10 :52: **"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."**

The literature numbers choose where to spend work. They do not answer the question. Tamburini's neutral yield effect, Sridhar's 20 to 38 percent gain, and Raveloaritiana's long-term profitability are all filters: they tell a grower which trial to fund and which horizon to check. None of them is a witness for a verdict, and they are not independent witnesses of each other because the agronomy syntheses partly share a primary-study pool. The essays add the prestige rule: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."*

Verdict `general`, triangulated across Fig 2, Fig 7, Fig 9, Fig 10, and the opened essays (the essays share the framework author's evidence pool and carry little independent weight).

**Refuter:** a single genuinely low-overlap source measuring the same estimand at the same slots and horizon, offered as a witness rather than a filter, with its ground reading stated.

### F7 — The literal net yes/no is a `gap` at this budget, not underivable in principle

Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"**

Fig 13 :115: **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."**

Fig 6 :40: **"What is unavailable is a sensor for something that has never registered anywhere, at any resolution."**

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**

No instrument in the assembled sources measures shock absorption and recovery in the same currency as yield and cost, at the same slots, over a declared horizon. The three formula routes remain distinct:

- **SEARCHED:** is a direct shock discriminator already in the assembled literature and merely unfound? Cheapest, asked first. Handayani et al. 2026 say direct evidence exists but is narrower than proxy evidence; that is a reason to search, not an answer.
- **INWARD:** sharpen an instrument already returning something. Yield stability under weather clusters (Gaudin et al. 2015) is already returning a reading. Sharpen it toward a recovery trajectory, in our own terms.
- **OUTWARD:** import a frame whose closure predicts the shape of the residual. Candidates, each with provenance and a shadow test: quantitative genetics on genotype by environment by management interactions; ecology on diversity and temporal stability; earth-system assessment on adaptation limits. Import from a live peer is debited against that peer's independence (Fig 6 :23).

Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts with different instructions (Fig 8 :39 to :43). This is the second: the term is plausibly in a neighbouring closure and was not found at this budget. Importing against a budget limit would add an atom that was not needed.

Verdict: `gap`, unreached at this budget. Reported as a verdict, not filled by invention.

**Refuter:** a direct shock-discriminating instrument, plus a demonstration that its gain exceeds the yield and cost loss in a declared common currency across a second environment-type distribution, would close the gap and convert the verdict.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. These six conditions were written before §4 and §5. Any one firing moves the solution to a re-census.

1. **Figure refuter.** A verbatim figure line licenses adding the three currencies, or names a standing diversification percentage that moves the ground. Breaks F1.
2. **Instrument refuter.** A direct shock-response measure, independent of yield proxies, shows that diversified packages absorb and recover, and that the gain exceeds the yield and cost loss in a declared common currency at the same slots and over a second environment-type distribution. Breaks F4 and closes F7.
3. **Rank-invariance refuter.** Response ranks stay invariant across crops, shocks, and years with no sign flip. Breaks F2.
4. **Habitat refuter.** A diversification practice removes a habitat actor and the removal persists without maintenance. Breaks Fig 11 :61 and :63 (F3).
5. **Union refuter.** A single low-overlap source measures resilience, yield, and cost in one currency at one horizon and finds the conditions form a partition, not a union. Breaks F5.
6. **Capability refuter.** A change in the diversification label with no change in contact moves the ground. Breaks Fig 11 :40.

If none fires, the shape stands as `specified` and the literal net verdict remains `gap`. Nothing here is `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Do not answer yes or no in the abstract. Unbundle the question into shock response, yield, and cost, each with its own instrument and horizon (F5).
2. State the currency and the horizon of any comparison before computing it. Without them, "offset" has the wrong operand type (F1).
3. Keep verdicts per crop, region, shock, and year. Never average the live slot signal into a global percentage (F2).
4. Treat diversification as a maintained practice with a refusal condition, not a purchased certificate (F3).
5. Build or import a shock-discriminating instrument before claiming a resilience gain; yield variance is a proxy, and the source of the reading is not identified (F4).
6. Use effect sizes to choose trials, not to decide the verdict. Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong (F6; Fig 2 :42).
7. Report the net question as `gap`, unreached at this budget, with SEARCHED, INWARD, and OUTWARD kept distinct (F7).
8. Send the generator (the residual, the shape, and the refuter), not the conclusion. Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**

## 7. Open type question (named, not hidden)

The comparison bond ⋛ is defined as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* (Operators :49). If a resilience trajectory and a cost total are each bound to a quantity at a declared level, comparison is legal. Whether a *composed package* counts as a quantity at that level is a type question no figure settles. The figures' own open-item rule applies: *"A relation that fails all seven is not an operator you are missing; it is a claim that has not been made yet."* An instrument and a declared currency are owed, not a new bond. This is recorded in `06_isomers_and_order.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` and is not used to hand either side a verdict.

## 8. Closing verdict

The shape survives: diversification is a maintained change of contact, indexed per slot, whose contribution to resilience is relative to a residual; the currencies do not add; the conditions form a union that must be unbundled; and the objective link needs a shock discriminator that yield variance does not supply. The literal yes/no is a `gap`, unreached at this budget. No domain ended in a clean `grounded` result, and none was averaged into another. The second seat in `11_audit.md` is simulated and shares this evidence pool; by Fig 2 :40 and Fig 2 :35 its agreement is internal consistency, not corroboration.
