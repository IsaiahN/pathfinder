# Solution CMP-0037

**Question:** Can causal mechanisms be identified in systems where interventions change several connected parts at once?

**Source:** complexity science.txt line 46

**Section:** 4. Prediction, uncertainty, and causal inference

**Mode:** `specified` on the three per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural skeleton. Nothing here is `grounded`: no intervention on a real complex system was performed for this derivation, and no measurement was taken.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

## The question restated as an effect

An action `a` is applied to a system and the reading `o′` comes back. The intervention changes several connected parts at once, so one action produces a vector of per-slot residuals, `R = |Γ(b,a) − o′|` indexed by object slot `s` (Symbols table). The question asks whether the **mechanism** can be identified, where a mechanism is the typed arrangement plus the route by which the action produced the reading, rather than merely the size of the change.

- Option A: one intervention plus its outcome suffices to name the mechanism. This treats the parts that moved as the mechanism and the reading as the source.
- Option B: identification is possible only as a shape under stated conditions, a per-slot residual, an unbundled union, low-overlap frames against a non-updating anchor, an enumerated habitat, and a ground that settles a minted term. This returns a checkable shape, its pre-registered refuters, and the instruments that would close it.

What each leads to is recorded in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` and `09_watson_answer.md`. The verdicts are kept separate by domain and are never averaged.

## Standing of this derivation

This folder is a **simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures**. It is not a ground reading. By the framework's own validation rule, an output generated this way cannot settle the question it reports on. It can say what a mechanism-identification claim must look like, what is unreachable at this budget, and what would reverse the verdict. It cannot name the mechanism in any particular system.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.

The reason is structural, not administrative. Fig 2 :35 says *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Fig 2 :40 says agreement is disqualifying for a verdict when the frames share an evidence pool. Fig 10 :52 says *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* The first contact is therefore the residual and its refuter. The figures are offered only after a verdict on stage 1 is recorded.
