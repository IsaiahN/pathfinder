## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

The Symbols table defines `R` as *"The gap between prediction and observation. The central quantity: everything the system does is driven by it."* An intervention is the action `a`, *"What it does."* The observation is `o′`, *"What actually happened."*

Here the action changes several connected parts at once. So:

```
one action a  ->  a vector of outcomes o'(s)  ->  a vector R(s) = |Γ(b,a)(s) − o'(s)|
```

The question is **not** whether a change was observed. It is whether the **mechanism** is identifiable: the typed arrangement and the route by which the action produced the vector. `Γ(b,a)(s)` is the prediction at slot `s`; the mechanism would be the term that makes that prediction pay.

A shape claim is bound before the run, as the formula's Direction rule requires. What would an answer look like? It would look like (i) a typed molecule of held atoms, (ii) a per-slot residual pattern, (iii) a lineage recording what fired in what order, (iv) a second low-overlap frame ranged against the same non-updating ground, and (v) an instrument that discriminates this arrangement from the other arrangements that would give the same reading. That is the ordinary term we now let accrue a residual. It is refusable by the test in section 6.

## 1. Domain census, before choosing any figure line

### R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity.** One action, many slots. The action-to-residual map is one-to-many. Under Fig 12, a conjunction `+` of changed parts is symmetric and gives `k!` spellings and one term, while a sequence `→` or a production `⇒` is ordered and gives different terms. The mechanism claim is therefore about an **ordered/production arrangement**, not about the set of parts that moved. The arity question is: how many operands, and under which bond.
- **Symmetry.** The parts are not interchangeable. Fig 12 :95: *"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."* Swapping which part changed first, or which part produces which, changes the mechanism while leaving the ingredient list untouched.
- **Scale.** First order inside one part, second order across the cascade, and cross-scale wherever the intervention is applied at one level and read at another. Fig 13 :31 puts the randomness and the chaos in the remainder that re-enters the next turn: *"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise."* A mechanism identified at the wrong scale is not a mechanism identified.

### One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1. Identification logic**, framework-internal | `Γ`, `atoms(Γ)`, bonds, `φ`, `R(s)`, the lineage | composition inside `closure(Γ)` and settlement against the ground | the figures as metalaws; the ground for any settlement | a real domain, but the specific mechanism has no anchor until the ground pays, so it stays a slot |
| **D2. Intervention and habitat** | the action, the parts changed, the conditions, the relations, the control | actions spent, and the per-slot residuals they produce | the intervention record (what changed, when, in what order) plus an unchanged control | a real domain: the record is nameable |
| **D3. Measurement and frames** | instrument, second frame, verifying seat | bits to state the mechanism, actions to settle it (Fig 12 :126, different units) | something that does not update when a frame thinks (Fig 2 :29) | a real domain for the shape; the specific mechanism is `gap` at this budget because no discriminator is described |

No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet. The "true mechanism in system X" has no anchor in this derivation, so it is recorded as a slot, not promoted to a domain with a verdict.

### Partition or union

The multi-part intervention is a **union**, not a partition. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* The overlapping regions are compounds with their own loops. Where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, Fig 9 :4 says *"Split it rather than search."* A single lever clears only the region where one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.

### Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. Describe the residual per slot before naming any frame (Fig 9 :27).
2. Enumerate the habitat outward from the residual (Fig 11 :44).
3. Unbundle the union into the separate well-formed questions (Fig 9 :54).
4. Choose low-overlap frames and a non-updating anchor (Fig 2 :29, :31).
5. Mint or import a mechanism term only under the three guards (Fig 5 :33).
6. Settle against the ground (formula step 5).
7. Carry up the method, never the recording of one success (Fig 4 :22).

Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* And Fig 3 :22: *"A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can. Which is why a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing."* If step 1 or step 4 is missing, a claim about step 6 is a reading of nothing.

## 3. Findings F1 to F10

### F1. A mechanism is a bonded molecule with a lineage, not the list of parts that moved

Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."** On the flat-form collapse, Fig 12 :14: **"the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not"**, and Fig 12 :15: **"nothing downstream can tell them apart, and nothing signals that anything was lost."** Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**

Fig 13 :128: **"Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available."** Fig 13 :129: **"the order is not recoverable from the artefact. It is recoverable from the lineage."** Fig 13 :130: **"A term carries the atoms it was built from and the bonds that joined them. That record is not documentation of the term. It is the term."**

So the outcome vector `o′` is the artefact. The intervention record is the lineage. An identification that holds only the artefact has recorded the ingredients and lost the molecule. This is the decisive structural reason a single multi-part reading cannot identify a mechanism.

**Mode:** `general` (structural). **Status:** triangulated (Fig 12 plus Fig 13, low overlap between the bond definition and the lineage claim). **Refuter:** show that a route is recoverable from the artefact alone for the class of systems in question, that is, that two different typed arrangements with the same ingredient set and the same outcome vector can always be distinguished without the intervention record. That breaks Fig 12 :5 and Fig 13 :128.

### F2. The residual must be indexed per slot, never averaged over the changed parts

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** Fig 1 :28: **"The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."**

One intervention that changes several parts at once produces one **action** and many **slots**. Collapsing the many per-slot residuals into one global effect size is exactly the named error: the live slot disappears into the average and the search loses its aim. Verdicts stay per part and are never averaged. This also resolves a possible strain with "one intervention": the action is one, the residual is indexed by object. Action and residual are different quantities.

**Mode:** `general` (structural), `specified` when applied to a named system. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 1 plus Fig 9's union split, which also refuses a single pooled answer). **Refuter:** demonstrate that a global effect measure reliably locates the live slot for this class of systems, including the case where the global residual is near zero and one slot carries positive mass.

### F3. The simultaneous multi-part change is a union of overlapping conditions, not one mechanism

Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."** Fig 9 :4: **"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."**

Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**

A big lever that changes several parts at once is valuable precisely because it makes the residue describable, but it does not by itself return one mechanism. Where the parts respond under different rules, the honest output is several mechanisms with their own loops. Where they share conditions, the overlap is a compound requiring its own loop. Searching harder for a single mechanism in a union is the named error of treating a motion as progress (Fig 7 :13, **"agreement compounds, evidence does not"**).

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 9 plus Fig 3, different figures, same union conclusion). **Refuter:** show that one rule covers every subset of the response with a residual that trends down, with no leftover intersection that needs a second loop.

### F4. Read the residual against the habitat cascade, not against direct contact

Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."** Fig 11 :44: **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."** Fig 11 :52: **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."** Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**

The mechanism is often three steps out from the part that was changed. If the habitat is scoped to direct contact, the residual that matters is never enumerated and the mechanism is reported as the nearest neighbour. Valence is residual-relative, so the same part is a benefactor for one residual and a malfactor for another; there is no standing list of causal parts.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** single-source for the figure's DDT case (Fig 11), corroborated on the world side by the public-health intervention literature, which reports that effects accrue from combinations of components in contingent ways that cannot be reduced to any one level. **Refuter:** show that second- and third-order contact terms contribute no residual mass in the class of systems at issue, so first-order enumeration is sufficient.

### F5. Identification needs low-overlap frames against an anchor that does not update

Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."** Fig 2 :31: **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."** Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."** Fig 2 :39: **"Correlation has valence."** Fig 2 :40: **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"** and Fig 2 :42: **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."**

Fig 8 :45: **"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."** Fig 8 :50: **"Only the record of where each came from separates them"** (the full line distinguishes convergent derivation from adopted import). Fig 8 :53: **"Residual first, frame second."**

An intervention and its outcome are one frame. A second frame must differ in closure and draw on different evidence: a different measurement modality, a different intervention regime, or an observational context with a different source of variation. Two readers of the same report are one frame wearing two names. The control in an experiment is the anchor only if it does not update on the treatment frame's own prediction.

**Mode:** `specified`, with the independence requirement at `general`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 2 plus Fig 8). **Refuter:** show two frames with high overlap producing an independent verdict by the figure's own test, or show that a frame can be its own anchor without the coherent drift of Fig 2 panel 1.

### F6. Isolation is substitution, not removal

Fig 11 :66: **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."** Fig 11 :67: **"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."** Fig 11 :29: **"what you failed to reproduce, and what you brought with you"** are the two silent failure modes. Fig 11 :70: **"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."**

A `do` intervention does not hold the rest of the world fixed. It replaces one habitat with another and imports actors the analyst did not choose. This is the deepest reason a mechanism identified in a simplified setting fails to transfer: what was left out is invisible until the goal fails, and what was introduced is invisible until it acts. Fig 11 :40: **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** single-source for the figure (Fig 11); corroborated by world sources on soft interventions in cyclic systems and on adaptation in complex interventions, both of which report that the system's response to the intervention deviates from its designed form. **Refuter:** exhibit a real intervention that removes a part while leaving the habitat unchanged in every respect that bears on the residual, including second-order contact.

### F7. A mechanism vocabulary must express catalyst, cycle and functional group

Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."** Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."** Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

A flat chain of interventions cannot close a feedback loop by construction. A mechanism in a complex system commonly needs a catalyst, a cycle and a functional group, and none of these is nameable from an ingredient list. "Several connected parts at once" is therefore a bond question before it is a data question: which bond joins the parts, and does the vocabulary even contain it.

**Mode:** `general` (structural); the presence of a cycle in a specific system is `specified` and unmeasured. **Status:** single-source for the figure definitions, corroborated by two low-overlap world fields (cyclic causal models in machine learning; feedback-loop limits in public-health evaluation). **Refuter:** show that a chain vocabulary with no cycle, catalyst or functional group can represent a mechanism that closes a loop and still returns the same counterfactual predictions.

### F8. Reachability is bounded by instruments that are already returning something

Fig 6 :32: **"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."** Fig 6 :35: **"the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."** Fig 6 :29 and :30 give the two distinct instruments: **"inward: is our representation adequate to hold it? The instrument extends access."** and **"outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels."** Fig 6 :31: **"Neither answer tells you much about the other. Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other."**

Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"**, and Fig 13 :135: **"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."** Fig 9 :44: **"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."**

So the specific mechanism in a given system is a `gap` at this budget, not a failure of the method and not a negative answer. If no instrument anywhere is returning a reading that fails to resolve the competing arrangements, there is no edge from which to build a discriminator.

**Mode:** `specified` on the budget claim, `general` on the instrument rule. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 6 plus Fig 13 plus Fig 9). **Refuter:** exhibit a discriminator built from a description alone, where nothing at any resolution was previously returning a reading, and show that it settles the mechanism.

### F9. A causal claim must name its bond; a flat association is not a mechanism

Fig 2 :39: **"Correlation has valence."** The Operators table defines production `⇒` as **"A produces B; B did not exist before"** with the test **"is there a B before A fires? No."** Sequence `→` is only *"A then B; B needs A to have happened."* Fig 9 :30: **"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."**

An intervention establishes a production relation only when the second operand was absent before the first fired. Observing that several parts changed together establishes conjunction or sequence, not production. The question asks for mechanisms, so the answer must state which bond is asserted and pass that bond's test. Naming the effect rather than the cause is a discipline the figures impose on the searcher, not a detail of wording.

**Mode:** `general` on the bond distinction, `specified` on any claim that a given relation is production. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 2 plus the Operators table plus Fig 9). **Refuter:** show a case where temporal order and co-change alone distinguish production from sequence at the ground, without an intervention or a mechanism-modification interpretation.

### F10. The price of a mechanism is in two currencies, and the cuts must stay reversible

Fig 12 :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."** Fig 12 :127: **"They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them and will not notice the shortfall."** Fig 9 :41: **"so keep every cut ranked and reversible"**, because Fig 9 :40 says **"a wrong cut removes the answer and speeds up"**. Fig 5 :42: **"The acceptance test is a bargain, not a threshold."** Fig 5 :43: **"A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before."**

A mechanism can be cheap to state and unaffordable to settle. A tractability score or a venue tier is a filter for the budget, never a witness for the verdict: Fig 9 :60, **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."** Every decision about which part to hold fixed must be ranked and reversible, since a wrong cut deletes the answer from the reachable set while appearing to speed the search.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated (Fig 12 plus Fig 9 plus Fig 5). **Refuter:** show a mechanism claim where the bit cost and the action cost are the same quantity, or where an irreversible cut cannot remove the answer.

## 6. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the formula's Direction rule and the standing instruction that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument.

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Route-from-artefact.** Two different typed arrangements over the same ingredient set, producing the same per-slot outcome vector, can be distinguished without the intervention record, for the class of systems at issue. This breaks F1 (Fig 12 :5, Fig 13 :128).
2. **Adequate aggregate.** A single global effect measure reliably locates the live slot, including the case where the global residual is near zero and one slot carries positive mass. This breaks F2 (Fig 1 :19, :22).
3. **One-rule union.** One rule covers every subset of the response with a residual that trends down and no intersecting condition needs its own loop. This breaks F3 (Fig 9 :54-57).
4. **No cascade.** Second- and third-order contact terms carry no residual mass in the class at issue. This breaks F4 (Fig 11 :52).
5. **Self-anchor.** A frame can settle its own mechanism claim without a second low-overlap frame and without touching an anchor that does not update. This breaks F5 (Fig 2 :29, :35, :40).
6. **Subtractive isolation.** A real intervention can remove a part while leaving the habitat unchanged in every respect that bears on the residual. This breaks F6 (Fig 11 :66).
7. **Flat vocabulary suffices.** A chain vocabulary with no cycle, catalyst or functional group returns the same counterfactual predictions as one that has them. This breaks F7 (Fig 12 :121).
8. **Discriminator from nothing.** A discriminator can be built from a description alone where nothing at any resolution was previously returning a reading. This breaks F8 (Fig 6 :32-35).

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the domain verdict that rests on it is recomputed. Until one is run, no part of this folder may be labelled `grounded`.

## 7. Recommendation

1. **Lead with the shape and the refuter, never with a named mechanism.** Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* The first sentence to an outside frame is what would refute the claim.
2. **Index the residual per part.** Refuse any report that averages the changed parts into one effect size (F2).
3. **Unbundle before searching.** Ask which conditions are separate and which intersect; keep every cut ranked and reversible (F3, F10).
4. **Enumerate the habitat outward before attributing a cause.** Include second- and third-order contact (F4).
5. **Buy a second frame in proportion to the cost of being wrong.** Prefer a frame with a different closure and a different evidence source, and an anchor that does not update on either (F5).
6. **State the bond.** Say whether the claim is production, sequence, conjunction or threshold (F9).
7. **Name the gap, do not fill it.** Where no instrument discriminates, record `unreachable` and state what a discriminator would have to look like (F8).
8. **Send the generator, not the recording.** Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*
