# Outreach draft: check one output before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. Record the recipient's verdict on stage 1 before any framework material is shown.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the mode labels and the pre-registered refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are intact in the packet you attach. The offer collapses on contact if the output cannot survive the check.

---

**Subject:** Can one intervention identify a mechanism when it changes many parts at once?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** an intervention is one action, but it produces a vector of outcomes, one per connected part. The framework's residual is indexed by part, not aggregated. From that rule and the figures on composition, four things follow.
>
> 1. A mechanism is a typed arrangement plus the record of what fired in what order. The list of parts that changed is the ingredient list, and the arrangement is the half that a flat reading does not record. The route is not recoverable from the outcome alone; it is recoverable from the lineage.
> 2. A change that hits several parts at once is a union of overlapping conditions, not one mechanism. Where different rules work on different subsets, the honest answer is several mechanisms, not one harder question. Unbundle before searching.
> 3. Contact cascades. The residual that matters can be three steps out from the part that was changed, so the habitat must be enumerated outward, not scoped to direct contact. Isolation substitutes a habitat; it does not remove one.
> 4. Identification needs two frames with different closures and an anchor that does not update on either. One intervention and its outcome are one frame. Agreement between readers of the same evidence is the pool talking, not corroboration.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that this names the mechanism in any particular system. The framework returns a shape, not a name. The specific mechanism is recorded as a gap at this budget, pending an instrument that discriminates this arrangement from the others that would give the same reading. Nor am I claiming that a test harness identifies a mechanism: a substituted habitat proves wiring, not capability.
>
> **What would refute the output.** I wrote these down before sending this.
> 1. Two different typed arrangements with the same parts and the same outcome vector can be distinguished from the outcome alone, without the intervention record.
> 2. One global effect measure reliably locates the live part, including when the global residual is near zero.
> 3. One rule covers every subset of the response with an error that keeps shrinking, and no overlapping condition needs its own loop.
> 4. Second- and third-order contact contributes no residual mass.
> 5. A frame can settle its own mechanism claim without a second, independent frame and without a fixed anchor.
> 6. A real intervention can remove a part while leaving everything else that bears on the result unchanged.
>
> A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them. I would value your verdict on stage 1 before pointing you at any of it.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical figures README: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - The formula and figures folder: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

— [name]
