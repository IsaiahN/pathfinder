# Outreach draft: check the residual before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** send the residual and the refuter first. The theory, the figures and the source links come only after the recipient has recorded a verdict on stage 1. If that order is reversed, the check is spent.

---

**Subject:** One check on an economic-policy objective, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question in welfare economics and would value a check on one output. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question in effect terms.** A policy designer has to install one target: total output, total welfare, individual freedom, or some combination. Which of these can be a legitimate objective, and what form must any legitimate objective have?
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The figures I am using give a shape rather than a verdict:
>
> 1. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces. Total output is produced by the economy it would score, so it cannot be the sole metric.
> 2. A total discards the per-slot signal. The residual is indexed by object, not aggregated, so a single sum can be near zero while one group is badly off.
> 3. For a question about people, the anchor is human judgment about people's lives. Output and welfare metrics are instruments that range against that anchor, not substitutes for it.
> 4. "Some combination" is a union of overlapping conditions, not a list. It has to be unbundled before weights are assigned, and the welfare/freedom overlap needs its own loop.
>
> The framework does not pick one of the three as *the* answer and does not certify a unique weighting. What it offers is the shape any admissible objective must have, with the open slot being the instrument that would measure it.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that output is bad, that averages are always wrong, that no metric may be used, or that this settles the normative question. Those are stronger claims than the figures support and I am not making them.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim (written before this check):**
> 1. a single global aggregate that preserves the live per-slot signal for the decision at hand;
> 2. an objective metric shown to be external to the frame that produces it and pinned when that frame is pushed;
> 3. a demonstration that output, welfare and freedom form a partition with no overlapping region;
> 4. a grounded social-welfare rule that all three domains admit as their objective, with grounded rather than authored weights;
> 5. an anchor for this question that is not human judgment, is constitutively about the question's subject, and does not move when any frame pushes on it.
>
> If any holds, I will narrow or retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the source links. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Arrival of the Fittest: The Tether Architecture (public essay): https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the formula is in the figures. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
