## Three findings in the wider literature that bear directly on this question

1. **Aggregation is a lossy transform.** OWID's happiness page gives a concrete confound: *"the average-by-age figure from the snapshot confounds two factors"*, so a total can point the wrong way. This is the empirical twin of Fig 1 :22, *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* It is corroboration, not the source of the verdict.
2. **The means and the end are different things.** Robeyns (2025) writes that *"money or economic growth will not be valued for their own sake, but only in so far as they contribute to an expansion of people's capabilities."* Fig 10 :47 says *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*. The two fields agree from different evidence pools.
3. **There is no automatic common preference.** Morreau (2025) writes that *"collective decisions cannot in general be made on the basis of a common preference"*. List (2022) adds Sen's richer-information route. This is why "some combination" cannot simply be weighted and summed without a grounded rule.

Overlap note: the four SEP entries share a venue and several citations (notably Sen). They are not four independent witnesses. The two OWID pages share authors and a data culture. The agreement between the SEP cluster and the OWID cluster is low-overlap and carries more weight than agreement within either cluster. All of it remains secondary: the verdict comes from the figures.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22**, *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full architecture before a recipient has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the residual and the refuter; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42**, *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A reader who has absorbed the framework before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28**, *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*. Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the sources.
- **Seat Map :163**, *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* A send selected for likely agreement produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Seat Map :283**, *"A room does not upgrade the label."* Adding recipients does not add an anchor.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the residual and the four-part shape, (2) the five pre-registered refuters and a request for a failure, (3) record their verdict, (4) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

**Stage 1.** Send the residual in effect terms and the refuter. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Do not ask for agreement and do not ask for trust. Record the verdict before any theory is shown.

**Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the source links. Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Their stage 1 verdict is never pooled as corroboration later, because a later agreement shares the evidence pool the links created (Fig 2 :40).

**Stage 3, method reuse.** The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer (Fig 4 :22). Each user states their own ground and writes their own refuter before running it, because `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction" says a frame cannot check the direction of its own question.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 work only if the stage-1 output survives. The output in `02_outreach_email.md` is the current form; if a refuter fires, it is revised before any further sending.
