# Is "some combination" a bond, and what order does the objective come in?

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The question's fourth option, "some combination", is not yet a term. Two things are owed: (1) what kind of composition a combination would be, and whether output, welfare and freedom are **isomers** under Fig 12's definition; (2) in what dependency order the objective link sits relative to the instrument and the policy lever.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 : What kind of term is "maximize X"?

Fig 12 :112: **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."** The Operators table defines ⋛ as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*. So *"maximize X"* installs a ⋛ comparison with X as the quantity compared. It is a **goal**, not a description of the world. The question "which objective?" is therefore asking which comparison to install, not which object exists. That is why Fig 3 :9-10 places the objective at link 3, *"it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms"*.

**Falsifier:** show that "maximize X" is a description rather than a comparison, so that installing it does not commit to a threshold. If so, the Fig 12 reading does not apply.

### Q2 : Are the three objectives isomers?

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

- Written as the question writes them, output `{goods}, welfare {utility states}, freedom {opportunities}` share no atom except the people affected. Under Fig 12 they are **not** isomers; they are members of a **functional group** of policy evaluations, with different atoms in the slots.
- The stronger version: write all three over the full shared basis `{persons, states of affairs, opportunities, time}` and let the bond differ. Output is production (⇒) of goods; welfare is comparison (⋛) of utility states; freedom is disjunction (∥) of feasible opportunity sets. Same atoms, different bonds, different substance: a real isomer claim under Fig 12.
- That stronger version is `specified` and unmeasured. It requires showing each objective can be written over the full basis without remainder. Fig 12 :97-98 gives the precedent: *"silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition and different properties, which is what forced structure into the notation."*
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if any one objective requires an atom the others do not (for example, if freedom requires a non-domination relation irreducible to an opportunity set), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. Triangulated for the figure definition (Fig 12), single-source for the economic mapping.

### Q3 : Is a weighted sum a legal bond for the combination?

Fig 12 :14-17 describes the flat-formula collapse: *"the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not"*, so *"nothing downstream can tell them apart"*. A weight vector **is** an arrangement, but only if its provenance is recorded. Without provenance, "40 percent output, 40 percent welfare, 20 percent freedom" is an ingredient list with the bond left implicit.

Fig 10 :47: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**. Fig 10 :49: **"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."** Weights that decide policy outcomes are conventions that have crossed into verdicts. A source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :12-13) are owed before they can be used.

**Falsifier:** supply a bond with typed operands, a recorded provenance for each weight, and a shadow test showing which gap the weight explains. Then the combination is a checkable term rather than the defect named here.

### Q4 : What is the order?

Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** Formula step 2 refuses before it mints. So the order is:

1. **Refuse** a frame-internal sole metric (Formula step 2, Fig 1 :27).
2. **Describe R** in effect terms, per slot (Fig 1 :19, Fig 9 :27-28).
3. **Unbundle** the union and give the welfare/freedom overlap its own loop (Fig 9 :54-55).
4. **Build or identify the instrument** for link 3 (Fig 3 :27). This is the open slot of the shape.
5. **Settle** the metric against the ground (Formula step 5). Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."** Only then can the metric serve as an atom in a policy composition.
6. **Compose** the policy lever (link 4) over settled metrics. Not before.

**Falsifier:** show that a policy lever can be legitimately optimized before its objective has a ground-settled instrument, in the sense that the lever's success is checkable without that instrument. If so, steps 4 to 6 are not a dependency order.

### Q5 : What analogue does the framework itself supply?

Fig 8 :45: **"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."** A low-overlap welfare-measurement frame and a low-overlap capability-measurement frame, each ranged separately against the ground, can reveal what neither alone carries. This is Fig 2 :35, **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."** The analogue governs the *acquisition* of the instrument in step 4, not the choice of objective. Agreement between two frames that share the same data is one witness wearing two names (Fig 2 :40).

## CONCLUSION

"Maximize X" is a comparison bond installing a goal, not a description. At minimum the three objectives form a functional group; over a full shared basis they are a testable isomer claim with a stated falsifier. "Some combination" is not a legal term until a typed bond and provenanced weights are supplied, and until the union is unbundled first. The objective is link 3 and is upstream of the policy lever, so the instrument must be settled before the lever is optimized. The framework returns this order and this shape; it does not name which metric fills the slot.
