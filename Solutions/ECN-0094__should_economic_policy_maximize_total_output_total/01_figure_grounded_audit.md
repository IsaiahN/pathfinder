## 0. The question under audit

> Should economic policy maximize total output, total welfare, individual freedom, or some combination?

There is no author's draft to audit here, only the question. So the audit starts from the question's own terms and tests each candidate objective against the figures. The four options are not four answers to the same well-posed question until their domains are named. That naming is the census below.

---

## 1. The residual in effect terms

**R:** a policy designer must install one objective function (or one weighted bundle) as the target that policy increases. The residual is the gap between what that objective predicts will count as progress and what actually registers against people's lives.

Describe R by the three predicates Fig 9 :22 asks for, arity, symmetry and scale:

- **Arity:** three candidate operands (output, welfare, freedom) plus a fourth instruction (combine them). A combination is not a new objective until a bond is named. Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* Maximizing is the comparison, not the operands.
- **Symmetry:** the three operands are not interchangeable. Output is a quantity produced by the economic frame. Welfare is an aggregate of positions across people. Freedom is a per-person opportunity condition. Swapping any two changes what is being measured and what the ground would register. Under Fig 12 they are distinct terms, not spellings of one term.
- **Scale:** output and welfare are presented as totals, that is, one global number. Freedom is presented as individual, that is, per slot. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* The mismatch of scale is the first thing the figures register.

**Strain test before census:** if "total welfare" only fits the figures by reading "total" loosely, the word is doing work the figures do not license. It is kept as the question's word and tested in F2 and F4 rather than smoothed over.

## 2. Domain census

One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor. No nameable anchor means it is not yet a domain.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1, the policy-design frame | planner, instruments, objective function | the score being maximized | the ground: outcomes for people, which do not move because a frame relabels them | partition boundary (what may be the metric) |
| D2, the measurement frame | statistical offices, survey designers, index builders | metrics (GDP, HDI, life satisfaction, capability indices) | the thing measured, provided the metric does not become the target | partition boundary (instrument versus anchor) |
| D3, the social-choice / aggregation frame | individuals with preferences and rights | preference orderings, interpersonal comparisons | unanimity where it is genuine; otherwise no unique admitted common preference | union with overlap (welfare and freedom conflict) |
| D4, the ethical ground | people, their capabilities and freedoms | well-being, agency, real opportunity | human judgment, because the question is constitutively about people | ground, not a frame |

**Anchor checks.** D1 has an anchor only if there is something that settles the objective and does not move when the planner pushes on it. Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* D2's anchor is legitimate only while the metric remains an instrument. D3 has no unique common preference under the conditions social choice requires (world source, Morreau 2025; List 2022), so its anchor is conditional. D4 is the legitimate anchor for a "should" question: Fig 2 :36-37: *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."*

**Partition or union?** The question's own phrase "or some combination" proposes a union. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Welfare and freedom overlap (the liberal paradox in the world literature), so the combination is a **compound with its own loop**, not three independent maximands added together.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** The objective is link 3 of the dependency chain: Fig 3 :9-10: *"3 · the objective"* / *"it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms"*. Policy planning (link 4) consumes what the objective produces. Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Before choosing a lever, the objective needs an instrument: Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* So D2 is upstream of D1. Refusal precedes mint (Formula step 2), so D1 must refuse a frame-internal metric before minting a new combined one. D4 anchors all of them.

**Re-census note.** An earlier pass treated "individual freedom" as a fourth domain. It was folded into D3 and D4: as a preference/right it belongs to the social-choice frame, as a real opportunity it belongs to the ethical ground. The correction is kept here rather than erased.

## 4. Findings

### F1 : "Maximize total output" lets the frame score itself (decisive against that option)

Fig 1 :23: **"The ground enters as the only metric."** Fig 1 :24-26 names the excluded quantities (coverage, compression achieved, predicates minted, credibility accrued). Fig 1 :27: **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."**

Total output is produced by the economy, which is the system whose policy is being scored. Installing it as the sole metric makes the economic frame its own judge. That is the named failure, not a matter of degree. Fig 6 :23 adds: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."** More output optimization stays inside the same closure and cannot import welfare or freedom.

**Refuter:** show an output metric that is not produced by the scored system and that does not move when the policy frame pushes on it (Fig 2 :29 anchor test). If such a metric exists, F1 does not apply to it.

**Mode:** `specified`. Triangulated across Fig 1 and Fig 6 (two different figures, low overlap: one about metric legitimacy, one about closure).

### F2 : "Total" discards the live slot (decisive against the aggregate form)

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** Fig 1 :13: **"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"**.

A total can be near zero change while one group's position is badly wrong. Fig 1 :20: a *"one live slot"* is *"a legal state and not an inert"* one. The distribution is not a secondary refinement of the total; it is where the signal lives. World corroboration: Ortiz-Ospina, Acisu and Roser (2017) write that *"as with any other aggregate indicator of social progress, averages need to be interpreted carefully, even if they make sense arithmetically"* and give the age/cohort confound as the mechanism.

**Refuter:** demonstrate that a single aggregate preserves the per-slot signal that a disaggregated reading carries, for the decision at hand. If it does, F2 fails for that aggregate.

**Mode:** `specified`. Triangulated across Fig 1 and an empirical data source (different evidence pools).

### F3 : The anchor for a "should" question about people is human judgment, not a metric

Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."** Fig 2 :36-37: **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."** / **"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."** Fig 2 :37-38: *"It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."*

"Should policy maximize X" is constitutively about people. So the ground is people's judgment about their lives, and output, welfare and freedom indices are instruments that range against it. An instrument can be wrong on axes it does not measure. This is why no single index can be installed as the final answer without a ground check.

**Refuter:** show an anchor for this question that is not human judgment, does not move when any frame pushes on it, and is constitutively about the question's subject. If one is produced and passes the Fig 2 test, F3 is withdrawn.

**Mode:** `specified`. Triangulated across Fig 2 and the capability-approach world source (Robeyns 2025: ends versus means), which is a different field.

### F4 : "Maximize total welfare" needs a social choice rule the figures do not supply

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."** The figures return a shape: a social welfare ordering must be pinned to an anchor and must keep the per-slot signal. They do not name a particular aggregation rule. World sources make the same point from a different direction. Morreau (2025): *"collective decisions cannot in general be made on the basis of a common preference, that assimilates the tastes and values of all the individual men and women who make up a society."* List (2022) reports Sen's response: *"social decisions require a richer informational basis"*, and that it may be *"justifiable and even necessary to use additional information over and above ordinal preferences"*.

So "total welfare" is not a single well-formed objective until a rule for interpersonal comparison is chosen and grounded. That choice is a convention that decides an outcome: Fig 10 :47: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"** and Fig 10 :49: **"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."**

**Refuter:** exhibit a welfare aggregation rule that satisfies the conditions the discipline requires, is admitted by all three domains, and whose weighing is itself grounded rather than authored. Then "total welfare" is a well-formed candidate and the figures would permit comparing it directly.

**Mode:** `specified`. Triangulated across Fig 9, Fig 10 and two social-choice sources.

### F5 : "Individual freedom" resists the total form and overlaps with welfare

Freedom is indexed per person, so the Fig 1 :19 per-slot rule applies to it directly. It also overlaps with welfare rather than sitting beside it: the liberal paradox in the world literature (List 2022, Sen 1970a) shows that a Pareto condition and a minimal personal-domain condition conflict. Fig 9 :55 therefore governs it: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** The overlap is a compound with its own loop, not a harder case of either condition. Fig 9 :56-58: *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*

**Refuter:** show that freedom and welfare form a partition (no overlapping region), so each can be maximized on its own domain without a compound loop. If they do, F5 fails.

**Mode:** `specified`. Triangulated across Fig 1, Fig 9 and the social-choice source.

### F6 : "Some combination" names no bond and skips the unbundling step

Fig 12 :112: **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."** A weighted sum of output, welfare and freedom would be a bond, but no bond is named. If it is a comparison (⋛), the Operators table defines it as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* and three operands are not two. If it is conjunction (+), Fig 12 treats it as a symmetric bond under which the ingredients are merely co-present; it records the ingredients and not the arrangement, which is the flat-formula collapse Fig 12 :5-8 names.

More important, Fig 9 :54 requires unbundling **before** searching: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** Picking weights first and calling the result "the combination" is frame-first, residual-second, which Fig 9 :27-28 forbids: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**.

**Refuter:** supply an explicit bond with typed operands, a provenance for the weights, and a prior unbundling of the overlap. Then the combination is a checkable term rather than the defect named here.

**Mode:** `specified`. Triangulated across Fig 9 and Fig 12, with Fig 10 :47 for the status of the weights.

### F7 : The objective is link 3, and it needs an instrument before policy can be planned

Fig 3 :9-10: **"3 · the objective"** / **"it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms"**. Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** This is the link welfare economics research actually works on: constructing metrics that can fail. Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** Effort spent designing better policy instruments (link 4) cannot rescue an objective that has no instrument.

**Refuter:** show that the objective link already has a ground-pinned instrument for each of the three candidates, so the dependency order does not bite. The literature cited here says the opposite; a changed literature would fire this refuter.

**Mode:** `specified`. Single-source for the figure rule (Fig 3), corroborated by the measurement literature (different pool).

### F8 : A metric that becomes the target stops being a convention

Fig 10 :47: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**. Fig 11 :40: **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."** If a government maximizes a metric, the metric is no longer a neutral description and no longer an independent check on the policy. Fig 4 :30 requires the round trip: **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."** The gap between the metric and the thing it stands for has to be measured, not assumed zero.

World corroboration: Our World in Data (2017) reports two different survey instruments for subjective well-being that correlate but are *"not identical (there is substantial dispersion, with many countries sharing the same score in one variable but diverging in the other)"*. One reading does not determine the source.

**Refuter:** show a metric that remains pinned (Fig 2 :29) after being made the policy target, with a measured round-trip gap that stays within the instrument's stated resolution. If so, the target/convention collapse does not occur for it.

**Mode:** `specified`. Triangulated across Fig 10, Fig 11, Fig 4 and an empirical source.

### F10 : Stating this as a verdict would violate the mode rule

Seat Map :281: *"An unmeasured quantity is a specification of what to measure and is worth having, it is not a verdict."* The figures can rule options out (F1, F2, F6) and constrain the shape of what remains. They cannot pick one of the three as *the* answer without a ground reading, and claiming they can would be a frame certifying its own choice.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Required before any outreach, Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* Written here before the conclusion in section 8.

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A single global aggregate is demonstrated to preserve the live per-slot signal for the decision in question (breaks F2).
2. An objective metric is shown to be external to the frame that produces it and pinned under push (breaks F1 and F3).
3. Output, welfare and freedom are shown to form a partition with no overlapping region, so each can be maximized separately (breaks F5 and F6).
4. A grounded social-welfare rule is produced that all three domains admit as their objective, with its weights grounded rather than authored (breaks F4 and F6).
5. An anchor for this question is exhibited that is not human judgment, is constitutively about the question's subject, and does not move when any frame pushes on it (breaks F3).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed accordingly. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Refuse "maximize total output" as the sole objective before trying to repair it (F1). This is a Formula step 2 refusal, not a softer reading.
2. Replace every "total" with a per-slot reading plus an explicit statement of which slot is live and what the aggregate hides (F2).
3. State the anchor (people's judgment about their lives) and rank output, welfare and freedom metrics as instruments ranging against it (F3, F8).
4. Unbundle the combination before assigning weights. Give the welfare/freedom overlap its own loop (F5, F6).
5. Build or identify the instrument for link 3 before optimizing link 4 (F7).
6. Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** Send the method (refuse, measure, unbundle, ground) rather than the conclusion. Ask what would refute it.

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is any option already a ground?** No. All three are frame terms; Fig 2 :29 requires the anchor to be outside frames.
2. **Does output qualify?** No as sole metric (F1). Yes as instrument if ranged against the ground.
3. **Does a total qualify?** No for the decision at hand (F2), because the per-slot signal is the aim.
4. **Does welfare qualify?** Not until a grounded interpersonal comparison exists (F4). Morreau and List corroborate from social choice.
5. **Does freedom qualify?** As a per-slot real opportunity, yes in principle; as an addend in a total, no (F5).
6. **Does the combination qualify?** Not as written: no bond, no unbundling, unprovenanced weights (F6).
7. **Route when no term closes R?** SEARCHED first (are we missing an instrument, Fig 3 :27?), then INWARD (sharpen the per-slot instrument), then OUTWARD (import a frame whose closure predicts the shape, Fig 6 :23). INWARD and OUTWARD were kept distinct.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives, narrowed: an admissible policy objective must be anchored to people's judgment, keep the per-slot signal, remain an instrument rather than a self-scoring frame quantity, and be unbundled before any combination is assigned. The framework does not select output, welfare or freedom as *the* answer, and it does not certify a unique weighting. What survives, what is narrowed and what is retracted are all recorded above. Strains after re-census: none hidden; the one live item is that no measurement establishes which metric best serves as instrument in a given polity, which is recorded as `grounded` and not established here.

## 8. Conclusion (written after the refuter in section 5)

No single one of the three objectives is licensed by the figures as *the* maximand, and "some combination" is not licensed in the form the question states it. What is licensed is a shape with four constraints and one open slot. The open slot is the instrument for link 3, which a welfare economics ground could fill. The answer is `specified` on the constraints and `general` on the shape, and it is not a verdict that settles the question. A failure of any refuter in section 5 would narrow it; a ground reading of the instrument would move it toward `grounded`. Nothing in this folder claims that such a reading has occurred.
