# Expert verification packet: ECN-0094

**What this is:** a claim in a form a welfare economist or social-choice theorist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the four constraints, `general` on the shape. Not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed to settle which objective a policy should maximize.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

No single one of total output, total welfare and individual freedom is licensed by the figures as *the* maximand, and "some combination" is not licensed as stated. An admissible objective must have four properties: it must not be a frame-produced quantity used to score that frame; it must keep the per-slot signal rather than collapse into a total; it must treat its metrics as instruments ranged against human judgment about people's lives, which is the legitimate anchor for a question constitutively about people; and any combination must be unbundled before weights are assigned, because the objectives overlap and the overlap is a compound with its own loop. The framework returns the shape and the order, not the name of the metric that fills the slot.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 : framework-internal; checkable by reading Fig 1 and Fig 2

- **Statement:** a total cannot be the sole objective, and the anchor for a question about people is human judgment, not a metric the frame produces.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19, *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22, *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 1 :27, *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Fig 2 :36-37, *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."*
- **What to check:** is the per-slot reading a constraint on the objective, or only a refinement of measurement? Does the anchor test (Fig 2 :29, *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks"*) actually apply to a "should" question?
- **Refuter:** exhibit a single aggregate that preserves the live per-slot signal for the decision at hand, or an objective metric external to the frame that produces it and pinned under push. Either fires F1 or F2.

### C2 : economics-facing; checkable against social choice and measurement practice

- **Statement:** "maximize total welfare" is not a well-formed objective until a grounded interpersonal-comparison rule exists, and welfare and freedom overlap rather than partitioning the space.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55, *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :54, *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :32, *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- **What to check:** does the social-choice literature support the no-common-preference premise (Morreau 2025, List 2022)? Is the Sen liberal paradox a genuine overlap between the welfare and freedom conditions, or a separable constraint? Is the capability approach's means/ends distinction a different frame or the same evidence pool restated?
- **Refuter:** produce a grounded aggregation rule all three domains admit, or show that output, welfare and freedom form a partition with no overlapping region. Either fires F4, F5 or F6.

### C3 : framework-internal derivation of the order

- **Statement:** the objective is link 3 of the dependency chain and is upstream of the policy lever; a metric must be settled before it can serve as an atom in a policy composition.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 3 :10, *"it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms"*. Fig 3 :19, *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Fig 3 :27, *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Fig 12 :104, *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."*
- **What to check:** is the dependency order right, or can a policy lever be legitimately optimized before its objective has a ground-settled instrument? Does Fig 12's settled-molecule rule transfer from composed terms to metrics?
- **Refuter:** show a lever whose success is checkable without a settled objective instrument, or that a settled metric cannot serve as an atom. Either breaks the order.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that output is bad, that growth is unimportant, or that averages are always wrong.
- Not that welfare or freedom is the true objective, and not that any weighting is certified.
- Not that the combination is forbidden. It is not yet a term until a typed bond, provenance for the weights and an unbundling are supplied.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by measurement.
- Not a theory of everything and not a final answer. The slot stays open.
- Not a ground reading. This packet is a simulated derivation checked against the figures.

## 5. What would settle it

A measurement, not agreement.

1. A distribution-sensitive metric that changes the decision when a badly off group is exposed (fires C1's first refuter).
2. An anchor test (Fig 2 :29) showing a candidate metric is pinned under push (fires C1's second refuter).
3. A grounded aggregation rule admitted by all three domains, or a demonstrated partition (fires C2).
4. Evidence that a lever can be checked without a settled objective instrument (fires C3).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, :22, :27 and Fig 2 :36-37, four short passages.
2. Check C2 against your own knowledge: is the no-common-preference premise sound, and is the welfare/freedom overlap real?
3. Name one instrument that would preserve the per-slot signal for a policy decision, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
