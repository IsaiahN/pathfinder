# Outreach draft: check the diagnostic before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the pre-registered refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 has been read and the three sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` have been checked. If the diagnostic does not survive, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** What would actually make you revise a settled cosmological model?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific question I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** there is a difference between a surprising observation and a model-changing one. A new datum whose residual shrinks when the model is re-fit is rebinding: the model has absorbed it, and nothing new is owed. What would force a substantial revision or an abandonment is a residual that persists and does not trend down, that comes from frames drawing on genuinely different evidence, and that survives a better instrument built on the same signal. Abandonment is a different operation from revision: revision adds a term, abandonment refuses one.
>
> In figure language, this is a shape, not a name. It does not say which new physics is right. It says what any candidate piece of evidence would have to be like before the community's verdict should move: independent, persistent, source-discriminated, and paid for by a replacement that still fits the whole suite of observations.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the Hubble tension is already decisive, that any particular anomaly kills ΛCDM, or that elegance or consensus can settle it. A disagreement that will not shrink may be several well-formed questions rather than one, and it should be split before it is searched. A large collaboration's agreement is a budget to try a claim, not a verdict.
>
> **What would refute the diagnostic** (written down before this was sent):
> 1. a case where the community treats an observation as model-changing although the residual trends down under a re-fit;
> 2. a case where a model changes on agreement or authority with no independent ground reading that could have gone the other way;
> 3. a case where a single instrument sharing the model's evidence pool changes the model;
> 4. a case where a new term is kept although it fits one occasion and breaks the rest of the data.
>
> If any of these holds, I withdraw the classification. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local path shared on request): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - The eight-step loop and the mode rule: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - Public examples that bear on this question: Karl Popper, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/popper/ ; Scientific Revolutions, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/scientific-revolutions/ ; Hubble's law and the Hubble tension, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubble_tension
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Two-stage protocol

1. **Stage 1:** send the diagnostic and its refuter. Record the reader's verdict on it **before** any framework material is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the links. The figures, not the summary, are the authority. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40: agreement from a shared evidence pool is not a verdict).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the eight-step loop as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and labels their own mode.
