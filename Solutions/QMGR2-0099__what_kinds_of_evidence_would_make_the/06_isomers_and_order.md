### Q1 — What order do the tests come in?

`Fig 3 :20-21` (verbatim figure line):

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**

`Fig 3 :25-27`: perception and planning have obvious tests; the middle link, having terms for what a situation affords and saying what would count as progress, has neither, "so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments". `Fig 3 :27`: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

Mapping the five links onto this question:

| link | question form | arrives when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 perception | is anything actually returning a reading that fails to resolve? | an instrument already returning something (Fig 6 :34) |
| 2 vocabulary | is this re-binding, a new term, or a refusal? | the five bins are named (Fig 5 :10-14, :53) |
| 3 objective | does the residual persist and not trend down? | the shrinking test (Fig 5 :59). **This is the break.** |
| 4 planning | which probe is aimed next, and is it independent? | Fig 9 :27-28, Fig 2 :31 |
| 5 carry | what is recorded with provenance for the community? | Fig 12 lineage, Fig 10 :62-66 |

Optimising link 4 (better telescopes, more data) cannot rescue a broken link 3 (no criterion for what would count as progress). `Fig 3 :19`: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* So the order is: persistence first, independence second, discriminator third, replacement bargain fourth, community verdict last. A checklist printed in the opposite order is a recording of past practice, not a generator.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a case is found where the community correctly revised a model with link 3 unmeasured (no persistence or shrinking test), the dependency order is wrong and must be re-censused.

### Q2 — Are "abandon" and "revise" isomers?

`Fig 12 :117` (verbatim figure line):

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

`Fig 12 :46-47`: a bond is the typed relation between two operands, and *"which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."* `Fig 12 :49`: *"The seven bonds, and whether the order of the operands matters. Negation is the eighth operator and is not a bond."* The Operators table adds (verbatim): *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*, and its amendment states that negation acts on one operand and does not enter the bond count.

So:

- **Substantial revision** is a **mint**: a new molecule composed from held atoms inside closure(Γ) (`Fig 6 :21`: *"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."*). It uses a binary bond, and the reach grows by what was composed.
- **Abandonment** is a **negation**: a unary operator on one held term. `Fig 5 :55` (verbatim): *"What it removes is not a term but a term's standing to be composed over. Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach."*

Under `Fig 12 :117` they are **not isomers**: they are not the same atoms under a different binary bond. One joins operands; the other refuses one. They are different operator classes, distinguished by arity before the symmetry question even arises (`Fig 12 :134-136`). Calling them "two degrees of theory change" is the flat-formula error Fig 12 names: the notation records an ingredient list and loses the relation that carries the meaning.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a case is found where refusing a held term can be written as a binary bond between that term and another (so the symmetry test applies), the operator-class distinction fails and the two outcomes must be re-censused as isomers. The Operators table's seven tests would then need to identify it; if it fails all seven, it is a claim not yet made, not a missing bond (`Fig 12 :90-91`).

### Q3 — Is "early-universe H0" versus "late-universe H0" an isomer pair?

Over the shared atom set {CMB, ΛCDM parameters, distance ladder, BAO, gravitational lensing}, the two determinations compose differently:

- early: ΛCDM parameters plus CMB acoustic scale, composed through the model to an inferred H0;
- late: Cepheids plus supernovae, composed through a distance ladder to a measured H0.

They share atoms and differ in route. Under `Fig 12 :117` that is an **isomer** reading at the level of determinations: same atoms, different arrangement, different quantity in the sense that one is model-inferred and the other ladder-measured. `Fig 13 :128-130` (verbatim): *"the order is not recoverable from the artefact. It is recoverable from the lineage. A term carries the atoms it was built from and the bonds that joined them. That record is not documentation of the term. It is the term."*

This is a structural analogy, not a claim about particle physics. Its value is procedural: a disagreement between these two determinations can only be diagnosed from the lineage, which is exactly why the community's response has been to re-measure each rung and to build independent determinations (time delays, TRGB, kilonova). `Fig 8 :50-52`: convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents; only the record separates them.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if both determinations can be written with the same atoms in the same bond arrangement, the isomer reading fails and they are one method with two error bars.

### Q4 — What about "shape, not a name" here?

`Fig 9 :32`: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* The order and the operator classes are structural results about the form of the evidence; they do not name which systematic or which new physics is correct. Naming one is a binding from the physics frame with provenance, as `05_what_shape_means.md` section 5 narrows the rule. No seventh term is introduced anywhere in this derivation.

## CONCLUSION

The evidence tests are **ordered by dependency**: register a reading, name its bin, apply the persistence test, check independence, aim the discriminator, then record with provenance. The persistence link is where the chain usually breaks, and optimising the other links cannot rescue it. "Substantially revise" and "abandon" are **not isomers**: revision is a binary-bond mint inside the closure, abandonment is a unary negation that removes a term's standing to be composed over and lowers the reach. The early- and late-universe determinations are a level-relative isomer pair whose difference is carried by the lineage, which is why the community's work is a re-measurement of routes rather than a single decisive number.

What survives is a generator: an ordered checklist that distinguishes rebinding from revision from refusal. What does not survive is any claim about which new physics would win.
