## Q1 - Are two business units with the same resources isomers?

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** (Fig 12 :117)
> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** (Fig 12 :124)
> **"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."** (Fig 12 :92)
> **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."** (Fig 12 :5)

Two units with an identical list of assets and capabilities have the same atoms. If their bonds differ, one composes those atoms as a repeated sequence and the other as a reconfigurable network, they are **isomers** under Fig 12 :117: same atoms, different bond, different behaviour, with the difference invisible in the resource list. This is the figure-grounded version of "it is not what you own, it is how the units are linked", but stronger, because the arrangement is itself a substance carrying information the ingredients do not.

If the two units share only a pattern (both run `unit → shared activity → market`) while differing in which atoms occupy the slots, the weaker claim holds: they are members of the same **functional group** (Fig 12 :124), the qualifier saying which member.

The distinction is testable. A resource-list comparison that finds no difference between two units cannot distinguish isomer from functional-group; the bond has to be recorded. Fig 8 :50 adds the operator: *"shared atoms are not the signature. Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."* So identical atoms may be independently reached, and that is corroboration, not overlap.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two units with identical atoms and identical bonds nevertheless diverge in the surplus they produce with no difference in ground contact or imported atoms, the isomer framing is insufficient and the difference must be habitat (D2), which moves the explanation out of D3.

**Status:** `specified`, triangulated for the legality of the reading (Fig 12 and Fig 8, different figures), single-source for the business application. The world corroboration is Porter's *"sharing of activities"* and Iversen's complementarities, but both are the same strategy evidence pool.

## Q2 - What order do the conditions bind in?

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."** (Fig 3 :20-21)
> **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** (Fig 3 :19)
> **"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."** (Fig 3 :26)

Order for a center facing a proposed addition:

1. **Perception.** Can it read the market's return at all, per unit? Failure here is visible and has obvious tests (segment P&L).
2. **Vocabulary.** Does Γ have terms for what the added business affords, and can it say what would count as progress there? This is the middle link.
3. **Objective.** Can it state the comparison that would count as synergy? Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* A vocabulary with only ingredients cannot state a goal.
4. **Planning.** A route to that objective under incomplete information (the integration plan).
5. **Learn and carry.** Name what worked, verify it, carry it to the next unit.

The two conditions map onto this chain: C1 (closure surplus) is mostly a failure of links 2 and 3, because the center has no term for what the target affords and cannot state the comparison that would count as a surplus. C2 (membrane capacity) is mostly a failure of link 5, because the center names what worked but carries a recording rather than a method (Fig 4 :25). That mapping is why a single lever cannot clear both (Fig 9 :55).

The order also decides which instrument to buy first. Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Link 1 has accounting and share data; link 5 has almost none. Budget therefore piles up at links 1 and 4 while the break sits at 2 and 5.

**Falsifier:** a diversified firm whose chain demonstrably works at 1, 2 and 3 and fails only at 4. That would falsify the "usually middle" claim of F8 in the primary audit.

## Q3 - What must the membrane preserve?

> **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** (Fig 4 :22)
> **"upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."** (Fig 4 :23)
> **"The level a method lands on remakes it. A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."** (Fig 4 :34-35)
> **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"** (Fig 10 :24)

A portfolio is manageable when the center carries up methods, that is, rules that can be reapplied under new conditions, and sends down head starts, never replays. Simon 1962 names the structural condition: *"Intra-component linkages are generally stronger than intercomponent linkages"*, so the short-run behaviour of a unit is approximately independent of the others. Brooks 1975 names the failure: *"Adding manpower to a late software project makes it later"*, because training and intercommunication consume the gain from partitioning. Translated to diversification: if the center becomes the required path for every inter-unit coordination, intra-unit linkages are no longer stronger than inter-unit ones, and the hierarchy stops being nearly decomposable.

The round trip is the measurement. Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* The gap is not an error to eliminate but *"the only honest report of how much was given up"* (Fig 4 :31-32). A diversification decision that never measures R_T is navigating by dead reckoning.

**Falsifier:** a firm where the center's abstractions can be pushed back down into units with no loss that changes a decision, across two different habitats. That would break the lossy-transform claim in Fig 4 :27 and Q3 with it.

## Q4 - Competing explanations, and what each can and cannot reach

| candidate explanation | what it names | figure-line bearing | what it cannot reach |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Synergy by sharing** (Porter 1987: transfer skills, share activities) | the bond between units | close to Fig 8 :46, *"that surplus is where imports live"* | A business-pool binding with no described residual; does not say what would refute it; does not separate C1 from C2 |
| **Related diversification** (Berger and Ofek 1995: same two-digit SIC code reduces the discount) | an atom-level label for similarity | re-census R1: relatedness is a proxy for closure difference, not the variable (Fig 8 :50) | Cannot distinguish convergence at maximum independence from one frame wearing two names; an average, not a named unit (Fig 1 :22) |
| **Diversification discount / internal capital markets** (Berger and Ofek; Lamont and Polk) | cross-subsidy, overinvestment | corroborates the cost side and channel failure (Fig 10 :26) | Reports an average effect; a variance-based filter for budget, never a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60) |
| **Complementarities** (Iversen 2000: indivisible assets, vertical and horizontal fit) | a priced bond | matches Fig 12 :99, *"A bond costs what it could have been"* | Names the category, not the residual that aims it; no source-discriminating instrument |
| **Span of control / bureaucracy** (practitioner management) | communication overload | matches Fig 10 :26 and Brooks 1975 at a different stratum | Different evidence pool and different anchor; corroborates mechanism, not the firm-level fact |
| **Complexity and hierarchy** (Simon 1962: near decomposability) | the membrane's structural condition | matches Fig 4 :22 and :27 | A general mechanism, not a measurement of a particular portfolio |
| **Response diversity and resilience** (Angeler et al. 2018; Mori et al. 2013) | value of difference relative to disturbance | low-overlap corroboration of F1 and F6 | An analogue from ecology; no firm-level market measurement |
| **Luck / information asymmetry** | an unearned early reading that persists while unfuted | Fig 8 :40: *"Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts"* | Cannot be excluded from inside a frame; Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Honest `gap`, not a rival verdict |

No candidate is discarded by prestige or familiarity. Each is ranged against the same residual, in the order Fig 8 :53 requires (*"Residual first, frame second"*). The point is not to pick one; it is to notice that the first four are all in one evidence pool (Fig 2 :31) and that the low-overlap fields corroborate the mechanism, not the firm-level fact.

## Q5 - Does the shape generate the literature's answers?

Partly. The shape can generate:

- the sharing-and-complementarities emphasis, as a priced bond where closures differ and a residual was described (C1);
- the related-diversification finding, as a proxy for closure difference after re-census R1, not as the variable itself;
- the diversification discount and cross-subsidy findings, as channel failure and pooled readings (C2);
- the core-rigidity emphasis, as valence relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49-51) and catalyst/spent parts (Fig 12 :119);
- the span-of-control concern, as membrane overload (Fig 4 :27, Fig 10 :26) and the loss of near-decomposability (Simon 1962).

It cannot generate the *name* of the specific business or bond that will work next. That is the `gap` recorded in F10 of `01`. It is a gap at the instrument boundary, not a silence: Fig 13 :134 (*"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*) and Fig 8 :39 apply. The source essay states why: an instrument that reads a result *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists"*.

## CONCLUSION

Two units with the same atoms but different bonds are isomers under Fig 12 :117; two units sharing only a `unit → shared activity → market` pattern are members of one functional group under Fig 12 :124. The conditions bind in dependency order, with the middle link (vocabulary and objective) and the carry link the usual breaks, which is why C1 and C2 need different instruments. The membrane must carry methods upward and head starts downward and must measure R_T, or the firm navigates by dead reckoning. The standard diversification explanations are mostly bindings of one evidence pool and are generated by the shape only up to the point where a name would be required. The isomer claim, the order claim and the membrane claim each have a written falsifier; the naming gap is declared rather than filled.
