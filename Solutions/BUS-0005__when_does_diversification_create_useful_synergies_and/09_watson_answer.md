## QUESTION

When does diversification create useful synergies, and when does it make a company harder to manage?

Restated as an effect: why does adding a business unit lower the gap between what the portfolio predicts the market will return and what the market actually returns in some cases, and raise it in others? ## VERDICT (from the figures)

**The figure-derived verdict:** diversification creates a useful surplus when the added unit's closure differs from the portfolio's at the evidence level, the union is aimed by a described residual and its bond is priced, and the organizational membrane can carry the composition with methods crossing up, head starts coming down, the channel reachable and R_T measured. It makes a company harder to manage when either condition fails, and the capability that produced the first win can change valence when the new residual arrives. The verdict is the union kept separate per domain, never averaged. Mode `specified`. The business evidence pool corroborates the cost side and the need for real linkages, but its main apparent conflict (related diversification vs low-overlap surplus) was returned to the census and re-mapped, not used to flip the verdict. Low-overlap fields corroborate the mechanism, not the firm-level fact. Which specific businesses occupy the slots is a declared `gap`, not a hidden answer.

- **Cost if wrong:** misdirected intervention. A firm failing C1 (paper synergy) would be sold better integration machinery, or a firm failing C2 (real surplus, unmanageable) would be told to find more different businesses, and a single lever would be applied to a union that needs two. In outreach terms, an expert's twenty minutes would be spent refuting a shape that was never the right shape. The cost is real but bounded and largely reversible.
- **Undoable:** yes. No term is deleted if the claim fails; a refutation is added, and the reach falls because *"something was added rather than because anything was deleted"* (Fig 6 :42-43). The shape and its six falsifiers are recorded in a provenance trail so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-64).
- **Reversed by:** any of the six pre-registered falsifiers, in particular (1) a same-closure pair producing a sustained, ground-settled surplus after a control for selection, (2) an unaimed, unpriced diversification paying prospectively per unit, (3) unit count doubling with no rise in communication load or pooling and R staying low, (4) one metric predicting both description and verification costs and confirmed at the ground, (5) capability valence invariant across a diversification-driven change of residual, or (6) one lever restoring both conditions with no categorically different residue. A source with a genuinely different evidence pool showing that per-unit surplus is fully predicted by SIC relatedness alone would also reverse the re-census R1, and with it F1.
