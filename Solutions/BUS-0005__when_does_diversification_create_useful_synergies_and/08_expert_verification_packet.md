## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Whether diversification creates a surplus or makes a firm harder to manage is decided by two conditions that have to hold at once, not by whether the businesses are "related" on a list. First, the added unit must differ from the existing portfolio in the evidence it actually uses, so that the union reaches a return neither reaches alone; that difference must be aimed by a stated residual and paid for by a real link, a shared activity or transferred skill that carries information the operands do not, rather than by a synergy narrative. Second, the organization must be able to carry the composition: methods must travel from units to the center rather than recordings of one success, the round trip between the center's abstract view and the unit's actual situation must be measured, and the readings of demand, price and share must not be stale, saturated or pooled into an average. These are two different failure modes with two different instruments. A firm whose units are "two names for one frame" needs a different fix from a firm with a real surplus its center cannot handle. The capability that made the first business win can change sign when the new unit changes what the market rewards, so the strength and the rigidity can be the same object. The market, not internal fit claims, is the anchor. The claim is a shape that any candidate answer must have; it does not name which businesses, targets or industries win.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 8, 12 and 2

- **Statement:** synergy is a union surplus that exists only where closures differ; shared atoms are not the signature; high overlap produces two names for one frame and no surplus; the market is the anchor, not internal fit claims.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 8 :45 - *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."*
  - Fig 8 :46 - *"Composition crosses the boundary between them, and that surplus is where imports live."*
  - Fig 8 :49 - *"Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."*
  - Fig 8 :50 - *"But shared atoms are not the signature. Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."*
  - Fig 8 :5 - *"vocabulary, never an atom"* (the blend collapse)
  - Fig 2 :29 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."*
- **What a researcher can check against their own knowledge:** Berger and Ofek 1995 report that the *"value loss is smaller when the segments of the diversified firm are in the same two-digit SIC code"*, and Porter 1987 recommends related diversification. Does SIC relatedness measure evidence overlap, or only an atom-level label? If two same-SIC units can hold different closures and reach the same atom by convergence (Fig 8 :50), the apparent conflict with Fig 8 dissolves. A "no" to that is a real refutation of the mapping.
- **Refuter:** a pair of units with demonstrably the same closure (one evidence pool, one frame wearing two names by the Fig 8 :49 test) that nevertheless produces a sustained, ground-settled surplus under a source-discriminating measurement; or a diversification with no described residual and no priced bond that pays prospectively, per unit, after a control for selection. Either fires against C1.

### C2 - Framework-internal derivation plus a checkable engineering analogue

- **Statement:** "harder to manage" is a failure of the channel and the membrane, not a decay of the market; the transform between scales is lossy and must be measured as R_T; methods must cross up rather than recordings.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 10 :24 - *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*; Fig 10 :26 - *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*; Fig 10 :28 - *"you have lost touch"*
  - Fig 4 :22 - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*
  - Fig 4 :25 - *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*
  - Fig 4 :30 - *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*
  - Fig 12 :125 - *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*; Fig 12 :130 - *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."*
- **What to check against your own knowledge:** Simon 1962 writes that *"Intra-component linkages are generally stronger than intercomponent linkages"*; Brooks 1975: *"Adding manpower to a late software project makes it later"*, because, in Brooks's words, *"they could not coordinate"* once the teams could not talk with each other. Does a diversified firm whose center must approve every inter-unit coordination still satisfy Simon's near-decomposability condition? A documented case where it does, with R_T measured and small and performance high, is a real refutation.
- **Refuter:** a firm where the number of units doubles while communication load per unit and metric pooling do not increase, R_T stays measured and small, and R stays low; or a single prospective metric that predicts both the description cost and the verification cost of a diversification move and is confirmed at the ground. Either breaks idempotence and C2 with it.

### C3 - Structure and competing explanations

- **Statement:** the union must be unbundled before a lever is chosen; two units with identical atoms but different bonds are isomers, so a resource inventory alone cannot predict the surplus; the strength-to-rigidity flip follows from valence being relative to the residual.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 9 :57 - *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
  - Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
  - Fig 11 :50 - *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."*
  - Fig 12 :119 - *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."*
- **Refuter:** one intervention that restores both conditions in both failure types and leaves no categorically different residue; or two units with identical atoms and identical bonds whose surplus diverges with no difference in contact or imported atoms, which would move the explanation out of composition and into habitat alone; or a capability whose measured valence is invariant across a documented change of residual caused by diversification.

- Not `grounded`. No measurement has paid for any part of this.

## 5. What would settle it

A prospective, per-unit study that measures C1 and C2 separately, records bonds as well as atoms, and reports which condition failed before the decline. Concretely:

1. **Closure test:** do the two units draw on different evidence and hold different terms for what the market affords, or are they one frame wearing two names? Fig 8 :49-50.
2. **Aim and price test:** was a residual described before the target was chosen, and is the connecting bond priced (Fig 8 :21, Fig 12 :99)? A narrative alone fails this.
3. **Channel test:** are readings stale, saturated or pooled while the market keeps paying someone? Fig 10 :25-26.
4. **Membrane test:** is R_T measured (Fig 4 :30), are methods crossing up and head starts coming down (Fig 4 :22), and are intercomponent linkages still weaker than intracomponent ones (Simon 1962)?
5. **Source-discriminating instrument:** can the study distinguish a real shared activity from a paying habitat from cross-subsidy from luck when all produce the same valuation premium? The source essay says a reading alone *"cannot comment on the source"*.

## 7. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 8 :45-50, Fig 12 :117 and :99, Fig 10 :24-26, Fig 4 :22 and :30, Fig 9 :57. Ten short passages, about eight minutes.
2. Check C1 against your own knowledge of the related-diversification evidence: is SIC relatedness a measure of evidence overlap, or only a label? About five minutes.
3. Name one instrument that would discriminate a real shared activity from a paying habitat from cross-subsidy from luck using observable data, or say why none exists. About five minutes.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
