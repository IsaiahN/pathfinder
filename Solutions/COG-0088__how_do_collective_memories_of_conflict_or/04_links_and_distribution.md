## Wider literature that bears on the question

Three findings change how the shape claim should be stated, and all three were read before the conclusion was written.

1. **The memory is not a fixed record.** Rime et al. and Orianne and Eustache both describe reconstruction and present-context dependence. That is why the memory cannot be an anchor (F1) and why a "correct the content and the effect disappears" prediction is too simple.
2. **The effect splits by question type.** Li et al. report adaptive as well as maladaptive consequences. Fig 2 :39-41 explains the split without hand-waving: shared agreement is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention. This is the single most useful bridge between the figures and the field.
3. **Trust can be moved from outside the closure.** The out-group acknowledgment result in Figueiredo et al. and the vicarious-extinction and social-buffering results in Debiec and Olsson both say that an atom crossing from a different frame changes the response. That is Fig 6 :23 at the human and animal scales, and it is independent corroboration from a low-overlap field, not a restatement of the framework.

None of these sources is a reading of the Tether figures. They corroborate a shape; they do not settle it, and they cannot turn a `specified` claim into a `grounded` one.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 4 :22**: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Sending the full architecture before a recipient has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the refuter and the shape claim; offer the links after a verdict is recorded.
- **Fig 10 :42**: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Seat 4 must not hold the interiors. A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28**: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the article.
- **Fig 2 :40**: "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." A send selected for people already inclined to agree produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 10 :60**: "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it." State that the author of the framework has an interest in the reading; do not present the derivation as neutral because it is formal.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the residual and the two readings; (2) the shape claim and its four refuters; (3) ask what they would count as a failure and record the answer; (4) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer (and the optional third stage)

1. **Stage 1:** check the output against the ground. The output is a shape and four refuters, not a theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, read how it was produced. Their stage-1 verdict must already be on record; later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3, optional:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode. A method may cross up (Fig 4 :22), but each user still needs a ground.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. `08_expert_verification_packet.md` is the artifact to send; it carries the verbatim lines and the refuters in front of the links.
