# The Tether's answer: collective memory, reasoning, and trust

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the shape. Not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading and not a measurement of an effect size.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question bundles four conditions, not one: how the memory is transmitted, how it enters a present inference, how it affects trust between frames, and what survives displacement. Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either". Fig 9 :4-5 requires splitting before searching. Verdicts below are kept per domain and never averaged (Fig 1 :19, "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated").

## 2. The shape

1. **The memory is a prior, not a ground.** Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." A collective remembrance moves with the present social context (Rimé et al., 2015/2016), so it fails the anchor test. It is a belief held in individual frames, "Formed from its own history and from what it inherited, mixed by w" (Symbols :20), and a channel that can go stale: Fig 10 :24, "the ground does not decay, the channel does". Its effect on reasoning is therefore a change in what is treated as given, not a newly discovered fact about the present.
2. **The reasoning bias sits at the evidence rule, not only at the recalled content.** Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." The memory names a state; when it is used as a route, the frame sorts a present actor into an old category before the evidence is read. Paez and Liu (2009) report exactly this: collective memory "plays a cognitive-perceptual role by shaping the perception of interest, threat and others intentions", and "even conciliatory gestures from the out group may be misinterpreted as menacing". Fig 9 :28 explains why: "any frame you pick will seem to fit" once the frame is chosen first.
3. **Trust is an ordered comparison that must reach an anchor separately for each frame.** Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison." Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." People who inherit the same narrative are not two witnesses: Fig 2 :40, "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". So a population's uniform mistrust is not evidence that mistrust is warranted; it is the predicted shape of correlated frames.
4. **The effect splits by question type.** Fig 2 :39-41: "Correlation has valence." For a question constitutively about identity, shared memory is "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it". For a verdict about another group's trustworthiness, the same memory is disqualifying. This is why Li et al. (2023) find both adaptive and maladaptive consequences. Which question is asked decides which rule governs.
5. **What crosses generations is usually a recording, not a method.** Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." Fig 7 :13: "agreement compounds, evidence does not". More retelling increases uniformity, not contact with the ground.
6. **Trust changes when an atom crosses from a frame whose closure differs.** Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." The out-group acknowledgment result in Figueiredo et al. (trust rising when a rival leader acknowledges past suffering) and the vicarious-extinction and social-buffering results in Debiec and Olsson (2017) are instances of that move. It is an import with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :12-13), not a change in the in-group's memory.
7. **Displacement is habitat substitution, not removal.** Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :67: "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose." The two silent failure modes are what you failed to reproduce and what you unintentionally introduced. Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that conflict memory is always harmful. For identity questions it is a convention and is load-bearing (Fig 2 :41).
- Not that a remembered event is false, or that remembering it is a pathology. The framework refuses its *standing as ground*, not its content.
- Not a named mechanism, effect size, or causal estimate. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Candidate carriers (family narrative, schooling, commemoration, media) are bindings from the world frame, provenanced as such, not figure-derived names.
- Not a single population-level effect. Per-slot verdicts are required (Fig 1 :19, :22).
- Not a theory of everything, and not `grounded`. The framing returns a shape and a stop, not a final explanation.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

The figures point to instruments, not further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*, read this run: a spectrometer reads green but "cannot comment on the source"; the question was settled "not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument". Here the corresponding tests are:

1. **Anchor test.** Does the shared memory itself, not an external record, satisfy Fig 2 :29, :33? Present evidence says no (Rimé et al.).
2. **Independence test.** Are two groups with the same inherited narrative low-overlap frames by Fig 2 :31-32? If yes, Fig 2 :40 does not apply and their agreement is corroboration.
3. **Contact test.** Does repeating the memory across generations increase discrimination of present actors, or only agreement (Fig 11 :40, Fig 7 :13)?
4. **Content test.** Is the entire reasoning and trust effect captured by factual content, leaving no residual for closure, pool, or import?
5. **Crossing test.** Does an out-group signal (acknowledgment, acknowledgment of past suffering) move trust in the predicted direction, with the independence and anchor conditions controlled? Figueiredo et al. and Debiec and Olsson give preliminary positive results; neither is a ground reading of the figure claim.

Standing falsifiers from `01` §6: any one of the five firing withdraws the corresponding domain verdict. Until one is run, no part of this is `grounded`.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The frame vocabulary is a visible guess at how to slice these phenomena, not nature's own list. Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" and :135: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it." The answer stops where the available instruments cannot distinguish source from reading, and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. One strain remains named and open: whether collective representations count as the "quantities" that ⋛ compares at population scale needs an instrument, not an argument (Operators table :49). The essays' warning applies: "You may all agree and be wrong." Indiscernibility is not identity.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a mechanism or an effect size for collective memory. It gives the shape any answer must have. A shared remembrance of conflict moves with the present, so it cannot be the ground for a present verdict; it is a prior held in individual frames, and people who inherit the same story are one evidence pool, not two witnesses, so their agreement cannot corroborate a judgment that the out-group is untrustworthy. The bias sits at the evidence rule: a remembered state is used as a route, so present actors are sorted into old categories before the evidence is read. What crosses generations is usually a recording rather than a method, so repetition increases agreement, not evidence, and displacement substitutes a habitat rather than merely removing one. The same memory is still legitimate for questions about group identity, where shared meaning is the product. Trust moves when a signal crosses from a frame whose closure differs, not when more of the same memory is added. The framework names the four tests that would settle it, and stops there.
