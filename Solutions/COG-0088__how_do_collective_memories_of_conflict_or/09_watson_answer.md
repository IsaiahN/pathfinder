## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about how a collective memory of conflict or displacement shapes present-day reasoning and trust. Option A: the memory is information about the past and its effect is the effect of its contents. Option B: the memory is an inherited prior and a shared evidence pool, so its effect is structural, acting on what counts as evidence and on whether frames can reach an anchor. What each leads to: A predicts a content effect, removable by correcting the facts, and treats agreement among the similarly socialised as evidence; B predicts a closure effect, removable only by changing independence or contact, and treats that agreement as the pool talking. The question is a union of four conditions and must be split before it is searched (Fig 9 :4-5; Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either").

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry, and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, union or partition, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Arity: four conditions bundled (transmission, inference, trust, displacement). Symmetry: the relations are ordered, transmission is stronger in-group than out-group, and comparison ⋛ is a threshold relation, not a symmetric join. Scale: the memory crosses a membrane, from population to person and across generations (Fig 4 :19-25). Union, not partition.
- **D1 Belief inheritance.** Actors: individual frames as group members, inherited prior `w`, belief `b`. Currency: belief weight and residual `R`. Anchor: the ground for whatever the belief is about. The memory is not itself an anchor. Question type: can an inherited belief still be corrected?
- **D2 Present-day reasoning.** Actors: the recalling frame, present actors and events. Currency: evidence and the comparison ⋛. Anchor: the ground, separately for each frame. Question type: which slot does the memory occupy in the inference?
- **D3 Trust between frames.** Actors: in-group frames, out-group frames, institutions. Currency: agreement and independence (Fig 2 :31-32). Anchor: the ground, never the agreement. Question type: verdict versus convention.
- **D4 Intergenerational and displacement carry.** Actors: a population, a generation, a displaced group, the new habitat's actors. Currency: methods versus recordings; contact. Anchor: the ground, reached through a channel that decays. Question type: what crosses the membrane, and is displacement removal or substitution?
- **Rejected domain.** A "collective mind as remembering subject" has no nameable anchor and is dropped at census. World sources decline the strong reading (Figueiredo et al.; Orianne and Eustache).
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Enumerate habitat (Fig 11 :43-48), describe the residual (Fig 9 :27-28), check the anchor (Fig 2 :29), then judge D2 and D3. D4 governs the carry between steps.

Strain test: the apparent conflict between "the anchor must not update" and a memory that changes with context is resolved by re-census, placing the memory in D1 as a belief and channel, never an anchor. The apparent conflict between "shared agreement is worthless" and the adaptive effects reported by Li et al. is resolved by Fig 2 :39-41: valence depends on question type. No live strain remains. One item is left open and named: whether collective representations count as quantities for ⋛ at population scale.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; a mechanism, an effect size, and any single population-level claim do not. A shared remembrance of conflict or displacement fails the anchor test and occupies the prior slot. It biases reasoning at the evidence rule by supplying a pre-fixed comparison, and it makes agreement among the similarly socialised worthless as a verdict about the world, while remaining efficient as a convention about identity. It crosses generations as a recording, so agreement compounds without evidence; displacement is habitat substitution with unchosen inherited actors. Trust moves when an atom crosses from a frame whose closure differs, not by adding more of the same memory. The content reading (Option A) is not erased but refused standing as ground and corroboration (Fig 5 :53-56; Fig 6 :42-43). This is a `specified`, ungrounded result, and the four tests in `07_the_tether_answer.md` §4 would be required to move it.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable, but real. The main cost is intervening on memory content when the figure shape says the operative quantities are frame independence, anchor contact, and crossing atoms. A second cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a recording instead of a generator (Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."). Both are avoidable by leading with the shape claim, its four tests, and the refuter, and by keeping positive convention uses beside the verdict uses.
- **undoable:** yes. The binding of field observations onto held atoms can be withdrawn without touching any primitive, any world source, or any figure line. The per-domain verdicts are independent, so a failure in D4 does not bring down D1 to D3. A sent packet can be superseded by a corrected one.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An anchor test showing the memory itself satisfies Fig 2 :29; an independence test showing two inheriting groups are low-overlap frames; a contact test showing repetition improves discrimination of present actors; a content test leaving no residual for closure; or a crossing test failing in a controlled design. A figure re-census that places the memory in a different domain would also reverse the verdict. Prestige, agreement, and a larger evidence pool are not such instruments.
