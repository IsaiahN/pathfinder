# Expert verification packet: collective memory, reasoning, and trust

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in collective memory, conflict studies, social psychology, or political psychology can check. Mode, derivation, and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the shape. Not `grounded`.** No measurement in this simulated derivation settles an effect size or a mechanism.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A shared remembrance of conflict or displacement cannot be the ground for a present verdict about another group, because it moves with the present and is held by correlated frames. It occupies the prior slot: it supplies the terms in which present actors are sorted, so the bias sits at the evidence rule rather than only at the recalled fact. It crosses generations as a recording of one occasion rather than as a method, so repetition compounds agreement without adding evidence. Displacement substitutes a habitat rather than removing one, and the two silent failures are what was not reproduced and what was unintentionally introduced. The same memory is still legitimate for questions constitutively about group identity, where shared meaning is the product. Trust moves when a signal crosses from a frame whose closure differs, not when more of the same memory is added. The figures return this shape and four tests, not a mechanism or an effect size.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: the anchor and the pool, checkable against Fig 2 and the memory literature

- **Statement:** the memory fails the anchor test, and agreement among people who inherit it is not corroboration.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Fig 2 :41 "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it".
- **What to check:** do collective remembrances in the literature move with present context, as Rimé et al. (2015/2016) report? If so, the memory is not an anchor. Is the valence rule (disqualifying for verdicts, efficient for conventions) consistent with the adaptive and maladaptive consequences reported by Li et al. (2023)?
- **Refuter:** show the shared memory satisfying Fig 2 :29 and :33 ("The anchor must not update"), or show two groups with the same inherited narrative to be low-overlap frames under Fig 2 :31-32.

### C2: the comparison and the evidence rule, checkable by a social psychologist

- **Statement:** the effect is at the evidence rule, not only at the factual premise. A remembered state is used as a route, so a present actor is sorted into an old category before the evidence is read.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :111 "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." Fig 12 :112 "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond." Fig 9 :28 "any frame you pick will seem to fit".
- **What to check:** are the Paez and Liu (2009) characterization ("plays a cognitive-perceptual role by shaping the perception of interest, threat and others intentions") and the 9/11 commemoration misattribution reported by Figueiredo et al. better explained by a content-only model or by a pre-fixed comparison? A content-only model should predict no dependence on frame independence or on the order of description and evidence.
- **Refuter:** show the entire effect captured by recalled content, with no residual for frame closure, shared evidence, or an import, and with the comparison and anchor left unchanged.

### C3: the carry and the crossing, checkable by a conflict researcher or historian

- **Statement:** what crosses generations is a recording rather than a method, and trust changes only when an atom crosses from a frame whose closure differs.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Fig 4 :25 "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." Fig 7 :13 "agreement compounds, evidence does not". Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."
- **What to check:** does the transmission literature (family narratives, textbooks, commemoration) record verification against a separate anchor before the downward carry, or after? Do the out-group acknowledgment result in Figueiredo et al. and the vicarious-extinction results in Debiec and Olsson (2017) behave like imports from a different closure rather than like more of the same memory?
- **Refuter:** show repetition improving discrimination of present actors in the Fig 4 :28 sense ("makes the next agent's search cheaper"), or trust rising within one closure with no crossing atom and no anchor change.

## 4. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a mechanism, an effect size, or a causal estimate. The shape is `specified`; the carriers are unproven bindings from the world frame.
- Not that conflict memory is always harmful. For identity questions shared meaning is a convention and is load-bearing (Fig 2 :41).
- Not that a remembered event is false, or that remembering is pathological. What is refused is its standing as ground and as corroboration.
- Not one population-level effect. Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
- Not `grounded`. No instrument in this packet measures the claim.
- Not a claim that the framework selects which carrier operates (family narrative versus schooling versus media). That is a name where the framework returns a shape (Fig 9 :32).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29, :31-32, :39-41 and Fig 12 :111-112. Six short passages.
2. Say whether a shared remembrance can satisfy the anchor test in your field, and whether the convention/verdict split matches the adaptive/maladaptive findings you know.
3. Name one instrument that would separate a content effect from a frame-closure effect, or say why none is available.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
