## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as `gap(x, (T_E o T_A)(x))`, the gap between what was sent up and what came back down. Fig 4 :30 requires measuring it rather than assuming zero. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 2 :29, :40-41 (anchor and valence), Fig 4 :22-25 (carry), Fig 6 :23 (crossing), Fig 11 :66-69 (substitution), Fig 12 :111-112 (state versus route).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07` §2, that memory is a prior, agreement from a shared pool is not a verdict, repetition is a recording, and trust moves on a crossing.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the concrete claim fielded in `08`, with four falsifiers and a list of candidate carriers.

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **valence split** is easy to flatten into "collective memory distorts trust", which would lose the legitimate convention use (Fig 2 :41) and turn a `specified` shape into a `general` verdict;
- the **refusal is lost** if one restates the result as "memory is a false belief", because ¬ is not − (Fig 12 :140; Operators :58) and the content reading must retain its support while losing its standing;
- the **operand type of ⋛** at population scale (Fig 12 :87, "a threshold relation between two quantities") is not carried by the shorthand at all; whether a collective representation counts as a quantity at that level is still unmeasured.

One thing survives cleanly: a memory that moves with the present cannot be the ground for a present verdict, and a pool of correlated frames cannot corroborate a world claim. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the figures" as identity would set R_T to zero and commit the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
