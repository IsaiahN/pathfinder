# Isomers, bonds, and order: the two readings compared

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

F7 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` refuses to mint a new "memory effect" term. Two questions are owed: (1) are the content reading (Option A) and the structural reading (Option B) two arrangements of the same atoms, that is, **isomers**, or are they different atom sets? (2) what is the dependency order of transmission, comparison, and trust, and what does each bond type commit us to?

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Are the two readings isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- As the field usually writes them, the two readings share most atoms: an event in the past, a present person, an inference, a trust judgment. They differ in the *bond*:

| reading | arrangement | bond between memory and present judgment |
|---|---|---|
| Option A, content | `remembered event → present judgment` | **sequence** (→): "A then B; B needs A to have happened" (Fig 12 :72) |
| Option B, structural | `remembered event ⇒ inherited prior + shared pool → comparison ⋛ → trust` | **production** (⇒) then **comparison** (⋛): "A produces B; B did not exist before" (Fig 12 :76); ⋛ is "a threshold relation between two quantities" (Fig 12 :87) |

- Fig 12 :95-96: "Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond." So **yes: they are isomers** under the figure's own definition. Same atoms (past event, person, inference, trust), different bond, different substance (a content effect versus a closure effect). The difference is invisible if one records only that "memory influences trust".
- The chemistry precedent is not an analogy imported for decoration: Fig 12 :97-98 cites silver fulminate and silver cyanate, "same composition and different properties, which is what forced structure into the notation." A flat claim that memory affects trust is the flat formula Fig 12 warns about.
- **Falsifier (written before this conclusion):** if either reading can be stated with an atom the other does not require (for example, if the structural reading needs a seventh primitive or the content reading needs no frame at all), the isomer claim fails and the two are merely different domains, not isomers.

### Q2: Functional group: what do both readings share?

- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Both readings share the substructure `a state (the remembered past) bonded to a structure (a representation) that acts on a present state (the judgment)`. Fig 13 :74: "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one." A memory is never a bare state acting on a bare state; a representation must sit between, exactly as a structure must sit between space and time in the exemplar.
- The **group** says *kind*: any claim of this form is a memory-to-judgment claim, and nothing outside the group counts as an answer to this question. The **qualifier** (which bond: →, ⇒, ⋛, or an import) says *which member*, and that choice fixes the outcome. A questionnaire that measures "how much the memory influenced trust" records the ingredients and loses the bond, which is Fig 12 :14-16's failure: "the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not / nothing downstream can tell them apart".
- **Falsifier:** show a memory-to-judgment claim that does not require a representation between the remembered state and the present judgment. Then the functional group is wrong, not merely incomplete.

### Q3: What is the order of transmission?

- Fig 3 :15: "name what worked, verify it, and carry it to the next problem." A memory that is carried without being re-verified has lost the "verify it" link. Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
- Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." A narrative carried from a population to a person travels *down* the membrane. Fig 4 :23: "upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success." Fig 4 :24: "downward: something that makes the next search cheaper. Never a replay of what worked once."
- Fig 7 :12-13: "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not." Fig 7 :36: "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."
- Therefore the dependency order is: **enumerate habitat → describe residual → verify against an anchor → carry a method downward → only then compose the present comparison.** The observed order in the world literature is the reverse: narrative is transmitted first (family, textbooks, media), and verification against a separate anchor is optional or absent (Figueiredo et al.; Orianne and Eustache, both read). That inversion is the structural defect F5 names, and it is a claim about order, not about content.
- **Falsifier:** show a transmission chain in which verification against a separate anchor reliably precedes the downward carry and in which the carried object changes the next generation's discrimination of present actors. Then the order claim is narrowed to the cases where verification is absent.

### Q4: What does negation add that subtraction cannot?

- Operators table :58: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."
- Fig 12 :139-140: "Without a unary operator a system can write what a situation contains, what follows what, and how far along it is - and it cannot write that any of those is false. A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate."
- This matters here because the content reading is not wrong in the sense of *absent* (−). It is present and has support. What F1 and F2 do is refuse its standing to serve as an anchor and as corroboration (F10 in `01`). A framework with only − and no ¬ could say "the factual content is missing from the model" and could not say "the factual content is not the right *kind* of thing to settle this verdict." Formula step 2 requires the refusal before the mint: the fifth gap "sorts a gap by what is wrong", not by what is missing (Fig 5 :50-53).
- **Falsifier:** show that the content reading is genuinely absent from the model in the relevant cases, so − suffices and no ¬ is needed. Then F10 is withdrawn.

## CONCLUSION

The two readings are **isomers**, not different domains: same atoms, different bond, different substance. Their common substructure is a **functional group** (`past state → representation → present judgment`), and the bond, not the ingredient list, decides which substance is produced. Transmission is ordered (habitat, residual, verification, carry, comparison), and the observed inversion of that order is the defect. Negation is required to refuse the content reading's standing without erasing it. No seventh primitive, no new operator, and no import that moves the wall is needed for any of this.

Every claim above is `specified` and structural. Nothing here measures which isomer actually dominates in a given population. That is the ground's job, and the relevant instruments are named in `07_the_tether_answer.md` §4.
