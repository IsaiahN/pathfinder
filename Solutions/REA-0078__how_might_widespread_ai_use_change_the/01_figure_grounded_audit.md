## 0. The residual in effect terms

The question asks for a direction of change under a permanent change of habitat. State it as `R = |Γ(b,a) − o′|` per slot (Symbols table; Formula step 1), not as one global verdict.

> **R (effect statement).** A person enters a situation with a belief `b` built from private history and inherited prior, acts `a`, and observes `o′`. Widespread AI use inserts a new actor into the habitat: an output that can be consumed without the act that produced it. The residual is the gap between what the person's library predicts will happen to their own capability and what happens. It arrives in three slots:
> - **memory:** what is encoded and retrievable versus what is known to be findable;
> - **expertise:** what the person can still do in a fresh habitat without the aid;
> - **decision-making:** what the person treats as settled when an instrument and a judgment disagree.
>
> Arity: three conditions. Symmetry: the human-AI relation is not one bond; it is an instrument in one slot and a peer closure in another. Scale: individual learner, practitioner-in-habitat, person-in-arrangement.

Two candidate answers, stated as an ordinary term so the sought-for shape can accrue a residual (Formula, "Direction"; Fig 5 :65-66):

- **A:** AI use is an agent-only improvement. Its direction is one number: net gain or net loss to human cognition, averaged across the three slots.
- **B:** AI use changes **contact and what crosses between person and aid**. The direction is per slot and conditional on what crosses and on closure overlap. The answer is a shape with a flip condition, not a single signed verdict.

What an answer would look like (bound before the run): for each slot, name the actors, the currency, the anchor, what crosses the boundary, and the condition under which the direction reverses. If a candidate cannot state its flip condition, it has not answered the question in effect terms.

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." So option A destroys the signal it claims to measure. That is a reason to refuse A before choosing any figure line.

## 1. Domain census, before choosing any figure line

Describe R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor. No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.

- **R.** Arity three, so a **union compound**, not one hard question. Fig 9 :53: "Nobody chooses. The gap does, and the gap is only as good as its description." And the unbundling rule: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either; it is a compound with its own loop.
- **Symmetry.** In the decision slot the AI is an instrument, not a peer frame. In the expertise slot it can be a peer closure with a different wall. These are different bonds, so they cannot share a verdict. An averaged "human plus AI" symmetry is a type error.
- **Scale.** Memory is one frame across a boundary. Expertise is agent-and-habitat. Decision-making is a checking arrangement with an anchor. Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it." A settled skill in slot one becomes an operand in slot two, so the domains are ordered but not identical.

## 2. Findings

### F1 - Option A is the wrong question: capability lives in agent-and-habitat (decisive for the framing)

Fig 11 :39: "And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."
Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

Option A asks whether AI makes the isolated agent better or worse. The figure says that question cannot be answered, because capability is not a property of the isolated agent. Widespread AI use is a change to the habitat: a new actor in contact with the person. So the effect to look for is a change of contact, of what crosses, and of the ground's reachability, not a change of an intrinsic "human memory" or "human expertise" quantity.

Fig 11 :36: "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran." A blanket claim that AI "makes humans less intelligent" applies a label to an agent in isolation. What can be said is that the contact changed.

**Refuter:** a controlled study in which an agent-only improvement (better AI output with human contact, closure and habitat held fixed) moves the measured unaided human capability on a fresh task. Fig 11 :71-72 itself names this: "an agent-only improvement that moved the ground would refute the capability claim." If that fires, F1 falls and option A is back in play. Until it fires, A is refused, not merely unproven.

### F2 - Memory: a recording crosses down, a method crosses up (D1)

Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
Fig 4 :23: "upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."
Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."
Fig 4 :26: "Replayed downward, it produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one."

An AI answer is a recording of an occasion. Consumed as a head start it makes the next search cheaper, which is permitted. Treated as the thing itself it produces a person who can repeat that success and cannot produce a new one. The memory effect is therefore not "less memory" but a change in *what is stored and retrievable*: the route or the recording.

Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."
Fig 11 :70: "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."
Fig 11 :68: "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails". Fig 11 :69: "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts".

A no-AI check is not the old habitat with an actor subtracted. It is a substituted habitat, and the two silent failures are exactly the ones an "AI helped" study misses: the missing method appears only when the goal changes, and the introduced dependency appears only when it acts.

Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."

**Refuter:** after AI use that supplied only recordings, a person reproduces the same class of success on a novel task with the aid removed, at the same rate as a method-trained control. If that holds, F2's recording-versus-method distinction did not bite in that slot and the memory claim must be narrowed or withdrawn.

### F3 - Expertise: the direction is a function of closure overlap, not of the tool (D2)

Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."
Fig 8 :49: "Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names."
Fig 8 :50: "But shared atoms are not the signature. Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."
Fig 8 :51-52: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."

AI trained largely on human output overlaps the human frame. Under Fig 8 such a pair is one frame wearing two names: the union adds nothing. An AI whose closure genuinely differs creates a surplus, but only if a residual is described first (Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"). So "does AI build or erode expertise" has no single answer; it turns on overlap and provenance.

Fig 11 :49-50: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."
Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

The same tool is beneficial to a novice's residual and harmful to an expert's. Fig 11 :49 makes this structural, not anecdotal.

**Refuter:** two frames shown to have genuinely differing closures (different evidence pools, a source gate and shadow test passed) produce no surplus on the residual in question; or a high-overlap pair produces a surplus that survives an independence audit. Either firing breaks the overlap condition and F3 is wrong.

### F4 - Decision-making: the AI is an instrument, not an anchor (D3)

Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."
Fig 2 :37-38: "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."

For a decision about people, human judgment is the anchor. For a technical decision, human judgment is an instrument that can be wrong on axes it does not measure. A standing rule "keep the human in the loop" is therefore a type error: it does not say what the question is about.

Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."
Fig 2 :31-32: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."

A human and an AI agreeing because the AI learned from human output is the pool talking, not two frames confirming. Fig 7 :12-13: "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not." Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict."

**Refuter:** on the decision task at issue, the human and the AI are shown to draw on different evidence pools by an independence audit, and their agreement still predicts the ground better than either alone. Then the shared-pool verdict is false for that task and F4 must be re-censused.

### F5 - The channel to the ground decays silently (D1 and D3)

Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does"
Fig 10 :25-26: "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"
Fig 10 :27-28: "the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"
Fig 10 :29: "→ keep the anchor reachable"

The ground does not get worse when AI is adopted. The person's channel to it does: direct encoding, direct checking, direct failure experience. The output of that loss is not a wrong answer but "you have lost touch", which internal metrics report as green. Seat Map :285: "Every instrument points inward unless one is built to point out."

**Refuter:** a longitudinal measure of direct, unaided contact with the ground (not self-reported confidence) that does not decline as AI use rises, together with unchanged detection of seeded errors. If both hold, F5's channel claim is not supported in that population.

### F6 - The weighting `w` is where the change is mechanized (D3)

Symbols table :21: "What the system takes to be the case before it acts. Formed from its own history and from what it inherited, mixed by w."
Symbols table :24-26: "How far the belief leans on private history versus inherited prior. w_A + w_B = 1, and w is learnable from the outcomes of past weightings."

`b` is a mixture of private history and inherited prior. Persistent AI advice is a new supplier of inherited prior, so the natural first-order effect of widespread use is a drift of `w` toward the aid. Crucially, `w` is learnable from outcomes: the drift is not a law, it is a parameter that experience can reset. Fig 10 :59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying." A person who cannot tell which value the ground returned today will have `w` set by fluency rather than by contact.

**Refuter:** repeated outcome feedback restores `w` to its pre-AI value and unaided detection returns, with AI use held constant. Then the drift is correctable by design and F6 must be stated as a design condition rather than a tendency.

### F7 - More output is not more capability (cross-cutting)

Fig 5 :30: "the machinery worked; the answer was already known."
Fig 5 :48: "Its own output is never a measure of success. A system can be enthusiastically curious and produce nothing."
Fig 13 :115: "The set is closed. The arrangements are not."
Fig 13 :116-118: "Six members, and no amount of arranging produces a seventh: a new member would have to be a new kind, and composition only ever rearranges the kinds already held. Meanwhile the arrangements over those six do not run out. So a frame can search without end and still not arrive: infinite effort inside a finite reach."

An AI system can generate indefinitely inside its closure. Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." More generated answers do not add a primitive to the person's frame. Counting outputs as learning is counting the denominator (SEAT MAP :194: "a record that grows is not a reach that grows").

**Refuter:** the AI produces an import that passes the source gate and shadow test (Fig 6 :12-13) and the person's reach, not merely their library, measurably expands on a novel task. Then output became an atom and F7 does not apply to that case.

### F8 - The three slots must be reported per slot (method)

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
Fig 1 :25: "coverage, and compression achieved"
Fig 1 :26: "predicates minted, and credibility accrued"
Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."
Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

A single "AI improved cognition by X percent" figure aggregates the slots and uses a frame-internal quantity (output, time saved, credibility) as if it were the ground. Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The cost here is high and the independence is usually low.

**Refuter:** a single composite measure predicts unaided transfer in all three slots with the same sign and comparable effect size, and survives per-slot disaggregation without a sign flip. Then the union reading was unnecessary and F8 is refuted.

### F9 - Strains after re-census (named, not hidden)

1. **Anchor versus instrument in D3.** Fig 2 :37-38 makes human judgment an anchor for questions about people and an instrument for questions about code. That is a strain on any single "human oversight helps" claim. Re-census: the governing domain is the question's constitution, not the aid. Verdict: not a figure conflict, a type distinction. Live for any application that does not state what the question is about.
2. **The capability claim is explicitly forward-untested.** Fig 11 :71-72: "The isolation law and the capability claim fit cases already known and have not been tested forward, so they can still come back wrong". This is a falsifier written into the figure itself. F1 inherits it. Mark `specified`, never `grounded`.
3. **Fig 8 union versus Fig 2 shared pool.** Both speak about two parties; they govern different quantities (surplus from differing closures vs worthlessness of agreement from a shared pool). Re-census in §1 separates them. No strain after re-census, but a real risk of misapplication if the two are read together.
4. **Fig 4 recording versus Fig 11 substitution.** A recording crossing down (Fig 4) and a habitat being substituted (Fig 11) are different boundaries. Re-census: Fig 4 governs the membrane, Fig 11 governs the habitat. Both apply to D1 without contradiction.

After re-census: two live items, both named (F9.1, F9.2), none hidden.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per Seat Map :287: "And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device. Stating what failure looks like surfaces constraints the design does not, and the constraint arrives as a change to the experiment rather than as a note about it."

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **D1:** after AI use supplying only recordings, unaided performance on a novel task of the same class equals the method-trained control (breaks F2).
2. **D2:** an agent-only improvement that leaves human contact unchanged moves the ground on unaided capability (breaks F1 and Fig 11 :40).
3. **D2:** two frames with genuinely differing closures produce no surplus, or a high-overlap pair produces a surplus that survives an independence audit (breaks F3).
4. **D3:** an independence audit shows human and AI judgments drawing on different evidence pools on the task, and their agreement still predicts the ground better than either alone (breaks F4).
5. **Method:** a single composite score predicts all three slots with the same sign and no interaction, after disaggregation (breaks F8).
6. **Mode:** any direction is claimed as settled without a ground reading (breaks the mode rule).

If any fires, the relevant domain claim is withdrawn and re-censused. Until one is run, no part of this is `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Refuse option A (agent-only, averaged) before any measurement: Fig 11 :40 says it is the wrong object.
2. Report memory, expertise and decision-making as three slots with separate anchors (Fig 1 :19; Fig 9 :53). Do not average.
3. Test the membrane, not the output: did a method cross, or only a recording? Use a substituted habitat with a novel task and state both silent failures (Fig 4 :22-26; Fig 11 :68-70).
4. Audit overlap before asserting complementarity. Require a source gate and a shadow test before calling an AI contribution an import (Fig 6 :12-13; Fig 8 :50-52).
5. State what each decision question is constitutively about. For questions about people, the human judgment is the anchor; for technical questions it is an instrument (Fig 2 :36-38).
6. Keep the channel to the ground reachable and measure contact directly, not confidence (Fig 10 :24-29).
7. Lead outreach with the shape claim and the pre-registered refuter. Bring a characterised residual, never the source (Fig 10 :52).

## 6. Conclusion

The question does not license a single direction. It licenses a shape with three per-domain verdicts and a flip condition. Widespread AI use changes the human habitat, and capability lives in agent-and-habitat (Fig 11 :39-40). Where a recording crosses the membrane instead of a method, memory becomes retrieval-of-location and expertise becomes repetition of a past success (Fig 4 :22-26). Where closures differ and provenance is shown, the union reaches what neither party reaches alone (Fig 8 :45-52). In decisions, the AI is an instrument rather than an anchor for a question about people, and agreement from a shared pool is not corroboration (Fig 2 :37-40); the channel to the ground decays silently while internal metrics stay green (Fig 10 :24-29). The direction is a function of overlap and of what crosses, so it must be measured per slot. Status: `specified`, ungrounded, candidate, with six pre-registered refuters in §3.
