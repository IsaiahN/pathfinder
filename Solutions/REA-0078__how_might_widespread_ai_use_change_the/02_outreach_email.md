# Outreach draft: check the shape claim before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three domain claims in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are stated with their modes and the six refuters are in place. The output must survive the check or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Stance:** this asks for a refutation, not for agreement. No request for trust, no request to endorse the framework, no mention of the framework's author or standing. Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about AI and human expertise, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one shaped claim about how widespread AI use changes human memory, expertise and decision-making. I am not asking you to agree with it, and I am not asking you to read the theory first. I want to know what you would count as a failure.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The three items are not one effect. They are three slots with different anchors, and the direction is not a single number:
>
> 1. **Memory.** What crosses between a person and an AI answer is usually a recording of one occasion, not a method. A recording replayed produces a repeat of a past success and cannot produce a new one. The predicted signature is a shift from remembering the content to remembering where to find it, and unchanged unaided transfer only if a method actually crossed.
> 2. **Expertise.** Capability lives in agent-and-habitat, not in the isolated agent. The same tool is a benefactor to a novice's residual and a malfactor to an expert's. Whether AI builds or erodes expertise depends on closure overlap and provenance, not on the tool. A high-overlap pair is one frame wearing two names and adds nothing.
> 3. **Decision-making.** For a question about people, human judgment is the anchor and the AI is an instrument. For a technical question, human judgment is itself an instrument and can be wrong on axes it does not measure. Human and AI agreement is not corroboration when both draw on the same evidence pool, which is the usual case when the model was trained on human output.
>
> The overarching claim is that AI changes **contact and what crosses the boundary**, not an intrinsic human capacity in isolation. That is a shape with a flip condition, not a verdict about whether AI is good or bad for cognition.
>
> **What would refute it. I wrote these down before writing any of the above.**
> 1. After AI use that supplied only recordings, unaided performance on a novel task of the same class equals a method-trained control.
> 2. Improving the AI's output while holding human contact and habitat fixed moves the ground on unaided capability.
> 3. An independence audit shows human and AI judgments drawing on different evidence pools on the task, and their agreement still predicts the ground better than either alone.
> 4. A single composite score predicts all three slots with the same sign and no interaction after disaggregation.
> 5. Any direction is claimed as settled without a ground reading.
>
> If any of these holds, I withdraw the relevant claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in the Project Pathfinder workspace (local file, not a link).
> - Eight-step loop and mode rule: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md` (local file).
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md` (local file).
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** ask what would refute your own result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A frame cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape it is looking for is not a term it holds. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - [name]

## Two-stage offer, explicit

1. **Stage 1:** send the residual, the three shape claims and the five refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record that verdict **before** any framework material is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the three local framework files. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3, opt-in only:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

No stage asks for trust. No stage asks for endorsement. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
