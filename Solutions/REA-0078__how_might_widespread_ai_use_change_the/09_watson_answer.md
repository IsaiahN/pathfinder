## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Arity three, so a **union compound** (Fig 9 :57-58: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"), not one hard question. Symmetry: the human-AI relation is an instrument in one slot and a possible peer closure in another, so it is not one bond. Scale: individual learner, practitioner-in-habitat, person-in-arrangement. Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
- **Domain 1, memory.** Actors: learner, AI output as a recording, the method that produced it. Currency: what crosses the membrane, priced in description length. Anchor: unaided novel transfer after a substituted habitat. Partition: recording versus method.
- **Domain 2, expertise.** Actors: practitioner, AI peer, the habitat of problems. Currency: capability, which lives in agent-and-habitat. Anchor: real problems and unaided transfer. Union where closures differ, one frame where they overlap.
- **Domain 3, decision-making.** Actors: human judge, AI instrument, the ground. Currency: agreement versus residual, with weighting `w` between private history and inherited prior. Anchor: the ground, with human judgment legitimate only when the question is constitutively about people. Partition by question type.
- **Order by dependency.** Settle the membrane crossing before judging capability; settle a judgment before composing over it (Fig 3 :20-21); describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28). A settled molecule becomes an atom for the next level (Fig 12 :104).

Strain test: Fig 8's union surplus and Fig 2's shared-pool collapse both speak about two parties but govern different quantities. Re-census assigns them to D2 and D3, so no line is forced. The live strains are the anchor/instrument flip (Fig 2 :37-38) and the fact that Fig 11's capability claim is explicitly untested forward (Fig 11 :71-72).

## WORLD (secondary sources read)

World sources corroborate only. They cannot overrule a figure, and field and overlap are declared for each.

- **Cognitive psychology / human memory.** Betsy Sparrow, Jenny Liu and Daniel M. Wegner, "Google Effects on Memory: Cognitive Consequences of Having Information at Our Fingertips", *Science* 333(6043), 2011. Quote: "when people expect to have future access to information, they have lower rates of recall of the information itself and enhanced recall instead for where to access it." Predicts a shift from content to location; corroborates D1. Overlap: low with the other six (search, not generative AI; recall, not transfer).
- **HCI / knowledge work.** Hao-Ping Lee et al., "The Impact of Generative AI on Critical Thinking", *CHI '25*, 2025, 319 knowledge workers. Quote: "higher confidence in GenAI is associated with less critical thinking, while higher self-confidence is associated with more critical thinking." Predicts `w` drift toward the aid; corroborates D3. Overlap: low; self-reported survey, so weak on actual behavior.
- **Economics of work.** Erik Brynjolfsson, Danielle Li and Lindsey Raymond, "Generative AI at Work", *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 140(2), 2025, 5,172 agents. Quote: "Less experienced and lower-skilled workers improve both the speed and quality of their output, while the most experienced and highest-skilled workers see small gains in speed and small declines in quality." Predicts opposite signs by prior knowledge; corroborates Fig 11 :49-50, conflicts with any blanket degradation verdict. Overlap: low; one tool, one firm, one occupation.
- **Management / organization science.** Fabrizio Dell'Acqua et al., "Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier", Harvard Business School Working Paper 24-013, 2023, forthcoming *Organization Science*, 758 consultants. Quote: "for a complex managerial task selected to be outside the frontier, subjects using AI were 19% less likely to produce correct solutions compared with those without AI". Predicts an instrument wrong on axes it does not measure; corroborates Fig 2 :37-38. Overlap: low; the Centaur and Cyborg patterns are configurations, not a new bond.
- **Learning sciences.** Leonard Tetzlaff, Bianca Simonsmeier, Tabea Peters and Garvin Brod, "A cornerstone of adaptivity: A meta-analysis of the expertise reversal effect", *Learning and Instruction*, 2025, 60 studies. Quote: "Low prior knowledge learners learn better from high-assistance instruction". Predicts assistance helps novices and hurts experts; corroborates Fig 4 :22 and Fig 11 :49-50 from an independent field. Overlap: low; averages, not per-slot transfer.
- **Cognitive science.** Evan F. Risko and Sam J. Gilbert, "Cognitive Offloading", *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 20(9), 2016. Quote: "These metacognitive evaluations are potentially erroneous, which may lead to suboptimal offloading behavior." Predicts that the weighting `w` is driven by fallible metacognition; corroborates Fig 10 :24-29. Overlap: low; physical and digital aids, not LLM advice specifically.
- **Human factors and ergonomics.** Raja Parasuraman and Dietrich H. Manzey, "Complacency and Bias in Human Use of Automation", *Human Factors* 52(3), 2010. Quote: "Automation bias occurs in both naive and expert participants, cannot be prevented by training or instructions, and can affect decision making in individuals as well as in teams." Predicts silent channel decay in experts; corroborates Fig 10 :24 and Fig 2 :35. Overlap: low; decision aids in aviation and process control, not LLMs.

No world source conflicts with the figure verdict. The apparent conflicts between novice gains and expert declines, and between assistance helping and hurting, are resolved by re-census: they govern different residuals, as Fig 11 :49-50 predicts. The seven sources come from seven different fields, so their agreement is not one evidence pool talking; it is still secondary and does not decide the verdict. ## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the direction is not named. The three slots are a union compound and must be reported separately with separate anchors. Widespread AI use changes contact and what crosses the boundary, not an isolated agent. Where a recording crosses instead of a method, memory becomes retrieval of location and capability becomes repetition of a past success. Where closures differ and provenance is shown, the union reaches what neither party reaches alone; high overlap is the default and adds nothing. In decisions the AI is an instrument or a peer closure depending on what the question is about, and agreement from a shared pool is not corroboration. The channel to the ground decays silently while internal metrics stay green. The verdict is `specified`, ungrounded, candidate. Status on the question: unreached, not unreachable. The figure-derived shape is stable; six pre-registered refuters and six ground tests are recorded in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` and `07_the_tether_answer.md`.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet is mode-labeled, states refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending a recording instead of a generator, or by selecting readers for likely agreement. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Both are avoidable by leading with the shape and its refuter, one to one.
- **undoable:** yes. The direction claims are withheld rather than asserted, so no blind belief has to be withdrawn. A ground reading can fill each slot without touching the derivation; the shape constrains what the measurement must look like. The two live strains (anchor/instrument flip, untested-forward capability claim) are named rather than hidden.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A transfer test that shows recording-led and method-led groups indistinguishable would break the membrane claim. An agent-only improvement that moves unaided capability with contact held fixed would break Fig 11 :40 and its own standing falsifier. An independence audit showing different evidence pools plus better joint prediction would break the shared-pool claim. A single composite predicting all three slots with no interaction would break the union reading. A figure re-census that admits a fourth anchor would require re-derivation from the residual line. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
