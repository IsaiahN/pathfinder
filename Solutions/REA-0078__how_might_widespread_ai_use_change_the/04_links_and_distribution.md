## What in the wider literature bears on this question

Each source is a secondary witness only. Field, overlap and prediction are recorded; none overrules the figures.

1. **Human memory (low overlap with the other six).** Sparrow et al. predict a shift from content to location in memory when access is expected. This corroborates the D1 recording-versus-method shape, but it studied web search, not generative AI, and measured recall rather than unaided transfer. It is a precursor, not a test of the figure claim.
2. **HCI and knowledge work (low overlap with economics and learning sciences).** Lee et al. predict that confidence in the aid reduces critical engagement and that effort shifts toward verification and task stewardship. This corroborates the `w` drift and channel-decay claims in D3. It is self-reported survey data, so it is weak evidence on actual behavior.
3. **Economics of work (low overlap with cognitive psychology).** Brynjolfsson et al. predict heterogeneous, opposite-signed effects by skill level, and report that gains in part reflect durable worker learning rather than rote reliance. This is the strongest external corroboration of Fig 11 :49-50: the same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another. It conflicts with any blanket "AI erodes expertise" verdict. Note the paper's own caveat: one tool, one firm, one occupation.
4. **Management and organization science (low overlap with psychology).** Dell'Acqua et al. predict that performance flips when a task lies outside the aid's competence, and that the aid's surface quality can mask a wrong conclusion. This corroborates Fig 2 :37-38: instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure. The reported "Centaur" and "Cyborg" patterns are configurations of contact, which is the shape the figures predict.
5. **Learning sciences (low overlap with HCI and labor economics).** Tetzlaff et al. predict that instructional assistance helps novices and hurts experts, and that the effect is not symmetrical. This corroborates Fig 4 :22 (head starts) and Fig 11 :49-50 (valence relative to residual) from an independent field that does not cite the framework.
6. **Cognitive science (low overlap with the management and economics fields).** Risko and Gilbert predict that offloading is triggered by metacognitive evaluations that can be wrong, so offloading can be suboptimal even when it feels efficient. This corroborates the `w` mechanism and the Fig 10 :24-29 channel-decay claim.
7. **Human factors and ergonomics (low overlap with the above).** Parasuraman and Manzey predict that automation bias and complacency appear in experts as well as novices and are not removed by simple training or instructions. This corroborates Fig 10 :59 on declared stakes and Fig 2 :35 on mirrors. It conflicts with the idea that expertise alone protects judgment.

No world source conflicts with the figure verdict. The two apparent conflicts (novice gains vs expert decline; assistance helps vs hurts) are resolved by re-census: they govern different residuals, exactly as Fig 11 :49-50 predicts. The conflict rule was not triggered. These seven sources span seven different fields, so their agreement is not one pool talking, but they are still secondary: none was used to overrule a figure line.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 4 :22:** "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **Fig 10 :42:** "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who has absorbed the whole framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28:** "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what they would expect to see in unaided transfer before offering the theory.
- **Fig 2 :40:** "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Do not pool later agreement as corroboration, and do not select recipients for receptiveness.
- **Seat Map :163:** "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention." A list selected for likely agreement produces a convention, not a check.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the three shape claims and the six refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and what ground would settle it; (3) only then the three local framework files, with the note that the verbatim figure text, not the summary, is the authority.
