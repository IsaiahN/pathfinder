## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the three slots a scale or a union?

- Fig 9 :57-58: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."
- Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
- A single "cognitive impact" scale would require one currency for all three slots. The anchors differ: unaided novel transfer for memory and expertise, the ground for a decision about people or code. Different anchors mean different currencies; Fig 4 :19-20 of the kernel says only the actors, currency and anchor change, but a single scale would have to hold them fixed.
- ⇒ The three slots are a **union compound**, not a scale. A lever that helps a novice's memory (a head start) can harm an expert's decision-making (automation bias) at the same time. They are not points on one line.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a single latent factor accounts for the measured effects in all three slots with a stable sign and no interaction by prior knowledge, the union reading fails and the scale reading stands. The exemplar's method applies: report the disaggregated residuals before the composite.

### Q2 - What is the order, and what recurses across it?

- Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
- Dependency: a method crossing the membrane (D1) must be settled before it can serve as an atom for expertise (D2); a settled judgment (D3) becomes an atom for the next decision. Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."
- Fig 12 :107-109: "Settling compresses the description and not what it cost to establish. Whether that cost must be paid again depends on the substrate: where the arrangement is recorded, re-deriving it is a lookup and the actions were spent once. Where it is not, the result carries forward and the deriving does not, so every lineage pays again."
- ⇒ Each settled skill becomes a cheap operand for the next. AI makes the lookup cheap. Where the record crosses the boundary, the person does not re-pay; where it does not, the person pays again. This is the precise structural form of "deskilling": not an empty head, but a bill that was paid elsewhere and cannot be inspected here.
- The transform is lossy and directional. Fig 4 :28-30: "Going up throws away detail; that is what makes the result reapplicable. But it means the trip is not reversible, and something carried up and brought back down will not match what was sent. So take the round trip and measure the gap." Symbols table :77: "The gap between what was sent up and what came back down: gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))."
- **Falsifier:** if a settled skill can be carried from one slot to another with `R_T = 0` (no loss), the membrane claim fails and the skill is a recording, not a method. This is checkable: transfer to a novel task would be exact.

### Q3 - Which analogues bear, and where do they fail?

These are world sources only. They corroborate; they do not decide a figure line.

| analogue | field / author / year | what it predicts here | limits |
|---|---|---|---|

They are not imports that moved a figure wall. Their agreement is useful but it is still secondary.

Where they fail: none of the five measures the membrane directly (did a method cross?), none audits the evidence pools of a person and an AI, and none reports per-slot unaided transfer. Each sees one slot from one instrument. Fig 10 :37-38: "instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."

### Q4 - Are the two configurations isomers?

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Take the shared atom set `{person, AI answer, method, ground}`:

- **Recording-led configuration:** person + answer, with the answer bonded as a replayed success (`→` from the answer to the action, no ground check by the person). The method atom is present in the AI frame but absent from the person's composition.
- **Method-led configuration:** person + method, with the method bonded to the person's own attempt and re-checked against the ground. The answer is a head start, not the operand.

Same three of four atoms in the person's library, different bond to the answer, different substance: one repeats, one can produce a new success. That is Fig 12's isomer claim, not a metaphor.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if the two configurations are indistinguishable on unaided transfer to a novel task of the same class, they are not isomers; they are one functional group with different labels (Fig 12 :124). This is the check the verification packet asks the expert to run.

These are arrangements, not atoms, so they fall under Fig 12's "The arrangement is the substance" (:94). Fig 12 :49: "Seven is a count of bonds, not of operators." The framework does not contain a named "human-AI collaboration bond"; the two patterns are compositions of the existing ordered bonds. ## CONCLUSION

The three slots are a union compound, not a scale. Their dependency order is membrane crossing, then settled capability, then the next judgment, with a lossy transform at each crossing. A settled skill becomes an atom for the next level, so AI's cheap lookups shift where the bill is paid without deleting the bill. The recording-led and method-led configurations are a real isomer claim over a shared atom set, with a stated falsifier. The five world analogues corroborate from independent fields but do not measure the membrane or audit overlap, so they do not decide the verdict. Strains after re-census: none among the figures; the world analogues are single-source for each slot and are recorded as such.
