# The Tether's answer: how widespread AI use might change human memory, expertise and decision-making

**Mode: `specified` on the three per-domain claims, `general` on the overall shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading. Nothing here settles the question it reports on.

## 1. The residual it answers

A person enters a situation with a belief `b` mixed by `w` from private history and inherited prior, acts, observes `o′`, and carries what worked (Symbols table; Formula step 1). Widespread AI use inserts a new actor into that habitat: an output that can be consumed without the act that produced it. `R = |Γ(b,a) − o′|` per slot (Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."). It arrives in three slots with three anchors, and it is a union compound (Fig 9 :57-58: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either").

## 2. The shape, per domain, never averaged

### D1, memory

A recording crosses the boundary, a method crosses it differently. Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Fig 4 :25-26: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion. Replayed downward, it produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one." The predicted signature is a shift toward remembering where to find information rather than what it was, and unchanged unaided transfer only if a method actually crossed.

A no-AI check is not the old habitat minus one actor. Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Fig 11 :70: "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability." State both silent failures: what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails, and what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts (Fig 11 :68-69).

### D2, expertise

Capability lives in agent-and-habitat, not in the agent. Fig 11 :39: "And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." So "does AI help or hurt human expertise" is the wrong object; the right object is what changed in contact.

The direction is a function of closure overlap. Fig 8 :49: "Two frames that overlap almost completely are one frame wearing two names." Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone," but the surplus "lives only where the closures differ" (Fig 8 :51-52). Since AI is largely trained on human output, the default overlap is high, so the default expectation is no surplus, only cheaper replay. A surplus requires a provenanced import with a source gate and shadow test (Fig 6 :12-13). Valence is relative to the residual: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another" (Fig 11 :49-50). This is why novice gains and expert declines are not a contradiction.

A settled skill becomes an atom for the next level (Fig 12 :104), so AI makes the lookup cheap while the bill for the skill was paid elsewhere (Fig 12 :107-109). Deskilling, in this reading, is not an empty head. It is a capability whose cost can no longer be inspected by the person holding it.

### D3, decision-making

What the question is constitutively about decides the anchor. Fig 2 :37-38: "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure." A standing "keep the human in the loop" rule is a type error; it does not say what the decision is about.

Agreement is not corroboration when the pool is shared. Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Fig 7 :12-13: "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not." The `w` weighting (Symbols table :24-26) is the mechanism: persistent advice is a new supplier of inherited prior, so the natural first-order drift is toward the aid, but `w` is learnable from outcomes, so the drift is a design condition, not a law.

The ground does not decay; the channel does. Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Fig 10 :27-28: "the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch." Internal metrics stay green while contact is lost.

### The shape in one move

Widespread AI use changes **contact and what crosses the boundary**, not an intrinsic human capacity in isolation. Each slot has an anchor and a flip condition; the direction is measured, not assumed.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that AI necessarily makes people forgetful, deskilled or worse decision-makers. Valence is relative to the residual; the sign is a measurement, not a derivation.
- Not that AI necessarily augments expertise. High overlap is the default, and a high-overlap union adds nothing.
- Not that human oversight always helps. For a technical question human judgment is an instrument that can be wrong on axes it does not measure.
- Not that human and AI agreement corroborates a judgment from a shared pool.
- Not that a no-AI test restores the old habitat. It substitutes one.
- Not that a single "cognitive impact" number is meaningful. Slot indexing is a rule, not a preference.
- Not `grounded`. No longitudinal per-slot ground reading was taken here.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

1. **Membrane test:** with the aid removed, unaided transfer to a novel task of the same class, comparing recording-led and method-led groups. This is the isomer test in `06_isomers_and_order.md` Q4.
2. **Contact test:** an agent-only improvement (better AI output with human contact and habitat held fixed). If unaided capability moves, Fig 11 :40 is refuted.
3. **Overlap audit:** establish whether the human and AI draw on different evidence pools on the task, with a source gate and shadow test before calling any contribution an import.
4. **Anchor test:** state what the decision question is constitutively about, then measure whether the human or the AI is the legitimate anchor for it.
5. **Channel test:** direct, longitudinal measurement of unaided contact with the ground and of seeded-error detection, not self-reported confidence.
6. **Disaggregation test:** report the three slots separately before any composite; check for sign flips.

Standing refuters: the six in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §3, written before this conclusion. Any one firing withdraws the relevant claim.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The figure-derived constraints are stable; the directions are not. Fig 2 :44: "The effective-sample-size correction for correlated observations is standard in survey statistics and meta-analysis; the collapse is the same arithmetic read as a warning." Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Seven world sources from seven fields point in the same directions, but they are secondary, they do not measure the membrane or audit overlap, and they do not overrule a figure. *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "the output is only as good as the validator... the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." So this answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say whether AI is good or bad for human cognition. It says that memory, expertise and decision-making are three slots with different anchors, and that averaging them destroys the signal. Widespread AI changes the human habitat: a new actor that can hand a person a recording of a success without the method that produced it. A recording replayed gives repetition; a method gives a new success. Capability lives in agent-and-habitat, so the effect turns on what crosses the boundary and on how much the person and the aid overlap, not on the tool alone. In decisions the AI is an instrument or a peer closure depending on what the question is about, and agreement from a shared evidence pool is not corroboration. The channel to the ground decays silently while internal metrics stay green. What the framework offers is the shape any answer must have, six refuters written in advance, and the tests that would close it. The sign stays open until the ground settles each slot.
