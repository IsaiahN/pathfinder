## 4. Round-trip gap R_T

Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

- **T_A, going up:** carry the primary's two-condition shape to a one-sentence abstraction. Result: *outsourcing works when the outside partner brings what you lack and you keep the ability to check and to learn.*
- **T_E, coming back:** put that abstraction into a specific engagement. Result: *choose a good vendor, write a clear SLA, and keep an audit.*

**R_T = gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x)).** The gap is what the abstraction cannot hold:

1. The one-sentence form collapses the union into one lever. It loses Fig 9 :57, *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Coming back down, it tells a firm failing C2 merely to write a better contract, which is effort above the break (Fig 3 :29-30).
2. It loses the bond distinction. "Keep the ability to learn" does not separate a method from a recording (Fig 4 :22-25), so the concretisation accepts a deliverable as proof of learning.
3. It loses the anchor rule. "Keep an audit" does not say who authors the audit. Coming back down, it permits the vendor to grade its own work, which Fig 2 :47 and Fig 10 :43 forbid.
4. It loses the two-currency rule. "Brings what you lack" prices the import in one accounting, so the shortfall between performance and learning becomes invisible (Fig 12 :125-127).
5. It loses the habitat substitution. "Choose a good vendor" treats the action as an agent swap, not as moving the residual into a different habitat (Fig 11 :66-69).

This gap is not an error to eliminate; it is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold (Fig 4 :31). It is also why the outreach packet sends the generator, not the one-sentence version (Fig 4 :25). The one-paragraph version in `07` is a deliberately lossy artefact and is labelled as such.
