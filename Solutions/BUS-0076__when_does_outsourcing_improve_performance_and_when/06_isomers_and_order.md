## RESIDUAL

The primary audit (`01`) returned two conditions as a union, measured over four slots. Four questions are still owed before the answer can be stated cleanly:

1. **Structure.** Two firms outsource the same activity to vendors with the same visible capability. Why does one retain the method and the other not? Is that an isomer claim under Fig 12, or something weaker?
2. **Order.** In what order do the four slots and their instruments bind? Which link breaks first after a handoff?
3. **Competing explanations.** Can the standard make-or-buy answers be generated from the shape, or do they name something the shape cannot reach?
4. **Reversibility.** If the firm insources again, does that restore what was lost? The figures say isolation substitutes a habitat rather than removing one (Fig 11 :66), so the return trip needs its own check.

## Q1 - Are two outsourcing arrangements with the same atoms isomers?

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** (Fig 12 :117)
> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** (Fig 12 :124)
> **"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."** (Fig 12 :92)

Two arrangements that use the same vendor, the same price, the same SLA, and the same headcount have the same atoms. If in one case the know-how crosses back and is recorded inside the firm, and in the other it does not, the bonds differ. Under Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different behaviour, with the difference invisible in the contract and decisive in what happens at the next activity. This is the figure-grounded version of "it is not what you buy, it is what you arrange around it", but stronger, because the arrangement is itself a substance that carries information the ingredient list does not.

If the two arrangements share only the pattern `firm → vendor → deliverable` while differing in which atoms occupy the slots, the weaker claim holds: they are members of one **functional group** (Fig 12 :124), the qualifier saying which member. The presence of the pattern fixes the kind, not the outcome.

The distinction is testable. A contract comparison that records only prices and service levels cannot tell isomer from functional-group; the bond has to be recorded. Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two arrangements with identical atoms and an identically recorded bond (both carry the same documented method transfer, the same boundary-spanning routine, the same retained instrument) nevertheless diverge in next-activity capability with no difference in ground contact or imported atoms, the isomer framing is insufficient and the difference must be habitat (D3), which would move the explanation out of D2.

**Status:** `specified`, triangulated for the legality of the reading (Fig 12 and Fig 11 :50, different figures), single-source for the business application.

## Q2 - The method/recording distinction is a bond distinction, not a vocabulary distinction

> **"upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."** (Fig 4 :23)
> **"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion. Replayed downward, it produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one."** (Fig 4 :25)
> **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."** (Fig 12 :111)
> **"re-deriving it is a lookup and the actions were spent once"** (Fig 12 :107-108)

The firm can name the deliverable (a state) without holding the route to it (the method). The difference is not a matter of documenting more; a recording is already documentation. It is a matter of which bond crosses the membrane. A method is a re-applicable arrangement that becomes an atom at the firm's level (Fig 12 :104, *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it"*). A recording is one occasion's output and carries no re-application.

The practical test is re-derivation cost. If the next activity requires the firm to pay the full discovery cost again, no method crossed. If re-derivation is a lookup, a method did. Fig 12 :108-109: *"the result carries forward and the deriving does not, so every lineage pays again."*

**Falsifier:** a case where a pure recording, with no re-applicable arrangement and no recorded bond, nevertheless makes re-derivation a lookup on the next activity. That breaks the distinction.

## Q3 - What order do the conditions bind in?

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."** (Fig 3 :20-21)
> **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** (Fig 3 :19)

Order after a make-or-buy handoff:

1. **Perception.** Can the firm still read the realized outcome, independently of the performer? Failure here is visible and has obvious tests.
2. **Vocabulary.** Does the firm's Γ have terms for what the activity affords now, and can it say what would count as progress in each slot? This is the middle link. Fig 3 :26: *"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."*
3. **Objective.** Can it state a per-slot comparison that would count as getting somewhere? Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* A vocabulary with only ingredients cannot state a goal.
4. **Planning.** Vendor selection and contract design, under incomplete information.
5. **Learn and carry.** Name what worked, verify it against the ground, carry it to the next activity.

C1 (the paid import) is mostly a failure of link 4: the wrong closure was chosen, so the union had no surplus. C2 (retention) is mostly a failure of links 1, 2 and 5: the firm stopped reading, lost the terms, and carried nothing forward. That mapping is why one lever cannot clear both (Fig 9 :55).

The order also decides which instrument to buy first. Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Link 4 has RFPs, SLAs and scorecards; links 2 and 5 have almost none. So budget piles up at planning while the break sits at vocabulary and carry.

**Falsifier:** a firm whose links 1, 2 and 3 all measure as working and whose failure is at link 4 across documented cases. That would falsify the "usually middle" claim of F7 in the primary audit, though not the dependency ordering.

## Q4 - Competing explanations, and what each can and cannot reach

| candidate explanation | what it names | figure-line bearing | what it cannot reach |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Transaction cost economics** (Williamson: asset specificity, uncertainty, opportunism) | the governance form that minimizes the cost of the transaction | close to C1 and D4; Fig 6 :23 on contact with a differing closure | The learning and accountability slots. It prices the transaction and does not measure whether the method crossed back or who authors the verdict |
| **Core competence / core rigidity** (Prahalad and Hamel 1990; Leonard-Barton 1992) | what the firm should keep, and how a strength flips | Fig 11 :49-51 valence relative to residual; Fig 12 :119 catalyst | A selection rule per activity with a stated refuter. The phrase is a management-pool binding, not a derivation |
| **Organizational learning and knowledge loss** (Kjellström 2019; Schlosser et al. 2006) | loss of organizational memory and learning capital; boundary spanning as the remedy | directly matches Fig 4 :23 and Fig 12 :107-109 | A per-slot instrument that would discriminate a real method from a recording before the next activity fails. The remedy is named but not priced |
| **Co-sourcing / hybrid governance** | a contract form that keeps internal staff alongside the vendor | a practical attempt to keep the method crossing back; Fig 4 :22 | Whether the arrangement is an isomer or merely a functional group. A hybrid form is an atom list, not a bond record |
| **Cognitive offloading** (Risko and Gilbert 2016) | moving a cognitive demand to an external store reduces internal demand | low-overlap corroboration of the head-start-versus-method shape at the individual stratum | Firm-level control and accountability. Different anchor, so Fig 4 :20 requires re-naming |
| **Vendor reputation / prestige** | a well-known supplier reduces perceived risk | no anchor independent of delivered outcome; excluded as X1 | Any verdict. It can fund a trial and cannot settle one |
| **Luck / temporary wage differential** | an unearned early cost reading that persists while unfuted | Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* | Cannot be excluded from inside a frame. Honest `gap`, not a rival verdict |

No candidate is discarded by prestige or familiarity. Each is ranged against the same residual, in the order Fig 8 :53 requires. The point is not to pick one; it is to notice that the first four are overlapping descriptions of one evidence pool (Fig 2 :31), that the cognition source corroborates the mechanism at a different scale, and that the last two are honest limits.

## Q5 - Does insourcing restore what was lost?

> **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."** (Fig 11 :66)
> **"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."** (Fig 11 :67)
> **"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"** (Fig 11 :68)
> **"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."** (Fig 11 :61)

Insourcing is not the inverse of outsourcing. The firm moves the residual back into a habitat that has been substituted in the meantime, and it must reproduce everything the goal required. What it failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails again. The seat's power is asymmetric: it can add an actor back and it cannot subtract the habitat that formed while the work was outside. So the round trip is lossy (Fig 4 :27-30), and the gap must be measured rather than assumed zero. This is the reverse-direction falsifier for F1.

**Falsifier:** a case where insourcing restores the original next-activity capability at zero re-derivation cost with no new import and no recorded method. Under Fig 11 :66 that should be impossible; if observed, the habitat-substitution reading is wrong for that case.

## CONCLUSION

Two arrangements with the same vendor, price and SLA but different bond structure (method carried back and recorded versus recording only) are **isomers** under Fig 12 :117; two arrangements sharing only `firm → vendor → deliverable` are members of one **functional group** under Fig 12 :124. The method/recording distinction is a bond distinction, testable by re-derivation cost, not a vocabulary distinction. The conditions bind in dependency order, with vocabulary and carry the usual breaks, which is why C1 and C2 need different instruments. The standard make-or-buy explanations are mostly bindings of one evidence pool and are generated by the shape only up to the point where a name would be required. Insourcing does not undo the substitution; the return trip is lossy. The isomer and order claims have written falsifiers; the naming gap is declared rather than filled.
