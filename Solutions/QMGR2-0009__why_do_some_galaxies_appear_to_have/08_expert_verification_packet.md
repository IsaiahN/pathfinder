**What this is:** a claim in a form an astronomer can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape and the per-domain verdicts, `gap` on the physical cause, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. No measurement was taken in this derivation.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

For one galaxy, the residual is the difference between the mass a prediction frame expects from luminous mass and distance and the mass an instrument returns from kinematics. That residual is per galaxy, never a population average: the 2024 Gemini imaging separates DF4 (tails) from DF2 (no tails to a deeper limit). The measurement channel, chiefly distance and velocity tracer, is the contested anchor and must not move with theory preference. The gap must be sorted before a mechanism is minted: a wrong distance is a mis-attached gap to refit; a stripped halo or a modified force law is a mechanism-broken gap that owes a new term. The habitat of neighbouring galaxies must be enumerated before any neighbour is imported as the cause. A null tail search constrains rather than excludes. The figures return this shape and stop; the physical cause is a `gap` to be settled by a discriminator, not by agreement.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 1

- **Statement:** the residual is indexed per galaxy slot and must never be aggregated into a population statement.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Also Fig 1 :28 "The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."
- **Physics-facing check:** the 2024 Gemini result found tidal tails in DF4 and none in DF2 down to about 30.9 mag per square arcsecond (Golini, Montes et al., A&A 684 A99, 2024). Does a single "UDGs lack dark matter" statement remain defensible after that split?
- **Refuter:** show that averaging across the two galaxies preserves the directional signal that tells an observer where to look next, or exhibit a live slot whose positive residual disappears into a mean.

### C2 - framework-internal sort, physics-facing consequence

- **Statement:** sort the gap before minting a mechanism. The distance-error explanation belongs to the mis-attached bin; tidal stripping and modified gravity belong to the mechanism-broken bin.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :13 "mis-attached: refit what you have, do not invent" and Fig 5 :14 "mechanism broken: a new term is owed." The anchor requirement is Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Also Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
- **What to check:** the distance debate is live. Trujillo et al. (MNRAS 2019) argue for about 13 Mpc from five indicators, which makes DF2 ordinary; van Dokkum et al. (ApJL 2018) defend about 19 to 20 Mpc using a megamaser-TRGB-SBF ladder. Is either distance an anchor in the figure's sense, that is, does it stay put when the preferred mechanism changes? If the distance swaps the verdict, the gap is mis-attached and a mechanism must not yet be minted.
- **Refuter:** show that the distance is settled and independent of theory preference while the residual remains live, in which case the refit is exhausted and a mechanism-broken mint is licensed; or show that the two distance teams actually share the same calibration and are therefore one witness.

### C3 - habitat and null result

- **Statement:** enumerate the habitat before importing an actor, and treat a null detection as a constraint rather than a proof.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :44 "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." Also Fig 8 :39 "a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
- **What to check:** tails were detected around DF4 (Montes et al., ApJ 2020; ESA/Hubble 2020) and attributed to NGC 1035, while Ogiya (MNRAS Letters 2018) reproduced DF2 with a cored halo and a radial orbit around NGC 1052 and predicted such objects are *"not present in the field."* The 2024 data found no tails around DF2. Did the search cover a sealed frame, or only surface brightnesses above a limit? Does a field galaxy with a genuine deficit exist?
- **Refuter:** exhibit a field galaxy with a real deficit and no neighbour in contact (breaks the habitat import), or show that a null tail search at the stated depth can exclude every past interaction (breaks the null-as-constraint claim).

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a mechanism. The figures do not name tidal stripping, MOND, a distance error or a collision as the cause. That question is a `gap`.
- Not a claim that DF2 and DF4 share a cause. The 2024 imaging separates them.
- Not a claim that no stripping happened in DF2. A null result at a stated depth is a constraint.
- Not a claim that either the 13 Mpc or the 19 to 20 Mpc distance is correct.

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminator, not another argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved the case. Here:

1. **Anchor:** an independent distance ladder or stellar-based dispersion that does not move with theory preference.
2. **Habitat:** imaging at fainter limits and covering the candidate disruptor, with a stripped-mass budget, to name the import's source.
3. **Field:** a genuinely isolated galaxy with a real deficit, which would falsify the habitat import.
4. **Frame:** a MOND prediction at low overlap with the standard prediction, or a standard prediction that succeeds once the anchor is fixed.
