## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are DF2 and DF4 isomers?

> Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

The 2019 discovery paper described DF4 as *"a doppelgänger"* of DF2 with similar size, surface brightness, morphology, distance and globular-cluster population (van Dokkum et al., ApJL 2019, doi:10.3847/2041-8213/ab0d92). On that shared baryonic atom set they look like isomers: same ingredients, different arrangement of neighbour and halo, different observed state (one with tails, one without; one with a stripped halo, one with a deficit of unknown origin). Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance."

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if the two galaxies are shown not to share the atom set, for example different stellar masses outside the quoted errors, different metallicities, or different globular-cluster specific frequencies that place them in different formation channels, the isomer claim fails and the weaker functional-group reading stands:

> Fig 12 :124 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

The group would be "ultra-diffuse galaxy with a low velocity dispersion"; the qualifier would be "with a detected tidal tail" versus "without one." **Status: single-source** for the figure definition, with the physics-facing check unmeasured. The 2024 Gemini imaging keeps the isomer claim live by finding a decisive behavioural difference while leaving the shared-atom premise intact.

### Q2 - Is the neighbour a catalyst?

> Fig 12 :119 "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."

The perturber in the stripping story (NGC 1035 for DF4, possibly NGC 1052 for DF2) is not consumed by removing the halo. It survives its own use and can act again, which is Fig 12's catalyst. This matters because a spent and a regenerating part behave differently across uses, and an account that does not record which one it has cannot predict the next encounter.

**Falsifier:** if the neighbour is itself substantially disrupted or consumed in the interaction, so that it cannot act a second time, the catalyst reading fails and the neighbour is an ordinary reactant. The 2024 study notes that the identity of DF4's disruptor is still debated (NGC 1035 versus NGC 1052) and that deep data covering NGC 1035 in the same pointing would be needed; that debate is a catalyst-identity question, not a mere naming dispute.

### Q3 - What is the dependency order?

Fig 3 gives status per link and requires the chain to be read in dependency order, not in order of importance:

> Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

1. **Perception (D1):** does the instrument return a resolved dispersion and a pinned distance? If the distance is unset, everything below is unreadable.
2. **Vocabulary (D1 to D2):** do we have a term for what the luminous mass affords under a given halo relation? This is the middle link Fig 3 names as the one with no instrument and the fewest inspections.
3. **Objective (D2/D3):** what would count as progress, in the frame's own terms? For the standard frame, a halo mass consistent with the stellar mass; for MOND, a dispersion consistent with the internal plus external field.
4. **Planning (D4):** a route to the check, chosen under incomplete information, namely which neighbour to image and to what depth.
5. **Learn and carry:** name what worked, verify it against the ground, and carry it to the next galaxy.

Because D1 feeds all three other domains, effort spent on better simulations (D2) or more MOND variants (D3) while the distance is unsettled lands above the break. This is the figure's dependency rule, not a preference for one field.

### Q4 - Does the union need unbundling?

> Fig 9 :53 "Nobody chooses. The gap does, and the gap is only as good as its description."

> Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."

The four carriers overlap (distance touches the prediction, the external field and the neighbour association). A single lever, for example deeper imaging, clears the region where only the habitat condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the distance condition still binds. The residue after imaging DF2 is smaller and categorically different: it is no longer "tidal stripping versus no tidal stripping," it is "a real deficit at a still-contested distance." Fig 9 :59 calls that the second-order value of a big lever: not that the pile is smaller, but that the residue is finally describable.

## CONCLUSION

DF2 and DF4 are a live isomer claim over a shared baryonic atom set, with a stated falsifier; failing that they are members of one functional group distinguished by a tail. The neighbour in the stripping account is a catalyst in Fig 12's precise sense: it survives its own use, and an account must record which neighbour it is. The carrier domains sit in a dependency order with the distance measurement as the first link, so effort on theory above a broken distance link cannot rescue the verdict. The carrier structure is a union, so one instrument clears one condition and the intersection becomes a new, smaller, describable residual.
