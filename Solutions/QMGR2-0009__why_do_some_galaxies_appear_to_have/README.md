## What the question decides, in effect terms

A galaxy is one slot `s`. A frame predicts a halo mass from its luminous mass and distance (`Γ`), an instrument returns a dynamical mass from stellar or globular-cluster kinematics (`o'`), and the residual is `R_s = |Γ(b,a) − o'|`. "Appears to have less dark matter than expected" is the observation. The decision is whether that residual is a real deficit, a measurement-channel artifact (distance, tracer sampling, equilibrium assumption), a failure of the prediction frame, or an actor in the galaxy's habitat that removed the halo. Each option leads to a different instrument and a different refuter. An answer would look like: a per-slot verdict on which link carries the residual, the anchor that would settle it, and the observation that would reverse the verdict.
