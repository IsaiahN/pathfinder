## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Strategic coherence" and "frequently changing priorities" are not opposite ends of one axis; they govern different quantities. Coherence is the set of commitments and capabilities a company holds together with the anchor it ranges against; priority change is the rearrangement of those commitments and the selection of new rooms over time. The set is closed while the arrangements are not, so the first can stay fixed while the second changes often. Two conditions must hold at once: the market must be a genuine anchor that does not change its answer when the company pushes on it (otherwise the apparent coherence is coherent drift), and each priority change must be selected against what the previous room surfaced (otherwise it is motion, not progress). A priority change may merely rearrange held atoms or may import something from a differing closure; only the latter expands reach, and the priority list alone cannot tell you which kind you are looking at. The formerly coherent commitment can change sign when the residual changes, so the strength and the rigidity can be the same object. The claim is a shape that any candidate answer must have; it does not name which priorities win.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 13, 4 and 2

- **Statement:** set/kernel stability and arrangement/room change are different quantities, so coherence and frequent change can coexist; and the anchor must not move when pushed, otherwise "coherence" is a named collapse.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 13 :115 - *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."*
  - Fig 4 :20 - *"it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."*
  - Fig 2 :29 - *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* The same line names *"a market"* as an instance.
  - Fig 2 :34 - *"the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."* Fig 2 :35 - *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
- **Refuter:** show a system in which changing an arrangement necessarily changes the set, so Fig 13 :115 fails; or a company whose high internal agreement and stable published direction coexist with a market anchor that measurably moved against it, where its readings were not updated to match, and where it still retains contact. Either fires against C1.

### C2 - Framework-internal derivation plus one business-facing check

- **Statement:** frequent change is correct when the question moves, but is progress only when rooms differ and are selected against the surfaced residual; internal re-shuffling adds no atom, while only contact with a differing closure moves the wall.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 10 :72 - *"When the question changes, the room should change. A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom."*
  - Fig 7 :36 - *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."* Fig 7 :32 - *"one long room: motion, no progress"*
  - Fig 7 :37 - *"Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:"* Fig 7 :38 - *"the bracket’s fidelity across the handoff"* Fig 7 :39 - *"the ground’s pressure on the room"*
  - Fig 6 :23 - *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 8 :32 - *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."*
- **What a researcher can check against their own knowledge:** the ambidexterity literature says such organizations *"do not alternate between exploration and exploitation, but they do both simultaneously"* (Wikipedia summary of Raisch and Birkinshaw 2008, read 2026-11-22). Does that clause forbid the temporal room selection Fig 10 :72 requires, or does it describe structural allocation at a moment? A demonstrated incompatibility would be a real refutation of the mapping. Teece's page defines dynamic capabilities as *"the firm’s ability to integrate, build, and reconfigure internal and external competences to address rapidly changing environments"*; is that operator anything other than the import described in Fig 6 :23 plus the re-binding described in Fig 12 :117?
- **Refuter:** a company that changes priorities frequently, each change selected against the surfaced residual, and whose R does not trend down; or a company that expands demonstrated reach purely by internal re-prioritisation with no new atom from a differing closure. Either breaks C2.

### C3 - Structure and competing explanations

- **Statement:** the union of conditions must be unbundled before a lever is chosen; two companies with identical commitments but different bonds are isomers, so a resource inventory alone cannot predict coherence under change; the strength-to-rigidity flip follows from valence being relative to the residual.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 9 :57 - *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
  - Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
  - Fig 11 :50 - *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."*
  - Fig 12 :119 - *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."*
- **Refuter:** one intervention that restores both conditions in both failure types and leaves no categorically different residue; or two companies with identical atoms and identical bonds whose coherence under change diverges with no difference in contact or imported atoms, which would move the explanation out of composition and into habitat alone; or a commitment whose measured valence is invariant across a documented change of residual.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not `grounded`. No measurement has paid for any part of this.
- Not a theory of everything about management, and no final or unique theory claim.
- Not an average. No company-wide coherence score, no claim about the percentage of variance attributable to alignment, leadership or industry.
- Not that mission, culture, leadership or alignment are irrelevant. They are Γ and instruments; the claim is that they do not settle coherence on their own.
- Not that frequent change is always good. Change without residual selection is motion, not progress.
- Not that any specific priority or capability is the answer. That is a `gap` at the instrument boundary, not a hidden answer.
- Not that the strategy literature independently corroborates this. Ambidexterity and dynamic capabilities share one evidence pool. The low-overlap sources from systems biology and physiology corroborate the mechanism shape, not the company-level fact.

## 5. What would settle it

A prospective, per-slot study that measures C1 and C2 separately, records bonds as well as atoms, logs the provenance of each priority change, and reports which condition failed before coherence broke. Concretely:

1. **Anchor test:** does the market anchor move when the company pushes on it, through metric redefinition or internal redefinition of what pays? Fig 2 :29-30 says an anchor that moves is not one.
2. **Room-selection test:** does each new priority differ from the last *and* match the residual the last one surfaced? Fig 7 :36-39. Are bracket fidelity and ground pressure both nonzero?
3. **Import test:** did any new atom enter the company's reach from a closure that differs, before the period of coherence? If not, C2's reach claim is refuted.
4. **Source-discriminating instrument:** can the study distinguish an anchored kernel from coherent drift, and a hidden asymmetry from a paying habitat, when all produce the same coherence reading? The source essay says a reading alone *"cannot comment on the source"*.
