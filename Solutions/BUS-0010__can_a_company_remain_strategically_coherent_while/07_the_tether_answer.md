# The Tether's answer on coherence under frequent priority change

**Mode: `general` on the set/arrangement shape, `specified` on the company application, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading. No market measurement pays for any claim here.

## 1. The residual it answers

An answer would explain how the gap between what a company's commitments predict the market will return and what the market actually returns can stay small across slots while the company repeatedly changes what it does. R is indexed per slot, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19, :22). The question is about the **coexistence of two quantities**, not about the level of either.

The question arrives with a defective term. "Coherence" in ordinary use means internal agreement, and Fig 2 names internal agreement without a non-updating anchor as a collapse:

> **"everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."** (Fig 2 :34)
> **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."** (Fig 2 :35)

So the term must be re-grounded before the question can be answered. The union must then be unbundled (Fig 9 :54). It is two conditions, not one hard question:

- **C1:** the anchor and the kernel stay fixed, meaning the market still pays, it does not move when pushed, and the company's channel to it stays reachable.
- **C2:** the arrangements and rooms change, each change selected against the residual the last room surfaced, and each change classified as re-arrangement or import.

## 2. The shape

1. **Coherence and priority change are different quantities.** Fig 13 :115: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."* A company can hold the first fixed while changing the second frequently. The apparent contradiction is a category error produced by putting them on one axis.
2. **The kernel is constant; only the actors, currency and anchor change.** Fig 4 :20. The kernel is *"the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked"* (Fig 4 :19). What may change without losing coherence is which commitments are active, which rooms are booked, and which instruments are ranged against the ground. Under the framework's own metaphor: *"The kernel is fixed. The operating system grows."* (framework essay, read 2026-11-22).
3. **The market, not internal agreement, is the anchor.** Fig 2 :29 names *"a market"* as an anchor; :30: *"you push on it, it is not an anchor."* A strategy deck, an alignment score and a mission statement move when pushed, so they are instruments, not the verdict.
4. **Frequent change is required when the question moves.** Fig 10 :72: *"When the question changes, the room should change. A room that stays fixed while the question moves is the failure; the correlation is only its symptom."* Holding a plan while the market moves is not the coherent option; it is the named failure.
5. **Change must be room-selected or it is motion only.** Fig 7 :36: *"The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."* Fig 7 :32: *"one long room: motion, no progress."* Fig 7 :37-39 gives the two-factor instrument: bracket fidelity times ground pressure, and zero either stops the chain.
6. **Only contact with a differing closure moves the wall.** Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 8 :32: closure is idempotent, so re-shuffling priorities never adds an atom. A priority change can be a re-sequencing, a re-binding or an import; only the last expands what the company can reach, and it carries a provenance debt (Fig 8 :51).
7. **The coherent commitment can change sign.** Fig 11 :49-51: valence is relative to the residual and no standing list of good actors survives a change of residual. Fig 12 :119: a catalyst is a part that survives its own use, and *"nothing records which a term is"*. The core that made the company coherent may be the spent part that prevents the next match.
8. **The break is usually in the middle of the chain.** Fig 3 :24 and :27: the vocabulary/objective link has no instrument, and a link with no instrument is a link nobody has looked at. Buying a faster priority cadence is effort at planning, above the break.
9. **Two companies with the same commitments can still differ.** Under Fig 12 :117 they are isomers if the bonds differ: *"same atoms, different bond, different substance."* A resource inventory alone cannot predict which one stays coherent under change.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not `grounded`. No prospective market measurement has paid for this shape.
- Not a name. It does not say which priorities, which missions, which cultures or which capabilities produce coherent change. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- Not an average. It makes no claim about a company-wide coherence score. Such a number is a filter for budget, not a witness (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that alignment, mission or culture are irrelevant. They are Γ and instruments. The claim is that they do not settle coherence on their own (Fig 1 :27, Fig 2 :35).
- Not that frequent change is always good. Change without residual selection is motion, not progress (Fig 7 :32).
- Not that ambidexterity or dynamic capabilities are the framework's answer. They are bindings of one evidence pool, recorded as such (Fig 2 :31, :40).
- Not corroboration by agreement. The strategy sources share one evidence pool. The low-overlap sources from systems biology and physiology corroborate the mechanism shape, not the company-level fact.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. The source essay's metamerism rule: a reading of green *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"the answer is unreached until a better instrument exists"*. Here the reading is "this company is coherent"; the candidate sources are an anchored kernel, a market that has not yet moved, a hidden asymmetry, simple luck, and coherent drift.

The falsifiers, pre-registered in `01` and repeated in `08`:

1. changing an arrangement necessarily changes the set, so the two quantities cannot be separated;
2. high internal agreement and stable published direction with a market anchor that measurably moved against it, and continued contact;
3. frequent change selected against the surfaced residual with R not trending down;
4. reach expanded purely by internal re-prioritisation with no atom from a differing closure;
5. invariant commitment valence across a documented change of residual and habitat;
6. one intervention restoring both conditions with nothing categorically different left over.

A prospective study that measures C1 and C2 separately per slot, records bonds as well as atoms, logs the provenance of each priority change, and reports which condition failed before the coherence broke would turn this from `specified` toward `grounded`. It would also need an instrument that distinguishes an anchored anchor from one that moved to match the company's plan.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The shape stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. The missing things are a source discriminator (anchored coherence versus drift) and a provenance record (import versus re-arrangement). Both are characterized in effect terms but not held here. Naming a winner from a coherence reading would be the metamerism error the source essay forbids. The nine points in section 2 are a shape; the tenth thing, which priorities actually work next, is not pretended.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not rank companies or name the priorities that will keep one coherent. It says that coherence and frequent priority change are not opposite ends of one axis: coherence is stable contact with a market that does not move when pushed, while priorities are the arrangements and rooms that may change often. The set is closed, the arrangements are not, so a company can hold its kernel and change what it does. But two tests decide whether the change is real. Each new room must differ from the last and be selected against what the last one failed to explain, or it is motion rather than progress; and a priority change that only reshuffles what the company already holds does not expand what it can reach, because only contact with a way of working that differs can move that wall. The capability that made the company coherent can also change sign when the market changes, so the strength and the rigidity can be the same commitment. What is offered is that shape, six falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. Which priorities occupy the slots stays open until a source-discriminating instrument exists, and the stop is recorded rather than filled.
