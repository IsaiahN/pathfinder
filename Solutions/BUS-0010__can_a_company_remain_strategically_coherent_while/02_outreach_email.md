# Outreach draft - validate the output first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. No personal emails are used; the recipient must have an opt-in or public institutional route.

**Hard precondition:** the stage-1 output must survive the recipient's check before any theory is shown. The output here is a `specified` shape with no ground measurement, so the legitimate ask is a refutation of the shape, never endorsement of it. If the recipient cannot be shown a failure condition they can actually test, do not send.

---

**Subject:** Check one claim about coherence and changing priorities before you decide whether any of this is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one narrow claim about strategic coherence that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. I am asking for a failure. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** "staying coherent" and "changing priorities often" are not opposite ends of one axis. They govern different quantities. Coherence is the set of commitments and capabilities a company holds plus the anchor it ranges against; priority change is the rearrangement of those commitments and the selection of new rooms over time. A company can hold the first fixed while changing the second frequently. The framework's own words are that the set is closed while the arrangements are not, and that the kernel does not change with size while only the actors, currency and anchor do.
>
> Two things must hold at once for this to work:
>
> 1. the market the company is ranged against must be a real anchor, meaning it does not change its answer because of what the company thinks. If the company's metrics and its plan simply agree with each other while the market moves, that is not coherence. It is coherent drift, and it is invisible precisely because it looks like agreement.
> 2. each priority change must be selected against what the previous cycle failed to explain. Changing rooms often without that selection is motion, not progress. The next room must differ from the last *and* match the residual the last one surfaced.
>
> A third point follows: a priority change can rearrange what the company already holds without adding anything new, or it can bring in something from a way of working that differs from its own. Only the second kind expands what the company can reach. The priority list alone cannot tell you which kind you are looking at.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I can name which priorities a company should change, that any particular company is a case in point, that this is settled by evidence, that frequent change is always good, or that it is the only account of coherence. I am offering a shape any candidate answer would have to have. A shape is not a name. I am also not saying internal alignment is worthless; I am saying it is not the verdict.
>
> **What would refute it, written down before I sent this:**
> 1. a system in which changing an arrangement necessarily changes the set, so the two quantities cannot be separated;
> 2. a company whose high internal agreement and stable published direction coexist with a market anchor that measurably moved against it, and where it still retains contact;
> 3. a company that changes priorities frequently, each change selected against the prior cycle's surfaced residual, and whose market residual does not trend down;
> 4. a company that expands its demonstrated reach purely by internal re-prioritisation with nothing entering from a differing closure;
> 5. one intervention that restores both conditions in both failure types and leaves nothing categorically different behind.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the claim. A single line saying which one holds and why is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send the three public sources below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
>
> - Figure text with line numbers (I can point to the exact lines): the local canonical figure text in this solution folder.
> - Formula and usage rule: the `figures` folder on the public `general-agent` branch of the `tether` repository.
> - The essays on source, search shape and the prestige/action separation: my Substack archive.
>
> I will send URLs only after you tell me what you would count as a failure.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is public. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a real ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Residual and refuter first, theory last | *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* (Fig 10 :52). The recipient checks the claim without holding the framework's interior |
| Do not ask for agreement | *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35) |
| The word "coherence" is re-grounded, not assumed | *"the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."* (Fig 2 :34). Agreement without a non-updating anchor is a named collapse |
| Two conditions kept separate | *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"* (Fig 9 :57); a blended coherence score is a pooled reading (Fig 10 :26) |
| Set and arrangement distinguished | *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not."* (Fig 13 :115) |
| Pre-registered refuter | *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device"* |
| Mode stated on the claim | *"Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which."* (`THE_FORMULA.md`) |
| No name for the priorities | *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (Fig 9 :32) |
| Links only after a verdict | Fig 10 :42, *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Once the recipient has the framework, their later agreement is not corroboration (Fig 2 :40) |
| Prestige kept out of the ask | *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, read 2026-11-22) |
| Offer the formula as a method, not an answer | *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* (Fig 4 :22) |
| Ask the user for their own refuter | Formula, "Direction": a frame cannot check the direction of its own question; make the sought-for shape an ordinary term |

## Sending rules

- One recipient at a time. No distribution lists, no mail-merge, no forwarding chain.
- Use only a public institutional contact, a call for submissions, or an address the person has published themselves. Record the source and date.
- A Track B style reply is a conversation, never corroboration. If a recipient returns a refutation, copy it into `11_audit.md` with provenance and have the auditor seat re-evaluate it as if it came from any other source.
- Do not follow up with the theory after a "no". An interest in the outcome is unavoidable; an interest in the reading is disqualifying (Fig 10 :59).
