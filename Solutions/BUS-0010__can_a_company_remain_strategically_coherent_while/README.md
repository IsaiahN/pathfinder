## The question restated as an effect

What is being decided is not whether a company "sticks to its plan" in the everyday sense. It is whether two things can hold at once: a low, stable residual across a company's slots (call this coherence) and a high frequency of change in the action a company takes (call this priority change). In effect terms:

- **b** is the company's belief about what the market will pay for, formed from its own history and inherited priors.
- **a** is what the company does: which priorities it funds, which projects it starts and stops, which routines it runs.
- **Γ(b,a)** is what the company's library of commitments and routines predicts will happen.
- **o′** is what the market actually returns.
- **s** is the slot (a business, a product line, a capability, a customer relation). R = |Γ(b,a) − o′| is indexed per slot and per time, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22).

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** (Fig 1 :19)
> **"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"** (Fig 1 :13)

- Option A, "coherence and frequent change are opposites": leads to the prediction that a company must trade one for the other, and that a coherent company changes slowly.
- Option B, "coherence and frequent change govern different quantities": leads to the prediction that the set of commitments and the anchor can stay fixed while the arrangements and rooms change, and that the pair fails only when the anchor moves or when rooms change without selection against the residual.
- What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): a shape that names which quantity stays fixed and which quantity changes, plus an instrument that would discriminate a ground-anchored change from a motion-only change. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no loop check can see it. It is now bound, so the refuter in `01` section 5 can fire on it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B in `specified` mode, kept per domain and never averaged. The ordinary sense of "coherence" as internal agreement is **refused** as a named collapse (Fig 2 :34, Fig 2 :35), not adopted as the answer.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles the business question, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of strategic coherence.

Three figure-grounded reasons, plus one practical one:

1. A room is not an anchor. *"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35) and *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Gathering agreeable readers manufactures the correlation that disqualifies a verdict.
2. Correlation has valence. *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). A list selected for receptiveness is one witness wearing many names.
3. A verifier must be outside the interior. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, not the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
