## Q1 - Are two companies with the same commitments isomers?

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** (Fig 12 :117)
> **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** (Fig 12 :123)
> **"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."** (Fig 12 :92)
> **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."** (Fig 13 :115)

Two companies with an identical list of commitments, capabilities and assets have the same atoms. If their bonds differ, that is, if one composes those atoms as a repeated sequence and the other as a reconfigurable network, they are **isomers** under Fig 12 :117: same atoms, different bond, different behaviour, with the difference invisible in the resource list. This is the figure-grounded version of "it is not what you hold, it is how you arrange it", stronger than the slogan because it says the arrangement is itself a substance and carries information the ingredients do not.

If the two companies share only a pattern (both use `commitment → priority → market`) while differing in which atoms occupy the slots, the weaker claim holds: members of the same **functional group** (Fig 12 :123), the qualifier saying which member. The presence of the pattern fixes the kind, not the outcome.

The distinction is testable. A resource-list comparison that finds no difference between two companies cannot distinguish isomer from functional-group; the bond has to be recorded. Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*

This is the structural warrant for F1 in `01`. The apparent contradiction between coherence and frequent change disappears once you notice that the resource list (the set) and the arrangement over it are different objects.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two companies with identical atoms and identical bonds nevertheless diverge in persistence with no difference in ground contact or imported atoms, the isomer framing is insufficient and the difference must be habitat (D2), which would move the explanation out of D3.

**Status:** `specified`, triangulated for the legality of the reading (Fig 12 and Fig 11 :50, different figures), single-source for the business application. The world corroboration is the ambidexterity and dynamic-capabilities literature's emphasis on configuration and reconfiguration, but both are the same strategy evidence pool.

## Q2 - What order do the conditions bind in?

> **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."** (Fig 3 :20-21)
> **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** (Fig 3 :19)

Order for a company facing a changed market:

1. **Perception.** Can it read the market's return to the changed priority at all? Failure here is visible and has obvious tests.
2. **Vocabulary.** Does its Γ have terms for what the changed situation affords, and can it say what would count as progress there? This is the middle link. Fig 3 :26: *"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."*
3. **Objective.** Can it state a comparison that would count as getting somewhere? Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* A vocabulary with only ingredients cannot state a goal.
4. **Planning.** A route to that objective under incomplete information.
5. **Learn and carry.** Name what worked, verify it, carry it to the next problem.

The two conditions from the primary audit map onto this chain: C1 (anchor and kernel held) is mostly a failure of links 1 and 2 when the anchor is silently redefined; C2 (arrangements changed against the residual) is mostly a failure of link 5, because the company names what worked but never imports a new atom. That mapping is why a single lever cannot clear both (Fig 9 :55).

The order also decides which instrument to buy first. Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* The market-facing link (1) has accounting and share data; link 5 has almost none. So budget will naturally pile up at links 1 and 4 while the break sits at 2 and 5. Buying a faster priority cadence is effort at link 4.

**Falsifier:** a company whose chain demonstrably works at 1, 2 and 3 and fails only at 4. That would falsify the "usually middle" claim of F7 in the primary audit.

## Q3 - Is a priority change a re-arrangement or an import?

> **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."** (Fig 6 :21)
> **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."** (Fig 6 :23)
> **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."** (Fig 8 :51)
> **"the only operator that moves the wall"** (Fig 6 :14)

The priority list alone cannot answer this question. Three cases must be separated:

| case | what changed | figure | effect on coherence | effect on reach |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Re-sequencing | the order of held priorities | Fig 12 :112, `→` sequence | preserved | unchanged |
| B. Re-binding | which held atom is bonded to which | Fig 12 :117, isomer | preserved | unchanged, but new substances appear inside the same closure |
| C. Import | a new atom from a differing closure | Fig 6 :14, :23 | preserved only if the anchor is not replaced | expanded, with a provenance debt |

Case C is where the coherence claim is most at risk, not because importing is wrong, but because an import is often smuggled in as a re-definition of the anchor. The company adopts a new metric, a new "mission" or a new definition of what pays, and calls the resulting internal consistency coherence. That is Fig 2 :34 again under a different name. The source gate and shadow test (Fig 6 :12-13) are the instruments: where did the atom come from, and what gap does it explain?

Ramsay and Woods 2014 (read 2026-11-22) give a low-overlap analogy for case C: allostasis achieves *"stability through change"* by changing *"the defended level of a regulated value"* in response to environmental demand. The defended level changes; the regulation does not become a different system. That is the healthy case. The same review notes the concept *"remains diffuse"*, which is the warning that without a discriminator you cannot tell healthy re-setting from drift.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** a company whose demonstrated reach expands through case A or B alone, with no atom entering from a differing closure and no source gate, over a documented interval. That breaks idempotence and F4.

## Q4 - Competing explanations, and what each can and cannot reach

| candidate explanation | what it names | figure-line bearing | what it cannot reach |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ambidexterity** (March 1991; Raisch and Birkinshaw 2008 via the summary read 2026-11-22) | simultaneous exploration and exploitation, alignment plus adaptability | matches the "both coexist" shape; Fig 10 :72 governs the room sequence | Its *"do not alternate"* clause conflicts with temporal room selection if read as a universal. Re-census: structural allocation at a moment versus room sequence across time, different quantities (strain S4) |
| **Dynamic capabilities** (Teece, Pisano, Shuen 1997; Teece page read 2026-11-22) | the operator that reconfigures internal and external competences | close to C2 and to Fig 6 :23 | A business-pool binding, not a derivation. It does not say what would refute it, and it does not separate re-arrangement from import |
| **Success trap / core rigidity** (ambidexterity summary; Leonard-Barton name via BUS-0001) | the formerly good commitment turning bad | matches Fig 11 :49-51 and Fig 12 :119 | Names the flip, not the instrument that would detect the valence change before the residual moves |
| **Homeostasis as a coherence analogue** | a fixed set point defended by negative feedback | part of D3; useful contrast | Cannon's set point was never strictly invariant (Ramsay and Woods 2014), so the naive "hold the core constant" version is a weaker analogue than allostasis |
| **Allostasis as a coherence analogue** | stability through change of defended levels | Fig 13 :115, Fig 10 :72 at a different scale | Physiology, not companies. Fig 4 :20 requires the anchor to be re-named at the company scale. The concept is also *"diffuse"*, so it corroborates the gap, not a name |
| **Robustness and evolvability** (Daniels et al. 2008) | neutral networks allowing drift in genotype with stable phenotype | Fig 13 :115 and Fig 12 :119, low overlap | Systems biology, not this ground. Corroborates the mechanism shape only |
| **"Just keep the mission constant"** | a fixed direction as the coherence criterion | Fig 2 :29-30: if it moves when pushed it is not an anchor | No ground contact condition, so it cannot distinguish anchored direction from coherent drift (F2) |
| **Luck / hidden monopoly** | an unearned early reading that persists while unfuted | Fig 8 :40: *"Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts"* | Cannot be excluded from inside a frame; Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Honest `gap`, not a rival verdict |

No candidate is discarded by prestige or familiarity. Each is ranged against the same residual, in the order Fig 8 :53 requires. The point is not to pick one; it is to notice that the first four are all in one evidence pool (Fig 2 :31) and that the two low-overlap fields corroborate mechanism, not the company-level fact.

## Q5 - Does the shape generate the literature's answers?

Partly. The shape can generate:

- the ambidexterity emphasis on holding alignment and adaptability at once, as C1 and C2 kept distinct;
- the dynamic-capabilities emphasis on reconfiguration, as case B and case C in Q3;
- the success-trap emphasis on the flip, as Fig 11 :49-51 and Fig 12 :119;
- the coherence literature's emphasis on stable direction, as the anchor rule, with the crucial addition that the anchor must not move when pushed.

It cannot generate the *name* of the specific priority that will work next, and it cannot say, without a provenance record, whether a given change was an import or a re-shuffle. That is the `gap` recorded in F11 of `01`. It is a gap at the instrument boundary, not a silence: Fig 13 :134 (*"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*) and Fig 8 :39 apply. The source essay states why: a coherence reading *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"the answer is unreached until a better instrument exists"*.

## CONCLUSION

Two companies with the same atoms but different bonds are isomers under Fig 12 :117; two companies sharing only a `commitment → priority → market` pattern are members of one functional group under Fig 12 :123. The conditions bind in dependency order, with the middle link (vocabulary and objective) and the carry link the usual breaks, which is why C1 and C2 need different instruments. A priority change is a re-sequencing, a re-binding or an import, and only the third expands reach, but the priority list alone cannot tell you which; a provenance record is owed. The standard management explanations are mostly bindings of one evidence pool and are generated by the shape only up to the point where a name would be required. The isomer claim has a written falsifier; the order claim has one; the import claim has one; the naming gap is declared rather than filled.
