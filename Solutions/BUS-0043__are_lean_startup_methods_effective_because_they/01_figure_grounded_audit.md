## 0. The question under audit, in effect terms

> Are lean startup methods effective because they produce faster learning, or are they better suited to certain types of markets?

- **Option A, mechanism internal to the venture.** Effectiveness is a property of the loop. Faster build-measure-learn cycles reduce R = |Γ(b,a) - o'| per unit time. The intervention is to accelerate the loop in every venture.
- **Option B, contingency external to the venture.** Effectiveness is a property of the habitat. The same loop pays off only where customers, buyers, capital and regulators answer quickly, cheaply and with pressure. The intervention is to check the ground before prescribing the method.

**Residual R stated as an effect:** variance, across venture slots and time, in whether lean startup use moves a ground-visible outcome (payment, survival, validated customer problem) rather than only a frame-internal count (experiments run, hypotheses minted). R is indexed per venture and per time, never averaged (Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*). "Effective" is a threshold comparison, not a symmetric relation (Fig 12 :112: *"A goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*).

**Bound sought-for shape (before the run):** two conditions, one about maintained contact and one about renewed reach, both named so each can accrue a residual and be refused. This is Formula step "Direction" applied honestly, not a preference hidden from the checks.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.** Arity: at least two operands, a learning loop and a market ground, plus a comparison against a non-lean alternative. Symmetry: ordered (build then measure then learn) and comparative (effective relative to a threshold), not a symmetric join. Scale: one loop inside one venture; repeated loops as a cycle; a population of ventures ranked by a market; a habitat that updates underneath them. The membrane between the venture and the market is lossy (Fig 4).

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 The venture learning loop | founders, hypotheses Γ, MVP, experiments, pivots | residual reduction per slot, validated learning | the observation o' from the market | admitted |
| D2 The market habitat | customers, buyers, competitors, capital, regulators | payment, adoption, survival, permission to iterate | customer choice and *"a market"* (Fig 2 :29) | admitted |
| D3 The method as generator | build-measure-learn, MVP, pivot (¬), hypothesis | reach and cost of a term |φ| | settlement against the ground | admitted |
| D4 The instrumental channel | interviews, funnels, cohorts, metrics, prototypes | contact with the ground | *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"* (Fig 10 :24) | admitted |
| X1 "Faster learning" as an intrinsic property of the method | the loop's own outputs | experiments run, terms minted | none nameable; it scores itself with what it produces (Fig 1 :27) | **not a domain yet** |
| X2 "Market type" as a fixed list of good markets | a taxonomy of sectors | labels | absorbed into D2; valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49-51) | absorbed, not separate |

**No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.** X1 fails the anchor test: its metric is generated by the thing being scored. Fig 1 :25-27: *"coverage, and compression achieved"*, *"predicates minted, and credibility accrued"*, *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* So the mechanism-only reading cannot be a domain on its own terms.

**Partition or union: union.** A and B are overlapping conditions, not disjoint causes. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."* The compound is: a loop that maintains contact (bracket fidelity) **and** a ground that supplies pressure and new information. One lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Perception (can the venture tell what the market did?) to vocabulary (does it have terms for what a result affords?) to objective (can it say what counts as progress?) to planning (a route under incomplete information) to learn and carry (name what worked, verify, carry). Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*

Strain test applied before concluding: Fig 11 :40 governs an agent-only capability claim; Fig 2 :29 governs what counts as an anchor; Fig 6 :32 governs search inside a closure. They describe different quantities, so no strain is required to hold them together.

## 2. Findings

### F1 - "Faster learning" is frame-internal and cannot be the witness (decisive against option A as stated)

Fig 1 :23: *"The ground enters as the only metric."* Fig 1 :25-27: *"coverage, and compression achieved"*, *"predicates minted, and credibility accrued"*, *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Fig 5 :48: *"Its own output is never a measure of success. A system can be enthusiastically curious and produce nothing."*

A venture can run many experiments, mint many hypotheses and shorten every cycle while the market still refuses to pay. The number of loops completed is the loop's own output. Under the figures it is a **filter for the budget**, never a witness for the verdict: Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

This does **not** say learning is irrelevant. It says learning speed alone is the wrong quantity to score effectiveness with. Mode `general` for the metric rule, `specified` for the firm application. Refuter: show the framework admitting a loop's own output count as the ground metric for "effective," or a measurement where loop count tracks payment with the ground held fixed.

### F2 - Capability is agent-and-habitat, so an agent-only improvement changes nothing unless contact changes

Fig 11 :39: *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40: *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

Lean startup is a method applied to an agent (the founding team). Option A locates its effect entirely in that agent. Fig 11 :40 refuses that location. The loop matters because it can change contact, that is, because it puts a bet into the market and ranges the answer against the market. If a faster loop does not change what the ground returns, it changes nothing that the question asks about.

This is the strongest single figure-grounded point in the derivation. Mode `specified`. Refuter: a controlled habitat substitution that removes ground pressure while faster internal iteration leaves the ground-visible outcome unchanged.

### F3 - The market is an anchor, and method valence is relative to the residual

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Fig 11 :49: *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*

So option B is right about the location of the anchor but wrong if it claims a fixed taxonomy of suitable markets. Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."* A market that changes its answer because the founders believe in the method is not an anchor. And the same market can reward iteration for one residual and punish it for another: B2C software with many cheap, reachable respondents is a different ground from B2B materials with few buyers and slow, expensive change cycles (world source W1, secondary, cited in `09`). Mode `specified`. Refuter: an invariant method valence across a documented change of residual in the same market.

### F4 - Faster search inside a closure adds no atom; reach changes only on contact with a differing closure

Fig 6 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."* Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 13 :115: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."*

A team iterating faster on the same hypothesis vocabulary is searching more of the same space. It finds the answer that is already in closure(Γ), or it does not. What can move the wall is the market supplying a term the team did not hold: a use case nobody on the team imagined, a buyer who is not the user, a constraint the prototype did not contain. That is an import with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :12-13). Mode `general` for idempotence, `specified` for venture application. Refuter: a reach renewed by internal composition alone with no new atom from any differing frame.

### F5 - The loop is a cycle and a pivot is a refusal; a one-pass loop cannot close

Fig 12 :122: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* Operators table :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*.

Build-measure-learn is valuable exactly when the measurement re-enters as the next bet, and when a pivot refuses a held hypothesis rather than accumulating another feature. A vocabulary with no negation can correct a detail and cannot carry the correction. So "faster learning" that never re-enters and never refuses is a chain, not a cycle, and a chain cannot close by construction. Mode `general` (structural) with the business reading `specified`. Refuter: a lean loop shown to close without its output re-entering as input, or without any operator that can refuse a held term.

### F6 - Channel failure can imitate learning success

Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*. Fig 10 :26: *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*. Fig 7 :12-13: *"same closure, handed along;"*, *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*. Fig 7 :37-39: *"Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:"*, *"the bracket’s fidelity across the handoff"*, *"the ground’s pressure on the room"*.

Metrics can be saturated or pooled so that faster cycling looks like progress while contact has been lost. Repeat the same closure with the same customers and the agreement compounds while the evidence does not. This is why "faster learning" and "suitable market" are not two independent causes: the market's pressure is one of the two factors that make a handoff a progress step rather than motion. Mode `specified`. Refuter: advantage lost or gained while both bracket fidelity and ground pressure measure high and non-declining.

### F7 - No last room, so no universal method and no final market taxonomy

Fig 7 :6: *"the limit: no last room"*. Fig 7 :30: *"every room closes some of the gap; no finite number of them ever arrives"*. Fig 13 :90: *"One chain is one route through a space that has no last route."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*.

A claim that lean startup is effective everywhere, or that a fixed list of market types decides effectiveness once and for all, would be a terminal room. The figures forbid it. What is available is a shape (a ground that pays and pressures, a closure that differs, a channel that stays reachable), not a name. Mode `general`. Refuter: a closed, exhaustive taxonomy of suitable markets that no further handoff can extend.

### F8 - Naming the specific market attributes that make lean work is a `gap`

After a genuine re-census across D1 to D4, no figure line returns a list of market attributes. The figures give the shape of what the ground must do (settle, pay, pressure, supply a differing closure) and the shape of what the channel must do (stay reachable, not saturate, not pool). They do not say "B2C software" or "high market uncertainty, low technology uncertainty." Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*

World sources supply candidate attributes (market uncertainty versus technology uncertainty; buyer count; ease of modification; resource slack; community of inquiry), but they are secondary, share one evidence pool, and carry no verdict weight. Mode `gap`. **Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence.**

## 3. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach. A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A venture improves a ground-visible outcome with faster internal iteration while a habitat substitution removes ground pressure and the outcome is unchanged (breaks Fig 11 :40).
2. Two markets with equal measured loop speed show systematically different lean-performance slopes attributable to measured ground pressure, with the difference surviving a second, independent instrument (breaks the mechanism-only reading).
3. A single lever restores both conditions and leaves no categorically different residue (breaks the union reading, Fig 9 :57).
4. A frame-internal metric (experiments run, hypotheses minted) is admitted as the ground metric for "effective" (breaks Fig 1 :23 and :27).
5. A lean loop closes without its output re-entering as input, or a venture refuses a held hypothesis with no negation operator (breaks Fig 12 :122 and Operators :58).

If any fires, the union claim is withdrawn or narrowed, and the retraction is recorded here rather than erased. Until one is run, nothing in this folder is `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Refuse the forced either/or. Report the union: a loop that maintains contact **and** a ground that pressures and supplies a differing closure. Keep the two verdicts separate; never average them (Fig 1 :19).
2. Stop scoring "faster learning" with loop count. Score it with a ground-visible outcome (F1, F6).
3. Diagnose the break before prescribing: which link does this venture stop at (Fig 3 :29-30)? A team with no instrument for customer response cannot be rescued by more iterations.
4. Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term and let it accrue a residual. State what a suitable market would have to look like in effect terms before choosing a sector label (Fig 9 :27-28).
5. Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Send the refuter and the generator, not the conclusion.
6. Keep F8 visible as a `gap`. Do not promote a world source's market list into a figure result.

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is a slot-level measure available?** Yes. Fig 1 :19: residual per venture and time, never aggregated. A global "lean works" average would throw away the signal.
2. **Can the loop score itself?** No. Fig 1 :27 and Fig 5 :48. Learning speed is frame-internal.
3. **Does changing the agent change contact?** Only if the loop actually bets and ranges the answer. Fig 11 :40.
4. **What settles effectiveness?** The market. Fig 2 :29. Agreement inside the venture is a mirror (Fig 2 :35).
5. **Can faster search add what the team lacks?** No. Fig 6 :32. Only a differing closure can move the wall (Fig 6 :23).
6. **Is the answer a name?** No. The canonical README says *"a shape, not a name"* and Fig 9 :32 says *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Sector labels are bindings from the business frame, provenanced as such.
7. **Route.** Formula step 2: no term in closure(Γ) closes R after SEARCHED, so the residual is a union and owes two loops; INWARD is a better instrument for ground pressure, OUTWARD is the market as a frame whose closure differs. Do not read one against the other (Formula step 7).

### Updated verdict

The shape survives: lean methods are effective when the build-measure-learn loop maintains contact with a ground that pays and pressures, and when the market supplies a differing closure so reach can renew. "Faster learning" is necessary as the contact mechanism and insufficient as the witness; "market type" is the right location for the anchor and the wrong shape as a fixed taxonomy. The specific market attributes are a declared `gap`. Strains after re-census: none hidden; one live instrument question is named in F8 (what would measure ground pressure independently of the outcomes it is supposed to explain).
