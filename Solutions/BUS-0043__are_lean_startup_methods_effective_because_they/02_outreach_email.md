# Outreach draft - check the shape before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §3 reads correctly to you. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Honest standing:** the output is a `specified` shape from a simulated derivation, not a measurement and not a ground reading. Say that in the first paragraph.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about lean startup effectiveness, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output about lean startup methods I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at. It is `specified`, not `grounded`: it is a shape, not a measurement.
>
> **The question as an effect.** Are lean startup methods effective because they produce faster learning, or because they suit certain markets? The output says the two options are a union compound, not a choice between two causes. Effectiveness needs (1) a build-measure-learn loop whose measurement re-enters as the next bet and can refuse a held hypothesis, and (2) a market ground that pays, exerts pressure, and supplies at least one term the team did not already hold. Learning speed is necessary as the contact mechanism and cannot be the witness, because a loop's own output (experiments run, hypotheses minted) is frame-internal. The market is the anchor that settles effectiveness.
>
> **What is explicitly not claimed:** not that lean is universally effective, not that a fixed list of market types decides it once and for all, and not that any particular sector is the answer. The framework returns a shape, not a name. I am not claiming the empirical literature has settled which market attributes matter; I record that as an open gap.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. Faster internal iteration improves a ground-visible outcome while a habitat substitution removes ground pressure and the outcome is unchanged.
> 2. Two markets with equal measured loop speed show systematically different lean-performance slopes attributable to a separately measured ground pressure, and the difference survives a second instrument.
> 3. One lever restores both conditions and leaves no categorically different residue.
> 4. A loop's own output count is admitted as the ground metric for "effective."
> 5. A lean loop closes without its output re-entering as input, or a team refuses a held hypothesis without any operator that can refuse.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will narrow or withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula, public repository: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical figures README: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Local verbatim figure text with line numbers: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - [your name]
