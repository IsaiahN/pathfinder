# The method as an arrangement: cycles, refusals, isomers and order

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The main audit decides that effectiveness is a two-condition compound. A second question remains: what is the internal structure of the method that is supposed to maintain contact, and what order do its parts have? If "lean startup" is only a bag of practices, the union claim has no mechanism. If it is a bonded arrangement, the arrangement can be checked, and two methods with the same practices but different bonds can be compared.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Is build-measure-learn a cycle or a chain?

- Fig 12 :122: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**
- If the measurement re-enters as the next bet, the method is a cycle and can close. If each measurement is filed and the team moves to the next feature, it is a chain with one bond type and cannot close by construction.
- Fig 5 :5: *"fires on every action"*; Fig 5 :6-7: *"only when something scores"*. A cycle without a scoring event is motion, not learning.
- **Reading:** the effectiveness claim depends on the bond, not on the count of practices. A team that runs many experiments in a chain has a longer chain, not a loop.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** show a build-measure-learn sequence that closes without the output re-entering as input. If it closes, the cycle claim fails and "faster learning" as mere iteration count is rehabilitated.

### Q2 - Is a pivot a refusal or an addition?

- Operators table :57-58: **"is the point that it is false, rather than absent? Yes."** and **"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"**.
- Formula step 2: **"REFUTED - a term held is false; what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation"**; and the fifth kind cannot be reached by rearranging the other four.
- A pivot that drops a held hypothesis is a negation. A pivot that adds another feature while keeping the false hypothesis is accumulation. The framework can name state with vocabulary alone and cannot name a route to one (Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**).
- **Reading:** "learning" in the lean sense requires ¬. A method vocabulary with only ingredients can report what a venture contains and cannot report that a hypothesis is wrong.
- **Falsifier:** a documented pivot that refuses no held term and still moves the ground-visible outcome in the predicted direction. That would break the necessity of negation here.

### Q3 - Are lean and non-lean (stage-gate, planning) isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Over the shared atom set {hypothesis, MVP or prototype, customer feedback, decision, resource budget} the two methods read as:
  - **lean:** `hypothesis → MVP → feedback → (cycle back) → decision ¬(held hypothesis) → ...` (sequence + cycle + negation);
  - **stage-gate:** `hypothesis → prototype → review ⋛ threshold → decision → next gate` (sequence + comparison).
- Both use the same atoms with different bonds. By Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**, not variants of one method with different intensity. This is why "do more lean" cannot rescue a venture whose bond structure is the problem, exactly as Fig 3 :19 says optimising a working link cannot rescue a broken one.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either method requires an atom the other does not (for example a regulatory milestone that has no counterpart in the lean atom set), the isomer claim fails and the two are different sets rather than different arrangements. The weaker functional-group claim then stands.
- World check, secondary only: Harms, Marinakis and Walsh (2015) place lean, stage-gate and business planning in a 2x2 of market and technology uncertainty and note that high fixed costs per iteration make lean less desirable. That corroborates the structural distinction at the business level, but it is a world source and cannot decide the figure result.

### Q4 - What order do the parts have?

- Fig 3 :29-30: **"Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."**
- Dependency order: perception (can the team read the market?) → vocabulary (does it have terms for what a result affords?) → objective (what counts as progress?) → planning (a route under incomplete information) → learn and carry.
- Fig 12 :112: **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."** So a method name is a state label; a route needs the ordered bonds. "Faster" is a subtraction on time; "effective" is a comparison against a ground; "pivot" is a negation. These are three different operators and one cannot be substituted for another.
- Fig 12 :101: **"A bond that costs nothing is a bond that carries nothing, which is the flat case."** A practice list with no recorded bonds is the flat case.
- **Reading:** the diagnosis precedes the prescription. A team stopped at perception cannot be helped by more planning; a team stopped at vocabulary cannot be helped by a faster cycle. This is the operational content of "suitable market": some grounds keep the lower links alive, others break them.

### Q5 - Two currencies, not one

- Fig 12 :125-127: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."** Bits price the description; actions price finding out whether it holds. **"Neither figure predicts the other, so both are read or the choice is made blind."**
- A lean method is cheap to state (bits) and may be expensive to settle (actions, a real customer, a real payment). A stage-gate method may be expensive to state and cheap to settle where the gate is a legal requirement. Summing them into one "efficiency" score is the error Fig 12 :130 names.
- **Reading:** "faster learning" is a claim about one currency (time per action) being turned into a verdict about effectiveness (settlement by the ground). That is a category error unless the bond between them is shown.

### Q6 - Round trip R_T on the condensation

- **Sent up (x):** F1 to F5 of `01`, chiefly Fig 1 :27 (frame-internal metrics), Fig 11 :40 (agent-and-habitat), Fig 6 :32 (idempotent closure), Fig 12 :122 (cycle), Fig 9 :57 (union compound).
- **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand "lean works when the market suits it, and faster learning helps."
- **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete venture intervention, for example "run two-week cycles in B2C software."
- **R_T is nonzero.** Three constraints do not survive the trip: the negation requirement (a pivot must refuse), the union requirement (contact and reach both), and the shape-not-a-name limit (no sector label is a figure return). One thing survives cleanly: the loop must re-enter and the market must settle it. Fig 4 :30: **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."** R_T is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated dropped constraints; it is not measured numerically because no ground reading exists.

## CONCLUSION

The method is a bonded arrangement, not a practice list. Its load-bearing bonds are the cycle (output re-enters), negation (a pivot refuses), and comparison ("effective" is against a ground). Lean and non-lean methods over the same atoms are isomers, with a stated falsifier. Dependency order makes "suitable market" an operational claim about which link the ground keeps alive, not a sector label. The two currencies, bits and actions, are held apart. The round-trip gap is nonzero and enumerated.
