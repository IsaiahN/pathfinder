# Isomers, functional groups and cascade order in protected areas

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claim, `general` on the definition of the bonds and the dependency order.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

Two protected areas can have the same species list, the same area and the same legal category, yet one conserves and the other displaces. What is owed is a derivation of (1) what differs structurally between them, (2) the order in which the difference becomes visible, and (3) an analogue that makes the difference nameable without importing a new ecological primitive.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

- **R.** Arity two (inside persistence, outside displacement). Symmetry: not additive; a gain in one slot does not cancel a loss in another. Scale: first order at the boundary, second and third order in the cascade. Fig 9 :56 governs the union; Fig 1 :19 governs the per-slot indexing.

- **D2 management frame.** Actors: managers, boundary, legal category. Currency: area, compliance. Anchor: borrowed from D1; area is frame-internal (Fig 1 :27).

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are two same-list reserves isomers under Fig 12?

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :95: "Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."

A species list is the ingredient set. The relations among species and pressures are the bonds. Two reserves with the same list but one with an intact pollination or predation relation and the other with that relation severed are **isomers** in the figure's exact sense: same atoms, different bond, different substance. Their legal notation lists the same ingredients, so "nothing signals that anything was lost" (Fig 12 :16). This is the structural reason a species list cannot be the verdict witness. **Triangulated** for the definition (Fig 12 plus the "arrangement is the substance" claim at Fig 12 :94); the ecological binding is `specified` and unmeasured.

### Q2 - When are they only a functional group instead?

Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Two reserves can share the same contact pattern (a boundary that excludes a pressure while leaving the key relation intact) but hold different species. They are then members of the same functional group, not isomers: the group fixes the kind of outcome, the qualifier picks the member. This is the weaker, safer claim, and it is the one to send when the species lists differ. **Falsifier:** if two reserves sharing the contact pattern systematically diverge in outcome with no change in atoms or bonds, the functional-group claim fails and an unenumerated third-order condition is owed.

### Q3 - What is the order in which displacement appears?

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Fig 11 :52: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."

1. **First order.** The boundary acts on direct contact. The pressure is excluded from the inside; its first-order reading is a drop in extraction events at the site.
2. **Second order.** The pressure enters the adjacent habitat, which the reserve's goal did not enumerate. This is F4's inherited actors: "you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose" (Fig 11 :67).
3. **Third order.** The effect returns to the reserve's residual through a relation: a prey base, a pollinator, a disease vector, a market price. It is "invisible until it acts" (Fig 11 :69).

Reading first order alone is not a weak reading of the cascade. It is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22). Optimising the boundary (a link that already works) cannot rescue the missing enumeration (a link that does not). **Single-source** for the cascade law (Fig 11); the DDT instance is named in the figure itself, not borrowed from an external ecology text.

### Q4 - Does a successful reserve become an atom for the next level?

Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world." A reserve whose outcome is settled against the ground can serve as an atom in a network-level composition. That is the recursion, not an import, and it needs no new primitive.

Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond." A network of reserves described only by area can name a state and cannot name the route by which one reserve's pressure reached another. The four ordered bonds (sequence, production, subtraction, comparison) are what a displacement claim needs; a percentage of area covered supplies none of them. **Triangulated** (Fig 12 composition rules plus Fig 1 per-slot rule); the network binding is `specified`.

## CONCLUSION

Two reserves with identical species lists are **isomers** when their relations differ; with identical contact patterns but different species they are **functional group** members. Displacement is the third-order term of a dependency chain that starts at the boundary; reading only the first order measures a link that works while the binding link is missing. A settled reserve becomes an atom at the network level by Fig 12 :104, and needs no new primitive. The structural claim is `specified`; none of it is settled by the ground here.

## FALSIFIER (written before the conclusion, per Seat Map :287)

The structural claim is refuted if either:

1. Two reserves with the same atoms and the same typed relations return systematically different ground outcomes with no unenumerated condition found after the cascade is fully traced (breaks Fig 12 :117, the arrangement determines the substance).
2. Displacement appears at first order with no second or third-order relation, so the dependency chain is not ordered as Fig 3 :20 and Fig 11 :52 state.

If either fires, the isomer/order derivation is withdrawn and `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is updated with the retraction.
