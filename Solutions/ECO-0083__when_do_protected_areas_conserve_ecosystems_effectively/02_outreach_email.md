**Subject:** One checkable claim about protected areas and displacement

> Hello,
>
> I have one specific output about protected areas that I would like you to check against work you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms:** a protected area draws a boundary and expects the ecosystem inside to persist. Two outcomes are possible. Either the actors and conditions retained inside keep meeting the ecological ground, or the pressures that were excluded simply reappear outside the boundary or three steps away in the cascade, so the net effect is relocation rather than reduction. The question I am testing is whether the designation changed contact with the ground, or only changed where the pressures sit.
>
> **The shape claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** isolation is not removal of a habitat, it is substitution of one habitat for another. A protected area inherits the actors of the new habitat and must reproduce everything its goal required. The things it failed to reproduce are invisible until the goal fails; the things it unintentionally introduced are invisible until they act. So displacement is the default failure mode when contact is unchanged, and effectiveness has to be measured per site against a counterfactual, not as a global total of area protected. Area designated is a quantity the managing frame produces and then scores itself with, so it cannot be the witness for the verdict.
>
> **What would refute this shape claim.** I wrote these down before sending:
>
> 1. A controlled per-site measurement in which designation changes the ecological outcome with no measurable change in the contact structure of the main pressure actors.
> 2. A well-measured case in which the pressure field is genuinely removed rather than relocated, with the habitat enumerated outward until the cascade stops.
> 3. An actor that has the same valence for every residual in the system, so a standing list of good or bad boundary effects survives a change of residual.
> 4. An enumerated cascade, in a system where displacement was expected, showing none at second or third order. Lack of detection does not count here.
>
> If any holds, I retract the affected part. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula, canonical text with line numbers: local file `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in the Pathfinder project, cited as Fig N :line.
> - Figures README, canonical usage rule: local file `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`.
> - The habitat figure itself (SVG): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a protected-area case you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and a planning model proves wiring, not capability. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thank you for reading this far.
