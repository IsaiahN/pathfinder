## 0. The residual in effect terms

A governing body draws a boundary, calls the inside protected, and expects the ecosystem there to persist. Two outcomes are possible at the ground. In the first, the retained actors and conditions keep meeting whatever the residual is (a viable population, an intact interaction, a functioning process), and the measured trajectory improves relative to a comparable unprotected site. In the second, the actors that were excluded from the inside do not vanish: they reappear just outside the boundary, or three steps out in the cascade, so the measured net effect is a relocation of pressure rather than a reduction of it.

What is being decided is therefore not "does a protected area work" but: **does the designation change the contact between the ecosystem and the pressures acting on it, or does it only change where those pressures sit?** The two options lead to different actions. Option A (coverage is the win) stops once area is designated. Option B (changed contact is the win) requires per-slot measurement and an enumerated cascade before anyone may call the outcome conservation.

The shape claim this audit tests: a protected area is an isolated habitat, and isolation substitutes one habitat for another rather than removing the pressure field, so displacement is the default failure mode unless contact is actively managed and measured. Refuters are registered in section 3, before the conclusion.

## 1. Domain census (done before choosing any figure line)

### R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** two conditions. Conservation inside the boundary and displacement outside it are both in play, so this is a **union compound** with its own loop and its own ground, not one hard question (Fig 9 :56: "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds.").
- **Symmetry:** not symmetric, and not additive. An increase inside and a loss outside are different object slots, not offsets. The framework forbids aggregating them (Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated.").
- **Scale:** first order at the boundary (direct contact between the protected habitat and the pressure), second and third order in the cascade (displaced effort, prey, pathogens, people). This is the scale at which leakage appears (Fig 11 :52: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out.").

### One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | nameable anchor? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 ecological frame (the habitat under protection) | populations, species, pressures (extraction, fishing, logging, agriculture, poaching), conditions (water, fire, climate), relations (predation, pollination, competition) | abundance, persistence, ecosystem function | measured ecological outcomes against a counterfactual site | yes, the ground for this question |
| D2 management and policy frame | PA managers, regulators, funders, local communities | area designated, management effort, compliance, budget | none from inside; borrows D1's ground | no internal anchor, so not an independent verdict domain |
| D3 verification frame | monitoring scientists, meta-analysts, outside readers | agreement and residuals | the ground, never agreement | yes, but in this simulated run the evidence pool is shared, so independence is zero |

D2 fails the anchor test on its own: area designated is a quantity the frame produces and then scores itself with, which the figures disqualify (Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."). It is still a real domain, but its verdict is borrowed from D1's ground, so it is never averaged with D1.

### Partition or union

D1 and D2 form a **union**, not a partition. Effectiveness requires ecological persistence AND governance/management; a single lever (more area) clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing (Fig 9 :56). Displacement is exactly that intersection: a boundary where protection and an external pressure field overlap. D3 is a frame over both, not a separate ground.

### Dependency order (Fig 3)

1. Enumerate the habitat before acting (Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed.").
2. Describe the residual before choosing a comparator frame (Fig 9 :27: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit").
3. Settle each per-slot residual before aggregating (Fig 1 :19).
4. Assess the cascade only after first-order contact is measured; a reading of a link below the break is not a weak reading but a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :27).

### Strain test applied up front

The obvious apparent strain is: "isolation conserves" versus "isolation substitutes habitat, so pressures are merely moved." Re-census resolves it before any conclusion: Fig 11 governs the habitat in contact with a residual, while legal designation is a convention authored in D2. They are different objects. A protected area can be a maintained seat acting on the habitat (Fig 11 :58) while isolation substitutes the habitat for the residual (Fig 11 :66). No figure has to be bent. Strains that survive the full census are listed in section 6 and in `11_audit.md`.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The question is a union compound, not one hard question (`general`)

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." The seat map states the same law: "And when a split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before searching again." (Seat Map :211).

Conservation inside and displacement outside are two conditions that bind at the boundary. A single lever (designate more area) clears the region where only one holds. Asking "are PAs effective?" as one question guarantees that the displacement condition is answered by whichever metric the asker happened to pick. **Triangulated** (Fig 9 plus Seat Map :211), low overlap between the figure's union logic and the seat map's unbundling rule. Mode `general`.

### F2 - Effectiveness must be measured per slot, never as a global average (`general`)

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 1 :23: "The ground enters as the only metric."

A protected area's net "hectares protected" or "population trend across all reserves" is an aggregate. It can be near zero or positive while one site's live residual is still positive. That is a legal state in the figures, not an inert one, and averaging is precisely how it is hidden. **Triangulated** (Fig 1 plus Fig 2 anchor rule). Mode `general`.

### F3 - Coverage is a frame-internal quantity and cannot settle the verdict (`specified`)

Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." The same figure lists coverage among the disqualified quantities (Fig 1 :25).

Area designated is produced by D2 and then used by D2 as its own success metric. This is the named frame-internal failure, not a matter of opinion about targets. Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." So a 30 by 30 target is a budget filter for effort; it is not a witness for conservation. **Triangulated** (Fig 1 plus Fig 9 plus Seat Map :281 on mode). Mode `specified`.

### F4 - Isolation substitutes a habitat; it does not remove the pressure field (`specified`, core mechanism)

Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Fig 11 :67: "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."

A protected area moves a residual into a substituted habitat. It must reproduce what the conservation goal required (water, prey, pollinators, connectivity, enforcement) and it inherits the actors of the new habitat, which it did not choose. Two failure modes follow and both are silent: Fig 11 :68: "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails", and Fig 11 :69: "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts". Displacement of pressure is the second of these seen from the outside. **Triangulated** (Fig 11 plus Seat Map :255, which states the same isolation law in the checking arrangement). Mode `specified`.

### F5 - The seat's power is asymmetric, so exclusion is not available (`general`)

Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you." Fig 11 :63: "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."

Managers can add protection, instruments, staff and species. They cannot subtract contact. This is why a reserve is permanent work: the pressure field does not consult the boundary. It also explains why "set aside and protected" is a different quantity from "conserved", a re-census point, not a strain. **Triangulated** (Fig 11 plus Seat Map :225). Mode `general`.

### F6 - Leakage is a second or third-order contact effect, not a first-order leak (`specified`)

Fig 11 :52: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." Fig 11 :44: "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."

If the habitat is scoped only to the boundary, the pressure that was displaced one valley away or one market away is outside the enumeration, and its effect on the reserve's residual arrives two or three steps later. The world literature corroborates this at low overlap with the framework: Nowakowski et al. (2023, Nature) write that "optimizing the growing PA network requires targeted protection of sensitive clades and mitigation of threats beyond PA boundaries"; Mora and Sale (2011, MEPS) list "the overall failure of such areas to protect against the broad range of threats affecting ecosystems". **Single-source** for the figure mechanism (Fig 11 only); world sources corroborate, but all are in the same conservation evidence pool. Mode `specified`.

### F7 - Valence is relative to the residual, so spillover and leakage are not contradictory (`general`)

Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :50: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."

This dissolves an apparent conflict in the world sources. The marine literature reports beneficial "spillover" of biomass out of no-take reserves; the same literature reports that "no-take MPAs may not benefit biodiversity because they displace fishing effort outside the MPA" (Wikipedia, Marine protected area, quoting the fisheries-management debate). These are not two facts about different mechanisms. They are one contact (export across the boundary) read against two different residuals (adjacent fishery yield versus recipient reef integrity). No standing list of good or bad boundary effects survives a change of residual. **Triangulated** (Fig 11 plus Seat Map :217). Mode `general`.

### F8 - Changing the label without changing contact changes nothing (`general`)

Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 11 :39, in part: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."

A designation that leaves the pressure field in the same contact with the same ecosystem has not changed capability, however much area it renames. What would count is a changed contact: fewer extraction events reaching the residual, a restored relation, a bounded cascade. **Triangulated** (Fig 11 plus Seat Map :257). Mode `general`.

### F9 - A synthetic or planning-only result proves wiring, never capability (`specified`)

Fig 11 :70: "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."

Reserve-selection algorithms and map-based gap analyses are substituted habitats. They prove that the planner's model is wired, not that the contact in the real habitat changed. This is the same substitution as F4 at one level up: the planning environment is another habitat the residual has been moved into. **Single-source** (Fig 11; Seat Map :255 is the same law but not an independent witness). Mode `specified`.

### F10 - Agreement among PA assessments is not corroboration (`general`)

Fig 7 :13: "agreement compounds, evidence does not". Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

A review citing ten meta-analyses that all use the same before-after datasets is one long room. Outside readers must range the claim against the ground separately, and their verdict on the shape must be recorded before they see the framework's interiors (Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."). **Triangulated** (Fig 7 plus Fig 2, different collapse mechanisms). Mode `general`.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach: "And a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." (Seat Map :287).

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A controlled, per-slot measurement shows that PA designation changes the ecological outcome with **no** measurable change in the contact structure of the main pressure actors (breaks Fig 11 :40).
2. A well-measured case shows the pressure field is genuinely removed rather than relocated when a PA is created, with the habitat enumerated outward until the cascade stops (breaks Fig 11 :66 and :67).
3. An actor is shown to have the same valence for every residual in the system, so a standing list of good or bad boundary effects survives a change of residual (breaks Fig 11 :49 and :50).
4. In a system where displacement was expected, a fully enumerated cascade shows it is absent at second and third order, with the measurement instrument already returning something (breaks Fig 11 :52; absence of detection alone does not count, see Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence.").

If any fires, the affected finding is withdrawn or narrowed and the retraction is recorded in section 7 and in `11_audit.md`. Until one is run, no finding may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the residual, the shape claim and the four refuters. Send a generator, not a recording (Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down.").
2. Treat area targets as budget filters, never verdict witnesses (Fig 9 :60).
3. Require per-slot reporting with an explicit counterfactual site, and enumerate the habitat outward until the cascade stops before calling an outcome conserved (Fig 1 :19; Fig 11 :44).
4. Expect displacement as the default failure mode when contact is unchanged; measure for it rather than assuming it away (Fig 11 :66, :69).
5. Ask outside readers what they would count as a failure before showing any of this framework, and never pool later agreement as corroboration (Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source.").
6. Use the public institutional venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. No personal emails, no bulk send, no receptiveness rank.

## 8. Round trip note (expanded in `11_audit.md`)

The condensation used across this folder is: "PAs conserve when contact changes and the retained habitat meets the ground on a per-slot residual; they displace when isolation only substitutes habitat and the cascade is unenumerated". The full figure text carries constraints this shorthand drops: the union condition (F1), the frame-internal ban on coverage (F3), the valence rule (F7) and the instrument condition (section 7). The round trip gap `R_T` is therefore nonzero and qualitative; `11_audit.md` enumerates what does not survive the trip back down. Treating the shorthand as identical to the figures would set `R_T` to zero, which is the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
