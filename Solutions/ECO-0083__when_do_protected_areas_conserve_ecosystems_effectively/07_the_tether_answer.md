# The Tether's answer: when do protected areas conserve, and when do they displace?

**Mode: `specified` on the mechanism, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not an ecological result. It is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A protected area draws a boundary and expects an ecosystem to persist. The question splits into two conditions that bind at that boundary: persistence inside and pressure outside. Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." It is a union compound with its own loop and its own ground, not one hard question. Coverage is the first lever everyone reaches for; area designated is a quantity the managing frame produces and then scores itself with (Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."). So coverage is a filter for effort, never a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).

## 2. The answer, one loop per domain

**D1, ecological frame. Verdict: `specified`, triangulated.** A protected area conserves effectively when the designation changes the contact between the ecosystem and its pressures and the substituted habitat still reproduces what the goal required. It displaces when the boundary is only redrawn: the excluded actors reappear in the habitat the movement created, the goal's requirements are not reproduced, and the effect arrives at second or third order. Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Fig 11 :67: "Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose." Two modes of failure are silent by construction: Fig 11 :68: "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"; Fig 11 :69: "what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts". The cascade must be enumerated first: Fig 11 :52: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." Triangulated across Fig 11 (contact) and Fig 1 (per-slot residual), two low-overlap routes.

**D2, management and policy frame. Verdict: `specified`, single-source, anchor borrowed from D1.** This frame has no internal anchor. Its currency is area, compliance and budget. It can introduce protection, instruments and staff, but Fig 11 :61 says: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you." Fig 11 :63: "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state." So "designated and protected" and "conserved" are different quantities; designation is a convention, conservation is a claim about the ground.

**D3, verification frame. Verdict: `general`, single-source, zero independence in this simulated run.** Agreement among assessments is not corroboration. Fig 7 :13: "agreement compounds, evidence does not". Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Many studies using the same before-after datasets are one long room. Their verdict must be recorded before they see this framework (Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it.").

**Valence caveat, applied across domains.** Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Beneficial spillover of fish out of a no-take reserve and harmful displacement of fishing effort are one boundary contact read against two different residuals. There is no standing list of good or bad boundary effects. This is `general` and triangulated with the seat map's valence rule.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that protected areas fail. Changed contact can meet the ground; the answer is conditional, not a verdict against protection.
- Not that any named reserve, species or pressure is the answer. The framework returns a shape; the binding comes from the ecology frame and is recorded as such.
- Not that the framework's "habitat" is the ecological niche. The canonical article declines that mapping: a niche is an organism's relation to its environment, while the framework's habitat is contact with a residual. Legal boundary and contact field overlap; they are not identical (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).
- Not that a planning model or gap analysis demonstrates conservation. Fig 11 :70: "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."
- Not `grounded`. No per-slot counterfactual measurement was taken here.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating measurement, not further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source". Here:

- **Per-slot counterfactual.** Measured change at each site against a comparable unprotected site, with the residual indexed per slot (Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated.").
- **Enumerated cascade.** List everything in contact with the residual, then what contacts those, outward until the cascade stops mattering (Fig 11 :44: "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."). Displaced effort is a third-order term in this list, not an afterthought.
- **Changed contact test.** A designation that leaves the pressure field in the same contact with the same ecosystem has not changed capability (Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves.").
- **Standing falsifiers from `01` section 3.** No contact change with an outcome change; genuine removal rather than relocation; a pressure with identical valence for all residuals; an absent cascade in a fully enumerated system. Note that failure to detect is not proof of absence: Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The measure set is a visible choice, not nature's own list. *They're Scanning Books When They Should*: "A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six." The answer stops where instruments can no longer distinguish in-place recovery from relocated pressure, and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. The canonical README's "Indiscernibility is not identity" applies directly: a net positive trend indistinguishable from genuine in-place recovery is still not evidence that the source was in-place recovery.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say protected areas work or fail. It says the question is two conditions bound at the boundary, so the unit of analysis is a per-site residual, not area designated. Isolation substitutes one habitat for another instead of removing the pressure field: managers can add protection but cannot subtract contact, so a reserve is permanent work, and what the goal failed to reproduce stays invisible until it fails while what it unintentionally introduced stays invisible until it acts. Displacement is therefore the default when contact is unchanged, and it arrives at second or third order through the cascade, which is why a direct-contact reading is a reading of nothing. The same boundary export is beneficial spillover for a fishery residual and harmful displacement for a recipient reef residual, because valence belongs to the residual, not to the actor. What would settle this is a per-site counterfactual instrument with the cascade enumerated, not more agreement among assessments that share the same data. This is a `specified` shape with refuters written in advance, not a ground result.
