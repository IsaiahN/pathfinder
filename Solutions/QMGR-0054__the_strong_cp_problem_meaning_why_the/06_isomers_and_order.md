## RESIDUAL

The strong CP residual compares two CP-violating quantities: the QCD vacuum angle theta_bar and the weak CKM phase. Three questions are owed. (1) Is the residual one problem or a union of conditions? ## DERIVATION

### Q1: Union or partition?

- Fig 9 :57 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."* Fig 9 :58 *"It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific"* compound.
- The question "why no neutron EDM" bundles the QCD theta condition with the weak CKM condition. They have different anchors (EDM bound versus K/B decays) and different closures (strong versus electroweak).
- ⇒ **Union, not partition.** Unbundle before searching (Fig 9 :54). Trying to answer "why is CP conserved" as one hard question is the error: a single lever that clears one condition leaves the intersection standing.
- **Falsifier:** if a single measurement or single parameter simultaneously determined both CP phases, the union reading would fail and a partition would be correct.

### Q2: Are the two CP phases isomers?

- Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :124 *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- As the record stands, the two phases have different atoms: theta_bar is a vacuum angle in a topological gauge term; the CKM phase is a mixing-matrix phase in Yukawa couplings. They share only the property "CP-violating phase." By Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**. They are two members of a **functional group**: kind = CP violation, qualifier = strong versus weak.
- The stronger version would require writing both over a full shared atom set (gauge fields, quark fields, Yukawa matrices, CP violation), differing only in which structure carries the phase. That is a real isomer claim, but it is a `specified` hypothesis, not a figure derivation, and it needs an instrument to discriminate the two arrangements.
- **Why this matters to the residual:** the magnitudes differ by roughly ten orders, and that difference is the whole puzzle. If they were isomers (same atoms, different arrangement), the difference would be structural and derivable. Because they are a functional group with different atoms, the difference is a fact about which frame supplies the phase, which is why an import is owed rather than a derivation.
- **Falsifier:** if both phases can be written over the same atom set with the same bond and only a relabelling differs, the functional-group reading fails and the isomer claim stands. That would move the residual back into the closure.

### Q3: What order is the dependency chain?

- Fig 3 :20-21 *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*
- Order derived from the residual, not from preference:
  1. **Perception / ground.** Read the EDM bound. Without it there is no residual.
  2. **Vocabulary.** State both CP phases in effect terms (a bounded strong angle versus an order-one weak phase).
  3. **Objective.** Say what would count as progress: a mechanism that removes the ten-order gap while preserving the weak CP violation.
  4. **Refuse before mint.** A global CP symmetry is already refused by D3 (Fig 5 amendment: a term held as false owes a refusal, not a further explanation).
  5. **Plan / search.** SEARCHED in QCD's closure first; then OUTWARD for an import described in effect terms.
- Fig 12 :104 *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"* licenses treating the settled EDM bound and the settled K/B observations as atoms for the next step. Both were settled against the ground before this derivation began.
- **Falsifier:** if a mechanism is named before steps 1 to 4 are complete, Fig 9 :27-28 *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"* predicts the wrong mechanism will look right. That is a procedure failure, testable by asking the reader to state the residual before the frame.

### Q4: Analogue: an interval around zero is not exact zero

- Low-overlap field (statistics and reproducible science, essentially zero overlap with particle physics): Pawel, Heyard, Micheloud and Held, eLife 2024, *"Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence - the title of the 1995 paper by Douglas Altman and Martin Bland has since become a mantra in the statistical and medical literature."* (Rendered with a hyphen for the source dash.) The paper distinguishes *"testing for an exactly zero effect versus testing for an effect within an interval around zero."*
- The neutron EDM result is an interval `0.0 +/- 1.1 +/- 0.2` in units of `10^-26 e cm`, not a point mass at zero. It is evidence that the effect is negligible within a stated sensitivity, and silent below it.
- Fig 8 :39 *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The analogue corroborates; it does not decide. The verdict comes from the figure.
- **Falsifier:** a future analysis that reports the EDM as an exact zero with zero uncertainty would break both the figure line and the analogue, and the shape claim would have to be re-censused.

## CONCLUSION

The residual is a union compound of the strong and weak CP conditions, not one hard question (Q1). The two CP phases are a functional group, not isomers as written, and that is why their ten-order difference is owed to an import rather than derivable from a shared atom set (Q2). The dependency order is ground, vocabulary, objective, refusal, then search; refusing the global-CP answer before minting a mechanism is load-bearing (Q3). The null EDM is an interval around zero, corroborated by a low-overlap statistical analogue (Q4).

These four results rescue what is defensible in the strong CP question without naming a solution: the shape of any answer is fixed, while the slot stays open until a ground settles it. "The axion is the answer" remains an unproven binding with an incomplete source gate.
