# Outreach draft: check the output first, then decide about the mechanism

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note with a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. No request for agreement, no request for trust.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter survive the reader's own check. The two-stage offer collapses on contact if stage 1 does not hold.

---

**Subject:** One checkable output on the strong CP residual, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output fails, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** QCD permits a CP-violating vacuum angle theta_bar that would give the neutron an electric dipole moment of order `5.2 x 10^-16 e cm` times theta_bar. The measured value is `d_n = (0.0 +/- 1.1_stat +/- 0.2_sys) x 10^-26 e cm`, a bound that implies `theta_bar < 10^-10`. The angle is free in [0, 2 pi], so the theory does not fix the small value. Meanwhile the weak CKM phase gives order-one CP violation in K and B decays, but only about `10^-32 e cm` to the neutron EDM. So the residual is not "CP is conserved"; it is "why is the strong CP angle roughly ten orders below the weak one."
>
> What I derive from the figure set is a **shape**, not a mechanism. Any answer must (1) relax theta_bar_eff dynamically rather than set it to zero by hand, (2) coexist with the large weak CP phase, and (3) be falsifiable by a ground reading, which here means a nonzero EDM at the next sensitivity or an axion signal with the predicted relaxation. The framework explicitly returns *"a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (Fig 9 :32), so I am not asserting that the axion, a massless up quark, Nelson-Barr models, or anthropic selection is the answer. Those are names, not the shape.
>
> **What would refute this shape claim:**
> 1. a derivation inside the Standard Model closure that fixes theta_bar to the observed small value with no new field or high-scale condition;
> 2. a measurement of a neutron EDM near `10^-16 e cm` with theta_bar of order one, which would mean the residual itself was mis-stated;
> 3. a demonstration that the CKM phase can be rotated away while keeping the observed K and B CP violation, which would make "CP is conserved" a live answer again.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, I retract the shape claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
