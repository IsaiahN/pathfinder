**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` have been checked against the verbatim figure text. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** Check one shape claim about extinction cascades before you decide whether the theory is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output about extinction cascades that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the mechanism, not `grounded`):** a species loss can trigger secondary extinctions, and the scale is not a property of the species alone. In the framework I am using, the rule is to enumerate what is in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, outward until the cascade stops mattering. Contact is typed: pollination, predation, competition, dependency. A classic case in the figure text is DDT reaching eagles, where the effect was second order and the residual that mattered was three steps out. So first-order scoping undercounts. The framework also says an actor's role is relative to the residual being tracked, not a property the actor carries, which means no standing list of "most important species" transfers from one question to another.
>
> **What determines scale, in this reading:** the length of the live contact paths, the bond type at each step, and which slots are still carrying positive residual. Not an aggregate richness or connectance number. Those are useful filters for deciding where to spend effort, but they are not the witness.
>
> **What the framework does not give:** a number, a species name, or a threshold value. It returns a kind of thing, never the answer itself. I am not claiming it can tell you that cascade X will kill exactly Y species, or that a particular species is always the keystone.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. A removal experiment with measured live typed dependents that produces no secondary loss at any resolved contact distance, repeatedly.
> 2. An aggregate richness, connectance or R50 metric that predicts out-of-sample secondary losses as well as per-slot contact enumeration, across communities, with the slots and paths left unmeasured.
> 3. Two communities with the same species set and the same interaction strengths, whose interaction types are rearranged, producing indistinguishable cascade distributions over many runs.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the corresponding part of the claim is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`. See Fig 11 :43, :44, :49, :50, :52 to :53 and Fig 1 :19, :22.
> - The coextinction review I used as a ground-facing cross-check: Dunn, Harris, Colwell, Koh and Sodhi, 2009, "The sixth mass coextinction", Proceedings B: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2817118
> - Cascading failures in complex networks, for a low-overlap frame: Valdez et al., 2020, arXiv:2007.11075: https://arxiv.org/html/2007.11075
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the enumeration rule is in the figure text link. Run it on a community you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [your name]
