# Isomers, order and structure in an extinction cascade

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

Two communities can hold the same species and lose the same species first, yet differ in how many secondary extinctions follow. The one-species-one-loss vocabulary cannot express why. Three questions are owed: (1) can the same species set yield different cascades, (2) which compositional structures change the outcome, and (3) does the order of loss matter? These are structure and order questions, not new-primitive questions.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Can the same species set yield different cascades?

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 11 :47 gives the bond candidates: "relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency."

So a species list is a formula, not a community. Two webs with identical species but different typing are isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different cascade behaviour. The species list "cannot tell them apart" (Fig 12 :15-16) and nothing signals the loss. This is the structural reason why connectance or richness alone cannot settle scale.

Figure 12's bond table is a guide to *what a bond does*, not a licence to assert that ecology literally uses the glyphs. The mapping is a binding, provenanced here:

- `→` sequence, "A then B; B needs A to have happened" (Fig 12 :72): a dependency where loss of A precedes failure of B.
- `⇒` production, "A produces B; B did not exist before" (Fig 12 :77): a resource or habitat provision by an engineer species.
- `+` conjunction, "both hold at once" (Fig 12 :57): a mutualism requiring both partners present.
- `−` subtraction, "A with B removed or absent" (Fig 12 :82): the primary loss itself.
- `⋛` comparison, "a threshold relation between two quantities" (Fig 12 :87): an extinction threshold, where a partner's abundance crosses a viability line. The operand type at the ecological level needs an instrument; named, not hidden.

Binding a measured ecological relation onto one of these bonds is an import with a source gate (the field measurement) and a shadow test (it explains a cascade gap). It is not a new primitive.

### Q2 — First order, second order, and the settled-molecule rule

- **First order:** primary loss to direct dependent. This is one typed crossing, the ecological counterpart of Fig 13 :74 "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one" read at the composed level.
- **Second order:** dependent of a dependent. Fig 11 :52-53 is the governing line: "first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out." The DDT case is an instance, not a derivation.
- **Why higher order is legal:** Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world." A first-order loss set, once measured, can serve as an operand for the next step. No new primitive is needed.

This is the structural core of the answer to "what determines the scale": the number of settled crossings the cascade traverses before the residual stops arriving.

### Q3 — Four structures that change the outcome

Each is a figure-defined structure applied at the ecological level, with its mode.

1. **Isomer** (Fig 12 :117): same species, different typed bond, different cascade. `specified`.
2. **Catalyst** (Fig 12 :119): "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is." A generalist pollinator or an ecosystem engineer survives its own use, so it is not consumed by the service it provides. This is why generalists buffer a loss while specialists do not. `general` structural, `specified` per species.
3. **Cycle** (Fig 12 :121): "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." A feedback loop can damp or amplify. Which sign holds is a ground question; the figures only say a chain cannot represent it. `general` on the representational limit, `specified` on sign.
4. **Functional group** (Fig 12 :123): "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." Redundancy is a functional-group property. Two species in the same functional group are substitutes for a given residual, so one loss need not propagate. This is the figure-side counterpart of the world-source result that trophic redundancy lowers secondary-extinction probability (Sanders et al. 2018). `specified`.

### Q4 — Analogues, and where they strain

- **Mutualistic networks (nestedness).** A core of generalists linked to specialists maps onto functional group plus catalyst. Generalist loss is decisive; specialist loss is often buffered. Kehoe et al. (2021) corroborate from ecology. Low strain.
- **Food webs (modularity).** Modules map onto a partition of the contact graph; a disturbance stays inside a module. Kehoe et al. (2021) report this from theory. Low strain, but note that the bond typing inside a module still matters (Q1).
- **Power grids (islanding).** Valdez et al. (2020) describe islanding, separating a grid into independent parts, as a way to stop propagation. **Strain S5:** islanding looks like exclusion, but Fig 11 :61 says "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." The resolution is Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Islanding substitutes a habitat; it does not delete contact. Recorded as a strain resolved by re-census, not smoothed over.
- **Finance (modularity).** Haldane and May (2011) argue that modular configurations prevent contagion. Same shape, different evidence pool; corroborates only the shape (Fig 2 :40).

### Q5 — Does order of loss matter?

Fig 12 :72 defines sequence as "A then B; B needs A to have happened." For composed molecules order can be load bearing, and ecological question 21 in `Questions/ecology.txt` asks exactly this. But Fig 13 :76-77 warns that a gradient over space and one over time are different terms because the state bonded to is part of the term. So the order dependence is a `specified` hypothesis at the composed level, not a `general` law at the level of kinds. What would settle it is a sequenced removal protocol with interaction strengths measured between steps. `specified`, ungrounded.

## FALSIFIER, WRITTEN BEFORE THE CONCLUSION

The structure-and-order claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. Two communities with the same species set and the same measured interaction strengths, but with interaction *types* rearranged, produce statistically indistinguishable cascade distributions over many runs (kills Q1 and the isomer claim).
2. A "catalyst" actor is demonstrated to be spent by its own use in the relevant residual, so that its survival cannot be attributed to Fig 12 :119 (kills Q3.2).
3. A sequenced removal protocol shows no order dependence where the typed dependency graph predicts one, with strengths measured between steps (kills Q5).
4. An intervention is shown to remove a contact relation outright rather than substitute a habitat, contradicting Fig 11 :61 and :66 (kills the S5 resolution).

If any fires, the affected sub-claim is withdrawn and the remaining ones are re-censused.

## CONCLUSION

The same species set can yield different cascades because the arrangement is the substance (Fig 12 :94), not the ingredient list. First-order loss sets become operands for second-order propagation through the settled-molecule rule, and the DDT line shows why first-order scoping is not enough. Isomer, catalyst, cycle and functional group are four figure-defined structures that change the outcome without a new primitive. Order may matter at the composed level but is `specified` and ungrounded. The numerical magnitude remains a `gap`; this derivation returns structure, not size.
