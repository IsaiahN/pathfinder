# Expert verification packet — the Tether on cascading extinctions

**What this is:** a shape claim in a form a coextinction researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the domain mechanisms, not `grounded`.** No cascade magnitude is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status: simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.** It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A species loss can trigger secondary extinctions through typed contact, but not always: a slot must carry live residual. The scale is not an aggregate count. It is the contact structure enumerated outward from the loss (direct contacts, then contacts of those, until the residual stops mattering), reported per species and per relation. Roles are relative to the residual being tracked, so no standing list of "most important species" transfers between questions. The same species set under a different arrangement of interaction types is a different substance, not a variant. The framework returns that shape, not a cascade number and not a species name. Three pre-registered refuters are below.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — contact enumeration and the stop condition; checkable by reading Fig 11

- **Statement (`general`):** secondary loss propagates through typed contact; first-order scoping undercounts because the decisive residual can be several steps out; scale is the path length at which propagation stops.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :44 "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." Fig 11 :52-53 "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."
- **What to check:** does the observed secondary-loss set in a community you know follow typed contact paths outward, and does direct-contact scoping miss the later steps? Is the stop condition measurable with your instrument?
- **Refuter:** a removal experiment with measured live typed dependents that produces no secondary loss at any resolved contact distance, repeatedly. Also: a case where every secondary loss is a direct dependent despite resolved, live indirect contacts.

### C2 — residual-relative role and bond dependence; checkable by reading Fig 11 and Fig 12

- **Statement (`general` on relativity, `specified` on bond dependence):** an actor's valence is relative to the residual, not an intrinsic property; the same species set with different typed bonds yields a different cascade; redundancy is a functional-group property.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :123 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- **What to check:** does a species ranked as a buffer for one residual act as a transmitter for another? Does changing interaction type at fixed species set and fixed strengths change the cascade? Is redundancy explained by functional group rather than by raw species count?
- **Refuter:** a standing species list that predicts cascade scale across changes of tracked residual as well as a residual-relative enumeration; or two communities with the same species set and strengths but rearranged interaction types producing indistinguishable cascade distributions over many runs.

### C3 — per-slot reporting, not aggregation; checkable by reading Fig 1 and Fig 3

- **Statement (`general`):** cascade scale must be reported per species slot and per path; a pooled R50, richness or connectance score is a filter for budgeting effort, never a witness. The dependency chain is ordered, and the missing instrument is the objective link, what counts as scale.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
- **What to check:** does an aggregate predictor lose the live slot that actually drove the cascade? Is there a published instrument that defines cascade scale consistently across habitats, with error bars?
- **Refuter:** an aggregate metric that predicts out-of-sample secondary losses as well as per-slot enumeration, reproducibly across communities, with slots and paths unmeasured.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a cascade magnitude, threshold, or extinction count.
- Not a named keystone species, and not a trait list of "important" species.
- Not that the Tether habitat is the ecological niche. That mapping is declined in *Arrival of the Fittest*, section "Three mappings offered and declined" (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 5b).
- Not that the DDT case was derived by the framework. It is an *instance* the framework describes, arrived at independently.
- Not that agreement among network models settles anything. Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."
- Not `grounded`. No measurement here settles a magnitude.

## 5. What would settle it

A path-discriminating instrument, not more modelling. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a color reading "cannot comment on the source"; it was "proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." Here the tests are:

1. **Path test:** remove a species with measured live typed dependents; resolve losses at first, second and third order. Does the residual stop where the enumeration says it stops?
2. **Isomer test:** hold species set and interaction strengths fixed, rearrange interaction types, compare cascade distributions.
3. **Aggregation test:** per-slot enumeration versus a pooled predictor, out of sample, across communities.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :43-44 and :49-53, Fig 12 :117 and :123, Fig 1 :19 and :22. Eight short passages, all in the local extract.
2. Check C1 against one removal study you know: were the decisive losses first order or further out?
3. Check C2: does "keystone" behave like a residual-relative role in your systems, or like a standing property?
4. Name one instrument that would resolve typed indirect contacts at second and third order, or say why none exists.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
