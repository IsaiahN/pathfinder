## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about two linked claims: (A) a single species loss can trigger cascading extinctions; (B) the scale of such a cascade is determined by something statable. Option one: report a cascade number and a ranked list of important species. Option two: report the typed contact structure, the per-slot propagation path and the stop condition, and refuse the number. What each leads to: option one sends an aggregate score as a witness even though the framework says the residual is indexed per slot, and it names a role as an intrinsic property even though the framework says valence is relative; option two sends a checkable shape, pre-registered refuters, and an open magnitude until a ground settles it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** A primary extinction occurs; a one-step prediction says only direct partners should be lost; the observation shows additional losses, sometimes three steps out, sometimes none. Arity: two conditions (possibility and scale), so a **union compound** (Fig 9 :55 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"). Symmetry: contact is directed and typed, so order and bond type are load bearing. Scale: first order is the direct dependent set; second and higher order are indirect effects, enumerated until the residual stops mattering.
- **Domain 1, the ecological field of contact.** Actors: species, conditions (light, water, season, terrain), relations (Fig 11 :47 "relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"). Currency: persistence and demographic loss, interaction strength. Anchor: a measured removal or extinction record. Question type: habitat enumerated, never composed. Note: this field of contact is not the ecological niche; that mapping was declined in *Arrival of the Fittest*.
- **Domain 2, the network-topology frame.** Actors: nodes, links, modules, generalists and specialists. Currency: robustness, R50, connectance, secondary-extinction count. Anchor: empirical network datasets plus removal experiments. Question type: overlaps Domain 1 on the same data, so not an independent witness.
- **Domain 3, network physics and infrastructure.** Actors: nodes, connectivity links, dependency links, load, capacity. Currency: load redistribution, threshold, cascade size. Anchor: blackout records and percolation thresholds. Question type: different closure, low overlap with Domains 1 and 2.
- **Domain 4, verification and outreach.** Actors: expert readers, pre-registered refuter, ground record. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all domains draw on one evidence pool, so this domain's independence is zero.
- **Order by dependency.** Settle the typed relation before the sequence (Fig 12 :72); settle the first-order loss set before the second-order comparison (Fig 12 :104); describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).

Strain test applied: Fig 13 :74 governs the six-term substrate; interaction typing is a bond question at a composed level (Fig 12). Different objects, so no figure contradiction after re-census. One further strain, islanding versus exclusion, is resolved by habitat substitution (Fig 11 :61 and :66) and recorded in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

Yes, conditionally: a one-species loss can trigger cascading extinctions where the contact slot carries live residual. Scale is set by the typed contact structure enumerated outward from the loss, by the bond type at each step, by which roles are live relative to the residual, and by where propagation stops. It is not set by an aggregate richness or connectance number, and no standing keystone list transfers between residuals. Same set plus different arrangement is a different substance. The framework returns the shape and the refuters, not a cascade number and not a species name. Mode: `general` for the shape, `specified` for the mechanisms, ungrounded candidate throughout. The numerical magnitude is a `gap`, reported plainly rather than filled.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, three checkable sub-claims and refuters before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a pooled ranking as if it were a witness; Fig 2 :42 says "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The aggregate framing is removable without touching the enumeration rule; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove it. No primitive has to be withdrawn, and a sent claim can be superseded by the corrected packet.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A removal experiment resolving typed indirect contacts that shows the residual stops where the enumeration says it stops would move C1 toward settled; a path-discriminating instrument that names the source of a cascade count would do the same for the metamerism limit. A standing species list that predicts across residuals, an aggregate metric that beats per-slot enumeration out of sample, or two same-set-different-bond communities with indistinguishable cascades would reverse the specific verdicts. A demonstration that indirect contact is unobservable at any resolution would move the question from unreached to underivable and change the instruction from INWARD to import or stop. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
