# Outreach draft: check the distinction before deciding about the method

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until F1 and F8 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are reflected in the text. The distinction must survive a check before the method is offered.

---

**Subject:** Would you check a distinction between two kinds of AI performance limit?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one distinction I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the distinction does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** an AI performance shortfall can be produced by at least three different conditions, and a single average curve does not tell you which one is active.
>
> 1. *Outside the closure.* The needed capability cannot be composed from what the system holds, however long you search or however much data, compute, and energy you add. The wall moves only by importing something from a frame whose closure differs.
> 2. *Inside the closure, unfunded or unfound.* The capability is already reachable in principle but was not found or not settled at the available budget. The remedy is more search, better allocation, or a sharper instrument, not a new kind of system.
> 3. *Same reach, unfound.* More of the same space needs visiting. Nothing new is owed and no new atom is imported.
>
> There is also a fourth case in which the limit is in the contact between the system and its environment, so improving the system in isolation changes nothing.
>
> The two headline causes are different facts with different instructions, but from inside the frame they look the same. That is why I am sending you the distinction rather than a classification of any particular model.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** I am not claiming that any named current limit is known to be architectural, nor that scaling is known to solve the rest. I am not adding parameters, tokens, and FLOPs into one score. I am not claiming that a benchmark solve demonstrates capability. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> **What would refute the distinction:**
> 1. a single measure of model outputs that reliably assigns the active cause without any ground intervention;
> 2. more data, compute, and energy at strictly fixed closure adding a new kind rather than visiting more of the same space;
> 3. an agent-only improvement that moves the ground while contact and habitat are held fixed.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, I will withdraw the corresponding part. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Eight-step loop and mode rule (local copy): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** run the loop on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A frame cannot check the direction of its own question, so the pre-written refuter is the instrument, not the honesty disclaimer.
>
> — [your name]
