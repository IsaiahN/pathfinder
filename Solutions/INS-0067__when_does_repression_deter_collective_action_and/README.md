# Solution INS-0067 — figure-grounded package

**Question:** When does repression deter collective action, and when does it help unify opposition?

**Source:** institutional questions.txt line 82

**Section:** 7. Collective action, conflict, and political order

**Mode:** `specified` on the per-domain separator claims, `general` on the structural shape (union, per-slot residual, valence relative to residual), not `grounded`. No measurement settles the separator here, so nothing is `grounded`.

**Status:** done

**Registry status:** pending

**Standing of this folder.** Every substantive file below is a **simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures**, not a ground reading. The governing rule is from *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post" but "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself". This package cannot settle the question it reports on. It offers a shape, a refuter, and the instruments that would close it.

## Effect statement of the question

A state introduces a cost (repression) into a population and predicts a fall in collective action. The observed record contains three answers, not two: action falls, action rises and coalesces, or action changes form (nonviolent to violent, open to covert). The residual R is therefore per group and per time slice: for each slot s, the gap between what the repressor's frame predicted and what the opposition actually did after the intervention. The question asks for the separator that decides which answer obtains. What an answer would look like, written down and bound as an ordinary term before the run: a set of conditions such that when they hold together, the effect is deterrence, and when a different subset holds, the effect is unification. A single universal severity number is not that shape; it is a name the figures do not return.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the separator.
- No bulk-send plan and no receptiveness score used as evidence.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words. First, a room is not an anchor: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Second, selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that disqualifies their agreement: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* (Fig 2 :40). Third, showing a reader the interior before asking for a verdict spends the only independence they contribute: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42). Legally, unsolicited bulk email from scraped lists breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions; public opt-in venues avoid that failure as well as the structural one.

## Where the answer stops

The figures return a union of four conditions and a refusal of the universal number. They do not return a severity threshold, a probability, or a country-specific verdict. That is a declared `gap` of the shape-not-a-name kind, reported as unreachable rather than silence: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* (Fig 13 :134). The world literature agrees in the negative: the effect of repression on dissent is described as mixed, contradictory, and as a *"punishment puzzle"* by two independent reviews and a large experimental study. Agreement between those three sources is weaker than it looks, because they cite overlapping bodies of political-science work; the ledger records that overlap.
