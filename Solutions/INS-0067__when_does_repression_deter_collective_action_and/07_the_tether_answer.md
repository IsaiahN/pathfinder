# The Tether's answer: when repression deters and when it unifies

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain separator, `general` on the union shape, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7). Every figure quote below is verbatim from `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`.

## 1. The residual it answers

A state applies a cost and predicts a fall in collective action. The record returns suppression, unification, and tactical shift. The object to explain is therefore not a national rate. It is a gap indexed per group and per time slice (Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*). The question "when" asks for the conditions under which each effect obtains, not for a single average effect. The binary is a union of overlapping conditions (Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*).

## 2. The shape, in four conditions

The conditions are held terms in the figures. None is a new primitive.

1. **Channel to a non-updating ground.** Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*. Deterrence occurs where the channel decays and R stops arriving; from inside, a closed channel looks like a perfect prediction. Unification requires the opposite: a ground-visible event that every frame can read independently. Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
2. **Reachability of the opposition's composition.** A group that can still compose an action after the intervention has lost cost, not reach. A group that cannot has lost atoms. Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*
3. **Overlap between the frames touched.** Fig 8 :45: *"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."* Fig 8 :50: *"Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."* Where frames already share one evidence pool, their agreement is not corroboration; Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*.
4. **Legitimacy as an anchor test.** A state's declaration that its repression is legitimate updates on what the state needs it to say, so it fails Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."* Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*. The target's reading of legitimacy is a currency inside D2, not the ground; what matters is whether any reference point in the case is independent of both sides.

## 3. The answer itself

**Repression deters** where the following hold together: the channel to a non-updating ground is severed while the grievance persists; the opposition remains a single room whose agreement is variation rather than a verdict (Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict."*); the frames touched draw on the same evidence, so no low-overlap coalition can form; and the state's legitimacy claim is not corrected by anything outside itself. In that configuration the opposition's loop stops. Quiet follows, and the state reads the quiet as success. Formula, "Closure": the two states, perfect prediction and closed channel, look the same from inside. This is why silence must be treated as missing data until shown otherwise.

**Repression unifies** where the same event does three different things: it becomes a new ground-visible atom that no frame authored; low-overlap frames each range against it and hold the same residual by convergent derivation; and the coalition that forms is a chain of differing rooms selected by that residual (Fig 7 :36: *"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."*). Repression has not become a different kind of thing. Its bond has changed. Over a fixed habitat the two outcomes are isomers (Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*).

**The same actor has different valence for different residuals.** Fig 11 :50: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* So there is no global answer of the form "repression works" or "repression backfires". Verdicts are per group and per time slice and are never averaged; Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

**Short run and long run are different rooms.** Short-run fear and long-run micromobilization are ordered, not symmetric (Fig 12 sequence bond: "A then B; B needs A to have happened"), and the loop between them can close with either sign (Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*). This is why the literature's negative, positive, curvilinear and null findings are not mutually exclusive. Each rule works well on a different subset.

## 5. What it explicitly does not say

- **Not a severity threshold.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. The number is a declared `gap`, reported as unreachable rather than silence.
- **Not "repression always backfires".** The unification condition requires low overlap and a non-updating shared atom; without them the same event deters.
- **Not that anger, fear or identity is the mechanism.** Those are currencies inside the opposition frame. They update on framing, so they fail the anchor test. World sources predict them; the figures do not name them.
- **Not that silence means deterrence.** Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Successful preemptive repression leaves neither repression nor dissent observed in the record.
- **Not that a state can remove a grievance.** Fig 11 :61: *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* Isolation substitutes a habitat and inherits actors the state did not choose (Fig 11 :66).
- **Not grounded.** No measurement here settles `left(R,φ)`. No country verdict is returned.

## 6. What would settle it, the ground

The needed instrument is a discriminator, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened): an instrument reads green and "cannot comment on the source"; several paths create the same behavior, so "behavior is a projection with lossy information"; the case was resolved "not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument". Here the readings are visible protest, quiet, and tactical shift, and the possible sources include fear, latent deterrence, organizational substitution and shared outrage. The discriminator must:

1. measure per slot and per time slice, so the short-run and long-run rooms are not averaged;
2. fix an anchor that does not update on the state's reports or on the opposition's narrative, for example independently documented material conditions;
3. separate channel state from composition state, so a severed channel is not mistaken for a broken reach;
4. measure overlap between frames, so convergent derivation is not confused with one pool talking;
5. record whether a new ground-visible atom crossed, so the isomer claim can be tested for its fixed-atom condition;
6. handle censored observations, so absence of visible dissent is not coded as deterrence by default.

Standing falsifiers, written before the run and repeated in `08`: a union collapse (R1), a moved ground (R2), broken independence (R3), a derived universal threshold (R4), invariant valence across residuals (R5), and a cleared observability problem (R6). Any one fires the corresponding claim.

## 7. The honest stop

The answer stops at the limit of what the available instruments distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. The figures return a kind of thing, not the answer itself: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (Fig 9 :32). The missing thing is an instrument, not a primitive, and its shape is described above. The four essay-derived limits agree: a bundle must be split before it is searched (*The Library of Babel*), a reading cannot name its source without a discriminator (*The Hard Problem is About Source*), a plain description of effects is what can travel without contact (*Decoding Tacit Knowledge*), and a simulator cannot validate its own output (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*). Prestige and agreement are not such instruments: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."

## 8. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not return a number for how much repression deters. It returns a separator. Repression deters when it severs the channel to a ground that does not decay, leaves the opposition in one room whose agreement is only variation, touches frames that already share one evidence pool, and rests on a legitimacy claim that updates on what the state needs it to say. It unifies when the same event becomes a ground-visible atom that low-overlap frames can each range against, so their coalition is triangulation rather than negotiation. The same actor has different valence for different residuals, so the verdict is per group and per time slice and is never averaged into "repression works" or "repression backfires". Deterrence and unification are isomers over a fixed habitat, same atoms with a different bond, and short run precedes long run in an ordered loop whose sign decides whether control drifts or backlashes. The universal severity threshold is a declared gap. What would close it is a per-slot, time-resolved discriminator with an anchor neither side can move. What refutes it is written down in advance. The center stays open until the ground settles it.
