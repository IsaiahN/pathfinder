## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the claim that repression either deters collective action or unifies opposition. Option A: treat it as one dial with a severity threshold, where high repression suppresses and moderate repression mobilizes. Option B: treat it as a union of conditions indexed per group and per time slice, with the same actor taking valence from the residual. What each leads to: A imports a number the figures do not return, aggregates per-slot signals into a national rate, and reads silence as success; B returns a four-condition separator, keeps verdicts per domain, refuses the number as a declared gap, and names the instrument that would close it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Arity: three outcomes, suppression, unification and tactical shift, so a union (Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."). Symmetry: the dissent/repression correlation is symmetric, so a pooled association cannot choose the arrow (Fig 2 :39). Scale: micro fear and anger, meso organizations and sectors, macro regime type, plus short run versus long run.
- **Domain 1, the repressor's frame.** Actors: the state, its agents, its institutions. Currency: cost imposed and control maintained. Anchor: continued control only if it does not update on the state's own reports; otherwise it fails Fig 2 :33. Question type: a seat with an interest in the reading (Fig 10 :59). Its repression is an imposed refusal, not a ground-settled negation.
- **Domain 2, the opposition frames.** Actors: individuals, organizations, networks, sectors. Currency: perceived cost, fear, anger, efficacy, identity. Anchor: the lived grievance and its material conditions, which do not move when the state declares them settled. Question type: union of per-slot responses; valence relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49).
- **Domain 3, the habitat and channel.** Actors: media, infrastructure, intermediaries, platforms, audiences. Currency: contact and signal. Anchor: the same non-updating ground; the channel decays while the ground does not (Fig 10 :24). Question type: introduction available, exclusion not; isolation is substitution (Fig 11 :66).
- **Domain 4, the verification frame.** Actors: researchers, journals, reviewers. Currency: agreement and residuals. Anchor: measured outcomes, never agreement (Fig 2 :35). In this simulated run all seats draw on one evidence pool, so this domain's independence is zero and its agreement is one witness talking twice.
- **Order by dependency.** Establish the anchor and channel before the response (Fig 3 :27); describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); measure per slot before aggregating (Fig 1 :19); order short run before long run (Fig 7 :36).

Strain test applied: an initial reading put repression among the six substrate terms of Fig 13. Re-census moved it to the habitat (Fig 11 :45-48) because it is an actor or condition, not a state or structure of the minimum set. One live strain remains: the fixed-atom condition on the isomer claim in `06`, because habitats differ (Fig 11 :66). It is named, not hidden.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The binary is a union, not a dial. Repression deters where the channel to a non-updating ground decays, the opposition remains a single room, the frames it touches already share one evidence pool, and the state's legitimacy claim is not corrected from outside. It unifies where the same event becomes a ground-visible atom that low-overlap frames hold by convergent derivation, so the coalition is triangulation rather than negotiation. The same actor has valence relative to the residual, so verdicts are per group and per time slice and are never averaged. Over a fixed habitat the two outcomes are isomers; short run precedes long run in an ordered loop whose sign decides whether control drifts or backlashes. The universal severity number is a declared gap. Mode `specified` on the separator, `general` on the union shape, not `grounded`.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable in the argument, real in the world. The packet states its mode, four conditions and six refuters before its conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The practical cost is policy-relevant: reading silence as deterrence when the channel is closed, or reading a shared residual as mere noise when a coalition is forming. Fig 2 :42 says "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The cost is reduced by leading with the separator and the refuter, and by never pooling later agreement as corroboration.
- **undoable:** yes. The severity number is not asserted, so no primitive has to be withdrawn. A ground-settled discriminator can be added without touching the union shape; the six pre-registered refuters make each domain separately revisable. A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet. The one conditional piece, the isomer claim, is explicitly limited to a fixed habitat and can be replaced by composition plus import if a new atom crosses.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A per-slot, time-resolved discriminator with an anchor neither side can move would name which source produced a quiet; a within-slot case where the same event drives action one way with all four conditions fixed would collapse the union; a universal severity threshold derived from the figures or ground-settled across habitats would fire the gap; a case of invariant valence across residuals with different grounds would break Fig 11 :50; a demonstration that absence of visible dissent equals absence of latent motivation would clear the observability problem. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
