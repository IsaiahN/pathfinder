**Subject:** One checkable separator on repression and collective action, before any theory

> Hello [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at. A one-line failure is more useful to me than an endorsement.
>
> **The residual (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A state applies a cost, repression, and predicts a fall in collective action. The record returns three effects: action falls, action rises and coalesces into a broader opposition, or action changes form. So the object to be explained is not a single rate. It is a gap measured per group and per time slice: for each slot, what the repressor's frame predicted versus what the opposition did. The question is what separates the three effects.
>
> **The separator we derived, stated as an ordinary term before the run.** Four conditions recur, and they overlap rather than partition the cases:
> 1. Is the channel to a ground that does not update on the state's reports still reachable, or has it decayed while the grievance did not?
> 2. Can the opposition still compose an action at all, or has the intervention removed the atoms it needs?
> 3. Do the frames the repression touches draw on different evidence, so that the shared event can be a genuine landmark, or do they already share one evidence pool?
> 4. Does the state's legitimacy claim update on what the state needs it to say? If it does, it is not an anchor.
>
> **The verdict we recorded before writing any theory.** Repression deters where the channel is severed, the opposition stays one room, and no new ground-visible atom crosses in. It unifies where the same event becomes a non-updating shared residual for frames whose closures differ, so their agreement is triangulation rather than negotiation. The same actor has different valence for different residuals, so there is no national average and no universal severity number. That number is a declared gap, not an omission.
>
> **What we are explicitly not claiming:** not that repression always backfires; not that a severity threshold exists; not that anger or identity is *the* mechanism (those are currencies inside a frame, not a ground); not that silence means deterrence, because a successful preemptive repression leaves no visible dissent. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against a figure set. It cannot settle the question it reports on, and I am not claiming it does.
>
> **What would refute it (written before this run):**
> 1. a within-slot case where the same event drives action in one direction while all four conditions are held fixed;
> 2. a case where repression settles the grievance itself, measured by an anchor that does not update on the state's reports;
> 2b. two frames sharing one evidence pool whose agreement carries the same weight as two genuinely independent frames;
> 3. a universal severity threshold derived or ground-settled across habitats;
> 4. a design where absence of visible dissent is shown, independently, to mean absence of latent motivation and organization.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **Question for stage 1:** what would you count as a failure of this separator in the cases you know best? I will record your answer before showing you any theory.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figure text, not our summary of it, is the authority.
> - Canonical figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures README, canonical usage rule (local copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The repression and resistance literature we actually read: see `04_links_and_distribution.md` and the ledger in `10_source_ledger.csv`
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the formula is a method, not an answer. Run it on a problem you can check against a ground, write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Residual and refuter before theory | Fig 10 :52 — *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* The recipient checks the output without holding the framework's interior. |
| Verdict recorded before showing theory | Seat 4 must not hold the interiors; once the theory is shown, later agreement stops being evidence and is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40). |
| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35 — *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* |
| Do not ask for trust | *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself." |
| Pre-registered refuter | Seat Map :287, carried in the exemplar: "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." |
| Per-slot, not a national rate | Fig 1 :19 — *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 names the loss from averaging. |
| No severity number | Fig 9 :32 — *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*; the threshold is a `gap`, not a silence. |
| Interest declared | Fig 10 :59 — *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* The derivation's author cannot validate it; the recipient's independent ground must. |
| Links only after stage 1 | Fig 4 :22 — *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the architecture before a verdict sends a recording, not a generator. |
| Offer the formula as a method | Fig 4 :22 again. Giving away a generator does not give away an answer. |
| One-to-one, opt-in, no bulk | Fig 2 :40 and the accessibility rule: selecting recipients for likely agreement manufactures the disqualifying correlation. Legally, scraped bulk email also breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. |
