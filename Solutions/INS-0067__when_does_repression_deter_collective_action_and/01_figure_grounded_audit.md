## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

The question asks for a separator. A state applies a cost, repression, to a population and predicts a fall in collective action. The empirical record returns three different effects: action falls; action rises and coalesces into a broader opposition; or action changes form (nonviolent to violent, open to covert). So the residual is not "how much does repression reduce protest". It is: for each affected group s and each time slice t, what is the gap between the repressor's predicted effect and the observed response, and which conditions decide the sign and the form of that response?

- **Arity.** At least three outcomes (suppression, unification, tactical shift), so this is a **union** of conditions, not a two-way partition.
- **Symmetry.** The observed association between dissent and repression is symmetric: dissent predicts repression at least as strongly as repression predicts dissent (deMeritt 2016 calls this the "Law of Coercive Responsiveness" and the "punishment puzzle"). A pooled correlation cannot choose the arrow. Fig 2 :39: *"Correlation has valence."* Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*.
- **Scale.** Micro (individual fear, anger, perceived risk), meso (organizations, networks, sectors), macro (regime type, institutions), and short run versus long run. These are different slots, not one curve.
- **The ordinary term we bind before the run.** "A separator is a set of conditions such that, when they hold together, the effect is deterrence, and when a different subset holds, the effect is unification." Binding this makes the sought-for shape refusable (Formula, "Direction"). The unbound preference would be "repression is either a deterrent or a unifier", which no check could see.

## 3. Findings

### F1 — The either/or is a union compound, not a partition (decisive for the form of the answer)

Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**

Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

Fig 9 :5: **"Split it rather than search."**

World corroboration, low overlap with the figures: deMeritt (2016, Oxford Research Encyclopedia of Politics) writes that there is "some evidence to support 'almost every possible relationship between protest and repression'"; Honari (2018, Current Sociology) opens with "Recent reviews of research on state repression highlighted contradictory findings about this effect". These two are not independent witnesses in the full sense (they cite overlapping political-science literatures), so this is corroboration of the shape at reduced weight, not two votes.

**Verdict:** `triangulated` (Fig 9 plus two empirical reviews), `specified`. One lever cannot settle the binary because the binary is not one thing. Status of each domain is recorded separately below; they are never averaged.

**Refuter:** a within-slot, time-resolved case in which the same repression event changes collective action in one direction while C1 to C4 are held fixed. That would collapse the union to one mechanism and falsify this finding.

### F2 — Repression deters where it closes the channel to a non-updating ground

Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**

Fig 10 :26: **"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"**

Fig 10 :27-28: **"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"**

Formula, step "Closure": the loop stops when R stops arriving, "which is either a perfect prediction or a closed channel, and from inside those look the same." This is the exact mechanism of deterrence in effect terms: the opposition's loop stops not because the grievance was settled but because R stopped arriving. The repressor sees quiet and reads it as success; the two states are indistinguishable from inside.

World corroboration: Earl, Maher and Pan (2022, Science Advances) define digital repression as actions that "raise the target's costs for digital social movement activity" and note that "online information control can now also directly disrupt movement coordination and mobilization efforts". Their typology separates coercion from *channeling*, and information control from information coercion, which matches the difference between raising a cost and severing a channel.

**Verdict:** `triangulated` (Fig 10 plus an open-access systematic review), `specified`.

**Refuter:** a case where the channel is severed, the ground independently measured by an anchor that does not update on the state's reports, and collective action nevertheless continues to escalate in the same slot with no new atom imported. That breaks the channel condition.

**Conflict sent back to census (kept visible).** The same Earl et al. review reports that Mubarak's 2011 internet blackout was "slowly implemented and had the opposite effect on dissent". This appears to contradict F2. Re-census resolves it without flipping the verdict: the blackout attacked coordination (D3) but simultaneously introduced a new ground-visible atom into D2 (the blackout itself as a shared, non-updating event). Two conditions of the union were active, not one. This is precisely why Fig 9 :55 forbids a single-lever reading. No strain remains, but the conflict is recorded.

### F3 — Repression unifies where it is imported as a shared residual across low-overlap frames

Fig 8 :45: **"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."**

Fig 8 :50: **"Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."**

Fig 8 :53: **"Residual first, frame second."**

Fig 2 :31: **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."**

Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

The mechanism: a repression event is a ground-visible occurrence. It does not change its answer because any frame thinks it should (Fig 2 :29). It can therefore serve as the fixed landmark against which separate frames correct themselves. When groups that previously drew on different evidence (different sectors, regions, identities) each register the same event, they hold the same residual by convergent derivation, which is corroboration at maximum independence. The coalition that forms is a chain of differing rooms selected by the residual, not a negotiation among frames that already agreed: *"The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."* (Fig 7 :36).

World corroboration, genuinely low overlap with the political-science pool: Bartusevičius, van Leeuwen and Petersen (2023, Royal Society Open Science) analysed 101 nationally representative samples (N = 139,266) plus randomized experiments and report "a positive association between perceived levels of repression and intentions to engage in anti-government violence", with "Randomized experiments revealed that thoughts about repression also motivate participation in anti-government violence". Honari (2018) reports that "coalitions across different sectors of civil society can be formed to enhance the mobilization ability of the opposition". Wikipedia's *Reactance (psychology)* entry states that "Reactance can encourage an individual to adopt or strengthen a view or attitude which is indeed contrary to that which was intended" and that "When the freedom is completely eliminated, reactance becomes maximal". The last is a human-cognition analogue, not a political finding; it corroborates the direction of the effect, not the coalition claim.

**Verdict:** `triangulated` across Fig 8, Fig 2 and the experimental study, `specified`. The coalition-specific part rests on Honari plus Fig 7 :36 and is weaker than the direction finding; it is marked `specified`, not upgraded.

**Refuter:** a case where the repression event is ground-visible and non-updating, several low-overlap frames register it, and they nevertheless do not form a shared residual or a coalition. That breaks the triangulation condition.

### F4 — Valence is relative to the residual: the same actor deters one group and unifies another

Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**

Fig 11 :50: **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**

Fig 11 :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**

Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

This is the direct answer to "when". Repression is not a kind of thing with a fixed effect. It is a relation whose valence depends on which residual is tracked. For a group whose channel is intact and whose frames overlap heavily with the state's, the same event reads as a cost and deters. For a group whose channel has been severed and whose frames differ from the state's, the same event reads as a shared grievance and unifies. Any aggregate national rate mixes the two slots and throws away the only signal that says where to look.

World corroboration: deMeritt (2016) quotes Lichbach (1987, p. 266): "an increase in a government's repression of nonviolence will reduce the nonviolent activities of an opposition group but increase its violent activities"; Honari (2018) reports perceived repression can have a "direct negative effect" and "an indirect positive effect through 'micromobilization processes'"; Bartusevičius et al. (2023) find the positive association in 43 of 64 samples for intentions and 63 of 101 for self-reports, so even within one dataset the effect is per sample, not uniform.

**Verdict:** `triangulated` (Fig 11 plus three world sources spanning political science and political psychology), `specified`.

**Refuter:** a case where the same repression event has the same valence for two groups with different residuals and different anchors, measured per slot. That breaks Fig 11 :49-51.

### F5 — Legitimacy is an anchor test, and an outcome-fixing "anchor" is not an anchor

Fig 10 :47: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**

Fig 10 :59: **"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."**

Fig 2 :33: **"The anchor must not update."**

Fig 2 :34: **"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."**

When the state treats its own declaration of legitimacy as the ground, it has promoted a convention into a verdict. The declaration updates on what the state needs it to say, so it fails the anchor test. Fig 2 :34 names the resulting failure: the state and its own agencies drift together coherently, and because they agree, nobody notices. This is why a "successful" repression that produces no visible dissent is not evidence of deterrence (see F10).

World corroboration: Honari (2018), citing Kenney (2001), states that "if repression is framed as a legitimate act it can demobilize people, but if it is framed as illegitimate (unjust) behaviour of the state it can radicalize people"; *Reactance (psychology)* notes that "legitimacy may point to a set of behaviors threatened since there will be a general assumption that an illegitimate interference with a person's freedom is less likely to occur"; deMeritt (2016) notes that in democracies "citizens who see the state's use of coercion as illegitimate... can vote authorities out of office", which is a real non-updating anchor in that regime type.

**Verdict:** `triangulated` (Fig 10, Fig 2, three world sources at varying overlap), `specified`.

**Refuter:** a case where the state's legitimacy claim is shown to be independent of what the state needs it to be, and the opposition's response is unchanged. That breaks the interest-in-the-reading condition.

### F6 — Short run and long run are different rooms, not one inverted-U curve

Fig 7 :36: **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."**

Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict."**

Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**

A pooled estimate that averages across time slices is the Fig 1 :22 error in another costume. The short-run room (fear, immediate cost, demobilization) and the long-run room (micromobilization, diffusion, moral identity) must differ from each other and each must match the residual it surfaced. The two feedback signs are different cycles: a negative loop where quiet is read as success and invites more repression, and a positive backlash loop where repression manufactures the very residual it was meant to remove.

World corroboration: deMeritt (2016) reports Rasler (1996) finding "repression reduces dissent in the short term but that in the long term, it mobilizes individuals to mass demonstrations through micromobilization and spatial diffusion"; Francisco's "backlash hypothesis" where "extreme repression temporarily dampens protests but increases dissident activity over time"; and Sullivan, Loyle and Davenport (2012) finding repression increases dissent when dissent has recently decreased but subdues it when dissent has recently increased. Honari (2018) lists curvilinear accounts alongside linear and tactical-shift accounts.

**Verdict:** `triangulated` (Fig 7 plus a specialist encyclopedia and a review), `specified`.

**Refuter:** a time-resolved within-slot series in which the short-run and long-run effects have the same sign and the same mechanism with no intervening new atom. That breaks the different-rooms condition.

### F7 — Tactical shift is a bond change, and deterrence and unification are isomers only over a fixed atom set

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."**

Fig 12 :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**

Writing "repression reduces action" records an ingredient list, not the arrangement. Repression can be bonded to a cost (raising the price of acting), to a subtraction (removing an organizer), to a comparison (a threshold the group judges unfair), or to a production (creating a new shared identity). Same atom, different bond, different substance. This is why a meta-analysis of "repression" can find no stable effect: the formula it records hides the bond.

Over a **fixed** atom set, the deterrence and unification outcomes are isomers: same actors, channels, costs and grievances, different bond. The stronger claim fails if the unification case requires an atom the deterrence case lacks, for example a new intermediary introduced by the repression itself. Then the relation is composition plus import, not isomerism.

World corroboration: deMeritt (2016) quotes Lichbach's violent/nonviolent substitution and notes recent work on "diversification, decentralization and substitution of social movements"; Honari (2018) lists "alternative forms of expressing political discontent (i.e. tactical shift)"; Earl et al. (2022) distinguish coercion from channeling, which is a bond distinction.

**Verdict:** `single-source` for the figure definition of isomer (Fig 12), `triangulated` for the tactical-shift phenomenon with world sources. **Live strain:** the fixed-atom condition is not satisfied in the general case, because habitats differ (Fig 11 :66). Named, not hidden; see `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

**Refuter:** a case where the two outcomes share an atom set but the same bond, or where the unification case demonstrably imports a new atom and the isomer reading is therefore the wrong category.

### F8 — The repressor can introduce but cannot subtract the habitat

Fig 11 :61: **"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."**

Fig 11 :66: **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."**

Fig 11 :67: **"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."**

A state can add repression, a curfew, a platform ban. It cannot subtract the contact between a population and its grievance, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult the state. What it can do is substitute a habitat (exile, prison, a closed information environment), and then it inherits actors it did not choose and fails to reproduce contact it needed. Earl et al. (2022) document both halves: platforms "deplatformed activists", while the Egyptian blackout "had the opposite effect on dissent".

**Verdict:** `triangulated` (Fig 11 plus the digital-repression review), `specified`.

**Refuter:** a case where a state successfully removes the grievance itself from the habitat, measured by a non-updating anchor, with no substituted habitat and no inherited actors. That breaks the asymmetry claim.

### F9 — The universal severity threshold is a declared gap, not silence

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**

Fig 13 :134: **"So the system can report that it lacks something rather than producing an answer anyway. Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"**

Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

No figure line returns "repression above severity X deters" or "repression below severity Y unifies". The empirical literature also returns no portable coefficient: deMeritt calls the state of evidence a "punishment puzzle"; Honari calls the findings "contradictory findings about this effect". The honest report is a shape plus the instrument that would close it, not an invented number.

**Verdict:** `gap`, of the shape-not-a-name kind, not underivable in principle. An instrument is owed: per-slot, within-habitat, time-resolved measurement with a ground that does not update on the repressor's reports. Status `general` on the structural claim that no number is returned.

**Refuter:** derive a universal severity threshold from the figures, or exhibit a ground-settled coefficient that holds across habitats with the anchor specified. Either fires the gap.

### F10 — Successful deterrence can be unobservable, so silence is not evidence

Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

Fig 2 :44: **"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."**

World corroboration: deMeritt (2016) reports Ritter and Conrad (2016) observing that "if preemptive repression is completely successful, then dissent is never actually observed", and that in the fully deterred case "neither repression nor dissent is observed". This is the censored-observation problem: the cases that would falsify a deterrence theory are the cases the theory predicts you will not see.

**Verdict:** `triangulated` (Fig 8, Fig 2, political-science source), `specified`.

**Refuter:** a design in which absence of observed dissent is independently shown to correspond to absence of motivation and of latent organization, using an anchor independent of the state's reports. That would make silence evidence in that slot and narrow F10.

### F11 — Filters are for the budget, witnesses are for the verdict

Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

Fig 2 :42: **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."**

The cost of being wrong here is high (policy and human consequences), so independence should be bought rather than assumed. Two commits are cheap filters, not witnesses: whether to reply to a submission, and how much effort to spend checking it. The verdict must come from frames whose closures differ (Fig 6 :23) and whose evidence pools do not overlap (Fig 2 :31). In this simulated run the second seat shares the primary's pool, so its agreement is one witness talking twice; `11_audit.md` says so explicitly.

**Verdict:** `general` (structural, from the figures), `triangulated` across Fig 2, Fig 6, Fig 8, Fig 9.

**Refuter:** a demonstration that agreement between two frames sharing an evidence pool carries the same verdict weight as agreement between two frames that differ. That breaks the independence rule.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the standing instruction: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device.

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **R1, union collapse.** A within-slot, time-resolved case in which the same repression event drives collective action in one direction while C1 to C4 are held fixed.
2. **R2, ground moved.** A ground-visible case in which repression settles the grievance itself, not merely its expression, measured by an anchor that does not update on the state's reports.
3. **R3, independence broken.** Two frames that share an evidence pool and whose channels to the ground are severed nevertheless produce the same verdict with the same weight as two low-overlap frames.
4. **R4, threshold derived.** A universal severity threshold derived from the figures, or a ground-settled coefficient that holds across habitats.
5. **R5, valence invariant.** A case where the same actor has the same valence for two residuals with different grounds.
6. **R6, observability cleared.** A design showing that absence of observed dissent corresponds to absence of latent motivation and organization, with an anchor independent of the state.

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the separator is re-censused. Until one is run, no part of this folder is labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Send the separator, not the binary. Lead with the union of four conditions and the refuter; do not lead with "repression backfires" or "repression works".
2. Measure per slot and per time slice (Fig 1 :19, :22; Fig 7 :36). A national pooled rate is the wrong object.
3. Name the anchor before the effect (Fig 9 :27-28). Ask what did not move when the state and the opposition disagreed.
4. Treat silence as missing data until shown otherwise (F10).
5. State the mode on every claim. A separator is `specified`; the refusal to return a number is `general` and is a `gap`, not a failure.
6. Offer the figures only after a verdict on stage 1 is recorded (Fig 10 :52). Do not pool later agreement as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
7. Keep the world conflict in F2 visible. It is the demonstration that a single-lever reading fails.

## 8. Closing verdict of the audit

The figures do not answer "when" with a number. They answer with a separator and a refusal. Repression deters where it keeps the opposition in one mirror room and severs the channel to a ground that does not decay, while the state's own legitimacy claim is not promoted into an anchor. It unifies where the same event becomes a ground-visible, non-updating atom that low-overlap frames can each range against, so their agreement is triangulation rather than negotiation. The same actor has different valence for different residuals, so verdicts are per group and per time slice, never a national average. The universal severity threshold is a `gap`, recorded as unreachable rather than silence. Mode `specified` on the separator, `general` on the union shape, not `grounded`.
