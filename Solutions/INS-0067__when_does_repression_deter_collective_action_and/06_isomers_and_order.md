## RESIDUAL

The separator in `01` says the same repression actor has different valence for different residuals. Two structural questions remain before the answer in `07` is complete.

1. If deterrence and unification use the same ingredients, are they **isomers** under Fig 12's definition, that is, the same atoms under a different bond?
2. What **order** relates the short-run and long-run responses, and does the loop between them close? A flat "repression has an effect" cannot write a route, a goal, or a sign.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Are deterrence and unification isomers?

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**

Write the two outcomes over the same ingredient list, where `R` is the repression event, `C` the channel, `G` the grievance, `F` a frame, `k` a cost and `S` a shared object:

- Deterrence: `R - k` bonded to `F`, with `C` decaying and `G` unchanged. The event is coupled to a **cost**; the frame's composition becomes unreachable; R stops arriving. This is subtraction plus a broken channel (Fig 10 :24).
- Unification: `R ⇒ S` bonded to several `F_i`, where `S` is a new shared residual that no frame authored and every frame can range against. The event is coupled to a **production**, not a cost. This is the import of a ground-visible atom (Fig 6 :23) and convergent derivation at maximum independence (Fig 8 :50).

Same atoms in both lists, `R, C, G, F, k`, with `S` produced in the second by the bond rather than introduced as a primitive. Under the definition in Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance. This is why a formula that records only "repression" and "action" cannot tell them apart, exactly as the flat chemical formula could not tell silver fulminate from silver cyanate.

**Live strain, named.** The isomer claim requires a fixed atom set. Habitats differ (Fig 11 :66), and groups do not all hold the same intermediaries. If the unification case needs an atom absent from the deterrence case, the relation is composition plus import, not isomerism. The claim therefore holds **within a fixed habitat**, and is `specified` only under that condition. Falsifier below.

### Q2 — Functional group versus individual member

Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

Deterrence and unification share the substructure `grievance, frame, non-updating anchor`. That fixes the kind: both are cases of a frame ranging against a ground. The qualifier, which bond attaches the repression event, fixes the member: subtraction gives deterrence, production gives unification. This is the correct level for the world sources. Honari (2018) describes direct deterring and indirect radicalizing effects of the same perceived repression; that is one functional group with two members, not two independent mechanisms in different fields.

### Q3 — Order: short run then long run, and the arrow is not symmetric

Fig 12 :73 on the sequence bond: **"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."**

Operators table: sequence is defined as "A then B; B needs A to have happened". DeMeritt (2016), reporting Rasler (1996), finds repression reduces dissent in the short term and mobilizes mass demonstrations in the long term; Francisco's backlash hypothesis has extreme repression "temporarily dampen[ing] protests but increas[ing] dissident activity over time". Swapping the two outcomes is not a relabelling: a long-run coalition cannot produce the short-run fear it presupposes. The correct form is `short-run response → long-run response`, an ordered bond, not `+`.

This also explains the failure of an inverted-U reading. An inverted U is symmetric in severity and silent about order; the figure-set relation is ordered in time and sensitive to which room the residual surfaced in (Fig 7 :36). The upward half and the downward half are different rooms, not two halves of one curve.

### Q4 — The two cycles and their signs

Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**

Two loops are present in the world record and they have opposite signs.

1. **Quiet-reinforcement loop (positive with respect to repression).** Repression reduces visible dissent; the state reads quiet as confirmation that the strategy worked (a reading from the same evidence pool, Fig 2 :40); more repression follows. The output re-enters as input and the loop closes. It closes because the state's "anchor" is its own observed quiet, which updates on what the state did, so it fails Fig 2 :33 (*"The anchor must not update"*).
2. **Backlash loop (negative with respect to control).** Repression produces a shared residual; low-overlap frames converge on it (Fig 8 :50); mobilization increases; the state represses again; the shared residual strengthens. The output re-enters as input with the opposite sign. DeMeritt (2016) reports the backlash hypothesis and Sullivan et al. (2012) finding that repression increases dissent when dissent has recently been decreasing.

A chain cannot close by construction; a cycle can. This is why the long run cannot be read off a single cross-section: the sign of the loop is a property of the arrangement, not of the ingredients.

### Q5 — The repression event as catalyst

Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**

A repression event is usually spent: an arrest, a blackout, a beating, over and done with. In the unification case it becomes a catalyst: the event is gone but the shared object it produced survives and regenerates across retellings. Honari (2018) reports that "Movements can also utilize state repression and transform it into an opportunity" by reframing or publicizing it, creating "injustice frames" and delegitimizing the state. That is a spent part behaving as a catalyst. Nothing in a simple count of repression events records which a term is, which is one reason the macro literature is mixed.

### Q6 — Two currencies, and why a severity number mixes them

Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**

Fig 12 :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**

A proposed universal severity number tries to price two different things with one figure. The description cost is how compactly a separator can be stated: four conditions, per slot, is cheap to state. The action cost is what it takes to settle whether the separator holds in a country: independently anchored, per-slot, time-resolved data, which is expensive or unavailable in exactly the repressive contexts that matter (Honari 2018 notes "the data at the individual level are not easily available, particularly in contexts of severe repression"). Combining them into one number is the error Fig 12 names. The figures do not add, and neither should the verdict.

## FALSIFIER, WRITTEN BEFORE THE CONCLUSION

1. **Isomer falsifier.** If the unification case requires an atom the deterrence case does not hold (a new intermediary, a new actor introduced by the repression), then the two are not isomers over a fixed atom set. The correct relation is composition plus import (Fig 6 :23), and the isomer claim in Q1 is withdrawn in that habitat.
2. **Order falsifier.** If a time-resolved series shows the long-run coalition preceding the short-run fear with no shared residual, the sequence bond in Q3 is wrong and must be replaced by a symmetric or reversed relation.
3. **Cycle falsifier.** If the state's quiet-reinforcement loop is shown to use an anchor that does not update on the state's own observations, Q4's first loop is not a coherent drift and must be re-censused under Fig 2 :33-34.
4. **Catalyst falsifier.** If the shared object produced by repression disappears with the event and has no cascade beyond it, Q5's catalyst reading fails and the event is simply spent.

## CONCLUSION

Over a fixed habitat, deterrence and unification are **isomers** (Fig 12 :117): the same repression atom bonded to a cost in one case and to a production in the other. They are members of one **functional group** (Fig 12 :123), not two fields with two literatures. Their order is a sequence, short run then long run, with an ordered bond (Fig 12 :73), and each outcome re-enters as input to a cycle whose sign decides whether control drifts or backlashes (Fig 12 :121). The repression event can be spent or catalytic (Fig 12 :119). A severity number would price a description and a settling action in one currency, which Fig 12 :125-126 forbids. The one live strain is the fixed-atom condition on the isomer claim, carried into `07`, `09` and `11` rather than smoothed over.
