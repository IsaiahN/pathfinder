## RESIDUAL

The main audit leaves three structural questions open, all stated in effect terms:

1. What kind of structure is "essential function" when the reaction list changes? (isomer / functional group question)
2. What is the order of operations when a reaction fails: repair, mint, or refuse? (order question)
3. Does the answer depend on a specific chemistry, or is it a shape that any chemical carrier can fill? (analogue question)

## DERIVATION

### Q1: What kind of structure is the function that persists?

Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :124 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

A reaction list is like a flat formula: it records ingredients and not the arrangement that carries the behaviour. Two networks can share a molecular inventory and differ in which molecule catalyses which step; under a change, one closes and the other does not. That is an isomer relation in the figure's sense, not a metaphor. The function that persists is the **functional group** (the closing arrangement), and the individual reactions are the members that fill it.

Fig 12 :46 "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands." So knowing which molecules are present does not tell you whether the catalysis bond is a regeneration loop or a one-off consumption. The chemistry source makes the same point concretely: Piedrafita et al. 2010 report "multiple components have the same composition but different functions, depending on the arrangement of their elements."

**Result:** the object to preserve is the arrangement, not the ingredient list. Triangulated (Fig 12 :117 and :124 are different figure quantities, plus an independent chemistry observation).

### Q2: What closes a network, and what merely runs in it?

Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Fig 12 :119 "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."

A chain consumes its input and terminates; it cannot close. A cycle returns output as input and can regenerate the catalyst that drives it. The two figure lines govern different quantities: one is the topology of closure, the other is the persistence of the catalyst across uses. Both are needed and neither reduces to the other.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** a demonstrated primitive network that sustains itself indefinitely through open chains alone, with every catalyst supplied externally at the same rate it is lost and no output re-entering as input. If that holds, Q2 fails and the cycle requirement is withdrawn.

### Q3: What is the order when a reaction fails?

THE_FORMULA.md step 2 sorts a gap into held, new, mis-attached, mechanism-broken, and refuted. The order matters: "a refusing term pays the same bargain" and a refusal removes "a term's standing to be composed over". Fig 5 :55 "What it removes is not a term but a term's standing to be composed over. Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach."

Applied here:

- If the reaction still works but is attached to the wrong slot, the operation is **refit**, not invent.
- If the reaction is genuinely absent from the closure, the operation is **import** (Fig 6 :14 "the only operator that moves the wall"), not endless internal search (Fig 8 :32 "no search, however long, adds an atom").
- If a term the network holds is shown to be false (for example, a presumed catalyst is shown not to catalyse the step), the operation is **refuse** before mint. Formula step 2 owes a refusal first when the residual's content is that a held regularity is false.
- Settling is necessary but not sufficient for the repaired term to be composed over: Fig 5 :69 "AMENDMENT · settling is necessary for a term to be composed over, and it is not sufficient." A reaction can be confirmed and still not fit the loop.

**Falsifier:** a case where refusing a held catalytic claim leaves the network's reach unchanged, so that the refusal buys nothing and the order makes no difference. Then the refuse-first rule does not govern this domain. Single-source on the figures and the formula, one evidence pool.

### Q4: Record versus reach: why the loss happens at a boundary

Fig 12 :107 "Settling compresses the description and not what it cost to establish." The figure continues: "Where it is not, the result carries forward and the deriving does not, so every lineage pays again." Fig 6 :43 "So the closure and the reach are two quantities: one monotone, one not."

A library of reactions can grow while the reach of what can actually be composed falls. When a habitat substitution drops the structure that held the arrangement (a mineral surface that templated a step, a pore that retained a catalyst), the recorded product survives but the deriving does not. The next lineage must pay the cost again. This is the figure-grounded account of *why* conditions changing causes loss, and it is structural: no particular template is named.

Fig 12 :101 "A bond that costs nothing is a bond that carries nothing, which is the flat case." A network in which every molecule catalyses every step has free bonds, therefore carries no discriminating information, therefore cannot tell which arrangement closed. Flatness is the failure mode, not robustness.

### Q5: Two currencies, held apart

Fig 12 :125 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 12 :126 "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds." A compact repeating motif is cheap in bits and may be expensive in actions (each alternative path must actually run under each condition). A long chain of cheap parts can be expensive to describe and cheap to refute.

**Strain, named and not smoothed:** the figures price arrangements in bits (minimum description length). Chemical networks also have free energies and kinetic barriers, which are not bits. Mapping the figure's price onto thermodynamics is a binding, not a derivation. The solution reports the shape and keeps the two accounting systems apart. This is not a contradiction between figures; it is a limit of the analogy, recorded because the honesty rule requires it.

### Q6: The analogue question

Fig 12 :91 "A relation that fails all six is not a bond you are missing. It is a claim that has not been made yet." If a proposed primitive mechanism cannot be written as one of the figure's typed relations, the correct response is not to invent a new operator. It is to say that the mechanism has not been claimed yet in this vocabulary. Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."

So the analogues are layered, not substituted:

## CONCLUSION

The function that persists under change is an arrangement, not a list: a closing cycle with regenerated catalysts, with isomeric overlap over shared molecules, open to import when an atom is absent. The order of operations is refit, import, or refuse, with settle before compose and describe before look. The loss occurs at a boundary that drops the structure holding the arrangement, so the next lineage pays again. No chemical name is licensed by this derivation.
