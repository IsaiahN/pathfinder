## What these sources do and do not do

1. **None of them uses the Tether framework.** They are different authors, different methods (an ODE model, an evolutionary simulation, two reviews), and different evidence pools. Their agreement with the figure shape is therefore weaker evidence against circularity than the exemplar's framework-author essays, but it is still only corroboration. The figures decide.

3. **A shared pool is declared where it exists.** Piedrafita and Soyer and Pfeiffer both use computational models of network dynamics, so they are not fully independent of one another. They still differ in question and method, and their overlap is recorded as low to moderate, not zero.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 10 :52** "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and its four refuters; offer the links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who has absorbed the entire framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 2 :35** "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." A mass send selected for people likely to agree produces a room, not a refutation.
- **Fig 2 :40** "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration.
- **Fig 11 :70** "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability." Do not lead with a simulation result, including this one. This folder is a simulated derivation.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its refuter; (2) ask what the reader would count as a failure; (3) record the verdict; (4) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the email, are the authority.
