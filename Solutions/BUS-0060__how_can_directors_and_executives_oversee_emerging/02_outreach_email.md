# Outreach draft - check the separator before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the four failure conditions in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 are written into the version of the claim you are circulating. The offer collapses on contact if the refuter arrives after the conclusion.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about board risk oversight, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output about board oversight of emerging strategic risk that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** A board has to decide on AI, cyber, and geopolitical exposure with limited expertise and limited information. The failure is not a missing document. It is that the prediction the board holds about a risk does not match what happens on a particular slot, and the reporting channel has gone stale, pooled, or saturated while the underlying incidents and near misses kept happening. I am treating "limited expertise" as a closure bound and "limited information" as a channel bound, not as a request for more of the same report.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the conditions, not `grounded`).** An acceptable oversight arrangement must satisfy four conditions:
>
> 1. It keeps the residual indexed per named risk slot, so a live signal is not averaged into one enterprise risk posture.
> 2. It ranges against an anchor that does not move when management or the board pushes on it, and it treats a dead channel as loss of contact rather than as safety.
> 3. It makes the checking body unable to produce the risk claim it checks, so a second committee reading the same pack does not count as verification.
> 4. It imports a differing closure, such as independent technical expertise or an external assessor, only after the risk has been described in effect terms, with the source and its independence declared.
>
> The three risk classes are a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition. Verdicts are kept separate and never averaged. There is no claim here about which committee, cadence, or percentage of board time is right. That is a declared gap, not an omission.
>
> **What would refute it.** I wrote these down before sending this:
>
> 1. A risk readout preserves every live slot residual and is produced by a source that does not author the decisions it grades.
> 2. A risk rating is shown not to move under pressure from the frame it grades, and it changes the board's verdict when it changes.
> 3. A verifying body independently generated at least one reading it checks, from a source the maker did not supply.
> 4. A governance change improves a ground-measured risk outcome while changing no contact with the habitat and importing no differing closure.
> 5. AI, cyber, and geopolitical risk are a partition with no overlapping conditions, so one lever closes each region separately.
> 6. A low-risk board reading tracks an independently verified fall in incidents, near misses, and control failures at every material slot, with established instrument sensitivity and specificity.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw the corresponding condition. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Canonical figures and formula (SVG, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Worked narrative: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-intelligence-actually-works
> - Full architecture: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on an oversight problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so a flattering pre-commitment is a real failure mode here. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - [name]

## Stage sequence

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual, the four conditions, and the six refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict before showing any source.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, send the three links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because by then they hold the interiors (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a reusable method, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

No stage asks for trust, endorsement, or a show of hands.
