## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about board oversight of AI, cyber, and geopolitical risk under limited expertise and information. Option A: treat the problem as a shortage of expertise and data and add both. Option B: treat it as a checking-arrangement problem and return the shape an acceptable arrangement must have. What each leads to: A spends the budget on perception and vocabulary links that may already work, and leaves a single aggregated risk posture whose live slot residuals are hidden. B returns four refusable conditions, keeps verdicts per slot, and records the gap where no instrument discriminates. The three risk classes are a union with overlapping compounds, so one lever cannot close them all.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry, scale; one line per domain with actors, currency, anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The board's prediction about a risk fails against what happens on a particular slot while the reporting channel is stale, pooled, or saturated. Arity: several slots plus compound intersections. Symmetry: none, because escalation and acceptance are ordered threshold relations. Scale: first order at the firm, second and third order through the habitat. Fig 9 :54-58 names a union compound, not one hard question.
- **Domain 1, governance frame.** Actors: directors, executives, committees, management. Currency: decisions, oversight actions, reports. Anchor: incidents, near misses, losses, control failures, regulatory findings, which do not move when the board rates them. Question type: partition and dependency, where does the chain stop.
- **Domain 2, risk habitat.** Actors: AI systems and vendors, threat actors, states, regulators, employees, customers, competitors, infrastructure. Currency: contact and cascade. Anchor: the same ground, through a source-discriminating instrument. Question type: union, enumerated never composed.
- **Domain 3, advice and expertise frame.** Actors: independent technical advisers, auditors, red teams, regulators, peer boards, standards bodies. Currency: imports with provenance. Anchor: the ground plus independence from management's evidence pool. Question type: outward lookup, which closure predicts R's shape.
- **Domain 4, verification arrangement.** Actors: internal audit, risk function, audit committee, external auditor, regulator, whistleblower. Currency: verdicts, conventions, records. Anchor: the ground, kept reachable by a seat that cannot author it. Question type: soundness, who may see what.
- **Order by dependency.** Locate the break before choosing the remedy: perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry (Fig 3). Search before importing: SEARCHED, then INWARD, then OUTWARD (Formula step 7). Describe the residual before naming the frame (Fig 9 :27-28).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The four-condition shape survives; the board recipe does not exist in the figures and is a declared `gap`. Keep R per slot. Range against a non-updating ground and treat a dead channel as loss of contact. Make the checker unable to produce the claim it checks. Import differing closures only after describing the residual in effect terms, with provenance and a shadow test. Limited expertise is a closure bound moved by contact, not by training alone; limited information is usually a decayed channel, not absent ground. AI, cyber and geopolitical exposure are a union with compounds, so verdicts stay per slot and are never averaged. Mode `specified` on the conditions, `general` on the shape, not `grounded`, and candidate until an instrument pays.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, four conditions and seven refuters before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The larger cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a governance recording instead of a generator, which Fig 2 :42 prices as buying independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong. That cost is avoidable by leading with the residual and the refuter. A second cost is real: a board that adopts the shape without a discriminator may treat an unmeasured posture as settled, which is the mode-as-verdict failure the framework names.
- **undoable:** yes. The binding to a particular committee or cadence is removable without touching the four conditions; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove it. A published claim can be superseded by the corrected packet, and no figure term has to be withdrawn. A board that has restructured around the shape cannot undo a real incident, which is why the ground test comes before adoption.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A source-discriminating instrument at a named slot showing that a control changes an incident or near-miss outcome would move that binding toward `grounded`. A rating that moves under pressure from the frame it grades would break the anchor condition. A verifying body that can reconstruct the claim would break soundness. A partition result for the three risk classes would break the union verdict. A figure re-census that names a board structure would close the `gap`. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
