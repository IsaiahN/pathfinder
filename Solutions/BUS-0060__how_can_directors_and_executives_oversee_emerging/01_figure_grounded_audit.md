## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms before any figure is chosen

The question asks how directors and executives can oversee AI, cyber, and geopolitical risk when expertise and information are limited. Stated as an effect, not as a topic:

> A board (a bounded frame Γ holding directors' expertise and management-supplied reports) bets, acts, and observes. Its prediction Γ(b,a) about a risk fails against what actually happens: Γ(b,a) ≠ o'. The gap R arrives on particular object slots (one AI deployment, one vendor, one jurisdiction), and the board cannot close it because it lacks the atoms to compose a candidate term φ and because the channel carrying the observation is saturated, pooled, or stale. "Limited expertise" is a closure statement; "limited information" is a channel statement. Neither is a request for more of the same report.

What each option leads to:

- **Option A (the usual answer): add more expertise and more information to the board.** Train directors, add a cyber director, commission more reports, buy more data. This optimizes perception and vocabulary inside the existing closure. It works where the break is actually at perception; it fails where the board has no term for what the risk affords and no way to say what would count as progress, because effort above that break arrives at a step that cannot use it (Fig 3 :19, :25-27).
- **Option B (the figure-derived answer): treat oversight as a checking arrangement that keeps a non-updating ground reachable, keeps R indexed per slot, and imports differing closures with provenance.** This changes the board's contact with the habitat rather than only the board's interior. It returns a shape and a set of refuters, not a board recipe.

Bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term now, so it can accrue a residual like anything else (Formula "Direction"): **an answer is acceptable only if it (i) keeps the residual per slot, (ii) keeps a ground that does not update on the organization's own report, (iii) makes the checker unable to produce what it checks, and (iv) adds differing closures only with provenance and a shadow test.** Those four conditions are the prediction this audit tests, and they are refusable below.

## 1. Domain census (before any figure line is used)

R by arity, symmetry, and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency, and anchor. A domain with no nameable anchor is not a domain yet.

**R (the residual).** Arity: several slots, not one number. AI deployment, third-party access, a sanctions regime, and a data centre are different objects, and R is measured on each (Fig 1 :19). Symmetry: none. "Escalate" and "accept" are ordered relations to a threshold, not a symmetric join. Scale: first order at the firm, second and third order through the habitat (Fig 11 :52-53). The three named risks are a **union of overlapping conditions**, not a partition: a model vendor failure is simultaneously a cyber event and a geopolitical exposure, so a single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :54-58). Verdicts per risk class are kept separate and never averaged.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Governance frame | directors, executives, board committees, management | decisions, oversight actions, reports | the ground: actual incident, loss, near miss, regulatory finding | partition / dependency (which link stops the chain) |
| D2 Risk habitat | AI systems and their vendors, threat actors, states, regulators, employees, customers, competitors, data, infrastructure | contact and cascade | the ground, through an instrument that does not update on the board's rating | union (overlapping conditions), enumerated never composed |
| D3 Advice and expertise frame | external technical advisers, auditors, red teams, independent directors, regulators, industry peers, standards bodies | imports: atoms and methods with provenance | the ground, and independence from management's evidence pool | outward lookup (which closure predicts R's shape) |
| D4 Verification / assurance arrangement | internal audit, risk function, audit or risk committee, external auditor, regulator, whistleblower | verdicts, conventions, records | the ground, kept reachable by a seat that cannot author it | seat / soundness (who may see what) |

**Partition or union.** D1 through D4 are **not** four independent causes to be averaged. They are a dependency chain with a union at D2: D4 can only furnish a verdict if D1's objective link works, D2's habitat has been enumerated outward, and D3 supplies an atom that was not already in the board's closure. A break in any one of them stops the chain, and a reading taken below the break is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :20-23).

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** (1) Perception: what is in front of the board. (2) Vocabulary: terms for what the risk affords. (3) Objective: what would count as progress. (4) Planning: a route under incomplete information. (5) Learn and carry: name what worked, verify it, carry it. The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Most board effort lands at (1) and (4), where the measurements already exist, while the link at (2) and (3) has neither an obvious failure nor an obvious test (Fig 3 :25-27). That is where the chain usually breaks.

**Strain test applied to the census.** Fig 13's six-term physical substrate (space, time, energy, spectrum, gradient, network) is a *physical* minimum set. Reading a board's risk register as those six atoms would be a forcing, and it is refused. Fig 4 :19-20 licenses the transfer instead: *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* The domain-agnostic kernel (residual, closure, import, ground, seat) governs here; the six physical terms do not. This refusal is recorded as strain S1 in §8 and is not silently dropped.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The single enterprise risk number is the named aggregation error (decisive at D1)

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**
Fig 1 :27: **"A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."**

A board that rolls AI, cyber, and geopolitical exposure into one "enterprise risk posture: amber" has thrown away the only signal that says where to look. The global reading can be near zero while one slot carries a live positive residual, and that is a legal state (Fig 1 :13): a low aggregate does not mean no risk. Worse, if the score is produced by the same management whose decisions it grades, it is a frame scoring itself with a quantity it also produces, which Fig 1 :27 disqualifies outright.

**Refuter:** show that the risk readout is indexed per named slot, that each slot's positive residual is visible to the board, and that the measurement is produced by a source that does not also author the decisions being measured. If any fails, the finding stands against that board.

### F2 - The anchor for risk oversight cannot be management's own rating (decisive at D1, D4)

Fig 2 :29-30: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."** and **"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."**
Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**
Fig 2 :40: **"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**

The anchor is the thing that would actually settle whether the risk is being overseen: an incident, a near miss, a loss, a regulatory finding, a control failure, a customer harm. Those do not move because the board rates them green. A self-assessment score does move when you push on it, because the people who supply it also want the answer. So the score is an instrument or a frame, not an anchor.

This is where the governance literature and the figures agree without sharing an evidence pool. The FRC's Provision 29 guidance says the board should make its own assessment using evidence obtained through monitoring, and specifically that it *"may wish to specifically consider any failings, near misses or weaknesses of the material controls and whether these controls are effective at mitigating or managing the underlying risks"* (FRC, 2024/2026). The NACD handbook says a *"trust but verify"* approach *"demands that directors validate information presented by management using objective benchmarks, performance dashboards, and third-party intelligence"* (NACD/ISA, 2026). Both are the figure's point: range against an anchor that management cannot move, do not negotiate a rating.

**Refuter:** exhibit a risk signal that (i) is not produced by the frame whose decisions it grades and (ii) changes the board's verdict when it changes. If no such signal exists, the arrangement has no anchor and the finding is confirmed, not weakened.

### F3 - "Limited information" is usually a channel failure, not a ground failure (decisive at D1, D4)

Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**
Fig 10 :25-26: **"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"**
Fig 10 :27-28: **"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"**

This is the most useful single re-description in the whole derivation. The losses and near misses did not stop happening. The reporting channel decayed: weak signals were dropped, near misses were pooled into "no material incidents," a metric saturated at green, frontline concerns did not cross the seam. The honest output of that state is *"you have lost touch"*, not *"risk is low"*. Seat Map :20 states the same law: *"The anchor does not decay. The channel to it does: stale readings, dropped signals, saturated metrics, populations pooled across a change."*

Vaughan's normalization of deviance is the low-overlap world corroborator. Her finding on Challenger is that *"a series of seemingly harmless decisions were made that incrementally moved the space agency toward a catastrophic outcome"* (Vaughan, 1996, quoted in the Sage Encyclopedia entry, 2010): not a single wrong decision but a channel that gradually stopped carrying the anomaly. The HRO primer makes the same operational point: *"The absence of errors or accidents leads not to complacency but to a heightened sense of vigilance for the next possible failure. Near misses are viewed as opportunities to learn about systems issues and potential improvements, rather than as evidence of safety"* (AHRQ PSNet, 2024).

**Refuter:** show a board whose low-risk reading survives an independently verified change in incident, near-miss, or control-failure data at the slot. If the reading moves with the data, the channel is live for that slot and this finding does not apply there.

### F4 - More information targets a link that already works (decisive at D1)

Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
Fig 3 :25-26: **"having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."**
Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

The standard response to limited information is more reporting, more dashboards, more data. That is perception, link 1. The chain usually breaks at links 2 and 3: having terms for what a risk affords and being able to say what would count as progress. Those links have no obvious test, so they attract the least attention. A board can receive abundant, accurate reports and still be unable to say what the report affords or what improvement would look like. The figure's instruction is blunt: a link with no instrument is not a link that works.

NIST's AI RMF points at the same object from a standards field: *"Actions do not constitute a checklist, nor are they necessarily an ordered set of steps"* and the map function exists so that *"Without contextual knowledge, and awareness of risks within the identified contexts, risk management is difficult to perform"* (NIST AI RMF 1.0, 2023). Context and objective, not volume of reading, are the scarce terms.

**Refuter:** show that the board's blocker is genuinely link 1 by exhibiting a decision that would change if a specific, currently missing observation were supplied, with the objective already well defined. If the blocker is vocabulary or objective, this finding governs and more data cannot fix it.

### F5 - Limited expertise is a closure bound, and only differing closures move it (decisive at D3)

Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**
Fig 6 :24: **"Import from a live peer is debited against that frame's independence. A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."**
Fig 8 :45: **"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."**

A board cannot derive cyber or AI expertise from inside a closure that does not hold it, however many sessions it holds. What can cross is an import from a frame whose closure differs: an independent technical adviser, a regulator, a peer board that has met the failure, a red team. Fig 6 :24 prices the import: it is debited against the importing frame's independence and a wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives. This is why the NACD handbook advises that *"Boards can consider retaining access to independent experts or third-party assessors who can provide technical and strategic evaluations of the company's posture"* and that *"Board level-expertise also helps maintain the board's independence, so they are not reliant entirely on management's assessment and can practice healthy skepticism"* (NACD/ISA, 2026). The point is not to fill a skills matrix; it is to add a closure that does not share management's evidence pool.

**Refuter:** identify a candidate who supplies the missing term from a closure that does not draw on the same evidence as management. If every candidate was chosen, briefed, and paid by management, the import is adopted, not convergent (Fig 8 :50-52), and the surplus is zero.

### F6 - Inward and outward are opposed instruments; ask SEARCHED first (decisive at D3)

Fig 6 :29: **"inward: is our representation adequate to hold it? The instrument extends access. Fails by refining forever on an axis that cannot carry it. Sharpen in our own terms."**
Fig 6 :30: **"outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."**
Fig 6 :32: **"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."**
Fig 6 :34-35: **"Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."**

Formula step 7 splits the failure into three questions, not one: **SEARCHED** (is it in here and unfound? cheapest, asked first), **INWARD** (sharpen the representation from an instrument already returning something), **OUTWARD** (import the frame whose closure predicts R's shape). Applied here:

1. **SEARCHED** - is the expertise already in the room and merely unfound? The internal risk function, the CISO, the engineers, the whistleblower channel, and the procurement team may already be returning a signal that the board is not reading. Fig 11 :48 says read the habitat off the world, not off a template. Distribute the search and keep the searchers independent; searchers drawing on the same evidence are one searcher wearing many names.
2. **INWARD** - extend an instrument that already returns something. Near-miss reporting, time-to-detect, control-failure rates, and red-team findings are existing proto-instruments. You do not specify a perfect risk radar from an account of the risk; you improve the weakest reading you already have.
3. **OUTWARD** - describe R in effect terms first, then find the frame whose closure predicts its shape. Do not pick the consultancy and then make the risk fit.

The distinction between underivable in principle and unreachable at this budget matters. If the board already holds the relevant atom and did not find it, more search is the remedy, not an import. Importing against a budget limit adds an atom that was not needed.

**Refuter:** show a case where the board's missing term is in its own closure, findable with a distributed independent search, and the board imported an external adviser anyway. The import added no atom (Fig 6 :25) and costs independence.

### F7 - Describe the risk before appointing the adviser (decisive at D3)

Fig 9 :27-28: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**
Fig 9 :30-31: **"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"**
Fig 8 :53: **"Residual first, frame second."**
Fig 10 :52: **"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."**

The ordinary governance sequence is: invite a well-known adviser, let them frame the risk, then act on the framing. The figures reverse it. First write what failed to resolve, in effect terms: "a model vendor's access path to production data was not visible to us until an incident," not "we need AI governance." Then find the frame whose closure predicts that shape. This also governs the expert briefing itself: Seat Map, Seat 4 reads *"shape without referent"* and *"verifies nothing and can check nothing"*, producing *"corrections on shape, named failure classes, and one question: could this have been anything else?"* An external expert briefed on management's full framing has joined the frame they were meant to check.

**Refuter:** produce a board minute in which the risk residual is stated in effect terms before the adviser or committee is named, with enough specificity that a wrong adviser could have been rejected. If the minute names the adviser first, this finding governs.

### F8 - A chain of reviewing committees compounds agreement, not evidence (decisive at D4)

Fig 7 :12-13: **"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"**
Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**
Fig 7 :36: **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."**

Management writes the risk pack; the risk committee reads it; the audit committee reads the risk committee's summary; the board reads the audit committee's report; the external auditor reads management's controls. If all of them draw on the same pack, this is one long room with several labels. Adding rooms raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor (Seat Map :283). Progress per handoff has two factors (Fig 7 :37-39): the bracket's fidelity across the handoff and the ground's pressure on the room. Zero either and the chain runs in place. The practical instruction is to make the next room differ from the last and match the residual the last one surfaced, which is the figure-grounded reason for red teams, independent assurance, and regulator or peer challenge rather than another layer of review.

**Refuter:** identify two reviewing bodies in the chain whose evidence is drawn from genuinely different sources and whose verdicts can diverge when the ground moves. If divergence is impossible by construction, they are one room.

### F9 - Absence of a reported incident does not close the residual (decisive at D2, D4)

Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**
Fig 9 :44: **"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."**
Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"**

"No material incidents this quarter" is a search that found nothing, not a proof that no risk exists. Fig 8 :40-43 separates underivable in principle from unreachable at this budget: they are different facts with different instructions. A board that treats a clean quarter as settlement commits the error the figures name. The honest report is *unreached*, with the instrument that would be owed stated. This is not a counsel of paralysis; the figures still require the loop to run and the channel to be kept reachable.

**Refuter:** exhibit an instrument at the slot whose sensitivity and specificity are both established, such that a clean reading would genuinely discriminate "no risk" from "undetected risk." Most board dashboards have no such instrument. Where one exists, this finding narrows but does not vanish.

### F10 - The habitat must be enumerated outward, because the material residual is often third order (decisive at D2)

Fig 11 :39: **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"**
Fig 11 :40: **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**
Fig 11 :43-44: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."** and **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**
Fig 11 :52-53: **"contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**

This is the answer to the "limited expertise" framing at its deepest level. A board tries to become more capable by improving the directors in isolation, through training and recruitment. The figures say capability is agent-and-habitat: an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing. The lever is to change what the board is in contact with, by enumerating the habitat outward: the AI vendor's own supplier, the data broker, the jurisdiction's export controls, the insurer's exclusion, the customer's downstream dependency. Scoping to direct contact is how the residual three steps out goes unseen.

Perrow's normal accident theory is a low-overlap engineering and complex-systems corroborator: *"even if we had excellent regulation and everyone played it safe, there would still be accidents in systems that are highly 'interactively complex,' and if the systems are tightly coupled, even small failures will cascade through them"* (Perrow, 2011). A BIS review of accident models draws the governance lesson directly: *"the defenses that we establish to protect ourselves against small problems become the threats that can cause even larger problems"* (BIS, 2018). Adding a control layer to a tightly coupled system can add the very interaction that produces the cascade, which is why enumeration, not more layering, is the move.

Seat Map :61 also names the asymmetry the board operates under: *"The seat's power over the habitat is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* You can add a red team; you cannot subtract the geopolitical environment.

**Refuter:** show a board capability gain that changed no contact with the habitat and still moved a ground-measured risk outcome at the slot. If the gain was actually a new external reading, new reporting path, or new actor introduced, contact changed and the finding is confirmed.

### F11 - The verification arrangement must make the checker unable to produce what it checks (decisive at D4)

Fig 10 :42: **"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."**
Fig 10 :43: **"Soundness requires the checker to be unable to produce what it is checking."**

If management authors the risk assessment and the board simply reads and approves it, the board can reconstruct the claim from the same material it would need to check it. That is the named soundness failure. The checkable condition is structural, not personal: the verifying body must be unable to produce the thing it verifies. This is why an independent internal audit line, an external assessor with its own data, or a red team that was not given the plan is doing a different job from a second committee reading the same pack. It is also the framework's zero-knowledge condition (Seat Map :33): *"A checker that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it, because it can no longer tell whether the claim is right or merely whether it matches the picture the checker already built."*

**Refuter:** exhibit a verifying body that independently generated at least one of the readings it uses to check management's claim, from a source management did not supply. If all readings arrive through management, soundness is zero regardless of diligence.

### F12 - Stakes are structural and must be declared, not removed (D1, D4)

Fig 10 :59: **"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"**
Fig 10 :60: **"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."**

Management has an interest in a favourable risk reading: it affects compensation, capital allocation, and reputation. The board cannot remove that interest, and the figures do not ask it to. They ask that the interest be declared so the board can price it. An adviser paid by management, an internal audit function whose budget management controls, and a director with a standing interest in a prior decision are all modelled by the room whether named or not. Naming them is the only way to make the model challengeable. Seat Map :171 adds that *"standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on"*, which matters for directors who championed an AI investment and later oversee its risk.

**Refuter:** find a declaration of interest for each party that can move a risk reading. Where none exists, the finding governs and the reading is discounted by an unpriced stake.

### F13 - No figure returns the board recipe; that is a `gap`, not a silence (all domains)

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**
Fig 13 :115: **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."**
Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"**

The figures return the four-condition separator in §0 and nothing more. They do not name the committee, the cadence, the number of external advisers, the reporting line, the skills matrix, or the percentage of time to spend on AI versus cyber versus geopolitics. Those are bindings from the governance frame, provenanced as such, to be settled by a ground reading in a particular company. Declaring `gap` here is the framework's own instructed verdict. The world sources make the same negative point from a different direction: the FRC explicitly declines to set a template, saying *"The 2024 Code and accompanying guidance does not set out a template risk and internal controls framework. Risk and internal controls frameworks will be unique to each company"* (FRC, 2024).

**Refuter:** quote a figure line that names a board structure, cadence, or allocation. None does. If one is produced by re-reading, check whether it was smuggled in from a world source and mark the provenance.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach: Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Per-slot condition fails.** A board risk readout is shown to preserve every live slot residual and to be produced by a source that does not author the decisions it grades. (F1)
2. **Anchor condition fails.** A risk rating is shown to be non-updating under pressure from the frame it grades, and to change the board's verdict when it changes. (F2)
3. **Checker condition fails.** A verifying body is shown to be unable to reconstruct at least one reading it checks, from a source the maker did not supply. (F11)
4. **Import condition fails.** A governance change improves a ground-measured risk outcome while changing no contact with the habitat and importing no differing closure. (F5, F10)
5. **Union condition fails.** AI, cyber, and geopolitical risk are shown to be a partition with no overlapping conditions, so one lever closes each region separately. (§1, F10)
6. **Channel condition fails.** A low-risk board reading is shown to track an independently verified fall in incidents, near misses, and control failures at every material slot, with established instrument sensitivity and specificity. (F3, F9)
7. **Instrument condition fails.** A measure is produced that closes the residual at a named slot against a non-updating ground and survives independent re-checking. At that point the `gap` narrows and part of the answer moves toward `grounded`.

If any of 1 to 6 fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn. Until an instrument runs, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. **Stop reporting one risk number.** Keep R indexed per named slot with the live positive residual visible (F1).
2. **Name the anchor and keep the channel to it reachable.** Incidents, near misses, control failures, and regulatory findings from a source that does not move with the board's rating. Treat a dead channel as *"you have lost touch"*, not as safety (F2, F3).
3. **Fix the vocabulary and objective link before buying more data.** For each slot, write what the risk affords and what count as progress (F4).
4. **Ask SEARCHED, then INWARD, then OUTWARD, in that order.** Search the existing habitat independently first; sharpen an instrument that already returns something; only then import a differing closure, with the risk described in effect terms before the adviser is named (F6, F7).
5. **Make the next room differ from the last.** Independent red teams and independent assurance rather than another review of the same pack (F8).
6. **Declare the stakes of every party who can move a reading** (F12).
7. **Enumerate the habitat outward** to second and third order, and accept that you can add actors but cannot exclude the environment (F10).
8. **Report `unreached` where no instrument discriminates.** Do not convert a clean quarter into a verdict (F9, F13).

Send the generator, that is the four-condition separator and its refuter, not a recording of one board's success (Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*).

- **S3, the checker's stake (live, declared).** A real internal audit or external assessor is never stake-free. Fig 10 :58-60 says a seat with no stake does not exist and the interest in the reading is disqualifying. Boards cannot meet the ideal of a zero-stake verifier, so the interest must be declared and the reader discounts accordingly. Seat Map :169 warns that a prohibition nothing can satisfy is a prohibition stated wrong; the honest form is declaration, not a pretended absence of interest.
- **S4, "near miss" as a measure (live, instrument owed).** Near misses are the right shape of signal (HRO, FRC) but only if the reporting channel has sensitivity and specificity (Seat Map, observer's defect :275). A near-miss count that rises because reporting improved is not a risk increase, and one that falls because people stopped reporting is not a decrease. The instrument, not the argument, settles it.
