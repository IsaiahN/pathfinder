## 3. What the wider literature bears on the result

These are corroborations only. The figures decide; a conflict would send the derivation back to the census, and none occurred.

1. **Corporate governance practice (one pool).** NACD and FRC both say what Fig 2 and Fig 10 say in board language: independent challenge, trust but verify, near misses in the assessment, no template framework. Because they share the governance-practice pool, their agreement is one witness, not two.
2. **Standards and engineering (lower overlap).** NIST AI RMF separates map from measure and refuses the checklist reading. It corroborates Fig 3 :25-27: the scarce item is context and objective, not another action item.
3. **Safety science and decision psychology (low overlap).** HRO and the premortem corroborate Fig 8 :39 and Fig 9 :44 (absence is not safety) and the pre-registered refuter (make the failure cheap to state before the run).
4. **Sociology (low overlap).** Vaughan corroborates Fig 10 :24-28: the channel carrying the anomaly decayed incrementally, and nobody in the room saw a single wrong decision.
5. **Complex systems (low overlap).** Perrow and the BIS review corroborate Fig 11 :52-53: the material residual is often second or third order, and adding defences can add the coupling that produces the cascade.

The governance sources support the *binding* of the shape to a board; the lower-overlap sources support the *mechanism shape* itself. That distinction is kept in the WATSON answer.

## 4. Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22-25** - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down. A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Sending the full framework before a recipient has described a residual sends a recording. Lead with the four conditions and the six refuters; offer the links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** - *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the entire architecture before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** - *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"* - ask what the recipient would expect to see if the condition were true before giving them the source.
- **Fig 2 :40** - *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* - never pool later agreement as corroboration.
- **Seat Map :163** - *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* A list selected for people likely to agree produces a convention, not a check.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the residual in effect terms; (2) the four conditions and the six refuters; (3) ask what they would count as a failure; (4) record their verdict; (5) only then the three links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## 5. The two-stage offer

- **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"*, and Fig 2 :45, *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* The recipient checks the conditions without holding the framework's interior.
- **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. The stage-1 verdict is recorded before any source is shown (Fig 2 :40).
- **Stage 3 sends a method.** Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer. Each user still needs a ground per problem (Formula "Direction"), and must write the refuter first.
- **Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. If one of the six refuters fires, send the retraction instead of the pitch.
