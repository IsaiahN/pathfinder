# Unit systems as isomers, and the order of the arguments

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

If Planck, Stoney, strong and reduced Planck units are all built from the same small set of constants, are they different *substances* or different *spellings*? And what order must the argument run in so that a convention choice cannot masquerade as a ground result?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: `c`, `ħ`, `G`, plus `e`, `m_e`, `Λ` available from the physics frame. Bonds available: the seven typed relations and negation; for dimensional analysis the operative "bond" is the exponent chosen for each constant. No new primitive is available without import. Currency: description length and ground settlement. Anchor: experiment (D2), never the convention frame (D1). Partition inside D1; union across D1, D2, D3.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 Are unit systems isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Planck units: `l_P = sqrt(ħG/c^3)`, built from `{c, ħ, G}`. Stoney units: built from `{c, G, e}`. Strong units (Wilczek, as cited in Jacobs 2025 §4.1): built from `{ħ, e, m_e}` (or `Λ_QCD`), with a length about twenty orders of magnitude larger than `l_P`. Reduced Planck units: `{c, 8πG, ħ}`, with a mass about `2.4e18 GeV` instead of `1.22e19 GeV`.
- Under Fig 12's definition these are **isomers**: the same kind of atoms (dimensional constants), different bonding (which constants are selected and with which exponents), different behaviour (a length 20 orders apart). The difference is invisible in the phrase "natural units" and decisive when you ask where to point an instrument.
- They are also members of a **functional group**: the substructure "complete unit system from universal constants" fixes the kind; the qualifier (which constants) fixes the member. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a argument from dimensions alone selected one member uniquely, with no free choice of constants and no free dimensionless factor, the isomer claim fails and "natural" becomes a name. Jacobs §4.1 and Baez both give the counter: the factor is free and the constant set is a choice. The falsifier is therefore unfired, and the isomer claim stands as `specified`.

### Q2 What order must the arguments run in?

- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
- The dependency order for this question is: (1) vocabulary, what "scale" means (a form or a magnitude); (2) objective, what counts as progress (a discriminator between unit systems); (3) instrument, what is already returning something that fails to resolve; (4) theory proposals. Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
- The actual literature order is inverted: dimensional uniqueness (link 1-2) is used to assert a magnitude (link 4), skipping link 3. That is the "break between three and four" pattern of Fig 3, where "effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it" (Fig 3 :20-21).
- **Reversal test:** running the argument in the correct order does not change the dimensional form, but it changes the verdict. Form first gives a shape; instrument first would be needed for a name. Since no instrument returns a Planck-scale reading, the correct-order verdict is shape-only.

### Q3 Dimensionless ratios and why a number is not a scale

- Fig 12 :100: "The price of an arrangement is the choice it records, so it is logarithmic in the number of bonds available."
- A dimensionless ratio such as the electromagnetic-to-gravitational force ratio between protons (about `10^36`) is a *choice record*: it compares two couplings already in the library. It is not a new scale, and it does not move a wall. Fig 6 :25: "An import adds no atom to the world."
- The cosmological constant expressed in Planck units (about `10^-122 l_P^-2`, Wikipedia table 3) is another ratio, not a new scale. It is striking, but a ratio being striking is a budget/filter observation, not a witness. Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."
- **Falsifier:** a dimensionless ratio that is forced rather than measured, and that fixes which unit system nature uses. None was found.

### Q4 Is there a seventh "natural" primitive?

- Fig 13 :44: "A generating set must not contain what its own members produce." Fig 13 :43: "Each is indivisible: no arrangement of the other five yields it."
- "Naturalness" is not a seventh constant and not a seventh term. It is a preference over which already-held constants to bond. Adding it as a primitive would violate Fig 13 :44, because it is produced by the choice of constants. Fig 13 :143-144: "Indivisibility and minimality are claims and both are refutable: derive a member from the other five."
- So the feature the question calls "natural" is a property of the *composition*, not a member of the set. It cannot be derived from the constants and then used to rank them; that would be circular. This is the structural twin of F1 in `01`.

## CONCLUSION

Planck, Stoney, strong and reduced unit systems are **isomers** under Fig 12's definition: same constants as atoms, different selection and exponents as bonds, different magnitudes as behaviour. They are also members of one **functional group** (complete natural unit systems). The argument for any one of them as "the" scale must run in dependency order (vocabulary, objective, instrument, theory), not from dimensional uniqueness straight to a magnitude. Dimensionless ratios and "naturalness" are choice records, not primitives and not ground witnesses. The isomer and order claims are `specified` and unfalsified by the sources read; the magnitude claim remains owed to the ground.
