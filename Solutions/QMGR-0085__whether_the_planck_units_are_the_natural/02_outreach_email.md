**Subject:** A shape claim about the Planck scale, and the one measurement that would refute it

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output fails, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The question is whether the Planck units are the natural scale of quantum gravity. My check separates three things that are usually bundled: a unit-convention question (`c`, `ħ`, `G` force one length up to a factor), a physics question (where does current theory actually break), and a theory-parameter question (string length, extra-dimension volume, EFT cutoff). Dimensional analysis settles the first and leaves the other two standing.
>
> The claim I would like checked is only this: **dimensional uniqueness gives a shape, not a number.** A threshold at which the comparison of the two theories becomes operative is licensed. A magnitude is not, until a measurement fixes it. The Planck number itself moves under naming choices (`h` vs `ħ`, `G` vs `8πG`, or a Stoney/strong unit system), so it fails the test of an anchor, where an anchor is whatever settles a question without changing when a frame changes its mind. No instrument has yet returned a reading at `1e-35 m` that fixes which combination nature uses.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a theorem forced from the low-energy constants alone that fixes the dimensionless multiplier of the Planck length at order 1, with no naturalness assumption;
> 2. a source-discriminating instrument that returns a breakdown scale and fits it to the Planck length within a stated, convention-independent factor;
> 3. a demonstration that the three bundled conditions are actually one condition, so the split I rely on is unnecessary.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. Any one firing settles the question in a way my claim does not survive. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that quantum gravity definitely begins at the Planck scale; that `l_P` is a shortest possible length; that the Planck mass is a fundamental particle mass; or that my framework settles the numerical question. The literature I read includes a peer-reviewed survey of the five standard arguments for a Planck-scale threshold that finds none of them succeeds (Jacobs, Philosophy of Physics, 2025), and Baez's own concession that "we cannot prove that the Planck length is significant for quantum gravity." I am not asking you to take those on trust either.
>
