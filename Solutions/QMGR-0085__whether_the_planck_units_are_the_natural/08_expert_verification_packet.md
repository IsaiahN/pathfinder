# Expert verification packet: are the Planck units the natural scale of quantum gravity?

**What this is:** a claim in a form a physicist or philosopher of physics can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the structural and epistemic claims, `general` on the shape. Not `grounded`.** No measurement at the Planck scale is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Dimensional uniqueness gives the shape of a quantum-gravity scale, not its magnitude. The unique length from `c`, `ħ` and `G` is convention-stable only inside the dimensional frame that produces it; the number `1.616255e-35 m` moves under changes of convention (`h` vs `ħ`, `G` vs `8πG`) and under alternative complete unit systems (Stoney, strong), so it fails the anchor test. The question is a union of a unit-convention condition, a theory-breakdown condition and a candidate-theory parameter condition. The figures license a threshold at which the comparison of the two theories becomes operative, and they withhold the magnitude until a ground reading fixes it. Planck, Stoney, strong and reduced units stand as isomers: same constants, different bonding, different behaviour. The framework returns a shape, not a name, and the name is owed to an instrument.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 1, Figure 2 and Figure 9

- **Statement:** dimensional uniqueness is frame-internal and cannot decide a ground magnitude; the Planck number is not an anchor; the framework returns a shape.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."
  - Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :30: "you push on it, it is not an anchor."
  - Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself" and Fig 9 :42: "narrowing gets you close, the ground decides."
- **Refuter:** exhibit a theorem forced from the low-energy constants alone that fixes the dimensionless multiplier of `l_P` at order 1 with no naturalness assumption, or a verbatim figure line naming a magnitude. Either breaks this sub-claim.

### C2 Physics-facing; checkable against the dimensional-analysis literature

- **Statement:** the dimensional argument does not establish the magnitude. The dimensionless factor is free, alternative complete unit systems exist, and the strongest (nonrenormalisability) argument relies on Action-Universality, an unproved assumption.
- **What to check:** Jacobs, "Does Quantum Gravity Happen at the Planck Scale?", Philosophy of Physics (2025), DOI 10.31389/pop.159, §§4.1 and 4.4; Baez, "The Planck Length" (1999), which states "we cannot prove that the Planck length is significant for quantum gravity" and that the "unimportant numerical factor" "might actually be very large, or very small." Check whether the survey's five arguments are represented fairly and whether the conclusion ("our theories don't (yet) predict that quantum gravity happens at the Planck scale") is overstated.
- **Refuter:** a peer-reviewed argument showing the factor is forced, or a ground measurement fitting a scale to `l_P` within a stated convention-independent factor. Either settles the magnitude and overturns this sub-claim.

### C3 Phenomenology-facing; checkable against current bounds

- **Statement:** alternative scales are live, not refuted, and the union compound must be split before search.
- **What to check:** PDG 2025 Extra Dimensions review gives ADD with `M_D` near a TeV and `M_P = 1/sqrt(8 pi G_N) ~ 2.4 x 10^18 GeV`; Aydemir 2018 reports the LHC bound `M_U > 2.6 TeV` at 95 percent CL and that gravity has been tested down to about `0.01 cm`. So a fundamental scale far from `l_P` is not excluded by experiment, and no Planck-scale reading exists.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :39: "a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 9 :54: "when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- **Refuter:** show that convention, breakdown and theory parameter are one condition (the union is a partition), or produce an experimental result that fixes the fundamental scale at `l_P` and excludes the alternatives. Either dissolves this sub-claim.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that quantum gravity begins at `l_P`; not that `l_P` is a shortest possible length; not that the Planck mass is a fundamental particle mass.
- Not that dimensional analysis proves a threshold exists. It narrows the form a threshold would have if those three constants are the right ones.
- Not a unified theory, not a derivation of one theory from the other, and no new primitive.
- Not that "natural" is a fact about nature rather than a visible choice of constants.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not a claim that the world sources agree independently. They overlap on the same physics literature and are corroboration only (Fig 2 :40).

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. In order of cheapness:

1. **Theorem:** fix the multiplier `k` of `sqrt(ħG/c^3)` from theory alone, with no naturalness assumption.
2. **Instrument:** any of GRB dispersion, interferometer noise spectra, inverse-square-law tests or collider searches returning a breakdown scale and fitting it to `l_P` within a stated factor.
3. **Census:** show the three bundled conditions are one, so the split in C3 is unnecessary.

Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`, and the stop is unreached rather than unreachable.
