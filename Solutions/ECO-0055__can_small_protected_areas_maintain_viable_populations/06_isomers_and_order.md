## DERIVATION

### Q1 - What is the structure of connectivity?

Fig 13 :74: **"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."**
Fig 13 :75: **"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."**
Fig 13 :23-24: **"network that entities relate, and influence travels"**
Fig 13 :10: **"near, far, between, contained"**

Connectivity parses as **space (state) bonded to network (structure)**. Space supplies adjacency, distance and containment; network supplies the fact that entities relate and influence travels. Neither alone does the work: adjacency without relation is a map, and relation without space has no near or far to cross. This is a legal cross-kind composition, so it needs no new primitive and no import.

Consequence for the ecological question: connectivity is **not a quantity of area**. It is a structure. Asking "how many hectares" and asking "how are the patches related" are different questions in different kinds. This is the structural reason the area-only answer is a category error, independent of the empirical SLOSS result.

### Q2 - What is the order of the questions?

Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**
Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

The dependency order for this question:

1. **Perception:** identify the focal population and the patch boundary. Slot indexed per population (Fig 1 :19).
2. **Vocabulary:** name what the situation affords (resources, refuges, crossing points). This is the link that usually breaks, because it has no instrument (Fig 3 :24-27).
3. **Objective:** state what would count as viability: persistence over a stated period with a stated persistence threshold. This is a comparison (⋛), not a state.
4. **Planning:** choose which contact to test, under incomplete information.
5. **Learn and carry:** name what worked, verify it against a count, carry the method to the next species.

Formula step 7 separates three further questions, and they must stay distinct:

- **SEARCHED:** is the answer already in the closure and unfound? For a reserve: have the existing monitoring data actually been read per species? Fig 6 :49: *"more search at the same reach: is it in here and unfound? The closure does not change."* Asked first, because it is cheapest.
- **INWARD:** extend the instrument from one already returning something. For a reserve: finer demographic or genetic resolution, sharpened in our own terms.
- **OUTWARD:** import. Characterise the missing contact in effect terms, then find the frame whose closure predicts its shape (landscape genetics, movement ecology). Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*

INWARD and OUTWARD are opposed and must not be read against one another. Adding a genetic assay does not substitute for adding a corridor, and adding a corridor does not substitute for a better count.

### Q3 - Is a metapopulation a cycle or a chain?

Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**

Local extinction followed by recolonisation is a cycle: the output of one patch's disappearance is the input to another patch's occupancy, and the sign of the loop (whether immigrants arrive before the whole network empties) decides whether it closes. A one-way corridor model that moves individuals outward without return is a chain, and a chain cannot close by construction. So a "connectivity plan" that only exports animals from a source, with no return path and no recolonisation of emptied patches, is not a metapopulation model. It is a chain drawn on a map.

**Falsifier F6a, written before the run:** if a proposed corridor network yields no recolonisation of any emptied patch over the stated period, the cycle claim fails for that system and the network is a chain, not a viable metapopulation.

### Q4 - Are one large and several connected small reserves isomers?

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

Take the same total habitat and the same species pool. Write one configuration as a single large patch; write the other as several small patches joined by a network. The atoms are the same. The bond is different: one has a single boundary and internal adjacency, the other has several boundaries and external links. Under Fig 12's definition they are **isomers**: the formula (total hectares) cannot tell them apart, and the behaviour can differ decisively. This is exactly why the SLOSS literature finds that the answer depends on movement, spreading of risk and heterogeneity (Fahrig et al. 2022), and why Sarkar's *"no non-contextual answer to the SLOSS question"* is not a failure of the concept.

As the configurations are actually drawn, they also share a **functional group** (Fig 12 :124): each is a bounded habitat containing a population, which is what fixes the kind of thing under discussion. The qualifier (connected or isolated, one or several) says which member of the group is present. So the honest structural claim is:

> A single large reserve and a set of connected small reserves are isomers over a shared habitat-and-species atom set, not different kinds of object; which member persists is decided by the ground, not by the formula.

Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."** Once a local population is settled against the ground, it becomes an atom for the network comparison. This is the recursion that lets a per-species result compose to a reserve-network result.

**Falsifier F6b, written before the run:** if the same atoms under different bond arrangements yield identical persistence outcomes for every focal species in every matrix, the isomer claim fails and the two configurations are merely two spellings of one thing (Fig 12 :92-93: under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term).

### Q5 - What is the union?

Fig 8 :45: **"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."**

The union of two different closures (two reserves with different species or different matrix contacts) has a surplus: together they reach persistence states neither reaches alone, provided the surplus is real and not a shared evidence pool. Fig 8 :50-52: *"Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence"*, and only the record of where each came from separates convergent derivation from adopted import. Two reserves sampled by the same survey are one witness wearing two names; two reserves measured by independent methods are two frames.

## CONCLUSION

Connectivity is a cross-kind structure (space bonded to network), not an amount of area. The questions must be asked in dependency order: slot, affordances, objective, contact, carry. A viable metapopulation is a cycle, and a corridor plan without return is a chain. One large reserve and several connected small ones are isomers over a shared atom set, with a functional group in common; which member persists is a ground question. No new primitive, no import that moves the wall, and no hectare threshold is required for any of these claims.
