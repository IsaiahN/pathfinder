# Expert verification packet - the Tether on small protected areas

**What this is:** a claim in a form a conservation biologist or protected-area practitioner can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No field measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, and it cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A small protected area cannot be the unit of viability. A viable population belongs to a population-and-habitat system. When the surrounding landscape is heavily altered, the reserve is a substituted habitat: it has lost imports of individuals, mutualists and resources, and inherited matrix actors it did not choose. The shape of a surviving answer is a connected habitat network whose boundary still admits the crossings the focal population requires, with the relevant actors, conditions and relations enumerated outward through the matrix. Connectivity is a cross-kind structure (space bonded to network), not an amount of area; a viable network is a cycle in which local extinction is answered by recolonisation; and one large reserve and several connected small ones are isomers over a shared atom set. The framework returns this shape, not a hectare threshold, and its technical term "habitat" is not the ecological niche.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 11 and 13

- **Statement:** viability is a property of the population and its habitat, not of the area; isolation is substitution of one habitat for another; the field of contact must be enumerated, not composed.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39 - *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"*. Fig 11 :66 - *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Fig 11 :43 - *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 13 :62 - *"no region to make isolated mean anything"*.
- **Refuter:** R1 in `01` §3: a small reserve in a non-permeable altered matrix maintains a viable focal population over a stated period with zero imports and no managed reproduction of required actors; or a complete enumeration of required actors, conditions and relations that contains nothing outside the reserve and nothing at second or third order from the matrix.
- **Mapping caveat:** this sub-claim is structural. The architecture article's "Three mappings offered and declined" explicitly refuses to equate Tether habitat with the ecological niche. Rejection of the mapping (R4) is a legitimate refutation of the ecological binding.

### C2 - ecology-facing; checkable against monitoring and experimental data

- **Statement:** the deciding quantity is contact, not the quality of a closed interior; the residual must be indexed per focal population, not averaged over the reserve.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :40 - *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Fig 6 :23 - *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 1 :19 - *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
- **What to check:** does the persistence of the focal population change when a specific crossing is removed or restored? Does the immediate matrix affect demography even when the wider landscape is unchanged (Smith et al. 2025)? Does a reserve-wide aggregate hide a species declining to local extinction?
- **Refuter:** R2 and R3 in `01` §3: an area-only lever restoring viability with all contacts fixed; or a single aggregate metric preserving every per-species extinction signal. Note the re-census in `01` §2b strain 2: improving the **local matrix** changes contact, so it is not a counterexample to Fig 11 :40.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation with an ecological check

- **Statement:** connectivity parses as space (state) bonded to network (structure); a viable metapopulation is a cycle, not a chain; one large reserve and several connected small reserves are isomers over a shared atom set.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :74 - *"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."* Fig 12 :121 - *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- **What to check:** is there recolonisation of emptied patches (a closing cycle), or only one-way export (a chain)? Do the two configurations share the same habitat-and-species atoms and differ only in arrangement?
- **Refuter:** F6a and F6b in `06_isomers_and_order.md`: no recolonisation over the stated period, or identical outcomes under every different arrangement.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that every small reserve fails, or that one large always beats several small. The SLOSS outcome is ground-decided and context-dependent (Fahrig et al. 2022).
- Not that a universal minimum area or MVP number follows from the figures. That slot is recorded as `gap` (Fig 9 :32).
- Not that corridors alone fix persistence. A corridor is a candidate import path; whether it carries the required actor is measured.
- Not that a habitat-suitability model or an expert consensus is the anchor. Models and consensus are frames; a count is the anchor (Fig 2 :29).
- Not that the Tether's "habitat" means the ecological niche. It means the field of contact with a residual, enumerated. This is a mapping with declared limits.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a census, experiment or field measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. Boundary-level mark-recapture or genetic assignment: does persistence survive with zero imports?
2. A controlled before-after test of an area-only lever with matrix conditions fixed.
3. A per-species time series that can fire the aggregation refuter.
4. A recolonisation record that distinguishes a cycle from a chain.

Agreement among reviewers is not such an instrument (Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :39, :43, :40, :66 and Fig 1 :19 (five short passages). Say whether you accept the structural reading, or fire R4.
2. Name one focal species for which you know whether zero-import persistence is plausible. Say what measurement would decide it.
3. Look at one reserve you know: enumerate the first-order and second-order matrix contacts that actually reach the population. Are any of them at three steps out, DDT-style?
4. Check C3: does your system close a recolonisation cycle, or is it a one-way chain?
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
