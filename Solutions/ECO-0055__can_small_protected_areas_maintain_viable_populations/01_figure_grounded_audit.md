## 0. The claim under audit, restated as an effect

The question as written asks whether a small area can *maintain* a *viable population* when the *surrounding landscape* is *heavily altered*. Stated in effect terms:

> **Residual R:** the gap between the prediction "this reserve holds a viable population over the stated period" and what the ground returns (persistence or local extinction), measured **per focal population in its habitat**, not as one reserve-wide score.

Three things must be bound as ordinary terms before the search (Formula step "Direction"; Fig 5 :62-66: a sought-for shape held as a preference is invisible to the loop, so write it down and let it accrue a residual):

- **What a viable answer would look like:** a specified species, a specified period, a persistence or growth outcome that does not require perpetual import of individuals, and the identity of the contacts on which that outcome depends.
- **What the small area is:** a bounded patch of habitat, not a self-contained system.
- **What "heavily altered" is:** a change in the surrounding habitat that changes what can cross the boundary, not a change of label on the map.

What each option leads to:

- **Option A, area-only:** treat the reserve as the unit, ask whether its hectares are sufficient, answer yes or no. This averages across species, ignores the matrix, and produces a number the figures cannot license.
- **Option B, contact-and-habitat:** treat the population and its habitat as the unit, enumerate the contacts, ask whether the required imports still cross. This yields a shape with a stated refuter and an open slot for the ground to fill.

The figures decide between these in section 2. Option B survives; Option A does not.

**Strain note kept visible from the start.** The Tether's word "habitat" is **not** the ecological niche. The canonical article's section "Three mappings offered and declined" says: *"A niche is defined by an organism's relation to its environment. A habitat here is defined by contact with a residual, and the residual moves so the same actors constitute a different habitat when the question changes."* So every use of Fig 11 below is a **structural reading**, not a claim that the figure was written about reserves. The ecological instantiation is a binding that needs the ground. This is recorded again in section 5 and in `11_audit.md`.

### 1b. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

**R by arity, symmetry and scale.**

- **Arity: two conditions.** Small area **and** heavily altered matrix bind together. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*. This is a **union compound** with its own loop, not one hard question and not two independent questions.
- **Symmetry: asymmetric contact.** The manager can add actors and cannot subtract the habitat: Fig 11 :61, *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* The membrane has a direction: Fig 4 :21, *"Not a wall: a filter with a direction."* Dispersal inward and outward are not the same operation, and edge mortality is not the reverse of immigration.
- **Scale: two orders.** First order is local demography inside the patch (births, deaths, local extinction). Second order is the landscape network (colonisation, recolonisation, gene flow). Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."* A patch's population, once settled, becomes an operand for the network.

**One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor.**

1. **Domain 1, the population and its habitat.** Actors: the focal population, mutualists (pollinators, dispersers), predators, competitors, pathogens, seasonal resources. Currency: persistence and replacement of individuals. Anchor: a count on the ground, Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Question type: **enumeration**, not composition (Fig 11 :43).
2. **Domain 2, the reserve as membrane and closure.** Actors: dispersers, gene flow, edge effects, the altered matrix. Currency: what crosses the boundary. Anchor: observed occupancy and genetic measures. Question type: **reachability** (Fig 6 :23). The inside is a closure; the only operator that moves its wall is import.
3. **Domain 3, the management seat.** Actors: reserve staff, restoration practitioners, monitoring crews. Currency: introductions, corridor work, counts. Anchor: the ground kept reachable; the seat authors conventions, not verdicts (Fig 10 :24, :46-48). Question type: **asymmetric intervention** (Fig 11 :61).
4. **Domain 4, the verification frame.** Actors: external checkers. Currency: refutation and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all four domains draw on one evidence pool, so their agreement is one pool talking (Fig 2 :40), not independent corroboration.

**Partition or union.** Domains 1 and 2 are a **union compound**: the same individuals are both demographic actors and things crossing a membrane, and the overlap has its own loop (colonisation-extinction). Domains 1 to 3 are ordered by dependency, not averaged. Domain 4 ranges against the other three and may not author their verdicts.

**Dependency order (Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*).**

1. Enumerate the habitat, including the matrix, before assessing viability (Fig 11 :43-44).
2. Establish whether contact exists and what crosses, before judging the closure (Fig 6 :23).
3. Settle local demography before the network comparison (Fig 12 :104).
4. Describe the residual in effect terms before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).
5. Refuse the area-only term before minting any new one (Formula step 2; Fig 5 :53-56).

Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* A reserve with no monitoring of the target population is at the break, not merely unmeasured.

## 2. Findings

### F1 - Viability is a property of the population and its habitat, not of the area (decisive against Option A)

Fig 11 :39: **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"**
Fig 11 :40: **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

Substituting the object: a population is not viable "in an area". It is viable in a population-and-habitat system. The question as posed names the area as the bearer of the property, so it is malformed in the same way "is the seed capable" is malformed under Fig 4 :34-35: *"A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."*

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated. Route 1 is Fig 11's habitat law; route 2 is the substrate's space rule, Fig 13 :62: *"no region to make isolated mean anything"*, which says that "isolated" only has content once a region is named. Low overlap between the habitat law and the substrate minimality table. The ecological instantiation of the first route is a structural analogy, not an identity (see section 5). **Refuter:** a viable population sustained in a named area with no habitat outside the boundary contributing anything, measured as zero immigration, zero imported mutualists and zero external resources over the stated period.

### F2 - A heavily altered matrix is a substitution of habitat, not a background condition

Fig 11 :66: **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."**
Fig 11 :67: **"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."**
Fig 11 :68: **"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"**
Fig 11 :69: **"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"**

The altered landscape is not an empty surround. It is a different habitat substituted for the one the population's requirements evolved in. The small reserve therefore owes a full re-enumeration of actors, conditions and relations, and it inherits matrix actors (invasive species, edge predators, pollutants, people) it did not choose. Fig 11 :52-53: *"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* A boundary survey that stops at the fence misses the third-order effect.

Fig 11 :43-44: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."** / **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**
Fig 11 :45-47: **"actors: what acts on it, and what it acts on"** / **"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"** / **"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"**

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 11 (enumeration and substitution) and Fig 6 :23 (a bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs), two different figures with different subject matter. **Refuter:** a complete enumeration of the population's required actors, conditions and relations that contains nothing outside the reserve boundary and nothing at second or third order from the matrix.

### F3 - The residual is per population, not per reserve

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

A reserve-level "viability score" is exactly the aggregation the figure forbids. One species sliding toward extinction while the community total is stable is a live slot hidden in a near-zero global residual. The addressee of the answer is a species-population-slot, not a hectare.

**Mode:** `general` (structural). **Status:** single-source for the figure (Fig 1), corroborated in the world by Haddad et al. 2015, who found species-specific and trophic-level-specific responses under the same fragmentation treatment. One figure plus one world source is one witness plus corroboration, not two independent figures. **Refuter:** a demonstration that a single reserve-wide metric preserves every per-species extinction signal it aggregates.

### F4 - Contact decides viability, not quality inside a closed boundary (the strongest checkable prediction)

Fig 11 :40: **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**
Fig 6 :14: **"the only operator that moves the wall"**
Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**
Fig 8 :45: **"Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."**

If the matrix is heavily altered and the reserve's boundary admits no immigrants, mutualists or resources, then improving the inside alone does not move the system to a viable regime. What is owed is an import, in the figure's sense: a connection to a closure that differs. This is not a metaphor for a wildlife corridor; it is the structural reason a corridor matters. The concrete ecological binding (which corridor, which species, what permeability) is not supplied by the figure and needs the ground.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 11, Fig 6 and Fig 8, with low overlap (capability law, closure rule, union surplus). **Refuter:** a small reserve in a hostile, non-permeable matrix that maintains a viable focal population with zero imports and no managed reproduction of missing actors, measured over the stated period. This is the pre-registered refuter R1 in section 3.

### F5 - Small area and altered matrix are a union compound; one lever leaves the intersection standing

Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
Fig 9 :55-56: **"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**
Fig 9 :57: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"**
Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

Enlarging the reserve addresses the area condition. Restoring matrix permeability addresses the matrix condition. Doing only one leaves the compound case in place. This is why the SLOSS literature's "it depends" is not evasion: Sarkar (2012, quoted in Fahrig et al. 2022) says there is *"no non-contextual answer to the SLOSS question"*, which is the world-facing version of the union finding.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** single-source for the figure (Fig 9), corroborated by Fahrig et al. 2022. **Refuter:** an area-only lever that restores viability while matrix permeability and all external contacts remain unchanged.

### F6 - The seat can introduce but cannot exclude; management is permanent work

Fig 11 :61: **"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."**
Fig 11 :62: **"You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."**
Fig 11 :63: **"a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state"**
Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**
Fig 10 :25-26: **"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"**

A reserve manager can reintroduce a pollinator, build a crossing or supplement food. The manager cannot remove the altered matrix, stop edge effects by decree, or opt out of an invasive species that has arrived. The ground keeps returning counts; what decays is the monitoring channel. So the legitimate management claim is not "the reserve is secure" but "the channel to the ground is maintained".

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 11 and Fig 10 (different offices: intervention asymmetry and ground-maintainer channel). **Refuter:** a managed reserve whose exclusion of matrix actors is permanent without renewed work, or a monitoring channel that cannot go stale.

### F7 - The anchor is a population count, not a model, a metric, or a reputation

Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."**
Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**
Fig 10 :46: *"A convention makes no claim about the world; a verdict and an atom both do."*

A model of viability, a habitat-suitability index, and an authoritative review are frames. They range against the ground; they do not become it. A count that refuses the model is the anchor. If a population persists in the model and not in the census, the census settles it.

**Mode:** `general` (structural rule). **Status:** triangulated across Fig 2 and Fig 10. **Refuter:** a viability verdict that survives a contradictory, well-designed census without changing.

### F8 - The numeric threshold is a gap, not a conferred number

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**
Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*
Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

Deriving a universal area threshold from the six-term set would be naming a thing the framework says it cannot name, and would also be contradicted by the world literature's context dependence. This is recorded as `gap`, not filled with a plausible number.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** `gap` after re-census. **Refuter:** a universal hectare or MVP threshold derived from the figures alone and validated across taxa and matrices. None was found; the search is reported, not hidden (Fig 8 :39).

## 2b. Strain test and re-census

A figure that only fits with a strain governs another quantity. Three strains appeared; all were re-censused, none smoothed over.

**Strain 1, "hostile matrix" versus "permeable matrix".** Fahrig et al. 2022 describe a hostile matrix with very high dispersal mortality; Haddad et al. 2015 note that *"Habitat islands, or fragments, are surrounded by a matrix that may not be so unsuitable for some species."* These conflict in the world sources. The conflict does not flip any verdict. Re-census: Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* "Hostile" and "permeable" are properties of the species-residual relation, not of the matrix in itself. Both world claims can be true for different focal populations. Verdict unchanged; the strain is dissolved by moving the predicate from the matrix to the relation.

**Strain 2, "improvement that does not change contact changes nothing" versus local matrix quality.** Smith et al. 2025 (reported by Conservation Corridor, 2025-12-15) found that the matrix immediately around patches affects reproduction and adult survival, while the wider landscape matrix affects dispersal. Read loosely, Fig 11 :40 seems to deny the local effect. Re-census: "contact" in Fig 11 is the full set of what acts on the residual, not just between-reserve corridors. Improving the immediate matrix **changes contact**, so it is not a counterexample. What Fig 11 :40 refuses is an improvement that leaves all contact unchanged (for example, adding nest boxes inside a fully sealed patch whose pollinators and dispersers cannot cross). Strain dissolved; the figure's claim is sharpened, not weakened.

**Strain 3, Tether habitat versus ecological habitat.** The canonical article explicitly declines the niche mapping (quoted in section 0). Re-census: Fig 11's "habitat" is the field of contact with a residual, enumerated; the ecologist's habitat is a place with conditions and resources. They overlap in the things enumerated (actors, conditions, relations) but are not the same definition. All Fig 11 findings are therefore marked `specified` and structurally read. This strain is **not** dissolved into identity; it is declared as a limit. A conservation biologist checking this packet is entitled to reject the analogy outright, and that rejection would be a real refutation of the ecological binding, not of the figure.

**After re-census: one declared limit (strain 3), no hidden strain, no flipped verdict.**

## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion and before any outreach, per Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

- **R1 (contact).** A small reserve in a heavily altered, non-permeable matrix maintains a viable focal population over the stated period with zero imports and no managed reproduction of required actors, measured per species. This breaks F4 and Fig 11 :40.
- **R2 (compound).** An area-only lever restores viability while every matrix condition and external contact remains unchanged. This breaks F5 and Fig 9 :55-59.
- **R3 (slot).** A single reserve-wide aggregate metric preserves every per-species local-extinction signal it averages. This breaks F3 and Fig 1 :19-22.
- **R4 (mapping).** A refuter from a conservation biologist showing that the structural reading of Fig 11 cannot be instantiated in reserve ecology without changing the figure's definition of habitat. This breaks the binding, not the figure, and the ecological verdict is withdrawn.

Until one of these is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.

## 4. Recommendation

1. **Stop asking the area-only question.** Restate the residual per focal population, with a period and a stated outcome (F1, F3).
2. **Enumerate before judging.** List actors, conditions and relations outward until the cascade stops, including second- and third-order matrix effects (F2).
3. **Measure contact first.** An occupancy or genetic record that shows what crosses the boundary is the cheapest decisive instrument (F4, F7).
4. **Treat interior improvement as conditional.** It changes the system only if it changes contact; otherwise it is Fig 11 :40's inert improvement (F4, strain 2).
5. **Plan for permanent work.** Introduction, not exclusion; monitoring as the seat's office (F6).
6. **Return the shape, not a number.** A connected network large enough to carry the required actors and relations. Record the missing threshold as `gap` (F8).
7. **Send the generator, not the recording.** Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Lead with the refuter and the shape; offer the links after a verdict is recorded.

### Round trip

The condensation "the answer is connectivity" is lossy. Sent up: Fig 11 :39-44, :66-69, Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :45. Brought back down as an ecological recommendation: build corridors and monitor populations. Three things do not survive: that the unit is a population-and-habitat (not a reserve), that valence is relative to the residual (so which contact matters is species-specific), and that isolation is substitution (so the new habitat's unchosen actors must be enumerated). `R_T` is therefore nonzero and is recorded qualitatively, not numerically, because no ground reading exists in this derivation. Treating the condensed phrase as identical to the figure set would set `R_T` to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.

## 6. Verdict of this audit

The shape survives; the area-only question does not. A small protected area can maintain a viable focal population only as part of a population-and-habitat system whose membrane still admits the imports that system requires, and only when the required actors, conditions and relations have been enumerated outward through the altered matrix. This is a `specified`, triangulated, ungrounded result with a declared mapping limit. The universal numeric threshold is a `gap`. A ground reading, not a further argument, is required to move any of it to `grounded`.
