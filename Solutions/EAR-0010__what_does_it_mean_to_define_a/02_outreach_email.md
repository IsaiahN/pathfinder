# Outreach draft: check the shape of a definition before deciding whether to use it

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the audit findings in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are repaired. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** What would refute a "safe Earth system" label?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with me. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the per-slot claims, not `grounded`):** a definition of a "safe" or "stable" Earth system cannot be grounded by declaring it. Fig 10 calls this *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"* and gives the remedy: *"→ install what can be violated"*. A label is only useful if an instrument could show it false, and if what shows it false does not move when the definition is renegotiated. Fig 2 :29 puts the test plainly: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
>
> Three consequences follow, and they are what I would like you to check:
>
> 1. **The label is per slot, not a single global number.** Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A global "safe" index can read safe while one live slot carries the signal that matters.
> 2. **"Safe" and "stable" are two claims, not one.** "Safe" has the shape of a comparison against a bound; "stable" has the shape of a loop that keeps closing. They need separate checks, because a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either.
> 3. **"Stable" is not "nothing changes."** Fig 11 :63-64 says a managed ecosystem is *"permanent work rather than a solved state"*. Natural variability is in the signal, not an error to average away before defining anything.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** I am not giving you a number, a ppm threshold, or a reference period. The framework returns a shape, never a name: Fig 9 :32, *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Any specific threshold is a ground measurement, not something this derivation produces. I am also not asking a committee to agree on a definition; Fig 7 :35 says *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict."*
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before this message:**
> 1. A definition whose truth is settled by declaration alone, where its supposed anchor changes its answer when the defining frame is pushed.
> 2. A global aggregate that preserves every per-slot live residual, so no signal can disappear into the average.
> 3. An Earth system state that is a solved state requiring no maintenance, still settled against a non-updating ground.
> 4. A reduction of both predicates to one bond without losing a prediction.
> 5. A figure line that directly governs a numeric climate threshold.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw or narrow the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step formula and mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Output first, theory second | Fig 10 :52 - *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* The recipient checks the shape without holding the framework's interior. |
| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35 - *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* |
| Pre-registered refuter | A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device. |
| Mode stated on every claim | `THE_FORMULA.md` - *"Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which."* |
| No number offered | Fig 9 :32 - *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Numeric thresholds are `gap`. |
| Two predicates, not one | Fig 9 :54 - unbundle a union before searching; Fig 12 :87 and :121 give two different bonds. |
| No committee definition | Fig 7 :35 - *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict."* |
| No request for trust or prestige | *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: prestige can fund an action but cannot settle it; only the ground settles it. |
| Offer the formula as a method | Fig 4 :22 - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* A generator can be given away without giving away an answer. |
| Ask users to state their own ground | `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": a frame cannot check the direction of its own question; the sought-for shape must be made an ordinary term and allowed to accrue a residual. |
