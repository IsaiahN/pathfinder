# The Tether's answer: what does it mean to define a "safe" or "stable" Earth system?

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the per-slot claims, nothing `grounded`.** A shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The request is for a definition of a condition of the Earth system, while the thing to be defined is in motion. Restated as an effect: a label is wanted, but a label tied to a still baseline moves when the baseline is renegotiated, and a single global label hides the slots that carry the signal. It is a **union compound**, not one hard question: "safe" is a comparison to a bound, "stable" is a loop that keeps closing. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Each predicate needs its own loop and its own ground. Verdicts will be kept separate and never averaged.

## 2. The shape

4. **"Stable" is maintained work, not the absence of change.** Fig 11 :63-64: *"a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state"*. ## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a single global definition of "safe Earth system". That aggregates away the live slots (F2).
- Not "stable means nothing changes". Variability is in the signal (F4).
- Not that "safe" and "stable" are the same predicate. Different bonds, a union to unbundle (F3, F6).
- Not a ppm value, a temperature band, or a reference period. The framework returns a shape, not a name (F7).
- Not a definition by consensus. Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict."*
- Not `grounded`. No climate measurement was taken in this derivation.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A discriminator, not a further definition. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

- **Violation test:** name an observation that would make the proposed label false, and show an instrument that could make that observation. If none can, the label is not installed (Fig 10 :19).
- **Slot test:** show a global index that preserves every per-slot live residual, or accept that it can hide one (Fig 1 :22).
- **Maintenance test:** show that the condition persists without anyone or anything maintaining the channel to the ground, or accept Fig 11 :63-64.
- **Bond test:** show that the comparison and the cycle reduce to one bond without losing a violation prediction (Fig 12 :87, :121).
- **Standing refuters from the audit:** a declaration that grounds itself; a global aggregate preserving every signal; a solved Earth system state; a one-bond reduction; a figure line governing a number.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not hand you a number for a "safe" or "stable" Earth system, and it does not let a committee define one into being. A declared meaning is not a grounded one, so the definition has to be installed as something that can be violated against a ground that does not move when the definition is renegotiated. The label is per slot and per disturbance, because a global average can hide the one live signal that matters. "Safe" has the shape of a comparison to a bound, "stable" the shape of a cycle whose sign decides whether it closes, and they are two conditions rather than one, so they must be checked separately. Stability is not the absence of change; natural variability is in the signal, and the condition is maintained work rather than a solved state. Smoothness and "climate states" are derivatives, not primitives, and the framework returns a shape, never a name. What it offers is the form any candidate definition must have, the refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The number stays open until the ground settles it.
