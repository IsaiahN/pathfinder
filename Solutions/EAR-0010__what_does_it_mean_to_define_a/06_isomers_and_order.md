# Supporting derivation: two bonds, dependency order, and analogues

**Mode: `general` on the structural claims, `specified` on the per-slot claim.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question bundles "safe or stable" into one definition. Domain census in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` shows two predicates with different evidence conditions. Two questions are owed: (1) what structure does each predicate have, and (2) what is the dependency order between defining, enumerating and aggregating?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: the six substrate terms (Space, Time, Energy states; Spectrum, Gradient, Network structures), the seven bonds plus negation, the habitat inventory (actors, conditions, relations), and the per-slot residual R. Bonds relevant here: ⋛ comparison, → sequence, and the structural analogues in Fig 12 (cycle, catalyst, functional group, isomer). No new primitive is available without import. Anchor for each domain: an instrument that does not move on redefinition (domain 1), observation of the habitat (domain 2), per-slot readings (domain 3).

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - What bond does "safe" have?

- Fig 12 :87: **"a threshold relation between two quantities"** (comparison ⋛).
- Operators table :51: **"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"**.
- Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**
- ⇒ "safe" is not a substance or a state. It is a **goal stated as a comparison** between a measured quantity and a bound. A vocabulary without ⋛ could say what a situation contains and could not say whether it is safe.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** exhibit a "safe" predicate that carries no threshold relation to any quantity, and show that it still decides a violation. If it does, the comparison reading fails.

### Q2 - What bond does "stable" have?

- Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**
- Fig 11 :63-64: **"a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state"**.
- Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does."*
- ⇒ "stable" is a **loop that keeps closing against a ground that does not decay**, with the channel maintained. It is not the absence of change and not a fixed point. The sign of the loop is load-bearing: a self-amplifying remainder is chaos (Fig 13 :31-32: *"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos"*), not stability.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a "stable" label whose referent is a fixed point requiring no maintenance and whose loop sign is irrelevant. If exhibited, the cycle-and-work reading fails.

### Q3 - Are the two isomers, or a functional group?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :123-124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Read over the same slot set (a given region, variable and disturbance), two definitions "safe" and "stable" share every atom and differ only in the bond: comparison versus cycle. That is precisely Fig 12's isomer case. Over different slot sets they are not isomers; they are two members of the same functional group (a violable, grounded, per-slot claim) with different qualifiers.
- The field's own nine planetary boundaries are a functional group in this sense: the group says what kind of claim (a quantitative safe limit on a critical process), the qualifier says which process. The Stockholm Resilience Centre page states the boundaries are *"quantitative assessments of the safe limits for human pressure on these nine critical processes"* and that they *"cannot be considered... in isolation"*.
- **Falsifier:** show that the two predicates reduce to one bond over the same atoms without losing a prediction about what would count as a violation. If shown, the isomer claim fails and a single-predicate definition is back on the table.

### Q4 - What is the dependency order?

- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** Fig 3 :20-21: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces."*
- Fig 9 :27-28: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**.
- The order is: (1) state the residual in effect terms; (2) install a violable ground (Fig 10 :19); (3) enumerate the habitat actors, conditions and relations (Fig 11 :46-48: *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world"*); (4) read per-slot residuals and only then consider a global index (Fig 1 :19, :22); (5) unbundle the union before searching again (Fig 9 :54).
- A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed (Fig 3 :22-23). Defining a global "safe" index before per-slot readings exist is a reading below the break: it is a reading of nothing.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a working global definition whose per-slot inputs were never measured, and show that it still ranges against the ground. If exhibited, this dependency order fails.

### Q5 - Analogues that clarify without importing

- **Catalyst**, Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."** A monitoring institution that survives the disturbances it measures is catalyst-like; a metric that is saturated or pooled loses that property (Fig 10 :25-26: *"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"*). This is an analogue, not an identity; the framework's own rule is that a mapping needs a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 8 :50-52).
- **Functional group**, Fig 12 :124, as in Q3.
- **Isomer**, Fig 12 :117, as in Q3.
- **Negation**, Operators :58: **"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"**. "Unsafe" is not a smaller quantity of "safe"; it is a refusal of a claim. This matters for a label whose whole purpose is to be violated.
- **Strain named:** "catalyst" and "cycle" are both Fig 12 structures, but they are not interchangeable. A catalyst survives its own use; a cycle re-enters its own output. Applying both to a monitoring institution at once is a union, not a single structural description. Kept separate, no figure contradiction; merged, a categorization error.

## CONCLUSION

"Safe" and "stable" are structurally **isomers over a shared slot set** (same atoms, different bond) and **members of one functional group** over different slot sets. Their dependency order is residual, then ground, then habitat enumeration, then per-slot reading, then any global index. The analogues (catalyst, functional group, negation) clarify what a definition must preserve and how it may be refused, but each needs provenance before being treated as a derivation. Falsifiers for all four questions were written before the run and are stated above. Mode: `general` plus `specified`, no `grounded` claim.
