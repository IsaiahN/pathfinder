## 0. The claim under audit, stated as an effect

The question asks for a **definition**: what does it take to call the Earth system "safe" or "stable", given that natural conditions have always varied?

Restated as an effect, the residual R is this: a label is wanted that picks out a condition of the Earth system, but the thing to be labelled is in motion, so any label tied to a fixed baseline looks arbitrary. The word "varied" is not decoration; it is the observation that the ground is not still.

Two options, with what each leads to:

- **Option A:** define "safe" and "stable" as a value or band of a global state variable (a mean temperature, a mean state), relative to a reference period. This gives a single number that can be announced. It leads to a threshold that moves if the reference period is renegotiated, and to a single slot standing in for many.
- **Option B:** define them as a checkable relation between specified state variables and a ground that does not move when the definition is renegotiated. This gives no single comforting number. It leads to per-slot, per-disturbance claims that can be refused.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run (formula step "Direction"): a definition is acceptable only if (i) it can be violated, (ii) the thing that violates it is not moved by redefining it, (iii) it is indexed to a slot and a disturbance rather than to a global average, and (iv) it says whether it means a threshold or a persistence. That is the shape claim. It is bound here so it can accrue a residual like anything else.

## 0b. Domain census, before any figure line is chosen

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.**

- **Arity:** at least two. "Safe" and "stable" are not one predicate. "Safe" reads as a threshold against harm (a comparison), "stable" reads as persistence through disturbance (a loop). A definition that merges them is a union, not a single condition.
- **Symmetry:** the two are not interchangeable. Swapping "safe" for "stable" changes what would count as evidence, so the bond between them is not a symmetric one. They must be kept as separate verdicts.
- **Scale:** planetary, multiple strata, long timescales, and per-slot at the same time. The phrase "the Earth system" is one slot in the sentence and many slots in the world.

One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor. A domain without a nameable anchor is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | nameable? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Definition and grounding | definers, thresholds, baselines, metrics | declarations and measurements | the ground: an instrument or record that does not move when the definition is renegotiated | yes |
| 2. Earth system dynamics (habitat) | climate states, actors, conditions, relations | contact, flows, disturbances | observation of the system, which does not update on our labels | yes |
| 3. Measurement and aggregation | indices, averages, monitoring slots | residuals per slot | instrument readings per slot | yes |
| 4. Numeric threshold content | ppm, degrees, rates | numbers | an instrument that would settle a given number | **no figure line governs this** |

**Partition or union.** Domains 1 to 3 form a dependency chain, not a partition of one question. Domain 4 is where a union must be unbundled: "safe or stable" is a union of a comparison and a persistence claim, not one harder condition (Fig 9 :54). Overlaps between domains 2 and 3 are compounds with their own loop: a global index is a composition of per-slot readings, so it must be checked in domain 3 before it is used in domain 1.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Ground the label (domain 1) before using it; enumerate the habitat and read its conditions (domain 2) before aggregating; aggregate only after per-slot residuals are checked (domain 3). "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not" (Fig 3 :19). Effort spent defining a global index before per-slot readings exist lands at a step that cannot use it.

Strain test applied as we go: a line that only fits by forcing "stable" to mean "safe", or "climate state" to mean one of the six substrate states, is governing another quantity and sends us back to this census.

## 2. Findings

### F1 - A declared definition is not a ground (decisive, triangulated)

Fig 10 :18: **"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"** and Fig 10 :19: **"→ install what can be violated"**. Fig 10 :49: **"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."** Fig 10 :47: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**.

Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."** Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

A definition of "safe" that is true because a body agreed to it is a convention that has decided an outcome. It has stopped being a convention and has not become a ground. The figure's test is simple and hard: if pushing on the definition changes what counts as safe, it is not the anchor. What a definition needs instead is something installable that can be violated, that is, a claim whose falsity an instrument could show.

Mode: `general` (structural). Triangulated across Fig 10 (the seat that installs what can be violated) and Fig 2 (the anchor that does not update), two figures with low overlap. Refuter: exhibit a definition whose ground truth is settled by declaration alone, where the supposed anchor changes its answer when the defining frame is pushed. If that works, F1 fails.

### F2 - The label is per slot; a global average throws away the signal (triangulated)

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** Symbols table :38-40: R *"is the aim, not only the error signal, and it is always a slice: what cannot be perceived yet is residual and simply cannot be read."*

"Is the Earth system safe?" is one slot in grammar and many slots in the world. A single global index can read safe while one live slot carries the residual that matters, which is a legal state under Fig 1 :13 (*"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"*). The ecological analogue is not far away: the habitat's conditions are enumerated, not composed (Fig 11 :46: **"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"**; :47: **"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"**), and contact cascades beyond first order (Fig 11 :52: *"And contact cascades."*). A global mean is a composition of those slots, and composition loses information (Fig 4 :27).

Mode: `specified`. Triangulated across Fig 1 (per-slot residual) and Fig 11 (enumerated habitat and cascading contact). Refuter: show a global aggregate that preserves every per-slot live residual, so that no signal can disappear into it. If shown, F2 fails.

### F3 - "Safe or stable" is a union to unbundle, not one question (triangulated)

Fig 9 :4-5: **"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones."** Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** Fig 9 :56: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*

The practical history of the field matches this: "safe operating space" and "planetary boundaries" are nine processes, not one global safe flag (Stockholm Resilience Centre, Planetary boundaries, 2026). The framework's own rule is the reason: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either, it becomes a specific compound. A definition that tries to settle "safe" and "stable" in one sentence invites exactly the non-shrinking disagreement Fig 9 warns about.

Mode: `general`. Triangulated across Fig 9 (union/partition) and the field's own nine-process structure (secondary, low overlap with the framework). Refuter: give one predicate that covers both the threshold and the persistence senses without losing what either would count as evidence, and show that a disagreement about it shrinks with effort. If given, F3 fails.

### F4 - Stability is maintained work, not a solved state (triangulated)

Fig 11 :63-64: **"a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state"**. Fig 11 :73: **"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."** Fig 11 :61: *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."*

The question's premise, "natural conditions have always varied", is exactly the habitat condition Fig 11 describes. The ground does not decay; the channel does (Fig 10 :24). So "stable" cannot mean "nothing changes". It means the loop keeps ranging against a ground that does not move, and that this takes permanent maintenance. The IPCC's Summary for Policymakers says a single year *"can vary above or below the long-term human-induced trend, due to substantial natural variability"* (2021), which is the same observation from a different frame: variability is in the signal, not an error to be averaged out before the definition is made.

Mode: `specified`. Triangulated across Fig 11 (habitat is maintained work) and the IPCC statement on natural variability (independent frame, low overlap). Refuter: exhibit a state of the Earth system that is a solved state, requiring no maintenance and still settled against a non-updating ground. If exhibited, F4 fails.

### F5 - Smoothness and continuum are derivatives, not primitives (triangulated)

Fig 13 :43-44: **"A continuum was the seventh and was removed for failing exactly this: it is a spectrum without gaps, or smoothness over space and time."** Fig 13 :74-75: **"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."** and **"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."** Fig 13 :31-32: *"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos"*.

There is a terminology strain to resolve before this finding can be used. The field phrase "climate states" does **not** name one of the six substrate states (space, time, energy). A climate state is a distribution over space and time, that is, a spectrum-like composition (Fig 13 :46: *"An ordering varying across space gives a distribution, and a distribution is a fact that stays put."*). Re-census: "climate state" is a derivative, not a primitive. Therefore "stability" is a property of the arrangement, not a member of the six. The framework explicitly removed the continuum as a seventh member, so a definition that treats smoothness as a basic given is asking the set to contain what its own members produce.

Mode: `general`. Triangulated across Fig 13 (continuum removed, cross-kind composition) and *How Intelligence Actually Works* (Rule 2: derivatives of cross-kind composition, such as smoothness and continuums, fall out of composition; secondary, same author, so weak corroboration). Refuter: derive a seventh member from the six, or show that the framework admits smoothness as a primitive. If either, F5 fails.

### F6 - "Safe" and "stable" use different bonds (single-source for the figure definition)

Fig 12 :87: **"a threshold relation between two quantities"** (the comparison bond ⋛). Operators table :51: **"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"**. Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**

"Safe" has the shape of a comparison: a quantity against a bound. "Stable" has the shape of a cycle: a loop whose sign decides whether it closes. These are two different bonds, and Fig 12 :94 says *"The arrangement is the substance."* Collapsing them into one word loses the bond, which is the half the flat formula does not record. This is why "safe or stable" is a union (F3) rather than a synonym pair. A further asymmetry: a vocabulary with subtraction but no negation can say what is missing and cannot say what is false (Operators :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*). So "not stable" is not merely "less stable"; it can be a refusal of the term.

Mode: `specified`. Single-source for the figure definition (Fig 12 and Operators are one framework census), with the field's resilience dictionary as independent corroboration that resilience and stability are distinct measures. Refuter: show the two predicates reduce to one bond without losing a prediction. If shown, F6 fails.

### F7 - Describe the failure before choosing the definition (triangulated)

Fig 9 :27-28: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**. Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**. Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."*

If we pick a definition framework first ("resilience", "planetary boundaries", "safe operating space") and then read the Earth system through it, any framework will appear to fit. The remedy is the derive-first order used in this audit: state the residual in effect terms, then see which frame predicts its shape. Fig 9 :32 is also the limit: what comes back is a kind of thing, never the number. The figures cannot return a ppm value or a temperature band; that is `gap`, not silence.

Mode: `general`. Triangulated across Fig 9 (describe before looking) and *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* Refuter: produce a figure line that names a numeric climate threshold directly. None exists after re-census; if one is produced, this finding and the `gap` verdict fall.

### F8 - Agreement among definers cannot settle the definition (triangulated)

Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 2 :40: **"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**. Fig 2 :42: **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."** Fig 10 :59: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying"*.

A consensus process for defining "safe" is a convention, which is useful for settling what the word means and useless for settling what the world is doing. The two must be kept apart. A definition that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention (Fig 10 :47). Where a stake cannot be removed, it must be declared: a definition of "safe" will be advanced by people who care about the answer, and that interest is in the outcome, which is allowed, not in the reading, which is disqualifying.

Mode: `general`. Triangulated across Fig 7 (room agreement) and Fig 2 (shared evidence pool). Refuter: show a defining room whose agreement tracks the ground separately in each member, with no shared pool. If shown, F8 fails.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per Fig 10's discipline and the standing rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A definition whose ground truth is settled by declaration alone is exhibited, and its supposed anchor changes its answer when the defining frame is pushed (breaks F1).
2. A global aggregate is shown that preserves every per-slot live residual, so no signal can disappear into the average (breaks F2).
3. An Earth system state is exhibited that is a solved state requiring no maintenance, while still settled against a non-updating ground (breaks F4).
4. Both predicates are reduced to one bond without losing a prediction (breaks F6, F3).
5. A figure line is found that directly governs a numeric climate threshold (breaks the `gap` verdict and F7).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, nothing here may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Updated verdict

The shape survives: a non-arbitrary definition of "safe" or "stable" is a violable, per-slot, per-disturbance relation to a ground that does not move when the definition is renegotiated, with "safe" read as a comparison and "stable" as maintained work, and with variability kept in the signal rather than averaged away. What does not survive is the request for a single global definition or a number: the framework returns a shape, never a name, so numeric thresholds are `gap`. Strains after re-census: one named union (two bonds), zero hidden, zero unresolved figure contradictions. Mode: `general` plus `specified`, no `grounded` claim.
