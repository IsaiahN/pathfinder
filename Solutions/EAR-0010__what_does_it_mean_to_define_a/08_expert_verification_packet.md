# Expert verification packet: the Tether on a "safe" or "stable" Earth system

**What this is:** a claim in a form a climate or Earth system researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the per-slot claims, nothing `grounded`.** No climate measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A definition of a "safe" or "stable" Earth system cannot be grounded by declaring it. It must be installed as something an instrument could violate, against a ground that does not move when the definition is renegotiated. The label is per slot and per disturbance, because a global average can hide a live signal. "Safe" is a threshold comparison to a bound; "stable" is a cycle whose sign decides whether it closes, maintained by work rather than a fixed point. These are two conditions, not synonyms, and natural variability belongs in the signal rather than being averaged away before defining anything. Smoothness and "climate states" are derivatives, not primitives. The framework returns the shape any candidate definition must have, not a number. Numeric thresholds are a `gap` to be settled by instruments.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - Grounding: a declaration is not a ground (framework-internal, checkable by reading Fig 10 and Fig 2)

- **Statement:** a "safe" label whose truth is settled by agreement alone is a convention that has decided an outcome; it needs to be violable against a non-updating anchor.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :18 *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"*; Fig 10 :19 *"→ install what can be violated"*; Fig 10 :47 *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*; Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
- **What to check:** does the proposed definition specify an observation that could make it false, and is the thing that makes it false independent of the definition? A reference period that is renegotiated to keep the label true fails.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a definition whose ground truth is settled by declaration alone, where the supposed anchor changes its answer when the defining frame is pushed. If one works, C1 fails.

### C2 - Aggregation: the label is per slot (framework-internal plus field-facing)

- **Statement:** a single global "safe" index can read safe while one live slot carries the residual that matters. The habitat's conditions and relations must be enumerated, not composed.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*; Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*; Fig 11 :46 *"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"*; Fig 11 :47 *"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"*; Fig 11 :48 *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."*
- **Field-facing corroboration:** the 2026 Earth System Dynamics paper on spatial patterns of indirect human effects reports *"the risk of information loss inherent in commonly used aggregation approaches."* This is independent of the framework.
- **What to check:** can the recipient name a real index and a real slot where the aggregate would read safe and the slot would not? If they can, the claim is confirmed; if they can show the aggregate preserves every live residual, it is refuted.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a global aggregate that preserves every per-slot live residual, so no signal can disappear into the average. If exhibited, C2 fails.

### C3 - Two bonds: comparison versus maintained cycle (framework-internal, field-facing check)

- **Statement:** "safe" is a threshold comparison, "stable" is a cycle that keeps closing against a ground that does not decay. They are two conditions. Over a shared slot set they are Fig 12 isomers; over different slot sets they are members of one functional group.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :87 *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*; Fig 12 :121 *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*; Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*; Fig 11 :63-64 *"a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state"*.
- **What to check:** does the field actually use "safe" and "stable" as distinct measures? The Stockholm Resilience Centre resilience dictionary defines ecosystem resilience as *"a measure of how much disturbance (like storms, fire or pollutants) an ecosystem can handle without shifting into a qualitatively different state"* and defines regime shifts separately, which supports the distinction.
- **Refuter:** show both predicates reduce to one bond without losing a prediction about what would count as a violation, or exhibit a state that is a solved point requiring no maintenance. If either works, C3 fails.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a number, a ppm threshold, a temperature band, or a reference period. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- Not a single global definition of a safe Earth system.
- Not "stable means nothing changes". The IPCC SPM states a single year *"can vary above or below the long-term human-induced trend, due to substantial natural variability"*.
- Not that "safe" and "stable" are synonyms, and not a definition by committee (Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict."*).
- Not a claim about which climate thresholds are correct. That is a measurement question and this packet does not answer it.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not a claim that "climate states" are one of the six substrate states. They are spectrum-like compositions (Fig 13 :46).
