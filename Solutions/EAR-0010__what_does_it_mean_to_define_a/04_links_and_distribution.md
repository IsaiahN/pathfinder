## What in the wider literature bears directly on this question

1. **The definitional problem is live in the field itself.** The 2026 ESD paper *"To tip or not to tip"* says the literature uses "tipping" in several meanings with *"ambiguous definitions"* and proposes a shared definition. That is a field-facing instance of the framework's own MDL bargain (Fig 5 :42-44), and it corroborates F3 without borrowing the framework's vocabulary.
2. **Aggregation loses information.** A 2026 ESD paper on spatial patterns of indirect human effects reports *"the risk of information loss inherent in commonly used aggregation approaches."* This is a low-overlap corroboration of Fig 1 :22 (*"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average"*).
3. **Natural variability is not measurement noise.** The IPCC SPM explicitly separates single-year variation from the long-term trend. This corroborates the question's premise and F4: the definition must be built on the trend-and-residual structure, not on a still baseline.
4. **Stability has a maintenance component.** The ERL *"derailment risk"* paper makes the social and political conditions part of what sustains a trajectory. That is a field-specific form of Fig 11 :63-64 (*"a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state"*).
5. **The vocabulary itself needs checking.** The resilience dictionary distinguishes resilience from vulnerability and from regime shifts. Using "stable" as if it meant "resilient" would be a source error before any derivation begins.
6. **All framework essays share one author and one evidence pool.** Their agreement with the figures is expected and carries little independent weight (Fig 2 :40). The Earth-system sources above are the genuinely low-overlap witnesses.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 10 :52** - *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Sending a finished definition before the recipient has described the failure sends the source, not the problem.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** - *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the framework.
- **Fig 7 :35** - *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict."* A showing selected for people likely to agree produces a convention, not a check.
- **Fig 2 :42** - *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* The cost of a bad definition of "safe" is high, so spend the effort on independent checkers rather than on a large audience.

Recommended order in a reply: (1) the shape claim and its refuter; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the folder summaries, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1:** send the residual in effect terms and the pre-registered refuter. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict **before** showing any framework material.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the figure text and formula links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because by then they hold the interior (Fig 2 :40; Fig 10 :42).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode. A generator can be given away without giving away an answer (Fig 4 :22).

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. As drafted, the common failure is a single global definition with no violation condition; that does not survive F1 and F2 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`. Repair before sending.
