## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

From *The Library of Babel*: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*

For this question a **shape** is the form a non-arbitrary definition must have, stated before any number is chosen. It says: violable, per slot, per disturbance, grounded on something that does not move when the definition is renegotiated, with the comparison and the persistence senses kept apart. It does not say 350 ppm, 1.5 degrees, or 1850 to 1900. Those are names and numbers, and the framework does not produce them.

The distinction is sharp here because the question asks what a definition **means**, not what value to adopt. `SPACE(???)TIME` is a shape in the exemplar; `SAFE(???)STABLE` is the analogous shape here. A filled-in `SAFE(350 ppm)` would be a name, and a binding from the measurement frame, not a figure derivation.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary already held that is closest to the thing, then recurse on recurring new terms. *"With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."*
- The same essay names the failure mode this question is prone to: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"* That is the figure's union rule (Fig 9 :54) in plain language, and it is why "safe or stable" is split before any definition is written.
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."*
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* Applied here: instead of sending a definition, send what you would expect to observe if the label were true, and let an Earth system researcher check that.
- *Library of Babel* on the anchor: *"you need something to tether and anchor you to reality"* and *"This anchor is provided by your own question."* In this solution the anchor is the residual, not a preferred definition.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays."* A threshold becomes a name when an instrument pays for it, not when it is announced.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test. A number taken from the planetary boundaries literature is an import; it needs its source and the gap it explains recorded.

*They're Scanning Books When They Should* (listed, not freshly quoted here) makes the related point in the canonical set: the real import is a new dimension of the space, not a new term in the existing space. That is why borrowing "resilience" into this answer changes the frame rather than filling a slot.

The practical consequence: "climate states" are not one of the six substrate states. They are spectrum-like compositions over space and time (Fig 13 :46: *"An ordering varying across space gives a distribution, and a distribution is a fact that stays put."*). Naming a "state" here is naming a derivative, which is a binding from the climate frame, not a new primitive.

## 4. The ground and the source problem

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. A colorimeter reads green and *"cannot comment on the source"*. Several sources can give the same reading; *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* And: *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."* It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved the case.

Applied to this question: a healthy-looking global index can be produced by many different underlying slot configurations. Reading "safe" off the index alone is the metamerism error. What is owed is a better discriminator, per slot and per disturbance, not a more confident name. The framework's own limit applies: Fig 9 :32, *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*

Two field-facing checks corroborate this from outside the framework: the 2026 ESD paper on aggregation reports *"the risk of information loss inherent in commonly used aggregation approaches"*, and the resilience dictionary defines regime shifts as *"large, persistent changes in the structure and function of social-ecological systems"*, which is what a global average can hide until it is too late to reverse.

## 5. Answer to the shape question

**What does a definition of "safe" or "stable" mean, under the figures?**

It means a **bounded claim that can be refused**, not a label awarded by agreement. Its parts:

- a **slot** (which process, which region, which variable), because R is indexed per slot (Fig 1 :19);
- a **disturbance** it is supposed to withstand, because valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49);
- a **ground** that does not move when the definition is renegotiated (Fig 2 :29; Fig 10 :18);
- a **mode**: `general` for the structural shape, `specified` for the per-slot claim, nothing `grounded` until an instrument pays;
- a **refuter** written in advance, because a frame cannot check the direction of its own question.

"Safe" has the shape of a comparison to a bound (Fig 12 :87; Operators :51). "Stable" has the shape of a cycle that keeps closing (Fig 12 :121) and of work that keeps being done (Fig 11 :63-64). A single word covering both is a union that must be unbundled (Fig 9 :54).

**What it does not mean:** a fixed baseline, the absence of change, a committee consensus, or a number produced by the framework. The IPCC's own statement that a single year *"can vary above or below the long-term human-induced trend, due to substantial natural variability"* is the field's version of the same refusal of a still baseline.

## 6. Standing of this derivation

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* LLM output simulates an approximate answer. So this reading of "shape" is a **simulation checked for internal consistency against the figures**, not a ground reading. It cannot settle what a real Earth system observation would show. All eight essays share one author and one evidence pool, so their agreement with the figure text is expected and is not independent corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
