# Outreach draft: check one output before deciding whether the framing is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** the stage-1 output below must survive the recipient's check before any framework material is offered. If it does not, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about what the cosmic web can and cannot tell us

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output about large-scale structure that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, not `grounded`):**
>
> 1. The web's structure is a network whose *arrangement* carries the cosmological information. A catalog of galaxy positions is the flat ingredient list; the connectivity, geometry and tidal field are the part that is doing the work.
> 2. That arrangement constrains the *shape* of dark matter's distribution and dark energy's geometric footprint, and it supplies a real consistency test of gravity through lensing versus growth.
> 3. It does not uniquely name a source. A many-to-one reading can narrow the class of sources consistent with it, but several sources give the same reading. So the particle identity of dark matter, and the split between dark energy and modified gravity, stay open until a source-discriminating instrument is applied.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the web determines what dark matter is made of; that void shapes alone distinguish dark energy from modified gravity; that any of this is a measurement I made. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Naming a source from a reading is the error the underlying metamerism argument warns about, and the correct response is a better instrument, not a cleverer interpretation.
>
> **The three verdicts are kept separate, never averaged:** dark matter existence and distribution (triangulated across lensing and surveys), dark energy footprint (single-source from the web until growth data is added), gravity (triangulated across lensing, growth and local tests), and dark-matter particle identity (a declared `gap`, not a hidden answer).
>
> **What would refute this, written down before I sent it:**
> 1. A web observable that uniquely fixes the particle identity of dark matter or the nature of dark energy, with no auxiliary assumption.
> 2. A web-only measurement that breaks the dark-energy versus modified-gravity degeneracy without a second anchor.
> 3. A pattern in which the arrangement carries no information beyond the ingredient catalog.
> 4. A systematic, systematics-surviving divergence between lensing and dynamical potentials that one potential cannot recover.
> 5. One observable with one anchor that settles all three targets at once.
>
> If any of these holds, I retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the public sources. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Tracing the cosmic web (Libeskind et al., MNRAS 2018): https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stx1976
> - Large Scale Structure and the Cosmic Web (Tojeiro & Kraljic, 2025): https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2503.21759
> - A direct empirical proof of the existence of dark matter (Clowe et al., 2006): https://arxiv.org/html/astro-ph/0608407
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the figures and the formula are public. Run them on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [your name]
