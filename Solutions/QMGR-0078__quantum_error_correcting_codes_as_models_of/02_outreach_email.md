**Subject:** Check one shape claim about holography before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the framework's membrane figure says the boundary between two sizes is "not a wall: a filter with a direction," and that "only methods cross up. Only head starts come down" (Fig 4 :21, :22). Applied to AdS/CFT, this predicts the *shape* of what a quantum error-correcting code can supply: an isometry that carries logical (bulk) information to physical (boundary) degrees of freedom, with reconstruction of bulk operators from boundary subregions, and a nonzero round-trip gap off the entanglement wedge. The code is an import from the information frame, with provenance (Almheiri, Dong and Harlow 2014; Pastawski, Yoshida, Harlow and Preskill 2015; Harlow 2016), and it passes a shadow test: it explains radial commutativity, subregion duality and an area law. That is a shape, not the name of a unique theory.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that a tensor network *is* the bulk geometry; that a code proves AdS/CFT; that it yields bulk dynamics; or that it singles out one CFT and one bulk. The authors themselves call these "toy models." My framework returns "a kind of thing, never the answer itself" (Fig 9 :32). Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents and only the record separates them; until provenance is shown the mapping carries zero evidentiary weight.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. show the bulk-boundary relation is a same-level equivalence with no lossy directional filter, so the membrane figure is the wrong figure;
> 2. exhibit a correspondence where the boundary holds only a fixed recording of bulk states, with no reusable reconstruction method;
> 3. show the round-trip gap is zero for an operator outside a subregion's entanglement wedge, or that the area term cannot be recovered from the code;
> 4. derive the encoding map from the CFT closure alone with no imported coding structure;
> 5. single out a unique CFT and bulk pair from code structure with no extra measurement.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape claim is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Harlow, "The Ryu-Takayanagi Formula from Quantum Error Correction" (2016): https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.03901
> - Pastawski, Yoshida, Harlow and Preskill, "Holographic quantum error-correcting codes: Toy models for the bulk/boundary correspondence" (2015): https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.06237
> - Almheiri, Dong and Harlow, "Bulk Locality and Quantum Error Correction in AdS/CFT" (2014): https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.7041
