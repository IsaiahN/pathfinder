# Structure, order and analogues: same species, different web

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6). No field measurement was used.

## RESIDUAL

The word "recovery" is used as if a species list were the whole state of an ecosystem. Two questions are owed: (1) can two food webs with the same species set be different substances under Fig 12's definition, and (2) what order governs the answer, both the dependency order of the investigation and the sign of the feedback loop that closes or flips the web?

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Are two webs with the same species set isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :95: "Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing."
- Fig 12 :46-47: "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."
- Ecological binding, independent: Ings et al. 2009, "it is the distribution of interaction strengths and the configuration of complexity, rather than just its magnitude, that governs network stability and structure." Holling 1973 defines resilience as persistence of *relationships*.
- ⇒ Two webs with identical species lists but different interaction-strength matrices are **isomers** under the figure's definition. A species census is the flat formula that cannot tell them apart. Same cast, different bond, different substance.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a controlled pair of webs shares every species and every measured interaction strength and sign, yet behaves as different substances on all response variables, then the bond adds nothing over the ingredient list and the isomer claim fails.

### Q2: What is the order of the investigation?

Fig 3 gives a dependency order, not an importance order. Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."

1. **Perception** of the habitat, enumerated to the order where the cascade stops mattering (Fig 11 :44). This includes second- and third-order contacts, per the DDT case at Fig 11 :52-53.
2. **Vocabulary** of what a situation affords. Fig 3 :7: "it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked". In ecology: which actors can be introduced, which contacts can be substituted, which bonds can be measured.
3. **Objective**, stated as a comparison. Fig 3 :10: "it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms". The reference configuration is bound here.
4. **Planning** under incomplete information. Only now does suppression, reintroduction or substitution become thinkable.
5. **Learn and carry.** Name what worked, verify it against the ground, and carry the method, not a recording (Fig 4 :22).

The likely break is between steps 2 and 3, because that is the link with no instrument: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at" (Fig 3 :27). A reading taken below that break is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22-23). This predicts that monitoring effort lands on species counts and intervention outcomes while the definition of "recovered" stays implicit.

- **Falsifier:** if a representative set of recovery studies states an operational reference and disturbance before measuring, with a dedicated instrument, then the predicted middle break does not occur there and this claim fails.

### Q3: What is the order *inside* the web?

Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."

A food web is not a chain. Treating it as one is the "free bond" failure of notation: the arrangement is not recorded, so nothing downstream can tell changed arrangements apart. The sign of the feedback loop (reinforcing versus balancing) decides closure or flip. May 1972 supplies the mathematical shape from an independent field: a sharp transition from stable to unstable as connectance or interaction strength crosses a critical value. Scheffer et al. 2009 supply the instrument branch: recovery rate slows near a threshold, which is already a reading from an existing time series (Fig 6 :34: "Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from").

So the order inside the web is **cyclic**, and the quantity that decides is the sign, not the count. Whether the loop recloses on the original reference or settles in a different basin is a ground question, not a figure question.

- **Falsifier:** a web with unchanged loop sign that flips, or reversed sign that recloses, breaks the sign-to-closure claim.

### Q4: What are the analogues, and how far do they travel?

| frame | field | shape carried | overlap with other sources | limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jones, Lawton and Shachak 1994 | ecology | introduction of an actor for its cascade rather than its direct effect | high with Fig 11 (the figure cites it) | an instance, not independent corroboration |
| Hollnagel / Resilience Engineering Association 2019 | engineering | adjust functioning before, during and after events; sustain operations under expected and unexpected conditions | low evidence overlap with ecology | "resilience" is borrowed vocabulary; an organisation has a seat, an unmaintained habitat does not |
| Su and Junge 2023 | business | cope with and recover from adversity by adjusting and preserving functions | low evidence overlap | a firm chooses; D1 does not |
| May 1972 | mathematics | sharp stability-instability transition as connectance or strength crosses a value | low overlap | random-matrix result; natural webs are not random assemblies |
| Anton et al. 2020 | animal behaviour | prey naivety as failure to recognise a novel predator | low overlap | changes a recognition bond, not a species label |
| ecological niche (declined) | ecology | seemingly close to "habitat" | not admitted | the canonical article says "The resemblance is real and the definitions do not align" |

Fig 8 :50: "Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence." The engineering, business, mathematics and behaviour frames are genuinely low-overlap in evidence, so their agreement on shape is weak corroboration, not proof of the ecological instance. The niche mapping is refused on the source's own authority.

### Q5: How is a removal written?

Not as subtraction. Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." The two silent failures are "is invisible until the goal fails" (what you failed to reproduce) and "is invisible until it acts" (what you unintentionally introduced). In bond terms: an invader's removal deletes an actor from the ingredient list, but the bonds that actor carried either reattach to other actors or disappear with no substitute, and only the reference comparison reveals which.

- **Falsifier:** a case where removal genuinely removes a contact with no substitution and the reference is regained with no introduction. That breaks the asymmetry law.

## CONCLUSION

Two webs with one species set and different interaction-strength matrices are **isomers** under Fig 12. The investigation is ordered by dependency (Fig 3), with the likely break at the objective link, and the web itself is ordered by cyclic feedback whose sign decides closure or flip (Fig 12 :121). Removal is substitution, not subtraction (Fig 11 :66). No new primitive is required for any of this; what is required is a declared reference, a per-slot instrument, and a recorded provenance for every binding from a world frame.

This rescues the checkable part of the question without pretending the figures give a universal recovery rule. `metric = bond` style bindings are provenance obligations; the isomer and cycle claims are structural.
