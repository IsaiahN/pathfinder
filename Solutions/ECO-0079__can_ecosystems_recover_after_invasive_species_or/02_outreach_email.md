# Outreach draft: check the output before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. The field for these contact points is ecosystem recovery research.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its five refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are stated plainly and the recipient has had a chance to answer stage 1. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about "recovery" after a food web is altered

> Hi [name],
>
> I am writing about one specific output, not about a theory. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree that ecosystems recover. I am asking what you would count as a failure of the claim below. If it fails, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** "Recovery" after an invasive species or pathogen rewires a food web is not a property of the ecosystem. It is an ordered comparison against a reference configuration that has to be stated, measured per object slot, at the level of bonds (which species interact, at what strength and sign), not at the level of a species list. Contact has to be enumerated outward until the cascade stops mattering, because the residual that matters can be three steps out. In the unmaintained case what survives is what the ground kept, so "recovered" is a label applied afterwards. In the managed case the seat can introduce and cannot subtract: eradication substitutes a habitat rather than removing one, and the work is permanent rather than a solved state. Whether the web recloses or flips is decided by the sign of the feedback loop. This is a shape and a route, not a measurement and not a universal yes or no.
>
> **What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming that ecosystems always recover, that they never recover, or that any particular invasion has a known outcome. I am not giving a threshold. I am not saying the invader is bad by kind: the same actor can be a benefactor to one reference and a malfactor to another. I am not equating the framework's "habitat" with an ecological niche; the source article explicitly declines that mapping. And I am not treating agreement from several ecologists as corroboration if all of them read the same literature.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim.** I wrote these down before this email:
> 1. Recovery is established and reproduced with no stated reference configuration or disturbance, as an intrinsic property of the system.
> 2. The species list and every measured interaction strength return to the reference, and the system still behaves as a different substance on every response variable, so measuring bonds buys nothing.
> 3. An aggregate index predicts every per-slot failure as well as the per-slot time series does, so decomposition is unnecessary.
> 4. Removing an actor genuinely removes a contact with no substituted habitat and no introduction, and the reference is regained with no further seat action.
> 5. The feedback sign is unchanged and the web flips, or the sign reverses and the web recloses.
>
> If any one holds, I retract the corresponding part. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **Stage 1 question:** what would you expect to see in your system if this shape were right, and what observation would make you reject it? I will record your answer before showing you any theory.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Figure text extract with line numbers (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`. Fig 11 (The Habitat) and Fig 12 (The Bond) carry most of the claim.
> - Canonical usage rule (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`.
> - The formula and its mode rule (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** run it on a system you know and can measure. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state whether your output is `general`, `specified`, or `grounded`. The source article warns that outputs generated this way can be "contrived to fit the formula", so a reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
