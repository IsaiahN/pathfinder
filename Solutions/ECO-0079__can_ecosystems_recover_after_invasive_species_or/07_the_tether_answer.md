# The Tether's answer: can ecosystems recover after a food web is altered?

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural rules, `gap` on the universal yes/no. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading. No ecosystem was measured.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks whether an interaction network returns to a reference after an invasive species or pathogen rewires it. It is a **union compound**, not one hard question: an unmaintained habitat runs selection with no seat, and a managed habitat runs a seat that adds to a habitat it did not author (Fig 9 :54; Fig 11 :63-64). A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either, so the two get separate verdicts and are never averaged.

## 2. The shape, per domain

### D1, unmaintained

1. Nothing selects; "nothing selects; what survives is what the ground kept" (Fig 11 :14-15). "recovered" is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground (Fig 11 :36), not a state the ecosystem is trying to reach.
2. The description still constrains: bonds, not species, are the substance. Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
3. Contact cascades. Fig 11 :52-53: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."

### D2, maintained

4. The seat's power is asymmetric: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." (Fig 11 :61). Removal is "substitution of one habitat for another" (Fig 11 :66), with two invisible failures: what you failed to reproduce and what you unintentionally introduced (Fig 11 :68-69).
5. "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state." (Fig 11 :63). Recovery is a maintained condition with an ongoing bill.
6. Two currencies, not one: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds." (Fig 12 :126). A cheap species inventory and an expensive third-order measurement are not interchangeable.

### D3, measurement

7. Recovery is an ordered comparison against a stated reference, per slot (Fig 3 :10; Fig 1 :19). "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." (Fig 1 :22). A global index near zero with one live slot is a legal state.
8. The sign of the loop decides whether the web recloses or flips: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." (Fig 12 :121). The threshold is ground-only.
9. The actors' valence is relative to the residual: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." (Fig 11 :50). No permanent label of "good" or "invasive" survives a change of reference.

### The universal question

10. **No figure line gives a recovery threshold.** This is `gap` in the exact sense of Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,". The route is SEARCHED first, then INWARD from an instrument already returning a recovery rate, then OUTWARD to the resilience and invasion-science frames with a source gate and a shadow test (Formula step 7; Fig 6 :34). Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "ecosystems recover" and not "they do not". No universal yes/no is figure-derived.
- Not that eradication restores a web. It substitutes a habitat.
- Not that the invader is bad by kind. Valence is relative to the stated residual.
- Not that a species list, a diversity index, or an ecosystem-health score is the state variable. Those are projections with loss.
- Not that the habitat here is the ecological niche. The canonical article declines that mapping: "The resemblance is real and the definitions do not align".
- Not `grounded`. No measurement at any site settles `left(R,φ)`.
- Not a claim that expert agreement corroborates. If the frames share a literature, "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool" (Fig 2 :40).

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a reading that several paths produce identically "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

- **Reference test:** state the reference configuration and disturbance before the field season, and ask whether the observed return is to that reference or to a different basin with the same cast.
- **Bond test:** measure interaction strengths and signs per slot, not presence and absence. Two isomer webs must be distinguishable by the instrument.
- **Cascade test:** enumerate to the order where the residual stops mattering; verify that a third-order effect was not missed by first-order scoping.
- **Sign test:** estimate the feedback loop sign before and after; does it reclose or flip?
- **Seat test:** if a manager is acting, record what was introduced, what was substituted, and the continuing cost. A claimed solved state fails this test.
- **Standing refuters** from `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §5: reference-free recovery, substance-free recovery, aggregate sufficiency, true subtraction, signless flip.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The figures return the shape any answer must have, plus the refuters and the route. They do not return the ecology of any particular site. The beta of an instrument, the choice of reference and the cascade cutoff are declared by the field frame and recorded as such. Indiscernibility is not identity: a web that looks recovered on a species census has not been shown to be recovered on bonds.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer yes or no to whether ecosystems recover, because that question is a union of two different questions. In an unmaintained habitat nothing chooses and what survives is what the ground kept, so "recovered" is a label applied afterwards. Where a manager acts, the seat can introduce and cannot subtract, so eradication substitutes a habitat rather than restoring one and the work is permanent. In both cases the thing that returns, if anything does, is not a species list but the bonds between species: two webs can share every species and still be different substances, and the residual can be three steps out from the actor that was introduced. Recovery is therefore a per-slot comparison against a reference you have to state, decided by the sign of the feedback loop, and measured with an instrument that can tell a restored web from a different web with the same cast. The figures give that shape and five ways to refute it. They give no universal recovery threshold, and the question of any actual ecosystem is left to the ground.
