## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Union or partition?

So this is a union with a specific compound at the intersection, not three instances of one failure. **Triangulated** (Fig 9 and Fig 3 dependency order).

### Q2 — Are the two frames isomers, or members of a functional group?

Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

Fig 12 :124 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

As written, `F` and `L` do not share an atom set: `F` holds instruments, models and statistics; `L` holds a lifetime of place-based discriminations. So they are **not isomers** under Fig 12's definition. What they share is a functional group: both are frames that range against the same ground and return a residual. Fig 12 :124 says the group gives the kind and the qualifier gives the member. The qualifier here is the closure: which atoms each frame can compose from.

The stronger claim is available but unproven: if both are rewritten over a full shared atom set (the same ecosystem, the same seasons, the same species), differing only in which discrimination mediates which observation, then they are **isomers** in Fig 12's sense. That is a real checkable structural claim, and it is the one an expert can test. As in the physics exemplar, the weaker functional-group reading is what the record actually supports; the isomer reading is the stronger version worth testing.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if either frame can be described without an atom the other requires, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. Triangulated for the definition (Fig 12), single-source for the mapping onto these two frames.

### Q3 — What is the order of composition?

Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."

The dependency order is:

1. **Describe the residual in effect terms.** Fig 9 :27-28 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Without this, any chosen frame fits.
2. **SEARCHED.** Is `p` already in `closure(Γ_f)` and unfound? Fig 8 :41-43 "Underivable: go outside. Unreachable-at-budget: the term is already in the closure and was not found, so the remedy is more search, not another atom." This is asked first because it is cheapest and the other two assume its answer.
3. **INWARD.** Extend the formal instrument from one already returning something (Fig 6 :32-33). The practitioner's discrimination is that something.
4. **OUTWARD.** Import the pattern from `L` with a source gate, a shadow test, and a debit against independence (Fig 8, Seat Map). Never read INWARD against OUTWARD (Fig 6 :31).
5. **Settle separately.** Each frame ranges against the ground on its own; agreement is then a check only if independence was real (Fig 2 :45-46).

Optimising step 4 cannot rescue a break at step 1 (Fig 3 :19). This is the order that the outreach artifact respects.

### Q4 — What is lost in the round trip, and how is it measured?

Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."

Fig 4 :31-32 "The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold, and it is the only honest report of how much was given up."

Fig 8 :51 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"

The abstraction `T_A` takes a local observation up into a formal claim. The concretisation `T_E` brings that claim back to a specific place and season. The round-trip gap `R_T` is what the formal claim cannot carry back. *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* gives a way to measure it in practice: "The size of a correction tells you how much of the knowledge is still tacit for that learner, and it should shrink as their experience builds. That makes tacit transfer measurable: watch the trend in correction size, not the count of lessons given." A shrinking correction trend is an instrument; a count of extracted interviews is not.

The walrus study is a world instance of measuring this gap rather than ignoring it: it defined the spatial and temporal limits of what participants had actually observed, then used internal validation workshops to exclude observations participants could not confirm. Without that step a map of walrus distribution would have been a map of hunter effort, and the paper says so explicitly.

### Q5 — Can the local frame be subtracted after import?

Fig 11 :61 "Introduction is available; exclusion is not."

The figure governs a seat's power over a habitat: an actor can be added, but the habitat cannot be un-made by decree, because contact is a fact about the world. Applied carefully here, it means an imported discrimination can be added to a formal frame but the practitioners' habitat cannot be removed by declaring the knowledge extracted. It does **not** mean a formal institution may take knowledge without consent. That is an ethical and legal condition on the import gate, not a figure derivation, and it is stated as such in `07_the_tether_answer.md`.

**Falsifier for Q5:** if a figure line is produced that grants a seat exclusion power over a habitat, this reading is withdrawn.

## CONCLUSION

The two frames are a functional group (both range against the ground), not isomers as described; the stronger isomer claim requires a full shared atom set and has a stated falsifier. Their composition is ordered: describe the residual, SEARCHED, INWARD, OUTWARD, settle separately. The compression from local observation to formal claim is lossy, and the loss is measured by the round-trip gap `R_T`, operationally by the trend in corrections rather than the volume of extracted statements. No new primitive is needed for any of this, and no import is declared against a budget limit.
