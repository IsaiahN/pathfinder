# Isomers and order: the cycle as a bond structure

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading. It supports `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` and `07_the_tether_answer.md`; it introduces no new primitive.

## RESIDUAL

The question asks whether heating and cooling can concentrate molecules and encourage reactions. Three readings were conflated in the question: transport, kinetics, and a record across the cycle boundary. Before any figure line is chosen, the residual is described in effect terms: arity three, symmetry ordered (heating and cooling are not interchangeable), scale cross-scale (a macroscopic forcing driving molecular contact).

Three supporting questions are owed:

- Q1. Is the cycle a sequence bond, or a symmetric join?
- Q2. Is temperature a sixth primitive, or a habitat condition and an ordering over energy?
- Q3. Are two cycling regimes with the same ingredients isomers under Fig 12, and what would falsify that?
- Q4. What analogue survives re-census, and which first instinct is retracted?

## DERIVATION

### Q1. The cycle is a sequence bond, not a symmetric join

Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." The Operators table defines → as "A then B; B needs A to have happened" with the test "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes." Under the symmetric bond +, by contrast, Fig 12 :92 says "Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term", so order carries no information.

Heating and cooling pass the ordered-bond test. Swapping them changes the process: evaporation removes solvent but not solute; freezing excludes ions into brine and thawing releases them; a surface adsorbs on one half and releases on the other. So the thermal cycle is written with → (sequence), not + (conjunction). Its sign is part of the term. Fig 13 :31: "each turn leaves a remainder (the residual) that re-enters the next". A cycle whose two halves return the system exactly to its start closes the loop and leaves no remainder; its net accumulation per cycle is zero. Concentration requires the remainder to be nonzero and to re-enter.

The bond is priced, not free. Fig 12 :43-44: "one bond type costs nothing; seven cost 2.8 bits each" and "the arrangement is priced or it is free". Calling a thermal cycle "heat plus cold" records the ingredients and drops the arrangement, which is the flat-formula error the figure names.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** exhibit a cycle whose heating and cooling halves are interchangeable under Fig 12's swap test and that nevertheless produces net accumulation of a concentrating species per cycle. If one exists, the sequence-bond reading is wrong and the cycle must be re-censused as a symmetric join.

### Q2. Temperature is not a sixth primitive

Fig 13 :3: "No system functions without any of them, none is built from the others, and everything else is a composition across the two kinds." Fig 13 :43: "A continuum was the seventh and was removed for failing exactly this". My first reading wanted to add temperature as a seventh member because it is everywhere in the question. That reading is **retracted**.

Fig 11 :46 lists temperature as a habitat condition: "conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain". Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Temperature is read off the world, not minted inside Γ. In the six-term set, energy is the state ("conserved, finite, directionless alone") and spectrum is the structure that gives ordering ("ordering, so more and less can be said"). A temperature is an ordering over energy states, so it is spectrum-like. A *temperature difference that can be spent* is gradient-like, and gradient is already held: Fig 13 :22 "a difference that can be spent" and Fig 13 :46 "What makes a gradient a gradient is that it can be spent, and neither an ordering nor a span of space has directedness."

Fig 12 :13-14 gives the reason heating alone cannot do the work: "three different changes, one written formula" and "the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not". Melt, freeze and boil share the ingredients temperature and state; what distinguishes them is the arrangement, which the flat formula drops. The cycle's effect lives in the arrangement and in what survives it, not in a new primitive named temperature.

**Falsifier:** derive temperature from the other five and show it is redundant, or show that a thermal cycle requires a seventh member that none of the six can compose. Either would break the six-term substrate and force a re-census. Until one fires, no primitive is added.

### Q3. Are the two regimes isomers?

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :95-96: "Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."

Take two regimes with the same ingredient set: heat, cold, solvent, solute, mineral or mica surface. Regime A is ordered heat-then-cold with the product retained on the surface into the next turn. Regime B is the same heating and cooling with the product fully dispersed between turns, so the record is dropped. Under Fig 12 these are **isomers**, not variants of one regime: same atoms, different bond structure (retained sequence versus broken sequence), different behaviour (compounding concentration versus re-paying the full cost each cycle). Fig 12 :109 states the boundary: "Which boundary matters is the one the record does not cross. Inside a run the arrangement persists and nothing is re-earned; across a boundary that drops it, everything is."

This is a checkable structural claim, mode `specified`, single-source for the figure definition. It is corroborated in the world at low overlap by Ross and Deamer 2016 ("simple Le Châtelier's principle shifts were insufficient, but the introduction of oligomer-stabilizing factors of 5-10 kcal/mol both moved the process to the right...") and by Eiby et al 2026 ("a robust prebiotic selection mechanism is required to concentrate these monomers and maintain their close proximity"). Those two share no framework author, but overlap partly with each other through the Deamer and Hassenkam clusters; they are recorded as witnesses, not as the verdict.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** show a regime that drops the record between cycles (product fully dispersed, no surface or pore retention) accumulating product across cycles at the same rate as a retention-enabled control. That would collapse the isomer distinction and break Fig 12 :109 as applied here.

### Q4. Analogue after re-census, and a retracted first instinct

My first instinct was to use the Fig 4 membrane to govern cycle-to-cycle retention: a head start is sent down and a method is carried up. It **strains**. Fig 4 :22 says "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Fig 4 governs crossings between *sizes*, between the one-agent and the population scale. Cycle-to-cycle retention here is a record *within one scale*, governed by Fig 12 :107-110. Re-census sends retention to Fig 12 and demotes Fig 4 to an analogue: the round-trip discipline still applies, because what is carried between descriptions loses information. Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."

The surviving analogue is the Brownian ratchet, a world source with different authors and a different field. Its own text says "if the entire device is at the same temperature, the ratchet will not rotate continuously in one direction but will move randomly back and forth, and therefore will not produce any useful work." Cast in effect terms: a cycle plus asymmetry plus a temperature difference gives directed transport; a cycle at uniform temperature gives none. It corroborates Q1's sequence/sign reading and Q2's spendable-gradient reading. It does **not** triangulate them as a second figure; it is one low-overlap world witness (Fig 2 :40).

**Falsifier for the analogue:** exhibit a physical ratchet at uniform temperature producing net directed transport with no asymmetry and no external source. That would remove the corroboration; it would not by itself change the figure lines.

## CONCLUSION

The cycle is a sequence bond with a sign, not a symmetric join. Temperature is a habitat condition and an ordering over energy, not a seventh primitive. Two regimes that differ only in whether the product crosses the cycle boundary are Fig 12 isomers, and the retention regime is the one that compounds. Fig 4 survives only as a round-trip discipline, and the Brownian ratchet is a low-overlap world witness, not a second figure. Every claim here is `specified` and ungrounded; the refuters above are written before the run and stay live.
