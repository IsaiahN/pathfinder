## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, gap(x, (T_E composed with T_A)(x)), measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. **R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **identity of the asymmetry**. "Ratchet" names a shape, not an asymmetry. Evaporation retaining solute, freezing excluding ions into brine and a surface adsorbing one species are three different instantiations. The shorthand cannot choose among them; it would have to drop one to become concrete.
- the **identity of the recording substrate**. A mineral surface, a pore and a film are different habitats with different anchors. Fig 12 :109 says which boundary matters, but not which physical boundary it is here.
- the **temperature regimen and water chemistry**. Fig 11 :46 enumerates temperature and terrain but does not select a value. The world evidence shows that 80 °C versus room temperature, and one Icelandic water versus another, flip the sign of the effect. That discrimination is entirely absent from the shorthand.

Conversely, two things survive cleanly: the union of three conditions, and the requirement that the loop leave a remainder that crosses a boundary and is recorded there. Those are the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "a working cycle needs a ratchet and a record" as identical to the figures would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
