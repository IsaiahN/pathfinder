**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until a checker can see the three refuters in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` without first reading the framework. If the stage-1 output does not stand on its own, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** Check one structural claim about thermal cycling before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I am writing about a claim in origins-of-life chemistry, and I would like you to check one output against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The question "could natural heating and cooling cycles concentrate molecules and encourage reactions?" is not one question. It is three conditions joined: (1) concentration, a transport and separation effect; (2) reaction promotion, a kinetics effect; and (3) retention of the product between cycles, which is what lets the next cycle start from what the last one built. A single lever that clears one condition can leave the others standing.
>
> From that split, three necessary structural conditions follow for any cycle that actually concentrates:
>
> 1. **The loop must not close.** A perfectly reversible thermal cycle returns the system to its starting state, so its net accumulation per cycle is zero. Concentration requires an asymmetry between the hot and cold halves, something retained on one side and not returned on the other. The figures put it this way: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
> 2. **There must be a spendable gradient.** A bare difference in temperature is an ordering. It drives transport or activation only when it has directedness that can be spent, for example through evaporation, phase separation, or a surface that holds one species.
> 3. **There must be a substrate that records the product between cycles.** Where the arrangement is retained, the next cycle starts from a head start and the effect compounds. Where it is dropped and everything diffuses back to bulk, every cycle starts from zero and pays the full cost again.
>
> I am not claiming that a particular setting (a hot spring, a tidal pool, snow) is the answer. The framework returns a shape, not a name, so the setting has to be identified by your ground, not by mine. I am also not claiming that heating alone increases contact. Concentration increases contact; heating by itself does not.
>
> **What would refute the claim.** I wrote these down before sending this:
>
> 1. A perfectly symmetric cycle, with no retained asymmetry, still produces net accumulation per cycle.
> 2. A system that drops its record between cycles accumulates product as fast as one that retains it.
> 3. A controlled cycling experiment shows concentration and reaction with no spendable gradient and no regenerating surface.
>
> If any holds, the shape claim is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed. If you do reply, I would like your verdict on stage 1 before I show you any theory.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figure text with line numbers (local copy in the package): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures and formula for direct download (branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - The architecture essay: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Two-stage offer, staged explicitly

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual, the three conditions and the refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record that verdict before any theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If the recipient is curious, offer the three links, with the note that the figures, not our summary, are the authority. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool").
3. **Stage 3, opt-in only.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode. Sending a method is permitted by Fig 4 :22. Sending an answer is not.

**Hard precondition repeated:** stages 1 to 3 work only if the stage-1 output survives a check. That is what `08_expert_verification_packet.md` is for. Do not attach the whole architecture to the first message.
