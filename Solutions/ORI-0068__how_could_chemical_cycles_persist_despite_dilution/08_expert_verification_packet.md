# Expert verification packet - the Tether on chemical-cycle persistence

**What this is:** a claim in a form a prebiotic chemist, geochemist, or origins-of-life researcher can
check. Mode, derivation, and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to
look for and what would settle it. This is **the one to send**.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the dependency order, `gap` on thresholds. Not
`grounded`.** No early-Earth measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground
reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A chemical cycle persists only as a relation with three things: a reaction set closed under its own
products with a part that survives its own use; a difference that can be spent, measured as a flux
in the specific habitat; and a habitat that keeps supplying actors and lets selection run. Dilution
is loss of contact with actors, degradation is a reaction at a rate the gradient sets, and changing
conditions are habitat changes, not changes in what settles the question. Encapsulation substitutes
a habitat; it does not remove one. The framework returns that shape, not the name of a molecule or a
site, and no figure line gives a quantitative loss threshold.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - structural; checkable by reading Fig 12 and by inspecting a reaction network

- **Statement:** a persisting cycle is a closure under its own products with a regenerated catalyst.
  A one-through chain cannot close by construction, and a spent catalyst behaves differently across
  turnovers in a way a flat species list does not record.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :121, "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop
  decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Fig 12 :119, "catalyst. A
  part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and
  nothing records which a term is."
- **What to check:** is the object being discussed a closed reaction set or a molecular trajectory?
  In the latter case the figure line is being strained and the object must be re-censused.
- **Refuter:** a linear reaction set, with no product re-entering as input and no regenerated
  catalyst, holds a steady product population indefinitely under the same measured loss rates as a
  matched closed cycle. If that is shown, C1 fails.

### C2 - thermodynamic; checkable against the specific setting's measurements

- **Statement:** a spendable difference is required. Energy supplied without a gradient is
  directionless on its own. The anchor is a measured flux, not a universal dissipation bound.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :46-47, "What makes a gradient a gradient is that it can be spent, and
  neither an ordering nor a span of space has directedness." Fig 13 :76, "Energy and a gradient
  compose a flow."
- **What to check:** is there a measured pH, redox, thermal, or concentration gradient in the
  setting, and does it decline when closure declines? England 2013 and Kolchinsky 2024 conflict on
  whether dissipation universally bounds replication; that conflict is noted, and neither side is
  used as the anchor here.
- **Refuter:** a cycle holds closure across repeated turnovers at zero measured spendable gradient,
  using an instrument that would register a flux if one were present. If that is shown, C2 fails.

### C3 - habitat and ground; checkable against a coupled-versus-isolated experiment

- **Statement:** persistence is a property of cycle and habitat together; changing conditions move
  the habitat, not the ground; isolation substitutes a habitat and inherits its unchosen actors.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39, "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent
  alone." Fig 11 :40, "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it
  improves." Fig 10 :24, "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Fig 11 :66, "Isolation is not
  removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Fig 7 :35, "A room's
  agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."
- **What to check:** does the proposed setting keep supplying actors across the perturbation
  schedule; does an encapsulated system depend on which actors and conditions came with the new
  habitat; is there a measurable difference between coupled and isolated systems in product
  survival and reuse?
- **Refuter:** persistence in an isolated, unchanging habitat equals persistence in a replenished
  habitat with selection acting, under the same perturbation schedule and starting chemistry. Or:
  the settling quantity moves when the claim is re-described. If either is shown, C3 fails.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a theory of everything and not a final mechanism. The union compound keeps its own loop.
- Not the name of a molecule, vent, pool, or epoch. The framework returns "a kind of thing, never
  the answer itself" (Fig 9 :32).
- Not that membranes abolished dilution. Isolation "is substitution of one habitat for another"
  (Fig 11 :66).
- Not a universal thermodynamic bound. The world literature conflicts on that and the conflict is
  recorded rather than resolved by preference.
- Not a quantitative dilution threshold, hydrolysis constant, or turnover count. Those are a stated
  `gap` (Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence.").
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here has been settled by an experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

An instrument, not an argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads
green "cannot comment on the source" - the spectrometer resolved the case, not a debate.

1. **Closure:** coupled-versus-isolated reactors with matched feedstock, tracking whether the set
   stays closed under its own products and whether the catalyst is regenerated, across a controlled
   loss schedule.
2. **Flux:** an instrument that would register a spendable gradient if present, testing closure at
   zero measured gradient.
3. **Habitat:** a substitution panel in which the actors and conditions carried into a new habitat
   are varied independently, measuring product survival and reuse.

Kato 2026 proposes test 1 explicitly and states no experiments were run. Its proposal is a ground
shape, not a ground reading.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 12 :119 and :121, Fig 13 :46-47 and :76, Fig 11 :39-40 and :66, Fig 10 :24, and Fig 7
   :35. These are short passages; check them against the verbatim extract, not a summary.
2. Check C1 against a reaction network you know: is it a closure, or a chain written as a list?
3. Name one instrument in your setting that would register a spendable gradient, or say why none is
   possible.
4. Say what you would count as a failure. A refutation is more useful than an endorsement.
5. Send the failure. Do not send agreement; agreement from a shared evidence pool is one witness
   talking (Fig 2 :40).
