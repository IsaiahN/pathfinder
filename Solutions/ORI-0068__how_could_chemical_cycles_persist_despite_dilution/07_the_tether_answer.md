# The Tether's answer on chemical-cycle persistence

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the dependency order, `gap` on quantitative
thresholds. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked
against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Dilution removes actors from contact, degradation transforms products and bonds, and changing
conditions move the habitat. Fig 9 :55 calls this kind of object "a bundled problem is usually a
union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." It is a union compound with three
slots, not one hard problem. Each slot has its own loop and its own ground, so verdicts are kept
separate and never averaged (Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated.").

## 2. The shape

1. **A persisting cycle is a closure, not a chain.** Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as
   input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
   The relevant object is the reaction set closed under its own products, not one molecule going
   round. A catalyst is a part that survives its own use; a spent part behaves differently across
   turnovers and nothing in a flat formula records which it is.
2. **It must be driven by a difference that can be spent.** Fig 13 :14: "Conserved, finite,
   directionless alone." Fig 13 :22: "a difference that can be spent." Energy without a gradient is
   not enough. The anchor is a measured flux in the specific habitat, not a universal dissipation
   bound; the world literature conflicts on that bound and the conflict is recorded, not used to
   flip this verdict.
3. **Persistence belongs to cycle and habitat together.** Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of
   agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Dilution is a loss of contact, so the habitat is
   "enumerated, never composed." In an unmaintained setting there is no seat, so selection, not
   design, decides what remains: Fig 10 :24, "the ground does not decay, the channel does."
4. **Encapsulation substitutes a habitat; it does not remove one.** Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not
   removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." A membrane moves the
   residual into a new habitat with actors that were not chosen, with two silent failure modes: what
   failed to be reproduced and what was unintentionally brought along.
5. **The second order needs no new primitive.** Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for
   whatever composes over it." Once settled, a cycle can be an operand for habitat-level selection.
   Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search,
   however long, adds an atom."
6. **The framework returns a shape, not a name.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the
   answer itself." It does not say which molecule or which setting. A particular vent or pool is a
   provenanced import from a world frame, not a derivation.
7. **The quantitative thresholds are a gap.** Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a
   proof of absence." No figure line gives a critical dilution rate or hydrolysis constant. That is
   unreached at this budget, not declared unreachable in principle.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a theory of everything and not a final mechanism. The union compound keeps its own loop.
- Not a claim that a particular molecule, mineral, or site was the answer.
- Not a claim that membranes abolished dilution. They substituted a habitat.
- Not a derivation of a loss rate, a threshold, or a universal thermodynamic inequality.
- Not a claim that this derivation is a ground reading. It is a simulation.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement at any early-Earth analogue settles `left(R,φ)` here.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

An instrument that discriminates the three slots, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About
Source* gives the pattern: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source," and the
spectrometer, not a debate, resolved the case. Here the corresponding questions are:

- **Closure test:** in a coupled-versus-isolated reactor with matched feedstock, does the reaction
  set remain closed under its own products across repeated turnovers, and is the catalyst regenerated
  or spent? The linear-versus-cycle refuter in `01` §5 fires here.
- **Flux test:** with an instrument that would register a spendable gradient if present, does closure
  persist at zero measured gradient? If it does, F3 fails.
- **Habitat test:** does persistence in an isolated, unchanging habitat equal persistence in a
  replenished habitat where selection can act, under the same perturbation schedule? If it does,
  F4 fails.
- **Anchor test:** does the thing that settles persistence move when a frame re-describes it? If it
  does, agreement has replaced the ground and F2 fails.

Kato 2026 proposes the coupled-versus-isolated reactor explicitly as a test and states that no
experiments were run. That is a ground-shaped proposal, not a result.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence." The
six-term substrate is "a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes"; declaring it makes
the choice visible, not neutral. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and
records the lineage so the stop is checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity" applies both to the
shape and to any future molecule that seems to fill it.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name the molecule that made chemical cycles persist, and it does not claim a
> final mechanism. It says such a cycle must be a closure under its own products rather than a
> one-through chain, with a part that is regenerated rather than spent; that it must be driven by a
> difference that can be spent, with a measured flux as the anchor rather than a universal bound;
> and that persistence belongs to the cycle and its habitat together, because dilution is a loss of
> contact and encapsulation substitutes a habitat instead of removing one. Changing conditions alter
> the habitat, not the ground. What it offers is the shape any candidate answer must have, four
> refuters written in advance, and the experiments that would close it. The quantitative thresholds
> stay open until a reactor settles them.
