# Outreach draft: check the separation before deciding about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the stage-1 output below survives a check by someone who has not read the framework. If it does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about definitions of intelligence, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output about definitions of intelligence that I would like you to check against what you already know from cross-cultural intelligence research. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a definition of intelligence is a convention, and cultural assumptions about rationality, creativity and autonomy are the terms that a community uses to build that convention. That part is legitimate: agreement about a shared meaning is the product of a convention, not a corruption of it. The figures I am working with put it this way: *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"* (Fig 2 :41). The other half of the output is that the same agreement cannot settle a capability question. The same source says *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure"* (Fig 2 :36-38). And it replaces the definitional question outright: *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"* (Fig 11 :39).
>
> So the shape claim is: cultural assumptions shape the label by supplying its terms, fix which slots get measured, and can even confer standing by agreement; none of that changes contact with a habitat. When a definition is used to decide who is capable, it stops being a convention and becomes an undeclared verdict.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that this produces a new definition of intelligence, that cultural variation makes capability undefined, that rationality or creativity or autonomy is a single lever, or that a culturally agreed definition is worthless. The framework returns a shape, not a name. I also am not claiming this is settled by experiment. It is a `specified` structural claim.
>
> **What would refute it, written down before I sent this:**
> 1. an agent-only improvement that moves the ground, or a definition change with no habitat change that moves a capability measurement;
> 2. a community's definition shown to be independent of what it values in rationality, creativity and autonomy, measured with an instrument that does not borrow the community's own vocabulary;
> 3. a single-scale definition that preserves per-slot residuals which then change a ground reading;
> 4. one cultural variable that predicts all three definitional effects with no interaction terms.
>
> If any one holds, I will withdraw the relevant part. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **Question for you first, before any theory:** what would you expect to see in the literature if the separation between convention and capability were false? I want your verdict on stage 1 recorded before I show you where the claim came from.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the source material is below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
