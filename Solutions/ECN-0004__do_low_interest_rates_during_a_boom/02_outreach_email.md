# Outreach draft: check the separation before deciding which school you belong to

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the two live strains in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 8 are understood by the sender. The offer must survive a check or the two-stage structure collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One separation to check before we argue about rates and recessions

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one narrow output to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to
> endorse either the Austrian or the demand-shock explanation of recessions, and I am not
> asking you to read a theory first. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else
> to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the separation, `general` on the shape, not `grounded`):** the
> question "do low rates during a boom cause malinvestment, or are recessions better
> explained by demand shortfalls and financial shocks?" is not one question with two
> answers. It is a union of overlapping conditions with different anchors. The policy rate
> is moved by the policy maker whose action is under examination, so by the anchor test it
> cannot be the thing that settles whether the action was a mistake. The comparator both
> sides need, the rate that would have prevailed without the policy and the spending level
> that would have prevailed without the shock, is not observed by either side. The residual
> must be indexed per project, because an average over all projects loses the only signal
> that says which one was wrong.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that low rates never distort investment; that malinvestment is
> a meaningless word; that demand shortfalls and credit breaks do not matter; that I have
> measured a natural rate or a potential-output gap; and that either school has won. The
> honest result is a separation plus a gap. Both schools need an instrument that does not
> update on the school holding it. Until such an instrument resolves the case, the verdict
> on which mechanism dominated a given episode is unreachable, and I am reporting that as
> a verdict, not as a silence.
>
> **What would refute the separation (written before this run):**
> 1. a figure line or a measurement that returns which explanation wins in general, making
>    the union reading unnecessary;
> 2. project-level failure and aggregate shortfall shown to be one quantity under one
>    instrument, so that indexing per slot adds nothing;
> 3. an independently fixed comparator with bands narrow enough to classify boom-time rate
>    gaps, showing either no relation to later project failure or a relation that makes the
>    financial and demand channel unnecessary;
> 4. a documented episode with a rate gap and clustered failure but intact spending and
>    credit, or the mirror case with a demand shortfall and no rate gap;
> 5. two genuinely independent frames that return a single winner at the slot level and
>    survive the anchor test.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful
> to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **Stage 1 ask:** what would you count as a failure of the separation? Please answer that
> before reading anything I have written about the framework.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I can send the derivation and the three source links.
> The figures are the authority, not my summary of them. Your verdict on stage 1 is
> recorded before any theory is shown, and I will not pool a later agreement as
> corroboration.
>
> **If you want the method:** it is a loop for describing residuals and locating the
> instrument owed. Run it on a problem you know and can check against a ground. Write down
> what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A
> reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
