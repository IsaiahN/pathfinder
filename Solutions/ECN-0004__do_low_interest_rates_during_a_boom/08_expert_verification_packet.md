# Expert verification packet: the Tether on low rates and recessions

**What this is:** a claim in a form a monetary economist or economic historian can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the separations, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No episode was measured. This is a shape and a route, not an economic result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question as posed is a false either/or. Low rates during a boom, clusters of wrongly started projects, demand shortfalls and credit breaks are overlapping conditions with different anchors and different currencies, so the case must be split and each domain keeps its own verdict. The policy rate is moved by the actor whose action is under examination and therefore fails the anchor test; the comparator both schools need, the rate that would have prevailed without the policy or the spending level that would have prevailed without the shock, is not observed by either side. The residual must stay indexed per project. "Malinvestment" is admitted only as a forward claim (projects clearing the actual rate but not an independently fixed comparator), not as a label applied after the bust. Where the instrument is missing, the framework reports unreachable rather than picking a winner.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1. Framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 9 and 2

- **Statement:** the either/or is a union, and the residual must be indexed per project slot. The policy-moved rate cannot be the anchor.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
  - Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
  - Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
  - Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
  - Fig 2 :29-30 "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."
- **Refuter:** show that project-level failure and aggregate shortfall are one quantity under one instrument, so that indexing adds no information; or exhibit a rate quantity that does not move when the policy maker pushes it and that both schools can range against separately.

### C2. Economics-facing; checkable by a monetary economist against the estimation literature

- **Statement:** both schools depend on an unobserved comparator. The natural-rate and potential-output estimates already return something that fails to resolve, so the discriminating case is unreached rather than empty. This is a claim about the *instrument*, not a verdict for either school.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 6 :34-35 "The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve."
  - Fig 8 :39 "so a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
  - Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"
- **What to check:** the neutrally formatted source states that the natural rate "cannot be observed directly" and reports large revisions across vintages. Does any available estimate classify a set of boom-time rate gaps tightly enough to separate the mechanisms? A yes with documented bands would move the break; a no with documented bands leaves the gap standing.
- **Refuter:** an independently fixed comparator with bands narrow enough to classify the gaps, showing either no relation to subsequent project failure (breaks the forward malinvestment claim) or a one-to-one relation that makes the financial and demand channel unnecessary (breaks the union claim).

### C3. Framework-internal and method-facing; checkable by reading Figures 5, 10 and 11

- **Statement:** the retroactive use of "malinvestment" fails the description-length bargain; the credit channel and the allocation error are different objects; the rate's valence flips with the residual.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 5 :43-44 "A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before. A term that explains everything by saying nothing fails this, and so does a term that explains one occasion perfectly."
  - Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does"
  - Fig 11 :49 "An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
- **What to check:** is there a forward criterion, fixed before the bust, that classifies projects as wrongly initiated and pays for itself in description length? Is the observed failure better read as a channel break (financeable projects that could not be financed) or as an allocation error (projects that would not have cleared the independent rate)?
- **Refuter:** a forward criterion that predicts project failure at a rate above chance with a documented false-positive rate; or a demonstration that both descriptions collapse to one bond with the same operands.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that low rates are harmless, and not that they are the cause of recessions.
- Not that the Austrian school is refuted or that the demand/financial school is confirmed.
- Not that "malinvestment" is empty. Only its retroactive use is refused.
- Not that a natural-rate estimate settles the case, and not that the framework names the missing instrument.
- Not a universal percentage split between the mechanisms.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by an episode measurement.
- Not a claim that the four low-overlap analogues (ecological trap, outcome bias, Simpson's paradox, Goodhart's law) are economic evidence. They corroborate shape only.

## 5. What would settle it

1. An independently fixed comparator with published bands, model vintage and revision history, applied to a named set of boom-time rate gaps and project outcomes.
2. A discriminating episode in which the rate gap and clustered failure occur with intact spending and credit, or the mirror case with a demand shortfall and no rate gap.
3. Two genuinely low-overlap frames with separately documented ground contact returning the same slot-level verdict and surviving the anchor test.
4. A refutation of the separation itself under the seven conditions in `01` section 5.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, :22; Fig 9 :55, :57; Fig 2 :29-30. Four short passages.
2. Check C2 against the estimation literature you know. Do the published bands resolve the gap, or do they only return something that fails to resolve?
3. Name one forward criterion for "wrongly started project" that would survive the description-length bargain, or say why none is available.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
