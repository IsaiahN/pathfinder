## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, whether low interest rates during a boom create harmful malinvestment, or whether recessions are better explained by demand shortfalls and financial shocks. Option A: keep one winner and attribute the recession to the rate signal or to the spending shortfall. Option B: split the union, keep the per-domain verdicts separate, and report the missing comparator as unreachable. What each leads to: A produces a general-mode verdict with an unobserved comparator and an averaged slot, and it spends the independence of the people asked to check it. B produces a shape, five written refuters, and an honest stop at the instrument owed.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Clustered failure of particular capital projects after a cheap-credit boom, plus the aggregate fall in spending. Arity: at least two, possibly three with the comparator counted. Symmetry: one reading is ordered (rate gap, longer projects, liquidation), the other is a threshold (spending against a benchmark) plus a channel break. Scale: project slot and capital structure versus aggregate and credit channel. Fig 9 :4-5: "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones." So a **union**, not a partition.
- **Domain 1, intertemporal price signal.** Actors: policy maker, savers, borrowers, market rate, saving-determined comparator. Currency: a rate comparison. Anchor: owed. The policy rate moves when pushed, so it fails the anchor test.
- **Domain 2, real allocation at the project slot.** Actors: projects, hurdle rates, capital structure, liquidation. Currency: cash flows and project survival. Anchor: a discount rate fixed independently of the decision. Not yet supplied in this run.
- **Domain 3, aggregate spending and credit channel.** Actors: households, banks, balance sheets, credit spreads. Currency: spending flow and credit availability. Anchor: observed counters for spending, employment, lending and default. These do not change because a frame thinks otherwise.
- **Domain 4, verification and outreach.** Actors: expert readers and schools. Currency: agreement and residuals. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all seats draw on one evidence pool, so this domain's independence is zero.
- **Order by dependency.** Refuse the false partition before minting (Fig 9 :54); fix the comparator before the project verdict (Fig 3 :20); describe the residual before selecting a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).

Strain test applied: assigning the interest rate to a substrate term strains the six-term physical set. Re-census carries only the structural template across scale (Fig 4 :19-20), so the rate is a quantity in the economic library, not a seventh primitive. Two strains remain live and are named in section 8 of `01`: the comparator symmetry on the demand side and the fixed-atom condition on the isomer claim.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The question is a false either/or, and the figures return a separation plus a gap. Keep the residual per project slot; refuse the policy rate as an anchor; require an independently fixed comparator of both schools; admit "malinvestment" only in its forward, refutable form; keep credit-channel failure distinct from allocation error; keep verdicts per domain and never average them. Where the source-discriminating instrument is owed, the verdict is `unreachable at this budget`, which is a verdict rather than silence. This is `specified` on the separations, `general` on the shape, not `grounded`. The strongest single point is canonical: Fig 9 :54 says to unbundle a union before searching again, so no amount of further argument between the schools counts as progress at the missing instrument.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states its mode, refuters and provenance before its conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a verdict instead of a residual. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the separation and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The union reading can be withdrawn without touching the figure text or the source ledger; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already state the withdrawal conditions. A comparison that shows the three conditions to be one quantity, or an instrument that fixes the comparator, reverses the specific findings one at a time. No primitive has to be invented or retired.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. An independently fixed comparator with narrow bands applied to named boom-time rate gaps and project outcomes would either kill the forward malinvestment claim or make the union unnecessary. A documented discriminating episode (rate gap with clustered failure and intact credit, or the mirror case) would break one side's necessity claim. Two genuinely low-overlap frames with separately documented ground contact returning the same slot-level verdict would also reverse the gap. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
