# The Tether's answer on low rates, malinvestment and recessions

**Mode: `specified` on the separations, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not an economic result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The unexplained effect is clustered failure of particular capital projects after a boom in which borrowing was cheap, together with the aggregate fall in spending that accompanies it. Two readings arrive with it: an intertemporal price signal that was wrong relative to saving, and a spending or credit channel that broke. The question asks which one is right. The framework's first answer is that the question is malformed: the two readings have different anchors, different currencies and different slots (Fig 9 :4-5, :54-58).

## 2. The shape

1. **Keep the residual per project.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A recession is an aggregate reading; a wrong investment is a slot. An answer that averages them loses the only signal that says where to look.
2. **The policy rate is not the anchor.** Fig 2 :29-30: "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." The rate under examination is moved by the actor whose action is under examination. What settles the case is a comparator fixed independently of that actor, plus project-level outcomes that do not change because a school thinks otherwise.
3. **Both schools owe the same missing instrument.** The Austrian comparator is the rate that would have prevailed from real saving; the demand-side comparator is the spending or output level that would have prevailed without the shock. Neither is observed. Fig 6 :34-35 asks whether anything at any resolution is already returning something that fails to resolve. Natural-rate and potential-output estimates do return something, with wide bands and large revisions, so the case is `unreachable at this budget`, not empty. Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence".
4. **"Malinvestment" survives only as a forward claim.** Fig 5 :43-44: "A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before. A term that explains everything by saying nothing fails this, and so does a term that explains one occasion perfectly." The retroactive label (the investments that failed were wrong) explains the bust by naming it. The forward version (projects that clear the actual rate but not an independently fixed comparator) is a real candidate with a falsifier.
5. **The credit channel is a different object from allocation.** Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does". A broken channel between saving and investment is a contact failure; a wrongly started project is an allocation failure. They can coexist, and each keeps its own verdict.
6. **The rate's valence flips with the residual.** Fig 11 :49: "An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Low rates help when the residual is a spending shortfall and hurt when it is intertemporal misallocation. No standing verdict on the rate survives a change of residual.
7. **No averaged winner.** Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." A percentage contribution, a consensus statement or a count of Supporting papers is a filter for where to spend attention, not a witness for what caused an episode.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that low rates are harmless, and not that they are the cause of every bust. No causal verdict on rates is returned.
- Not that the Austrian school is rejected. Its forward claim is a well-typed candidate with an unresolved operand.
- Not that the demand and financial account is accepted by default. It has its own unresolved comparator.
- Not that "malinvestment" is empty. The retroactive use is refused; the forward use is admitted as a candidate.
- Not that a natural-rate estimate settles the case. An estimate without provenance (model, vintage, revision history) and a shadow test is a name presented as a fact.
- Not `grounded`. No episode was measured in this run, and no instrument was built.
- Not a theory of everything about recessions. A universal winner would be a terminal room, and Fig 7 names "the limit: no last room".

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating instrument is owed, not a further synthesis of the literatures. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved the case. Here the corresponding questions are:

- Does an independently fixed comparator separate projects that were wrongly started from projects that were right but became unfinanceable, at the per-project level, before the bust is known?
- Does the credit channel remain intact in a case with a rate gap and clustered failure, or does it break in a case with no rate gap? These are the two discriminating episodes in the pre-registered refuter.
- Does the natural-rate estimate survive a change of model and vintage with bands narrow enough to classify the rate gap? If it does not, it is a proto-instrument still failing to resolve.
- Standing falsifiers: a figure line returning a single winner; per-slot failure and aggregate shortfall shown to be one quantity; two independent frames returning a single slot-level verdict that survives the anchor test.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable in principle.** The estimation instruments exist and return something, but they do not yet discriminate sources. The framework stops there and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Naming a missing primitive is not available, because naming it would mean already holding it (Fig 13 :134-135). What is available is the shape of the discriminator and the conditions under which the verdict would change.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not pick a winner between malinvestment and demand shortfalls. It says the question is a false either/or: a rate moved by policy cannot be the anchor that settles whether the policy was a mistake, both schools need a comparator neither observes, and a wrong project is a per-slot fact while a recession is an aggregate one. Credit-channel failure is kept separate from allocation error because the ground does not decay, the channel does. "Malinvestment" is admitted only in its forward, refutable form; the retroactive version is an outcome bias with a figure-derived name. What the framework returns is the shape any adequate account must have and five refuters written in advance. It stops at the gap where the source-discriminating instrument is owed, and calls that unreachable rather than silence. No measurement in this run settles which mechanism dominated any episode.
