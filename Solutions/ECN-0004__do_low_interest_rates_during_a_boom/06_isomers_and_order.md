# Isomers, bonds and order in the rate-gap question

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the union/partition distinction, not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 5).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks for a choice between two explanations. The supporting derivation owes three things: (1) the correct object type of the choice (union or partition), (2) the bond structure of each candidate mechanism, and (3) whether the two mechanisms are isomers over a fixed atom set, with a falsifier written before the derivation.

## DOMAIN CENSUS (carried from `01`, not re-derived)

D1 intertemporal price signal (currency: a rate comparison; anchor: owed), D2 real allocation per project (currency: cash flows and project survival; anchor: a discount rate fixed independently of the decision), D3 aggregate spending and credit channel (currency: spending flow and credit availability; anchor: observed counters), D4 verification (currency: agreement and residual; anchor: the ground). The first three form a union with overlapping conditions. Dependency order: settle the comparator before the project verdict; describe the residual before choosing a frame; refuse a false partition before minting any new term.

## DERIVATION

### Q1. Is the choice a partition or a union?

- Fig 9 :4-5: "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones." and "Split it rather than search."
- Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."
- **Result:** union. An episode can carry a rate gap, a cluster of marginal projects and a broken credit channel simultaneously. A lever that clears the pure rate-gap region leaves the intersections standing. The either/or is a false partition, and a reported "percentage contribution" is a filter for the budget, not a witness for the verdict.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a dataset in which the three conditions never co-occur and one condition always suffices, with co-occurrence ruled out by construction rather than by sample size.

### Q2. What bond does each mechanism use?

- Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance."
- Operators table: ⋛ is a threshold relation, with the test "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"; − is subtraction, with "progress is a subtraction, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot say how far along it is"; → is sequence: "A then B; B needs A to have happened".
- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- **Austrian mechanism:** `actual rate ⋛ saving-determined comparator → longer project set − liquidated set`. Ordered sequence plus a comparison plus a subtraction. The Operators table also supplies ¬ (negation): a refusal of a project's standing to be composed over, not the deletion of a term.
- **Demand/financial mechanism:** `spending ⋛ benchmark` plus a broken channel between saving and investment. Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does". The real resources are still there; the link that carries the signal is not.
- **Result:** the two read as different arrangements of a shared economic atom set, not as inverse spellings of one claim. They are candidates for isomerism (Q3), not for averaging.
- **Falsifier:** show that both reduce to one bond with the same operands, in which case F5 of `01` is withdrawn.

### Q3. Are they isomers? (live strain S5)

- Isomerism requires the same atoms (Fig 12 :117). Over the shared set {market rate, saving-determined comparator, project, hurdle rate, credit channel, spending, benchmark} the two mechanisms select different bonds and different slot bindings, so the stronger claim is: **same atoms, different bond, different substance**.
- As commonly written in the literature the atom sets are not identical: the Austrian account foregrounds the capital structure and the comparator, the demand account foregrounds balance sheets and aggregate spending. On that narrower reading, they are not isomers but neighbouring members of a functional group. Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either mechanism requires an atom the other does not, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands instead. This strain stays live and is not smoothed. It is not a defect in the union verdict, which holds either way.
- **Mode:** `specified` on the condition, not `grounded` on the atom census itself.

### Q4. The comparator is a missing operand, not a refuted one

- A comparison bond needs two quantities. The Operators table defines ⋛ by the test "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes." One operand (the actual rate) is observed; the other (the saving-determined rate) is estimated with wide and revised bands. The neutral-rate source states that it "cannot be observed directly".
- Formula step 7 distinguishes underivable in principle from unreachable at this budget. Fig 8 :39: "so a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 6 :34-35 requires a proto-instrument "already returning something that fails to resolve"; natural-rate estimates satisfy that description, so the case is unreached, not empty.
- Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself".
- **Result:** the Austrian mechanism is a well-typed candidate with an unresolved operand. It is not refuted by the absence of a perfect comparator, and it is not settled by an estimate presented without provenance. The same analysis applies to the demand side's benchmark (potential output), which is recorded as S4 in `01`.
- **Falsifier:** a measurement that fixes the comparator tightly enough to classify a set of boom-time rate gaps and shows either no relation or a one-to-one relation to project failure.

### Q5. Order and dependency

- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
- The chain runs perceive failure, name the affordances of a rate and a project, say what would count as progress (the comparator), plan the discriminating test, then carry the method. The break is at link 3. Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
- **Consequence for the debate:** more elaborate versions of either theory are effort above the break. They arrive at a step that cannot use them. What is owed is an instrument, not a synthesis of the two literatures.
- **Falsifier:** an accepted instrument for either comparator, after which the break moves and this finding is withdrawn.

### Q6. Analogues from low-overlap fields

These corroborate the *shape*, never the economic verdict.

| analogue | field | source quote | what it corroborates | limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ecological trap | animal behaviour / ecology | "If either the habitat quality or the cue changes so that one does not reliably indicate the other, organisms may be lured into poor-quality habitat." | A signal decoupled from what it indicated can guide choice wrongly; this is the shape of F3 and F6. | A cue, not a price; no balance sheets, no policy maker. |
| Outcome bias | human cognition | "the outcome bias is an error made in evaluating the quality of a decision when the outcome of that decision is already known" | The retrodictive label in F4. | Laboratory judgments, not market allocation. |
| Simpson's paradox | mathematics / statistics | "a trend appears in several groups of data but disappears or reverses when the groups are combined" | Per-slot indexing in F2. | Reversal is possible, not demonstrated for any episode here. |
| Goodhart's law | monetary policy / institutional | "Any observed statistical regularity will tend to collapse once pressure is placed upon it for control purposes." | A policy-moved rate cannot be an anchor (F3). | An adage with exceptions, not a theorem. |

The four analogues are four fields with low overlap with each other and with the framework essays. They are one set of corroborators, not four independent witnesses for the economic claim. Fig 8 :48-49 warns that frames overlapping almost completely are "one frame wearing two names"; here the overlap is genuinely low, but the analogues still speak to shape only.

## CONCLUSION

The either/or is a false partition (Q1). The two mechanisms use different bonds over a partly shared atom set and are at minimum a functional group, possibly isomers under a fixed-atom condition that remains live (Q2, Q3). Both need a comparator that neither holds as a settled quantity, which puts the discriminating break at the objective link and makes the empirical winner unreachable at this budget (Q4, Q5). Low-overlap analogues corroborate the shape of these findings and not the economic verdict (Q6). No figure line returns which school is right about recessions in general, and no figure line could: the framework returns a shape, and the ground must settle the rest.
