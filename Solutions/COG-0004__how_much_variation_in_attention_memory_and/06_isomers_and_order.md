# Are the three capacities isomers? Union order and the nature/culture analogue

**Mode: `specified`.** Supporting derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The audit closed on a shape, not a number. Two secondary questions remain for the expert packet:

1. Is the relation among attention, memory and reasoning an **isomer** relation under Fig 12, a **functional group**, or merely a union of unrelated tasks?
2. What is the dependency order, and what does a laboratory test harness actually prove about the culture/biology split?

## DERIVATION

### Q1. Are attention, memory and reasoning isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

As the question names them, the three capacities do **not** share an atom set. Attention is read by selection and cueing tasks, memory by encoding and recall, reasoning by inference over propositions. Different atoms, different tasks, different grounds. Under Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**; calling them three substances with one coefficient would be the flat-formula error.

The shared substructure is narrower: each is a frame that bets, acts, observes and accrues a per-slot residual against its own ground. That is the functional group. "The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member" gives the correct name: a *capacity-in-contact-with-a-habitat*, qualified as attention, memory or reasoning. The three are members of one functional group, not isomers of one substance.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** exhibit one atom set and one bond arrangement that generates all three capacities with only the qualifier changing, and show that the three remain distinguishable in behaviour. If that holds, the isomer claim succeeds and a shared coefficient becomes thinkable. Failing it leaves the functional-group reading standing.

### Q2. What is the union order?

- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."
- Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."

Dependency runs: ground reading of a slot, then per-capacity residual, then transmission history and inherited prior per frame, then habitat enumeration, then the interaction. Effort spent on a pooled coefficient arrives at a step that cannot use it if the slot reading is broken. Fig 3 :27: a link with no instrument is a link nobody has looked at, not a working link. In this question the middle link, having terms for what the situation affords a learner, attracts the least instrumentation and the most assumption.

The outer pair is a union, and the intersection is a compound with its own loop: a memory task performed under a cultural schema is not a harder memory task, it is a different case. Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

### Q3. What is the analogue for "how much"?

*How a Society of Agents* asks the structurally identical question about groups and refuses the single number: "None of this gives one number for 'how much smarter.' It gives the dials". The dials named there are independence of members, fraction of work that must be sequential, cost of staying in sync, and number of generations. The corresponding dials here are per-slot instrument resolution, separation of transmission frames, contact change under intervention, and the measured value of `w` per frame. Each is checkable. None is a constant to pick.

World corroboration at low overlap: the Flynn effect meta-analysis reports different annual rates for fluid, spatial, full-scale and crystallized performance (0.41, 0.30, 0.28 and 0.21 IQ points respectively), which is a per-slot result, and Trahan et al. say a working magnitude and measurement error are "not well established, leaving unanswerable the question of how much of a correction ... to apply". That is the same refusal from inside the measurement tradition.

### Q4. Does a controlled task prove the cultural claim?

- Fig 11 :70: "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."
- Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."

A twin study or a laboratory attention task is a substituted habitat. It can show wiring, that a capacity is present and how it varies under the controlled conditions, but it cannot prove what the capacity does in the natural habitat from which the conditions were abstracted. The two silent failure modes are "what you failed to reproduce, and what you brought with you" (Fig 11 :29-30). For a cultural claim the second mode is decisive: the experimenter's own cultural priors enter through task design and are brought along unnoticed.

This is a reason to require a habitat intervention, not a reason to distrust the laboratory. Capability is agent-and-habitat; the harness tells you about the agent in one substituted habitat.

### Q5. Is there a "how much" operator?

The Operators table defines ⋛ comparison as "a threshold relation between two quantities" and lists it as ordered, with the test "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present?". A variance apportionment is a comparison of two quantities, so ⋛ correctly names the *form* of the question. But the figures price comparison as a goal: "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one". Writing the question as a comparison does not make the operands commensurable. Fig 12 :125 adds that bits and actions are two currencies that do not add. A cultural share in bits of explained variance and a biological share in covariance are not the same currency until a ground makes them so.

**Falsifier for Q5:** a measurement that returns both sources in one currency under one declared code, with the conversion rule provenanced rather than assumed. Without it the comparison is well formed but unsettled.

## CONCLUSION

The three capacities are members of one functional group, not isomers, and the union is ordered by dependency rather than pooled. The nature/culture question is a ⋛ comparison whose operands are not yet commensurable, and a controlled harness proves wiring in a substituted habitat, not capability in the natural one. What survives is a per-slot shape with dials and falsifiers. No atom is minted, no import moves the wall, and no new primitive is named.
