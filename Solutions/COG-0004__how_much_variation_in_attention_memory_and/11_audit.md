## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines `R_T` as "The gap between what was sent up and what came back down: gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x)). Measures what the coarser description cannot hold." Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :19 and :22 (per-slot, no aggregation), Fig 9 :54 (union, unbundle), Fig 11 :39 and :70 (agent-and-habitat, harness), Fig 13 :134 (`gap` as verdict), and the Symbols table's `w`.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07`: keep slots separate, unbundle the union, do not subtract the habitat, `w` is a learnable dial, the number is a gap owed to a ground.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete world claim, for example "culture explains 50 percent of the variance in attention and 20 percent in memory, so policy should target X".

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **per-slot rule**, which the concrete claim collapses into two different percentages that are then pooled for policy;
- the **union compound**, which the concrete claim treats as two independent variables with additive effects;
- the **agent-and-habitat bond**, which the concrete claim prices as two buckets in one currency.

Conversely, two things survive cleanly: a structure must carry a per-slot reading, and identical architecture can diverge through history and weighting. That is the residue worth sending. `R_T` was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set `R_T` to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
