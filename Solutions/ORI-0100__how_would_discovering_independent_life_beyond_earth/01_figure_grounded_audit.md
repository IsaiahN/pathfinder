## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

The quantity in question is a **rate**: how often life emerges per eligible site, per unit time, over a population. It is currently constrained by exactly one observation slot (Earth life emerged). The discovery described in the question adds one more slot, explicitly independent.

Describe R by arity, symmetry and scale before choosing any figure line:

- **Arity:** one slot now, two after the discovery. The rate is a quantity over a population; a single observation is not a rate.
- **Symmetry:** an independent origin is a second, low-overlap witness. A shared-ancestry origin (panspermia, common cause) is not a second witness even if it looks like one. The symmetry of the "two observations" decides whether they count as two.
- **Scale:** the observation is per site; the estimate is galactic. The move from one to the other is a scale crossing, so it carries a lossy transform and a nonzero round-trip gap.

So the residual in effect terms is: **one atom cannot support a rate estimate; a discovery adds an atom, but it only adds a second witness if it is genuinely independent, and only licenses a galactic statement if the per-site to galactic gap is measured rather than zeroed.**

Binding the sought-for shape as an ordinary term before the run (Formula "Direction"): *a valid estimate requires at least two low-overlap independent origin slots, an enumerated habitat for each, and a measured round-trip gap.* This is written down now so it can be refused.

### F1 : One atom cannot license a rate, and search inside the closure cannot add a second (triangulated)

Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."

Fig 8 :32: "no search, however long, adds an atom"

Fig 9 :44: "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."

Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"

The estimation frame holds one atom, Earth. No amount of re-analysis of Earth's stratigraphy, genetics or prebiotic chemistry can manufacture a second independent origin. The absence of a second observation is therefore **not** evidence that origins are rare; it is a statement about the closure of the frame. World corroboration is direct: the Drake-equation article (Wikipedia, 2026) states that from a hypothesis-testing standpoint with one planet "there are zero degrees of freedom, permitting no valid estimates to be made." Same structure, different vocabulary: one slot, no estimate.

**Refuter:** show that a single slot plus a declared prior already determines the rate without any new observation, that is, that closure is not idempotent with respect to observations. Fails if SUPPORT (Formula step 3, `|R⁺_s| > 0`) cannot be shown for the estimation slot.

Triangulation: Fig 6 (composition), Fig 8 (metaframe) and Fig 9 (search) have low overlap, so the agreement is structural, not a shared phrase. Marked `triangulated`.

### F2 : The discovery is an import, and import is the only operation that moves the wall (triangulated)

Fig 6 :22: "IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure."

Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."

Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone."

The discovery is contact with a frame (another biosphere) whose closure differs from the Earth-only frame. The new atom is the second independent origin. This is the *only* figure-legal way the estimation reach changes. It licenses moving the question from "no valid estimate" to "a bounded estimate exists", not to a specific number.

**Refuter:** show that the discovery can be absorbed into the existing atom set without adding a primitive (for example, that it is fully predicted by the Earth-only model), in which case no wall moved and Fig 6 :22 was misapplied.

Marked `triangulated` across Fig 6 and Fig 8 (reachability and union surplus are different claims).

### F3 : The new atom counts only if it is independent; shared ancestry is the common-cause collapse (triangulated)

Fig 2 :18-21: "so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other"

Fig 8 :50-52: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."

Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."

Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

This is the exact reason the question specifies *independent* life. If the second detection shares ancestry with Earth life, transfer by meteoroids or comets is a common cause: the two readings agree because one evidence pool is talking, not because two origins were sampled. The estimate does not move. If the second detection is a genuinely separate origin, it is convergent derivation at maximum independence, a second witness, and the estimate does move. The figure gives a procedure, not a label: check the record of where each came from (genetic code, biochemistry, isotopic fractionation), not the resemblance of the reading.

World corroboration: the same Drake-equation article adds that a second life would raise degrees of freedom from zero to one, "there would remain a great deal of uncertainty on any estimate due to the small sample size, and the chance they are not really independent." The "Hard Problem is About Source" essay (2026) gives the instrument-level reason: a detector "cannot comment on the source", and a better instrument, not an argument, is what distinguishes sources. The post-detection question is an ancestry discriminator, not a count.

**Refuter:** show a case where shared ancestry still yields two independent data points for the emergence rate (for example, if panspermia transfer is itself counted as a separate emergence event), or show that genetic and isotopic similarity cannot discriminate ancestry even in principle. Either would break the independence gate.

Marked `triangulated` across Fig 2 and Fig 8 (alignment collapse and union provenance, low overlap).

### F4 : Per slot, never aggregate (single-source, one figure)

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."

Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

Fig 1 :13: "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"

A rate is an aggregation by construction. The figure's warning is that aggregation is where the signal about *where to look* disappears. So the update must keep the second site as a named slot: its habitat, its chemistry, its time to emergence. Only then may the slots be folded into a rate, and the fold must be reported as a lossy step. Averaging the two lives into one posterior destroys the only thing the discovery added.

**Refuter:** show that the slot-level record can be collapsed into a single posterior with no residual left on any slot. If such a collapse pays (Formula step 3 bargain), the per-slot requirement is refuted.

Marked `single-source`: the claim comes from Fig 1 alone. It is not inflated to triangulated by the world sources, which merely restate the statistical version.

### F5 : The site to galaxy jump has a nonzero round-trip gap (single-source, one figure)

Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."

Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."

The Symbols table defines R_T as gap(x, (T_E composed with T_A)(x)). Going from a per-site observation to a galactic rate is abstraction (T_A); the concretisation (T_E) is the concrete rate claim. The gap is not zero. It contains at least three dropped constraints: the Earth site was selected by the presence of observers (anthropic bias), "suitable" conditions are defined from one example, and detection is biased toward life resembling ours. These must be enumerated and reported, not assumed away. The anthropic-principle article (Wikipedia, 2026) names the same selection effect as an "observation selection effect": observations are limited by the requirement that observers exist.

**Refuter:** exhibit a transform from the site reading to the galactic rate whose round trip returns the site reading unchanged (R_T = 0). Until such a transform is shown, any single-number galactic estimate is dead reckoning.

Marked `single-source` for the figure line (Fig 4); the R_T definition is from the Symbols table, which is the notation for the same move, not an independent witness.

### F6 : Life is a property of agent-and-habitat, so the second site's habitat must be enumerated (single-source, one figure)

Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"

Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."

Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

A detection without its conditions is a name without the things that produced it. To say the rate is high "once conditions are right" you must enumerate what was right at the second site: energy source, water or solvent, mineral gradients, temperature, time. Fig 11 :44 says the list is read off the world, not invented. The Drake article's fl paragraph uses the same structure: abiogenesis "may be relatively common once conditions are right", and this evidence "only looks at the Earth (a single model planet)". A second site with an enumerated habitat turns "once conditions are right" from a phrase into a constrained condition.

**Refuter:** show that two origins in habitats with disjoint enumerated conditions still license the same rate parameter, that is, that the habitat list does not bear on the estimate. If it does not, Fig 11 is the wrong frame for this domain.

Marked `single-source` (Fig 11).

### F7 : The observing site was selected by its own outcome (single-source, one figure)

Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update."

Fig 2 :34: "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."

Earth was not drawn at random from eligible sites; it was selected by the presence of observers, which is the outcome being estimated. The single observation is therefore not independent of the quantity it is used to estimate. Reading the rate off our own existence lets the anchor update on the frame, which is Fig 2's coherent-drift collapse. The Drake article calls this "anthropic bias, as the planet of study was not chosen randomly, but by the living organisms that already inhabit it (ourselves)."

**Refuter:** exhibit a sampling procedure for the Earth observation that is independent of the presence of observers. Without one, the one-slot estimate is conditioned on a selection effect and is not a rate estimate.

Marked `single-source` (Fig 2 anchor rule). F3 also uses Fig 2, but for a different clause (independence, not anchor updating), so this is not double-counted as corroboration.

### F8 : The Fermi silence is not evidence of rarity (triangulated)

Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

Fig 9 :44: "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."

Fig 8 :40-41: "Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions."

The absence of observed alien life cannot set the emergence rate low. That absence is a search result inside a closure with one atom, not a measurement of rarity. The distinction matters operationally: underivable means go outside (find a new frame or instrument); unreachable-at-budget means the term is already in the closure and needs more search, not a new atom. The Fermi-paradox article (Wikipedia, 2026) frames the same thing as a conflict "between the argument that scale and probability seem to favor intelligent life being common in the universe, and the total lack of evidence of intelligent life having ever arisen anywhere other than on Earth." The "Great Filter" section notes "The most commonly agreed-upon low probability event is abiogenesis", which is a claim about where the filter might sit, not a measurement of the rate.

**Refuter:** show a search over a sealed closure of eligible sites (a genuinely exhaustive surveyed set) that returns no life. Only such a sealed search would turn silence into a bound. No frame is a sealed room, so this refuter is a design specification, not an available measurement.

Marked `triangulated` across Fig 8 and Fig 9 (low overlap: metaframe theorem versus search procedure).

## 3. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Closure refuter.** A single observation slot plus a declared prior is shown to determine the rate with no new atom, that is, composition inside the closure is not idempotent with respect to observations (breaks F1 and Fig 6 :21, Fig 8 :32).
2. **Independence refuter.** A second life sharing ancestry is shown to count as a second independent witness for the *emergence* rate, or genetic and isotopic evidence is shown incapable of discriminating ancestry even in principle (breaks F3).
3. **Per-slot refuter.** The slot-level record is shown to collapse into one posterior with zero residual on every slot, that is, the aggregation is lossless (breaks F4 and Fig 1 :22).
4. **Round-trip refuter.** A site to galaxy transform is exhibited whose round trip returns the site reading unchanged, R_T = 0 (breaks F5 and Fig 4 :30).
5. **Selection refuter.** A sampling procedure for the Earth observation is exhibited that is independent of the presence of observers (breaks F7).

If any fires, the derivation is withdrawn or repaired at that point, and the correction is recorded here rather than silently applied. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Lead with the shape claim and the five refuters, not with a number. The first question to an expert is "what would you count as a failure here?"
2. Keep the second site as a named slot. Its habitat, chemistry and time to emergence are the content of the discovery, not prelude to an average (F4, F6).
3. Gate every estimate on the ancestry result. Until ancestry is discriminated, report the discovery as one evidence pool with two readings, not two origins (F3).
4. Report the round-trip gap R_T explicitly with any galactic extrapolation. Do not set it to zero by convention (F5).
5. Treat the Fermi silence as an open channel, not a low rate. Say "unreachable is a verdict, not a silence" rather than "life is rare" (F8).
6. Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Send the derivation and the refuter, not a conclusion.

### Which of the three step-7 questions applies

- **SEARCHED:** no. A second independent origin is not already in the one-slot closure and merely unfound; re-searching Earth's rocks cannot produce it (F1).
- **INWARD:** partly. Sharpen the ancestry discriminator (genetics, isotopes) and enumerate the second habitat. This extends the instrument from one already returning something (Fig 6 :32).
- **OUTWARD:** yes, and this is the discovery itself. Characterise R in effect terms, then make contact with a frame whose closure differs (Fig 6 :23). This is the import (F2), debited against independence.

The three are kept distinct. The discovery is an OUTWARD event that must be followed by INWARD work before any rate is reported.

The shape survives: one slot yields no rate; a confirmed independent origin is the only legal update; the update is per slot, conditional on ancestry, and conditioned by a measured round-trip gap; the framework returns the shape of a valid estimate and an honest "unreachable" until the ground pays.
