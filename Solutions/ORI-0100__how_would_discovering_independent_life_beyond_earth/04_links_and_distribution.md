## Wider literature that bears directly on the question

1. **The one-slot limit.** The Drake-equation f_l paragraph is the cleanest public statement that one planet gives zero degrees of freedom and no valid estimate, and that a second independent life raises the count to one while leaving a large uncertainty and the possibility that the two are not really independent. This is the world-side twin of findings F1 and F3. It corroborates, it does not decide.
2. **Absence is a bound, not a rate.** The rule of three gives an upper bound from zero observed events; it does not give a rate. This is a mathematics-side corroboration of F8 ("a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence") from a field with no overlap with the figures.
3. **The source problem.** The ancestry question (is the second life independent or transferred?) is a source-discrimination problem. The spectrometer example in the Hard Problem essay is the exact structure: an instrument reads a value and cannot say which source produced it, and only a better instrument resolves it. So the discovery claim owes an ancestry discriminator, not a resemblance argument.
4. **Selection bias.** The anthropic principle article's "observation selection effect" is the world-side wording of F7: our site was selected by the presence of observers.
5. **Habitat.** Drake's phrase "once conditions are right" and Fig 11's "The habitat is enumerated, never composed" are the same demand: enumerate the conditions before quoting a frequency.

No world source conflicts with the figure verdict. The conflict rule was therefore not triggered. Overlap is recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv`: the Wikipedia pages share an evidence pool on the history of the Drake equation, so their agreement is one witness, not three. The statistics and anthropic-principle pages are genuinely low-overlap fields.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22** "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Sending the full framework before anyone has described the residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the claim and its refuters; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who has absorbed the interiors before giving a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before showing them the sources.
- **Fig 2 :40** "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". A send selected for people already inclined to agree produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 7 :13** "agreement compounds, evidence does not". Ten agreeing replies are one long room, not ten witnesses.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its five refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure, especially on the independence gate and the round-trip gap; (3) record their verdict; (4) only then the three links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (5) offer the formula as a method with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.
