**Subject:** A checkable claim about what a second independent origin would do to an estimate

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question is how discovering independent life beyond Earth would change estimates of how often life emerges. The figure-derived shape is that the estimate currently has one observation slot, Earth, and one slot does not support a rate. A confirmed independent origin is not a second number to average; it is a new atom added to the estimation frame, and that is the only operation that changes what the frame can reach. Three conditions gate the update: the new life must be genuinely independent (shared ancestry would make the two readings one evidence pool with two names); the site must be kept as a distinct slot with its habitat and chemistry recorded; and the step from a per-site reading to a galactic rate carries a lossy gap that has to be measured rather than assumed to be zero. The result is a bounded, per-slot, habitat-conditioned family of estimates, not a number.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that one discovery sets the probability of life near one; that the silence of SETI is evidence that life is rare; that a similar biochemistry would prove common ancestry; that the framework produces a numeric posterior. It returns a shape. Mode on the substantive claims is `specified`, which means a specification of what to measure, not a measurement.
>
> **What would refute it, written before this message:**
> 1. A single observation plus a declared prior already determines the rate with no new observation, so that composition inside a closure is not idempotent.
> 2. A second life sharing ancestry still counts as a second independent witness for the emergence rate, or ancestry cannot be discriminated even in principle.
> 3. The two sites' records collapse into one posterior with no residual left on either slot.
> 4. A per-site to galactic transform is exhibited whose round trip returns the site reading unchanged, so the gap is zero.
> 5. A sampling procedure for the Earth observation is exhibited that does not depend on the presence of observers.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract or repair the claim at that point. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figures and figure text (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in this project, cited as `Fig N :line`.
> - The eight-step loop and mode rule: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`.
> - Reported astrobiology context: the Drake equation and the abiogenesis rate discussion at https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drake_equation, and the public post-detection protocol at https://www.seti.org/research/seti-101/protocols-for-an-eti-signal-detection/
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the formula is in the figures. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
