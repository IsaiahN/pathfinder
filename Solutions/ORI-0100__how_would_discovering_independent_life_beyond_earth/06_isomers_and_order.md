# Independent origin, isomers, and the order of the update

**Mode: `specified`. Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).**

## RESIDUAL

The question's word "independent" needs a structural reading. Two biospheres can look alike and be related, or look different and be unrelated. What does the figure set say about same-substance-by-different-route, and what order must the update follow? One supporting question: are two independent origins **isomers** under Fig 12's definition, and does that help decide whether they share ancestry?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 : Does "same atoms, different bond" help read independence?

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

Fig 13 :121: "Different routes can arrive at the same substance."

Fig 13 :128: "Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it"

Putting these together gives the structural reading of the ancestry problem:

- Two origins using the same biochemistry (same atoms, same bond arrangement) are **not** thereby shown to be one lineage. Fig 13 :121 says different routes can arrive at the same substance. This is convergent derivation at maximum independence, the case the question asks about.
- Two origins with different biochemistry (same atoms, different bond, different substance) are Fig 12's **isomers**. That makes them different substances, but it still does not tell you, from the artefact, which route produced either.
- Therefore resemblance and difference are both silent on ancestry. Only the record separates convergent derivation from adopted import: Fig 8 :50-52, "Only the record of where each came from separates them".

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a biochemical reading is exhibited from which the route (independent origin versus transfer) follows without any additional instrument, Q1 is refuted. The spectrometer example in the Hard Problem essay predicts this falsifier will fail: a reading cannot comment on its source.

### Q2 : Are the two origins isomers, and why does it matter?

Under Fig 12 :117 the isomer claim requires the *same atom set* differently bonded. Two independent biospheres both using {Space, Time, Energy, Gradient, Spectrum, Network} but mediating different state pairs differently would qualify: same six, different arrangement, different substance. If one uses a genetic polymer and the other a metabolism-first architecture, the atom set as written is still the six structures and states; the bond arrangement differs. That is an isomer claim in the framework's sense.

Why it matters for the estimate: if the two origins are isomers (same atoms, different arrangement), then observing one does **not** give you the other by substitution. You have two substances, not two spellings of one. That is the structural reason a second origin adds information rather than repeating the first. Under a symmetric bond, k operands have one term (Fig 12 :92-93); here the bond is ordered and different, so there are two terms. The second atom is genuinely new.

But the weaker reading must be stated too: we cannot assert the two are isomers until both atom sets are enumerated, which is a ground task (Fig 11 :43). Before that, "two lineages, possibly isomeric" is the honest `specified` form.

**Falsifier:** if the second biosphere can be written with an atom not required by the first (or vice versa), the isomer claim fails and the weaker "two lineages with different habitat-conditioned atoms" claim stands instead. This is the same falsifier structure as the exemplar's GR/QM isomer test, applied to a different subject.

### Q3 : What is the order of the update?

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces". Fig 3 :22-23: "A reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing."

The dependency order for this question:

1. **Perception (D2):** is there a signal at all, and does it fail to resolve as nonliving chemistry? Instrument must already be returning something (Fig 6 :32).
2. **Vocabulary (D3):** is it life, and is it independent? This is the link with the fewest instruments, exactly the middle link Fig 3 :25-27 says chains usually break at. "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." The ancestry discriminator is that missing instrument.
3. **Objective (D1):** only now state what would count as progress in the rate estimate, in the estimate's own terms.
4. **Planning:** decide which additional sites to search, keeping the searchers independent (Fig 6 :49-50).
5. **Learn and carry:** name what worked, verify it, carry the method (not a recording) to the next site (Fig 4 :22-23).

Putting the rate estimate before step 2 is a reading below the break. The estimate would consume an input (independence) that has not arrived.

**Falsifier:** show a case where the rate estimate can be stated and paid without first discriminating ancestry, by exhibiting an estimate whose value is invariant under the independent/transferred distinction. If one exists, the dependency order is wrong.

### Q4 : Partition or union?

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either".

Detection and ancestry are a **union compound**: both conditions must hold before the estimation frame can be updated. A single lever (better telescopes) clears the region where only detection fails and leaves the intersection standing, because ancestry still binds. After both are settled the residue is "smaller and categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either" (Fig 9 :57-58). So the solution has two loops before it has one estimate. They are not averaged.

### Q5 : The round trip as the order's error signal

Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."

The abstraction (T_A) is: two sites, each an independent origin, become a rate parameter. The concretisation (T_E) is: apply that rate back to a specific galactic census. The gap is what the rate parameter cannot hold: selection bias at Earth, habitat specificity at the second site, detection bias toward familiar chemistry. R_T is the error signal that orders step 5 back to step 1. It is recorded qualitatively here (the three dropped constraints above), not numerically, because no ground reading exists. Treating "two sites give a rate" as an identity would set R_T to zero and be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 names.

## CONCLUSION

The figure set does not give a numeric rate. It gives an ordering and a shape:

1. Two biospheres with the same biochemistry are **not** thereby one lineage (Fig 13 :121); two with different biochemistry are isomers in the framework's sense (Fig 12 :117), but the artefact still does not reveal the route (Fig 8 :50-52, Fig 13 :128).
2. The update is a **union compound** and must be unbundled: detection first, ancestry second, estimation third (Fig 9 :54-57, Fig 3 :20-23).
3. The per-site to galactic move carries a nonzero R_T that must be measured (Fig 4 :30).

Each of the three has a written falsifier above. None was run, so all remain `candidate`.
