## The answer in one line

The spatial scale of local interactions does not change the rule of emergence (the kernel is scale-free, Fig 4 :19-20); it changes the cast, that is which actors fall inside the enumerated habitat and how far the contact cascade reaches. Within a fixed closure a wider interaction range enlarges the habitat and therefore the maximum size of a structure that can settle, but composition cannot add an atom, so beyond the closure's reach more range does nothing. Persistence is a different quantity with a different anchor: a structure persists while it stays settled against a ground that does not update and the channel to that ground stays reachable, so a wider contact radius can stabilize or destabilize depending on the residual, not on size. The numerical scaling law is a declared `gap`.
