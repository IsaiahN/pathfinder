# The Tether's answer on interaction scale, size and persistence

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, `general` on the scale-invariance of the kernel, `gap` on the numerical law, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a measured result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks for the effect of the spatial reach of local interactions on two outcomes: the size and the persistence of emergent structures. The literal reading assumes one law with two outputs. The derivation finds a **union of two quantities with different anchors** (Fig 9 :54), so the answer has to be given per domain and never averaged. The residual is indexed per structure, not per system (Fig 1 :19). A global claim such as "wider interactions make bigger, longer-lived structures" is the aggregation failure Fig 1 :22 names.

## 2. The shape

1. **The rule is scale-free; the cast is not.** Fig 4 :19-20: "The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." Changing `ell` does not change how emergence works. It changes which actors are in contact, which currency is in play and which ground settles the result. Fig 10 :68 and Fig 12 :103 corroborate from two low-overlap routes.

2. **Size is bounded by the habitat and the closure.** Fig 11 :43-44: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." A wider reach enlarges the enumeration radius, so more atoms become available to compose and larger settled structures become reachable. Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Therefore the predicted shape within a fixed closure is **growth then saturation**, with a jump only when an import moves the wall (Fig 6 :23). More range after the reach is exhausted does nothing. Per-slot indexing means some structures grow while others do not; the average hides where to look.

3. **Persistence is a channel property, not a size property.** Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does." A structure persists while its term remains settled against a ground that does not update and the channel carrying its residual stays reachable. Fig 11 :39-40: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." A large structure can fail with a saturated channel (Fig 10 :26); a small one can persist with maintained contact. Because a wider reach widens both habitat and cascade (Fig 11 :52-53), its effect on persistence has residual-relative valence (Fig 11 :49): it can stabilize or destabilize depending on what the ground is, not on how big the structure is.

4. **Size and persistence do not add.** Fig 12 :125-126: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add. Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds." Space and time are distinct states (Fig 13 :35-36), so the two outcomes cannot be merged into one score without losing the signal. Verdicts are kept separate.

5. **The numerical law is a `gap`.** No figure line returns an exponent for `ell -> S` or `ell -> P`. The framework returns a shape, not a number (Fig 9 :32). The world literature does contain exponents inside named models, but those are instance-level results from frames whose closures differ; importing one requires a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 8 :50-52) and gives a model-bound number, not a framework verdict. The gap is unreached, not silence (Fig 13 :134), and it is not proof that no law exists (Fig 8 :39).

6. **A scale change is a substitution, not a dial.** Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." Any experiment that claims to vary only `ell` must declare what it failed to reproduce and what it unintentionally introduced (Fig 11 :68-69). A synthetic test proves wiring, not capability (Fig 11 :70).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a universal power law, and not a monotone "more is better" rule.
- Not that size and persistence are independent in every instance. They are different quantities; they can co-vary when the same contact change moves both. The claim is that they cannot be averaged.
- Not that wider contact always stabilizes. The sign is relative to the residual (F6).
- Not that the habitat is preserved when the range changes. Fig 11 :66-69 applies.
- Not that the figures name a seventh term. Interaction scale is a parameter of the held space and network atoms (Fig 13 :10, :24).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement of size or lifetime was taken here.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

An instrument, not a further argument.

- **Habitat test:** enumerate contact outward from a declared residual and show whether new atoms actually enter the reachable set as `ell` grows. This closes the per-structure part of claim 2. Fig 11 :44 gives the enumeration rule.
- **Reach test:** show whether size saturates inside a fixed closure, or grows without bound. The latter breaks F2 of `01` and Fig 6 :21.
- **Channel test:** measure whether the channel to a non-updating ground remains reachable for a declared structure. Sever it and see whether persistence ends. This closes claim 3. Fig 10 :24, :26.
- **Union test:** show whether size and persistence can be expressed in one declared unit with no loss. This closes claim 4, or refutes it.
- **Instrument test:** obtain a stable exponent for `ell -> S` or `ell -> P` across low-overlap fields under a declared code. This converts the gap into a provenanced import rather than closing it by derivation.
- **Standing falsifiers from `01` section 4:** a kernel that changes with scale; unbounded growth inside a fixed closure; persistence with a severed channel; a shared anchor for the two outcomes; a figure line that states the number.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The six-term set is "a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes" (carried from the exemplar's mapping of *They're Scanning Books*); declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." Indiscernibility is not identity. The open exponent slot is part of the result, not an omission.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a universal power law for interaction scale and emergent structure. It says four things. The rule of emergence does not change with scale; only the actors, the currency and the ground change. Within one closure a wider interaction range brings more atoms into the habitat, so larger structures become reachable, but composition cannot add an atom, so the shape is growth then saturation. Persistence is a different quantity: a structure lasts while its channel to a ground that does not update stays reachable, so wider contact can stabilize or destabilize depending on the residual, not on size. The two outcomes have different currencies and cannot be averaged, and the numerical exponent stays open because the framework returns a shape, not a number. What is offered is that shape, four refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it.
