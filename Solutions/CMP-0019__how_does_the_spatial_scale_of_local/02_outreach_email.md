**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the union split in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F4 is accepted as a restriction on what the email claims. If the email merges size and persistence into one outcome, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** Check one shape claim before you decide whether the theory is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a figure-grounded derivation of a question in spatial complex systems: how the spatial scale of local interactions affects the size and persistence of emergent structures. I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual:** the question is usually read as if there were one law, interaction range to structure size and lifetime. The derivation says that is a union of two quantities with different anchors. Size is spatial and settles against a ground by composition. Persistence is temporal and settles against a ground by maintained contact. A single answer that averages them loses the signal, the same way a global residual hides a live slot.
>
> **The shape claim (`specified` on the per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural part, not `grounded`):**
>
> 1. The rule of emergence does not change with interaction scale. What changes is the cast: which actors are inside the habitat and how far the contact cascade reaches. (Fig 4 :19-20: "The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change.")
> 2. Within a fixed closure, a wider interaction range enlarges the habitat, so larger settled structures become possible. Composition cannot add an atom, so beyond the closure's reach more range does nothing. The predicted shape is growth then saturation, with a jump only when a new atom is imported.
> 3. Persistence is a channel property, not a size property. A structure persists while it stays settled against a ground that does not update and the channel to that ground stays reachable. Wider contact can stabilize or destabilize depending on the residual, not on size.
> 4. The numerical exponent relating range to size or lifetime is left open. The framework returns a shape, not a number. I am not supplying an exponent and I am not claiming one follows.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that there is a universal power law; that bigger interactions always make bigger or longer-lived structures; that a wider habitat is a substituted habitat with no cost (it is one, with things you failed to reproduce and things you unintentionally introduced); or that this is a measured result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. show that the composition rule itself changes with interaction scale while actors, currency and anchor are held fixed;
> 2. show a structure whose size grows without bound as range grows while the closure and the atom set are fixed;
> 3. show a structure persisting indefinitely after the channel to its non-updating ground is severed;
> 4. show that size and persistence share one anchor and can be averaged without loss.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the corresponding part of the claim is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: `https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md`
> - The wider literature that bears on this question: the ledger in `10_source_ledger.csv` of the packet.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - [ your name ]

## Stage order (three contacts, not one)

1. **Stage 1.** Send the residual, the four shape points and the four refuters. Ask what they would count as a failure. Record their verdict **before** showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the figures link and the ledger. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.
