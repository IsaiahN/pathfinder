## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Why two currencies and not one outcome

Fig 12 :125-127:

> **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**
> **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**
> **"They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them and will not notice the shortfall."**

Fig 13 :35-36:

> **"Space, time and energy are not three readings of one quantity. Neither are spectrum, gradient and network. Each answers a question the other two cannot: adjacency is not duration, an ordering is not a pathway, a pathway is not a direction."**

Size is a description-side quantity: how many slots are bonded into one settled molecule, and over what spatial span. Persistence is an action-side, contact-governed quantity: whether the channel to the ground still carries a residual (Fig 10 :24). A system that reports "size times lifetime" as one score is holding one accounting where two are required. This is not a preference about measurement; it is the structural reason F4 of `01` refuses the averaged answer.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** exhibit an anchor with respect to which size and persistence are the same quantity, so an increase in one is definitionally an increase in the other under a declared code. If that exists, Q1 fails and the two currencies can be added.

**Status:** `general`, triangulated across Fig 12 and Fig 13.

### Q2 - What changes when the range changes

Fig 4 :19-20 governs: the kernel does not change, only actors, currency and anchor. Fig 11 :43-44 governs the enumeration: "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."

So a change in `ell` is a change in the **membership of the habitat** and in the **depth of the cascade that matters**, not a change in the rule and not a new atom. In bond terms, the range parameter changes which operands are available to be bonded and therefore which arrangements are reachable; it does not change the bond types. Fig 12 :92: "Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing." The symmetry of the bonds is fixed; the set of operands is what grows.

This closes the apparent puzzle of F2 in `01`: a larger habitat does not force a larger structure, it makes a larger structure *reachable*. Whether it is minted still depends on the MDL bargain (Fig 5 :42: "The acceptance test is a bargain, not a threshold") and on settlement (THE_FORMULA step 5).

**Falsifier:** show a case where changing `ell` changes the bond set itself, for example where a symmetric bond becomes an ordered one because of the range alone while the operands are held fixed. That would break Fig 4 :19-20 and Fig 12 :92.

**Status:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 4, Fig 11 and Fig 12.

### Q3 - Are two structures at different ranges isomers

Fig 12 :117:

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fig 12 :94:

> **"The arrangement is the substance."**

Take two systems with the same agents, the same local rules and the same habitat conditions, differing only in the reach of interaction. Over the **full shared atom set** they have the same atoms. If the wider reach changes which structure mediates which state pair, the two have different arrangements, so by Fig 12 :117 they are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance. Their size and lifetime differences are then not a dose response to one variable; they are the behavioural difference between two substances.

The weaker reading is also available. If the two systems differ in habitat membership as well as arrangement, they are not isomers, because the atom set is not shared. They are a composition plus an import (Fig 6 :23) or a substituted habitat (Fig 11 :66). The isomer claim is therefore **conditional on holding the atom set fixed**, and that condition must be stated, not assumed.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if either system requires an atom the other does not, the isomer claim fails and the composition-plus-import reading stands. This is the same falsifier structure the exemplar used for GR and QM, here applied to scale.

**World analogue, corroboration only.** Landge et al. 2020 (ledger row 3) describe two regimes over the same reaction-diffusion ingredients: classical Turing patterns with a fixed wavelength that yields different numbers of peaks in differently sized domains, and wave-pinning patterns whose dimensions scale with the domain. Same components, different arrangement, different behaviour. That is an isomer reading at the instance level, and it corroborates the shape of Q3 without supplying a figure verdict. The two regimes share a literature pool with the other biology sources, so this is one witness.

**Status:** `specified`, single-source on the figure side (Fig 12), conditional on the fixed-atom clause, with world corroboration.

### Q4 - What is the order here, and what is not

A parameter change is not a bond. Fig 12 :46: "A bond is the typed relation between two operands." Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." A report that says "we increased the range and measured the size" has recorded the ingredient change and not the bond change. Under Fig 12 :95-96, two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing when the bond differs; the difference is carried entirely by the bond.

Order enters in two places, both already fixed by `01`:

1. **Dependency order among domains:** D1 before D2 before D3 (Fig 3 :20-21). The rule must be read before size, and size before persistence, because an unsettled molecule cannot persist (Fig 12 :104).
2. **Order inside a structure:** sequence (`->`) is an ordered bond, and whether range changes an ordered relation is an empirical question. The figures do not say that it does. Claiming it would add an ordering the derivation does not license, the same error the exemplar found in the QM/GR chain.

**Falsifier:** show that a change in range converts an ordered bond into a symmetric one, or reverses a dependency link, without changing the operands. Q4 would then fail.

**Status:** `general` on the parameter/bond distinction, `specified` on the dependency order.

### Q5 - Does the isomer reading help the outreach

Yes, in one narrow way. It converts the question from "how much does range matter" into a checkable structural question: "over a fixed atom set, are these two arrangements isomers, and which structure mediates which state pair?" A refuter can answer that in twenty minutes from the figure text plus their own system knowledge. It does not require them to accept the framework, and it does not ask them to trust a simulation. It also keeps the exponent slot open: isomers differ in substance, not by a scalar dose.

## CONCLUSION

Size and persistence are two currencies and cannot be averaged (Q1). A change in interaction range changes habitat membership and cascade depth, not the kernel and not the bond set (Q2). Over a fixed atom set, systems at different ranges are Fig 12 isomers, same atoms arranged differently; without a fixed atom set the honest relation is composition plus import or habitat substitution (Q3). A parameter change is not a bond, and no order is introduced by range alone (Q4). This rescues what is checkable in the question without filling the numerical slot or inventing a seventh term.
