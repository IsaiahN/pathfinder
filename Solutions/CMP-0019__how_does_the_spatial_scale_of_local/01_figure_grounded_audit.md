## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

The question asks for the effect of one control parameter on two outcome quantities. Write the parameter as `ell`, the spatial reach over which an agent's action changes another agent's situation. Write the outcomes as `S` (the size, extent or wavelength of an emergent structure) and `P` (its persistence, lifetime or resistance to dissolution).

State the options and what each leads to:

- **Option A (the literal reading):** there is a universal function, ideally a monotone one or a power law, `ell -> (S, P)`. Accepting A commits us to a single answer that can be stated before any domain is named, and it commits us to averaging size and persistence into one outcome or to reporting two numbers from one law.
- **Option B (the figure-derived reading):** the rule of emergence does not change with `ell`; what changes with `ell` is which actors are inside the habitat and how far the contact cascade reaches. Size and persistence are different quantities with different anchors, so they require separate verdicts that are never averaged. The numerical function is not returned by the figures.

The residual R is the gap between the question as posed (one law, two outcomes) and what the figures can license (a scale-free rule, a habitat-bounded size, a contact-governed persistence, and an open numerical slot). It is indexed per structure, not per system (Fig 1 :19): "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." A global statement such as "larger interactions make larger structures" is exactly the move Fig 1 :22 warns about: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, Direction): an answer is acceptable if it (i) states what changes with `ell` and what does not, (ii) gives separate verdicts for `S` and `P`, (iii) names an instrument that could settle each, and (iv) says plainly where the figures stop. This is the sought-for shape made refusable, as the Formula requires.

## 1. Domain census (before any figure line is chosen)

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** three quantities. One input (`ell`) and two outputs (`S`, `P`). Two outputs with different anchors is the signature of a union, not a single function (Fig 9 :54).
- **Symmetry:** `S` is spatial (Fig 13 :10, "near, far, between, contained"), `P` is temporal and contact-governed (Fig 13 :12, "the ordered succession of differences"; Fig 10 :24). They are not interchangeable. Fig 13 :35-36: "Space, time and energy are not three readings of one quantity. Neither are spectrum, gradient and network. Each answers a question the other two cannot: adjacency is not duration, an ordering is not a pathway, a pathway is not a direction." A single "size-and-persistence" score would multiply two non-commensurable readings, the error Fig 12 :125 names: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."
- **Scale:** this question is itself about scale, so the scale membrane (Fig 4) is a primary domain, not a metaphor. One loop closes within one scale; crossing between scales is a separate loop with `R_T` as its error signal (THE_FORMULA, Closure).

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor

A domain is admitted only if it has a nameable anchor. Where no anchor can be named, the item is not yet a domain and is recorded as a gap.

**D1. The scale membrane and the composition rule.**
Actors: an agent at one scale, the population at another, and the structure that composes between them (Fig 4 :4-17). Currency: methods crossing up, head starts coming down (Fig 4 :22). Anchor: the ground that settles a method before it may cross (Fig 4 :7-9, "carry up a method that can be reapplied verified, and compressed to its essentials"; Fig 5 :69, settling). Nameable anchor: yes, the ground. Question type: partition (which compositions are legal at every scale).

**D2. The size of an emergent structure.**
Actors: slots/objects, the atoms in the library, and the habitat enumerated outward from the residual (Fig 11 :43-44). Currency: composition inside `closure(Γ)`, indexed per slot (Fig 1 :19). Anchor: the ground settling a term, after which it becomes an atom for what composes over it (Fig 12 :104). Nameable anchor: yes. Question type: bounded reach (how many atoms can be composed into one settled structure).

**D3. The persistence of an emergent structure.**
Actors: the ground, the channel, the habitat and the seat that keeps the anchor reachable (Fig 10 :1-3, :20-29; Fig 11 :4-9). Currency: contact (Fig 11 :39-40). Anchor: the ground, which does not update on what a frame thinks (Fig 2 :29; Fig 10 :24). Nameable anchor: yes. Question type: maintenance (what keeps a settled term in contact with its anchor).

**D4. The numerical scaling law (candidate domain, refused).**
Actors: would be an instrument returning a scaling exponent for `ell -> S` or `ell -> P`. Currency: a number. Anchor: none available in this folder. No instrument was read that returns this number for the general case, and the framework explicitly says it returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32; Fig 13 :134). No nameable anchor, so **D4 is not admitted as a domain**. It is recorded as a `gap` in section 8, not as a silent omission. "Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence" (Fig 13 :134).

### 1.3 Partition or union

D1, D2 and D3 form a **union of overlapping conditions**, not a partition. The overlap is real: the same atoms that compose a structure (D2) are the ones whose contact must be maintained (D3), and the membrane rule (D1) governs both. Fig 9 :55-56: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds." A single lever, here "increase the interaction range", clears D2's region in some cases and leaves D3's intersections standing. Therefore verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged.

### 1.4 Dependency order (Fig 3)

Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." The order here is:

1. **D1 first.** Whether the composition rule is invariant with scale decides what a change in `ell` can even mean. Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." The link from "interaction range" to "the rule" has an instrument (the figure text itself), so it can be read.
2. **D2 second.** Size can be assessed only after the composition rule and the habitat enumeration are fixed.
3. **D3 third.** Persistence can be assessed only after a structure has settled (Fig 12 :104); an unsettled molecule cannot be said to persist.
4. **D4 last and separately.** A numerical law presupposes all three above and is refused as a domain because no anchor is available.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The kernel does not change with interaction scale (decisive for D1)

Fig 4 :19-20:

> **"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."**

Fig 4 :21:

> **"The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction."**

Fig 10 :68:

> **"Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between."**

Fig 12 :103:

> **"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."**

These are three low-overlap routes (a membrane between sizes, a ground maintainer's crossing, and the recursion of composition) to the same structural claim. The rule that governs emergence is identical at every scale; what varies is the cast. Therefore the literal Option A, in which `ell` changes the rule itself, is refused. What `ell` changes is which actors are in contact and therefore which atoms are available to compose.

**Mode:** `general` (structural), triangulated across Fig 4, Fig 10 and Fig 12.
**Refuter:** show a case where increasing the number of interacting agents or the spatial reach changes the loop or the naming rule itself, while actors, currency and anchor are held fixed. F1 would fail and the scale membrane would be a wall rather than a filter.

### F2 - Size is bounded by the enumerated habitat and the closure, not by range alone (D2)

Fig 11 :43-44:

> **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**
> **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**

Fig 6 :21:

> **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."**

Fig 6 :23:

> **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**

Fig 11 :52-53:

> **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**

A larger `ell` means the enumeration radius grows: more agents fall into direct contact, and second-order effects that were previously out of scope enter the habitat. That enlarges the set of atoms available for composition, so larger settled structures become possible. But composition stays inside `closure(Γ)`; when the reach is exhausted, additional range adds no atom and cannot enlarge the structure further. The predicted shape is therefore **growth then saturation within a fixed closure**, with a jump only when an import moves the wall (Fig 6 :22: "IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure").

Fig 5 :41 warns against reading mere machinery firing as a result: "Any one of them at zero stops the mint entirely. Which is why a system that is minting nothing may have nothing wrong with its minting." A wider interaction range that produces no new settled structure is not a failure of the system; it may simply be a closure with no new atom.

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 11 and Fig 6, with Fig 1 :19 on per-slot indexing.
**Refuter:** show a structure whose size grows without bound as `ell` grows while the closure and the habitat's atom set are held fixed. That would break the reach bound. Or show two structures in the same closure whose size is unaffected by a change in enumeration radius despite new atoms entering contact.

### F3 - Persistence is a channel property, not a size property (D3)

Fig 10 :24:

> **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**

Fig 10 :26-28:

> **"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"**
> **"the output is not a verdict, it is you have lost touch"**
> **"keep the anchor reachable"**

Fig 11 :39-40:

> **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**
> **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

Fig 2 :29:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**

A structure persists while two things hold: the term remains settled against a ground that does not update, and the channel carrying the residual to that ground remains reachable. Neither condition is the size of the structure. A very large structure can fail if the channel saturates (Fig 10 :26); a small structure can persist if its contact is maintained. Because a wider `ell` widens both the habitat and the cascade (F2), its effect on persistence has **residual-relative valence** (Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries"): extra contact can supply the redundancy that keeps an anchor reachable, or it can import a second-order effect that turns a convention into an outcome-fixing verdict.

Fig 10 :47:

> **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 10, Fig 11 and Fig 2.
**Refuter:** exhibit a structure that persists indefinitely after the channel to its non-updating ground is severed, or a case in which persistence is strictly a function of size with the channel held fixed. Either would break the contact verdict.

### F4 - Size and persistence cannot be averaged (union verdict)

Fig 12 :125-126:

> **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**
> **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**

Fig 13 :35-36 (quoted in section 1.1) and Fig 9 :55-56 (quoted in section 1.3) complete the case. Size is a spatial, description-side quantity; persistence is a temporal, contact-side quantity. The question's grammar makes them sound like two fields of one outcome, but Fig 9 says to unbundle a union before searching again, and Fig 12 says the two currencies do not add.

**Mode:** `general` (structural), triangulated across Fig 12 and Fig 13.
**Refuter:** show an anchor with respect to which size and persistence are the same quantity, so that an increase in one is definitionally an increase in the other. No such anchor is named by the question or the figures.

### F5 - The numerical law is a gap, not a silence

Fig 9 :32:

> **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**

Fig 13 :134:

> **"So the system can report that it lacks something rather than producing an answer anyway. Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"**

Fig 8 :39:

> **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

No figure line returns an exponent or a function relating `ell` to `S` or `P`. The world literature does contain such laws inside particular models (percolation correlation-length exponents, Turing wavelengths set by diffusion ranges), but those are instance-level results from frames whose closures differ from the six-term set. Under Fig 6 :23 and Fig 8 :50-52 they would have to be imported with a source gate and a shadow test, and the import would be debited against independence. The honest report is therefore: the figures license the shape in F1 to F4; the numerical law is a `gap` of the unreached kind, not an underivable-in-principle claim.

Fig 9 :44: "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing." This is why the gap is not converted into "no law exists".

**Mode:** `general` on the limit, `gap` on the number, triangulated across Fig 9, Fig 13 and Fig 8.
**Refuter:** produce a figure line that states a numeric scaling relation between interaction scale and structure size or lifetime. F5 would then fail and the slot would close by derivation rather than by import.

### F6 - Wider contact can either stabilize or destabilize; valence is not carried

Fig 11 :49-51:

> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
> **"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."**
> **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

This blocks the tempting blanket statement "more local interaction means more persistent structure". The sign of the effect depends on the residual in question and on whether the new contact keeps the anchor reachable (F3). The world sources corroborate this: in spatially extended ecosystems, the recovery trajectory depends on dispersal rate and on the scale of the perturbation, not on dispersal alone (van de Leemput et al. 2017/2018, ledger row 5).

**Mode:** `specified`, single-source on the figure side (Fig 11), with world corroboration.
**Refuter:** show that the sign of the persistence effect is fixed by `ell` alone across residuals, with the ground held constant.

### F7 - Isolation is substitution, not removal

Fig 11 :66-67:

> **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."**
> **"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."**

Changing the spatial scale of interaction is not the same as shrinking or enlarging an otherwise identical habitat. It substitutes one contact field for another, with two silent failures: what you failed to reproduce, and what you unintentionally introduced (Fig 11 :68-69). This applies directly to any experiment that claims to test "the same system at a different interaction range": the test harness is a substituted habitat, and Fig 11 :70 says "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."

**Mode:** `specified`, single-source on the figure side (Fig 11), strongly corroborated by the spatial-ecology sources.
**Refuter:** exhibit a scale change that preserves the entire habitat except one parameter, with no inherited actors and no missing conditions. In practice this is what a controlled numerical experiment attempts; the claim here is only that the substitution cost must be declared.

### F8 - A repeated range inside one closure is motion, not progress

Fig 7 :12-13 and :35-36:

> **"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"**
> **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**
> **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."**

Running many systems at many interaction ranges inside the same closure multiplies observations, not evidence about a new kind of structure. Each new room must differ from the last and match the residual the last one surfaced. This is the figure-grounded reason the outreach packet asks for a refuter, not a larger sample of agreeing simulations.

**Mode:** `general`, triangulated across Fig 7 and Fig 2 :40 ("disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool").
**Refuter:** show two frames at different `ell` whose agreement carries independent evidence about a new atom, with the closures demonstrated to differ.

### F9 - The round trip from the figure shape to a concrete prediction is lossy

Fig 4 :27-32:

> **"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."**
> **"Going up throws away detail; that is what makes the result reapplicable. But it means the trip is not reversible, and something carried up and brought back down will not match what was sent."**
> **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."**
> **"The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold, and it is the only honest report of how much was given up."**

The shorthand "interaction range sets structure size" is a condensation. Sending it back down to a concrete prediction drops at least three constraints: the per-slot indexing, the closure bound, and the channel condition on persistence. The gap `R_T` is recorded qualitatively in `11_audit.md`. Treating the shorthand as identical to the figure set would set `R_T` to zero and reproduce the dead-reckoning error the figure names.

**Mode:** `general`, single-source on the figure side (Fig 4), checked in the second-seat audit.
**Refuter:** show a concrete instance where the shorthand and the full figure set return the same size and persistence with no dropped constraint.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Required before the conclusion, per the exemplar's discipline: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. Written here before sections 5 to 8 were completed.

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Kernel failure.** The loop or the naming rule changes with the number of agents or the spatial reach while actors, currency and anchor are held fixed. This breaks F1 and Fig 4 :19-20.
2. **Reach failure.** A structure's size grows without bound as `ell` grows while the closure and the habitat's atom set are fixed. This breaks F2 and Fig 6 :21.
3. **Channel failure.** A structure persists indefinitely with the channel to its non-updating ground severed, or persistence is shown to be a strict function of size with the channel fixed. This breaks F3 and Fig 10 :24.
4. **Union failure.** Size and persistence are shown to share one anchor such that they can be averaged without loss. This breaks F4 and Fig 12 :125-126.
5. **Gap closure.** A figure line is found that states a numeric scaling relation between `ell` and `S` or `P`. This breaks F5 and closes the slot by derivation.
6. **Instrument condition.** An instrument returns a stable exponent for `ell -> S` or `ell -> P` across low-overlap fields under a declared code. This does not break F5 as a statement about the figures, but it converts the gap into an import with provenance and a shadow test under Fig 6 :23 and Fig 8 :50-52.

If any of 1 to 5 fires, the corresponding verdict is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with F1: the rule does not change with scale. It is the strongest figure-grounded point and it is triangulated across three figures with low overlap.
2. Report F2 and F3 as **two separate verdicts**, never as "bigger and longer-lived" in one sentence. Unbundle the union (Fig 9 :54).
3. Keep the numerical slot marked `???` rather than filling it with an exponent borrowed from a single model. If a number is wanted, import it with provenance and a shadow test and debit it against independence (Fig 8 :50-52).
4. Treat every scale-change experiment as a substituted habitat and declare what was failed to reproduce and what was unintentionally introduced (Fig 11 :66-69).
5. Send the generator, not a recording. Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion." The generator here is the residual, the domain census and the refuter.

## 7. SEARCHED / INWARD / OUTWARD

Kept distinct per Formula step 7.

- **SEARCHED (asked first, cheapest):** is the numerical law already inside the six-term closure and merely unfound? Answer: no figure line states it. More searching inside the closure will not add an atom (Fig 8 :32). The slot stays open as a gap.
- **INWARD (extend the instrument from one already returning something):** sharpen the measurement of the habitat radius and the channel state. The instrument already returning something is the enumeration of contact (Fig 11 :44) and the return-time measurement used in the spatial resilience literature (van de Leemput et al. ledger row 5). Sharpening those in our own terms can improve a per-structure prediction without importing a new kind.
- **OUTWARD (import the frame whose closure predicts the shape):** the percolation and reaction-diffusion frames already hold numeric laws for `ell -> S` inside their own closures. Importing one requires a provenance gate and a shadow test (Fig 8 :50-52), and the import is debited against independence. It gives a number for a named model, not a framework verdict.

INWARD and OUTWARD were run as two tracks and never read against one another (Fig 6 :31: "Neither answer tells you much about the other. Run them as two tracks, and never read one against the other.").
