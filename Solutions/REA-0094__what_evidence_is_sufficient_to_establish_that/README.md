## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals, and no purchased or scraped contact list.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree that a system is safe.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that any named model, lab, or deployment is unsafe. The question is structural: what would count as sufficient evidence.

Three figure-grounded reasons, then one practical one:

1. **Agreement is not a verdict.** Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 7 :13: "agreement compounds, evidence does not."
2. **Selecting respondents for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation.** Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Seat Map :163: "High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."
3. **Showing the whole frame before asking for a check spends the reader's independence.** Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source."
4. **Legal.** Unsolicited bulk email to harvested addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in and institutional submission routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
