# Outreach draft - check the shape of the evidence before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the stage-1 shape below survives the recipient's own check. If it does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.

---

**Subject:** What would count as evidence that a pattern causes its parts?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific shape of evidence I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the shape does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question in effect terms:** a large-scale pattern P and its parts. Two options. Either varying P while holding the parts and their local rules fixed changes the parts in a way a parts-only model cannot predict, or P is only a summary and nothing new happens at the part level. I am asking what observation would tell these apart.
>
> **The stage-1 shape (`specified`, not `grounded`):** evidence that would discriminate them must have four properties.
> 1. **Per slot, not per average.** A global fit improving is exactly how a live signal disappears. The residual has to stay visible on individual parts.
> 2. **Directed, not a correlation.** A correlation is symmetric, so it cannot tell pattern-to-parts from parts-to-pattern or from a shared cause. The evidence has to be an intervention on the pattern with the parts held fixed.
> 3. **A measured round trip.** Carry the pattern up to a coarse description, bring it back down, and measure the gap. A closed gap means summary. A nonzero, locatable gap is the honest report of what the coarse description holds that the parts alone do not.
> 4. **Two independent instruments against a fixed ground.** Agreement among people is a convention here, not evidence. The two checks must draw on different evidence pools.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular real system has been shown to have top-down causal power; that this settles the philosophical debate on strong emergence; that correlation, prediction, or expert agreement would be enough; or that the framework names the instrument. It returns a shape, not a name.
>
> **What would refute the shape, written before this message:**
> 1. an intervention on the pattern leaves the per-slot residual at zero under every macro perturbation;
> 2. the observed relation is symmetric, or a common cause accounts for it;
> 3. the round trip closes with a zero gap everywhere;
> 4. the apparent surplus vanishes under a second instrument from a different evidence pool.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Causal emergence, Hoel, Albantakis and Tononi, PNAS 2013: https://www.pnas.org/doi/full/10.1073/pnas.1314922110
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures folder. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
