## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Why a correlation cannot be the evidence

- Fig 12 :59 *"swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No."* identifies the symmetric bonds.
- Fig 12 :74 *"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."* identifies the ordered bonds.
- A correlation between pattern P and part state p has no arrow: swapping the operands leaves the relation unchanged. It is a `+`-type fact. The claim "P has causal power over p" is ordered: P to p is not p to P.
- Fig 13 :47 *"If the whole carries a property neither part has, the whole is not the parts arranged."* The claim is about directedness, so the evidence must be directed.
- ⇒ A correlation, however stable, cannot fire the bond the claim needs. An **intervention on P with p held fixed** can. This is the figure-level reason the world literature's do-operator test is relevant, and why the test in `01` F3 is specified the way it is.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a case where swapping pattern and part leaves a genuinely causal relation intact. Under the table's tests, that relation was not ordered and the claim fails.

### Q2 - Sequence, production, and the two kinds of top-down claim

- Fig 12 :72 *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"* (sequence).
- Fig 12 :77 *"A produces B; B did not exist before"* (production).
- A weak reading of top-down power is **sequence**: changing the pattern precedes and constrains a change in the parts, but produces nothing new at the part level. A strong reading is **production**: the pattern brings about a part state that did not exist before and is not derivable from the parts alone.
- The figures license ordering the tests weak first. If the sequential test fails (no per-slot change after intervention), the production test is not reached. If it passes, production still needs its own ground: a new part state with no prior existence.
- The philosophical sources match this split: the SEP entry records weak emergence as dependence plus distinctive efficacy, and strong emergence as fundamentally novel powers; McLaughlin's *"there is not a scintilla of evidence"* applies to the strong reading. The folder does not assert the strong reading.
- **Falsifier:** a case where the pattern produces a demonstrably novel part state under intervention would move the strong reading from `candidate` to a ground-backed claim; a case where only sequence holds keeps the weak reading and refuses the strong one.

### Q3 - Cross-level isomers: same parts, different causal power

- Fig 12 :117 *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 13 :121 *"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."*
- The world source makes this concrete. Hoel, Albantakis and Tononi (2013) construct a micro system whose macro coarse-graining has higher effective information than the micro even though the macro supervenes on it. Two descriptions share the same underlying parts and differ in grain; their causal effectiveness differs. Under Fig 12's definition that is an **isomer** relation across levels: same atoms, different arrangement (here, different grain), different causal substance.
- This is an **analogue and an import, not a derivation**. It carries a source gate (PNAS 2013, read 2026-11-22) and a shadow test: it explains why an intervention-based, cross-scale comparison is the right shape of evidence. It does not show any real complex system has such a surplus.
- **Falsifier:** if two grains over the same parts always yield equal causal effectiveness under intervention, the isomer reading collapses into mere redescription and the pattern is a summary.

### Q4 - Dependency order of the test

- Fig 9 :27-28 *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*: write the per-slot residual before choosing the instrument.
- Formula step 2: refuse the symmetric-correlation proxy before minting a causal term. Negation is not a bond; the refusal adds a term and removes the standing of the wrong one.
- Fig 12 :104 *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it"*: settle the first-order part-to-part model before composing a cross-level comparison.
- Fig 4 :30 *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*
- Order: **describe residual, refuse correlation, settle part model, intervene on pattern, measure R_T, triangulate**. Effort spent above a break cannot rescue one below it (Fig 3). A test run out of order returns a reading of nothing.

## CONCLUSION

A correlation is the wrong bond because it is symmetric; the claim needs an ordered intervention (Q1). The weak reading (sequence/constraint) is testable before the strong reading (production of novel part states), and the strong reading has no current empirical support (Q2). Two grains over the same parts can differ in causal effectiveness, which is a cross-level isomer relation, but this is an imported analogue rather than a figure derivation (Q3). The whole test has a fixed dependency order, and violating it makes failures unlocatable (Q4).
