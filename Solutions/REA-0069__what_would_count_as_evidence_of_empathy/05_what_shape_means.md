## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

Essay (5), *Library of Babel*: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

For REA-0069 this means: the figures can return the shape of the instrument that would settle source (discriminating, non-updating anchor, per-slot, built from a proto-instrument), and the shape of the necessity verdict (per habitat, residual-relative, ablation-tested). They cannot return the name of the instrument, and they cannot return a standing list of which psychological atom is required for every social task. "A source discriminator that already returns something" is a shape. "Use functional near-infrared spectroscopy on the machine's affect module" is a name, and no figure licenses it.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, then look for recurring new terms that overlap with it. *"With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."*
- Essay (3), *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."*
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: *"You will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place, as there is nowhere else to search and nothing else that can describe the answer better."*

Applied here: start from the closest available vocabulary, which is the machine-empathy literature's own distinction between detecting a state, predicting a reaction, and responding in a way that simulates empathy (Wikipedia, *Artificial empathy*). Narrow toward an effect description: an instrument would be one whose output changes when the internal source changes while the outward behavior is held fixed. That is a description anyone can check, and it does not require the reader to share the framework's vocabulary.

## 3. What licenses a name

Two things, both outside the shape: the ground settles it, or a provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs (Fig 6 :23; Fig 8 :50-52). Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*

In this question, a name such as "this system's response came from affective resonance" is licensed only by a source-discriminating instrument that returns that answer and does not move when the system's own report changes. A name such as "empathy is required for social competence" is licensed only by an ablation in a named habitat against an outcome that neither side can move. Until then, the name is the searcher's own contribution and is recorded as such.

## 4. The decisive essay for this question: source, not behavior

Essay (7), *The Hard Problem is About Source* (2026-08-28), is the governing essay. Its central claim, quoted verbatim:

> *"You can have several underlying causes that create identical readings but the definition of the output would be read as the same."*

and

> *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information (it cannot define consciousness in full)."*

The color case: an instrument reads green and *"the instrument is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source."* Green may be one green light, yellow plus blue, RGB, CMYK, or *"a completely novel color scheme that humans have not yet discovered."* The full set of ways to produce a reading *"is not known to be closed or determined."*

The resolution is explicitly instrumental, not argumentative:

> *"What the Spectrometer Example Reveals: The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."*
> *"It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."*
> *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it."*
> *"This is why the True Hard Problem is about determining the Source."*

That argument transfers directly. An empathic utterance is a green reading. A rule-based policy, a trained predictor of mental states, and an affective-resonance architecture can all produce it. The question "what would count as evidence" is therefore the question "what would discriminate the source." The honest status is `unreached`, not "no evidence is possible," because a better instrument may arrive (Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence"*).

## 5. Validation and prestige: why the answer cannot validate itself

Essay (2), *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (2026-11-19), gives two rules that govern this solution's status and its outreach.

> *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself. An external validator, or ground, is always needed to tether the veracity of the output to reality."*

So a derivation that concludes "machines can be empathetic" using the same fluency it is trying to explain is the named failure. This folder is a simulated derivation and says so on every substantive file.

> *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly."* And: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."*

A widely cited rating of chatbot empathy funds a trial; it cannot settle the source question. The same essay's closing disclaimer is carried forward verbatim: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."*

## 6. Fluency versus accuracy, and the bundle warning

Essay (3), *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* (2026-11-10):

> *"Sounding right isn't the same as being checked against the world, which is why I call the Turing test a test of fluency, not of accuracy."*

An empathy benchmark is the Turing test in a clinical coat: it measures whether the response sounds right to a rater. The essay adds that *"text is mostly context with some subtext and almost no pretext,"* and that a model trained on the record *"reproduces 'the traces the thought left, not the thought'."* That is the structural reason a text-trained system's empathic reply cannot be taken as a reading of its source: the pretext (the lived history that makes an empathic act what it is) is the part that does not travel in the text.

Essay (5), *Library of Babel*, supplies the union warning:

> *"If the final book in your exhaustive search doesn't answer the question, then there are only two possibilities: the nature of your question was unanswerable, or malformed."*
> *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"*

REA-0069 is exactly such a bundle: evidence and necessity, each with its own anchor. Unbundling is the first move (Fig 9 :54-55).
