# Isomers, catalysts and cycles: supporting derivation for ECN-0043

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the shape.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The primary audit leaves three structural questions open. (1) When two economies, sectors or periods differ in productivity, are they mixtures of the same atoms in different amounts, or isomers over a shared atom set? (2) What is the order of the four domains, and in particular what is the relation between the reinforcement cycle of compound growth and the closure wall of finite resources? (3) Does "shape, not a name" forbid naming a productive mechanism? This file answers all three and attaches a falsifier to each.

## DERIVATION

### Q1. Does "shape, not a name" forbid naming a productive mechanism? CORRECTION TO MY FIRST READING IN `05`

- The canonical figures README: "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." The subject is a primitive **not held** in Γ.
- Energy, gradient, network and spectrum are already among the six (Fig 13). An economic binding onto one of them is a **binding from another frame**, not the invention of a seventh term.
- Fig 13 :74-75: "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one." and "Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state." These lines fix the *form* of a composition, not which held atom fills a slot.
- ⇒ Naming a held atom, or binding an economic stock onto it with provenance, is permitted. What is forbidden is inventing a seventh, or claiming that the binding moves the wall: Fig 6 :21 "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."
- **I overstated this earlier.** My first pass turned a limit on missing primitives into a ban on all naming. Retracted as applied to held atoms; the narrow rule stands. Status: `general`, triangulated (README + Fig 13 + Fig 6). Strain recorded in `01` section 5.

### Q2. Are two economies or sectors isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- **Weak reading.** Two sectors compared only by their TFP numbers, with no shared atom list, are not shown to be isomers. They are two entries in a table. A difference in a scalar cannot establish a difference of bond.
- **Strong reading.** Over a fixed atom set, for example the same labour, capital, energy, materials and institutional network, two sectors that differ only in which structure mediates which state pair are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance. Their TFP residuals can differ decisively while their ingredient lists are identical. This is the checkable version and the one worth sending.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** exhibit a compared pair whose difference is only a quantity of the same atom in the same bond, with an identical arrangement. The isomer claim fails and the mixture reading stands instead.

Status: `specified`, single-source for the figure definition (Fig 12), with the atom-set condition carried as a live constraint. The *They're Scanning Books* essay corroborates the general shape from a different direction: "Adding data and parameters to a fixed architecture is the whale strategy: more units, same structure, diminishing returns." More of the same atom in the same bond is a whale strategy; the structural change is the isomer.

### Q3. What is the order of the domains?

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

1. **Measurement first.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Without the per-slot reading, the next three steps operate on a number that has already hidden the signal (Fig 1 :22).
2. **Habitat and closure before lever.** Fig 11 :43-44: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed. List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." Fig 9 :53: "Nobody chooses. The gap does, and the gap is only as good as its description." Aging is enumerated as habitat; the material stock is recognised as a closure wall. They are not the same move.
3. **Refuse before mint.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 2: a term held that is false owes a refusal, not an explanation. In this question the held term is "growth is one dial driven by one lever." Fig 12 / Operators :58: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate." Negation is unary, not a bond (Fig 12 amendment), so the refusal is not a new arrangement of the four domains.
4. **Settle before composing.** Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world." A per-slot productivity finding must be settled against the ground before it becomes an operand in a cross-domain claim.
5. **Import only after the wall is described.** Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." The import in F3 of `01` is sought only after the material closure is characterised in effect terms.

**Falsifier:** show that capital deepening (step 4 type effort) can rescue a chain broken at the measurement link (step 1). If it can, the dependency order is wrong.

### Q4. Cycle versus wall: do they conflict?

Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Compound growth has this shape: output becomes capital and research, which re-enter as input. Fig 8 :32: "no search, however long, adds an atom." Fig 13 :14 names the finiteness condition: "conserved, finite, directionless alone."

These govern different quantities. The cycle rule governs the **dynamics of a loop** given its atoms; the closure rule governs the **reach** available to the loop. A reinforcing loop can run until it meets the wall; when it does, adding more of the same input buys search at the same reach (Fig 6 :49-50). The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, but the wall decides what a closure would have to consist of. No contradiction after re-census. This is recorded as S3 in `01` section 6 because the two are habitually conflated in growth discourse.

**Falsifier:** produce a reinforcing productive cycle whose output re-entry adds an atom to its own closure without any import from a frame whose closure differs. That breaks Fig 8 :32 and dissolves the distinction.

### Q5. Catalyst, functional group and the demographic case

Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is." General-purpose methods, institutions and research capability have catalyst shape: they survive their own use and change the rate of composition without entering the stock as a consumed operand. A stock of routine equipment is a spent part; a method that keeps being reapplied is a catalyst (Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down.").

For aging, Fig 12 :123 (functional group) is the right shape, not Fig 12 :117 (isomer). An age cohort is a qualifier inside a functional group; what fixes the outcome is the contact relation it holds (care, mentorship, physical substitution), not the bare count. Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." So a policy that treats one age group as permanently good or bad is the standing-list error.

**Falsifier:** show that an age cohort's valence is invariant across two residuals whose contact structures differ. That breaks Fig 11 :49-51 and the functional-group reading.

## CONCLUSION

1. Two economies over a fixed atom set differing only in bond are **isomers** (Fig 12 :117); without a shared atom list the claim is not even well posed. Weak scalar comparisons are refused.
2. The dependency order is measurement, habitat/closure, refusal, settlement, import (Fig 1 :19, Fig 11 :43-44, Fig 9 :27-28, Fig 12 :104). Effort above a broken measurement link cannot rescue it (Fig 3 :19).
3. The reinforcement cycle of compound growth and the material closure wall are **not** in conflict: one governs loop dynamics, the other governs reach (Fig 12 :121 versus Fig 8 :32).
4. Catalyst shape applies to methods and institutions; functional-group shape applies to cohorts. Neither returns a numerical growth contribution.
5. The "shape, not a name" limit governs missing primitives, so naming a held atom or a provenanced binding is allowed; claiming the binding moves the wall is not (Q1, Q4).

What this framing rescues is the structural core of the productivity debate without inventing a seventh term or a universal percentage. What it refuses is the scalar mixture reading, the standing age-group list, and the claim that a bigger input of the same kind escapes the wall.
