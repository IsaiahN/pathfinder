## 0. The residual, in effect terms

The question asks whether a measured flow, economic growth, continues because of a compositional change, productivity, or is held back by two conditions, an aging population and finite resource stocks. Stated in effect terms:

> R = the gap between the growth the current frame predicts from its own aggregate measure and what the ground returns at each sector slot when the age structure and the material channels change.

Three features matter before any figure is chosen.

1. **Arity.** R has three terms, not two. Productivity is a change in arrangement. Aging is a change in the structure of contact. Resource limits are a finiteness condition on what can be spent. The question presents them as one either/or, so the first defect to test is whether the two sides form a partition or a union (Fig 9 :54-58).
2. **Symmetry.** "Improving productivity" is ordered. It is a change from one arrangement to another, a sequence or a production (Fig 12 :70-79), and a pooled correlation cannot choose the arrow on its own. "Aging populations" and "resource limits" are threshold relations, comparisons against a count or a stock (Fig 12 :85-89). Treating all three as symmetric dials on one growth number is a type error.
3. **Scale.** Productivity improvement is a within-frame rearrangement (Fig 6 :21). Aging is a population-level habitat change (Fig 11 :43-48). Resource limits are a substrate-level closure condition (Fig 8 :32, Fig 13 :14). One loop at one scale cannot absorb all three.

The residual must stay indexed per slot. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A national growth rate is exactly such an average. The BLS 2026 industry releases make the point without any theory: total factor productivity fell in 70 of 86 four-digit manufacturing industries in 2023, and rose in only 7 of 19 major industries in 2025. A single number would have buried both distributions.

**What an answer would look like (bound before the run).** It would return per-slot residuals, an actor/currency/anchor line for each domain, verdicts kept separate with modes, a stated import requirement where the closure is finite, and an instrument that could settle each verdict. It would not return a universal percentage or a named winner. This is the Direction discipline of `THE_FORMULA.md`: make the sought-for shape an ordinary term so it can accrue a residual and be refused.

## 1. Domain census before choosing any figure line

R by arity, symmetry, scale is given in section 0. One line per domain follows: actors, currency, anchor. A domain without a nameable anchor is not yet a domain and is marked as such.

### D1, the productivity and arrangement domain

- **Actors:** firms, workers, capital, methods, research teams, technologies, patents.
- **Currency:** output per unit input, priced in two incommensurable currencies, bits for the description and actions for finding out whether it holds (Fig 12 :125-127).
- **Anchor:** a market that pays for the composition and a measurement that does not move when the frame changes its own prediction (Fig 2 :29-30, Fig 2 :33).
- **Type:** composition inside `closure(Γ)` (Fig 6 :21); arrangement over held atoms (Fig 13 :115).
- **Question asked here:** can the arrangements go on being recombined, and does a recombination add a new atom or only a new bond?

### D2, the demographic and habitat domain

- **Actors:** workers by age, dependents, caregivers, automation, schools, health systems, migration.
- **Currency:** contact, workers per dependent, hours, care relations (Fig 11 :45-47: actors, conditions, relations).
- **Anchor:** the same market ground read per slot, plus a demographic count that does not update on what anyone thinks about it.
- **Type:** habitat (Fig 11 :43-44), enumerated not composed. Capability is a property of agent-and-habitat (Fig 11 :39).
- **Question asked here:** does an age structure change capability by changing contact, and is its valence fixed or residual-relative?

### D3, the material and closure domain

- **Actors:** energy, materials, stocks, conversion technologies, research frames that could supply a new atom.
- **Currency:** a difference that can be spent (Fig 13 :22: "a difference that can be spent"), against finiteness (Fig 13 :14: "conserved, finite, directionless alone").
- **Anchor:** physical measurement of stocks and flows, a ground that does not update on a forecast.
- **Type:** closure wall (Fig 8 :32, Fig 6 :23). Imports move the wall and need a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 8 :12-13).
- **Question asked here:** can more search inside the closure add what the closure lacks, and what would have to be imported to move the wall?

### D4, the measurement and aggregate domain

- **Actors:** statistical agencies, national accounts, index-number constructors, sector classifications.
- **Currency:** description length and settlement cost (Fig 12 :125-127), not a single growth number.
- **Anchor:** the ground, never the measure. A convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention (Fig 10 :47).
- **Type:** frame-internal quantity (Fig 1 :24-27); a residual that depends on the frame's own estimates.
- **Question asked here:** does the measure register the live slot, and does it stay a measure once policy starts optimising it?

### Partition or union

Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." The four domains overlap: productivity research consumes energy and is shaped by the age of researchers; demographic change alters which sectors have live productivity residuals; the resource wall conditions which arrangements are affordable. This is a **union**, not a partition. Overlaps are compounds with their own loops and their own grounds. One lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.

### Dependency order (Fig 3)

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

1. Repair the measurement link first (D4). Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." An aggregate growth rate with no per-slot instrument is a broken middle link. More investment in capital deepening above that break cannot rescue it.
2. Describe the residual per slot before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28). Otherwise any frame will seem to fit.
3. Enumerate the habitat and the closure before choosing a lever (Fig 11 :43-44, Fig 9 :53). Age structure is habitat; resource stock is closure; confusing them sends a habitat intervention to a wall.
4. Within D1, refuse before mint (`THE_FORMULA.md` step 2) and settle each first-order molecule before composing a second-order one (Fig 12 :104).
5. Read D2 and D3 as conditions on the same per-slot ground, keeping verdicts separate.

## 3. Findings F1 to F9

### F1. The question is a union of conditions, not a two-sided dial (decisive for the framing)

Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."

The productivity claim, the aging claim and the resource claim have different actors, currencies and anchors (section 1). A single answer of the form "yes, growth continues" or "no, it is constrained" would average three incommensurable verdicts. Fig 1 :19 and :22 forbid the averaging move before the union is even named. Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." A growth-rate target is a filter for effort allocation; it is not a witness for any of the three domains.

**Mode: `general` (structural), triangulated** across Fig 9 :54-58 (union/partition law) and Fig 13 :115 "The set is closed. The arrangements are not." Low overlap: one is a search-structure rule, the other a substrate rule.

**Refuter:** show that the three terms share one anchor and one currency such that an increase in one can be subtracted from a decrease in another, with an assignable shared fraction. That would make it a partition. None of the opened sources supplies it; the TFP article explicitly says the residual depends on estimates of the other components, which is the opposite of independent anchors.

### F2. Productivity growth is a rearrangement that adds no atom; new capability requires an import

Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 13 :115: "The set is closed. The arrangements are not." Fig 13 :116: "six members, and no amount of arranging produces a seventh: a new member would have to be a new kind, and composition only ever rearranges the kinds already held."

So there are two distinct things called productivity improvement:

| kind | figure reading | what it buys | what it cannot buy |
|---|---|---|---|
| rearrangement of held atoms (better bonds, better sequencing, diffusion of a known method) | composition inside closure, Fig 6 :21; isomer, Fig 12 :117 | more output from the same held set, a new arrangement | a new atom, a moved wall |
| genuinely new productive capability (a new energy conversion, a new material, a new institutional frame) | import from a differing closure, Fig 6 :23; source gate and shadow test, Fig 8 :12-13 | a moved wall, new reach | it is not free; "the only operator that moves the wall" (Fig 6 :14) |

Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Two economies with the same labour, capital, energy and institutions but different arrangements are isomers, not different mixtures. This is why a TFP residual can move without any new stock appearing.

World corroboration (secondary): Bloom, Jones, Van Reenen and Webb (NBER 23782, 2017; AER 2020) report that "research effort is rising substantially while research productivity is declining sharply." More researchers at the same reach buy more search, not more atoms, matching Fig 6 :49-50 "more search at the same reach: is it in here and unfound? The closure does not change." The Wikipedia closure-operator entry states the mathematical reason neutrally: "cl(cl(X))=cl(X) (cl is idempotent)."

**Mode: `specified` on the distinction, `general` on the closure law. Triangulated** across Fig 6 :21/:23, Fig 13 :115-116 and Fig 12 :117 (three different figure objects), plus a low-overlap mathematical witness. Not grounded: no technology-level settlement was examined.

**Refuter:** demonstrate a case where a productive capability is composed inside a frame's closure without any import from a frame whose closure differs, in the precise sense that the new term is not derivable from the frame's held atoms before the change. That would break Fig 6 :21. A merely larger TFP number does not fire it.

### F3. Resource limits are a closure wall, and efficiency inside the wall does not move it

Fig 8 :32: "no search, however long, adds an atom." Fig 13 :14 names the substrate term: "conserved, finite, directionless alone." Fig 13 :22 names its spendable form: "a difference that can be spent." Fig 6 :14: "the only operator that moves the wall" is import, with "source gate: provenance" and "shadow test: explains a gap" at Fig 6 :12-13.

The resource claim is therefore not a claim that growth must stop at a date. It is a claim about the kind of remedy that can work. More search and higher efficiency inside the current closure cannot add what the closure lacks (F2). What can move the wall is an import from a differing closure, and the figures require a provenance gate and a shadow test before that import counts. Which atom would be imported is not returned: Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself" and Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."

The Jevons paradox source corroborates at the mechanism level. Jevons wrote: "It is a confusion of ideas to suppose that the economical use of fuel is equivalent to diminished consumption. The very contrary is the truth." Efficiency lowers effective cost, induces demand, and can accelerate the depletion it was meant to avoid. That is the world-facing shadow of Fig 6 :21: a rearrangement inside the closure changes the flow without adding a stock. The TFP article adds that "external costs including attributes of the workforce, public infrastructure such as highways and environmental sustainability costs such as mineral depletion and pollution are not traditionally included" in the measure, so the wall can be invisible in the very number used to declare growth.

**Mode: `specified` on the wall and the import requirement, `gap` on the identity of the atom. Triangulated** across Fig 8 :32, Fig 13 :14/:22 and Fig 6 :12-14 (closure theorem, substrate finiteness, import rule). Low overlap between the three figure objects; world corroboration is genuinely low-overlap resource economics.

**Refuter:** show that the economy's productive closure is not finite in the relevant sense, or that a new stock was added by composition alone, or that the efficiency gain actually changed contact with a non-updating physical ground rather than only the ratio. None was run here.

### F4. Aging changes capability through habitat and contact, and its valence is residual-relative

Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

An aging population is not a wall like a finite stock. It is a change in the habitat: which actors are in contact with the productive residual, which conditions prevail, which relations (care, mentorship, migration) carry influence (Fig 11 :45-47). Older workers are not a fixed "malfactor"; the same age structure is a benefactor for a residual that needs accumulated tacit knowledge and a malfactor for a residual that needs rapid physical substitution. The source on population ageing records exactly this openness: it notes both that "the older workers would spend more time on work and human capital of an ageing workforce is low, reducing labor productivity" and that "An ageing population may provide incentive for technological progress, as some hypothesise the effect of a shrinking workforce may be offset by automation and productivity gains." These are not two opposing verdicts to be averaged. They are a valence that flips with the residual at the slot, which is the figure's prediction.

Niche construction corroborates from a genuinely low-overlap field: "niche construction is the ecological process by which an organism alters its own (or another species') local environment." Capability sits in the agent-and-habitat loop, not in the agent alone.

**Mode: `specified` on the habitat/contact reading, `gap` on the net sign and size in any particular country. Triangulated** across Fig 11 :39-40/:49-51 and Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Low overlap: habitat law versus channel law.

**Refuter:** show that an economy's capability can be raised by improving productive agents in isolation, with their habitat and contact structure held fixed, at the same ground. That would break Fig 11 :40. Or show that an age group has a valence that does not change with the residual, breaking Fig 11 :51.

### F5. The aggregate measure is frame-internal and can stop being a measure

Fig 1 :24-27: "coverage, and compression achieved", "predicates minted, and credibility accrued", "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." The TFP article says it plainly: "As a residual, TFP is also dependent on estimates of the other components." The official BLS description says MFP "compare[s] growth in output to the growth in a combination of inputs that include labor, capital, energy, materials, and purchased services." The measure is a subtraction (Operators :46: "progress is a subtraction, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot say how far along it is") and its inputs are chosen inside the frame.

Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." Once a growth target allocates budgets and bonuses, it is no longer a neutral reading of the ground. Goodhart's law, as quoted by Strathern (1997), predicts the same thing from a different direction: "When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure." Fig 10 :26 lists the failure mode: "saturated metrics, pooled readings".

This does not make measurement useless. Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does." What is owed is a channel that stays reachable, not the abandonment of measurement. The BLS industry-level releases are a good shape: per sector, per year, with the declines visible.

**Mode: `specified`, triangulated** across Fig 1 :19/:22/:24-27 and Fig 10 :24/:47, with a low-overlap measurement witness. No claim that any specific agency's number is false.

**Refuter:** show a growth statistic that (a) preserves the per-slot residual rather than averaging it away, and (b) does not update on the frame's own estimate of its inputs, and (c) remains a measure when used to allocate budgets. If such a statistic exists, the frame-internal finding does not apply to it.

### F6. Isomer reading: same atoms, different bond, different growth substance

Fig 12 :117 (F2) plus Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." Over a fixed atom set, two economies or two sectors that differ only in which structure mediates which state pair are isomers, not different mixtures. If they do not share the full atom set, they are members of the same functional group with different qualifiers. This is worked out in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use." Research institutions and general-purpose methods have catalyst shape: they survive their use and change the rate without entering the stoichiometry. Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Compound growth is a cycle, and the sign of the loop (reinforcing or depleting) decides whether it closes against a wall. These are shape claims, not calibrated mechanisms.

**Mode: `specified` on the isomer/catalyst/cycle shape, `gap` on which atoms are actually shared by any two named economies. Single-source for the figure definitions (Fig 12) with the physics-free analogy kept visible; corroborated for the general shape by niche construction's feedback loops, which are a different field.**

**Refuter:** exhibit two compared economies where the difference is only a quantity of the same atom in the same bond. That kills the isomer reading and leaves a mere mixture.

### F7. The objective link is the one that usually breaks, and it has no instrument

Fig 3 :24-27 names the middle link: having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress. Fig 3 :25: "The middle link is where chains usually break." Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

Most growth effort lands in capital deepening, R&D spending and labour participation, which are links above the break if the broken link is the ability to say what counts as progress per slot. The figures give a condition, not a name: what is owed is an instrument at the objective link. Fig 6 :32: "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something." Existing industry-level TFP releases and physical stock accounts are such worse instruments; they are where a sharper one would be built.

**Mode: `general` (structural), single-source on the figure (Fig 3) with the instrument condition corroborated by the measurement literature.**

**Refuter:** show a domain within this question where the objective link is measured and not assumed, and where extra effort above it still fails. That would move the break.

### F8. What would settle each verdict is a ground, not agreement

Fig 2 :29-30: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update." Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."

## 4. SEARCHED, INWARD, OUTWARD, kept distinct

Per `THE_FORMULA.md` step 7, when no line closes the residual, ask all three, starting with the cheapest.

- **SEARCHED (is it in here and unfound?)** Yes. The relevant lines are in the closure already: Fig 6 :21 (composition), Fig 8 :32 (wall), Fig 11 :39-40 (habitat), Fig 1 :19 (per slot). Fig 6 :49-50 says more search at the same reach only visits more of the space: "Distribute the search, and keep the searchers independent." The eight world sources were opened as independent searchers where their fields allowed.
- **INWARD (extend the instrument from one already returning something).** Owed: a per-sector, non-updating ground reading of productivity, a physical stock-and-flow reading at the same slot, and a demographic contact count. Existing BLS industry releases and physical accounts are the worse instruments to sharpen (Fig 6 :32). No such instrument was built here, so this track remains open.
- **OUTWARD (import a frame whose closure predicts the shape).** Owed for the resource wall specifically: a frame that already holds the missing atom, with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :12-13, Fig 8 :52). Niche construction (habitat) and closure theory (mathematics) were imported as *shape corroboration* only, not as new productive atoms. Fig 8 :53: "Residual first, frame second." The residual was described in section 0 before any of these frames were named.

INWARD and OUTWARD were not read against one another. Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget remain different facts: the national percentage is unreachable at this budget (no ground reading), not underivable in principle.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required by the seat discipline: "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." The shape claim is refuted if any of the following fires:

1. **Union refuter.** The three terms are shown to share one anchor and one currency with an assignable shared fraction, making them a partition (breaks F1, Fig 9 :54-58).
2. **Closure refuter.** A productive capability is composed inside a frame's closure without any import from a differing closure, where the new term is not derivable from held atoms before the change (breaks F2, Fig 6 :21).
3. **Wall refuter.** The productive closure is shown not to be finite in the relevant sense, or an efficiency gain is shown to have changed contact with a non-updating physical ground rather than only a ratio (breaks F3, Fig 8 :32, Fig 13 :14).
4. **Habitat refuter.** An economy's capability is raised by improving productive agents in isolation with habitat and contact held fixed, or an age group is shown to have a valence that does not change with the residual (breaks F4, Fig 11 :39-40/:51).
5. **Measure refuter.** A growth statistic is produced that keeps the per-slot residual, does not update on its own inputs, and remains a measure when used to allocate budgets (breaks F5, Fig 1 :19, Fig 10 :47).
6. **Isomer refuter.** Two compared economies differ only by a quantity of the same atom in the same bond (breaks F6, Fig 12 :117).
7. **Ground refuter.** A per-slot, non-updating market or physical reading shows growth continuing or stopping in a specific economy over a specific interval. This settles the empirical instance, not the shape, and is recorded here as the instrument owed rather than a result.

If any of 1 to 6 fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn. Until one is run, nothing in this folder may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Strains, re-census, and visible corrections

### S1. Fig 13 :14 is a physical substrate term, not an economic measure

First pass: I treated "resource limits" as if the figure directly named oil, minerals or land. Re-census: Fig 13 :14 governs the held atom "energy" as "conserved, finite, directionless alone"; Fig 13 :22 governs its spendable form. Applying that shape to economic stocks is a **binding** from the resource frame onto a held atom, not a derivation of a seventh primitive. Naming the specific stock needs provenance. The strain is narrowed to the binding and kept visible. Not retracted, because the shape itself is load-bearing in F3.

### S2. Fig 6 :21 versus the observation of wholly new productive capabilities

First pass: read as a contradiction, since economies plainly acquire new capabilities. Re-census: Fig 6 governs the closure of a *frame*, that is, what can be expressed from its atoms, not the world's physical stock. A new conversion technology is an import from a frame whose closure differs, with source gate and shadow test. Different objects, so no contradiction after re-census. The boundary of "the frame" remains a live design choice and is named as such in F2.

### S3. Fig 12 :121 cycle versus the closure wall

A reinforcing cycle (output re-enters as input) can run until it meets a wall; the sign of the loop decides whether it closes. Fig 12 :121 governs loop dynamics; Fig 8 :32 governs reach. Both apply, neither overrules the other. Recorded as a composition of two figures, not a strain after re-census, but kept visible because the two are often conflated in growth discourse.

### S4. Fig 1 :19 per-slot residual versus the GDP aggregate

Dissolved by re-census: the figure governs where the residual lives; national accounts produce a frame-internal aggregate. A live type question remains from Fig 10 :47: at what point does a measure that allocates budgets stop being a convention? This is a real type question, named in F5 and carried into `08` and `11`, not smoothed away.

### S5. The contact claim versus the ordinary reading that productivity improvement raises growth

Dissolved by re-census: a gain that changes only an accounting ratio does not change contact and therefore does not change capability; a gain that crosses as a method into a reproduced habitat does. Different objects, so the figure is not contradicted. Kept visible because it is the most tempting misreading of F4.
