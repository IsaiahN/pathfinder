# The Tether's answer: can researchers experimentally create a chemical system that grows, maintains itself, and undergoes heritable change without modern enzymes?

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain verdicts and the union reading; `general` on the structural shape. One narrow sub-claim is `grounded` by an experiment (the Lincoln and Joyce 2009 cross-replicating ribozymes), not by this derivation. Not `grounded` on the headline question.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question is delivered as a yes or no about a conjunction: grows, maintains itself, undergoes heritable change, no modern enzymes. In effect terms it is a **union compound** with a boundary condition (Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*). The three properties have different actors, currencies and anchors, and the exclusion says where the information needed to evolve must live. An answer that returns one verdict on "life" aggregates across slots and throws away the only signal that says where to look (Fig 1 :19, :22).

The sought-for shape was written down before the run (`01` section 0): locate each property in a figure-governed composition, say where the information lives, keep the verdicts separate, and name the instrument that would close the gap.

## 2. The shape

1. **The question is a union, not one dial.** Growth, maintenance and heritable change overlap without reducing to one another. One lever does not clear the intersection (Fig 9 :54-55). Verdicts below are per domain and are never averaged into "sort of alive".

2. **Growth and heritable change without modern enzymes: yes, narrow and `grounded` by the experiment.** Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* The Lincoln and Joyce cross-replicating RNA enzymes amplify exponentially "in the absence of proteins or other biological materials"; recombinant variants arose and dominated a shared substrate pool. The loop closes inside a closure that carries its own informational atom. This settles two of the three properties, under the exclusion, and nothing more. It is a `grounded` sub-claim because the measurement pays for it, not because the framework says so.

3. **Maintenance: yes in the chemical sense, `specified` mapping with a `grounded` anchor in the RAF algorithm.** Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."* A reflexively autocatalytic, food-generated set maintains itself on an ambient food supply; the RAF algorithm returns the maximal set or the empty set, which is a nameable anchor. This is catalytic closure, not thermodynamic self-sufficiency. The open-system critique of "self-sustaining" governs energy flow, a different quantity, so there is no conflict after re-census.

4. **The exclusion is a boundary, not a missing ingredient.** Fig 8 :51: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*. A ribozyme catalyst is generated inside the system and satisfies the exclusion. A protein polymerase supplied by the experimenter is "information for free" evolved elsewhere; Joyce calls the picture "a puppeteer that handles all the information offstage". "Catalyst" is a functional group (Fig 12 :123) whose modern-enzyme and ribozyme members are alternatives, so the question excludes one member, not the group. Whether supplied tools count as part of the system is a provenance stipulation that must be recorded, not a fact the chemistry decides.

5. **All three at once, complete and open-ended: not demonstrated.** Coupled vesicle growth and division with retained contents exists (Zhu and Szostak 2009), nonenzymatic copying of short templates inside vesicles exists, and cyclophospholipid vesicles now tolerate Mg2+ plus RNA extension (Toparlak et al. 2023). But the same 2023 paper says there is "no plausible path for the emergence of a self-replicating protocell" under prevalent formulations, and Joyce says of his own system "It doesn't make it" and "Our laboratory system is capable of evolving, but thus far it has not evolved any new functions." This is a `gap` of the **unreached at this budget** kind (Fig 8 :40-43), not underivable in principle: the missing thing is an instrument that discriminates "capable of evolving new functions" from "has evolved in a test tube for a while". Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence (Fig 13 :134).

6. **"Is it alive?" is the wrong question.** Fig 11 :36: *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran."* Capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone (Fig 11 :39). The laboratory flask is a substituted habitat: a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability (Fig 11 :70). The answerable question is what makes a chemical system capable of growth, maintenance and heritable change in contact with a food supply, not whether a boundary can be declared around it and the word "alive" applied.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that "life has been created in a test tube". "Life" is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground (Fig 11 :36); even the co-author of the NASA working definition refused it for his own system.
- Not that the JCVI minimal cell answers this question. JCVI-syn3.0 (Hutchison et al. 2016) is a synthesised genome in a modern recipient cell with 473 genes, 149 of unknown function. Different closure, different question.
- Not that any system has yet evolved a genuinely new biochemical function in the lab. None of the cited sources claims this, and Joyce says explicitly that his has not.
- Not that enzymes of any kind are excluded. Catalysts are required (Fig 12 :119); the question excludes a modern protein member of that functional group.
- Not that the framework names RNA, peptides or RAF networks as *the* answer. It returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32). These chemistries are provenanced bindings from the laboratory frame.
- Not `grounded` as a whole. Only the narrow experimental sub-claim is.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

An instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

- **Source discriminator:** an experiment that distinguishes a self-contained evolving system from a harness whose catalysts and information were supplied offstage. It would read whether the information necessary to evolve is inside the system, in Joyce's phrase "Where are the bits of information?"
- **Novel-function test:** a demonstration that a chemical system without modern enzymes evolves at least one new function not supplied by the experimenter. Joyce: "If there's enough complexity to invent a new biochemical function, that would do it for me." This is the test that would collapse the union verdict into a clean yes and fire refuter 1.
- **Complete-protocell test:** a single system combining a self-replicating genome, a membrane that grows and divides with content retention, and nonenzymatic copying of mixed-sequence templates long enough to encode function, in a plausibly prebiotic habitat with no refreshed feed. Fig 11 :70 says the harness result proves wiring; this test would move it toward capability.
- **Standing refuters from `01`:** overturn the 2009 ribozyme result; prove heritable change impossible without protein enzymes; show the cycle/catalyst lines govern other quantities; re-census the substrate to admit a seventh member.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The six-term set is *"a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes"*; declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral. Another sensorium's minimum set need not be these six. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

The live boundary strain is real: drawing the system boundary to include or exclude supplied tools changes whether the exclusion is met, and no figure line draws it uniquely. That is recorded as a design constraint (S1 in `01`), not hidden. The type question on ⋛'s operands at the population scale is likewise named.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer "yes, we have made life" or "no, it is impossible". It says the question is a union of three conditions plus a boundary, with verdicts kept separate. Growth and heritable change without modern enzymes have been achieved: the 2009 cross-replicating RNA enzymes amplify in the absence of proteins and pass inherited variants to descendants that take over. Self-maintenance has a formal anchor too: food-generated autocatalytic networks close the loop that keeps a chemical system going. But no single system does all three in a complete, open-endedly evolvable way in an unmaintained habitat, and the celebrated synthetic-cell results use a modern enzymatic apparatus. The hard part is the boundary: tools supplied by the experimenter were evolved outside the system, so they are "information for free" unless they evolve with it. What is offered is the shape any answer must have, the three refuters written in advance, and the instrument still owed: a discriminator that would tell a self-contained evolving system from a puppet show. The centre stays open until the ground settles it.
