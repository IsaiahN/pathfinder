# Expert verification packet: can researchers experimentally create a chemical system that grows, maintains itself, and undergoes heritable change without modern enzymes?

**What this is:** a claim in a form a specialist in synthetic life or origins of life can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape and the per-domain verdicts; `general` on the structural claim. One narrow sub-claim (C2) is `grounded` by a published experiment, not by this derivation. Not `grounded` on the headline question.** This packet is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question is a union compound, not one yes or no. Growth, food-generated self-maintenance and heritable change can each be realised without modern protein enzymes, but not yet as one complete, self-sustaining, open-endedly evolvable system in an unmaintained habitat. The cross-replicating RNA enzymes of Lincoln and Joyce (2009) grow exponentially and transmit heritable fitness differences in the absence of proteins, which settles two of the three conditions narrowly. Reflexively autocatalytic, food-generated networks provide a formal, decidable anchor for maintenance. The exclusion "without modern enzymes" is a boundary and provenance condition: an RNA catalyst is inside the closure, a protein polymerase supplied by the experimenter is "information for free" from outside it. "Life" is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground, so the answerable question is what makes a chemical system capable, not whether it can be declared alive. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument and a novel-function test.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 9, 11 and 12

- **Statement:** the three properties are different compositions with different bonds, and the enzyme exclusion is a unary negation that constrains the closure rather than composing inside it. Growth is production plus cycle; maintenance is catalyst plus cycle; heritable change is sequence plus comparison. "Catalyst" is a functional group whose protein-enzyme and ribozyme members are alternatives, so excluding modern enzymes does not exclude catalysis.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :121 - *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Fig 12 :119 - *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."* Fig 12 :123 - *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Fig 9 :57 - *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."* Operators table :73-74 - *"− is A with B removed or absent, which is the absence of an operand. Negation is the refusal of a claim."*
- **Refuter:** show that the three properties reduce to one quantity with one anchor (one lever clears all three), or that a system can grow, maintain and evolve with no catalyst of any kind. Either result breaks the bond mapping and the union reading.

### C2 - chemistry-facing; checkable against the published experiments

- **Statement:** a chemical system has been demonstrated that grows and undergoes heritable change without modern protein enzymes, but it has not evolved a new function. This is the single `grounded` sub-claim, and it is grounded by the measurement, not by the framework.
- **What to check:** Lincoln and Joyce, *Science* 2009 (PMC2652413): two RNA enzymes catalyse each other's synthesis from four oligonucleotide substrates; the system "undergo[es] self-sustained exponential amplification in the absence of proteins or other biological materials", doubling in about an hour; in serial transfer, recombinant replicators arose and grew to dominate. Joyce's 2009 CSHL review calls it "the first example, outside of biology, of evolutionary adaptation in a molecular genetic system" and says "thus far it has not evolved any new functions". If those statements hold, the narrow claim stands and the boundary at "capable of evolving new functions" is where the gap sits.
- **Refuter:** show an undisclosed protein or other biological material in the reaction, show the recombinants did not transmit differential fitness, or exhibit a new biochemical function evolved in such a system without experimenter-supplied information. The last fires refuter 1 and makes the answer a clean yes.

### C3 - boundary and instrument; checkable against the Q&A source and the protocell literature

- **Statement:** "without modern enzymes" is satisfied inside the ribozyme closure but is a stipulation, not a property, when the experimenter supplies polymerases. A test-tube evolution result is a reading of a substituted habitat and cannot by itself establish capability.
- **Source, verbatim (world):** Joyce, Astrobiology Q&A: "You can evolve RNA in the test tube using biological proteins to copy it, mutate it and do all those fun things that make it evolve. But it's not alive because all those tools that you're using, protein polymerases and so on, were evolved not in the system, and can't evolve in the system." Also "there's not a puppeteer that handles all the information offstage". Fig 11 :70 - *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."* Fig 8 :51 - *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"*
- **What to check:** in any claimed system, list every catalyst and every source of activated substrate and ask whether it is produced inside the closure or supplied from outside. If supplied, note that the exclusion is met only by drawing the boundary around the system, and that the record must say so.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a system that continues to grow, maintain and evolve when the substituted habitat is removed or made plausibly prebiotic with no refreshed feed. Then the harness limitation does not apply.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that life has been created in a laboratory, and not that "life" is a settled term. Fig 11 :36: *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran."*
- Not that the JCVI minimal cell (Hutchison et al. 2016, 473 genes, 149 of unknown function) is relevant to an enzymes-free system. It is transplanted into a modern recipient cell.
- Not that any system has evolved a genuinely new biochemical function without experimenter-supplied information. None of the cited sources claims this.
- Not that catalysts are unnecessary. Only modern protein enzymes are excluded; ribozymes and mineral catalysts are members of the same functional group.
- Not that the framework names RNA or RAF networks as *the* chemistry. It returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32).
- Not `grounded` as a whole. This packet is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency.

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Source discriminator:** an instrument or protocol that reads whether the information necessary to evolve is inside the system, rather than supplied offstage. Joyce's own question: *"Where are the bits of information?"*
2. **Novel-function test:** a chemical system without modern enzymes evolves a new function the experimenter did not supply. This fires refuter 1 and turns the union verdict into a clean yes.
3. **Complete-protocell test:** one system combining a self-replicating genome, a membrane that grows and divides with retained contents, and nonenzymatic copying of mixed-sequence templates long enough to encode function, without a refreshed feed. The 2023 ACS Nano paper records the current gap.
4. **Against the shape:** overturn the 2009 result, prove heritable change impossible without protein enzymes, or re-census the substrate to admit a seventh member.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 12 :119, :121, :123, Fig 9 :57, Fig 11 :70 and the Operators table on ¬ versus −. Six short passages.
2. Check C2 against the 2009 paper's abstract and results: is the amplification genuinely protein-free, and did recombinants transmit fitness?
3. Read the two Joyce quotes in C3. Decide whether your own claim about "synthetic life" survives the puppeteer test.
4. Name one instrument that would distinguish a self-contained evolving system from a harness, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
