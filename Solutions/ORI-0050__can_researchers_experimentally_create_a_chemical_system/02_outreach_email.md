**Subject:** Check one output before you decide whether "we made life" is the right description

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the shape, one narrow `grounded` sub-claim, not a verdict on "life"):** the question "can researchers experimentally create a chemical system that grows, maintains itself and undergoes heritable change without modern enzymes" is not one yes or no question. It is a union of three conditions plus a boundary condition, and the three have different actors and different anchors. So the honest answer is per condition:
>
> 1. **Growth and heritable change without modern enzymes: yes, and this one is settled by experiment, not by my derivation.** The Lincoln and Joyce cross-replicating RNA enzymes (Science, 2009) amplify exponentially in the absence of proteins and other biological material, and recombinant variants arose and took over a shared substrate pool. I mark this `grounded` because the measurement pays for it. It does not mean the system is alive, and it does not mean it invented a new function.
> 2. **Self-maintenance: yes in the narrow chemical sense.** Reflexively autocatalytic, food-generated (RAF) networks maintain themselves on an ambient food set, and the RAF algorithm returns a set or the empty set. This is catalytic closure, not thermodynamic self-sufficiency, and I am not claiming the latter.
> 3. **All three at once in a complete, self-sustaining, open-endedly evolvable system: not demonstrated.** Coupled vesicle growth and division with retained contents exists, nonenzymatic copying of short templates inside vesicles exists, and open-ended evolvability does not. The gap is an instrument, not an argument.
> 4. **The boundary condition is the subtle one.** If a lab supplies protein polymerases to copy the RNA, those tools were evolved outside the system and cannot evolve inside it. Joyce calls that "information for free" and warns against "a puppeteer that handles all the information offstage". I record exactly which catalysts and substrates the experimenter supplies, and I do not count them as part of the system unless they evolve with it.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that a synthetic cell has been made from scratch. The JCVI minimal cell (Hutchison et al., Science, 2016) contains 473 genes, 149 of unknown function, and it works by transplantation into a recipient cell with a full modern enzymatic apparatus. It is a different closure. I am not claiming my own derivation settles anything: it is a simulated check for internal consistency against a framework, not a measurement.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. A single system that grows, maintains itself on a food supply without externally supplied protein enzymes, undergoes heritable change, and evolves a genuinely new function the experimenter did not supply, with no puppeteer. That would make the answer a clean yes and kill my union reading.
> 2. Proof that heritable change is impossible without protein enzymes, overturning the 2009 result.
> 3. A source-discriminating instrument showing the test-tube systems were never capable of open-ended change.
>
> I wrote these down before writing the conclusion. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
