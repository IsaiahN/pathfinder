# The Tether's answer on permafrost infrastructure

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the refuter and the measurement procedure, not `grounded`.** No measurement was taken. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks whether the observed rate of infrastructure destabilization from thaw exceeds the expected rate. In effect terms this is a union compound (Fig 9 :55): several asset classes, several failure mechanisms, and a comparison between two rates at each slot. It has its own ground and its own loop. "Northern infrastructure" as a single number is the wrong unit (Fig 1 :19).

## 2. The shape

1. **Measure per slot, not in aggregate.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Buildings, roads, pipelines and railways can move at different rates and for different reasons. A single global percentage is an advocacy statistic, not a residual.
2. **The anchor is the ground.** Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." Fig 2 :36: an anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it. This question is constitutively about ground state.
3. **The "expected" rate is a frame, not an anchor.** It moves when the inventory beneath it is corrected. Manos et al. 2025 added buildings that earlier maps had missed and roughly doubled the loss estimate while using the same hazard product. That is a frame moving. Hjort et al. 2018 acknowledge their threshold is conservative because structures can fail before the mapped thaw threshold is crossed. Both are refusals of the expected term, not measurements of a new rate.
4. **The comparison is ordered.** Fig 12 :74: "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes." `observed ⋛ expected` is a different claim from `expected ⋛ observed`. The Operators table defines ⋛ as a threshold relation between two quantities; two rates qualify. The operand-type caveat is named, not hidden (`01` F9).
5. **The cascade is at least three steps deep.** Fig 11 :44: "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." Warming, ground temperature, ice loss and settlement, structural damage, service loss. The harm a community experiences is rarely the first-order reading.
6. **The instrument gate is asymmetric.** Fig 6 :32: "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something." Where no borehole, survey or observer record exists, there is no edge to extend.
7. **The figures stop at the shape.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence." No figure line supplies a rate, a date or a cost. Those are world imports, provenanced and secondary.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that thaw is definitely destabilizing infrastructure faster than expected everywhere. That is a measurement claim and no measurement was taken here.
- Not that any published percentage or dollar figure is correct. The 70 percent and 80 percent figures come from world sources with stated scopes and inventories; the 2025 study explicitly argues earlier ones were too low.
- Not that one global risk number can be repaired by averaging better. Fig 1 :22 forbids treating the aggregate as the signal.
- Not a six-term substrate mapping onto permafrost. That reading was retracted as a strain (`01` F9).
- Not a derivation of permafrost physics from the framework. The framework is a barrier theorem about residuals, grounds and composition, not a cryosphere model.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by an instrument in this run.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating measurement, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (essay): "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists." The corresponding questions here are:

1. For a named asset class at a named site, does a settlement or tilt time series from InSAR, levelling or a borehole-mounted sensor exceed the design-expected rate over a declared interval?
2. Does the expected term move when the inventory and the design assumption are corrected? If it does not, the frame/anchor distinction in section 2 fails.
3. Does the third-order harm (utility rupture, service loss, spill) occur at the predicted place in the cascade, or is the first-order ground signal the whole story?
4. Standing falsifiers from `01` section 3: a figure line asserting a direct rate; an expected term that behaves as an anchor; asset classes sharing one mechanism and one anchor; a partition where a union was claimed.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The figure closure contains the shape and the falsifiers but no empirical rate. The permafrost literature contains rates but shares evidence pools, so its agreement is one witness, not several (Fig 2 :40). The answer stops where the instruments stop and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *They're Scanning Books* (essay): "A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes"; here the relevant guess is which slots and which intervals get measured. That choice must be declared, not hidden.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say whether thaw is faster than expected everywhere, and it takes no measurement. It says that the question must be asked per asset and per site, because a residual is indexed by slot and an aggregate average hides the live signal; that the ground is the anchor while the "expected" rate is a frame that moves when the infrastructure inventory is corrected, as a 2025 Alaska study showed by roughly doubling earlier loss estimates; that the comparison is ordered and the persistent residual points at the expected model rather than at the observation; and that the harm to a community is usually several steps down a cascade from the first ground-temperature reading. What it offers is the shape any checkable answer must have, three falsifiers written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The rate stays open until the ground settles it.
