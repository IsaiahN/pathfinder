## 0. The claim under audit, stated as a residual

The question asks whether the rate at which thaw is destabilizing northern infrastructure exceeds the rate at which it was expected to. In effect terms the claim is a comparison between two quantities:

- **o′ (observation):** the rate at which a particular asset at a particular site actually loses support, tilts, cracks, settles or ruptures.
- **Γ(b,a) (prediction):** the "expected" rate, built from design codes, hazard maps, permafrost inventories, climate scenarios and maintenance assumptions.

The residual is `R = |Γ(b,a) − o′|` taken per object slot. "Faster than expected" is the case where `R⁺_s > 0` at one or more slots and the residual persists without trending down. Fig 5 :59 names the diagnostic: **"a term whose residual PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN is the one to point the refusal at"**. In this question the persisting residual points at the expected-rate term, not at the ground.

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term (Formula step, "Direction").** A checkable answer is: *for asset class s at site n over interval t, the measured destabilization rate exceeds the design-expected rate by δ, with δ and its instrument stated, and with the expected-rate model identified as the term that failed.* Anything short of that is a preference for a conclusion, not an answer.

### R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** at least two quantities (expected rate, observed rate) and multiple slots (buildings, roads, pipelines, railways, airports). The question is a **union compound**, not one hard question. Fig 9 :57: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"**.
- **Symmetry:** not symmetric. The comparison is ordered ("faster than" has a direction), and the causal chain is ordered (warming precedes thaw precedes settlement precedes failure). Fig 12's sequence bond applies where order matters.
- **Scale:** per site and per asset, over decades. Ground thermal response near the surface is on the order of years; infrastructure design life is typically 20 to 50 years. The residual is local even when the forcing is circumpolar.

## 2. Findings

### F1 — The residual is per slot; a global percentage hides the live signal

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**

Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

The Symbols table says the same at the notation level: R is measured per slot, never as a single global number. Buildings, roads, pipelines, railways and airports are different slots with different failure modes and different cost curves. The world literature confirms the divergence. Melvin et al. (PNAS, open access) report that about 90 percent of building damages in their Alaska model came from near-surface permafrost thaw, while roughly 75 percent of road damages came from flooding; pipeline damages were a small fraction of the total. A single "70 percent of Arctic infrastructure at risk" statistic, however useful for advocacy, is not a residual measurement at any slot.

**Verdict:** triangulated (Fig 1 plus the Symbols table and the per-asset tables in Melvin et al.; figure rule and empirical table have low overlap). **Mode:** general.

**Refuter:** show that the asset classes share one mechanism and one anchor, so that aggregating them discards no signal. If that holds, this finding fails.

### F2 — The ground is the anchor; the expected rate is a movable frame

Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."**

Fig 2 :36: **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."** The question is constitutively about ground state, so the ground is the legitimate anchor.

Fig 2 :45: **"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."**

Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**.

The "expected" rate is a frame. It moves when you push on it. Manos, Witharana and Liljedahl 2025 (open access) explicitly report that "losses are underestimated in existing literature due to incomprehensive infrastructure maps", and that adding mapped buildings roughly doubled the loss estimate while using the same hazard product. That is the expected term moving under a corrected inventory, exactly what a frame does and an anchor does not. The 2018 Hjort study likewise notes that its threshold is conservative because "infrastructure (e.g., buildings) could experience thaw settlements and failure before the thaw of near-surface permafrost", which is an explicit statement that the expected model trails the ground event.

**Verdict:** triangulated (Fig 2 and Fig 10, low overlap between the anchor definition and the channel-decay claim). **Mode:** general on the anchor rule, specified on the claim that the expected term moved in the 2025 study.

**Refuter:** produce an expected-rate term that does not move under correction of the inventory or the design assumption. That would make it an anchor and break this finding.

### F3 — "Faster than expected" is a persistent residual, and it points at the expected model

Fig 5 :59: **"a term whose residual PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN is the one to point the refusal at"**.

The comparison is a ⋛ relation between two rates. The Operators table defines ⋛ as "a threshold relation between two quantities" with the test "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes." That fits the structure of "faster than" (which rate is larger), with a type caveat recorded in section 6: the operands are rates, not the structures the framework's ⋛ examples usually compare. Under Fig 5 :59, the term to refuse is the one whose prediction keeps being wrong without improving. The world record supplies three concrete refusals of that kind: the incomplete inventories documented by Manos et al. 2025, the conservative thaw threshold acknowledged by Hjort et al. 2018, and the explicit exclusion of permafrost hazards from the US National Risk Index noted by Manos et al. 2025. Each is a failure of the expected term, not of the observation.

**Verdict:** single-source within the figures (Fig 5), with three independent world refusals as low-overlap corroboration of the mechanism. **Mode:** specified.

**Refuter:** show that the observed rate tracks the expected rate within instrument error across all slots over multiple intervals, using an instrument that has previously returned a nonzero signal elsewhere.

### F4 — The habitat must be enumerated; the residual that matters is several steps out

Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**

Fig 11 :44: **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**

Fig 11 :48: **"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."**

Fig 11 :52 to :53: **"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**

The cascade here is: atmospheric warming, ground temperature rise, active layer thickening and loss of excess ice, reduced pile bearing capacity and differential settlement, foundation tilt and pavement cracking, pipe strain or utility rupture, and finally service loss and environmental release. Scoping to first contact (warm ground) misses the third-order effect. Melvin et al. document a water pipe break that left an Alaska clinic without water for days; Hjort et al. 2018 note that damage to pipelines and industrial facilities may lead to large-scale oil spills. The residual that matters to a community is not the millimetre of settlement but the clinic without water.

Fig 11 :40: **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."** A better climate model does not change contact at a foundation.

**Verdict:** triangulated (Fig 11 plus Fig 3 dependency order, different figures with low overlap). **Mode:** general.

**Refuter:** show that the cascade terminates at first contact for every asset class, so that second and third-order enumeration adds nothing.

### F5 — "Could" is governed by the search limit, not by a figure rate

Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

Fig 9 :44: **"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."**

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**.

Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"**.

The question uses "could", which is a possibility claim. No figure line asserts any rate or direction for permafrost infrastructure. A search of the figure set that finds no permafrost content therefore proves nothing about the world; it only locates the boundary of the closure. What the figures do yield is the shape an answer must have: a per-slot, positive, persistent residual, settled at the ground. The specific rate is a provenanced import from permafrost science and remains a `gap` within the figure closure.

**Verdict:** triangulated (Fig 8, Fig 9 and Fig 13, three separate figures). **Mode:** general.

**Refuter:** produce a figure line that directly asserts a rate, date or direction for permafrost infrastructure damage. That would convert the content from a gap into a figure-derived result.

### F6 — The instrument gate is decisive and asymmetric

Fig 6 :32: **"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."**

Fig 6 :34 to :35: **"Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."**

Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

Manos et al. 2025 report that most remote Alaskan communities lack digital maps of infrastructure and property ownership, that few have completed site-specific permafrost risk assessments, and that permafrost hazards are excluded from the US National Risk Index and from NOAA national climate and weather loss accounting. In those slots there is no instrument returning a failing signal, so there is no edge to sharpen. Where instruments do exist (GTN-P boreholes, satellite interferometry, local environmental observers), the residual is already registering and can be refined. The gate is asymmetric: adding an instrument where one exists is work; inventing one where nothing has ever registered is not available to the loop.

**Verdict:** triangulated (Fig 6 and Fig 3, different figures, low overlap). **Mode:** specified on the world claim, general on the figure rule.

**Refuter:** show a site where no prior instrument returned any signal and a rate comparison was nevertheless settled by measurement. That would break the instrument gate.

### F7 — Agreement among the world sources is one witness, not several

Fig 2 :40: **"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**.

Fig 7 :13: **"agreement compounds, evidence does not"**.

Hjort et al. 2018, Hjort et al. 2022, Melvin et al. and Manos et al. overlap heavily: several share authors, and they reuse OpenStreetMap infrastructure data, GTN-P ground temperatures and the same Streletskiy-derived hazard and damage functions. Their shared headline that roughly 70 percent of Arctic infrastructure sits in high-thaw-potential areas is the pool talking. However, Manos et al. 2025 is a genuine second witness on the specific "larger than expected" dimension: it uses a different instrument (deep learning on 0.5 m satellite imagery) to correct the inventory, and it reports a result that conflicts with the earlier magnitude estimates. Fig 8 :50 to :52: **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"**. The record, not the headline, is what makes this a conflict rather than a repetition.

Fig 2 :42: **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."**

**Verdict:** triangulated (Fig 2 and Fig 7). **Mode:** specified.

**Refuter:** show that the 2025 estimate is derived from the same inventory as the earlier estimates, in which case it is a re-analysis rather than a second witness.

### F8 — The question is a union compound; unbundle before searching

Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**

Fig 9 :56: a single lever "clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."

Fig 8 :53: **"Residual first, frame second."**

"Buildings, roads, pipelines, and other northern infrastructure" bundles at least four conditions: ice-rich ground, warming, structural load and design or maintenance practice. Thermosyphons and convection embankments clear the region where warmth is the only condition. They leave the intersections (warm ground plus ice-rich soil plus a missing inventory plus deferred maintenance) standing. The Library of Babel essay (opened 2026-11-22) names the same failure: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"

**Verdict:** single-source within the figures (Fig 9), corroborated by the differentiated asset results in the world sources. **Mode:** general.

**Refuter:** show that one lever clears the whole region, which would make this a partition rather than a union.

### F9 — What the figures do not give

Declined mappings, kept visible rather than silently erased:

1. **The six-term substrate does not govern this question.** Fig 13 :74 says "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one." Mapping permafrost onto space, time, energy, spectrum, gradient and network would be a strain, because the six terms govern the composition of primitives and carry no empirical rate. *They're Scanning Books* (essay, opened 2026-11-22) warns that "A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six." Mapping the six onto permafrost would turn a visible choice into an invisible one. **Retracted as a strained reading.** Fig 13 is used here only for its claims about unreachability and narrowing.
2. **No figure gives a rate, date or cost.** The figures are a barrier theorem about residuals, grounds and composition. They are not a permafrost model. Any number in `09_watson_answer.md` comes from a world source with provenance.
3. **"Faster than expected" is not a verdict about the whole Arctic.** It is a question about whether a residual is positive at particular slots. The honest figure-only answer is a `gap` filled by a secondary import, not a silent non-answer.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Required before any outreach. Written before the conclusion, as a design instrument.

The shape claim ("this is a per-slot residual comparison, settled at the ground, with a movable expected term and a multi-step cascade") is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A figure line is found that directly asserts a rate, date or direction for permafrost infrastructure damage. That would make the content figure-derived rather than a gap.
2. A ground measurement shows that at every asset slot the observed destabilization rate does not exceed the design-expected rate, taken with an instrument that has previously returned a nonzero signal somewhere. This is `R⁺_s = 0` across all slots with a live channel, not a dead instrument.
3. The expected term does not move when the inventory and design assumptions are corrected. That breaks Fig 2 :29.
4. The asset classes share one mechanism and one anchor, so aggregation loses no signal. That breaks Fig 1 :22.
5. A re-census shows the question is a partition of independent single-condition questions rather than a union compound. That breaks Fig 9 :55.

If any fires, the shape claim is withdrawn. Until one is run, nothing in this package is `grounded`.
