**Subject:** One checkable claim about permafrost infrastructure damage rates

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` shape, `specified` refuter, not `grounded`):** "Destabilize faster than expected" is a comparison between two rates. One is what the ground is actually doing at a particular asset, measured per site and per asset class. The other is the expected rate: a design assumption, a hazard map, a cost model. The figures' rule is that a residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated. So "northern infrastructure" is the wrong unit. Buildings, roads, pipelines, railways and airports are different slots, and they can move at different rates. A single 70 percent number hides which slot is live.
>
> A second rule says the anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change because of what any frame thinks. The ground is that anchor. The expected rate is not: it moves when you correct the inventory underneath it. A 2025 study of Alaska added buildings that earlier maps had missed and roughly doubled the loss estimate without changing the hazard model. That is the expected term moving, not the ground moving.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that thaw is happening faster than expected everywhere, that any particular percentage is correct, or that this email reports a measurement. I did not take one. The figures return a shape, not a rate. The world literature is secondary evidence, and I have recorded where each item came from.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before this contact:**
> 1. A ground measurement showing that at every asset slot the observed destabilization rate does not exceed the design-expected rate, taken with an instrument that has previously returned a nonzero signal somewhere.
> 2. Evidence that the expected rate is itself an anchor, that is, that it does not move when the inventory or the design assumption is corrected.
> 3. Evidence that asset classes share one mechanism and one anchor, so that aggregating them loses no signal.
>
> If any one holds, I retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
