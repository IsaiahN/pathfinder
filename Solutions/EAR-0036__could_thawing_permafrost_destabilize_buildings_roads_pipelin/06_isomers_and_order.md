# Structure, order and analogues in the permafrost residual

**Mode: `specified` on the structure claim, `general` on the order claim.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The question bundles a comparison ("faster than expected") with a compound object (buildings, roads, pipelines, "other infrastructure"). Two supporting derivations are owed: (1) what structure the comparison has, and (2) what order the causal and investigative chains have. If either is mis-specified, the main audit in `01` inherits the error.

## DOMAIN CENSUS (supporting)

Atoms held: a rate at a slot, an expected rate at that slot, an instrument returning a signal, an anchor (the ground), and a set of conditions in contact (ground ice, warming, load, maintenance, inventory). Bonds held: comparison ⋛ between two rates, sequence → for ordered causal and investigative steps, and negation ¬ for refusing the expected term. No new primitive is available without import. The six-term substrate of Fig 13 is **not** used here; that mapping was retracted in `01` F9 as a strain.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — What structure is "faster than expected"?

- The Operators table defines ⋛ as "a threshold relation between two quantities" with the test "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."
- "Faster than" is therefore a comparison of two rates, not a conjunction of two states. The operands are quantities (rate observed, rate expected), so the type is admissible. The caveat is that the framework's usual ⋛ examples compare structures, so the operand type at this level is named as a strain in `01` F9 and carried forward.
- Fig 12 :74 gives the ordered-bond test: **"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."** Swapping observed and expected yields "slower than expected", a different claim. The bond is ordered.
- The residual R is the gap between the two operands. Fig 5 :59: **"a term whose residual PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN is the one to point the refusal at"**. The refusal attaches to the expected term when the gap persists.

**Result:** the structure is `observed_rate ⋛ expected_rate`, an ordered comparison whose persistent residual points at the expected term. Triangulated (Operators table plus Fig 12 plus Fig 5).

### Q2 — Is this one question or several?

- Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
- The Library of Babel essay asks: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"
- The asset classes are not independent. They share warming and ice-rich ground, but differ in load geometry, failure mode, adaptation options and cost curve. Melvin et al. report that about 90 percent of building damages in their model came from thaw while roughly 75 percent of road damages came from flooding. A shared forcing with different failure modes is a union, not a partition.
- Fig 9 :57: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"**.

**Result:** union compound. Each (asset, mechanism) pair needs its own loop. Single-source for the figure rule (Fig 9), corroborated by the differentiated world results.

### Q3 — What order does the causal chain have?

- The chain is warming, ground temperature, active layer and excess ice, bearing capacity and settlement, structural damage, service loss. Each step consumes the previous one.
- Fig 3 :20 to :21: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**
- Fig 11 :52 to :53: **"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**
- The third-order effect is the one communities report: a water pipe break leaving a clinic without water (Melvin et al. and the LEO Network record cited by Manos et al. 2025), not the first-order ground-temperature anomaly.

**Result:** ordered, cascading, and at least three steps deep. Triangulated (Fig 3 plus Fig 11).

### Q4 — What order does the investigation have?

- Fig 8 :53: **"Residual first, frame second."** Describe the effect before choosing the discipline.
- Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**
- Formula step 2 sorts the residual before minting; Fig 10 :52 says "Bring a characterised residual, never the source."
- Therefore: enumerate slots, name the anchor, identify the expected term, check whether an instrument is returning a signal, then refuse the wrong assumption. Theory is offered only after a stage-1 verdict is recorded.

**Result:** investigate in this order. Refusing before minting is required; reversing the order is the apophenia failure the exemplar names.

### Q5 — Analogues that carry, and one that does not

- **Contact cascade (carries).** Fig 11's DDT example is a direct analogue: a first-order reading misses a third-order harm. Here the analogue is not a metaphor but the same enumeration rule applied to a different habitat.
- **Metamerism (carries).** *The Hard Problem is About Source*: one reading, several possible sources. The expected-damage number is such a reading; only a different instrument discriminates the source.
- **Isomer (does not carry).** Fig 12 :117 defines an isomer as "same atoms, different bond, different substance." There is no candidate pair of permafrost theories sharing an atom set here, so invoking isomerism would be a decoration, not a derivation. **Declined and recorded.** The relevant Fig 12 item is the ordered-bond test in Q1, not isomerism.

## FALSIFIER (written before the run)

The supporting structure claim fails if any of the following holds:

1. A comparison of two rates is shown not to be an ordered ⋛ relation, so that swapping observed and expected leaves the claim unchanged.
2. The asset classes form a partition with a single mechanism and a single anchor, so that the union-compound reading is wrong.
3. The causal chain is shown to terminate at first contact for every asset, so third-order enumeration adds nothing.
4. A figure line is admitted that directly settles a permafrost rate, making the import unnecessary.

If any fires, `01` F1, F4 and F8 are withdrawn and `07_the_tether_answer.md` is rewritten.
