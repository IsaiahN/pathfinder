## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a rate

The canonical rule is local and explicit. The figures README says the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)", then stops at the limit of what the instruments can distinguish and records the lineage. Fig 9 :32 puts it in the figure text: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**. Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"**.

From *Library of Babel*: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

For EAR-0036 the shape is therefore:

> A checkable answer is a comparison of two rates at a named asset slot and site, with the observed term measured at the ground and the expected term exposed as a movable frame, where the residual persists without trending down and the term to refuse is the expected model.

That is a shape. "Faster than expected across the Arctic" is not. The first constrains what a valid answer must contain; the second asserts the answer before any measurement.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel* on vocabulary: "you will have to use the existing vocabulary you do know that is closest to the words that you wish to describe and grasp the shape of." Applied here: start from "residual", "slot", "anchor" and "instrument", the terms already held, and let the permafrost vocabulary attach to them.
- Fig 6 :30: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30 to :31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause." So the residual is described as "a rate exceeding an expected rate at a foundation", not as a theoretical construct of the framework.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: "the finish line will also be the starting place" and "you have already composed the answer through the process of locating it." For this question the stop is the point at which the per-slot comparison can be measured with a named instrument. Beyond that, further framework work is motion, not progress.
- *Library of Babel* on bundles: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?" This is the figure's union-compound rule (Fig 9 :55) in the essays' vocabulary.

## 3. What licenses a number

Nothing in the framework licenses a permafrost rate. Two things can license a number, and both are outside the shape:

1. **The ground settles it.** THE_FORMULA.md step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." An instrument reading at a site is a ground reading. A model output is not.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50 to :52), with provenance and a shadow test. The Hjort, Melvin and Manos numbers are such imports. They are admitted as world evidence, recorded with author, venue, year and quote in `10_source_ledger.csv`, and never promoted into figure verdicts.

*They're Scanning Books* sharpens the second point: "The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space." A corrected infrastructure inventory is closer to an import of a new dimension than to a new term inside the old model: it changes what can be perceived at all, which is why it moved the damage estimate so sharply.

## 4. Why "expected" is the wrong object to trust

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. An instrument reads a color and "cannot comment on the source". Metamerism: several distinct sources produce one reading. The essay states: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information" and "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it."

The "expected" rate is such a reading. One number can come from a complete inventory plus a conservative hazard model, or from an incomplete inventory plus an aggressive hazard model. The reading alone does not identify the source. It was a different instrument, sub-metre satellite imagery with deep learning detection in Manos et al. 2025, not a further argument, that discriminated one source and moved the estimate. The essay's conclusion applies directly: "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."

## 5. Prestige is not a rate

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it." A well-cited 70 percent statistic can buy a study; it cannot settle whether a foundation in a particular community is moving faster than its design assumed. Fig 2 :40 disqualifies agreement drawn from a shared evidence pool, and several of the permafrost papers share inventories, hazard functions and authors.

## 6. This audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

Same essay: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post. It will often be better than the generic outputs that you will usually get otherwise too", but "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." And: "the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."

So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, `06_isomers_and_order.md` and this file are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are not tests of the permafrost question. They cannot settle anything they report on. The grounding, if it comes, comes from a borehole, an InSAR time series, a settlement survey, or a design standard actually checked against a site.
