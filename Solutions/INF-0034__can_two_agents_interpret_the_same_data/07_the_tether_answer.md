# The Tether's answer: can two agents interpret the same data differently while both remain rational?

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on the conditions, nothing `grounded`.** This is a
shape and a route, not a measurement. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the
figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

One reading D is held fixed. A returns I_A, B returns I_B, with I_A different from I_B, and both are labelled
rational. The question asks whether that is a legal state. It is a **union compound**, not one hard question
(Fig 9 :57: *"A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*): it unbundles into (U1) can one
reading have more than one rational inverse, and (U2) what does "rational" require once a ground is in play.
A lever that clears U1 leaves U2 standing.

## 2. The answer, by domain

1. **Yes, the difference is legal (D1).** Symbols table :25-26: *"Two frames with identical architecture
   diverge because their histories and their w diverge."* Divergence needs no error, no different hardware
   and no different data. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Two agents
   can have different live slots on the same reading. Verdict: `general`, triangulated (Symbols + Fig 1).
2. **The reading does not determine its source (D2).** Fig 13 :128-129: *"Nothing about a composed term tells
   you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available."* A reading is an
   artefact; it carries neither its route nor its source. Fig 8 :51: *"Convergent derivation and adopted import
   look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them."* Verdict:
   `general`, triangulated (Fig 13 + Fig 8 + Fig 12 isomer definition).
3. **"Rational" has two parts, and the second is the ground (D3).** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles
   the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* A frame can be
   loop-correct and still have lost touch: Fig 10 :24 *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*, :28
   *"you have lost touch"*. While no discriminator fires, both interpretations can remain rational in the only
   sense available. Once a source-discriminating instrument is applied and one fails it, keeping it unchanged
   is a closed channel, not a second rational view. Fig 10 :18: *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one."*
   Verdict: `specified`, triangulated (Fig 2 + Fig 10 + Fig 11).
4. **The agents' agreement or disagreement is not evidence (D4).** Fig 2 :31: *"Two people reading the same
   report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."* Sharing the data makes them one
   evidence pool. Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the
   agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Fig 2 :41 preserves the other half: for a shared convention,
   agreement is *"the product, not a corruption of it."* The question is about data, so the disqualifying half
   applies. Verdict: `general`, triangulated (Fig 2 + Fig 8 :48-49).

**Combined answer, with its condition attached:** yes, two agents can interpret the same data differently
while both remain rational, *provided* the reading is a lossy projection that does not determine its source
and the agents' frames differ in history, w, live slot or Γ. The licence lasts until a source-discriminating
ground is applied. At that point the ground, not the number of agreeable agents, decides. The difference is a
fact about the frames; it is never itself a verdict about the data.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "any disagreement is rational." The condition in §2 is the answer, and it is falsifiable.
- Not "grounding is subjective" or "truth is relative to a frame." The anchor does not update (Fig 2 :33).
- Not "two people with one report are two independent witnesses." They are one pool (Fig 2 :31, :40).
- Not a definition of rationality as a new primitive. Frame, residual, anchor and instrument are already in
  Γ; no seventh term is introduced, and no import moves a wall (Fig 6 :26).
- Not a choice between I_A and I_B. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Naming one is the metamerism
  error (`05` §4).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement of "rational" against a discriminator was taken.
- Not a claim that "rational" is "internally consistent." That reading was retracted in `01` §6 after the
  canonical check.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not more argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument
that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; *"It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of
a better instrument."*

- **Discriminator test:** for a named data class, name an instrument that maps the two candidate sources to
  different readings, and run both interpretations against it. If one fails and its holder keeps it, refuter
  1 in `01` fires and "both rational" is withdrawn there.
- **Injectivity test:** if `H(source | reading) = 0` for the class (`06` Q3), the reading determines one
  interpretation and refuter 3 fires.
- **Peer test:** if the agents share prior, information and common knowledge of posteriors, Aumann's theorem
  requires convergence; refuter 2 fires.
- **Definition test:** if "rational" is set to "ground-settled," the answer must be restated (refuter 4).

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The framework returns *"a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* (Fig 9
:32). It can say what any answer to this question must look like and what would close it. It cannot supply the
discriminator from inside the library, because *"instruments extend what can be represented at all"* (Fig 6
:26) and *"What is unavailable is a sensor for something that has never registered anywhere, at any
resolution"* (Fig 6 :34). Fig 13 :134 names the limit: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."*
The record stops where the instrument begins and records the lineage so the stop is checkable.
*"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that anything goes. It says that one reading can have more than one rational
> inverse, because a reading is a projection that does not carry its source and two frames with identical
> architecture diverge through history and weighting. The difference between the two agents lives in their
> frames, not in the data, and their agreement or disagreement is not evidence either way, because two people
> holding one report are one evidence pool. What ends the licence for "both rational" is not a vote or a
> convention but a source-discriminating instrument: when one interpretation fails it and its holder keeps it
> anyway, that is a closed channel, not a second rational view. So the answer is yes, conditionally, and the
> condition is the part worth testing. The framework offers the shape any answer must have, five refuters
> written in advance, and the name of the instrument that would close it. Until that instrument exists, the
> stop is honest and the lineage is on record.
