# Expert verification packet: new technologies, new categories, everyday thought

**What this is:** a claim in a form a cognitive scientist, STS scholar or HCI researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the mechanism, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the specific category, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. No measurement of human categorization was taken.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question bundles four overlapping conditions: an artifact changes what registers at a slot; a term is minted under a product of guards and settles against a ground; an institution stabilises the shared meaning as a convention; and the term crosses to other people as a head start. In addition, "new category" splits into two quantities: a new axis imported from a differing closure (which moves the wall) and a new arrangement over held atoms, an isomer (which does not). Both change per-slot prediction in everyday reasoning. The framework returns this shape and the discriminator that would decide the route, not a forecast of which word will appear. Strong linguistic determinism and the naming-only reading are refused.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — The residual is per slot and the question is a union; checkable by reading Figures 1 and 9

- **Statement:** the effect must be measured per object slot, and the four conditions overlap, so one lever does not clear the compound.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
- **What to check:** does a published result in your field report the effect only as an aggregate ("language influences thought", "technology changes cognition"), or does it identify which slot changed and which did not? If only the former, the live signal has been averaged away.
- **Refuter:** show that a single operator with a single anchor accounts for the instrument, mint, convention and import conditions, or show the four conditions never overlap in real cases.

### C2 — The artifact precedes the category, and the new category is either an added axis or an isomer; checkable by reading Figures 6, 12 and 13 plus your own domain record

- **Statement:** contact with the artifact changes what can register before a term is minted. A genuinely new axis requires contact with a differing closure and moves the wall; a new arrangement over held atoms is an isomer and does not.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :26 *"The world does not gain primitives; a frame gains access. Composition explores what the operators reach; instruments extend what can be represented at all."* Fig 13 :115 *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."* Fig 12 :117 *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- **What to check:** in a case you know, can the new category be written over the old atom set with a different bond (isomer), or does it require an atom or axis that was not present? Does the artifact precede the word in the record, or does the word precede any change in what registers?
- **Refuter:** exhibit a category that changes a per-slot prediction with no change in any instrument or affordance and no rearrangement of held atoms; or show an import that moves the wall without contact with a differing closure; or show that a new arrangement over held atoms requires an added atom.

### C3 — Agreement is a convention, not a verdict; checkable by reading Figures 2, 4 and 10

- **Statement:** an institution maintains a shared meaning, and that agreement is the product; it cannot settle whether the category tracks the world. Descent across people is a head start, not a recording.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :41 *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."* Fig 10 :47 *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* Fig 4 :22 *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Also Fig 2 :37 *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."*
- **What to check:** when a category is adopted by a community, does it make the next discrimination cheaper, or does it just get repeated? Is the check independent of the community's own agreement? For a question about people, is the human judgment used as anchor one that does not update on the frame's own report?
- **Refuter:** show a category whose verdict about the world owes its weight to agreement among frames sharing one evidence pool; or show a verbatim recording producing new discriminations in every receiver without further contact; or show an outcome-fixing convention that still functions as a convention.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that language determines thought. The strong form is refused; only weak influence is claimed, and that corroboration is secondary.
- Not that every technology creates a category, or that a new instrument is always required.
- Not that the framework names which category appears. That is a binding from the world frame, provenanced as such.
- Not that institutions are good or bad category-makers (Fig 11 :51 *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*).
- Not a final taxonomy; Fig 7 :6 *"the limit: no last room."*
- Not `grounded`. No measurement is claimed.

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminator, not more agreement.

1. An instrument that distinguishes an imported axis from a held-set rearrangement at the moment a category appears, instead of inferring the route from the word.
2. Per-slot residuals before and after learning, under an instrument capable of detecting a change. No change at any slot refutes C1's effect claim.
3. A head-start test: does the transmitted category make the next discrimination cheaper, or only produce a replay?
4. The seven failure conditions in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §5, written before this run.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19 and :22, Fig 9 :55 and :57, Fig 13 :115 and Fig 12 :117, six short passages.
2. Take one case from your own field. Identify the slot that changed, and decide whether the new category is an added axis or an isomer over held atoms.
3. Say which of the seven failure conditions you would try first, and what instrument you would use.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
