# Two routes, isomers, and the order across scales

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** Supporting derivation for F2, F3, F4 and F8 of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`. Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks how a technology or institution creates a *new* category. "New" is ambiguous between two different quantities: a new atom or axis available to the frame, and a new arrangement of atoms already held. A derivation that treats them as one will strain either Fig 6 (only import moves the wall) or Fig 12 (same atoms, different bond). The residual is this ambiguity.

## DOMAIN CENSUS FOR THIS DERIVATION

Atoms held: the six of Fig 13 (Space, Time, Energy as states; Spectrum, Gradient, Network as structures) plus, at the human scale, whatever vocabulary a community already has for the slot. Bonds held: the seven, including `→` sequence and `⇒` production, plus the unary negation operator. No new primitive is available without import.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Are the two routes different objects? CORRECTION TO MY FIRST PASS OF `01`

- **Route A (wall-moving).** A new axis or atom becomes available by INWARD instrument extension or OUTWARD import. Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 6 :14: *"the only operator that moves the wall."* Requires provenance and a shadow test; prices actions.
- **Route B (wall-preserving).** A new arrangement over already-held atoms. Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 13 :115: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."* Prices bits; reachable by search inside the closure.
- **Correction kept visible.** I first read Fig 6 :32 (*"An instrument is not built from a description"*) as requiring a new instrument for every new category. That is wrong. Fig 6 :32 governs the construction of instruments; Fig 12 :117 governs arrangements. Different objects, so no contradiction. The world-side version in *They're Scanning Books* separates *"extends what can be said"* from *"extends what can be perceived at all"*. Retracted as applied to all new categories; retained as applied to Route A.

### Q2 — Are two categories built over the same held set isomers?

- Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."*
- Fig 12 :46: *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."*
- Consider two categories that name the same atomic content, for example a community that once grouped "a portable device for taking a picture of oneself" under a general *portrait* category and later groups it under a distinct *selfie* category. If the atoms are the same (a person, a camera, an image, a sharing context) and only the bond between them changes (portrait: `maker ⇒ image`; selfie: `subject ⇒ image → audience`), the two categories are **isomers** under Fig 12 :117. Same atoms, different bond, different substance.
- If instead the new category requires an atom the old one did not have (a front-facing camera as a new perceptual axis, or an audience that did not previously register at the slot), the relation is **composition plus import**, not isomerism. Route A, not Route B.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either category can be written without an atom the other requires, the isomer claim fails and the relation is composition plus import; if both can be written over the full shared set and differ only in bond, the import claim fails for that case and the isomer claim stands.

### Q3 — What is the order of the mechanism?

- The operators table distinguishes `→` sequence (*"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*) from `⇒` production (*"A produces B; B did not exist before"*). Using the wrong one is the same class of error as the glyph error in the exemplar.
- The correct first-order chain is `contact → residual → mint → settle → cross`. `⇒` is appropriate only at `contact ⇒ new slot`, where a distinction becomes available that was not there before. The rest is ordered sequence.
- The order is **not** symmetric. Fig 12 :92: *"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."* A symmetric bond would make `contact` and `category` interchangeable, which is the naming-only reading the audit refuses.
- Formula step 2 adds a term before the sequence: **refuse** the held claim that a category is just a label. Operators table :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."* Negation is unary and is not a bond, so it does not enter the sequence price.
- **Falsifier:** show that the order can be reversed (a category appears, settles and descends before any change in what registers at the slot) and the sequence claim fails.

### Q4 — Two scales, and what crosses the membrane

- Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* At the individual scale a category is minted and settled. At the population scale it becomes part of a shared vocabulary.
- Fig 4 :23: *"upward: a method that can be reapplied. Never the recording of a particular success."* What crosses upward is the *method* for drawing the distinction, not the word. A community that copies only the word gets a recording.
- Fig 4 :24: *"downward: something that makes the next search cheaper. Never a replay of what worked once."* A category taught to a newcomer is a head start: it makes their next discrimination cheaper.
- Fig 4 :34: *"The level a method lands on remakes it."* A category that crosses into a different habitat is not the same category; its valence is relative to the residual there (Fig 11 :49).
- **Falsifier:** show a word crossing a population boundary that makes no subsequent search cheaper and still produces new discriminations. That would break the head-start claim.

### Q5 — Analogues as the question needs them

- **Engineering / animal behaviour.** Japyassú and Laland 2017 (opened, *Animal Cognition* 20(3):375 to 395) use the mutual manipulability criterion: *"if experimental changes in the external entity ... lead to changes in the cognitive system ... and, reciprocally, changes in the cognitive state of the system, entail changes in the external entity, then this entity can be regarded as a part of the cognitive system."* They find reciprocal causation between web tension and spider attention, concluding that *"the web threads and configurations are integral parts of the cognitive systems."* This is an analogue of an artifact becoming part of the perceptual loop at the individual scale, from a genuinely low-overlap field. It corroborates the shape of F2; it does not settle the human case.
- **Mathematics.** The history of mathematical notation (opened) moves from rhetorical to syncopated to symbolic stages. A notation is an inscription technology: a new arrangement over held quantities makes a new relation writable. This is Route B at a population scale, from a different evidence pool.
- **Sociology of technology.** Pinch and Bijker's *closure* (*"the interpretative and design flexibility collapse through closure mechanisms"*) is the population-scale stabilizer of F5. It is an analogue of a convention, not of ground settlement.

## CONCLUSION

The "new" in "new category" is two quantities. One is an added axis or atom, moving the wall and requiring a provenanced import; the other is a new bond over held atoms, an isomer that needs no new primitive. The mechanism has an order: refuse the naming-only reading, register the residual, mint under the three guards, settle, then cross as a head start. Both routes alter per-slot residual in thought; only one moves the closure. Which route an observed category took is decided by a discriminator and a lineage, not by the word alone (Fig 8 :50-52).

This framing keeps what is checkable and drops what failed: no claim that a new instrument is always required (retracted in Q1), no claim that the framework names which category will appear (F7 of `01`), and no conflation of a convention with a verdict (F5 of `01`).
