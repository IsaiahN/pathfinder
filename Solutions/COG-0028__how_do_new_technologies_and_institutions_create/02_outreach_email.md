# Outreach draft: check the output before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F1 to F8 is stated with its refuter intact. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about how new technologies create categories

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question as an effect.** After a technology or institution arrives, people divide the world differently and the same situation produces a different prediction. I want to know the mechanism, not a list of examples.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the mechanism, `general` on the shape, not `grounded`):** the question is a union of at least four overlapping conditions, not one lever. An artifact can extend what registers at a slot (an affordance or instrument); a term can be minted only if it has support in some slot, composes from what is held, is genuinely new, and costs less to state than the confusion it removes; an institution can stabilise a convention, but agreement is the product there and cannot serve as a verdict about the world; a category reaches other people as a head start that makes the next search cheaper, not as a recording of one occasion. There is also a split I nearly missed: a "new category" can be a new axis added by contact with a different frame, or a new arrangement of atoms already held. Only the first moves a wall. Both alter thought.
>
> **What it explicitly does not say:** that the strong Sapir-Whorf claim is true, that language determines thought, that any named technology necessarily produces a named word, or that a final taxonomy of categories is possible. It returns a shape and a discriminator, not a forecast list. Which specific category a given artifact will force is a declared `gap`, not a silence and not an invitation to guess.
>
> **What would refute it, written down before I ran this:**
> 1. a category admitted with no positive residual in any slot, and the mint is not inert;
> 2. a category admitted as novel that was already available, and the ground pays for it anyway;
> 3. adding a settled category changes nothing in what a person predicts or does at the slot;
> 4. an instrument capable of discriminating a change finds identical per-slot residuals before and after learning;
> 5. a verbatim recording of one occasion produces new discriminations in every receiver without further contact;
> 6. the verdict owes its weight to agreement among people reading the same evidence rather than to something that does not update on what they think.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the source links are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figures and formula for direct download (branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Worked six-term derivation and the shape vocabulary: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-intelligence-actually-works
> - Arrival of the Fittest, the architecture: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. In this domain the anchor can legitimately be human judgment, because the question is constitutively about people; that still has to be a judgment that does not update on the frame's own report. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [name]
