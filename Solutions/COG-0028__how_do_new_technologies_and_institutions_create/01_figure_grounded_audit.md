## 0. The question under audit, restated in effect terms

> **How do new technologies and institutions create new categories that alter everyday thought?**

Question source: `Questions/cognitive science.txt` line 35, section 3 (Concepts, categories, and language).

**The effect to be explained.** After a technology or institution arrives, people divide the world differently: new distinctions are drawn, new names appear, and the same physical situation yields a different prediction or action. Examples that recur in the world literature include the "empty" gasoline barrel that was treated as harmless (Whorf's fire-inspection case), the front-facing camera followed by the word *selfie*, and a notation system that makes a previously unwritable relation writable.

**The two options and what each leads to.**

- **Option A, the naming reading.** Categories are labels laid onto a world that was already divided; "creating a category" is naming, and "altering thought" is substituting words. This leads to a claim about vocabulary only. It has no discriminator for why the world divides differently after the technology, and it cannot tell a new category from a new name.
- **Option B, the contact reading.** A technology or institution changes what registers at a slot; a term is minted or imported, settles against a ground, and descends to other people as a head start; per-slot predictions change. This leads to a mechanism with a discriminator, an anchor and refuters.

**Binding the sought-for shape as an ordinary term (Formula, "Direction").** A frame cannot check the direction of its own question because the shape it seeks is a preference, not a term. So I write the shape down before the run and let it accrue a residual like anything else:

> An answer must show (a) a new distinguishable slot becoming registerable, (b) a term minted or imported and paid for by a ground, (c) the term crossing to others as a re-applicable method or head start, not a recording, and (d) a change in the per-slot residual of everyday reasoning. A list of technologies and their words is not this shape.

Fig 5 :62: *"A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term."* Fig 5 :65: *"The remedy is a discipline rather than a branch: make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM."* This paragraph is that discipline.

## 1. The residual R in effect terms

**Arity.** R is indexed per slot, not per population. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A technology does not change "thought" in general; it changes particular slots. The "empty" barrel case changes the slot of a drum of volatile residue while leaving the rest of the fire inspector's categories intact. Aggregating to "language influences thought" destroys the only signal that tells us where to look.

**Symmetry.** "Create" and "alter" are ordered, not symmetric. The operators table distinguishes `→` sequence (*"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*) from `⇒` production (*"A produces B; B did not exist before"*). Contact with an instrument precedes the category; the category then spreads. A correlation between a technology and a word cannot choose this arrow (Fig 2 :39 *"Correlation has valence"*), so the direction is part of what must be derived, not assumed.

**Scale.** Two scales are in play, with a membrane between them. Fig 4 :19-20: *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* At the individual scale the loop is perception, mint, settlement. At the population scale a category becomes a convention and descends as a prior. Fig 4 :21: *"The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction."*

**R, stated:** after contact with a new actor or instrument, at least one slot carries positive mass that the held category system did not predict, and the system has no term that composes a response to it at acceptable cost.

Fig 6 :26: *"The world does not gain primitives; a frame gains access. Composition explores what the operators reach; instruments extend what can be represented at all."* Symbols table :38-40: *"what cannot be perceived yet is residual and simply cannot be read."*

## 4. Findings

### F1 — The residual is a union compound, not one mechanism

Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."* Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*

Technologies and institutions create categories by at least four overlapping routes: an instrument extends what can register (D1); a term is minted from held atoms (D2); a convention stabilises meaning across people (D3); a primitive is imported from a differing closure (D4). Asking "how do technologies create categories" as one question invites one lever, which clears single-condition cases and leaves the intersections standing. Each domain needs its own loop and its own verdict.

**Refuter:** show that a single operator accounts for all four routes with the same anchor, or show that the union is actually a partition by demonstrating that the conditions never overlap (for example that a category cannot be both a term in a library and a convention across people).

**Status: `specified`, triangulated** (Fig 9 :54-57 union rule, low overlap with Fig 4 membrane and Fig 8 surplus).

### F2 — A technology is first an instrument or affordance, not a category

Fig 6 :26: *"instruments extend what can be represented at all."* Fig 3 :7: *"it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked."* Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."*

The front-facing camera made the self-portrait a perceivable and repeatable act before the word *selfie* was in general use. The *Selfie* page records a first known written use in 2002 and its adoption as Oxford's word of the year in 2013; the camera preceded the category. The affordance literature is direct: Gibson (1979) defines an affordance as *"what it offers the animal, what it provides or furnishes, either for good or ill"*, and Williams and Costall (2000) note that by learning an artifact's conventional meaning a child *"enters into the shared practices of society"*. Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* The artifact is read off the world as an actor in the habitat, not invented as a term.

**Strain named, not hidden.** Fig 6 :32 governs the building of new *instruments*. It does not say every new category requires a new instrument. A category can also be a new arrangement over held atoms (F8). The essay *They're Scanning Books* sharpens this: *"Instruments extend the sensory window but not the conceptual one, they reach what we cannot see but can imagine measuring."* So an instrument extends what can be *represented* within held axes; a new *axis* requires import. Re-census: two different quantities, no contradiction.

**Refuter:** find a category that is minted with no change in what any instrument or affordance registers, and no rearrangement of held atoms, that still changes a per-slot prediction. That would break the contact requirement.

**Status: `specified`, triangulated** (Fig 6 plus Fig 3 plus Fig 11, low overlap with the instrument/axis distinction in the world source).

### F3 — A new category is licensed only by three guards and a bargain

Fig 5 :21: *"the gap has real mass somewhere."* Fig 5 :23: *"it composes from what we already have."* Fig 5 :25: *"it is not already in the library."* Fig 5 :26: *"and then: does the new term cost less to state than the confusion it removes?"* Fig 5 :42: *"The acceptance test is a bargain, not a threshold."*

Applied to the Whorf barrel case: SUPPORT is satisfied because the vapor risk registers where "empty" predicted no risk; REACHABILITY holds if "volatile residue" composes from held chemistry terms; NOVELTY fails for "empty" and passes for the new description; the bargain pays because one new term removes a class of lethal mistakes. When NOVELTY is zero, Fig 5 :29-30 applies: *"nothing new here"*, *"the machinery worked; the answer was already known."* That is what happens when an institution merely renames an existing category.

Fig 5 :41: *"Any one of them at zero stops the mint entirely. Which is why a system that is minting nothing may have nothing wrong with its minting."* A community that produces no new categories may simply have no residual, not a defective naming process.

**Refuter:** show a term admitted to the library with zero support in any slot, or a term admitted as novel that was already in atoms(Γ), and a ground that nevertheless pays for it.

**Status: `specified`, triangulated** (Fig 5 guards plus Formula step 3 plus Fig 1 :19 per-slot support). The specific cost in bits and actions is unmeasured here, so the numerical bargain is `single-source` on the figure and not claimed as measured.

### F4 — "Alter everyday thought" is a change in per-slot residual, not a global relabelling

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 2 :36: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* Fig 2 :37: *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."*

This question is constitutively about people, so human judgment is a legitimate anchor here. That is unusual and worth stating: we are not forced to borrow a physical instrument as the anchor for a question about everyday thought. The ground is what people actually predict and do at the slot, checked by a method that does not update on the researcher's frame.

The world literature supports the weak form and refuses the strong form. The *Linguistic relativity* page states that the strong hypothesis *"has fallen out of acceptance by contemporary linguists"* while research supports *"a weaker version of linguistic relativity: that a language's structures influence a speaker's perceptions, without strictly limiting or obstructing them."* Whorf's own formulation is a convention claim: *"we cut nature up, organize it into concepts, and ascribe significances as we do, largely because we are parties to an agreement to organize it in this way."* That is Fig 2 :41 in other words.

**Conflict recorded and returned to census, not used to flip the verdict.** The same page records that the "empty barrels" anecdote was criticised by Lenneberg and Pinker as circular reasoning rather than demonstrated causality. So the anecdote illustrates the mechanism; it does not settle it. Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*

**Refuter:** show that adding a settled category to a person's library leaves every per-slot prediction and action unchanged, or show that a population-level change in thought can be measured only by aggregating away the slot structure.

**Status: `specified`, triangulated** (Fig 1 plus Fig 2 plus the weak-form world result). Strong determinism is refused.

### F5 — Institutions are rooms that stabilise conventions; closure by agreement is not settlement

Fig 2 :41: *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."* Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* Fig 10 :49: *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."*

An institution is a seat that maintains a convention: it fixes which words count and which distinctions are named. Whorf's "agreement that holds throughout our speech community and is codified in the patterns of our language" is a convention in exactly this sense. SCOT supplies a world analogue: Pinch and Bijker's *interpretative flexibility* followed by *closure*, where *"the interpretative and design flexibility collapse through closure mechanisms."* But SCOT closure is agreement, not ground settlement. Fig 5 :70: *"A term is settled when the ground pays for it. A term is usable when something can compose over it."* A convention that merely decides an outcome has crossed Fig 10 :47 and stopped being a convention.

The STS *Principle of Symmetry* (*"the same kind of explanation in the cases of success as in cases of failure"*) is a methodological analogue of the framework's independence rule: do not let the outcome decide the explanation. It is an analogue, not corroboration, because it shares a different evidence pool.

**Refuter:** show that a category can be used to compose new predictions without ever having been paid for by a ground, only by institutional agreement, and that this survives a test where the institution is wrong.

**Status: `specified`, triangulated** (Fig 2 plus Fig 10 plus Fig 5 :69-72).

### F6 — Descent across the population is a head start, not a recording

Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Fig 4 :24: *"downward: something that makes the next search cheaper. Never a replay of what worked once."* Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*

A category taught to a child or picked up from a platform is a prior that descends. Formula step 6: *"priors descend, replays never do."* If it crosses as a recording of one occasion (a slogan, a label with no discriminating contact), it produces repetition and cannot produce the new distinction. The tacit-knowledge essay states the same operational test: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."*

Fig 4 :34: *"The level a method lands on remakes it."* The same category lands differently in different habitats, which is why a borrowed word can alter thought in one community and be inert in another. Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*

**Refuter:** show a category crossing a population boundary as a verbatim recording of one occasion that nevertheless generates new discriminations in every receiver without further contact.

**Status: `specified`, triangulated** (Fig 4 plus Formula step 6 plus the tacit-knowledge essay, with the overlap declared).

### F7 — Which specific category a technology produces is a `gap`

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."* Fig 13 :134: *"It can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands, so something has to be imported before it is asked again."*

No figure line maps a technology to the category it will create. The framework returns the shape of the mechanism and the discriminator that would let an instrument decide the case. It does not return a forecast list. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* So this is reported as `unreachable`, not as silence, and not as an invitation to invent a list.

**On the "shape, not a name" rule, narrowed and kept visible.** That rule governs a *missing* primitive, one not held in Γ. Naming an already-catalogued category as a binding from the world frame is provenance, not the invention of a new primitive. What remains outside reach is the *missing discriminator* that would tell us which category a new instrument will force, in a population where the word has not yet appeared.

**Refuter:** produce a figure line that predicts the specific lexical item or category a given artifact will generate, or an instrument that discriminates the category before it is coined, with provenance and a shadow test.

**Status: `general`, single-source for the figure rule (Fig 9 and Fig 13 agree), `gap` reported plainly.**

### F8 — The "new category" residual splits into two routes with different operators

This is the re-census that the naive reading misses.

- **Route A, wall-moving.** A genuinely new axis or atom becomes available, by INWARD instrument extension or OUTWARD import from a frame whose closure differs. Fig 13 :115: *"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."* Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 6 :14: *"the only operator that moves the wall."* Requires a source gate and a shadow test; prices actions.
- **Route B, wall-preserving.** A new arrangement over atoms already held: an **isomer**. Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 13 :115 again: the arrangements are not closed, so search inside the closure can keep producing new categories without adding an atom. Prices bits.

*They're Scanning Books* gives the world-side version: *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space. Importing a term extends what can be said; importing an axis extends what can be perceived at all."* Route A is the second clause; Route B is the first.

Both routes alter everyday thought. Only Route A moves the wall. Keeping them separate prevents the strain in F2 from being smoothed over.

**Refuter:** show that a new arrangement over held atoms requires an added atom (collapsing Route B into Route A), or show an import that moves the wall without differing closure.

**Status: `specified` on the split, triangulated** (Fig 13 :115, Fig 12 :117, Fig 6 :23-26, lower overlap with the essay's axis/term distinction).

### F9 — Prestige and receptiveness are disqualified, not used

Fig 7 :13: *"agreement compounds, evidence does not."* Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Fig 10 :59: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."*

A list of people likely to agree that technology changes thought is a list selected to agree. It produces a convention, not a check. The *Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."*

**Refuter:** show a verdict about this mechanism that owes its weight to the agreement of frames sharing one evidence pool rather than to a ground that does not update.

**Status: `general` structural, triangulated** (Fig 7 plus Fig 2 plus Fig 10 plus the essay).

### F10 — The valence of a technology is relative to the residual, not carried by it

Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :50: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."* Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*

The front-facing camera is a benefactor to the residual "make a self-portrait cheaply" and a malfactor to the residual "attend to the present conversation". The question asks *how* categories are created, not which technologies are good. A standing list of category-creating technologies would be a standing list of good actors, which Fig 11 refuses.

**Refuter:** exhibit a technology whose valence survives an arbitrary change of residual, which would make valence a property rather than a relation.

**Status: `general` structural, single-source on the figure line (Fig 11), with a low-overlap analogue in the spider-web case (Japyassú and Laland 2017) that is corroborative only.**

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the standing instruction: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following fires.

1. A category is admitted to Γ with SUPPORT = 0 (no slot carries positive residual) and the mint is not inert.
2. A category is admitted as novel that was already in atoms(Γ), and the ground pays for it anyway.
3. Adding a settled category to a person's library changes nothing in their per-slot prediction or action.
4. The same physical situation yields identical per-slot residuals before and after the category is learned, under an instrument capable of discriminating a change.
5. A category crosses a population boundary only as a verbatim recording of one occasion and still generates new discriminations in receivers without further contact.
6. The verdict depends on agreement among frames sharing one evidence pool rather than on a non-updating ground.
7. Fig 6 inward/outward and Fig 12 isomer are shown to govern the same quantity, so Route A and Route B collapse into one.

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the census is rerun. Until one is run, nothing here is labelled `grounded`.

## 8. Strain register

- **S1, live and narrowed:** instruments extend what can be represented within held axes but not what can be conceived without an import. Recorded in F2, resolved at the level of Route A versus Route B but not measured.
- **S2, live and named:** the cost of a category in bits and the cost in actions are two currencies that do not add (Fig 12 :125-127). No measurement here, so the bargain in F3 is structural, not numerical.
- **S3, dissolved by re-census:** Fig 6 instrument rule versus Fig 12 isomer rule. Different quantities; kept visible in the retraction log.
- **S4, dissolved by re-census:** Fig 2 convention rule versus Fig 10 convention-that-decides rule. Both govern conventions, at different stages: maintaining a shared meaning versus fixing an outcome. No contradiction.
- **After re-census: two live strains, both named, none hidden.**
