# Structure, order, isomers, and analogues in the customer decision

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The main audit leaves three structural questions open: (1) does the order of the decision equipment matter, or only its contents; (2) are two decision procedures that use the same cues but different relations the same procedure; (3) do the low-overlap analogues (animal mate choice, credence goods) carry the structure across fields, and where do they fail?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: belief `b`, action `a`, observation `o'`, threshold ⋛, import, and the six substrate terms where a physical analogy is needed. Bonds held: the seven, including ⋛ comparison and → sequence. No new primitive is available without import. Order: perception → vocabulary → objective → planning → learn and carry (Fig 3).

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Is a list of cues a decision procedure?

- Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
- Fig 12 :46: *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."*
- A customer who has price, brand, and reviews has a bag of atoms, not a decision. The bond decides whether price is compared (⋛), whether brand is used as a prior input to `b` (Symbols :24), whether a review is imported as a new atom (Fig 6 :14), and whether the rule is carried after a bad outcome (Fig 3 link 5).
- ⇒ The unit of analysis for this question is a **bonded arrangement**, not a cue list. A "top ten cues" answer commits the flat-formula collapse Fig 12 names.
- **Falsifier:** two decision procedures with identical cue sets and identical bond assignments that make different choices against the same ground reading. If that were shown and reproduced, the bond would not be carrying the information and this derivation fails.

### Q2 - Does the dependency order matter?

- Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."*
- Fig 3 :20-21: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*
- Fig 3 :24-27 says the middle link (terms for what a situation affords, and what counts as progress) is where chains usually break because it has neither an obvious failure nor an obvious test.
- In this question the middle link is "what counts as good enough here." A customer who chooses before binding that objective is running the chain backwards. Choice overload (world source, human cognition) is what the reverse order looks like from inside: many equivalent options, no objective to range them against, and a coping rule adopted under time pressure.
- Formula step 2 orders refusal before mint: a held belief that a cue is diagnostic must be refused before a new cue is sought. A customer who keeps adding review sites while never asking whether the first site was independent is skipping the refusal.
- **Falsifier:** a demonstration that the five links can be reordered without loss of prediction across categories, with the objective bound after planning. That would break the dependency claim.

### Q3 - Are two decision procedures isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- Procedure A: compare reviews by rating, then buy the highest. Procedure B: compare prices, then buy the second cheapest as a quality proxy. Both use an imported signal and a ⋛ comparison over a set of products. They share a **functional group** (threshold selection over an imported signal). Fig 12 :124: *"the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- Stronger isomer reading: if both procedures are written over the full shared atom set `{belief, action, observation, price, brand, review, warranty, threshold}` and differ only in which atom is imported first and which bond carries it, then they are Fig 12 isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different decision.
- The distinction matters because a study that records only which cues were available (the ingredient set) cannot tell the two procedures apart. It will report "same information, different outcomes" and look for a personality variable, when the difference is the bond.
- **Falsifier:** show that for a fixed ground and a fixed atom set, every reassignment of bonds yields the same choice and the same residual. Then the isomer claim is empty.

### Q4 - Do the low-overlap analogues carry?

Two analogues were opened this session, both outside marketing:

- **Animal behaviour, mate choice copying.** *"Mate-choice copying, or non-independent mate choice, occurs when an individual of an animal species copies another individual's mate choice."* It *"reduces the error frequency in mate-choice among inexperienced females"* when private discrimination is costly, and it has a stated cost: *"depending solely on social cues to choose a potential mate is not always advantageous."* This carries the import-and-independence shape across a wide phylogenetic gap. What it does **not** carry: the human-specific content of warranty, brand reputation, or return rights. Analogues transfer syntax, not semantics (Fig 9 :30-31, *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"*).
- **Information economics, credence goods.** *"Credence goods: those that are difficult or impossible to evaluate even after consumption has occurred."* Also *"when price is the only possible indicator of quality"* and *"Branding and detailed product specifications act to transform a product from an experience good into a search good."* This carries the perceiving-link failure (F1) and the wall-moving import (F5) into a different discipline. What it does not carry: the per-slot residual rule, which is framework-internal.
- **Status:** triangulated for the mechanism shape (animal behaviour plus information economics plus the figures, genuinely low overlap); single-source for any specific consumer-level prediction. No analogue is used as a witness for the verdict.

### Q5 - What is the bond that moves the wall, at second order?

- Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."*
- A decision procedure that has paid against experienced quality becomes an atom for the next purchase. The customer's `Γ` grows; the reach may grow with it (Fig 8 of the main audit, F2). This is recursion, not a new cue.
- Strain, named rather than hidden: the Operators table defines ⋛ as *"a threshold relation between two quantities."* At second order the operands are settled procedures now serving as atoms. Whether an atom counts as a quantity at that level needs an instrument, not an argument. This is the same open type question the exemplar recorded for its ⋛ at second order; it is recorded here as a strain, not a correction.

## CONCLUSION

The decision is a bonded arrangement, not a cue list. Its dependency order runs from perceiving to objective to planning, and the middle link is where the chain breaks when quality is hard to judge. Two procedures with the same cues but different bonds are functional-group members or isomers, not variants of one thing. The low-overlap analogues carry the import-and-independence shape across fields, but not the framework's per-slot rule. The whole derivation survives its stated falsifiers only if a bond, not an ingredient, is shown to carry the difference.
