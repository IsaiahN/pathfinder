# WATSON answer, BUS-0061

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** Figures are primary and decide. World sources are secondary, corroborate but never overrule, and a conflict returns the derivation to the domain census. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. No consumer measurement was taken.

## QUESTION

How do customers make decisions when products are complex, quality is hard to judge, or information is incomplete? Restated in effect: what does a single customer do when `R = |Γ(b,a) - o'|` will not come down because the local library cannot predict experienced quality? Option A: answer with a universal list of cues and heuristics. Option B: answer with the shape any such decision must have and leave the category-specific cue to the ground. A sends a named answer the framework says it cannot give and treats a shared evidence pool as evidence. B sends a checkable shape, written refuters, and an open slot until a ground settles it. The question is constitutively about a person, so the person's experienced quality, not an external metric alone, is the anchor (Fig 2 :36).

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry, scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Ternary, customer x product x occasion, indexed per slot (Fig 1 :19). Symmetry: "good enough" is a threshold comparison, not a symmetric join (Operators table :51). Scale: one customer loop, a market habitat, a social frame, with lossy transforms between them (Fig 4).
- **D1 Customer loop.** Actors: one chooser, her `Γ`, belief `b` with weight `w`. Currency: reduction of `R` against experienced quality; description cost of a rule. Anchor: experienced quality on use. Admitted.
- **D2 Market habitat.** Actors: products, prices, brands, sellers, warranties, returns. Currency: purchase, willingness to pay, survival. Anchor: the market (Fig 2 :29). Admitted.
- **D3 Signal frame.** Actors: price, brand, certification, expert test, specification. Currency: imported atoms that predict quality. Anchor: source gate and shadow test (Fig 8 :10-13). Admitted.
- **D4 Social frame.** Actors: other customers, reviewers, ratings. Currency: agreement. Anchor: independent instruments only, otherwise a mirror chain (Fig 7 :12-13). Admitted.
- **X1 True quality independent of any instrument.** No nameable anchor, fails the Fig 2 :29 anchor test. Not a domain yet.
- **X2 One best product for everyone.** Absorbed into D1 and D4; valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49).

Union, not partition: D3 and D4 overlap whenever brand and reviews draw on the same pool, a compound with its own loop (Fig 9 :54-57). Dependency order: perception -> vocabulary -> objective -> planning -> learn and carry (Fig 3). The break when quality is hard to judge is at perception and vocabulary (Fig 3 :27).

Strain test: Fig 2 :36 makes human judgment an anchor for a question about people; Fig 2 :29 names a market as an anchor. Different questions, different domains, no strain after re-census. The claim that price is a signal versus Fig 2 :40 on shared-pool agreement was also returned to census and dissolved: a seller-set price is inside the seller's closure, a price formed by independent buyers is a different closure.

Quote: "the quality of goods traded in a market can degrade in the presence of information asymmetry between buyers and sellers." Also "Sellers have no credible disclosure technology." Prediction: without disclosure, buyers fall back on averages and good goods exit. **Corroborates** D1 and D3 perceiving-link failure. - **Marketing, goods classification.** Philip Nelson, "Information and Consumer Behavior," Journal of Political Economy 78(2), 1970, read via the SEC classification record, Wikipedia, 2026-11-22. Quote: "Credence goods: those that are difficult or impossible to evaluate even after consumption has occurred." Also "when price is the only possible indicator of quality." ## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; a universal cue list does not. The decision is a per-slot threshold with a learnable belief weight; when quality cannot be read, more same-pool information adds no atom and a differing closure with provenance is required; agreement counts only under independence; decision quality belongs to customer and habitat together; an averaged rating is a lost channel. The specific diagnostic cue for any product category is a declared `gap`, not a hidden answer. This is `specified`, ungrounded, candidate. The world sources corroborate the mechanism shape after re-census, but they share a broad pool and carry no verdict weight over the figures.

- **Cost if wrong:** misdirected remedy. A failing perceiving link would be sold more consumer education, or a shared-pool echo would be treated as independent evidence, and a single lever would be applied to a union that needs several. Bounded and largely locatable, because the packet records the residual, the census, and the falsifiers before the conclusion.
- **Undoable:** yes. No term is deleted; a refutation is added and the reach falls because something was added rather than anything struck out (Fig 6 :42-43). The shape and its five falsifiers are recorded in a provenance trail so a later reader can locate the error.
- **Reversed by:** any of the five pre-registered falsifiers, in particular an aggregate residual with no loss of prediction, a new quality atom from more same-pool search, an unprovenanced cue as good as an independent test, or accuracy improving under customer-only training with an unchanged habitat. A source with a genuinely different evidence pool showing that per-cue predictions are fully accounted for by a single shared anchor would also reverse the independence claim. A source-discriminating instrument in a named category would close the F10 gap rather than reverse the shape.
