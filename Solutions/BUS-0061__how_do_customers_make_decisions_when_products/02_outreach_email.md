**Subject:** One checkable claim about decisions under opaque quality

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** A customer buys from a belief formed before purchase and observes the quality only after use. Call the gap between predicted and experienced quality the residual. It belongs to one customer-product-occasion, not to a market average. The question is what the customer does when that gap will not come down: complex product, quality hard to judge, incomplete information. An answer has to say what moves the gap, when other people's agreement counts as evidence, and what instrument would settle it.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):**
> 1. The decision is a threshold comparison against "good enough," not a full optimization over every option.
> 2. When the customer's own information cannot predict quality, more of the same information adds nothing. What is required is contact with a source whose evidence is actually different.
> 3. Price, brand, certification, reviews, and warranties are imports. They count only if their provenance is shown and they explain a gap the customer could not close. A cue that merely repeats the seller's own claim is inside the same frame and moves nothing.
> 4. Agreement among customers is evidence only when the customers are independent instruments. A thousand reviews that trace to one narrative are one witness with many names.
> 5. Decision quality belongs to the customer and the information habitat together. Changing the customer without changing the habitat changes nothing.
> 6. The framework does not name which cue is diagnostic for which product category. It gives the shape a diagnostic cue must have; the ground selects the cue.
>
> This is a shape and a route, not a result. I am explicitly not claiming it is `grounded`.
>
> **What would refute it (written before this message was sent):**
> 1. a decision made on an aggregate residual with no loss of prediction;
> 2. more search inside one pool demonstrably adding a new quality atom;
> 3. an unprovenanced cue tracing to the seller predicting experienced quality as well as an independent test;
> 4. better decisions from customer-only training while the habitat supplies no new quality feedback.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula for direct download: `FRAMEWORK/figures/` in the local framework, with the canonical usage rule at `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`.
> - Figures verbatim with line numbers: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`.
> - Wider field literature I actually read: the ledger in `10_source_ledger.csv`, including Simon on satisficing, Akerlof on quality uncertainty, Nelson's search/experience/credence distinction, and the choice-overload and anchoring reviews.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
