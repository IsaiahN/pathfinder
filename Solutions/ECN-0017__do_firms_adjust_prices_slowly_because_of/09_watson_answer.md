## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, whether firms adjust prices slowly because of costs and contracts or because they change them strategically. Option A: a single cause, an obstacle in the adjustment channel. Option B: a single cause, a strategic choice. Option C: the either/or is a union of overlapping conditions, and the answer is a per-slot shape plus a discriminator. What each leads to: A and B each name a source from the same reading and cannot say which slot is failing; C keeps the conditions separate, states refuters in advance, and records the instrument that would settle it. The figures decide C.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** A price spell: the price at a firm-slot stays fixed while cost, demand, information or rivals' prices move. Arity: at least belief `b`, action `a`, observation `o′`, slot `s`, ordered by time `t`. Symmetry: ordered, not interchangeable; a decision to reprice is a comparison of gain against cost, not a symmetric conjunction. Scale: first order inside the firm loop, second order at the market and index. Fig 9 names the question a union compound, not one hard question.
- **D1, the firm pricing loop.** Actors: managers, pricing routines, the firm's Γ, buyers. Currency: price quotes, markups, margin protection. Anchor: the realized transaction price and cost at the slot.
- **D2, the market habitat and the index.** Actors: firms, rivals, consumers, input suppliers, monetary authority. Currency: survival, share, price level. Anchor: realized market outcome at the slot. The index frequency is a pooled reading of it, not the anchor.
- **D3, the cost and contract structure.** Actors: marginal cost, overhead, fixed cost, staggered contracts, expected future cost. Currency: cost changes, repricing cost, pass-through. Anchor: realized cost and contract terms. Withheld candidate names: menu cost, Taylor or Calvo schedule.
- **D4, the strategic interaction structure.** Actors: rival firms, oligopoly, price leadership. Currency: rivals' price responses, strategic complementarity. Anchor: realized rival price moves, a reading distinct from the focal firm's own cost.
- **D5, the information and attention channel.** Actors: price-setters, information-acquisition cost. Currency: information and updates. Anchor: realized state versus acted-on state.
- **D6, the measurement channel.** Actors: price statisticians, interviewers, contract taxonomists. Currency: contact with the ground. Anchor: the ground persists while the channel decays.
- **X1, the firm's reason as a directly observed cause.** Rejected before any figure line: no anchor independent of the price reading and the manager's report.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Information arrives, cost and contract state are evaluated, rival response is anticipated, the comparison is made, the price is held or reset, the index pools the result. The middle link, a term for the source of a spell, has no instrument in the ordinary frequency statistic.
- **Partition or union.** Union. D1 overlaps D3, D4 and D5; D2 overlaps D6. Overlaps are compounds with their own loops and anchors.

Strain test applied: Fig 13's six physical substrate terms only fit "costs and contracts" by renaming a repricing cost a kind of energy, so Fig 13 governs the honest stop only. Treating the published frequency as the ground was retracted; the slot-level market is the ground. Treating time-dependent and state-dependent pricing as a partition was retracted; sticky-information models combine them ("dual stickiness"), so they are union conditions. See `01` section 7.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

Slow price adjustment is not a choice between an obstacle and a strategy. It is a union of per-slot conditions: a cost and contract state reaching the rule, an adjustment-cost threshold that makes small shocks inert, a strategic feedback whose return path is the rival's price, an information channel that can be stale without the price being sticky, and a measurement channel that pools the spells into a frequency. The two named causes carry different bonds and different anchors, so they are not opposite ends of one dial. The load-bearing condition changes sign with the shock regime, the cost side is a threshold, the strategic side is a cycle, and the firm-to-index transform is lossy. The universal choice of one side is a `gap` of the unreached kind, not underivable in principle. Mode `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`; candidate until a ground reading pays.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet is mode-labeled, states refuters before the conclusion and records the lineage, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a theory before a residual, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter. A second cost is a wrong policy inference: treating a two-share split as a verdict would direct attention at one condition while the intersections still bind.
- **undoable:** yes. No primitive is withdrawn. The shape, the five domain verdicts and the falsifiers stand; the omitted conditions are added by provenance, not by discarding what was derived. A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A firm-level design recording repricing cost, contract term, rival price and information set at each decision moment, drawn from a frame that does not reuse the CPI microdata estimator, would name the load-bearing condition and close the gap; a source that discriminates all four candidates would break F2; an invertible firm-to-index aggregation would break F8; an invariant effect across shock regimes would break F6; a verbatim figure line settling the source would break F10. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
