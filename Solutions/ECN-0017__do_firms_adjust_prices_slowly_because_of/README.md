## What is being decided, in effect terms

An answer could be a named cause: "firms are slow because repricing costs and contracts bind" or "firms are slow because they wait on rivals." An answer could instead be a shape: the set of conditions that govern the per-slot price-adjustment residual, the bonds that connect them, and the instrument that would tell them apart. The residual is stated in effect terms in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 0. It is the gap between what a firm-slot's pricing routine predicts will happen to its margin and sales after a shock, and what the market actually returns as a realized transaction price. "Slowly" is the observation that this price stays fixed for a spell while cost, demand, information or rivals' prices move. The question asks for the SOURCE of that lag. The figures say a reading does not determine its source, and that the either/or is a union of overlapping conditions rather than a partition into two shares. The verdict is therefore a refusal of the requested either/or plus a checkable separator. The universal choice of one side is a `gap` verdict, not a silence.

## What an answer would look like

Binding the sought-for shape as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"): an answer would be (i) the conditions whose conjunction governs the per-slot price spell, indexed by firm and time; (ii) the bond type of each condition, so that an obstacle in the adjustment channel is not confused with a strategic feedback loop; (iii) an instrument that discriminates a menu-cost or contract source from a rival-response source at the moment of the price decision; and (iv) an account of what is lost when those spells are pooled into an economy-wide frequency of price change. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no loop check can see it. It is now bound, so the pre-registered refuter can fire on it (Fig 9 :27 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit").
