# The Tether's answer on ECN-0017

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a separator, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A price stays fixed at a firm-slot while cost, demand, information or rivals' prices move. The question asks whether the stillness comes from an obstacle (repricing cost, contract) or from a choice (strategic waiting). The residual is the per-slot gap between what the firm's pricing routine would set and what the market returns, indexed by firm and time. The stillness is a reading with several possible sources. The user's either/or treats two of those sources as exclusive answers.

## 2. The shape

1. **A price spell is indexed per firm-slot, and "slowly" is a duration distribution, not one speed.** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." A single economy-wide frequency throws away the per-slot signal. The observed frequencies range from 9 percent per month in Italy to 27 percent in the US in the CPI microdata summarized by the *Nominal rigidity* article, and the US mean spell more than doubles when temporary sales are removed.

2. **The fixed price does not determine its source.** Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." From *The Hard Problem is About Source*, opened 2026-11-23: "the instrument is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source." Menu cost, contract, rival response and stale information can each produce the same stillness. The reading selects none of them.

3. **The either/or is a union of five conditions, not a partition into two shares.** Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." The conditions are: cost and contract state reaching the rule; an adjustment-cost threshold; a strategic response band; an information lag; and a measurement channel that pools the spells. One lever clears the single-condition regions and leaves the intersections (Fig 9 :56).

4. **The two named causes are different bonds, not opposite ends of one dial.** The obstacle case is a threshold comparison: Fig 12 :87 "a threshold relation between two quantities." The strategic case is a feedback cycle: Fig 12 :121 "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." They share at most a functional group, not an identical atom set (`06_isomers_and_order.md`, Q1).

5. **The load-bearing condition changes sign with the shock regime.** Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." The same repricing-cost structure that holds a price still through a small shock produces faster repricing when the shock is large; the *Nominal rigidity* article records that "Prices change faster and monetary shocks are over faster under state dependent than time." There is no standing coefficient for "the effect of adjustment costs on speed."

6. **Sticky information is a separate channel condition.** Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does." Sticky-information models have flexible prices and sticky information; the *Nominal rigidity* article records that this is "at odds with the empirical evidence on prices" and motivated "dual stickiness" models. Collapsing the information lag into "costs and contracts" loses a distinct condition.

7. **The firm-to-index crossing is lossy.** Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." A per-slot source classification does not survive unchanged into a frequency; the round-trip gap `R_T` is nonzero and is measured qualitatively in `11_audit.md`.

8. **No figure line picks a universal side.** Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,". The universal answer is a `gap` of the unreached kind, not underivable in principle.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "firms are slow because of menu costs." That is a binding for a named slot, not a derivation from the framework.
- Not "firms are strategic, so contracts are irrelevant." The conditions compound; Dixon and Hansen (as reported in the *Menu cost* article) show a small menu-cost sector making other sectors less responsive to demand.
- Not a two-share decomposition of stickiness. A share is a filter for the research budget, never a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict.").
- Not grounded. No firm-level source-discriminating measurement was run here.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

A source-discriminating instrument at the firm-slot, not a better argument. The corresponding questions are:

- At the decision moment, was the repricing cost below the gain from changing the price? (threshold test)
- Was a contract term binding on that date? (contract condition)
- Did a rival's recent or anticipated price change alter the gain? (cycle test)
- Had the relevant information arrived and been attended to? (channel test)
- Does the per-slot classification survive aggregation to the index, or is the resulting frequency a pooled reading with a nonzero `R_T`?
- **Threshold Test:** does a ⋛ comparison hold between a gain molecule and an adjustment-cost molecule at the slot, or demonstrably not? The Operators table types ⋛ as "a threshold relation between two quantities"; whether a firm's position counts as a quantity at that level is a type question, named not hidden.

Standing falsifiers from `01` section 3: a single predictive speed across index composition changes; an instrument that discriminates all four sources; a single intervention restoring all five conditions; a zero-width threshold band; rival response functions without a discontinuity; invariant sign across shock regimes; information updates that remove every spell; an invertible firm-to-index aggregation; two disjoint evidence pools agreeing; a verbatim figure line settling the source.

## 5. The honest stop

The answer stops where the instruments can no longer distinguish sources. The slot-level market is the anchor; agreement among CPI-based studies is one evidence pool, not several witnesses (Fig 2 :31 "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."). The framework returns a shape, five separable verdicts, and a named instrument. It does not return a side. All claims remain `candidate` until a ground reading pays for them.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not choose between costs and strategy as the cause of slow price adjustment. It says that a price spell is a stillness at one firm-slot while the world moves, and that stillness is a reading with several sources, so the reading cannot name its own cause. The candidate causes form a union of conditions with different bonds: an adjustment-cost threshold, a strategic feedback whose return path is the rival's price, an information channel, and a measurement channel that pools the spells into a frequency. Their load-bearing weight changes with the size and sign of the shock, so no standing coefficient or two-share split is licensed. What is offered is the shape any settleable answer must have, ten falsifiers written in advance, and the design that would close the question: record cost, contract, rival price and information at each price decision and let the slot-level market settle which condition bound. Until that design exists, the answer is a `gap`, not a silence.
