## Wider literature that bears on the question

These were encountered inside the fetched pages above and are recorded as cited there, not as separately opened full texts. They are used as leads for a checker, not as independent witnesses here.

- Sheshinski and Weiss 1977, *Inflation and Costs of Price Adjustment*, Review of Economic Studies. The origin of the fixed cost of repricing; reported in the *Menu cost* page.
- Mankiw 1985, *Small Menu Costs and Large Business Cycles*, Quarterly Journal of Economics. Small costs, large welfare effects; reported in the *Menu cost* page.
- Akerlof and Yellen 1985, *A Near-rational Model of the Business Cycle, with Wage and Price Inertia*, QJE. Bounded rationality as an inertia source; reported in the *Menu cost* page.
- Taylor 1980 and Calvo 1983 staggered contracts. Exogenous timing rules; reported in the *Nominal rigidity* page.
- Klenow and Kryvtsov 2008 and Nakamura and Steinsson 2008, CPI microdata studies. Share the CPI evidence pool with the other frequency studies.
- Golosov and Lucas 2007, *Menu Costs and Phillips Curves*, JPE. The implausibly large cost result and the need for real rigidity; reported in the *Menu cost* page.
- Sims 2003, *Implications of rational inattention*, Journal of Monetary Economics. Reported in the *Rational inattention* page.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22** "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape and its refuter; offer the links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who has absorbed the entire framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27** "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the article.
- **Fig 7 :13** "agreement compounds, evidence does not." A list selected for people who already agree produces a convention, not corroboration.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its refuter; (2) ask what they would count as a failure; (3) only then the verbatim figure lines and the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.
