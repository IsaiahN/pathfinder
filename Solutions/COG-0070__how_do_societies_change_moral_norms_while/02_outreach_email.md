# Outreach draft: check the shape claim first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the four-condition split in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §1 is stated plainly and the seven refuters in §3 are written down. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Would this shape claim fail in your field? A one-line refutation is enough

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know about moral change. I am not asking you to read any theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a society changing a moral norm while keeping a sense of continuity is not one mechanism with one cause. It is a union of four conditions with different anchors, and they cannot be averaged:
>
> 1. Whether a change is warranted is a verdict. Because the question is constitutively about people, the anchor is structured human judgment of conduct case by case, not the society's majority report and not an external instrument. Fig 2 :37 "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people." Agreement among people who share one evidence pool is disqualifying for this verdict (Fig 2 :40).
> 2. "We are still us" is a convention about shared meaning, where agreement is the product (Fig 2 :41). It is legitimate until it is used to decide an outcome, at which point it has stopped being a convention (Fig 10 :47).
> 3. Continuity is not identity of the norm list. It is the recoverable lineage: Fig 13 :128 "Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available." The record holds the old norm and the refusal of it at once.
> 4. Change is a refusal that adds without deleting: Fig 6 :43 "A refutation adds a term that refuses; it removes nothing, and the reach falls because something was added rather than because anything was deleted." What crosses generations is a method or a seed, not a frozen list of conclusions (Fig 4 :22).
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I can name which norm should change in your society, that any particular tradition is legitimate, that majority consensus settles a moral question, or that this is a finished theory. The framework returns a shape, not a name. The specific-norm slot stays open as a declared gap until something discriminating measures it.
>
> **Seven refuters I wrote down before running this:**
> 1. Pure erasure of an old norm with no refusing term added, and the same lineage evidence survives.
> 2. Continuity reports unchanged when the shared backdrop is replaced and the old one is falsified.
> 3. A tradition that decides an outcome and yet remains a checkable coordination convention.
> 4. A change settled by headcount or prestige alone that survives independent contact.
> 5. Agreement within one evidence pool shown to carry information beyond that pool.
> 6. A source-discriminating measurement that settles a disputed change per person and across independent instruments. This would move the gap toward a result, not break the shape.
> 7. One mechanism with one anchor found to cover both the verdict and the convention, so the split is unnecessary.
>
> If any of 1 to 5 or 7 holds, I withdraw the shape. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed. I am recording your verdict on this claim before showing you any source, so we can tell a check from an endorsement.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local path in the packet): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop and the mode rule: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the formula is in the figures. Run it on a problem you know and can check against a ground, write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state whether what you get is `general`, `specified` or `grounded`. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Claim first, theory second | Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." The recipient checks the claim without holding the framework's interior. |
| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." |
| Seven refuters before the conclusion | The framework's own rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, and Formula step 2: refuse before mint. |
| Verdict recorded before theory is shown | Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Once the recipient has the full architecture, their agreement stops being evidence. |
| Mode stated on every claim | `THE_FORMULA.md`: "Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which." |
| Majority report refused as an anchor | Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." |
| Convention and verdict kept apart | Fig 2 :39 "Correlation has valence." Fig 2 :41 makes agreement the product for a convention and Fig 2 :40 disqualifies it for a verdict. |
| No claim about which norm, no name for the slot | Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"; the figures README: *"a shape, not a name"*. |
| Precedent is an import, not a veto | Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"; Fig 8 :51 requires the record of where each came from. |
| No prestige ask and no bulk list | *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* |
| Offer the formula as a method to reuse | Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." |
| Later agreement never pooled as corroboration | Fig 2 :40; a Track B reply is a conversation, never a witness. |
