## Question restated as an effect

A society holds norm set N at time t and a different set at t+1 (change), while its members report that the community is still the same moral community and the past still binds them (continuity). The residual is the gap between a frame that predicts "if norms change, identity breaks, so one of the two reports is false" and the observation that both reports arrive together and often without anyone noticing a break. What is being decided is the shape of the arrangement that lets both be true at once. It is not being decided which norm is right. An answer would look like: a partition of the question into differently anchored loops, a rule for how an old norm can be given up without erasing it, and a rule for what crosses between generations. That sought-for shape is bound as an ordinary term at the start of `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` so it can accrue a residual like anything else (THE_FORMULA.md, "Direction").

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to believe the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three figure-grounded reasons: Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."; Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"; Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that would make their agreement worthless, and sending the full framework before asking for a verdict spends the independence of the person asked.
