## RESIDUAL

The primary audit leaves three structural questions open once the four-condition shape is bound: (1) if two societies hold the same moral foundations, are their codes **isomers** under Fig 12, or merely lists with different items; (2) in what **order** must the four conditions be serviced; (3) what exactly crosses the intergenerational membrane, a norm list or a generator, and how is the loss measured. This file answers those three and states a falsifier for each.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are two moral codes with the same foundations isomers?

- Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :123 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Fig 12 :92 "Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."
- Fig 13 :115 "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."

If two societies draw on the same six foundations and differ only in which foundation mediates which kind of judgment and in how the binding operators are arranged, then under Fig 12 they are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance. A list of foundations cannot tell them apart, which is why arguments that compare only "values held" miss the difference. If one society requires an atom the other does not hold at all, the isomer claim fails and they are different substance sets.

- **Falsifier Q1:** find two societies judged to have different moral codes where one uses a foundation wholly absent from the other under the same coding. Then the difference is in the ingredient list, and the isomer reading is wrong. The weaker functional-group reading (both codes are `state → structure → judgment` shaped) would still stand.

### Q2 - In what order must the four conditions be serviced?

- Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."
- Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
- Fig 3 :24 "Why the middle link is where chains usually break."
- Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

The dependency chain is:

1. **Split verdict from convention.** This is the middle link. Both sides say "moral" and "traditional", so there is no instrument that distinguishes the two claims, which is exactly why Fig 3 :24 applies. Work above this link (better moral reasoning, more data, more speakers) arrives at a step that cannot use it.
2. **Aim the refusal.** Formula step 2 sorts the failure before minting: a rebinding is not a new term, a broken mechanism is, and a false held term needs a refusal rather than an explanation. The candidate to refuse is the one whose residual persists and does not trend down.
3. **Carry the method and measure the round trip.** A method abstracted from one case and applied to another loses information. The loss is the measurement, not noise.
4. **Re-range the new term against the ground.** Fig 2 :45 "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."

- **Falsifier Q2:** show a case where a refusal succeeds, a new norm is settled and transmitted, and the verdict/convention split was never made, with no downstream failure. Then the split is a preference of the framework, not a dependency.

### Q3 - What crosses the membrane?

- Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
- Fig 4 :25 "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."
- Fig 4 :34 "A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."
- Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."
- Fig 4 :31 "The gap is not an error to eliminate."

What crosses is the **generator**: how to recognise harm, how to refuse a held norm, how to re-range against a ground that does not move on opinion. A frozen norm list is a recording. It produces a society that can repeat the past success and cannot produce a new one (Fig 4 :26 "Replayed downward, it produces a system that repeats a past success and cannot produce a new one."). The framework essay *How a Society of Agents Would Work* makes the same distinction for a group: *"A method is a reusable pattern... settled by the ground before it is shared"*, while a recording is *"press this button at this spot."*

The round trip matters because the same method lands differently in different habitats: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves"* (Fig 11 :40). A society that imports a method without checking what it returns in the new habitat is navigating by dead reckoning.

- **Falsifier Q3:** a society whose only transmitted item is a fixed norm list, with no method for revising it, which still produces a novel correct norm under conditions outside the list. That breaks the seed claim and makes recording-only transmission sufficient.

### Q4 - The stable kernel and the growing operating system

The 2026-11-22 version of *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* gives the closest plain-language analogue to the continuity question: *"A virus rewrites its own code... A human keeps a stable kernel (DNA) but changes as a person over time. The Tether is like a human. The kernel is fixed. The operating system grows."* It also states the temporal version of the problem directly: *"Humans in the 1800s had different values, customs, and cultures than humans of today. What does it mean to align an AI to human values when human values are a moving target? You cannot align to something that changes."*

Under the figures this maps to Fig 4 :19-20 *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change"* and Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world". What is stable is the loop and the ground-anchoring discipline; what grows is the library of settled norms. The analogy is useful but not load bearing: if the kernel metaphor fails, F3 and F4 still stand on their figure lines.

- **Falsifier Q4:** show a society that changes its kernel, meaning its ground-anchoring discipline and its refusal operator, while retaining a stronger continuity report than one that keeps them. That would reverse the stable/growing assignment.

## CONCLUSION

The same moral atoms differently bonded yield different codes, so comparing foundation lists misses the difference (Q1). The four conditions are ordered by dependency, with the verdict/convention split as the uninstrumented middle link (Q2). What crosses generations is a generator with a measured round-trip gap, not a norm list (Q3). The stable part is the loop and the anchor; the growing part is the settled library (Q4). Every one of these has a falsifier written before the conclusion. The isomer claim is `specified` and analogical; it does not carry the verdict in `01`.
