# Expert verification packet: the Tether on moral change and continuity

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in moral change or cultural evolution can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain sub-claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No measurement of a society is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result, and it is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A society changing a moral norm while keeping a sense of continuity is not one mechanism. It is a union of four conditions with different anchors. Whether a change is warranted is a verdict anchored in per-person judgment of conduct; "we are still us" is a convention about shared meaning that is legitimate until it decides an outcome. Continuity is not identity of the norm list but recoverability of the lineage. Change is a refusal that adds a term without deleting the old one, so the record grows while the reach changes. What crosses generations is a method and a seed, not a frozen norm list. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged. The specific norm and the specific legitimate narrative are a declared gap.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 2, 9 and 10

- **Statement:** the question must be split into a verdict loop and a convention loop, and agreement has opposite valence in the two.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"; Fig 2 :41 "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"; Fig 2 :39 "Correlation has valence."; Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"; Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- **What to check:** is the distinction drawn between a claim about the world (is this change warranted) and a claim about shared meaning (are we still the same community)? If the two are combined into one "moral consensus" measure, the claim says that measure has thrown away the signal.
- **Refuter R5/R7:** show that frames sharing one evidence pool carry information beyond that pool for a warrant verdict, or find one mechanism with one anchor that covers both loops so the split is unnecessary.

### C2 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 5, 6 and 12

- **Statement:** a moral change is a refusal that adds without deleting. Continuity is the record half, change is the reach half, and they are different quantities.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :53 "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"; Fig 6 :42 "What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall."; Fig 6 :43 "A refutation adds a term that refuses; it removes nothing, and the reach falls because something was added rather than because anything was deleted."; Fig 12 :140 "A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate."; Operators table :58 "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate".
- **What to check:** is the change modelled as a new entry that refuses the standing of the old norm while retaining it on record, rather than as an overwrite or erasure? If a vocabulary has subtraction but no negation, it can say what is missing and cannot say an arrangement is wrong (Fig 12 :112).
- **Refuter R1:** a norm change implemented by pure erasure with no refusing term added, which nevertheless preserves the same lineage evidence. If that is possible, this sub-claim fails.

### C3 - world-facing; checkable against research on transmission and identity

- **Statement:** continuity across generations is a recoverable lineage carried by methods and seeds, not by an unchanged norm list.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."; Fig 4 :25 "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."; Fig 13 :128 "Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available."; Fig 11 :40 "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
- **What to check against the literature:** Wikipedia *Invented tradition* reports that traditions that *"appear or claim to be old are often quite recent in origin and sometimes invented"* and Beiner's *"reinvention of tradition... a creative process involving renewal, reinterpretation and revision"*. Rendell et al. 2010 (Science, full text opened) found that the winning social-learning strategy *"weighted information according to the time since acquisition"* and that copying errors let *"new behavior... enter the population without explicit innovation"*. Do these support lineage-plus-method rather than content-preservation? The framework essay *How a Society of Agents Would Work* states the analogue: *"The Tether places identity in the lineage, not on a single timeline."*
- **Refuter R2/R3:** continuity reports unchanged when the shared backdrop is replaced and the old one is falsified, or a tradition that decides an outcome while remaining a checkable coordination convention.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a theory of everything; no final room, no terminal norm set (Fig 7 :6, Fig 13 :118).
- Not that majority consensus, prestige, or a felt sense of continuity settles what is right. Prestige *"can fund an action... but it cannot settle an action"* (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*).
- Not that tradition may veto a change. A convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one (Fig 10 :47).
- Not that continuity is identity of the norm list. It is lineage and arrangement (Fig 13 :128, Fig 12 :94).
- Not a name for which norm should change in any society. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by a measurement of a society.
- Not an independent corroboration from the essays: they share the framework's author and evidence pool.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. Does continuity survive replacing the shared backdrop with an independent one and falsifying the original? (separates D2 from D1)
2. Do independent instruments agree per case, not as a majority average, on the consequences of a disputed norm? (Fig 1 :22)
3. Does the transmitted method produce a novel correct norm in a new habitat, with a measured round-trip gap? (Fig 4 :30)
4. Does a change occur by pure erasure with no refusing term and keep the same lineage evidence? (fires R1)
