# The Tether's answer: moral change with continuity

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result about any particular society. It is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Societies report two things at once: norms change, and "we are still us." A frame that treats identity as persistence of content predicts that one report must be false. The residual is the gap between that prediction and the co-occurrence. Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." The question is a union of four conditions, not one mechanism with one cause, so the answer keeps verdicts separate and never averages them.

## 2. The shape, per domain

**D1, warrant verdict.** Whether a change is warranted is a verdict about the world. Fig 2 :36 "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Fig 2 :37 "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people." Because the question is about people, the anchor is structured judgment of conduct case by case, not an instrument and not the majority report. Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Agreement among frames sharing one evidence pool is disqualifying here (Fig 2 :40). Verdict: `specified`, per person, never averaged.

**D2, continuity convention.** "We are still us" is a convention about shared meaning. Fig 2 :41 "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it." This is legitimate until it is used to decide an outcome: Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." So "tradition" may coordinate a community, and may be imported as provenance into a warrant claim, but may not itself decide which norm may change. Verdict: `general` on the rule, `specified` on the veto failure.

**D3, transmission.** What crosses generations is a method and a seed, not a norm list. Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Fig 4 :34 "A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes." A change is real only if contact changes: Fig 11 :40 "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." The round trip must be measured, not assumed (Fig 4 :30). Verdict: `general` with a `specified` contact test.

**D4, versioning.** Change is a refusal that adds without deleting. Fig 6 :43 "A refutation adds a term that refuses; it removes nothing, and the reach falls because something was added rather than because anything was deleted." Fig 12 :140 "A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate." Continuity is the record half of this operation; change is the reach half. They are different quantities, so they do not compete. Verdict: `specified`.

**Continuity itself** is lineage and arrangement, not identity of content. Fig 13 :128 "Nothing about a composed term tells you, from looking at it, which route produced it or whether that route was available." Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance." Verdict: `specified`, triangulated across Fig 12 and Fig 13 (low overlap between the bond definition and the lineage proof).

**The specific norm and the specific legitimate narrative** are a declared `gap` of the unreached kind. Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Reportable as a verdict, not a silence.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not one mechanism. No single anchor covers both the warrant verdict and the continuity convention.
- Not that majority consensus, prestige, or a sense of continuity settles what is right (Fig 2 :40, Fig 7 :35).
- Not that tradition decides an outcome. A convention that does so has stopped being one (Fig 10 :47).
- Not that continuity is identity of the norm list. It is recoverability of the lineage.
- Not that any particular norm should change in any particular society. That is a name where the framework returns a shape.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement of a society's harm and welfare was used.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the template: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*, and it was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved it. Here the corresponding questions are:

- Does a reported sense of continuity survive when the shared backdrop is replaced by an independent one and the original is falsified? This separates D2 convention from D1 verdict.
- Can the consequences of a disputed norm be read by instruments that do not share the society's evidence pool, and do those readings agree per case rather than as an average (Fig 1 :22)?
- Does the carried method produce a new correct norm in a habitat it never saw, with a measured round-trip gap (Fig 4 :30)?
- Does a change occur by pure erasure with no refusing term added and retain the same lineage evidence? That would fire R1 and break F4.

Standing pre-registered refuters R1 to R7 are in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §3 and are carried unchanged into `08_expert_verification_packet.md` and `09_watson_answer.md`.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The four conditions are bindings onto operators and atoms the framework already holds, not new primitives. What is missing is a discriminator for the sources of a felt continuity reading and independent frames that do not share the society's backdrop. Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 13 :134 "It can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands, so something has to be imported before it is asked again." Fig 7 :6 "the limit: no last room." The record stops here with the residual, the refuters and the lineage visible so that the stop is checkable.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say which norm your society should change, and it does not offer one mechanism for moral change. It says that the question is a union of four conditions that must be kept apart: whether a change is warranted is a verdict anchored in per-person judgment of conduct, while "we are still us" is a convention about shared meaning that becomes illegitimate the moment it decides an outcome; continuity is the recoverable lineage of refused and re-settled rules, not identity of the norm list; change is a refusal that adds a new term without deleting the old one, so the record grows while the reach changes; and what crosses generations is a method and a seed, not a frozen list. Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged. Which specific norm ought to change is a declared gap, not a silence: it stays open until something discriminating measures it. What is offered is the shape any answer must have, seven refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it.
