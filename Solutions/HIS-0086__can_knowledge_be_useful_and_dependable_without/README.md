## Verdict in one line

Yes, conditionally: dependability is a maintained relation to a ground that does not update on what frames think, while the forms of proof favored by institutions are a convention inside one frame. The specific claim that some named institutional form is necessary is a declared `gap`, not a hidden answer. The answer is `specified`, ungrounded, and it refuses "anything goes".

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation settles the question.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. A room does not supply an anchor. *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* (Fig 7 :35)
2. Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation. *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* (Seat Map :163)
3. Showing a verifier the interior before asking for a verdict spends the only thing they contribute. *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* (Fig 10 :42)
