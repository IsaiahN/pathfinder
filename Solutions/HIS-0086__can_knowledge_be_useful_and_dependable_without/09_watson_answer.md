## QUESTION

Can knowledge be useful and dependable without fitting the forms of proof favored by scientific institutions?

Restated in effect terms: for each practical slot s, is membership in a proof convention P necessary for R_s = |required outcome - held prediction| to stay dependably small? Option A says P is necessary and discards ground-paid knowledge that cannot be written in P. Option B says P is a convention inside one frame and dependability is a maintained relation to an anchor, checked by a low-overlap frame. Each leads to a different treatment of craft, oral, Indigenous and apprenticeship knowledge, and to a different standard for what counts as corroboration.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain giving actors, currency, anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Arity: one index per practical slot, many slots, never one global score (Fig 1 :19). Symmetry: dependability is a threshold comparison (does it pay at the ground), not a symmetric join. Scale: the single practitioner loop, the transmission habitat across generations, and cross-system verification. Union, not partition: the case of a claim rejected by an institution but used locally is a case in two conditions (Fig 9 :57).
- **D1 Practitioner loop.** Actors: practitioner, tools, observations, held library. Currency: residual reduction per slot, patient recovers, landfall, harvest. Anchor: the practical outcome, a patient outcome or a market in the figure's own list. Admitted.
- **D2 Transmission habitat.** Actors: apprentice, master, practice, conditions, relations. Currency: survival of the practice, contact maintained. Anchor: the ground through repeated practice. Admitted.
- **D3 Institutional science frame.** Actors: scientists, journals, peer review, statistical methods. Currency: publications, proofs, replications. Anchor: experiment and measurement, while the proof form is a convention. Admitted.
- **D4 Cross-system verification.** Actors: two or more knowledge systems with differing closures. Currency: independence and triangulation. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Admitted.
- **X1 Proof form itself as necessary condition.** No nameable anchor: a convention cannot settle. Not a domain yet.
- **Order by dependency.** perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry (Fig 3). A frame with no term for what a situation affords cannot rank its residuals; adding proof form above that break cannot rescue it.

Strain test applied: Fig 3 governs links inside a frame and Fig 11 governs the habitat around it. First read as a contradiction, dissolved by re-census (different objects). The Feyerabend "anything goes" conflict was returned to census and dissolved: his target is imposed method, which is the Fig 10 convention failure, not ground contact. See `01` F6 and F10.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

Yes, conditionally. Dependability does not require fitting the proof forms favored by scientific institutions. It requires an anchor that settles and does not update on what frames think, a maintained channel to that anchor, and a low-overlap checker. Institutional proof forms are a convention for coordinating meaning inside one frame, efficient for that and not the discriminator of truth. Practical, craft, oral and Indigenous knowledge can meet the conditions, and institutional science can too. The specific claim that some named form is necessary is a `gap`, not a hidden answer. "Anything goes" is refused. Mode `specified`, ungrounded, `candidate`. The two knowledge systems are a functional group as encountered, with an isomer claim available only after a shared atom set is enumerated (`06`). Verdicts are kept separate per domain and never averaged.

- **Cost if wrong:** if the conditional yes is wrong and a practice's channel is in fact dead, we endorse a stale reading and the error stays invisible because internal instruments read through the dead channel (Fig 10 :25-26). If the conditional no were true, we would discard ground-paid capability and concentrate all verification in one closure, the common-cause collapse of Fig 2. The figures price this: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." (Fig 2 :42). The cost is bounded and largely avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuters.
- **Undoable:** yes. A refutation adds a term that refuses and removes nothing, so the reach falls because something was added rather than deleted (Fig 6 :42-43). No primitive is withdrawn, and the shape plus its four refuters are recorded with provenance so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-64).
- **Reversed by:** R1 to R4 in `01` §3. Specifically, a verbatim figure line making a named proof form necessary; a case where dependability vanishes when the form is removed with ground contact held constant; a dead-channel system whose claims still pay at a fresh measurement; or an anchor that updates on institutional opinion. A source with a genuinely different evidence pool naming and demonstrating the necessary form would move F9 from `gap` to `grounded`. Prestige or agreement is not such an instrument.
