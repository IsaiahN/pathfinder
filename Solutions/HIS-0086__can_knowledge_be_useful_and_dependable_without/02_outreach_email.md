# Outreach draft: check the output before deciding about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and the refuter below are stable. The stage-1 output must survive a ground check or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about dependable knowledge, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question in practical epistemology: can knowledge be useful and dependable without fitting the proof forms favored by scientific institutions? I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** dependability is not membership in a proof convention. It is a relation with three parts: (1) the claim is settled by a ground that does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks, an outcome, a measurement, a patient result, a landfall; (2) the channel to that ground is maintained, not stale, pooled, or saturated; (3) a second frame with a different evidence pool can check the claim, so agreement is triangulation rather than negotiation. Institutional proof forms are a grammar for coordinating shared meaning inside one frame. They are efficient for that, and they are not the thing that settles.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that anything goes, that a practice is dependable merely because it is old or local or widely believed, that scientific institutions are worthless, or that I can name which specific proof form is necessary. That last question is a declared open item in my notes, not a hidden answer. The derivation returns the shape of the discriminator, not its name.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before this message:**
> 1. a case where a practical knowledge system loses dependability when the institutional form is removed while ground contact is held constant;
> 2. a case where a knowledge system's channel to the ground is demonstrably dead, yet its claims keep paying at a fresh measurement;
> 3. a case where agreement between frames that share an evidence pool turns out to be genuine corroboration rather than one pool talking;
> 4. an anchor that updates on institutional opinion, so that consensus itself becomes the thing that settles.
>
> If any one holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the public sources. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - The canonical figures README: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Arrival of the Fittest: The Tether Architecture: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Stage order

1. Stage 1: residual, shape claim, refuter. Ask what they would count as a failure. Record their verdict **before** any framework is shown.
2. Stage 2, opt in only: if they are curious, offer the three links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. Stage 3: offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

One recipient, one message, one opt-out line. No list, no automation, no follow-up unless they reply.
