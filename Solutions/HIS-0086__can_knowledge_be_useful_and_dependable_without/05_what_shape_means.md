## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (**a shape, not a name**)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*.

From *The Library of Babel*, read this session: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

So for HIS-0086 a **shape** is the form of dependable knowledge that can be stated before naming any institution or any specific proof form:

> A claim whose terms are settled by an anchor that does not move when frames push on it, whose channel to that anchor is maintained, and which can be checked by a frame drawing on a different evidence pool.

That constrains. It does not pick a credential. "Ground-settled, channel maintained, independently checkable" is a shape. "Double-blind randomized controlled trial" is a name.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: start with the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. "With each new term gained, you narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary. It's this recursive process that will help you converge on the answer."
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"*. So we describe dependability as effect (does the claim keep paying at a fresh slot), not as cause (which institution certified it).
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." The essay's three-part account of contact (context, subtext, pretext) explains why a proof form travels while the pretext behind practical skill does not.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: "You will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place, as there is nowhere else to search and nothing else that can describe the answer better."

The derivation in `01` stopped when the residual was described as a three-part shape and the specific-form question was declared a `gap`. That is a stop at a shape, not at an answer.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays."* The README: the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest"*.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-51), with a source gate and a shadow test.

For this question, "institutional proof" is a name offered by one frame. It is licensed inside that frame by its own conventions, and those conventions are efficient for coordinating meaning (Fig 2 :41: *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*). It is not licensed as the discriminator of dependability without a ground reading.

*The Hard Problem is About Source* sharpens this. The color instrument reads green and "cannot comment on the source". Metamerism: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." The reading does not determine the source. "It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." Here the reading is "complies with P" and the source we actually want is "keeps paying at the ground". A credential is not a source-discriminating instrument. What is owed is an instrument, not a stronger credential.

## 4. Why the shape claim is careful about "local knowledge"

A careless version of the yes answer would say: old practices are dependable because they survived. That is a `general` claim about lineage with no per-slot check, and Fig 1 :19 forbids the aggregation: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*

*Decoding Tacit Knowledge* supplies the right form: the safeguard that stops two people drifting is not agreement but a ground both are corrected by separately. "Only the ground does that." And: "Two navigators don't find their position by agreeing with each other. Each takes a bearing on the same fixed landmark, and nobody can talk the landmark into moving." That is Fig 2 :35 in a different vocabulary: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*

So the shape for this question includes a refusal. A local practice is not dependable because it is local; it is dependable if its terms are settled by a landmark nobody can move and a second frame can take its own bearing.

## 5. Q: does "shape, not a name" forbid naming institutional science at all? ANSWERED with a correction

**Initial reading, recorded so it stays visible:** "shape, not a name" forbids naming any knowledge system as the answer to dependability. Under that reading, even saying "scientific institutions are one frame" would be out of bounds.

**Correction, 2026-11-23.** The README's phrase is *"what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Its subject is a primitive not held in Γ. Institutional science and local practice are both already present in the census as frames with their own actors, currency and anchor. Naming them is not inventing a missing primitive. What is forbidden is promoting either one into the missing discriminator, or claiming that naming it moves the wall (Fig 6 :25: *"An import adds no atom to the world."*).

So the honest form is: the framework returns the shape of the discriminator, not the name of an institution. We may name the frames we are comparing, because we can see them and read their ground contact. We may not name a proof form as the thing that settles, because no figure line licenses that binding. My initial overstatement is retracted; the narrower claim stands.

## 6. Why this audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, read this session: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post." But: "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself." The same essay: "You may all agree and be wrong." And: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."

So `01` and this file are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are not a test of whether any particular knowledge system is dependable, and they cannot settle the question they report on. Any verdict here is `candidate` until an instrument or a fresh ground reading pays for it.

## 7. Consequence for the outreach

Lead with the shape and the four refuters, never with a claim that a community's knowledge is or is not dependable. The recipient's first job is to say what they would count as a failure in the shape itself. Their verdict is recorded before any theory is shown, and later agreement is never counted as corroboration (Fig 2 :40). Prestige, including the prestige of the institution being discussed, can fund a hearing for the claim and cannot settle it.
