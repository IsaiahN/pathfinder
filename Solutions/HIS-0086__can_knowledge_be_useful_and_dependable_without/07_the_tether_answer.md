# The Tether's answer on knowledge outside formal science

**Mode: `specified` on the application to knowledge systems, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

For each practical slot, R_s is the gap between what the situation requires and what the held vocabulary predicts. The question asks whether fitting institutional proof forms is necessary for that residual to stay dependably small. This is a **union compound**, not one hard question: the case of a claim rejected by an institution but used successfully by a community is a case in two conditions, and a single lever cannot clear it (Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"*). It has its own loop and its own ground.

## 2. The shape

1. **The anchor is the outcome, not the proof form.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* A convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one (Fig 10 :47). So dependability is not membership in a proof convention.
2. **Dependability is per slot and ground-paid.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* A single credential across all slots throws away the signal that says where the failure is. Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."*
3. **No single closure can certify its own completeness.** Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."* A search inside institutional proof forms that finds no dependable knowledge has proved nothing about knowledge outside them (Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*).
4. **What crosses generations is a method and a head start.** Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Apprenticeship and oral tradition are maintained habitats in which a lineage keeps meeting its ground (Fig 11 :36), not defective copies of a written proof.
5. **Agreement counts only at low overlap.** Fig 8 :50: *"Two frames can hold the same atom by convergence, reaching it separately, which is corroboration at maximum independence."* Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in content; only the record separates them (Fig 8 :51).
6. **"Anything goes" is refused.** Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*. A frame with stale percepts, saturated metrics, or pooled readings has lost contact, and internal instruments will not report it. Dependability requires a maintained channel and a second, independent bearing.

**Answer:** yes, conditionally. Knowledge can be useful and dependable without fitting the proof forms favored by scientific institutions, provided its terms are settled by a ground that does not update on what frames think, its channel to that ground is maintained, and a low-overlap frame can check it. Institutional proof forms are a grammar for shared meaning inside one frame; they are efficient for that and they are not the discriminator of dependability.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that anything goes. Ground contact and independence are required (§2.6).
- Not that old, local, or widely held practices are dependable by virtue of being old, local, or held (Fig 1 :19; `05` §4).
- Not that scientific institutions are worthless. They are a genuine ground-contacting frame (Fig 2 :29); only the upgrade of their grammar into the ground is refused.
- Not that a particular proof form is unnecessary. Whether any named form is necessary is a declared `gap` (§4), not an answer.
- Not that the two frames are isomers as encountered. The enumeration in `06` leaves the weaker **functional group** reading as the honest one until a shared atom set is written.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement at a fresh slot was taken.

## 4. What would settle it the ground

An instrument that discriminates dependability from proof-form compliance at a new slot, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (read 2026-11-23): a reading "cannot comment on the source", and the case "was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument". Here the questions are:

- **Fresh-slot test:** does the claim keep paying at a slot never used in its certification, with the proof form held constant and ground contact varied, or the reverse?
- **Channel test:** does the frame's channel to the ground still return a reading, or only a recording of a past one (Fig 10 :25-26)?
- **Independence test:** do the two checking frames draw on different evidence, so their agreement is a landmark bearing rather than a shared pool (Fig 2 :31-32)?
- **Provenance test:** can the record show whether agreement came from convergent derivation or adopted import (Fig 8 :51)?
- **Standing refuters:** R1 to R4 in `01` §3, including a figure line making a named form necessary, and an anchor that updates on opinion.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*. The specific-form question is reported as a `gap`, with the search that produced it recorded in `01` §5. We do not import a frame to fill the gap, because importing against a budget limit adds an atom that was not needed. The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity"* (figures README).

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say that anything goes, and it does not dismiss scientific institutions. It says that what makes knowledge dependable is not the proof form a community uses but three things any dependable claim needs: an anchor that settles the question and does not move when people push on it, a channel to that anchor that is still returning fresh readings, and a second checker drawing on different evidence. Proof forms are a grammar for agreement inside one frame, useful for coordinating meaning and not the thing that settles truth. Apprenticeship, oral tradition and craft knowledge can satisfy the three conditions; institutional science can too; and when they agree independently their agreement is corroboration only if the record shows they got there separately. What the framework cannot do is name a specific institutional form as the necessary one. That question stays open until an instrument discriminates dependability from compliance at a new slot. The shape, the refuters written in advance, and the lineage are what this answer offers; the ground has the last word.
