## DERIVATION

### Q1 : Are the three curvature regimes isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :46: **"A bond is the typed relation between two operands."**
- Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**
- All three FLRW regimes are written over the same atom set `{Space, Time, Energy, Gradient, Network, Spectrum}`. They differ in which structure composes with Space: a directedness-like composition for positive curvature, an ordering-like composition for zero curvature, and the opposite directedness for negative curvature. Over that fixed set they satisfy the *same atoms* half of the definition.
- The decisive half is *different bond*. If the three regimes differ only by the sign of one scalar in the spatial metric, the difference is a **parameter**, not a bond, and they are one substance under three parameter values. That would fail Fig 12 :117. The stronger reading, that the sign records which structure bonds to Space and therefore a different typed relation, satisfies it.
- The honest verdict: the isomer claim is `specified` and **single-source** on the figure side (Fig 12 :117), with the physics-facing check still unmeasured. The weaker claim survives without it: the three regimes are members of a **functional group**, Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** The group is `state → structure → state` over Space and Time; the qualifier is the sign of the curvature quantity.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if all three regimes can be written with identical typed bonds and differing only in a scalar magnitude, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands alone.

### Q2 : What order does the measurement chain have, and where does it break?

- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
- Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**
- Chain for this question: perceive the acoustic scale, name a curvature term, state what would count as a sign decision, choose an instrument, carry the result.
- Links 1 and 2 work. The acoustic scale is measured to high precision and `Omega_K` is a standard fitted parameter. The break is at the objective link: below a curvature of order `10^-4`, the public reference states that even future experiments such as the SKA cannot distinguish flat, open and closed, so there is no instrument that can return a sign there. It is a link with no instrument, not a weak reading. Fig 3 :22-23: a reading taken below the break is *"a reading of nothing."*
- More precision on the acoustic scale is effort above the break and cannot rescue it. Fig 3 :29-30: **"Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."**
- The chain is therefore `unreached` at this instrument budget, not `unreachable` in principle. Fig 6 :37: **"It tells a question that nothing could ever answer apart from one that is merely hard. It is not a rule about theory."**

### Q3 : What kind of operator is the decision?

- Operators table :49-51: **"a threshold relation between two quantities"** and **"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"**
- "Flat or curved" is a comparison against zero, not a conjunction of ingredients and not a new substance. It asks which side of a threshold a measured quantity lies on. The figures can bind the decision as an ordinary term (what an answer would look like, `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction"), but the side of the threshold is supplied by the ground. Fig 9 :42: **"narrowing gets you close, the ground decides"**
- There is a second threshold in a different currency: "measurable" refers to the instrument's resolution (`10^-4` to `10^-3`), not to the magnitude of the parameter. Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."** A question can be well posed in parameter space and unanswered in instrument space. Both are read; neither is summed into the other.

### Q4 : Does recursion help settle the value?

- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"**
- Each measurement regime, once settled against the ground, becomes an atom at the next level: a CMB acoustic-scale result, a BAO distance result, a lensing reconstruction. Their comparison is a second-order composition over settled molecules, and it needs no new primitive. What it needs is provenance: which readings settled, on which instrument, under which model (Fig 8 :50-52: *"Only the record of where each came from separates them"*).
- This is why the CMB-only and combined-fit disagreement is a second-order composition problem with a provenance gap, not a call for a new curvature atom. Fig 13 :74: **"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."**

## CONCLUSION

The three curvature regimes are at minimum a **functional group** over the fixed six-term atom set, and the stronger **isomer** reading is `specified`, single-source, with a stated falsifier (Q1). The measurement dependency chain currently breaks at the objective or sign-test link, where no instrument resolves curvature below order `10^-4`; the status is `unreached` at this budget, not `unreachable` in principle (Q2). The decision itself is a threshold comparison, and "measurable" is a second threshold in the instrument currency; the two do not add (Q3). Recursion lets settled readings become operands but does not supply the missing ground reading (Q4). No numeric value of `Omega_k` is derived, and none is owed from this derivation.
