# Expert verification packet - the Tether on emergent gravity and real spacetime

**What this is:** a claim in a form a physicist or philosopher of physics can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape-level claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the substrate and anchor upgrade. Not `grounded`.** No cross-anchor measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

An analogue of gravity reproduces the **shape** of relativistic behaviour because the framework's minimum set predicts that a continuum and an effective geometry are products of composition, not primitives. The analogue is the same kernel with different actors, currency and anchor, so its agreement with relativistic predictions is convergent derivation from a second closure, not a new atom. It does **not** follow that real spacetime has the analogue's substrate, because the lab anchor (a measured spectrum) and the spacetime anchor (an astrophysical observation) are different anchors, and only a provenanced import moves the wall between frames. The shape claim is checkable; the substrate claim is a declared gap. What would close it is a cross-anchor instrument or a source discriminator, not further agreement.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal, checkable by reading Figures 13 and 6

- **Statement:** an emergent effective geometry is predicted by the six-term set, and reproducing it in another substrate adds no seventh primitive and moves no wall.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :43-44 *"A continuum was the seventh and was removed for failing exactly this: it is a spectrum without gaps, or smoothness over space and time. A generating set must not contain what its own members produce."*; Fig 13 :124 *"Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are each what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member."*; Fig 6 :25 *"An import adds no atom to the world."*
- **What to check:** is the acoustic metric a derivative of the composition in the same sense as smoothness, rather than a new kind?
- **Refuter:** derive a seventh kind from arrangements of the six, or exhibit an analogue term not composable from the six that real gravity requires.

### C2 - physics-facing, checkable against the analogue literature

- **Statement:** the analogue and relativistic cores are the same kernel with different casts, so their agreement is convergence rather than import. Over a fixed atom set they are isomers; as ordinarily written they are a functional group.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 4 :20 *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."*; Fig 8 :51 *"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."*; Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- **What to check:** Steinhauer 2016 reports *"spontaneous Hawking radiation... emanating from an analogue black hole in an atomic Bose-Einstein condensate"*; Munoz de Nova et al. 2019 report that *"the correlation spectrum of Hawking radiation agrees well with a thermal spectrum, and its temperature is given by the surface gravity"*. Is that a reproduction of the relational structure, or does the analogue require a term outside the relativistic closure (a preferred frame, a discrete dispersion)? The second outcome breaks the strict isomer claim and leaves the functional-group claim standing.
- **Refuter:** show a required term outside the held set, or show that the two cores cannot both be written as `state - structure - state` at first order.

### C3 - the anchor claim, checkable by anyone who can name a ground

- **Statement:** lab agreement cannot settle the real-spacetime substrate claim, because the two frames range against different anchors. This is the declared `gap`.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*; Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*; Fig 10 :24 *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"*
- **What to check:** can a reader name one instrument whose reading ranges against both the analogue and real spacetime on a single axis? If not, there is no shared anchor and the verdicts stay separate.
- **Refuter:** produce that instrument and show divergent readings where the shape predicts identity, or supply a provenanced import that adds an atom to the real-gravity frame.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that a condensate, fluid or circuit is the substrate of real spacetime. The framework returns a shape, not a name.
- Not that a lab measurement settles astrophysical black-hole thermodynamics.
- Not that GR is wrong or that spacetime does not exist. GR is settled in its own domain.
- Not a derivation of gravity from condensed matter, and no new primitive.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by the relevant cross-anchor ground.
- Not a theory of everything, and not the only true framework. A frame cannot certify its own limit.
- Not a claim that the round-trip gap is globally zero. Universality may make it small for particular observables; for substrate questions it is recorded as nonzero and quantity-specific.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A **cross-anchor instrument**: one measurement axis on which the analogue and real spacetime both range, independently corrected by each.
2. A **source discriminator** that distinguishes a metric-like reading produced by a discrete preferred-frame substrate from one produced by a relativistic substrate. That would name the slot.
3. A **provenanced import** with source gate and shadow test that adds an atom to the real-gravity frame.
4. The standing falsifiers: derive one of the six from the other five; show the analogue needs a term outside the held set; demonstrate a zero round-trip gap for the substrate claim.
