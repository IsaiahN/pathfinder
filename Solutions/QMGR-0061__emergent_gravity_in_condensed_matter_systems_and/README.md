## The verdict in one line

An analogue can model the **shape** of relativistic behaviour: a different cast running the same cross-kind composition, a convergence of two closures at maximum independence (Fig 4 :20, Fig 8 :51). It cannot, by rearranging its own closure, add the atom that would make its substrate the substrate of real spacetime (Fig 6 :21, Fig 6 :25, Fig 13 :115). The lab anchor and the astrophysical anchor are different anchors, so lab agreement is not a verdict about real spacetime (Fig 2 :29, Fig 2 :35). The first claim is `specified` and triangulated; the second is a declared `gap`, reported as a verdict and not as silence.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **Agreement is not an anchor.** Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* An analogue that agrees with a theory under a shared derivation is one pool talking, not a second witness.
2. **Selecting for receptiveness manufactures the disqualifying correlation.** Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*
3. **A verifier must not hold the interiors.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, never the source.

Legally: scraped personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breach GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in and submission venues avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
