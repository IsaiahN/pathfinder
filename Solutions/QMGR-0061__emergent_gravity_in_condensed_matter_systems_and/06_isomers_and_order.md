# Are the analogue and the relativistic core isomers? Order, structure and analogues

**Mode: `specified`, with the fixed-atom condition named as a live constraint. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The audit left two structural questions open. (1) If an analogue core and a relativistic core are both written as `state to structure to state`, are they **isomers** under Fig 12, or do they have different atom sets? (2) What order is licensed, given that the ordinary word "emergent" suggests a sequence that the figures may not supply?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held in D3: Space, Time, Energy (states) and Gradient, Network, Spectrum (structures). Bonds held: the seven plus negation. No new primitive is available without import. The analogue core also holds, in D1, a background flow and a discrete microscopic dispersion, which are measurements in that frame, not new kinds in D3.

## DERIVATION

### Q1  -  Both cores are first-order cross-kind compositions

- Fig 13 :74: **"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."**
- Fig 13 :75: **"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."**
- Fig 13 :76: **"A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."**

A first-order molecule is `state - structure - state`, one crossing. The relativistic core reads `Space - metric-like structure - Time`. The analogue core reads `Space - acoustic structure - Time`, with the flow supplying the directedness. Both are legal first-order molecules. Fig 13 itself licenses more than one gradient-like term, since which state it bonds to is part of the term.

### Q2  -  Isomer, but only over a fixed atom set

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**

Two readings:

- **As the physics is usually written**, the analogue core needs a background flow and a microscopic dispersion that the relativistic core does not, and the relativistic core needs a dynamical metric that the analogue core only mimics. The atom sets differ, so they are **not** isomers in the strict Fig 12 sense. They are two members of the same **functional group**: `state - structure - state`, giving an effective horizon when the structure supports one.
- **Under the fixed-atom condition**: if both cores are written over the full shared set {Space, Time, Energy, Gradient, Network, Spectrum}, differing only in which structure mediates which state pair and in the bond that joins them, then they **are** isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. This is the stronger and checkable claim worth testing.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if either core requires an atom the other cannot express even after the fixed-atom rewrite (for example, if the preferred frame is a primitive in the analogue and not expressible at all in the relativistic closure), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands instead.

### Q3  -  Order: a sequence is licensed, a ranking of the kinds is not

- Fig 12 :111: **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**
- Fig 13 :75 uses commas, not arrows: **"It is not space-time but space, a structure, time"**.
- Fig 4 :20: **"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."**

The cross-kind pattern is required, so `state - structure - state` is a genuine route and not a flat list of ingredients. But the framework does not say which state composed first or whether order matters at the level of the kinds. The exemplar QMGR-0037 records the same point from its source article: *"we cannot say which state composed first, and we don't know if the order mattered."* Writing `Space to Gradient to Time` with a strong arrow adds an ordering the derivation does not license. The honest notation is the figure's own: commas, with the bond typed separately.

For this question that means: "the fluid comes first and spacetime is produced from it" is a claim about D1's history, not a figure-derived sequence over D3's kinds.

### Q4  -  Recursion: a settled analogue becomes an atom, but only inside its own reach

- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"**
- Fig 12 :103: **"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."**

The analogue result is settled in D1: the experiments have an anchor that pays. It can therefore serve as an atom for whatever composes over it **in D1**. It does not thereby become an atom in D2; that would require a settle in D2's ground, which is exactly the `gap` the audit reports. This is the precise reason the shape transfers and the substrate does not. Fig 6 :26: **"The world does not gain primitives; a frame gains access."**

### Q5  -  Analogy as a third structure, and where it breaks

The cognitive-science source on analogy (Wikipedia, "Analogy", opened 2026-11-23) describes structure mapping theory after Gentner: the mapping *"takes place not only between objects, but also between relations of objects and between relations of relations."* That corroborates the isomer reading: what transfers is the relation and the relation between relations, not the identity of the actors. The same source warns that *"every analogy breaks down when stretched beyond its intended meaning"*, which is the ordinary-language form of the nonzero round-trip gap in F6 of the audit. Overlap is low (cognitive science, not physics), so this is genuine corroboration of the mechanism shape, not of any physical verdict.

## CONCLUSION

Both cores are first-order cross-kind molecules. They are a **functional group** as ordinarily written, and **isomers** under the fixed-atom condition, with a stated falsifier. The cross-kind order is required; a ranking of the kinds is not given and must not be imported from the word "emergent". A settled analogue becomes an atom only within its own reach and anchor, which is why the shape moves and the substrate claim does not. The analogy transfers relations, and it breaks at the substrate.
