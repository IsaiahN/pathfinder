**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the two verdicts in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are kept separate in the text. If the email collapses shape reproduction into a substrate claim, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** One figure-grounded output on analogue gravity, with a failure condition written in advance

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** A condensed matter system can produce readings that look relativistic: an effective metric, a horizon, a Hawking temperature. The question is whether that licenses a claim about *real* spacetime, or only about the form of the behaviour. Those are two conditions with different anchors, so I keep two verdicts and never average them.
>
> **Verdict 1 (`specified`, not `grounded`): the shape is reachable.** The framework's minimum set has six terms in two kinds, states (space, time, energy) and structures (spectrum, gradient, network), with composition running across kinds and never within one. It already removes the continuum from the primitive list because a continuum is what a composition *leaves behind*. So an effective geometry arising from another substrate is exactly what the rule predicts, not a refutation of it. The analogue is the same kernel with different actors, currency and anchor. Two frames reaching that shape from different evidence is convergence, not a new primitive.
>
> **Verdict 2 (`gap`): the substrate and the ground are not transferred.** The lab anchor is a measured spectrum in the apparatus. The anchor for real spacetime is an astrophysical observation. A mirror cannot correct you, and agreement between the analogue and the theory is one evidence pool talking. No figure line I can find licenses the step from "the analogue realizes the shape" to "real spacetime has this substrate." That is a declared gap, reported as a verdict, not as silence.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that a Bose-Einstein condensate or a fluid *is* what spacetime is made of; that the lab measurement settles black-hole thermodynamics; that this is a theory of everything or the only true framework; or that the round-trip gap between the fluid description and the spacetime description is zero. Universality may make that gap small for some observables. For substrate questions I record it as nonzero and quantity-specific.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before this message:**
> 1. derive one of the six from the other five, or produce a seventh kind by rearranging them;
> 2. show the analogue needs a bond or term outside the held set, so the kernel is not the same;
> 3. produce one instrument that ranges against the analogue and real spacetime on the same axis, and show the readings diverge where the shape predicts identity;
> 4. show, with provenance and a shadow test, a genuine import that adds an atom to the real-gravity frame rather than only to the analogue frame.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw the verdict. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Analogue Gravity, Barcelo, Liberati and Visser, Living Reviews in Relativity: https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0505065
> - Observation of thermal Hawking radiation and its temperature in an analogue black hole, Munoz de Nova et al., Nature 569 (2019): https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-019-1241-0
> - Canonical figures text and formula (local framework files): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` and `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
