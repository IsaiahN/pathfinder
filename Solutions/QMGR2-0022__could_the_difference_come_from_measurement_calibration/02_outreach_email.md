**Subject:** Check one output about the Hubble tension before you decide what causes it

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about distance ladder calibration. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with a conclusion. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the residual in the Hubble tension is the gap between two H0 determinations, about 67.4 km/s/Mpc from Planck plus LCDM and about 73.0 km/s/Mpc from the SH0ES distance ladder. The framework I am using says this residual must be indexed per measurement slot, that is per rung, anchor and independent method, and never averaged into one global number. It also says that a calibration error is a failure of the measurement channel, not a change in the thing being measured, and that the honest output of a channel failure is a loss of contact rather than a verdict about new physics.
>
> **What I am asking you to check:** if calibration or another systematic is the cause, the fault should be locatable. The three geometric anchors (Gaia parallaxes, LMC detached eclipsing binaries, the NGC 4258 maser) should fail an anchor test: a genuine anchor does not move when you push on it. An independent ladder, such as the tip of the red giant branch, should show where the gap sits. The deliverable I am offering is a per-rung residual table with the live slot marked, plus a description of the measurement that would discriminate a bad channel from a wrong early-universe model.
>
> **What would refute this shape:**
> 1. a zero-point shift that brings the ladder into agreement with Planck within 1 sigma while the anchors still agree, which would confirm calibration and refute new physics;
> 2. three or more late-universe methods with no shared calibration all returning H0 above 71 km/s/Mpc at more than 3 sigma, which would refute a common calibration cause;
> 3. a per-slot table in which no rung carries a live residual, which would leave the "locate the bad rung" programme with no target;
> 4. a sound-horizon measurement differing from the LCDM prediction, which would move the problem to the early-universe model.
>
> I wrote these down before putting this together. If one holds, I will retract the shape. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **A note on the literature, so I am not overselling:** the SH0ES team reports "no indication this arises from measurement errors or analysis variations considered to date", which is a result about the variants they checked, not a proof that no systematic exists. The TRGB ladder sits between the two values. I am treating both as evidence to range against, not as verdicts.
>
> **If the shape survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical figures README (local framework copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
