# Expert verification packet — the Tether on distance ladder calibration and the Hubble tension

**What this is:** a claim in a form a cosmologist or observational astronomer can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` — not `grounded`.** No new measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The Hubble tension residual must be reported per measurement slot, never averaged into one global number, because aggregation is how a live signal disappears. A calibration or systematic error is a failure of the measurement channel while the expansion rate it measures does not change, so the honest output of such a failure is a loss of contact rather than a verdict about new physics. A legitimate anchor must not move when pushed. The candidate causes form a union of overlapping conditions and must be unbundled before search. The output the framework can license is the shape of a discriminator, a per-rung residual table and refuters written in advance, not the name of a culprit. Which term is actually wrong in our world is left open.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 2 and 10

- **Statement:** the residual is indexed per slot; a calibration fault is channel decay, not ground decay; an anchor must not update.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 1 :19 — **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
  - Fig 1 :22 — **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**
  - Fig 10 :24 — **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**
  - Fig 10 :25 — **"stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"**
  - Fig 2 :29-30 — **"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."**
- **Refuter:** a per-rung residual table with no live slot and all rungs within 1 sigma of the combined value refutes the per-slot claim; a demonstration that the ladder readings change the expansion rate they measure refutes the channel distinction; an anchor whose value is stable under every push test leaves the anchor rule intact rather than refuting it.

### C2 — physics-facing; checkable by an observational cosmologist against the published ladder analyses

- **Statement:** the geometric anchors partially pass the push test, the independent TRGB ladder shows a nonzero round-trip gap, and the broad systematic search is closure-bounded rather than conclusive.
- **What to check:** Riess et al. 2019 report that removing any one of LMC DEBs, NGC 4258 masers or Milky Way parallaxes changes H0 by less than 0.7 percent. Riess et al. 2022 report approximately 70 analysis variants and state "no indication this arises from measurement errors or analysis variations considered to date." Freedman et al. 2019 obtain H0 = 69.8 +/- 0.8 (stat) +/- 1.7 (sys) km/s/Mpc from the TRGB ladder. Are those characterisations faithful, and is the between-ladder difference a structural gap rather than a statistical fluctuation?
- **Figure bearing:** Fig 4 :30 — **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."** And Fig 8 :39 — **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**
- **Refuter:** three or more probes sharing no calibration anchor all above 71 km/s/Mpc at more than 3 sigma would refute a common calibration cause; a documented zero-point shift that reconciles both values while the anchors still agree would confirm calibration and refute the new-physics reading; a full enumeration of the systematic closure shown to be sealed would permit a genuine proof of absence.

### C3 — framework-internal derivation

- **Statement:** the candidate causes form a union, and the two published ladders are members of one functional group of distance ladders rather than a single settled chain. Fig 9 :54 — **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** Fig 12 :124 — **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** Fig 12 :14-16 — **"the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not"**.
- **What to check:** does the reported H0 change when the middle distance indicator changes (Cepheid versus TRGB) while the geometric anchors and final SN Ia rung stay the same? If so, the arrangement is carrying information the final number does not record.
- **Refuter:** if every proposed condition is the same condition under different names, the union collapses to a partition and the unbundling instruction does not apply; if both ladders can be written over the full shared atom set with only the arrangement differing, the stronger isomer claim replaces the functional-group claim.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not "the tension is caused by calibration", and not "the tension requires new physics". Both are names, and no ground reading here pays for either.
- Not "systematics have been ruled out". The search was bounded by the variants considered.
- Not a new value of H0, not a derivation of one ladder from another, and no new primitive.
- Not a claim that the figures can name which rung is faulty. Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."**
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
- Not a claim that this framework is the only true account of cosmological measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

1. **Anchor push test:** vary each geometric anchor independently and record the H0 shift. A genuine anchor does not move (Fig 2 :29-30).
2. **Per-rung residual table:** report R+_s for every rung and method; identify the live slot.
3. **Common-cause test:** three or more non-shared-calibration probes all above 71 km/s/Mpc at more than 3 sigma.
4. **Zero-point confirmation:** a documented shift reconciling ladder and Planck while the anchors still agree.
5. **Sound-horizon test:** a measurement of r_s differing from the LCDM prediction moves the fault to the early-universe domain.
6. **Instrument limit:** a demonstration that no instrument at any resolution can discriminate channel fault from model fault is a recorded `unreached` verdict, not an excuse to choose a side.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, :22, Fig 2 :29-30, Fig 10 :24-28, Fig 8 :39 and Fig 9 :54. Six short passages.
2. Check C2 against the published numbers: is the less-than-0.7-percent anchor-removal statement correct? Is the TRGB value really between Planck and SH0ES?
3. Name one measurement that would distinguish a moving zero point from a wrong sound horizon, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
