**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Standing:** `specified`, not `grounded`. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

**Subject:** Check one structural claim about initial conditions before you decide which proposal is right

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to decide between Hartle-Hawking and Vilenkin first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the gravitational dynamics does not select a unique initial state. A boundary condition is owed, and that boundary condition is an import rather than something the dynamics derives. Hartle-Hawking and Vilenkin are two different arrangements over the same formal atoms: the action, the wave function of the universe, minisuperspace variables, and a semiclassical approximation. Same atoms, different boundary rule, different predicted weights over histories. That is a structural observation about the two proposals, not a verdict in favour of either one. I am explicitly not picking a winner.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the framework returns "no-boundary" or "tunneling." It returns a shape. The shape is the rule an answer must have: select a unique solution, declare the imported boundary condition, state what would refute it. Calling that shape "Hartle-Hawking" or "Vilenkin" is my binding from inside the physics frame, not a figure result, and I am not presenting it as one. I am also not claiming a theory of the initial state would be a final explanation. The framework has no last room.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written down before this run:**
> 1. a derivation inside the dynamics alone that yields a unique initial state with no boundary condition added;
> 2. a demonstration that the two proposals draw on genuinely independent evidence or require different primitives, so they are two frames rather than two arrangements in one closure;
> 3. an instrument, at any resolution, that returns a discriminating reading of the boundary condition;
> 4. a derivation of any one of the six substrate terms from the other five, or a terminal frame with no outside left to import from.
>
> If any one holds, the claim is wrong and I will withdraw it. A one-line "this fails because ..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.

> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`

> - Hartle-Hawking proposal, reference page: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartle%E2%80%93Hawking_proposal
>

The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and the author's essay warns that outputs can be simulated rather than derived. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
