## Three things in the sources that bear directly on this question

1. **The boundary condition is the added atom.** The boundary value problem page shows a general solution with free constants becoming a unique solution once a boundary condition is imposed. Lehners says the no-boundary proposal *"provides a quantum theory of initial conditions"* by supplying probabilities over solutions. Both are the same move in different fields: the dynamics is not the whole answer, and the missing piece is an import with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :14, Fig 8's gates).
2. **The two proposals share a closure.** The Hartle-Hawking page describes Euclidean path integration over geometries; Lehners describes the same canonical and path-integral framework in minisuperspace. They are not two independent instruments ranged against a ground. They are isomers (Fig 12 :117) in one frame (Fig 8 :49). Agreement between their practitioners cannot triangulate.
3. **The origin itself is unreached.** The Initial singularity page calls the singularity a breakdown of current models, the Quantum cosmology page locates the failure at the Planck time, and the SEP entry states that independent experimental probes at the relevant scales are lacking. These are three different fields pointing at the same stop, which the figures name as `unreachable` rather than silence (Fig 13 :134).

## Distribution: offer, do not push

- **Fig 4 :22** - *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the formalism before a recipient has described the residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the owed boundary condition, the isomer reading and the refuter; offer links after.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim, (2) the pre-registered refuter, (3) ask what the recipient would count as a failure, (4) record their verdict, (5) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.
