## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"* and stops *"at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :135: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*: you *"can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question:

- shape: *a boundary condition is owed; it must select a unique solution; it must be declared as an import; it must carry a refuter; no current instrument discriminates the candidates.*
- name: *Hartle-Hawking* or *Vilenkin*.

The distinction is not pedantry. A named proposal comes with a probability measure, a contour prescription and a set of predictions. The shape is what both must satisfy. Substituting the name for the shape is what §4 calls the metamerism error.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary already held that is *"closest to the thing,"* then look for new terms that overlap or correlate with it, and recurse. *"With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."*
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."* For the origin, the effect description is: a differential law plus boundary data yields a unique history; without the boundary data, the law alone leaves a family. Anyone can check that in a boundary value problem, whatever they think of quantum cosmology.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: the answer is reached when *"the finish line will also be the starting place,"* and the paradox is that by then you have *"composed the answer through the process of locating it."* Here the loop stops at the boundary-condition shape, not at a proposal.

Applied to R: first describe the six slots (per-slot residual, Fig 1 :19), then let the closest held vocabulary be "a law with a missing condition," then let the world sources supply the word "boundary condition." That is proximity bootstrapping, not a paraphrase of a summary.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* The canonical README: the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish."* For the initial state, no such instrument exists at present (see `01` F3).
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test. Importing "boundary condition" from the mathematics of differential equations is legitimate: the source is declared, and it explains a gap (the dynamics alone does not pick a unique solution). That import gives the *shape* of the missing thing. It does not give the name of the correct boundary condition.

*The Hard Problem is About Source* sharpens the difference. Metamerism: an instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source."* Several sources can give the same reading. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* The resolution came *"not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument,"* the spectrometer. Here, both HH and V can be made compatible with the same observed universe. Reading off one as "the source" of the other's predictions is the metamerism error. What is owed is a discriminating instrument, not a vote.

## 4. Q: Does the framework refuse to discuss HH and V at all? Answered with a correction

**First reading (recorded, then narrowed).** I initially wrote: the framework returns a shape, not a name, so it cannot say anything about Hartle-Hawking or Vilenkin. That was over-broad.

**Corrected reading.** The README's phrase governs a *missing* thing, a primitive not held in Γ. HH and V are not missing primitives. They are arrangements over atoms already held in the physics frame (action, wave function, minisuperspace, semiclassical approximation). Discussing them, comparing their structures, and noting that they are isomers is entirely within what the figures can do. What is not licensed is presenting a selection between them as a figure derivation. That selection is a binding from the physics frame and must be recorded as such.

**Why the correction matters here.** It lets the solution keep the useful result (the two proposals are isomers; an import is owed; no discriminator exists) without claiming the framework settles a physics debate it cannot settle. Both claims are now recorded so neither is quietly lost. Triangulated across the README and Fig 13, with Fig 9 :32 governing the limit.

## 5. Why the survey cannot name the answer

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself,"* and *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."*

A 2026 survey of physicists is prestige and agreement combined. Under Fig 2 :35, *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you,"* and Fig 7 :35, *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict,"* it cannot name the boundary condition. It can only show that the field is unsettled. That is the use made of it in `01` F6, and no other.

## 6. Two consequences for outreach

**A failure is more useful than an endorsement.** *The Hard Problem is About Source* says the spectrometer, not an argument, resolved the source. *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* says that without contact the best you can send is a plain description of what you would expect to see. So the outreach packet asks what the recipient would count as a failure, and records the answer before showing any theory.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* states that LLMs can *"simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula,"* that *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself,"* and calls LLM output *"simulacra."* So `01` is a simulated derivation, checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of the shape claim and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on. The same label appears in `07`, `08`, `09` and `11`.
