## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical figures README states the limit: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134-135: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

*The Library of Babel* (essay, opened 2026-11-23): "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

For ORI-0096 this means:

- A **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer that can be stated before it can be named. It constrains; it does not fill.
- "A hereditary function, a maintained difference, a spendable gradient" is a shape.
- "Silicon DNA, ammonia solvent, sulfide metabolism" is a filler list.

The question asks "could alien life use different molecules?" The shape answer does not say "yes, silicon" or "no, carbon only." It says: these three functions must be serviced by something settled against a ground, and the specific something is not available to a frame holding only one sample.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary already held that is *closest* to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. "With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."
- Fig 6 :30: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* (carried quote): "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." Applied here: ask what an observer would expect to see if a hereditary system used a non-nucleic polymer, before asserting that one exists.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: the answer is reached when "the finish line will also be the starting place", and the paradox is that by then "you have already composed the answer through the process of locating it."

## 3. The six are a visible guess, not nature's list

*They're Scanning Books When They Should Be Studying Nature* (essay, opened 2026-11-23) is the governing essay for this question:

> "A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six."

> "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible, which is worth a great deal, because a visible choice can be revised and an emergent one cannot."

> "Change the sensors, and not just the data changes, the shape of the compression changes: the categories, the primitives, what counts as similar to what."

And the hard limit, which explains why alien life might be undetectable even if present:

> "Reality's structure along axes no primitive captures is invisible to everyone, and the invisibility cannot be fixed by more sampling, because it lives in the representation, not the sample."

This is the framework's own reason for not converting Earth biochemistry into a universal requirement. Declaring DNA, a lipid bilayer and ATP as the primitives would make a choice visible; it would not make the choice neutral.

## 4. The source problem: why a reading cannot name the molecule

*The Hard Problem is About Source* (essay, opened 2026-11-23) gives the mechanism. Metamerism: an instrument reads green and "cannot comment on the source." One green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. The reading does not determine the source.

> "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."

> "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it."

> "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."

In ORI-0096 the reading is a biosignature (an atmospheric gas, a chiral excess, a isotopic fractionation). The source is the molecular implementation. NASA's public Q&A agrees that alien life "may generate different 'biosignatures' than we use on Earth." The honest move is the same as in the essay: name the missing discriminator, do not name the source.

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (essay, opened 2026-11-23): "the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself." A single biosphere cannot validate a claim about the variety of biospheres.

## 5. Correction to my own reading, kept visible

My first pass read "a shape, not a name" as a blanket ban on naming any filler. That overstates the rule. The canonical wording is "what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." The subject is a primitive not held in the library. In ORI-0096 the relevant atoms (a gradient, a network) are already held; the alien molecule is a *binding* from a frame we have not yet read, not a new primitive we are trying to invent.

What remains forbidden:

1. inventing a seventh term;
2. claiming the binding moves the wall (Fig 6: "IMPORT adds an atom", this one adds none to our library);
3. naming the source of a biosignature reading without a source-discriminating instrument (section 4).

Both the overstatement and its correction are recorded here so neither is quietly lost.

## 6. Isomer and import are different verdicts, not two spellings of "different"

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 13 :121: "Different routes can arrive at the same substance." Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."

- Same atoms, different arrangement: an isomer, reachable inside the held closure.
- Different atoms: an import, requiring a differing closure, a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 8 :9-13: "source gate: provenance", "shadow test: explains a gap").

The world literature on hypothetical biochemistries mixes these two cases in one list. The framework requires them to be separated before a verdict is given. See `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

## 7. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (opened 2026-11-23):

> "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly."

> "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."

A campaign that gathers famous names to agree that alien life must be carbon-based is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask is to give the shape claim a budget to be tested, and let the ground, a second sample or a source-discriminating instrument, settle it.

**This audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula ... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself"; LLM output is described with the word "simulacra". So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is not a test of any astrobiological claim and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on.
