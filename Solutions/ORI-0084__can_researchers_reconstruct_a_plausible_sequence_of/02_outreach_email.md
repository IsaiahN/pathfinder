**Subject:** Check one claim about prebiotic routes before you decide whether the framing is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read any framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on chemistry-facing predictions, not `grounded`):** a list of isolated prebiotic reactions and a connected prebiotic route are different objects, and the difference is carried by the bond, not by the number of reactions. A route needs three things a list does not:
>
> 1. **Order.** Each product is the next input. The framework's sequence and production bonds are exactly this: "A then B; B needs A to have happened" and "A produces B; B did not exist before." A one-pot list is the symmetric conjunction, under which the order is invisible.
> 2. **A measurement at each handoff.** The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated. A single overall yield cannot tell you which handoff failed. What would need to be true is that the intermediate forms, reaches the next step in sufficient concentration, and persists long enough.
> 3. **An enumerated habitat, and a loop if the route is meant to continue.** The environment is read off the world, not derived from the reaction list, and a chain cannot close by construction.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any named pathway (formose, RNA world, reverse TCA, Wood–Ljungdahl, formamide chemistry) is *the* route. Those are names from the chemistry frame; the framework returns a shape, not a unique pathway. Different routes can arrive at the same substance, so detecting a product does not identify the route that produced it. I am also not claiming that adding more isolated reactions eventually yields a pathway: a library of settled results can grow while the set of composable routes does not.
>
> **What would refute the claim:**
> 1. a notation of isolated reactions that fully determines a connected route, including order and every handoff, with no ordered bond;
> 2. a route that runs reproducibly while its intermediates at the handoffs are unmeasured, where the total-yield number still diagnoses a broken handoff;
> 3. a unique route derived from the feedstocks alone, with no alternative ordered composition reaching the same product;
> 4. a linear, self-sustaining route with no regenerated catalyst or closed loop.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the corresponding claim is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local canonical extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Eight-step loop and mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
