## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed zero. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 12 :111, :72, :77, :121, Fig 5 :72, Fig 1 :19, Fig 11 :43, Fig 13 :121.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07` and `09`: a route needs ordered bonds, per-slot measurement, an enumerated habitat, a cycle if continuing, and returns a shape.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete claim of the form "this named network reconstructed this product on the Hadean Earth."

**R_T is nonzero.** Four things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **per-slot measurement** requirement, which a product-only claim drops;
- the **order test**, which a list of reactions does not run;
- the **habitat enumeration**, which a flask-only demonstration substitutes;
- the **isomer alternative**, which a unique-route claim suppresses.

Two things survive cleanly: a bonded route is a different object from a list of ingredients, and settlement is necessary but not sufficient for composition. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set R_T to zero and commit the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
