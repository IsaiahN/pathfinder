# Expert verification packet: reconstructed routes vs isolated prebiotic reactions

**What this is:** a claim in a form a prebiotic chemist or reaction-network researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the structural shape, `specified` on chemistry-facing predictions, not `grounded`.** No measurement at a handoff is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A list of isolated prebiotic reactions and a connected prebiotic route are different objects. The route is an ordered composition of settled reactions in which each product is the next input, measured at each handoff, under conditions enumerated from the environment, with a closing loop if continuation is claimed. The framework returns the shape any plausible route must have; it does not select a unique route, because different routes can arrive at the same substance. What is offered is that shape, per-slot and order tests, and falsifiers written in advance.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 notation and structure; checkable by reading Figures 12 and 3

- **Statement:** a vocabulary of ingredients cannot name a route. The ordered sequence and production bonds are required; the flat one-pot list is the symmetric conjunction and cannot express handoff order.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :111 **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."** Fig 12 :77 **"A produces B; B did not exist before"**. Fig 12 :72 **"A then B; B needs A to have happened"**. Fig 12 :92-93 **"Under a symmetric bond, k operands have k! spellings and one term"**.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a notation of isolated reactions that fully determines a connected route, including order and every handoff, with no ordered bond; or show that the product distribution is invariant under every permutation of the steps for the system at issue.

### C2 measurement and composition; checkable against a real reaction network

- **Statement:** isolated demonstrations are necessary atoms but not a sufficient route. Each handoff is its own residual, and a growing library of reactions is not a growing reach.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :104 **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"**. Fig 5 :72 **"a library that grows is not a reach that grows"**. Fig 1 :19 **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
- **What to check:** for a network you know, is any handoff currently inferred rather than measured? Does adding a settled reaction change the set of routes composable from the network, or only the size of the catalogue?
- **Refuter:** demonstrate a route that runs reproducibly while every intermediate at every handoff is unmeasured, and show that the total-yield figure still diagnoses a broken handoff; or show reach is identical to library size.

### C3 shape and closure; checkable by chemistry and by Figure 13

- **Statement:** two routes over the same feedstocks are isomers, so the framework licenses a route shape and not a unique pathway; a self-sustaining route needs a cycle, which a chain cannot supply.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :121 **"Different routes can arrive at the same substance."** Fig 12 :117 **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** Fig 12 :121 **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**
- **What to check:** name two routes to the same product in your field. Are the intermediates different (isomers under the figure's definition) or only differently labelled? If continuation is claimed, is a catalyst actually regenerated?
- **Refuter:** show a unique route derived from feedstocks alone with no alternative ordered composition reaching the same product; or show a linear self-sustaining route with no regenerated species.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a named pathway. Formose, RNA world, reverse TCA, Wood–Ljungdahl and formamide chemistry are names from the chemistry frame, entering as provenanced imports, not figure derivations.
- Not "more reactions eventually make a pathway." Fig 5 :72 says a library can grow while the reach does not.
- Not that a flask route is geologically plausible. That is the membrane crossing of sibling question 85, with a round-trip gap to measure (Fig 4 :30).
- Not that no route existed if none has been reconstructed. Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**
- Not `grounded`. No handoff was measured in this derivation.

## 5. What would settle it

A route-specific instrument, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (read 2026-11-23): an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved that case.

1. **Handoff test:** time-resolved measurement of each intermediate at each handoff under one habitat, showing it forms, reaches the next step in sufficient concentration and persists long enough.
2. **Order test:** run the same reactions in a different order under the same conditions. A changed product distribution makes the ordered bonds load-bearing; an unchanged distribution weakens the route claim.
3. **Closure test:** for a self-sustaining claim, measure whether the catalyst is regenerated and whether the loop sign closes.
4. **Discriminating instrument:** a measurement that selects one isomeric route over another. Without it, the product reading cannot name the source.
