## What in the wider literature bears on this question

1. **The disagreement is structural, not a tie.** Clement's survey reports both the Schumpeterian thesis (quoted through Tirole: *"monopolies are natural breeding grounds for R&D"*) and Arrow's result (*"The incentive to invent is less under monopolistic than under competitive conditions"*), then Gilbert and Newbery's preemption reversal, and closes: *"Theory on the relationship between monopoly and innovation remains unclear."* That is the world-layer version of Fig 9 :4: split the question rather than average the literatures.

2. **The sign flips with the slot.** Aghion et al. 2005 supply both the inverted-U and the mechanism: rivalry discourages laggards and encourages neck-and-neck firms. Chen and Schwartz 2009 supply a second flip for product innovations. Fig 11 :49-51 is the figure that governs this: competition is a habitat relation whose valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries. World sources corroborate the figure; they do not set its content.

3. **A threshold form appears in a low-overlap field.** The ecology intermediate disturbance hypothesis says diversity peaks at intermediate disturbance, and its own page records that the predicted peak is contested. It corroborates only the *shape* of a middle balance point (Fig 12 :87), not the industrial-organization number. Use it with the critique attached so the analogue is not mistaken for evidence about firms.

4. **The switchover-disruption correction is the same contact condition.** Holmes, Levine and Schmitz, reported in Clement's article, find that *"with 'enough' switchover disruption, increases in market power now lead to decreases in industry innovation."* In figure terms, disruption is the cost of changing a recording into a method; a protected incumbent has more to lose from a lost channel, which is Fig 10 :24 read at the firm scale.

5. **Framework essays are canonical, not witnesses.** The Library of Babel and Recursive Self-Improvement Trap essays share the framework author's evidence pool with the figures. Their agreement carries no independent corroboration weight. They are used for the canonical reading in `05_what_shape_means.md` and for the honest simulation-status label.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22-23** — *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full framework before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and its refuter; offer the lines only after.
- **Fig 10 :42** — *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** — *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* Ask what the recipient would expect to see in their own field before giving them the article.
- **Seat Map :163** — *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* A send selected for people likely to agree produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 10 :59** — *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* Declare the stake; do not ask for trust.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and its seven refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure in their own field; (3) record that verdict; (4) only then the three figure lines and the formula, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer and its limits

- **Stage 1 before stage 2.** Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"*, and Fig 2 :45, *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* The recipient checks the output without holding the framework's interior.
- **Stage 2 is opt in.** Seat 4 must not hold the interiors. Once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence, so the stage 1 verdict must be recorded before any theory is shown, and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
- **Stage 3 sends a method.** Fig 4 :22 permits a generator to cross. The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer. Each user still needs a ground per problem (Formula, "Direction"), so each must write a refuter before running it.
- **Hard precondition.** Stages 1 to 3 only work if stage 1 survives. If any of the seven refuters fires, the shape claim is withdrawn before it is sent.
