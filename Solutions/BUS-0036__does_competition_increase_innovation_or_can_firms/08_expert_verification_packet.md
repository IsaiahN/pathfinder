# Expert verification packet: BUS-0036, competition versus protection for innovation

**What this is:** a claim in a form an industrial organization or innovation economist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the sub-claims, `general` on the structural split, `gap` on the numerical balance point. Not `grounded`.** No market payment for a named product is claimed to have been measured here. ## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question is not one dial with two opposite directions. It is a union of two conditions with different anchors. Competition supplies contact with a differing closure and can renew a firm's research reach, at a debit against independence; temporary protection keeps a gate open so the market can settle a research project whose fixed cost needs time. Neither is the ground: the market and the count of granted patents are the anchors, and only the market pays. The sign of competition is relative to the residual, so the same rivalry encourages a neck-and-neck race and discourages a laggard catch-up. Patent counts and market payments are two currencies that do not add. The framework returns this shape, the instrument owed to name the source of a reading, and falsifiers written in advance, not a competition index or an optimal patent term.

### C2: economics-facing; checkable against the competition-innovation literature

- **Statement:** The sign of competition flips with the firm's position relative to the frontier, and it flips again between process and product innovation. The world literature corroborates the sign flip; it does not supply the framework's verdict.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :87, *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*. Fig 12 :117, *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* World corroboration: Aghion, Bloom, Blundell, Griffith and Howitt 2005, *"We find strong evidence of an inverted-U relationship using panel data. We develop a model where competition discourages laggard firms from innovating but encourages neck-and-neck firms to innovate."* Chen and Schwartz 2009, *"This paper shows that the ranking can be reversed for product innovations."*
- **Refuter:** a regime in which the sign of rivalry is the same at every distance from the frontier, measured per slot; or evidence that process and product innovation require different atoms rather than different arrangements of a shared set. The Chen and Schwartz reversal is **not** a refuter here: Fig 12 :117 predicts it for isomers. If it were read as a contradiction, the derivation would return to the census, not flip the verdict.

### C3: measurement-facing; checkable against what counts as innovation

- **Statement:** A patent grant is a counted legal event and a gate; the market payment is the ground. The two currencies do not add, and high profits cannot settle a research claim that the market has not paid for.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29, *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Fig 2 :38, *"instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."* Fig 12 :125, *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 12 :130, *"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."* `THE_FORMULA.md` step 5: *"SETTLE the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."*
- **Refuter:** a single measure that converts patent counts and market payments into one additive unit and predicts prospectively which research will pay; or a firm whose research keeps paying after the market has stopped paying for the product and the channel is demonstrably severed.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a general winner: neither "competition always increases innovation" nor "firms need monopoly to justify research."
- Not a competition index, an optimal patent term, an R&D tax-credit rate, or an industry percentage. The framework returns a shape, not a number (Fig 9 :32).
- Not that patents measure innovation. A patent is a counter, an instrument that can be wrong on axes it does not measure.
- Not a derivation of Schumpeter from Arrow or of Arrow from Schumpeter. The disagreement is a union of subsets (Fig 9 :4), not a chain.
- Not that protection does nothing. A gate can be necessary for a mint to be settled though it cannot itself settle.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment or market reading.
- Not a claim that this simulated derivation settles the economic question. It is checked for internal consistency against the figures only.

## 5. What would settle it

A per-slot market measurement, not a further argument:

1. Does the same competition event have opposite signs for a frontier race and a catch-up problem in the same industry?
2. Does research keep paying after the market stops paying for the product and the channel is severed?
3. At what competition level does the sign flip for a named technology? No figure returns this; an instrument is owed.
4. Can patent counts and market payments be converted into one additive unit that predicts prospectively?
5. Do the seven standing falsifiers in `01` section 4 survive an attempt to fire them?

The ecology analogue of a threshold (intermediate disturbance hypothesis) supports the form only, and its own page records substantial disagreement with the predicted peak. It is not a measurement of firms.
