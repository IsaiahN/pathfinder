**Subject:** Check one output about competition and innovation before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about industrial organization and innovation. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the business claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the number. Not `grounded`):** the question "does competition increase innovation, or do firms need temporary protection and high profits to justify research?" is not one question with two opposite answers. It is a union of two conditions with different anchors. Competition supplies contact with a firm's differing closure and can renew its reach, at a debit against the firm's independence. Protection keeps a gate open so the market can settle a research project whose fixed cost needs time. Neither is the ground: the market and the count of granted patents are the anchors, and only the market pays. The sign of competition is relative to the residual, so the same rivalry encourages a neck-and-neck race and discourages a laggard catch-up. That predicts a threshold form, not a single direction and not a universal number.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that competition always increases innovation, that monopoly is always better for R&D, that an inverted-U has a known peak, or that patent counts measure innovation. I am not claiming a `grounded` result. The framework returns a shape, not a name or a number.
>
> **The residual it answers:** the gap between what a firm's research library predicts the market will pay and what the market actually pays, indexed per firm and per technology, never averaged into an industry rate.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. one competition or protection measure that predicts innovation monotonically in the same direction across all technology slots, prospectively, against an independent ground;
> 2. a firm that renews its reach solely inside its own closure, with no atom from a differing closure, and still passes the market test across a habitat change;
> 3. a firm whose research keeps paying after the market has stopped paying for the product and the channel is demonstrably severed;
> 4. a patent grant shown to add an atom to the firm's reach rather than only settling a term;
> 5. a competition event whose measured valence is invariant across a frontier race and a catch-up problem in the same industry;
> 6. a single lever that restores both the paying-channel condition and the reach-renewal condition with no categorically different residue.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I will retract the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the figures are the authority, not my summary of them. I can send the lines that govern this derivation and the formula. Ask for them and I will send only those.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** run it on a research market you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [name]
