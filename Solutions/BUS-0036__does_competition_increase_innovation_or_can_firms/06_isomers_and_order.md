# Supporting derivation: bond order, isomers and analogues

**Mode: `specified` on the business application, `general` on the operator structure.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The audit's residual is the gap between what a firm's research library predicts the market will pay and what it actually pays, indexed per firm and per technology slot. The question's own wording hides three operator questions that must be answered before any policy lever is chosen:

1. Which bond does the question put between competition and innovation? "Increase" is ordered, while a competition-innovation correlation is symmetric.
2. Are process innovation and product innovation the same substance under a different arrangement, or different substances with different atoms?
3. Where does the threshold sit in the order of composition: at the first order inside a firm, or at the second order across two settled regimes?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held in the two frames: the firm's library Γ and its technicians; the research action a; the market ground o′; the patent grant as a counted legal event; the competition relation (named as a habitat relation in Fig 11 :47); the protection gate; the fixed R&D cost. Bonds held: the seven of Fig 12 plus negation. No new primitive is needed for this derivation. The anchors are the market payment and the patent count (Fig 2 :29). Order by dependency: settle the gate and the product before comparing regimes (Formula step 5, Fig 12 :104).

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Which bond is "increase"?

- Fig 12 :71 gives the sequence test: *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*; Operators :33 gives *"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."*
- Fig 12 :87 defines comparison as *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*; Operators :51 says *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one."*
- Operators :15 defines conjunction `+` with the test *"swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No."*
- The question's phrase "competition increases innovation" asserts `competition → innovation`, an ordered bond. The measured association is `competition + innovation`, a symmetric bond. The two are not the same term. A symmetric association does not carry the arrow, which is why F8 in `01` refuses it as a witness (Fig 2 :39-40).
- "High profits" above a competitive baseline is a subtraction, `-`: Operators :45 gives *"progress is a subtraction, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot say how far along it is"*, and Fig 12 :78-84 distinguishes *"A without B"* by the test *"is the second operand’s absence the point? Yes."*
- Refusing the claim that a patent count settles innovation is a negation `¬`, which is not a bond: Operators :58 says *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."*
- **Conclusion:** the question bundles at least four operators (`→`, `⋛`, `−`, `¬`) into one ordinary sentence. Fig 9 :4 (*"Split it rather than search"*) governs: this is not one relation with two answers.

### Q2: Are process and product innovation isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Process innovation and product innovation as the two regimes are written over the same atom set: `{firm Γ, research action, market ground, patent gate, competition relation, protection gate, fixed R&D cost}`. What differs is the arrangement: in the process case the gate sits between the firm and the *cost* of changing production; in the product case the gate sits between the firm and the *new product's* market entry.
- Chen and Schwartz 2009 report, from the economics frame, that *"This paper shows that the ranking can be reversed for product innovations"* against Arrow's process result. Under Fig 12 :117 that is not a conflict to be averaged; it is the expected behaviour of isomers: same ingredients, different bond, different substance. Under the weaker reading, the two are members of one **functional group** (research behind a gate) with different qualifiers.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if process innovation requires an atom that product innovation does not, or vice versa (for example a production-line atom in one and no market-entry atom in the other), the isomer claim fails and the two are different substances in the plain sense, not isomers. Then F6 of `01` must be re-censused as a partition, not a union of isomers.
- **Status:** triangulated for the operator structure (Fig 12 and the Operators table), single-source for the firm-level binding of the atom set. The world quote corroborates the reversal but shares the economics evidence pool with Aghion et al. and Clement, so it is one witness.

### Q3: What order is the comparison?

- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"**
- A patent is settled as a legal event by the counter (Fig 2 :29). A product is settled by the market. Each is a first-order molecule: firm → gate → market. Their comparison, *which regime produced more settled new terms*, is a second-order composition over two molecules that have become atoms.
- This is why the question cannot be answered at the level of a single product. The unit of comparison is a regime, and a regime is a settled molecule serving as an operand at the next level. Fig 12 :103-104 licenses that recursion; no new primitive is needed.
- **Type caveat, open:** `⋛` is defined as a threshold relation between two quantities (Fig 12 :87). At second order the operands are regimes now acting as atoms. Whether a regime counts as a quantity at that level needs an instrument, not an argument. Named, not hidden.

### Q4: The threshold form and the ecology analogue

- Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**
- Aghion et al. 2005 provide the economics-layer form: rivalry discourages laggards and encourages neck-and-neck firms, producing *"an inverted-U relationship."* The sign of the competition loop flips as the firm's distance to the frontier changes. That is Fig 12 :121 with the sign named from world data.
- The intermediate disturbance hypothesis provides a genuinely low-overlap analogue: *"local species diversity is maximized when ecological disturbance is neither too rare nor too frequent."* Its own source records that the predicted peak is contested in roughly 80 percent of over 100 reviewed studies. Both sources support the **form** of a threshold, neither returns an industrial number. Fig 4 :20 requires the anchor to be renamed at the new scale, so the ecology case corroborates the shape only.
- **Falsifier:** a regime in which the sign of rivalry is the same at every distance from the frontier, measured per slot, would break both the threshold form and the isomer reading. It would be observed as a monotone relationship with no flip.

### Q5: The two currencies

- Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**
- Fig 12 :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them"**
- Fig 12 :130: **"Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."**
- Patent counts price the description: what was claimed and where. Market payment prices the action: whether the ground paid. An "innovation index" that adds them makes the named error. Fig 12 :119 adds the catalyst distinction: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."** A patent is a catalyst if it regenerates across products and a spent part if it shelters one product and then decays; nothing in the patent's description records which.

## CONCLUSION

The question bundles four distinct operators. Once split, the defensible structure is: process and product innovation are isomers or at least members of one functional group over a shared atom set, with a world-observed sign reversal that Fig 12 :117 predicts; the comparison of protection and competition regimes is second-order over settled first-order molecules; the balance point has a `⋛` threshold form but no number here; and patents and market payments are two currencies that do not add. No new primitive, no wall-moving import and no universal policy lever are required. The falsifiers are the monotone-sign observation, the unequal-atom observation, and a successful one-currency conversion.
