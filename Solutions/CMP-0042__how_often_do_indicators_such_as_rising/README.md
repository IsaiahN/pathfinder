## What is being decided, in effect terms

The question asks for a rate: out of all the occasions on which rising variance or slower recovery appears as an early warning signal, how many are false alarms, that is, no transition follows. Two readings are possible and they lead to different work.

- **Reading A (the requested number).** A single frequency, say "early warning signals are false 40 percent of the time." This is a `grounded` claim about an ensemble. It needs an anchor: a labelled set of systems in which each alarm is scored against whether a transition actually occurred. The figures carry no frequency term, so on this reading the answer is `gap`. That is a verdict, not a silence.
- **Reading B (the figure-reachable residue).** The shape any such rate must have before it can be measured: reported per system and per indicator, tied to a decision threshold and a base rate of transitions, settled by the ground rather than by agreement among experts. This is `specified` and is the substantive contribution of this folder.

An answer would look like a curve, not a scalar: for each system class and each indicator, the hit rate plotted against the false-alarm rate as the decision criterion moves, with the base rate of transitions stated. We bind that shape as an ordinary term in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` and let it accrue a residual, so it can be refused by the same test as anything else (Formula step 7, "Direction").

## What is deliberately not here

- No invented false-positive percentage. The literature contains model-specific numbers (Boettiger and Hastings 2012 report 49, 81 and 93 percent false alarms for one indicator at a fixed detection rate in three different datasets). Those are not a universal answer and are recorded as evidence that the rate varies, not as the rate.
- No personal emails of named individuals, no receptiveness ranking, no bulk-send plan. Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Selecting recipients for likely agreement would manufacture exactly the correlation that makes their agreement worthless.
- No claim that the framework settles an empirical rate. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
