## 0. The residual in effect terms

A species arrives in a habitat where no local predator or competitor checks it. What is owed is not a name for the species and not a count of species. It is the **shape of the change in the network**: which relations are added, removed or re-wired, over what distance of contact, and by what the sign of the change would be settled.

- **Arity.** Not one pairwise interaction. The residual has the newcomer, the resident actor set, the conditions, and the relation set as co-operands. It is a compound, not a single hard question.
- **Symmetry.** Predation and competition are directed relations. They are not symmetric bonds. The newcomer's standing is not a property it carries: Fig 11 :49 *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
- **Scale.** First order is direct contact. Second order is the contact of those contacts. Third order is where the residual that matters can sit: Fig 11 :52 *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*

**Direction, bound before the run.** An answer would look like: arrival is an introduction; the newcomer's valence is unopposed; the habitat is enumerated outward until the cascade stops mattering; the network's relations re-wire; the ground settles the sign. This is the ordinary term against which the findings are ranked, per THE_FORMULA.md "Direction".

## 1. Domain census before any figure line

One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor. No nameable anchor means it is not yet a domain.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 ecological habitat** | newcomer, residents, conditions (light, water, season, terrain), relations (pollination, predation, competition, dependency) | survival and reproduction; who eats whom, who excludes whom | measured population and interaction data in the field |
| **D2 management / seat** | managers, engineers, control agents, the introducing institution | introduction versus exclusion; effort and reversibility | the same ground, which does not move when a manager pushes it |
| **D3 verification / outreach** | ecologists, reviewers, public readers | residual and agreement | the ground, never agreement; field data or a manipulative experiment |

**Partition or union.** This is a **union compound**, not a partition. The arrival condition and the recipient-community condition overlap: a case in both conditions is not a harder case of either. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* The unbundled loops are D1 (what contact actually does) and D2 (who has standing to add an actor). They share a ground but not a seat.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Enumerate the habitat (Fig 11 :43) → locate the newcomer's unopposed valence (Fig 11 :49) → trace contact outward to the cascade's stopping point (Fig 11 :52) → let the ground settle the sign (Fig 11 :73). Effort spent on the sign question before the first three links is effort above the break; the instrument for the sign is the last link, not the first.

**Strain test applied before concluding.** A line that only fits by forcing is governing another quantity and sends us back to the census. Two strains were found and are named rather than smoothed: (a) whether "no local predators or competitors" means literally no checking actor anywhere, and (b) whether the figures decide the sign of the complexity change. Both are handled in F2 and F7.

## 3. Findings, each with a verbatim line and a refuter

### F1 - The arrival is an introduction, not a subtraction (D2, `specified`)

Fig 11 :61: *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."*

Fig 11 :32: *"→ introduce, never subtract"* (quoted as the substring *"introduce, never subtract"*).

Fig 11 :65: *"Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994. Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated."*

The newcomer is added to a habitat it did not author. Even a manager cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world. The network changes by addition of an actor whose cascade was not enumerated.

**Refuter:** show a case where a species arrival removes an actor or relation from the habitat as a direct operation of the arrival, with no intervening change in contact, and with the ground agreeing. If that is possible, the asymmetry is wrong.

### F2 - "No predators or competitors" means unopposed valence, not absence of contact (D1, `specified`)

Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*

Fig 11 :50: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."*

Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*

An actor with no local checker is not outside the network. It is in contact, eating, competing, dispersing, and being eaten only by actors that do not check its increase. Its valence is unopposed relative to this residual. The enemy-release literature corroborates the shape but corrects the literal wording: the introduced range's enemies are *"less effective at constraining the introduced species' populations"*, not necessarily absent. That is a corroboration of Fig 11 :49, not a conflict, so the verdict does not flip. See §6 for the record.

**Refuter:** find a newcomer with literally zero contacts in the recipient habitat that nonetheless changes the network. By Fig 11 :43 it is not in the habitat, so the claim fails at the census.

### F3 - The change is a cascade; enumerate outward, not first order (D1, `specified`, triangulated with the world source on trophic cascades)

Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*

Fig 11 :44: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."*

Fig 11 :52: *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*

Fig 11 :48: *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."*

A first-order inventory of who eats whom misses the residual that matters. The network change is the cascade itself, read off the world and enumerated until it stops registering.

**Refuter:** exhibit an enemy-free arrival whose measurable network effect is confined to direct pairwise links and is zero at second and third order, with an instrument capable of seeing second-order effects. Then the cascade claim is false for that case.

### F4 - What changes is the relation set and the conditions, not just the actor count (D1, `general` structural + `specified` reading)

Fig 11 :45: *"actors: what acts on it, and what it acts on"*

Fig 11 :46: *"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"*

Fig 11 :47: *"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"*

Fig 13 :24: *"that entities relate, and influence travels"*

In the six-term substrate, a network is a **structure**: it decides that entities relate and influence travels. So a network change is a change in which relation holds and where influence travels, not a change in the node count alone. Fig 12 :117 (*"Same atoms, different bond, different substance"*) is worked out in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

**Refuter:** show a network change that is fully described by the actor multiset and leaves every relation and condition unchanged. Then the structure reading adds nothing and is withdrawn.

### F5 - Two modes decide who acts, and only one has a seat (D2, `specified`)

Fig 11 :5: *"survival of the fittest"*

Fig 11 :56: *"Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept."*

Fig 11 :36: *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."*

Fig 11 :58 (substring): *"That is the garden, and it is how arrival is worked on"*

Fig 11 :63: *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."*

A natural arrival is unmaintained: nothing chooses, selection ran, and the ground kept what survived. A deliberate introduction is maintained: a seat acts, and because exclusion is unavailable the work is permanent. Calling the newcomer "fittest" in the unmaintained mode is a label applied afterwards; it does no explanatory work.

**Refuter:** show a managed introduction for which exclusion of the habitat is available as an operation, or for which the work reaches a solved state without further maintenance. Either breaks the asymmetry or the permanence claim.

### F6 - The newcomer is an atom already in the habitat, not a missing primitive named by the framework (D1 and D3, `specified`)

Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."*

Fig 6 :25: *"An import adds no atom to the world."*

Fig 8 :53: *"Residual first, frame second."*

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*

Fig 11 :73: *"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."*

The newcomer is an atom of the habitat, enumerated from contact. Nothing in the framework names it or imports a new primitive. Describing the residual first is what lets the right discipline be chosen; choosing the discipline first would make any frame seem to fit (Fig 9 :27-28, *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*).

**Refuter:** show a case where explaining the arrival requires a new primitive not enumerable from contact, and that the framework itself, not the ecologist, must name it. Then the update-not-import reading fails.

### F7 - The figures decide the shape, not the sign of the complexity change (D1 and D3, `general` limit)

Fig 2 :29-30 (substring): *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*

Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

Fig 11 :73 (above): the habitat updates, the ground does not.

Whether connectance rises or falls, whether nestedness increases, whether the network simplifies or compartmentalises, is a measured sign. The ecology sources disagree about it (the Enemy release hypothesis page records *"Support for the theory, however, is not universal"*; the Ecological network page records that *"No consensus on the links between network nestedness and community stability"* has been reached). That disagreement is not a reason to average the figures into a sign. It is the reason to stop at the shape and record what would settle it.

**Refuter:** produce a figure line that returns a sign for connectance change under enemy release. None was found after the census. If one is supplied and verified, the honest stop in `07_the_tether_answer.md` is replaced by that sign.

### F8 - Isolation is substitution, so moving the residual is not removing the habitat (D2, `specified`)

Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."*

Fig 11 :67: *"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."*

Fig 11 :68: *"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"*

Fig 11 :69: *"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"*

A control program that moves the problem to a test system or a different site has substituted a habitat. It inherits actors it did not choose, and two failure modes stay invisible until they act.

**Refuter:** show a substitution in which the new habitat's actors are all chosen and everything the goal required is reproduced and verified. Then the two silent failure modes are not universal.

### F9 - What actually survives, with modes kept separate

| claim | mode | figure |
|---|---|---|
| An arrival is an addition to a habitat; subtraction of the habitat is not available | `general` structural | Fig 11 :61, :32 |
| Valence is relative to the residual; an unopposed actor is still in contact | `specified` | Fig 11 :49-51 |
| The habitat must be enumerated outward until the cascade stops mattering | `specified` | Fig 11 :43, :44, :52 |
| The change is in relations and conditions, not only actor counts | `general` + `specified` | Fig 11 :45-47; Fig 13 :24 |
| Unmaintained arrivals are selection, not choice; managed arrivals are permanent work | `specified` | Fig 11 :36, :56, :63 |
| The sign of the change is not given by the figures | `general` limit | Fig 9 :60; Fig 11 :73 |

**Triangulation status, per domain.** D1 (habitat): the enumerate-and-cascade verdict is triangulated across Fig 11 (habitat mechanics) and the external trophic-cascade and ecosystem-engineer sources, which are low overlap with the framework. D2 (management): the asymmetry verdict is **single-source** within the figures (Fig 11 :61) and is corroborated only weakly by the ecosystem-engineer page, which shares the Jones, Lawton and Shachak citation the figure itself uses; that is one evidence pool, not two witnesses. D3 (verification): the stop-at-shape verdict is triangulated across Fig 2, Fig 9 :60 and the canonical figures README, with high author overlap. No verdict is averaged across domains.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Required before any outreach. *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device"* (THE SEAT MAP principle, as carried into the master plan; quoted here from the plan's own wording, not as a figure line).

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A first-order inventory fully predicts the measured network change, with a instrument capable of detecting second and third order effects.
2. An actor with literally zero contact in the recipient habitat changes the network.
3. The sign of the connectance change is returned by a verbatim figure line that was not forced (then the honest stop is wrong).
4. A managed introduction reaches a solved state with no further maintenance, or can subtract the habitat as an operation.
5. The relation set and conditions are unchanged while the network measurably changes.

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the answer is rebuilt from the census. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

| earlier reading | status after the check |
|---|---|
| F2 first drafted as "the newcomer has no valence and is outside the habitat" | **Retracted.** Re-census on Fig 11 :49: valence is *relative*, and absence of a checker is not absence of contact. The newcomer is enumerated in D1. |
| "Enemy release means no enemies in the introduced range" | **Narrowed, not retracted.** The world source says introduced-range enemies are less effective, not necessarily absent. A conflict would send us back to the census; here it corroborates Fig 11 :49. |
| Fig 11 :5 "survival of the fittest" read as covering managed introductions | **Narrowed.** It governs the unmaintained mode only (Fig 11 :36, :56). Managed introduction is a seat's act (Fig 11 :58). |
| Temptation to answer "the network becomes less complex" | **Refused as a verdict.** Figures give shape, not sign (Fig 9 :60). The ecology literature itself is unsettled. |
| "shape, not a name" applied to the newcomer species | **Not applicable.** That rule governs a missing primitive. The newcomer is an already-enumerated actor, so naming it is the ecologist's binding, not a framework output. |
| Apparent tension between Fig 11 :32 "introduce, never subtract" and real control programs that remove invaders | **Not strained.** Re-census: removal of an introduced actor by a seat is not subtraction of the habitat; the seat still cannot subtract contact. Different objects. |
| Strains after re-census | **Two named, none hidden.** Sign of the complexity change (F7), and the literal-versus-relative reading of "no predators" (F2). |

**Updated verdict.** The shape survives intact: an enemy-free arrival is an addition of an unopposed actor into a habitat that must be enumerated outward, with the network changing in its relations and conditions and the ground settling the sign. The literal "no enemies" reading, the "outside the habitat" reading, and any signed complexity claim do not survive. Nothing here is settled against the ground.
