# Expert verification packet: the Tether on enemy-free arrivals

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ecologist or network scientist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural limit. Not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

When a newly arrived species meets no local predator or competitor, the ecological network changes as an **addition to an enumerated habitat**, not as a subtraction of enemies and not as a change in a species count. The newcomer's valence is unopposed but its contact is real, so "no enemies" means no effective local check. The habitat must be enumerated outward from the residual until the cascade stops mattering, because the effect that counts can be third order. The change itself is in the relation set and the conditions: which predation, competition, pollination and dependency links hold, under what light, water, season and terrain. Over a fixed actor set this is a rewiring, a different substance invisible in a species list. In an unmaintained arrival selection ran; in a managed introduction a seat acts but exclusion is unavailable, so the work is permanent. The figures return this shape and deliberately withhold the sign of the complexity change.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - Ecology-facing; checkable against invasion and cascade data

- **Statement:** a first-order inventory of direct interactions is insufficient; the measurable effect can sit two or three steps out, and "no enemies" means no effective local check.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :52 - *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* Also Fig 11 :49 - *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
- **What to check:** in a case you know, did a first-order interaction list predict the outcome, or was a second or third order term required? Was the newcomer unchecked because no enemy existed, or because the resident enemies were ineffective?
- **Refuter:** a first-order inventory that fully predicts the measured change with an instrument capable of detecting second and third order effects; or an actor with literally zero contact that changes the network.

### C2 - Network-facing; checkable against the before/after interaction matrices

- **Statement:** with the actor set held as far as possible constant, the network changes by re-wiring; a species list cannot record the change. The cascade is ordered rather than symmetric, and may be a cycle whose sign is a measurement question.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :70-72 defines sequence as *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"* with symmetry "no". Fig 12 :121 - *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*
- **What to check:** do the measured interaction links and conditions change while the node set is stable? Are the effects ordered, or simultaneous co-occurrences? Is there a re-entrant loop?
- **Refuter:** a network change fully described by the actor multiset with every relation and condition unchanged; or measured effects with no ordered dependence (which would make the sequence reading wrong and require a symmetric conjunction instead); or no re-entrant loop in any measured case (which would make the cycle analogue decorative).

### C3 - Framework-internal; checkable by reading the figure text

- **Statement:** an arrival is an addition to a habitat and the habitat cannot be subtracted; isolation is substitution; the sign is withheld by the figures and is owed to the ground.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :61 - *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."* Fig 11 :66 - *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* Fig 9 :60 - *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
- **What to check:** is the asymmetry stated as a framework rule rather than an empirical generalisation? Does it survive a case where a control program removes an introduced actor? (It should: removing an actor is not subtracting the habitat.)
- **Refuter:** a managed introduction that reaches a solved state with no further maintenance, or that subtracts the habitat as an operation; or a verbatim figure line that returns a sign for connectance change under enemy release without being forced.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a sign: no claim that the network becomes simpler, more connected, less nested or more stable.
- Not that enemy release is universal, and not that the newcomer is "fittest".
- Not that the newcomer has zero contact or sits outside the habitat.
- Not that a control program can subtract the habitat; isolation substitutes one habitat for another.
- Not a derivation of ecology from the framework. The world sources corroborate shape only.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by measurement.

## 5. What would settle it

A field instrument, not a further argument. From *How Intelligence Actually Works*, *"indiscernibility is not identity"*: an output no instrument distinguishes from the measured network is still an output. The settling questions:

1. At what order does the measured cascade stop registering? Does a first-order inventory predict the outcome?
2. Does the newcomer have an effective local check, and is the check a relation that constrains its increase?
3. With nodes held constant, do the interaction links and conditions change, and is the change ordered or re-entrant?
4. Does a managed program reach a state needing no further maintenance, and does a moved residual inherit unchosen actors?
