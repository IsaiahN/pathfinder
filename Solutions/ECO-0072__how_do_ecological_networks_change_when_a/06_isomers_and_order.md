# Supporting derivation: structure, order and analogues for a network change

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

F1 to F4 of the audit establish that the arrival is an introduction, that the newcomer's valence is unopposed, that the change travels outward, and that what changes is the relation set. Two further questions are owed. (1) Can the network change while the actor list stays the same? (2) What order, if any, does a cascade have, and can it close on itself?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held for this derivation: the newcomer N, residents R1..Rk, conditions C, and the relation types predation, competition, pollination, dependency (Fig 11 :45-47). Bonds held: the seven typed relations of Fig 12, including the ordered sequence `→`, production `⇒`, subtraction `−`, comparison `⋛`, and the symmetric conjunction `+`. Network is a structure in the six-term set (Fig 13 :23-24). No new primitive is introduced.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Can the nodes stay the same while the network changes? Yes, by Fig 12's isomer rule.

Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*

Fig 12 :95-96: *"Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."*

Apply this to the invader case before and after arrival with the actor list held fixed:

- Before: `{N, R1, R2, C}` with N linked to nobody by predation or competition, and C unchanged.
- After: `{N, R1, R2, C}` with N competing with R1 and pollinating R2, while R1's former link to R2 is displaced.

Same atoms, different bonds, different substance. A species inventory that records only the node set cannot tell these two networks apart. This is the structural reason F4 says the change is in relations and conditions, not just actor counts.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a measured network change is fully captured by the actor multiset with every relation and condition identical, the isomer reading fails and F4 is withdrawn.

### Q2 - What order does the cascade have?

Fig 12 :70-72 defines `→` (sequence) as *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*, with symmetry *"no"*. Predation and competitive displacement are ordered: the newcomer's arrival precedes the resident's decline, which precedes the third-order effect. So a cascade is a chain of **ordered** bonds, not a symmetric join. Fig 11 :52 gives the empirical stopping rule: *"first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*

Two cautions, both from the figure text, not inventions.

1. Fig 12 :48: *"A chain is the degenerate case: one bond type, no branching."* A cascade that branches into pollination and predation at the same time is not a chain; it is a molecule with more than one bond type. Writing it as `N → R1 → R2` is a compression, and the round-trip gap `R_T` in `11_audit.md` measures what that compression drops.
2. Fig 12 :111-112: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one. A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* A before/after species list names a state. It cannot name the route from one to the other. The cascade claim is a route claim, so it needs the ordered bonds.

**Falsifier:** if the measured effects are simultaneous with no ordered dependence, the sequence reading is wrong and the cascade must be rewritten as a symmetric co-occurrence (Fig 12 :55-57, `+` conjunction: *"both hold at once"*).

### Q3 - Can the cascade close on itself? Yes in principle; sign decides.

Fig 12 :121: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."*

An enemy-free arrival can produce a feedback: the newcomer reduces a resident, the resident's decline changes a condition, and the changed condition favours the newcomer further. That is a cycle, not a chain. Whether it closes stably or runs away is a question about the sign of the loop, which is a measurement question. The figures supply the form (a cycle can close, a chain cannot) and withhold the sign, exactly as F7 withholds the sign of the complexity change.

**Falsifier:** show that no measured invader case has a re-entrant loop, or that every apparent cycle is an open chain truncated by the observation window. Then the cycle analogue is decorative and is withdrawn.

### Q4 - Which structural analogue actually governs: isomer, catalyst or functional group?

- **Isomer** (Q1) governs the network-state comparison: same nodes, different bonds.
- **Catalyst**, Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."* An ecosystem engineer that modifies conditions and persists (beaver, kudzu, zebra mussel in the world sources) is a catalyst in this sense. This governs why a single arrival can have effects that outlast the actor's own population pulse.
- **Functional group**, Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* This governs the comparison across cases: different species with the same relation motif (arrival, release, cascade) belong to the same functional group even though their atoms differ.

These are three different quantities. Averaging them into one "structure" claim would be a category error. Isomer compares states over one atom set; catalyst tracks persistence across uses; functional group classifies motifs across different atom sets. The verdicts are kept separate.

## CONCLUSION

The network change survives as a **bond change**, not an actor-count change: Fig 12 :117 makes a fixed-node rewiring a different substance, and Fig 13 :24 makes network a structure. The cascade is an **ordered** route (Fig 12 :70-72), not a symmetric co-occurrence, with a stopping rule given by contact enumeration (Fig 11 :44, :52). It may be a **cycle** whose sign the figures withhold (Fig 12 :121). Three Figure 12 structures, isomer, catalyst and functional group, govern three distinct comparisons and are not merged. Every claim here is `specified` and unmeasured; the falsifiers are written above rather than appended after the result.
