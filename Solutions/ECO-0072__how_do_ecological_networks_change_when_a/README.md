## Restatement as an effect

The question decides between three candidate pictures of the same event. (1) The network loses enemies, so the change is a subtraction of checks on the newcomer. (2) The network gains an actor, so the change is an addition to an actor list. (3) The network's relations re-wire and the effect travels outward through contact, so the change is a cascade whose sign the ground must settle. Option 1 leads to a static inventory with a missing entry. Option 2 leads to a count. Option 3 leads to an enumerable habitat, a checkable cascade, and a falsifier. Figures 11, 9, 6 and 2 decide in favour of option 3 as a shape while refusing to decide the sign of the change.

**Direction, written down and bound before the run.** An answer would look like this: the arrival is an introduction into a habitat; the newcomer's valence is unopposed because no local actor checks it; the habitat is enumerated by contact outward until the cascade stops mattering; the change is in relations (predation, competition, pollination, dependency) and conditions, not only in actor counts; the ground settles whether connectance rises or falls. This paragraph is the ordinary term the loop ranks against, per THE_FORMULA.md "Direction".

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words.

1. A room is not an anchor. Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
2. Selecting recipients for likely agreement manufactures the correlation that disqualifies their agreement as a verdict. Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*
3. Sending the theory before asking for a verdict spends the reader's independence. Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*

Legally, public opt-in and institutional submission routes avoid the GDPR and CAN-SPAM failures that attach to unsolicited bulk mail.
