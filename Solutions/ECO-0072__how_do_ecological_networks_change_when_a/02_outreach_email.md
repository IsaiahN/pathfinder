# Outreach draft: check one shape claim before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §6 has been read. The shape claim must survive the refuter below or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about enemy-free arrivals, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a figure-grounded derivation of how an ecological network changes when a newly arrived species meets no local predator or competitor. I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the change is not a subtraction of enemies and not merely an addition to a species list. The arrival is an *introduction* into a habitat. The newcomer has unopposed valence: it is still in contact, eating, competing and dispersing, but no local actor checks its increase. The habitat has to be enumerated by contact outward until the cascade stops mattering, because the effect that counts can be three steps out. What changes in the network is the relation set and the conditions: who eats whom, who competes with whom, who pollinates whom, and under what light, water, season and terrain. Whether connectance rises or falls is a measured sign, and the figures do not return it.
>
> Three consequences follow, and each is checkable on its own:
> 1. A first-order inventory of direct interactions is not enough. The residual may sit at second or third order.
> 2. "No predators or competitors" means no *effective local check*, not zero contact. An actor with no contact at all would not be in the habitat.
> 3. In an unmaintained arrival, nobody chose the newcomer; selection ran. In a managed introduction, a seat acts, but exclusion of the habitat is not available, so the work is permanent.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the network always becomes simpler or always more connected, that enemy release is universal, that the newcomer is "fittest" in any explanatory sense, or that any of this has been settled by measurement. The ecology literature itself is unsettled on the sign. I am offering a shape, not a verdict.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written down before this message:**
> 1. a first-order inventory that fully predicts the measured change, with an instrument capable of seeing second and third order effects;
> 2. an actor with literally zero contact in the recipient habitat that nonetheless changes the network;
> 3. a verbatim figure line that returns a sign for the connectance change under enemy release, without being forced;
> 4. a managed introduction that reaches a solved state with no further maintenance, or that subtracts the habitat as an operation.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`, Fig 11 in particular.
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`.
> - Public figures repository: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - [name]

## Stage order

1. **Stage 1.** Send the shape and its refuter. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record the verdict before any theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, send the source links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40, *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*).
3. **Stage 3.** Offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.
