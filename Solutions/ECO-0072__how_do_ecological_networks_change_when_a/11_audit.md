## 4. Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 11 :43-44, :49, :52, :61, :66 and Fig 12 :117, :121.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07_the_tether_answer.md`: arrival is an addition, valence is unopposed, enumerate outward, relations re-wire, ground settles sign.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete field claim about a particular invader, a particular network and a measured connectance value.

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down.

- The **ordering of bonds** in the cascade. The shorthand says "outward"; the concrete case may be a branching molecule with several bond types, not the degenerate chain `N → R1 → R2`. Fig 12 :48 notes that a chain is the degenerate case.
- The **identity of the effective check**. "No effective local check" is a relation relative to the residual. It does not tell the field worker which interaction to measure; two ecologists can populate it differently, which is the valence-relative problem one level down.
- The **sign**. This is the largest dropped constraint, and it is dropped deliberately. The shorthand withholds it; the concrete claim must supply it from measurement or remain `candidate`.

One thing survives cleanly: a structure must sit in the slot where a species list records only nodes, and the habitat must be enumerated outward until the cascade stops mattering. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the formula as identical to the figures would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
