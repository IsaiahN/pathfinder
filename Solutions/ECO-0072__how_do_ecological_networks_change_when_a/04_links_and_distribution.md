## What in the wider literature bears on the residual

1. **Enemy release supports the shape and corrects the wording.** The newcomer leaves co-evolved checks behind, but the introduced range's enemies are described as less effective rather than absent. This is why the solution reads *"no local predators or competitors"* as unopposed valence, not zero contact. A literal reading would put the newcomer outside the habitat and break Fig 11 :43.

2. **Trophic cascades supply the second-order instrument.** The cascade page gives the sea otter, urchin and kelp chain and the dingo, fox and hopping-mouse chain, both cases where a change at one link travels two or more steps. This is the world-facing version of Fig 11 :52. It predicts that a first-order inventory will miss the residual that matters, and it corroborates the figure.

3. **Ecosystem engineering supplies the managed-mode analogue.** The encyclopedia page and Jones, Lawton and Shachak 1994 are the same citation Fig 11 :65 uses. Agreement here is **one evidence pool, not two witnesses**: the figure already names the source. It is recorded as corroboration of the shape, with overlap declared.

4. **Network-structure sources refuse the sign.** The ecological-network page reports an unresolved complexity-stability debate and no consensus on nestedness and stability. The enemy-release page reports mixed empirical support. So the world sources, like the figures, do not supply a sign for connectance change. The honest stop in `07_the_tether_answer.md` is not a gap in the search effort; it is the limit of what the available instruments distinguish in this simulated derivation.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1 before stage 2** is Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* The recipient checks the shape without holding the framework's interior.
2. **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. Their verdict on stage 1 must be recorded before any theory is shown.
