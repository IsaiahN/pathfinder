# The Tether's answer: ecological networks after an enemy-free arrival

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the structural claim. Not `grounded`.** No field measurement, experiment or dataset was analysed in this derivation. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## 1. The residual it answers

A species arrives where no local predator or competitor checks it. The residual is the **shape of the network change**, not a name for the species and not a count. It is a union compound (Fig 9 :54, *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again"*): the arrival condition and the recipient-community condition overlap, so the answer has two loops, one about contact and one about who may act. Their verdicts are kept separate.

## 2. The shape

1. **The arrival is an addition, not a subtraction.** Fig 11 :61: *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."* Even a manager cannot subtract the habitat. (D2, `specified`.)

2. **The newcomer has unopposed valence, not zero contact.** Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* An actor with no contact at all would not be in the habitat. "No predators or competitors" is read as no effective local check. The ecology sources agree that introduced-range enemies are less effective rather than necessarily absent. (D1, `specified`.)

3. **The habitat is enumerated outward until the cascade stops mattering.** Fig 11 :44: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."* Fig 11 :52 gives the reason: a first-order inventory misses the effect that was three steps out. A trophic-cascade source corroborates the second- and third-order structure. (D1, `specified`, triangulated.)

4. **What changes is the relation set and the conditions.** Fig 11 :47: *"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"*; Fig 13 :24: *"that entities relate, and influence travels"*. Over a fixed actor set this is Fig 12 :117, *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance"*: a rewiring is a different substance, invisible in a species list. Worked out in `06_isomers_and_order.md`. (D1, `general` + `specified`.)

5. **Two modes, one seat.** Fig 11 :56: *"Unmaintained. No maintainer and no live frame in the seat. The habitat is pure contact, nothing selects, and what survives is what the ground kept."* In the unmaintained mode selection ran and "fittest" is a label applied afterwards (Fig 11 :36). In the maintained mode a seat acts, but because exclusion is unavailable the work is permanent: Fig 11 :63, *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."* (D2, `specified`.)

6. **The sign is withheld.** Whether connectance, nestedness or complexity rise or fall is a measured sign. Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* The figures return the shape; the ground returns the sign. (D1 and D3, `general` limit.)

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that the network always simplifies, always becomes more connected, or always loses diversity. No sign is asserted.
- Not that enemy release is universal. The world source records that support is *"not universal"*; that disagreement is recorded, not averaged away.
- Not that the newcomer is "fittest". In the unmaintained mode that is a retrospective label with no work to do (Fig 11 :36).
- Not that the newcomer sits outside the habitat, and not that it has literally zero contacts.
- Not that a control program can subtract the habitat. Isolation is substitution: Fig 11 :66, *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."*
- Not derived from the ecology literature, and not grounded. The world sources corroborate shape only; they share no stance with the ground.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A field measurement or a manipulative experiment, not a further argument. From *How Intelligence Actually Works*, *"indiscernibility is not identity"*: a description that no instrument distinguishes from the outcome is still a description. The questions an instrument would answer:

1. **Cascade depth.** At what order does the measured effect stop registering? Does a first-order inventory predict the outcome, or is a second or third order term required? This fires directly on claims 3 and on refuter 1.
2. **Contact census.** Does the newcomer have any effective local check? A check is a relation that constrains its increase, not merely an encounter. This fires on claim 2.
3. **Bond change with fixed nodes.** With the actor set held as far as possible constant, do the measured interaction links and conditions change? This fires on claim 4 and on the isomer reading in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.
4. **Maintenance and substitution.** Does a managed program reach a state requiring no further maintenance, and does a moved residual inherit actors it did not choose? This fires on claims 1 and 5 and on F8.

Standing falsifiers from `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4: a first-order inventory with a capable instrument that fully predicts the change; an actor with zero contact that changes the network; a verbatim figure line returning a sign; a solved managed introduction; a network change with an unchanged relation set.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The enumeration rule is *"outward until the cascade stops mattering"*, and that stopping point is empirical. The canonical rule says the framework returns *"a shape, not a name"* and stops where instruments stop, recording the lineage so the stop is checkable. Here the stop is at the sign: the figures give the cascade and the rewiring, and the available ecology sources are themselves unsettled about complexity and stability. Saying so is a verdict about the instrument, not a silence in the derivation. Fig 8 :39, *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence"*, is the standing caution against reading this stop as proof that no sign exists.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not say whether an invasion makes a food web simpler or more connected, and it does not name the invader. It says that an enemy-free arrival is an addition to a habitat, not a subtraction from one; that the newcomer's valence is unopposed but its contact is real, so "no predators or competitors" means no effective local check and not zero interaction; that the habitat must be enumerated outward because the effect that matters can be three steps from the arrival; and that what changes in the network is the relation set and the conditions, a rewiring that a species list cannot see. In an unmaintained arrival nobody chose the newcomer and selection ran; in a managed introduction a seat acts, but exclusion is unavailable, so the work is permanent. The figures return this shape and withhold the sign of the change. What they offer is the form any answer must have, four refuters written in advance, and the field measurements that would close it. The sign stays open until the ground settles it.
