## Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual line before reading the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Arity: three or more, strategies in the plural, a shock, and a response. Symmetry: competition is a relation between strategies, formally symmetric but not symmetric in effect, because whether a competitor helps or harms depends on what the ground returns after the shock. Scale: the strategy is individual-scale, the portfolio is population-scale (Fig 4), and the shock is habitat-scale. The response is therefore cross-scale and cannot be a property of one strategy.
- **Domain 1, the strategy population.** Actors: strategies, the shared library, the ranking. Currency: payoff and replication. Anchor: the ground via survival. Question type: partition, which variants remain in play.
- **Domain 2, the shock.** Actors: conditions, competitors, dependencies. Currency: contact. Anchor: the ground, which does not move when a frame thinks otherwise. Question type: the trigger, not a new frame to negotiate with.
- **Domain 3, the response.** Actors: post-shock population, selector, object slots. Currency: per-slot residual R. Anchor: the ground votes. Question type: read per slot.

Order by dependency: enumerate the habitat before ranking (Fig 11 :43), establish variation before reading selection (Fig 7 :33), read the per-slot residual after selection (Fig 1 :19). Refuse before mint (Formula step 2).

My census matches the primary's three domains and its partition-with-dependency reading. ## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 7 :33, :35, :32 and :26, Fig 11 :47, :49 and :39, Fig 6 :53, Fig 1 :19 and :22.
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07` and `09`: plurality supplies variation; shock triggers selection between variants; independence corrects the count; read per slot; two factors; no coefficient.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the concrete answer a reader might apply, "competition increases resilience, so hold more strategies".

**R_T is nonzero.** Three constraints in the shorthand do not survive the trip back down:

- independence as the parameter, which the concrete version drops in favour of a count;
- per-slot reading, which the concrete version replaces with a portfolio score;
- the two-factor condition, which the concrete version ignores when either fidelity or pressure is zero.

A fourth item is **not** a loss but a gap: no coefficient exists in the figure set to send up in the first place. Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: plurality supplies alternatives for selection, and the ground's vote happens between them. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the condensed chain as identical to the figure set would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
