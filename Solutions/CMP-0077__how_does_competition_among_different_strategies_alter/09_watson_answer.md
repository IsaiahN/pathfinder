## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what happens to a system's response to a shock when several strategies compete, compared with a system holding one strategy. Option A: competition only reallocates shares, so the response is the same and diversity is decoration. Option B: competition keeps variants in play, so the shock becomes an event at which the ground selects between alternatives rather than displacing one locked-in trajectory. What each leads to: A reduces the question to a single-strategy optimization and predicts no dependence on strategy structure; B predicts a dependence on variation, independence between strategies, and per-slot measurement, and supplies a refuter that A does not.

- **R (the residual).** The unexplained difference in post-shock response attributable to competition among strategies. Arity: at least three, strategies, a shock, a response. Symmetry: competition is a relation, formally symmetric between strategies but not in effect, because effect is relative to the post-shock residual. Scale: individual strategy, population portfolio, habitat under shock; the response is a cross-scale quantity. - **Domain 2, the shock.** Actors: conditions, competitors, dependencies. Currency: contact and what the ground returns now. Anchor: the ground, which does not change its answer because a frame thinks otherwise. Question type: the trigger that makes selection fire, not a new frame to negotiate with.
- **Domain 3, the response.** Actors: the post-shock population, the selector, the object slots. Currency: the per-slot residual R. Anchor: the ground votes. Question type: read per slot, never as a portfolio average.

- **Evolutionary game theory / Wikipedia contributors, "Evolutionary game theory" (Wikipedia, accessed 2026-11-22):** "The success of a strategy is determined by how good the strategy is in the presence of competing strategies (including itself), and of the frequency with which those strategies are used." Predicts relational, frequency-dependent success. Corroborates: competition is a relation and valence is not intrinsic. Overlap: none with the framework author.
- **Mathematics of evolution / Wikipedia contributors, "Replicator equation" (Wikipedia, accessed 2026-11-22):** "once a strategy becomes extinct there is no way to revive it" and "It assumes all strategies are present from the outset and models only the relative growth or decline of their proportions over time." Predicts that competition driving a variant to zero removes the raw material for a later shock response. Corroborates: variation is an upstream prerequisite. Overlap: none.
- **Evolutionary game theory / Wikipedia contributors, "Evolutionarily stable strategy" (Wikipedia, accessed 2026-11-22):** "A particular contest might stabilize into any one of these possibilities, but later a major perturbation in conditions can move the solution into one of the alternative ESS states." Predicts that a perturbation selects among pre-existing alternatives. Corroborates: selection between variants. Overlap: none.
- **Philosophy of biology / Alexander, J. M., "Evolutionary Game Theory", Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy (first published 2002, substantive revision 2021, accessed 2026-11-22):** "if a strategy is not present in the population at all, it has no chance of being adopted by imitation." Predicts that absent strategies cannot be recruited after a shock under imitative dynamics. Corroborates: reach is constrained by what is present. Overlap: none.
- **Ecology / Wikipedia contributors, "Ecological resilience" (Wikipedia, accessed 2026-11-22), quoting Holling 1973 and Walker et al. 2004:** "the capacity of a system to absorb disturbance and reorganize while undergoing change so as to still retain essentially the same function, structure, identity, and feedbacks"; and "this very act of specialisation weakens resilience by permitting systems to become accustomed to and dependent upon their prevailing conditions." ## VERDICT (from the figures)

Competition among different strategies alters the response to shocks by changing its kind, not by supplying a scalar improvement. It is a relation whose sign depends on the post-shock residual, so it has no fixed valence in advance. Plurality provides variation inside the population; a shock is the event at which the ground selects between variants. Without distinguishable variants the system gets motion, not adaptation. Distinguishability requires independence, so ρ corrects the strategy count. The response is read per slot, progress needs both bracket fidelity and ground pressure, and capability belongs to the portfolio plus habitat rather than to the winning strategy alone. This is a `specified`, ungrounded result. The figures give a shape and a measurement rule; they do not give a resilience coefficient, and that gap is reported rather than filled.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states three sub-claims with refuters before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending an outside reader's independence by sending a recording instead of a generator, or by selecting readers for likely agreement. Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."
- **undoable:** yes. The cross-level analogy (strategies as rooms) and the strained exclusion clause are removable without touching the core finding, and both are already declared in `01` and `06`. A wrong coefficient is not installed, because no coefficient was asserted. A sent packet can be superseded by a corrected one.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A controlled shock against a monomorphic control, with ρ measured between strategies and per-slot residuals reported, would test C1 to C3. An instrument that distinguishes selection between variants from one pre-adapted winner would settle the source question. Raw count beating ρ-corrected count, an agent-only improvement moving the ground, or progress with both factors nonzero and no residual fall would each break the corresponding claim. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
