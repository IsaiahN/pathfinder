# The Tether's answer: competition among strategies and the response to shocks

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A system holds several competing strategies. A shock changes the conditions it faces. The question is what competition changes about the response. Option A says competition only reallocates shares, so a single-strategy system would respond the same. Option B says competition holds variants in play so the ground can select between them when conditions change. The figures decide for a **shape of answer**: competition changes the *kind* of response available, from displacement of one trajectory to selection among variants. They do not return a coefficient.

## 2. The shape

1. **Competition is a relation, not a strategy property.** Fig 11 :47 "relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency". Its effect is relative to the residual after the shock: Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." So no fixed sign ("competition helps" or "competition hurts") can be attached in advance. The sign is a measurement, not a definition.

2. **Plurality is the variation; the shock is when the ground selects.** Fig 7 :33 "variation inside, selection between". Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Competition's role is to keep distinguishable alternatives present and ranked. Fig 4 :5 "many agents, a shared library, and something that ranks what they produce" names the population scale at which this happens.

3. **Without distinguishable variants, a shock gives motion, not adaptation.** Fig 7 :32 "one long room: motion, no progress". Fig 7 :36 "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced." This is the figure-grounded reason "different strategies" matters, as opposed to many copies of one strategy.

4. **Independence is the parameter; the count is not.** Fig 6 :53 "searchers drawing on the same evidence are one searcher wearing many names". Fig 2 :31 "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." Fig 10 :80 "And divergence regenerates unevenly. Frames whose independence is renewed by separate experience can be maintained; frames whose correlation is fixed at origin can only be rotated, because no amount of use makes them less alike and replacement is the only refresh." Strategies that share one evidence pool fail together under the shock. ρ corrects the count.

5. **The response must be read per slot.** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." The observable is a vector of per-strategy residuals after the shock, not a portfolio mean.

6. **Capability is portfolio plus habitat.** Fig 11 :39 "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone". Fig 11 :40 "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." After the shock, "selection ran" is the honest description (Fig 11 :36).

7. **Progress has two factors.** Fig 7 :37 "Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:" Fig 7 :38 "the bracket's fidelity across the handoff" and Fig 7 :39 "the ground's pressure on the room". Fig 7 :26 "progress per handoff: R = h² × S". Either factor at zero, and adding strategies cannot help.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that competition is always good. Valence is relative to the residual (Fig 11 :49-51).
- Not that more strategies is always better. An uncorrected count is a label count (Fig 6 :53).
- Not a resilience number, an optimal diversity level, or an importable ecological coefficient. Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself".
- Not that the strategy portfolio is an ecological niche. That mapping is declined in the canonical article; the habitat here is contact with a residual, which moves when the question changes.
- Not that an agent-only fix works (Fig 11 :40), and not that a designer can subtract the habitat (Fig 11 :61); the latter clause is strained for a designed strategy list and is recorded, not hidden.
- Not grounded. No shock experiment was run for this solution.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A controlled shock with a monomorphic control, reported per slot, with independence measured between strategies rather than assumed from their names. The discriminating questions, in the order the dependency chain requires:

1. **Enumeration.** Were all relevant actors, conditions and relations in contact with the residual listed, including second-order contact (Fig 11 :44)? An unenumerated competitor is the residual, not a known variable.
2. **Variation.** Were the strategies distinguishable in the bracket sense, and did they survive the handoff into the shocked habitat?
3. **Independence.** What is ρ among the strategies? A count without this is not evidence (Fig 2 :44 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong.").
4. **Ground pressure.** Was S nonzero, that is, did the shock actually change what the ground pays for?
5. **Source discrimination.** Did post-shock survival come from selection between variants, or from one strategy that was already best before the shock? A survival count cannot comment on the source; an instrument that can is owed.

Standing falsifiers from `01` §4: equal residual reduction against a monomorphic control; response carried entirely by the pre-shock best strategy; raw count beating ρ-corrected count; portfolio averages matching per-slot rankings; an agent-only improvement moving the ground; progress with both factors nonzero.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The answer stops where the instruments stop distinguishing sources from readings. "Indiscernibility is not identity." A search that finds no supporting experiment is not proof of absence (Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."). What is recorded is the lineage: the census, the figure lines, the world witnesses, the refuter, and the strains. That is checkable even though the coefficient is not yet measurable.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The figures do not give a resilience number for strategy competition. They give the shape of the answer. Competition is a relation whose sign depends on the residual after the shock, so it cannot be called good or bad in advance. What plurality does is supply variation inside the population; the shock is when the ground votes between the alternatives. With no distinguishable variants, the system gets motion, not adaptation. But "different" has to mean independent: strategies drawing on one evidence pool are one searcher wearing many names, so ρ corrects the count. The response must be read per strategy, not as a portfolio average, and progress needs both the fidelity with which the variants survive the handoff and the ground's pressure on them. Capability belongs to the portfolio plus its habitat, not to the winning strategy alone. What would settle it is a controlled shock with a monomorphic control, per-slot residuals, measured independence and an instrument that distinguishes selection between variants from one pre-adapted winner. Until that instrument exists, the shape is the result and the coefficient is owed.
