## 0. The residual, in effect terms

The question asks: when several strategies coexist and are in competition, and a shock arrives, what is different about the system's response compared with a system that held only one strategy?

Two options, stated as effects:

- **Option A (competition is friction).** The response is whatever the single best strategy does under the new conditions. Competition only reallocates shares; with one strategy the response would be the same. Diversity is decoration.
- **Option B (competition is the variance that selection uses).** Competition holds several strategies in play, so when the shock changes what the ground returns, the ground can select between pre-existing variants instead of displacing one locked-in trajectory. The response changes kind: from motion to adaptation.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the search (Formula "Direction"): a claim of the form "competition alters the shock response by <mechanism>, measurable as <quantity> against <ground>". The sought-for shape is written down here so it can accrue a residual like anything else rather than remain an invisible preference.

**R by arity, symmetry and scale.** Arity: at least three terms, strategies (plural), a shock, and a response. Symmetry: competition is a *relation*, symmetric in form between strategies but not in effect, because effect is relative to the residual. Scale: one strategy is an individual-scale actor; the portfolio of strategies is the population scale (Fig 4); the shock is a change at the habitat scale. The response is therefore a cross-scale quantity, not a property of one strategy.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type |

| **D2: the shock (habitat movement)** | conditions, competitors, dependencies (Fig 11 :46 "conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain") | contact; what the ground returns now | the ground, which does not change its answer because a frame thinks otherwise (Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks.") | the trigger that makes selection fire |

**Partition or union.** These three are a **dependency chain, not a union of independent questions**. Variation must be present before selection can act (Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."). The shock is the condition that makes the selector fire. If the chain is read below a missing link, the reading is of nothing (Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."). This is not the union compound of Fig 9, where a case in two conditions is a different kind of thing; here the conditions are ordered links in one loop.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Enumerate the habitat first (Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."), then establish that variation exists inside the population, then read selection between variants, then read the per-slot response. Refuse a term held to be false before minting a new one (Formula step 2). This order governs the findings below.

**Strain test applied at census.** The word "competition" invites the ecological-niche reading. The canonical article explicitly declines that mapping: a habitat here is defined by contact with a *residual*, and the residual moves, so the same actors constitute a different habitat when the question changes. The resemblance is real and the definitions do not align. I therefore do not equate the strategy population with a niche; I keep it as contact with R. Recorded as a strain in §7, not smoothed over.

## 3. Findings

### F1: Competition is a relation in the habitat, and its valence is relative to the residual

Fig 11 :47: "relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency".

Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."

Fig 11 :50: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."

Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

Competition is not a property that can be assigned a fixed sign ("competition helps resilience" or "competition hurts resilience"). It is a relation whose effect is read against the residual *after* the shock. A strategy that was a benefactor before the shock can be a malfactor after it, because the residual changed. This is the first reason a single scalar answer to the question is the wrong shape.

**Mode:** `specified` (structural), triangulated across the habitat figure and the canonical article's unchanged statement that this claim "is not borrowed".
**Refuter:** exhibit a case where the same strategy keeps the same valence for two different residuals that are otherwise identical in the relevant conditions, with the ground's verdict unchanged. That breaks the relativity claim.

### F2: The mechanism: variation inside, selection between

Fig 7 :33: "variation inside, selection between".

Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."

Fig 7 :24: "low ρ between rooms; the next room is picked against the residual the last one surfaced".

Fig 4 :5: "many agents, a shared library, and something that ranks what they produce" (the population).

The figure's answer to "what does plurality do" is: plurality supplies the variation *inside* a closure, and the ground's vote happens *between* the alternatives. Competition is the relation that keeps alternatives present and ranked. A shock is the event that changes which one the ground pays for. So competition does not produce the response; it determines whether selection has anything to select from when the shock arrives. The response is a selection event, not a single trajectory being nudged.

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 7 (checking arrangement across rooms) and Fig 4 (population scale with a ranking). These two figures have low overlap: one is about handoffs between frames, the other about scale and what crosses the membrane.
**Refuter:** a controlled shock in which a multi-strategy system and a single-strategy system show the same per-slot residual reduction, with the multi-strategy system's alternatives demonstrably still present and low-correlation. That would mean selection between variants is not doing the work.

### F3: Without variation, a shock produces motion, not adaptation

Fig 7 :32: "one long room: motion, no progress".

Fig 7 :36: "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."

Fig 7 :30: "every room closes some of the gap; no finite number of them ever arrives".

If all strategies are effectively one strategy (ρ near 1), the system is one long room. A shock moves it, but there is no alternative for the ground to pick, so the loop runs in place. The distinction the figure draws is not "big shock versus small shock"; it is "there is a next different room versus there is not". This is the strongest figure-grounded reason why competition among *different* strategies, as opposed to many copies of one strategy, matters.

**Mode:** `specified`, single-source for the precise phrase (Fig 7), corroborated at the world level by the non-innovative property of replicator dynamics (see `09_watson_answer.md`).
**Refuter:** a monomorphic system that matches a low-correlation polymorphic system's post-shock residual reduction across repeated novel shocks, under equal resources and equal bracket fidelity.

### F4: "Different" must mean independent, or it is only more spelling

Fig 6 :53: "searchers drawing on the same evidence are one searcher wearing many names".

Fig 6 :53: "Independence decides whether more of them buy more search or only more spelling, which makes it the parameter rather than a caveat."

Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."

Fig 10 :80 "And divergence regenerates unevenly. Frames whose independence is renewed by separate experience can be maintained; frames whose correlation is fixed at origin can only be rotated, because no amount of use makes them less alike and replacement is the only refresh."

A strategy count is not a diversity measure. Ten strategies reading one signal are one strategy wearing ten names. Under a shock that hits the shared evidence pool, they fail together. Independence is the parameter that decides whether competition among strategies buys real search or only additional labels. Fig 10 adds the maintenance condition: independence renewed by separate experience can be maintained, while correlation fixed at origin can only be rotated, so replacement is the only refresh.

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 2, Fig 6 and Fig 10 (three figures with different subject matter).
**Refuter:** a portfolio whose strategies are demonstrably drawn from a single evidence pool, yet whose post-shock response tracks its raw strategy count rather than its ρ-corrected count. That would demote independence from parameter to caveat.

### F5: The response must be read per slot, not as a portfolio average

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."

Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

A shock typically registers on one slot first. If the system's "response" is reported as a portfolio mean, the live signal that says where to look is thrown away. This governs the observable: the answer must be a vector of per-strategy residuals, and the question "how does competition alter the response" must be answered per slot before it is answered for the system.

**Mode:** `specified`, single-source (Fig 1). Adjacent material in Fig 9 on unions is related but governs a different quantity.
**Refuter:** a shock experiment in which the portfolio-average residual and the per-slot residuals rank the same strategies in the same order for every shock tested. Then the per-slot requirement adds nothing here.

### F6: Capability belongs to the portfolio plus habitat, not to the winning strategy

Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone".

Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

Fig 11 :36: "Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."

This rules out a common answer shape: "competition makes the strongest strategy stronger, and that improves the response." Improving one strategy in isolation does not change contact and therefore changes nothing at the ground. What changes the response is the arrangement of strategies and habitat, including which competitors are present. After the shock, "selection ran" is the honest description; "the system intelligently adapted" adds no explanatory work.

**Mode:** `specified`, single-source for the claim as stated, with the canonical article noting it is not borrowed from prior literature.
**Refuter:** an agent-only improvement, with habitat and competitor set held fixed, that moves the ground's post-shock verdict. That would refute the capability claim.

### F7: The ground does not negotiate, so "adaptation" cannot self-certify

Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update."

Fig 2 :45: "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."

Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

A shock is a change in the conditions the portfolio is ranged against, not a change in what the ground counts as a verdict. If the system's own agreement is allowed to define "successful adaptation", the anchor has become another frame and the drift is coherent (Fig 2, panel 1). The cost of being wrong here is a wrong attribution of cause, so independence of the checker is bought in proportion to that cost, through public, opt-in venues rather than a list of likely agreeers.

**Mode:** `specified` (structural), triangulated across Fig 2 and Fig 10.
**Refuter:** a shock study in which the system's self-reported adaptation score determines the ground's verdict, with no external measure. That is not a refutation of the figure; it is the named collapse, and it invalidates that study's result.

### F8: Progress has two factors, and either at zero stops the chain

Fig 7 :37: "Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:"

Fig 7 :38: "the bracket's fidelity across the handoff"

Fig 7 :39: "the ground's pressure on the room"

Fig 7 :26: "progress per handoff: R = h² × S"

This converts F2 to F4 into a checkable quantity. Post-shock progress needs (a) the variants to remain distinguishable across the handoff (bracket fidelity, tied to low ρ and per-slot reading) and (b) real selection pressure from the ground. If either factor is zero, competition among strategies cannot improve the response, no matter how many strategies are present. This is the sub-claim sent to the expert packet.

**Mode:** `specified`, single-source for the two-factor formula (Fig 7). The mapping "handoff" to "shock response" is a cross-level analogy and is named as such.
**Refuter:** a shock where ground pressure S is demonstrably nonzero and bracket fidelity h is demonstrably nonzero, yet post-shock residual does not fall relative to the monomorphic control.

### F9: A designer can introduce, not subtract, and the ground still decides

Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."

Fig 11 :32: "→ introduce, never subtract"

This bears on the practical reading of the question. A maintainer of a strategy portfolio can add a strategy and watch the cascade; it cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world. Where a portfolio is a designed list from which an item can be delisted, this clause is **strained** and does not transfer directly. Re-census: the asymmetry clause governs maintained habitats in which contact is not authorable. I record this as a strain, not a correction (see §7).

**Mode:** `specified`, single-source, strained in the designed-portfolio application.
**Refuter:** a maintained habitat in which a maintainer removes an actor and the ground's post-shock verdict changes only because of that removal, with no new introduction. That would break the asymmetry as stated for that domain.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before any outreach or conclusion, per the standing rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument.

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A multi-strategy system with low ρ among strategies shows no better per-slot post-shock residual reduction than a monomorphic control with equal resources, across repeated novel shocks.
2. The post-shock response is carried entirely by the strategy whose pre-shock residual was already lowest, with no evidence of selection between variants under the shock.
3. Raw strategy count predicts the response better than an independence-corrected count (that is, ρ is a caveat rather than the parameter).
4. Portfolio-average residuals rank strategies the same way as per-slot residuals for every shock tested (Fig 1's per-slot requirement does not govern here).
5. An agent-only improvement, with habitat and competitor set fixed, moves the ground's verdict (Fig 11 :40 is wrong).
6. With ground pressure S nonzero and bracket fidelity h nonzero, post-shock residual does not fall relative to control (Fig 7 :26 is wrong).

If any fires, the derived shape is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with F1 and F2: competition sets the *kind* of response available (selection between variants), not a scalar resilience value.
2. State F4 as a condition, not a footnote. A strategy count without independence is not a diversity claim.
3. Report the observable as per-slot residuals (F5), never as a single portfolio score.
4. Use F8 as the checkable sub-claim in outreach: bracket fidelity times ground pressure.
5. Send the generator, that is the residual, the mechanism and the refuter, not the conclusion. Fig 4's membrane rule: only methods cross up.
6. Ask for a refutation through a public venue in `03`, not for agreement by email. Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

## 7. Round-trip note and strains

The condensed chain carried into `07` and `09` is: habitat enumerated, variation inside, selection between, per-slot residuals, independence as parameter, two factors. The round trip from that chain back to the concrete question drops one thing and strains two:

- **Dropped:** any resilience coefficient. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". The framework returns the mechanism and the measurement shape, not a number.
- **Strain 1:** strategies mapped to rooms. Fig 7's ρ is between rooms in a checking chain. A strategy is not a checking frame. The mapping is a `specified` analogy with a falsifier (F2), not a derivation.
- **Strain 2:** maintained-habitat asymmetry mapped to a designed strategy list (F9). Named, not hidden.

After re-census: no figure contradiction. All three strains are domain-mapping strains, and each has a stated refuter. Consistent with the canonical rule, none was resolved by softening a quote.
