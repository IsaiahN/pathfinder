# Order of the response chain, and portfolio isomers

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

Two supporting questions are owed by `01`:

1. What is the order of dependence among habitat, variation, selection and response? The worry is that a reading taken below a missing link is a reading of nothing (Fig 3).
2. Do two portfolios with the *same* strategies but different competition structures count as the same system or different systems? The worry is the flat-formula collapse: ingredients recorded, arrangement not recorded.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Actors: strategies (Fig 4 :5 "many agents, a shared library, and something that ranks what they produce"), the conditions the shock changes (Fig 11 :46 "conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"), and the slots on which R is read. Bonds: the seven typed relations of Fig 12, plus negation. No new primitive is available without import. The habitat is enumerated, not composed (Fig 11 :43).

## DERIVATION

### Q1: What is the order of the chain?

- Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Each link consumes what the one above produces.
- Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
- Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." The list must be read off the world before any ranking.
- Fig 7 :33 "variation inside, selection between". Variation is upstream of selection.
- Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." The ground's vote is downstream of variation.
- Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." The response is read after selection, per slot.

Therefore:

```
habitat enumerated -> variation inside -> shock changes conditions
                   -> selection between -> residual read per slot
```

If variation is absent, the selection link has no input. Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." There is no instrument for selection between variants if no variants are present, so the response reading is a reading of nothing. The correct diagnosis is upstream, not "the response mechanism is weak".

**Falsifier, written before the run:** a system with no distinguishable variants whose post-shock residual still falls as far as a low-correlation polymorphic system, with the ground's pressure held equal. That would mean the variation link is not load-bearing.

### Q2: Are two portfolios with the same strategies "isomers"?

- Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :118 "1."
- Fig 12 :123 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Let portfolio P hold strategies {A, B, C} with no interaction between them, and portfolio Q hold the same {A, B, C} with a competition relation coupling them. As flat ingredient lists they are identical. Under Fig 12's definition:

- They share the same atoms.
- They differ in the bond (absence versus competition between the same atoms).
- Therefore they are **isomers**, not variants of one thing. The difference is invisible in a list of strategies and decisive in the shock response.

The weaker reading: both portfolios share the **functional group** "variation inside" (distinguishable strategies present). The qualifier, which bond holds between them, says which member. This is the correct match for a portfolio where strategies merely coexist without interacting.

### Q3: Which bond is being changed?

The competition relation is listed among habitat relations (Fig 11 :47 "relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"). It is not one of Fig 12's seven bonds. This is a **domain-mapping strain** and must be named: I am using Fig 12's *definition of isomerism* (same atoms, different relation, different substance) as a structural analogy, not claiming that "competes with" is one of the seven typed bonds. The Operators table's rule applies: "A relation that fails all seven is not an operator you are missing; it is a claim that has not been made yet." Competition as a typed bond is a claim this derivation does not make.

- **Falsifier, written before the run:** two portfolios with identical strategy sets and different competition structures that show identical per-slot post-shock residuals across all shocks tested. The isomer claim fails; the flat ingredient list was sufficient after all.

### Q4: What does the round trip lose?

- Fig 4 :27 "The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."
- Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."

Sent up: "competition among strategies changes the shock response". Brought back down: "add competition and resilience improves". The gap `R_T` contains at least three dropped constraints: independence between strategies, per-slot reading, and the two-factor condition. Treating the shorthand as identical to the figure set would set `R_T` to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error. The honest report is a lossy condensation with a measured gap, enumerated qualitatively here because no ground reading exists.

### Q5: Analogues and their limits

- **Replicator dynamics (world, low overlap).** The replicator equation is non-innovative: all strategies are assumed present from the outset, and a strategy that reaches zero cannot be revived. This is a precise, independent witness for Q1's claim that variation is an upstream prerequisite. It is an analogue at the level of population dynamics, not a figure line, so it corroborates but cannot decide.
- **Bet hedging (world, low overlap).** Diversified bet hedging deliberately holds several strategies at a cost in typical conditions in exchange for survival in stressful ones. This is a second independent witness for the same claim, and it adds a cost term the figures do not price. Note the difference: bet hedging is a strategy *within* an organism; the figure's variation is *between* strategies in a population. The analogy is at the level of the shape, not the mechanism.
- **Ecological resilience (world, low overlap).** Specialisation is described as weakening resilience because a system becomes dependent on prevailing conditions and loses the ability to adapt to unanticipated shocks. After re-census this does **not** conflict with Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." The ecology claim is about system-level dependence under unanticipated shocks; the figure claim is about actor-level valence relative to a residual. Different objects, so no conflict and no re-census beyond naming the difference.

## CONCLUSION

The response chain is ordered: habitat, variation, shock, selection, per-slot residual. A reading below a missing variation link is a reading of nothing. Two portfolios with identical strategy sets and different competition relations are isomers under Fig 12's definition, with the weaker functional-group reading available when strategies merely coexist. Both claims are `specified` and both carry falsifiers. The bond used for competition is an analogy, not one of the seven typed bonds, and that is recorded as a strain.
