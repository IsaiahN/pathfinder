# Expert verification packet: strategy competition and shock response

**What this is:** a claim in a form an evolutionary game theorist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the substantive claims, `general` on the structural shape. Not `grounded`.** No shock experiment is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Competition among different strategies does not set the response to a shock by itself; it changes the kind of response available. Competition is a relation in the habitat whose valence depends on the residual after the shock, so it has no fixed sign. Plurality supplies variation inside the population, and the shock is the event at which the ground selects between the variants. Without distinguishable variants the system gets motion, not adaptation. Distinguishability requires independence, because strategies sharing one evidence pool are one searcher wearing many names. The response is read per slot rather than as a portfolio average, and progress needs both the fidelity of the handoff and the ground's pressure. Capability belongs to the portfolio plus habitat, not to the winning strategy alone.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: The mechanism: variation inside, selection between
- **Statement:** competition's relevant effect is to keep alternatives present and ranked, so that a shock can be answered by selection between variants rather than by displacing one locked-in strategy. A monomorphic system with equal resources should not match a low-correlation polymorphic system's per-slot post-shock residual reduction across repeated novel shocks.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 7 :33 "variation inside, selection between". Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Fig 7 :32 "one long room: motion, no progress". Also Fig 4 :5 "many agents, a shared library, and something that ranks what they produce" (the population).
- **What to check:** is the mapping of "room" to "strategy under shock" legitimate in the model you use, or does it hide a level error? The figure's ρ is between checking rooms, not between strategies; this is a `specified` analogy, not a derivation.
- **Refuter:** a monomorphic control matches the polymorphic system's per-slot post-shock residuals with ground pressure held equal; or the response is carried entirely by the strategy that was already best before the shock, with no selection between variants.

### C2: Independence is the parameter, not the strategy count
- **Statement:** the number of strategies predicts nothing without a measure of independence. Ten strategies reading one signal count as one. Under a shock that hits the shared signal, correlated strategies fail together.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :53 "searchers drawing on the same evidence are one searcher wearing many names". Fig 2 :31 "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." Fig 10 :80 "And divergence regenerates unevenly. Frames whose independence is renewed by separate experience can be maintained; frames whose correlation is fixed at origin can only be rotated, because no amount of use makes them less alike and replacement is the only refresh."
- **What to check:** does your model already ρ-correct the strategy count? If it does, this sub-claim is a restatement of something you hold, not news. If it does not, the raw count is a label count.
- **Refuter:** raw strategy count predicts post-shock response better than an independence-corrected count across the shock series.

### C3: The observable and the two factors
- **Statement:** the response must be reported as per-slot residuals, and progress requires both bracket fidelity and ground pressure. A portfolio average can hide the live signal, and either factor at zero stops the chain regardless of how many strategies are present.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 7 :37 "Progress per handoff has two factors. Zero either and the chain runs in place:" Fig 7 :38 "the bracket's fidelity across the handoff" Fig 7 :39 "the ground's pressure on the room" Fig 7 :26 "progress per handoff: R = h² × S".
- **What to check:** do portfolio-average residuals rank strategies the same way per-slot residuals do for every shock tested? If yes, the per-slot requirement adds nothing here. Is ground pressure nonzero, that is, did the shock actually change what the ground pays for?
- **Refuter:** with ground pressure demonstrably nonzero and bracket fidelity demonstrably nonzero, post-shock residual does not fall relative to the monomorphic control; or portfolio averages match per-slot rankings for all shocks.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that competition is always beneficial, or that diversity always raises resilience. Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
- Not a resilience coefficient, an optimal diversity level, or an ecological coefficient imported without a source gate. Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself".
- Not that the strategy portfolio is an ecological niche. The canonical article declines that mapping.
- Not that an agent-only improvement helps. Fig 11 :40 "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
- Not that a maintainer can subtract the habitat. Fig 11 :61 "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." This clause is strained for a designed strategy list from which items can be deleted, and the strain is declared.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating shock experiment, not a further argument. A post-shock survival reading cannot say whether selection between variants or one pre-adapted winner produced it.

1. Enumerate the habitat in contact with the residual, including second-order contact, before choosing variables.
2. Measure independence among strategies; do not infer it from distinct names.
3. Run repeated novel shocks against a monomorphic control with equal resources.
4. Report per-slot residuals, never only a portfolio mean.
5. Add an instrument that distinguishes selection between variants from a single pre-adapted winner.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 7 :33, :35, :32 and Fig 6 :53, four short passages. Decide whether "room" maps to "strategy under shock" in your model or whether it is a level error.
2. Check C2 against your own work: does your model already ρ-correct strategy counts? If yes, say so and the sub-claim is not new to you.
3. Name one shock experiment in which a monomorphic control and a low-correlation portfolio are compared per slot. If none exists, say so; that is useful too.
4. Name one instrument that would distinguish selection between variants from a pre-adapted winner, or say why none is possible.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
