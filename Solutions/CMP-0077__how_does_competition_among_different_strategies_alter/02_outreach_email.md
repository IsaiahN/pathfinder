**Subject:** One checkable claim about strategy competition and shock response

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual, in effect terms.** A system holds several strategies that compete. A shock changes the conditions it faces. Does the competition among those strategies change the response, and if so how? Two answers are possible: (A) competition only reallocates shares, so a single-strategy system would respond the same way; (B) competition keeps variants in play, so when the shock arrives the ground can select between pre-existing variants instead of displacing one locked-in trajectory.
>
> **The recorded verdict (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The figures do not return a resilience coefficient. They return a shape: plurality supplies variation inside a closure; the shock is when selection happens between the alternatives; without distinguishable variants the system gets motion, not adaptation. Independence between the alternatives is the parameter, not the strategy count. The response has to be read per strategy, not as a portfolio average. Progress needs both the fidelity with which variants survive the handoff and the ground's pressure on them.
>
> **What would refute it (written before this message):**
> 1. A low-correlation multi-strategy system shows no better per-slot post-shock residual reduction than a monomorphic control with equal resources, across repeated novel shocks.
> 2. The response is carried entirely by the strategy that was already best before the shock, with no selection between variants.
> 3. Raw strategy count predicts the response better than an independence-corrected count.
> 4. Portfolio averages rank strategies the same way per-slot readings do for every shock tested.
>
> I wrote these down before making contact. If any one holds, I will narrow or withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that competition is always good for resilience; that a more diverse portfolio is always better; that the system is intelligent because it adapted; that an ecological niche is the same thing as the figure's habitat; or that any of this is settled by experiment. The valence of a competitor is relative to the residual after the shock, so it cannot be assigned a fixed sign in advance.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figure text, not my summary of it, is the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures and formula for direct download, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical article with the full figure set and citation list: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether
>

Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
