# Dependency order, union compounds and analogues

**Mode: `specified` on the derivation, `general` on the structural claims, not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

The question pairs a stable developmental sequence with variable cultural deployment. Three things are owed: (1) what order the shared part has, (2) whether the two halves are a partition or a union, and (3) what an analogue is and is not here, so that a cross-domain match is not mistaken for identity.

## DERIVATION

### Q1. What order does the shared part have?

- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."
- Fig 3 :20 to :21: "Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
- Fig 3 :22 to :23: "A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can. Which is why a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing."
- The chain is perception, vocabulary (terms for what a situation affords), objective, planning, learn and carry (Fig 3 :5 to :16). Its order is structural, so it is the same order in every habitat. Its *pace* and *endpoint* are not fixed: Fig 4 :34 to :35, "The level a method lands on remakes it. A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."
- World check (not a figure line): Rosati et al. 2014 distinguish rate of acquisition from order and causal structure of acquisition. Their data show that order can be shared while pace and terminal additions differ. Prediction: the universal half of COG-0003 is the dependency order, not a milestone list. Corroborates Fig 3. Status `specified`, unmeasured by this agent.

### Q2. Is the question a partition or a union?

- Fig 9 :55 to :56: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- Fig 9 :58: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."
- The child develops inside a habitat and the habitat is transmitted through developing children, so the overlap is a compound with its own loop, not a conjunction of two independent questions. A single lever (pure innatism or pure learning) clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a clean partition existed, that is, if the shared sequence and the variable use could be settled by two independent instruments with no overlap in population, task or analyst, the union claim would fail and the two topics should be split. This is testable in principle but not done here.

### Q3. Are the cultural uses isomers of one another?

- Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124: "A functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Take one settled capacity, for example tracking objects through space, as an atom. Community A composes it into a counting practice; community B composes it into a wayfinding practice. Under Fig 12 they share the atom and differ in the bond and the surrounding molecule, so they are **isomers** at that level: same atom, different arrangement, different substance. The flat inventory "they both have object tracking" cannot see the difference, which is exactly Fig 12 :94, "The arrangement is the substance."
- Weaker reading: if the two practices do not share the atom at all, they are not isomers; they are different members of a functional group (both are `state → structure → state` compositions). The isomer claim is the stronger, checkable version.
- **Falsifier:** if either practice can be written without the atom the other requires, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands.

### Q4. What is the order across scales?

- Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."
- A first-order capacity, settled against the ground in individual development, becomes an atom at the second order, where a culture composes over it. This is why "used differently" is not a contradiction of "develops similarly": the first-order molecule and the second-order compound are different levels, and levels are related by a lossy transform (Fig 10 :68: "Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between.").
- Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." What transmits between generations is a method or a head start, never the recording of a particular success (Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."). This is why an inventory list, a recording, cannot carry the capacity; the developing child must rebuild it through contact (Fig 11 :40).

### Q5. What is the analogue, and what is not?

- **Analogue that holds:** core knowledge systems (Spelke 2024) are a small, early, domain-specific set that scaffolds later learning. This matches the *shape* of the framework's minimum set: small, early, load-bearing, not derived from other members. Low overlap between the cognitive frame and the framework's physical-substrate frame, so this is genuine corroboration of the shape.
- **Identity that does not hold:** the six core knowledge systems are not the six framework terms. One is a psychological inventory; the other is space, time, energy, spectrum, gradient, network. Fig 8 :50 to :52: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them." The record here separates them.
- **Strain named:** Fig 2 :38 warns that an anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it and is otherwise an instrument that "can be wrong on axes they do not measure". A cross-cultural survey is the right anchor for "which practices are used where"; it is only an instrument for "is this capacity universal", and it can be wrong on that axis if its samples are narrow (Singh 2026). The strain is between the two uses of the same instrument, not between two figures.

## CONCLUSION

The shared part is a **dependency order**, not a milestone list (`specified`, falsifiable). The question is a **union compound** with its own ground, so one lever cannot clear it (`specified`). Cultural uses of one settled capacity are **isomers** over a shared atom when they share that atom, otherwise members of one functional group (`specified`, with a stated falsifier). The transmission across generations is a **method or head start**, not a recording, which is why the capacity must be rebuilt in each child (Fig 4 :22 to :25, Fig 11 :40). None of this names the universal inventory; that remains a `gap` (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134).
