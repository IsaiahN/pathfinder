**Subject:** One checkable claim about cognitive universals, before any theory

> Hello,
>
> I am working on a figure-grounded derivation of a narrow claim and would like you to check the output against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output fails, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the parts, not `grounded`):** the claim has two halves that must not be merged. First, what recurs in early development is an invariant *form*: an ordered dependency chain (perception, terms for what a situation affords, a notion of progress, planning, learn and carry) and a loop that names what worked. Second, what varies across cultures is the *cast*: which actors, which currency ranks outputs, and which anchor settles the question. A seed carries what will grow, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes. So "develops similarly" means the same loop and order, not identical milestones; "used differently" means the same settled capacities composed into different practices.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any framework has identified the specific universal inventory. It returns a shape, not a name. I am not claiming that a particular list of core knowledge systems *is* the six-term substrate of the framework, or that a widely observed pattern in industrialized samples is a human universal. Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents and only the record separates them. Until provenance is shown, such a mapping carries zero evidentiary weight.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written down before this message:**
> 1. a population that reliably acquires a later dependency link without the earlier one, stable across two independent instruments;
> 2. a community whose cognitive practices require a different kernel or loop, not a different cast over the same one;
> 3. two genuinely independent, low-overlap instruments finding no common early sequence across communities;
> 4. evidence that the two comparison frames share one evidence pool, so their agreement is one witness.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>

> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
