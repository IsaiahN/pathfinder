## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the figures say about the claim that there are basic cognitive capacities developing similarly everywhere but used differently across cultures. Option A: assert a universal inventory and treat cultural variation as local use of that inventory. Option B: keep the figure-derived shape, an invariant kernel and dependency order with a varying cast, and refuse the named inventory. What each leads to: A names a slot the figures leave open, merges a union compound into one mechanism, and turns a shared habitat pattern into a universal without a discriminating instrument; B sends a checkable shape, written refuters, and a `gap` where the inventory would go.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The observed pair: a stable, widely shared sequence in early cognitive development, and substantial cross-cultural variation in which capacities get elaborated and what they are used for. Arity: two conditions, so a **union compound** (Fig 9 :58: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"), not one hard question. Symmetry: asymmetric, because development is ordered and dependency-governed while deployment depends on which frame composes over the settled capacity. Scale: first order inside one child, second order at the cultural and historical level.
- **Domain 1, individual development.** Actors: infant, perceptual systems, early core systems, learning, language. Currency: residual per object slot; what gets settled and can be composed over. Anchor: the developmental experiment, a measurable ground. Question type: dependency sequence.
- **Domain 2, culture and transmission.** Actors: caregivers, language community, tools, practices, ecology. Currency: methods and head starts crossing the membrane; which actors, currency and anchor a community selects. Anchor: cross-cultural observation against the same world the community tracks. Question type: cast variation.
- **Domain 3, the comparison frame.** Actors: researchers, instruments, samples, categories. Currency: agreement versus residual; independence between frames. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Question type: triangulation versus drift. In this simulated run all three domains draw on one evidence pool, so this domain's independence is zero and its agreement is not a witness.
- **Partition or union.** D1 and D2 are a union, not a partition: the child develops inside a habitat, and culture passes through developing children (Fig 11 :39). The overlap, the child developing-in-a-culture, has its own loop. D3 is a checking frame over the compound.
- **Order by dependency.** Perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry (Fig 3); refuse before mint (Formula step 2); describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27 to :28); settle first-order capacities before second-order cultural elaboration (Fig 12 :104).

Strain test: Fig 4 states its variable as size (one agent versus a thousand), not cultures. After re-census the kernel/variable split is carried independently by Fig 11 :39 and Fig 2 :31, so the extension survives as `general`, not `grounded`. One strain remains named, not hidden: the cross-cultural survey is the right anchor for where practices occur, but only an instrument for universality, and it can be wrong on that axis (Fig 2 :37 to :38; Singh 2026).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The qualified answer is yes on shape and no on inventory. There is an invariant kernel and dependency order that develop the same way everywhere, and a varying cast of actors, currency and anchor that explains different cultural uses; capability is agent-and-habitat, and cultural uses of one settled capacity are isomers over a shared atom. The question is a union compound, so a pure-universal or pure-learning lever clears only one condition. The figures do not name which capacities are universal; that is a `gap`, and naming it from a cross-cultural projection without a source-discriminating instrument would be the metamerism error. Status: `specified`, ungrounded, candidate. All claims are simulated and internally consistent, not ground readings.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states modes, refuters and provenance before the conclusion, so an error is findable. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending an inventory claim instead of a shape, which Fig 2 :42 prices: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The withheld-inventory discipline and the union reading are separable from the derivation; `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already state them. No primitive has to be withdrawn, and a superseding packet can be sent.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A cross-cultural design with individually resolved measures and two low-overlap frames that discriminates a shared kernel from a shared habitat would name or refuse the inventory; a stable later-without-earlier dependency link, a community requiring a different kernel, or evidence that the comparison frames share one evidence pool would break the shape claim. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
