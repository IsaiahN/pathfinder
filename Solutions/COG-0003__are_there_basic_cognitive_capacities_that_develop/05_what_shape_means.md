## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)". Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence".

From *The Library of Babel*: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

For this question, a **shape** is the form the answer must have before the universal capacities can be named: an invariant kernel (loop plus dependency order) with a varying cast (actors, currency, anchor), and a stop where the instruments can no longer discriminate a shared invariant from a shared habitat. It constrains; it does not fill. "There is an invariant kernel and a varying cast" is a shape. "The universal capacities are object permanence, number, space, causality and theory of mind" is a name, and it is not licensed by the figures.

## 2. How the shape was obtained here

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary already available that is closest to the thing, then "With each new term gained, you narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary". The starting vocabulary here was residual, kernel, cast, anchor, dependency order, habitat.
- Fig 6 :30: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." So the residual was described in effects (stable sequence plus variable deployment), not in the framework's private vocabulary first.
- Fig 9 :30 to :31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause".
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world." That is why the outreach artifact asks what a refutation would look like before showing the theory.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: "you will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place". Here the stop is not a named inventory but a recorded gap, which is the honest form.

## 3. What would license a name

Two things, both outside the shape:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." For this question that means cross-cultural, individually resolved measurement with an anchor that does not move when a frame pushes on it (Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update.").
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs**, with a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8). The core-knowledge literature is such an import: it explains why early competence recurs across societies and species (shadow test), and its provenance is recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv`. It does not supply the framework's six-term substrate, so no atom crosses into the framework's closure and the specific inventory remains a `gap`.

## 4. The metamerism warning applies directly here

*The Hard Problem is About Source*: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." The color instrument reads green and "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer, not an argument, resolved it. "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."

For COG-0003 the tempting error is to read a cross-cultural regularity (the projection) and name its source: a universal innate inventory. But the same regularity could come from a shared kernel, from a shared habitat (similar environments and institutions), or from both. This is precisely upstream question COG-0002. Without an instrument that discriminates among those sources, naming one is the metamerism error. What is owed is a better instrument, not a name. Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."

## 5. Q for this question: what shape does the answer have?

**Answer (`general`):** the answer must have three features and one refusal.

1. An invariant **form**: the same dependency order and the same naming loop across development. Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."
2. A variable **cast**: different actors, currency and anchor across communities. Fig 4 :20: "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."
3. A **union** structure: the two halves are conditions of one compound with its own ground, not two versions of one question. Fig 9 :58: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
4. A **refusal** of the named inventory until a source-discriminating instrument exists. Fig 9 :32 and Fig 13 :134.

**Does the field literature commit the forbidden error?** A strong universal-inventory reading does: it names the source of a cross-cultural projection without an instrument that discriminates shared kernel from shared habitat. Singh 2026 makes exactly this case and is recorded as a conflict in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F6. After re-census the conflict narrows the verdict to the shape; it does not flip the figures' structural result.

## 7. Two consequences for the package

**Prestige is not action.** *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige." A list of distinguished people who agree that a capacity is universal is the named collapse, not corroboration.

**This audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: LLMs "can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output", but "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself". So `01`, `06`, `07`, `08`, `09` and `11` are simulated derivations, checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are not tests of the claim and must be labelled as such. They cannot settle anything they report on.
