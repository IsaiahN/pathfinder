## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07_the_tether_answer.md`: there is an invariant kernel and order; cultures vary the cast; the inventory is withheld; the question is a union compound.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the concrete question-level claim: "these are the basic cognitive capacities that develop everywhere and are used differently across cultures."

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **identity of the kernel with a cognitive inventory**: the shorthand's "kernel" is a loop and a naming rule over states and structures, not a psychological list; the question's "basic cognitive capacities" invites a list, and that list is not licensed;
- the **mode of the kernel line**: Fig 4 states size, not culture. The shorthand carries a `general` domain-agnostic extension; a reader who takes it as a measured cross-cultural universal has silently set R_T to zero;

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: development has a dependency order and the cast varies; capability is agent-and-habitat, so different contact yields different endpoints. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the formula as identical to the figures would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a measured gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` states.
