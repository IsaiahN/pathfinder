## 0. The residual, stated as an effect

Question as given: are there basic cognitive capacities that develop similarly everywhere but are used differently across cultures?

Stated as a residual R, before any figure is chosen:

> Children in every society studied reach a small set of early competencies in a broadly similar order (object tracking, number, gaze following, social-partner representation). What those competencies are used for, and which ones are elaborated into counting systems, spatial frameworks, categories and social inference, varies widely. The two observations are both well supported. A theory that explains the first by a fixed inventory inside the child cannot explain the second without adding culture as an afterthought. A theory that explains the second by local learning alone cannot explain why the order recurs. The residual is the gap between those two explanations.

- **Arity:** two conditions, not one. This is a union compound, not a single hard question (Fig 9 :58: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either").
- **Symmetry:** asymmetric. Development is ordered and dependency-governed (Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."), whereas deployment is a matter of which frame composes over the settled capacity, and different frames are not interchangeable (Fig 10 :68: "Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between.").
- **Scale:** first order inside one developing child; second order at the cultural/historical level, where a settled capacity becomes an atom for further composition (Fig 12 :104).

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term (Formula, "Direction").** An answer has two parts that must not be averaged: (A) a shared invariant that explains the common sequence, and (B) a variable cast that explains the different uses. It must say what would make either part fail. If a candidate answer merges A and B into one mechanism, it has not answered the question, it has dissolved one side of it.

## 3. Findings F1 to F9

### F1. The shared part is a kernel, not an inventory (governs A)

Fig 4 :19 to :20:

> "The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."

Fig 4 :34 to :35:

> "The level a method lands on remakes it. A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."

The kernel is invariant in *form* (loop plus naming rule). What varies is the cast: actors, currency, anchor. The seed reading is the exact shape of "develops similarly, used differently": what is inherited is what will grow, and local conditions decide what it becomes.

**Strain test and re-census.** Fig 4 states its quantity as *size*, one agent versus a thousand, not cultures. Applying it to cross-cultural variation is a domain-agnostic extension, not the line's stated subject. Re-census with other domains: Fig 11 :39 gives the same invariant at the level of contact ("capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"), and Fig 2 :31 gives independence between frames. Two different figures with low overlap carry the kernel/variable split, so the extension survives re-census. Mode stays `general`, not `grounded`: these are structural constraints, not a measurement of a human universal.

### F2. A minimum shared substrate exists, but the figures do not name it as cognitive (governs the identity question)

Fig 13 :3:

> "No system functions without any of them, none is built from the others, and everything else is a composition across the two kinds."

Fig 9 :32:

> "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"

The figures assert a minimum viable set that any system composes with. They do **not** assert that this set is a list of cognitive capacities. The six terms are states (space, time, energy) and structures (spectrum, gradient, network), the substrate the loop assumes. The claim "these six ARE the basic cognitive capacities of the question" is a **binding from the cognitive-science frame onto framework atoms**, not a derivation from the figures and not an import that moves the wall (Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs.").

So the two halves must be kept separate:
- the existence of a shared minimum substrate: `general`, triangulated (Fig 13 :3 with Fig 4 :19 to :20);
- the identity of that substrate with a named inventory of cognitive capacities: **gap**. It cannot be derived from the figures. "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" (Fig 13 :134), and "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence" (Fig 8 :39).

The world literature offers a candidate import (six early-emerging core-knowledge systems, Spelke 2024), but it is not the same six and its agreement with the framework is corroboration of a *shape* (a small number of early systems), not an identity. Provenance is recorded in `10_source_ledger.csv` and the overlap is declared as low between the cognitive frame and the framework's physical-substrate frame, so this is genuine low-overlap corroboration of the shape only.

### F3. "Develops similarly" means the same dependency order, not the same milestones (governs D1)

Fig 3 :20:

> "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."

Fig 11 :40:

> "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

The dependency chain (perception to vocabulary to objective to planning to learn and carry) is structural: each link consumes what the one above produces. What is universal in development is this order and the loop, not a calendar of identical milestones. Different habitats change contact, and changing contact changes what the capacity becomes, without changing the order of the links. Falsifier: a population that reliably acquires a later link without the earlier one, stable across two independent instruments, breaks the dependency claim. Mode `specified`.

### F4. "Used differently" means a different cast over the same kernel (governs D2)

Fig 4 :20: "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."
Fig 10 :68: "Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between."
Fig 12 :117 (the isomer principle): "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

A culture is a different frame: its library (Γ), the actors it composes, the currency that ranks outputs, and the anchor it uses differ. The same settled capacity composed into counting, wayfinding or social inference is the same atom under different bonds and in different compounds, which is exactly why a flat list of "capacities" cannot see the difference (Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance"). Triangulated across Fig 4, Fig 10 and Fig 12. Mode `specified`.

### F5. The question is a union compound, so one lever cannot clear it (method)

Fig 9 :58:

> "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

Fig 9 :55 to :56:

> "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."

A pure-universal mechanism clears the "shared sequence" condition and leaves the overlap standing. A pure-learning account clears the "variable use" condition and leaves the same overlap standing. "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different" (Fig 9 :57 to :58). The compound needs its own loop, with its own ground: cross-cultural, individually resolved measurement of how a shared sequence is deployed. This is the central methodological finding, and it answers upstream question COG-0002 as well: a widespread pattern caused by similar environments is a shared habitat condition, not a different kind of thing, and it is separated by frame independence (F6), not by introspection.

### F6. How a universal is told from a widespread pattern: independence, not agreement (governs D3)

Fig 2 :31:

> "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."

Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update."
Fig 9 :27 to :28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"

A sample drawn from industrialized societies and then declared the human baseline is a frame using its own library as the anchor. The anchor then updates on the frame, which is Fig 2's collapse 1. The remedy is not more agreement inside the same pool; it is two instruments in genuinely different closures ranging separately against a ground that does not move. Two pages quoting the same review are one witness. World source Singh 2026 ("What infant research can and cannot tell us about human universals") makes the same point from inside the field and is recorded as a **conflict** with a strong universal-inventory claim, which after re-census narrows this solution to the shape-only verdict rather than flipping it.

Verdict: triangulated (Fig 2 plus Fig 9 plus an independent world source), mode `general` as a method rule.

### F7. The specific inventory is a gap, and that is the honest stop

Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."
Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
Fig 6 :29: "inward: is our representation adequate to hold it? The instrument extends access."

Three distinct questions, kept separate per Formula step 7:
1. **SEARCHED.** Is the universal inventory already in the developmental-science closure but unfound? This solution's search found candidate inventories (core knowledge systems) but no figure-derived selection among them. Answer: not established.
2. **INWARD.** Sharpen the instrument: cross-cultural, individually resolved measures that discriminate a shared sequence from a shared environment. Possible and in progress in the field (Bohn et al. 2024), not settled.
3. **OUTWARD.** Import from a frame whose closure differs: the core-knowledge literature supplies a candidate shape with provenance and a shadow test (it explains why early competence recurs). It does not supply the framework's six, so no atom moves the wall (Fig 6 :25: "An import adds no atom to the world.").

Honest stop: the answer to the question as a **shape** is yes with a qualified structure; the answer as a **named universal inventory** is `gap` at this budget and with these instruments.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A child population reliably acquires a later dependency link (planning) without the earlier one (perception or vocabulary), across two instruments in different closures, with the effect persisting. Breaks F3 and Fig 3 :20.
2. A community's cognitive practices cannot be expressed as a different cast or arrangement over the same kernel, that is, they require a different kernel or loop. Breaks F1 and Fig 4 :19 to :20.
3. Two genuinely independent, low-overlap instruments (different samples, different tasks, different analysts) find no common early sequence across communities. Breaks the "develops similarly" half. One WEIRD-only study is not such an instrument.
4. A derivation shows the six-term substrate and a named cognitive-capacity list are the same set under Fig 12's identity test, so the identity is a derivation rather than a binding. Breaks F2's `gap` verdict (it would upgrade it, not downgrade it; still a refutation of this solution as written).
5. The two comparison frames are found to share the same evidence pool, so their agreement is one witness and the triangulation label must be withdrawn. Breaks F6.

If any fires, that claim is withdrawn or relabelled. Until one is run, no part of this package is `grounded`.
