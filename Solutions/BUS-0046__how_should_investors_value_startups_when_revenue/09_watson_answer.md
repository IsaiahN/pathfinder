## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about valuing a startup when revenue, user growth and future markets conflict. Option A: treat the conflict as one hard estimation problem, blend the three readings into a single price with weights, and invest at that price. Option B: treat the conflict as a union of well-formed questions with separate instruments and a non-updating anchor, keep the residual indexed per slot, and stage the commitment. What each leads to: A produces a precise number whose internal consistency checks cannot see the target preference that chose the weights, and it spends the independence of the people checking it; B produces a checkable shape, five refuters written in advance, and an open number until a realised clearing event settles it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

- **R (the residual).** The gap between the investor's predicted value Γ(b,a) and the realised outcome o′, indexed by slot s (revenue, user growth, future market, company). Arity: three-place relation plus a time index, a sequence rather than a set. Symmetry: asymmetric, since paying more does not make the company worth more, and "undervalued" is a threshold against a required return. Scale: one investment, a portfolio ranking, and a market habitat that settles many decisions. Fig 9 names it a union compound, not one hard question: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either.
- **Domain 1, the signal frames.** Actors: counters, metrics, narratives, the analysts who produce them. Currency: readings. Anchor: realised cash conversion and realised clearing (Fig 2 :29, a market and a counter). Question type: union of overlapping conditions.
- **Domain 2, the valuation act.** Actors: investor, model, term sheet, price, tranches. Currency: description cost in bits and settlement cost in actions (Fig 12 :125-126). Anchor: the payment the ground makes on the security. Question type: two currencies that do not add.
- **Domain 3, the settlement frame.** Actors: later rounds, acquirers, public markets, auditors, write-offs. Currency: cash, liquidation preference, realised return. Anchor: the arms-length clearing event. Question type: source-discriminating ground, not a vote.
- **Domain 4, the temporal channel.** Actors: investor instruments, metrics, board information rights. Currency: contact with the ground. Anchor: the ground, since it does not decay while the channel does (Fig 10 :24).
- **Excluded X1, "true intrinsic value" as a free-floating number:** no anchor independent of a transaction or cash flow, so not a domain yet. **Excluded X2, "market sentiment" as a separate ground:** sentiment updates when pushed and is a frame inside Domain 3.
- **Order by dependency.** Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28); refuse a false held regularity before minting a new weight (Formula step 2); settle each slot before composing the second-order price; keep the anchor reachable (Fig 10 :29).

Strain test applied: "market" as anchor was re-censused from the live comparable multiple to the realised clearing event, because the former updates on the opinions it is supposed to settle (Fig 2 :30). Fig 13's physical six-term substrate was refused as a valuation ontology and retained only for the honest-stop role.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

Option B. Keep the residual indexed per slot; split the union into well-formed questions with different instruments; use the realised clearing event as a non-updating anchor; buy independence where the cost of being wrong is largest; keep description price and settlement price in separate columns; bind the target number as a refusable term; stage the commitment so each tranche buys the missing reading. Syndicate agreement is a convention for closing a round, not a witness for the world. The company-specific price is a `gap`, unreachable at this budget: the figures return a shape, five refuters and the instruments that would close it, not a number. This verdict is `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, five refuters and provenance before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a number dressed as a verdict, which Fig 2 :42 prices ("Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."). That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuters.
- **undoable:** yes. The staging and the per-slot structure are design choices that can be changed without withdrawing the derivation; a sent number can be superseded by the corrected shape; no valuation primitive has to be withdrawn. What is not undoable is a capital commitment made at a blended price before any independent reading arrived, which is why C3 makes staging part of the answer.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A prospective test in which blended and per-slot scores predict realised outcomes equally would kill the indexing claim; convergence of the three readings with no instrument change would kill the union claim; a realised price that moves with the first investor's private model would kill the anchor claim; equal accuracy from shared and disjoint evidence panels would kill the independence claim; commensurability of the two currencies would kill the two-currency claim; a figure line that returns a numeric valuation rule after re-census would close the gap. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
