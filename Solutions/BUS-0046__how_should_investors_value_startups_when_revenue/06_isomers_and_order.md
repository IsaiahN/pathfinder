# Union, order and method types in startup valuation

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The ordinary question asks which of three signals should dominate the price. In effect terms the question is: given three readings that share a customer story but fail on different subsets, what structure must the investor's answer have so that a live slot is not lost, and in what order must the investor act on it? Two derivations are owed: (1) the dependency order of the signals and the instrument that is missing, and (2) the type relation among valuation methods (are they isomers, a functional group, or a union of overlapping conditions?).

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held by a typical investor: a cash count, a user count, a growth rate, a churn rate, a market-size estimate, a discount rate, a comparable multiple, a comparison to a required return, a sequence of financing events, and a negation (refusal to invest). Bonds held: the seven plus negation. No new primitive is available without an import from a frame whose closure differs (Fig 6 :14). The three "signals" are not atoms; they are molecules already composed from these atoms under different bonds.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Union or partition?

- Fig 9 :4: **"First: is there a fact here?"** The figure says disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are several well-formed questions.
- Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
- The three signals overlap: revenue is users times conversion times price, so a user-growth reading and a revenue reading share two of three operands. The future-market reading shares the customer story but adds a category claim that neither of the other readings holds. This is a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of three independent estimates.
- ⇒ A single lever, such as "raise the weight on user growth," clears the cases where only the growth condition binds and leaves the intersections standing. The intersections are categorically different compounds (Fig 9 :57: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**).
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if, for a defined population, the three readings converge under more measurement of the same kind with no instrument change, the union reading fails and the partition reading stands.

### Q2 — What is the dependency order?

- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**
- Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**
- Order: 1 perception (can the investor read any customer response?) → 2 vocabulary (are there terms for conversion, retention, expansion and churn?) → 3 objective (what would count as progress in this company's own terms?) → 4 planning (a staged term sheet) → 5 learn and carry (record what paid, carry it to the next deal).
- The middle link is where this question usually breaks. A TAM figure is an ingredient, not a term for progress. Fig 12 :112: **"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."** A vocabulary holding only ingredients can name what a company contains and cannot say how far it has moved.
- ⇒ Effort spent refining a discount rate above the break cannot rescue a missing conversion instrument below it. Most valuation effort lands above the break because that is where the measurements already exist (Fig 3 :29-30).
- **Falsifier:** if a company's realised outcome is predicted as well by discount-rate precision as by conversion-instrument quality across a matched sample, the dependency claim is false for that sample.

### Q3 — Are the three valuation methods isomers, a functional group, or something else?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- *Discounted cash flow* and *comparable multiples* do not share an atom set. DCF holds a forecast horizon, a discount rate and a terminal value; comparables hold a peer set and a multiple. By Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers** as usually written. They share a functional group: each is a comparison of a company against something else (a required return, or a peer), with different qualifiers.
- The stronger, testable version: if both methods are written over the full shared atom set of the investment case (cash, users, retention, price, time, required return), differing only in which atom is bonded to which and under which bond, then they **are** isomers. Same atoms, different arrangement, different realised behaviour. That is the claim worth testing, and it is `specified`, not settled.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either method can be written without an atom the other requires (for example a comparable multiple with no time axis, or a DCF with no peer comparison), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands instead. This is a live strain, named rather than smoothed.

### Q4 — What crosses between a signal and the price?

- Fig 4 :22: **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** A cohort-retention method can be carried from one investment to another; the particular retention number from one company is a recording of one occasion.
- Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."** The difference between a revenue multiple and a user multiple is carried by the bond each builds, not by the ingredient list. A flat scorecard records ingredients and loses the bond.
- Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."** The description (price) and the settlement (actions) are in different units. A system holding one accounting will propose arrangements it cannot afford to test.
- ⇒ The term sheet's tranches are the ordered bond that turns a method into a route. A price without a sequence is a state description, not a plan (Fig 12 :112).

### Q5 — What is the order of the investor's acts?

1. Describe the residual per slot before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**).
2. Bind the target price as an ordinary term so it can accrue a residual and be refused (Formula, "Direction"; Fig 9 :30: **"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"**).
3. Refuse before mint: if a held regularity is false (for example "user growth always converts"), the term owed is a refusal, not a new weighting (Formula step 2).
4. Name the anchor as the realised clearing event and keep it reachable (Fig 10 :29: **"keep the anchor reachable"**).
5. Stage the commitment so the next reading arrives before the whole capital is committed (Fig 3 :20).
6. Carry the method up, never the recording (Fig 4 :22).

## CONCLUSION

The question is a **union**, so per-slot verdicts are kept separate and never averaged. The dependency chain breaks at the vocabulary/objective link, where a company's affordances have no instrument. DCF and comparables are a **functional group** as usually written and a real **isomer** claim only under a fixed shared atom set, with a stated falsifier. A staged term sheet is the ordered bond that turns a price description into a route, and the two currencies of description and settlement are never summed. No figure line returns a weight, a multiple or a price; that remains a declared `gap`.
