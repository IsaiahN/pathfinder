## Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual line alone, before relying on the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency. I deliberately started from different figures than the primary (Fig 2 correlation valence and Fig 12 two currencies rather than Fig 1 and Fig 9) to reduce the chance of a circular route.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The gap between a predicted valuation and a realised payment. Arity: three-place (belief, action, outcome), indexed by slot and time. Symmetry: asymmetric, since payment does not follow from price and "undervalued" is a threshold against a required return. Scale: one deal, a portfolio ranking, a market habitat. It is a union compound: Fig 9 :57 — *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
- **D1, signal frames.** Actors: metrics, narratives, their producers. Currency: readings. Anchor: realised cash conversion and realised clearing (Fig 2 :29). Union of overlapping conditions.
- **D2, the price act.** Actors: investor, model, term sheet. Currency: bits for the description, actions for the settlement. Anchor: the payment the ground makes. Two currencies, non-additive.
- **D3, settlement.** Actors: later rounds, acquirers, auditors, write-offs. Currency: cash and preference. Anchor: the arms-length clearing event. A source-discriminating ground, not a vote.
- **D4, time channel.** Actors: instruments, metrics, information rights. Currency: contact with the ground. Anchor: the ground, which *"does not decay"* while the channel does (Fig 10 :24).
- **X1, "true intrinsic value" as a free-floating number:** no independent anchor, excluded. **X2, "sentiment" as a ground:** updates when pushed, absorbed into D3.

Order by dependency: describe the residual before naming a frame (Fig 9 :27); refuse before mint (Formula step 2); settle each slot before composing the price; keep the anchor reachable (Fig 10 :29).

## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :19 and :22 (per-slot indexing), Fig 2 :29 and :40 (anchor and correlation valence), Fig 9 :54 (union), Fig 12 :125-126 (two currencies).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07` and `09`: index per slot, split the union, use a non-updating anchor, keep two prices apart, stage the commitment, report a shape.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a working investor answer: weight the three signals, choose a discount rate, write a price, stage the tranches.

**R_T is nonzero.** Five constraints in the shorthand do not survive the trip back down:

- **per-slot indexing** collapses to a single weighted score as soon as a price is written;
- **anchor discipline** collapses to "use the latest round price," which is a moving frame;
- **independence** collapses to "ask three people," who usually share one data room;
- **two-currency non-additivity** collapses to one expected-value number;
- **the gap itself** is erased when a precise price is presented without the refuter.

One thing survives cleanly: a signal must be read per slot against a ground that does not move with the reader. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no company ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the shorthand as identical to the figures would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.
