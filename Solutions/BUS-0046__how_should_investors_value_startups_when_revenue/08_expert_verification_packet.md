# Expert verification packet — the Tether on startup valuation under conflicting signals

**What this is:** a claim in a form a valuation researcher or practitioner can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the company-specific number, not `grounded`.** No realised outcome is claimed to settle any part of this.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A startup valuation cannot be settled by blending revenue, user growth and future-market signals into one number. The residual is indexed by slot, so a blend hides the live slot that says where the error is. The conflict is a union of well-formed questions with different instruments, so split it before searching. The anchor is a realised, arms-length clearing event or an audited counter, not the current comparable multiple, which moves with the investing pool. Investor agreement from a shared evidence set is a convention, not corroboration. The description price and the settlement price are two currencies that do not add, so the term sheet's staging is part of the valuation answer. What the framework returns is that shape plus five refuters written in advance. It does not return a price, a weighting or a multiple.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — indexing and union; checkable by reading Figures 1 and 9

- **Statement:** each signal must be tracked as its own slot with its own live residual, and the conflict is a union of overlapping conditions, not one number to average.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 — *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 — *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 9 :54 — *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- **Refuter:** a prospective test in which a blended score preserves the discriminating information of the per-slot readings for two companies with equal blends and different live slots; or a demonstration that the three readings converge under more measurement of the same kind with no instrument or anchor change.

### C2 — anchor and independence; checkable by reading Figures 2 and 7

- **Statement:** the anchor is the realised clearing event, not the live comparable multiple, and agreement among investors sharing one evidence pool carries no verdict weight.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 — *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :30 — *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* Fig 2 :40 — *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* Fig 7 :13 — *"agreement compounds, evidence does not"*
- **What to check:** does the live comparable multiple in the reader's own practice move with pool sentiment? Are the investors whose agreement closes a round drawing on one data room? If both are yes, the claim stands in that practice.
- **Refuter:** a realised clearing price that moves systematically with the first investor's private model revision and nothing about the company; or equal realised outcome accuracy from disjoint-evidence and shared-evidence panels.

### C3 — two currencies and staging; checkable by reading Figure 12

- **Statement:** the description price and the settlement price are in different units and cannot be added; a staged commitment is the ordered bond that buys the missing reading.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :125 — *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 12 :126 — *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."* Fig 12 :112 — *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."*
- **Refuter:** a decision in which description cost and action cost are commensurable in one unit and adding them changes the recommendation; or evidence that staging changes nothing where no intermediate reading can arrive before final settlement.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a price, a multiple, a discount rate or a universal weighting. The company-specific number is a declared `gap` (Fig 9 :32 — *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*; Fig 13 :134 — *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*).
- Not that the three signals should receive equal weight, or that they are independent in fact. Whether they are independent is an empirical question; the claim is that independence must be bought where the cost of being wrong is largest.
- Not that consensus is worthless. Fig 2 :41: *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."* A shared price lets a round close; it simply does not certify the price.
- Not that the live comparable multiple is the market's verdict. It is a frame; the anchor is the realised event.
- Not `grounded`. No realised outcome in this folder settles any claim.

## 5. What would settle it

A realised clearing event, not a further argument. Concretely:

1. **Per-slot test:** equal blended score, different live slots, different realised outcome in the predicted direction.
2. **Independence test:** disjoint-evidence panels beat shared-evidence panels on realised outcomes.
3. **Staging test:** tranches tied to the currently missing instrument reduce realised loss at the same headline price.
4. **Anchor test:** the realised price does not move when the first investor privately changes their model.
5. **Gap-closing test:** a source-discriminating instrument that pays for a specific price before any realised event, or a figure line that returns a numeric valuation rule after re-census.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, :22; Fig 9 :4-5, :54; Fig 2 :29, :30, :40; Fig 12 :125-126. Six short passages.
2. Check C2 against your own practice: is the comparable multiple you use an anchor or a moving frame?
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish a live revenue slot from a live user-growth slot in a pre-revenue company, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
