# Outreach draft: check the measurement claim before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the three sub-claims in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` survive your own check. The offer collapses on contact if the measurement claim fails.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about advertising measurement, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I am writing with one specific output to check against what you already know about advertising measurement. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a company cannot tell "the ad changed this purchase" from "the ad reached someone who would have bought anyway" from exposure, reach, clicks, or attributed conversions. Those are quantities the measurement system itself produces, so they cannot score the system. The two cases produce the same purchase reading and are therefore different substances with the same formula, which is exactly the situation the bond formalism says a flat report hides. What separates them is a comparison of the same consumer slot with and without the ad, where the assignment does not move when the analyst changes their mind about who was likely to buy. In shorthand, the residual is per consumer, never a campaign average, and the two questions, did behavior change and did we reach likely buyers, are different questions with different instruments. A blended ROAS is a pooled reading that destroys both.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular attribution product, platform, or statistical package is the answer, that a holdout must be run on every campaign, or that observational methods are useless. I am claiming the shape a valid measurement must have, not naming the tool that fills it. I am also not claiming that my check here is a ground reading. It is a simulated derivation, and I have run no lift test.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. A statistic computed only from exposure and click logs that predicts, prospectively and per named consumer, whether that consumer's purchase was caused by the ad, verified against a randomised holdout.
> 2. An attribution instrument whose reading provably determines the source, so that a persuaded purchase and an otherwise identical likely-buyer purchase can be separated without a counterfactual assignment.
> 3. A single intervention that both raises incremental purchases and removes exposure to likely buyers in both failure types, leaving no categorically different residue.
> 4. A proof that no holdout or geo design could resolve the counterfactual for a class of campaigns at any budget, which would make it underivable rather than merely underpowered.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Local verbatim figure text and line numbers: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop and the mode rule: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the last link. Run it on a measurement problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> - [name]

## The two-stage offer, plus the optional third stage

1. **Stage 1:** send the residual, the shape claim, and the pre-registered refuter. Ask what they would count as a failure. Record their verdict **before** showing the figures or the essays.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the three source links. The figures are the authority, not this summary. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3, opt-in only:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user states a ground and writes a refuter before running it.

One-to-one only. No bulk send, no automated follow-up, no misleading subject line, and an opt-out line in every message. Public institutional or opt-in channels only, as listed in `03_public_contact_points.csv`.
