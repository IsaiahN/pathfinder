## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree that bureaucracy is good or bad.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **A room is not an anchor.** Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." A list of people chosen because they already lean toward a verdict manufactures the correlation that would make their agreement worthless.
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Fig 2 :41: "efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it." Selecting for receptiveness builds a convention, not a witness.
3. **Asking for trust before a check spends the reader's independence.** Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source."

Legally: scraping personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and will get the sending domain blacklisted. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one.
