# Isomers, catalysts and order in the bureaucracy question

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The main audit says that a rule protects as a method and suppresses as a recording, that an approval chain can be a mirror or a selection chain, and that a rule with no refusal can only accumulate. Three structural questions are owed: (1) are the enabling and coercive forms of formalization isomers over a fixed set of arrangements, (2) is standardization a catalyst or a recording, and (3) what is the order of the union, and which analogue decides which? Each gets its own falsifier.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

- **Atoms held:** a rule, a slot, a ground reading, a frontline actor, an approver, a past success, a novel case. No new primitive is available without import (Fig 6 :21 "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom").
- **Bonds held:** the seven plus negation. Relevant here: `→` sequence, `∥` disjunction, `−` subtraction, `⋛` comparison, `¬` refusal.
- **Currency:** description cost in bits versus action cost at the ground (Fig 12 :125 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add").
- **Anchor:** the unmoved ground at the slot (Fig 2 :29). Agreement is never the anchor.
- **Question type:** union with an intersection; the two forms of formalization overlap in their ingredient list and differ in bond.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Are enabling and coercive formalization isomers? CORRECTION TO MY OWN `05` SECTION 5

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Adler and Borys (1996), read via Lozeve (2026): the two forms share the same ingredients, written values, procedures, instructions, a hierarchy, a measurement system. They differ in the relation those ingredients bear to the operator. Coercive formalization checks compliance and treats deviation as wrong; enabling formalization supports response to real contingencies, explains the rationale, gives global transparency and treats deviation as a learning opportunity.
- Over the fixed atom set {rule, slot, ground reading, operator, deviation}, the two are the same atoms under different bonds. The relation to deviation is the bond, not an extra ingredient. Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance." Fig 12 :46: "A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands. A"
- Therefore they are **isomers** in the figure sense: same inventory, different arrangement, different behaviour. It is not a matter of degree. Adler and Borys: "what is important is not the *degree* of formalization... but its type".
- **I overstated the earlier reading.** "Shape, not a name" governs a missing primitive, not a binding onto an arrangement already available in another closure. Naming the two forms is a provenanced binding from organizational sociology; what is forbidden is claiming the figures derived the adjectives, or that the binding adds an atom. Retracted as applied to these labels; triangulated (README + Fig 12 :117 + Fig 6 :21).

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if enabling and coercive formalization can be shown to have different atom inventories, that is, one requires an element the other demonstrably does not (for example a refusal operator present only in one), the isomer claim fails and they are two different substances in the ordinary sense, not isomers over a shared set.

### Q2: Is standardization a catalyst or a recording?

- Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."
- Fig 4 :25: "A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion. Replayed downward, it produces a system that repeats a past success" and :26 "and cannot produce a new one."
- A standard that survives its own use and is regenerated by each new application (a checklist that is re-derived against the ground each shift) is a catalyst. A standard that is spent on the occasion it was written for and then replayed as if still live is a recording. The ingredient list is identical; the difference is whether the record shows regeneration.
- Fig 12 :119 adds the two-currency underside: "nothing records which a term is." A handbook does not normally record whether a rule was regenerated this shift or last decade. That is the figure-grounded reason a compliance audit cannot tell a catalyst from a recording without a fresh ground reading.
- HRO corroborates from a different field. Christianson et al. (2011): "reliability is fundamentally a dynamic set of properties, activities, and responses" and "high reliability is an ongoing process, not a state of achievement." A rule set treated as a completed state has become a recording.
- **Falsifier:** if a handbook can be shown, from its own record and without a fresh ground reading, to distinguish a catalyst from a spent recording at every rule, the instrument gap named here is closed and this sub-derivation is narrowed. Status `specified`, single-source for the catalyst reading (Fig 12 :119), low-overlap corroboration from reliability science.

### Q3: What is the order of the union?

- Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :56: "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."
- The two effects (guard, reach) are not ordered by importance but by dependency (Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step"). The guard must be settled before the reach is composed, because a second-order comparison over unsettled first-order molecules is not legal under Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."
- Order by dependency, stated: (1) refuse a false held rule before minting a new one (Formula step 2); (2) settle the guard at the slot against a non-updating anchor (Fig 2 :29); (3) only then compose the cross-room check; (4) only then judge whether a method or a recording crossed (Fig 4 :22).
- A lever applied at step 4 when step 1 has not run produces "motion, no progress" (Fig 7 :32 "→ one long room: motion, no progress"). This is the figure-grounded reason training and culture programs applied before the refusal question is settled often fail.
- **Falsifier:** show a firm whose refusal channel is absent and whose cross-room method transfer nevertheless produces a settled guard at the slot, measured at the ground. The dependency order would be wrong.

### Q4: The approval chain as cycle, and the sign of the loop

- Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
- An approval route whose output (the signed decision) re-enters as input to the same room at the next occasion is a cycle. Its sign decides the behaviour. A negative feedback cycle against an external ground closes and corrects. A positive feedback cycle against the room's own previous decision amplifies and drifts. Fig 2 :34: "If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."
- Fig 7 :36: "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced." A chain differs from a cycle: a chain cannot close by construction, so it must be fed by a selection between differing rooms (Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes").
- The analogue is deliberate: an approval hierarchy is not a chain of command for epistemic purposes until the rooms differ. Until then it is one long room with many signatures.
- **Falsifier:** show an approval route whose rooms share one evidence pool and whose cycle is nevertheless driven by a non-updating external ground at every signature. The mirror finding would be narrowed. Status `specified`, triangulated across Fig 12 :121, Fig 7 :35-36 and Fig 2 :34.

### Q5: Functional group, and what is shared

- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- A protective arrangement and a suppressive arrangement share the functional group "formalized decision procedure" (state, rule, actor, output). The qualifier, the bond to the ground and to deviation, decides which member and therefore which outcome. Fig 11 :50: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."
- Two chains built from different links may compose functionally identical terms, and the converse holds too: Fig 13 :121 "Different routes can arrive at the same substance." Fig 13 :124-125: "holds too: the same ingredients under a different bond are a different substance."
- This is why "bureaucracy" cannot be the unit of analysis. It names the functional group, not the member. The member is decided by the bond, and the bond is decided by the residual.
- **Falsifier:** show that the bond to the ground and to deviation carries no information beyond the ingredient list, so two formalizations with different bonds have the same behaviour at the ground. Fig 12 :5 "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording" would be broken.

## CONCLUSION

Over a fixed atom set, enabling and coercive formalization are isomers: same inventory, different bond, different behaviour (Q1). Standardization is a catalyst when it survives its own use and is regenerated against the ground, and a recording when it is replayed as a spent transcript; the handbook does not normally record which, so an instrument is owed (Q2). The union is ordered by dependency, refuse, settle the guard, compose the room, judge the crossing, and a lever applied out of order produces motion without progress (Q3). An approval route is a cycle whose sign is set by whether the reference updates, and without differing rooms it is one long room (Q4). "Bureaucracy" names a functional group, not a member; the valence is decided by the bond to the ground and the residual (Q5).

The correction to `05` section 5 is retained: naming forms already available in another closure is a provenanced binding, not a minted primitive and not a forbidden name.
