# Outreach draft: check the output before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and its refuter at the top of this draft survive the recipient's own check. If the stage-1 output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.

---

**Subject:** one claim about rules and error, with the failure condition written first

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output fails, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a rule protects a company from error only while it keeps a reference that does not move when the company's own reports move. It suppresses initiative and adaptation when the rule stops being a checkable coordination and becomes the outcome, when the report of the ground saturates and the seat loses touch, when every approver reads the same evidence and agrees, or when the rule is a recording of one past success replayed into a changed situation. The two effects are a union of overlapping conditions, not two ends of one dial, so I am not claiming a right amount of bureaucracy.
>
> **What would refute it, written before I sent this:**
> 1. show that protection and suppression are one measurable function with one optimum at the ground;
> 2. show a control system whose reference updates on its own reports and which still keeps an unmoved ground reachable at the slot;
> 3. show a formal rule whose effect on error and on adaptation is invariant across residuals;
> 4. show a replayed recording of one occasion producing a new capability in a changed habitat;
> 5. show an approval chain drawing on one evidence pool whose agreement adds evidence about the world;
> 6. show a rule set with no refusal operator that removes a false rule without adding a refusal and without losing reach.
>
> If any one holds, the corresponding part of the claim is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that less bureaucracy is always better; that standardization is the enemy of safety (the reliability literature I read says the opposite, and I take that seriously); that any particular span of control or rule count is optimal; that a compliance score measures safety. Nor am I claiming to settle the question. This is a shape with a refuter, not a result.
>
> **What I would count as your stage-1 verdict:** whichever of the six refuters you think is closest to firing in real organizations, and why. Please record that before reading any framework material, because once you have the framework's interior your agreement stops being evidence.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Arrival of the Fittest: The Tether Architecture: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures folder. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Signed, [name]

## Stage 2 and stage 3 (opt-in only)

- **Stage 2:** if and only if the recipient records a stage-1 verdict and asks for more, send the three links. Their later agreement with the theory is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool").
- **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse. Each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode (Formula "Direction": a frame "cannot check the direction of its own question, because the shape a frame is searching for is not a term it holds").

One-to-one contact only. No bulk send, no automation, no misleading subject line, and an opt-out line in every message.
