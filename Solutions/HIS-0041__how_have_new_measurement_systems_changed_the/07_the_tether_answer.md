# The Tether's answer: how new measurement systems change categories

**Mode: `general` on the separator, `specified` on each domain verdict, `gap` on the enumeration, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a historical result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The ordinary question asks for a historical list of categories that measurement changed. The residual behind it is a three-way ambiguity in the word "changed." A new reading can fill a slot in the category vocabulary, can mint a new category, can refuse a held one, or can change the contact of the things classified so that the reading itself changes. Those are different operations with different anchors, and a single "impact of measurement" verdict would average them into nothing. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."

## 2. The separator

1. **A measurement extends what can be represented; it does not compose its way to a new category.** Fig 6 :26: "Composition explores what the operators reach; instruments extend what can be represented at all." Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." So the first effect of a new measurement system is on the *perception* link of the dependency chain, not on the vocabulary link. A category change is a second step, with its own guards.

2. **The category change is an ordered structure over a state, not a label pasted on a thing.** Fig 13 :20: "ordering, so more and less can be said." Fig 13 :75: "Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state." A scale is the structure that must sit between a physical state and the categories applied to it. A categorization is a threshold cut in that ordering, not a symmetric join.

3. **A new category is a mint only if three guards pass and the bargain pays.** Support: some slot carries positive mass. Reachability: the term composes from atoms already held. Novelty: it is not already in the library. And cost(φ) plus what remains unexplained must be less than what was unexplained before (Fig 5, formula step 3). Otherwise the instrument produced a finer reading inside the old category, not a new category. Where a held term keeps being wrong without trending down, what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation (formula step 2; Operators table :58).

4. **The anchor is set by what the question is constitutively about, so the three domains have different anchors.** Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Fig 2 :37: "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people." For a physical state the instrument can be the anchor; for a person it is only an instrument, and "instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure" (Fig 2 :38). For social counts the anchor is conditional on whether a non-updating ground is reachable.

5. **For people, installing a category changes contact.** Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." The classified answer back, so the reading can change. Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." A category that changes no contact is a relabeling, not a category change in this domain.

6. **For social life, a count is a convention until it decides an outcome.** Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." The other failure is channel decay: "the ground does not decay, the channel does" (Fig 10 :24), so a changing category system detaches from a ground that did not move. The census record shows both: categories added and removed across enumerations, and a published warning that data are not directly comparable across the change.

7. **Old and new categories are isomers when the atoms are fixed and only the bond changed.** Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Where the instrument added a new ordering, the relation is composition plus import, not isomerism (Fig 6 :26).

8. **A cross-time comparison is valid only with the lineage.** Fig 13 :130: "A term carries the atoms it was built from and the bonds that joined them. That record is not documentation of the term. It is the term." A category label without its slot, instrument, mint or refusal, anchor, and date is not checkable, and a comparison that ignores the lineage is dead reckoning (Fig 4 :30).

## 3. Per-domain verdicts, kept separate

| domain | actors / currency / anchor | verdict | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 nature | instruments and physical states; readings in a declared scale; a non-updating fixed point | measurement extends representation, then a mint or refusal under the guards; old and new categories are isomers if atoms are fixed | `specified`, triangulated (Fig 6 + Fig 13 + Fig 5) |
| D2 people | classified people, classifiers, tests; assigned kinds plus consequences; human judgment about people | the instrument is not the anchor; installing a category changes contact and the classified answer back; a no-contact change is only a relabeling | `specified`, triangulated (Fig 2 + Fig 11 + Fig 12) |
| D3 social life | agencies and counts; counts and their categories; conditional on a non-updating ground | a count becomes a verdict when it decides an outcome; channel decay breaks comparability when categories move and the ground does not | `specified`, triangulated (Fig 10 + Fig 2 + Fig 9) |

No verdict is averaged with another. Compounds (medical thermometry, census-as-measurement) get their own loop and anchor test.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that measurement always improves categories. Precision is not validity, and a reliable test can be wrong on the axis that matters (Fig 2 :38).
- Not that a number is a category. A reading against a nominal scale is a classification; a reading against an ordering is a measurement; they are different bonds.
- Not that the figures can name a future category or produce a historical list. That is the declared `gap` (Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :135).
- Not that reclassification subtracts a category. Nothing is deleted; a refusal is added and the reach falls (Fig 6 :42).
- Not that more confirming cases help. "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict" (Fig 9 :60).
- Not grounded. No instrument, archive reading, or measurement was used to settle any claim here. This is a simulated derivation.

## 5. What would settle it - the ground

A ground reading, not agreement. Concretely:

- **Support test:** for a claimed category change, exhibit the slot with positive mass and the reading that failed to resolve. If no slot is live, the claim is empty (Fig 1 :20-21).
- **Mint test:** show the three guards and the MDL bargain, with the lineage and the date. If the category does not compose from held atoms or costs more than the confusion it removes, it is not a mint.
- **Anchor test:** state what the question is constitutively about in each domain. For people, show where human judgment, not the test, settled the verdict. For social life, show that the count did not decide the outcome it was reporting on.
- **Contact test:** show whether the classification changed any behavior or institution. If not, it was a relabeling.
- **Comparability test:** run the round trip across the category change and measure `R_T` (Fig 4 :30). If the gap is assumed zero, the comparison is dead reckoning.
- **Standing refuters:** the ten conditions in `01` section 4, written before the conclusion.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable, for the historical list; underivable for the names.** The figures deliver a separator with three domain verdicts, a mint/refusal test, an anchor rule, and a lineage requirement. They stop at the limit of what can be derived without an instrument. "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" (Fig 13 :134). The specific categories a future instrument will produce cannot be named by a system that does not already hold them, and a complete historical census is a budget question, not a principle question. Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." The record stops here, with the lineage written so a checker can locate the error.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give you a list of categories that measurement changed. It gives you a separator that any such list must satisfy. A new instrument first extends what can be represented; it does not compose its way to a new category. The category change that follows is one of three things: a mint, which needs a live slot, atoms already held, novelty, and a description that costs less than the confusion it removes; a refusal, because a held term kept being wrong; or a contact change, because classifying people changed the people and the reading with them. The anchor differs by domain: for physical states an instrument can be the anchor, for people it is human judgment, and for social counts the anchor survives only while the count does not decide the outcome it reports. Old and new categories over the same atoms are isomers, threshold cuts on a new ordering, not replacements of one label by another. And a comparison across time is valid only if the category's lineage travels with it. The framework returns a shape, not a name, and this is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency, not a ground reading. What would settle it is an instrument, an archive, or a board, not a longer list of people who agree.
