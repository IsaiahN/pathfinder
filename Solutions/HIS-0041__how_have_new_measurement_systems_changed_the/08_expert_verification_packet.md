# Expert verification packet - the Tether on measurement and categories

**What this is:** a claim in a form a historian of science, a historian of quantification, or a measurement scientist can check. Mode, derivation, and refuters are stated before the conclusion so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the separator, `specified` on each domain verdict, `gap` on the enumeration, not `grounded`.** No instrument, archive reading, or measurement is claimed as having settled this. This is a shape and a route, not a historical result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A new measurement system does not change categories by rearranging an old vocabulary. It first extends what can be represented, and the category change that follows is one of three operations: a mint (new term, with support, reachability, novelty, and a cost bargain), a refusal (a held term kept being wrong, so a negation is added and the reach falls), or a contact change (a classification of people changed the people and the reading with them). The anchor is set by what the question is constitutively about, so it differs between physical states, people, and social counts. Over a fixed atom set an old category and a new one are isomers: same atoms, different bond, different substance. A cross-time comparison is valid only when the category's lineage travels with it. The figures return this shape, not the names of the new categories.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 6, 5, and 13

- **Statement:** an instrument extends representation; a new category is a separate mint with guards; the product is an ordering over a state.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :26 "Composition explores what the operators reach; instruments extend what can be represented at all." Fig 6 :21 "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Fig 13 :20 "ordering, so more and less can be said." Fig 5 :43 "A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before."
- **Refuter:** a new category produced by pure composition with no extension of representation; or a stable category system partitioning a continuum with no ordering over any state; or a category admitted with zero support, no reachability, no novelty, or a cost above the confusion it removes. If any holds, C1 fails.

### C2 - people and social life; checkable against the anchor and contact tests

- **Statement:** for a question about people the anchor is human judgment, not the test; for a social count the convention becomes a verdict the moment it decides an outcome; a classification that changes no contact is only a relabeling.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :36 "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Fig 2 :37 "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people." Fig 2 :38 "It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure." Fig 10 :47 "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." Fig 11 :40 "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
- **What to check:** in a case you know (a diagnostic category, a census race category, an index used to allocate funds), identify the anchor, ask whether the instrument decided the outcome it was reporting, and ask whether the classified changed after the category was installed. Does a no-contact case exist that still deserves to be called a category change?
- **Refuter:** a measurement of people that settles a constitutively human question without human judgment and does not move when the classified change; or a count that decides an outcome yet remains a checkable convention with a non-updating ground and no saturated or pooled reading. If either holds, C2 fails.

### C3 - structural relation and order; checkable by reading Figures 12, 3, and 6

- **Statement:** old and new categories over the same atoms are isomers; reclassification is negation, not subtraction; the dependency order is perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Operators table :58 "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate." Fig 6 :42 "What is recorded only grows. What is reachable is derived, and it can fall. A refutation adds a term that refuses; it removes nothing, and the reach falls because something was added rather than because anything was deleted." Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
- **Refuter:** a category removed from a vocabulary where the record truly deleted the old term and the reach did not fall; or a case where a category change preceded any extension of representation and settled by the ground; or a case where old and new categories share no atoms yet are claimed as isomers. If any holds, C3 fails.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a historical list of categories and not a ranking of which measurement mattered most. That is a declared `gap` (Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself").
- Not that measurement improves categories, and not that a reliable instrument is valid on the axis that matters.
- Not that a number is a category. Classification is measurement against a nominal scale; an ordering is a different structure.
- Not that reclassification deletes an old term. Nothing is deleted; a refusal is added.
- Not that more confirming examples help. Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

## 5. What would settle it

An instrument, an archive, or a board, not agreement.

1. **Support test:** exhibit the slot with positive mass and the reading that failed to resolve. No live slot means no category change.
2. **Mint test:** exhibit the three guards, the cost bargain, and the lineage with a date.
3. **Anchor test:** state what the question is constitutively about; for people, show where human judgment rather than the test settled the verdict; for a social count, show that the count did not decide its own outcome.
4. **Contact test:** show whether the classification changed any behavior or institution.
5. **Round trip:** carry a category across the change and measure `R_T` rather than assuming it is zero (Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked.").
6. **Standing refuters:** the ten conditions in `01` section 4, written before this packet.
