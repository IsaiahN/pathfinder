# Outreach draft - check the separator first, then decide about the theory

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until a checker has been offered the residual and the refuter with no figure text attached. If the separator does not survive stage 1, there is nothing to send in stage 2.

---

**Subject:** Would you check one claim about measurement and categories before I send you anything else?

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a question in the history of measurement: how new measurement systems changed the categories societies use for people, nature, and social life. I have one small output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on each of three domains, not `grounded`):**
>
> The usual story says that better measurement gave us better categories. That is one operation among three, and the figures I am using keep them apart. A new instrument first *extends what can be represented*. Then one of three things can happen:
>
> 1. **Nature, physical kinds.** A new term is minted only if the new reading has real mass in some slot, composes from terms the vocabulary already holds, and is genuinely new, and only if stating it costs less than the confusion it removes. Otherwise the old category stays. This is a structural prediction, not a claim that any particular instrument did it.
> 2. **People.** The anchor is not the test or the census. Because the question is constitutively about people, the anchor is human judgment about people; the instrument is only an instrument and can be right on precision and wrong on the axis that matters. Installing a category of people changes contact, so the people classified can change, and the reading can change with them. A category that changes nothing is only a relabeling.
> 3. **Social life.** A count is a shared convention until it decides an outcome; at that point it is no longer a convention but a verdict, and the reference starts updating on the frame. Separate failure: the ground does not decay while the categories and reporting rules do, so the reading detaches from what it named.
>
> The effect is indexed per category slot, never as a global "society became more quantitative." The three domains overlap, so I keep three verdicts instead of averaging them. And I do not claim the framework can tell you *which* categories a future instrument will produce. It returns a shape, not a name.
>
> **What would refute it, written before this message:**
>
> 1. A new measurement changes a category while leaving representation unchanged, with no extension of what can be represented.
> 2. A stable category system partitions a continuum with no ordering over any state, or a category composes state-to-state with no intervening structure.
> 3. A category is admitted with no positive-mass slot, or fails the reach or novelty tests, or costs more than the confusion it removes.
> 4. A measurement of people settles a constitutively human question without human judgment as anchor, and does not move when the classified change.
> 5. A classification of people changes no contact and returns an identical reading before and after.
> 6. A count decides an outcome while remaining a checkable convention with a non-updating ground.
> 7. A genuine category change has zero residual in every slot.
> 8. One lever changes all three domains identically, including their anchors.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any specific historical case is settled by this; that measurement always improves categories; that a number is the same thing as a category; that the figures can name the next category; that a longer list of confirming examples would make the claim true. My own notes are a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency, not a ground reading, so they cannot settle the question they report on.
