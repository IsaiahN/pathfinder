## Wider literature that bears on the question

The sources above split into three low-overlap pools, which is why their agreement carries more than the framework essays alone would:

1. **Instrumentation and physical science** (History of measurement, Thermometer, Quantification (science)): a scale has to exist before the modern concept of temperature arises; classification is measurement against a nominal scale.
2. **Human kinds and psychometrics** (Ian Hacking, Intelligence quotient, Race and ethnicity in the US census): classifications of people interact with the classified; a score can be reliable and still not be a concrete measure; census categories change and break comparability.
3. **Quantification and public statistics** (Porter, Desrosières, Quantification (science) social-science section): public statistics are constructed reality; categories are created to describe society and then tracked with those definitions; numbers can change the world as well as describe it.

Overlap declared: Porter and Desrosières both work on the sociology of quantification and share a 1980s to 2000s evidence pool; they count as one witness for the "numbers change the world" claim, not two. The Hacking page and the IQ page share the human-sciences literature and also count as one pool for the looping claim. The instrumentation pool is genuinely low overlap with the other two. The Wikipedia pages are tertiary digests of the underlying scholarship, so they are used for the shape of the claims and not as primary authority for any disputed date; the underlying books and articles are named so a checker can go to them.
