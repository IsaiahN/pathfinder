## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :135: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

From *The Library of Babel* (carried): *"You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question the shape is the three-branch separator: an instrument extends what can be represented, then a mint, a refusal, or a contact change follows, with the anchor set by what the question is constitutively about. The names of the categories themselves, for example which racial categories a future census will adopt or which diagnostic threshold a future test will introduce, are outside the shape.

This matters because the question is phrased historically, "how have new measurement systems changed the categories." A historical list is always a filled answer. The figures can tell you what any such change must look like; they cannot tell you the list.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel* (carried): use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause." For this question, describe the change in effect terms first: some slot stopped resolving; an ordering became available; a count began deciding an outcome. Only then pick a historical case.
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* (carried): *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."*
- *Library of Babel* on stopping (carried): the answer is reached when *"the finish line will also be the starting place"* and by then you have *"composed the answer through the process of locating it."* In this solution that appears as the separator being both the starting residual and the final answer, with the same refuters written before and after.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." From the essay opened this session: *"The Tether ties alignment to the ground. Not to human preferences. Not to a reward model trained on human feedback. To the thing that settles a question regardless of what any frame thinks. An outcome. A measurement. A board that pays or does not."* Note the symmetry for this question: a measurement can be the ground for a physical state and only an instrument for a person. Fig 2 :36-38 makes the same distinction.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*They're Scanning Books* (carried): *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space."* Also: *"A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six."* And: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."*

The consequence for this question: when a historian names the categories a society used, that is a binding from the historical record onto held atoms, not a derivation from the figures and not an import that moves a wall. It must carry its provenance or it is a source named from a reading.

## 4. The metamerism error, and why it governs the people domain

*The Hard Problem is About Source* (carried) gives the mechanism. An instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source"*; one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered can all produce the same reading. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* And *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."* It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved it.

Applied here: a test score is a reading. It cannot comment on the source. Several different states of a person, and several different social histories, can produce the same score. Naming the category of person from the score is the metamerism error. What is owed is a discriminator, not a name. The IQ history makes the same point in a different vocabulary: the score is an estimate, and "a concrete measure of intelligence cannot be achieved given the abstract nature of the concept."

## 5. Correction: what "shape, not a name" actually forbids

I overstated the rule in an earlier reading and record the correction here rather than erasing it. The README's phrase is *"what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Its subject is a primitive not held in Γ. Naming a binding onto an atom already held is not forbidden; what is forbidden is claiming that the binding adds a primitive or moves the wall (Fig 6 :26: instruments extend representation; Fig 6 :21: mint composes inside the closure and cannot add an atom).

For this question the correction means: the figures can be used to derive the shape of a category change (support, ordering, mint or refusal, anchor, per-slot residual) without any ban on discussing real historical categories. The ban applies to claiming that the framework *generated* the name of a category it does not hold. The list of actual categories comes from the historical record, provenanced as such.

My metamerism point in section 4 is **not** retracted. It still says that a source named from a reading, where several sources could give the reading, is an error unless a discriminator exists. The reason is the missing discriminator, not a blanket ban on names. Both claims are kept so neither is quietly lost.

## 6. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** From the essay opened this session: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly."* And the fuller passage: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."* A campaign that gathers famous historians to agree with the separator is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask is the opposite: give the claim a budget to be tried and let an instrument, an archive, or a board settle it.

**Agreement is not ground truth.** Same essay: *"You may all agree and be wrong. You may set your clock to someone else's, who was setting your clock to yours, and be off by many deviations over time. You need ground truth to define where you stand."* This is Fig 2 :13-21 and Fig 2 :35 in prose: mutual update and a shared pool produce convergence, not correction.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay, directly quoted: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post. It will often be better than the generic outputs that you will usually get otherwise too (intelligence is essentially arrangement). However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* And: *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself. An external validator, or ground, is always needed to tether the veracity of the output to reality. Anything else is drift."* So `01` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is not a test of the claim and must be labelled as such.

## 7. What this question's shape is, in one line

The shape is a **separator with three domain verdicts and a lineage requirement**: an instrument extends what can be represented, a category change is then a mint, a refusal, or a contact change, the anchor is set by what the question is constitutively about, the effect is indexed per slot, and a cross-time comparison is valid only if the category's lineage is carried with it. No specific future category is named. That is a shape, not a name.
