## 0. The residual in effect terms

The question is about a change of vocabulary, not about better numbers inside an old vocabulary. Let Γ be the category vocabulary a society can currently use (its library of kinds). A new measurement system is an instrument that returns readings that the old vocabulary did not resolve. The residual R is the gap between what Γ predicts should be seen and what the instrument returns. Three things can happen, and they are different operations:

1. **The gap has no mass in any slot.** The instrument resolves the same distinctions more cheaply. Nothing is owed. This is the "held" case of Fig 5.
2. **The gap has mass in a slot and a new term composes from atoms already held.** A category is minted: support, reachability, and novelty all pass, and the new term must cost less than the confusion it removes (Fig 5, formula step 3).
3. **A held term is false and keeps being wrong without trending down.** What is owed is a refusal, not an explanation. Formula step 2 names this the REFUTED bin; Operators table :58 says a refutation is a negation, "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."

A fourth possibility matters for the "how have" part of the question. When the measured thing is a person, the classification can change the classified. The new category does not merely describe a slot; it changes the contact of the people in it, and the ground then returns a different reading. That is a contact change (Fig 11), and it is why the people domain cannot be handled with the same anchor as the nature domain.

**What would count as an answer: the sought-for shape bound as an ordinary term before the run.** The answer is a *separator*, written down now so it can accrue a residual like anything else: it must (a) say which of the three domains a new instrument changes by supplying support for a mint, (b) which it changes by altering contact so that the classified answer back, and (c) which it changes by turning a reachable ground into a convention that decides an outcome. The separator must keep three verdicts separate. Any answer that returns one number, one "impact of measurement," or one list of new categories has already failed this binding.

## 1. Domain census (before any figure line is chosen)

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity.** Three named domains (people, nature, social life) plus the measurement system as an actor that acts on all three. The question is a **union**, not a partition: one lever cannot clear all three because the anchor differs by domain. Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- **Symmetry.** A measurement returns an ordering over a state, and a category is a cut in that ordering. The relevant bond is ordered/comparative (⋛, a threshold relation), not a symmetric join. Fig 13 :20: "ordering, so more and less can be said." A category boundary is a threshold, not a conjunction.
- **Scale.** Three links of one dependency chain: instrument at the perception link, category at the vocabulary link, social consequence at the habitat link (Fig 3 :4-8, Fig 11 :43-48). The middle link is the one that usually breaks, because it has the fewest instruments (Fig 3 :25-27).

### 1.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

**D1, nature (physical kinds).** Actors: instruments, standards, physical states, observers. Currency: readings in a declared scale, paid in actions to calibrate. Anchor: a fixed point that does not move when a frame changes its mind (melting point of ice, a defined constant). This is a nameable anchor, so D1 is a domain. Question type: a partition boundary question, which compositions are legal over held atoms.

**D2, people (human kinds).** Actors: classified people, classifiers, tests, censuses, institutions. Currency: assigned kinds and the consequences that follow from them. Anchor: human judgment, because the question is constitutively about people. Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." Fig 2 :37: "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people." The instrument itself is not the anchor here; it is an instrument, and "instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure" (Fig 2 :38). This is a nameable anchor, so D2 is a domain. Question type: union of mint, refusal, and contact change.

**D3, social life (collections and institutions).** Actors: agencies, counts, reporting rules, programs that consume the counts. Currency: counts and the categories that organize them. Anchor: conditional. A count is anchored when the question is constitutively about something that does not update on the count (a market, a patient outcome, a legal compliance requirement). Where there is no such ground, the count becomes a convention that decides an outcome, and Fig 10 :47 says "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one." Where the anchor is absent, D3 is not yet a domain in the strong sense; it is a frame trying to locate an anchor. Recorded as a union condition with an anchor test, not silently treated as settled.

### 1.3 Partition or union

Union. D1, D2, and D3 overlap where a physical instrument is used on people (temperature as a medical sign), where a count is used as a physical fact (a census count as a population measurement), and where both feed a program. Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." An overlap is a compound with its own loop and its own ground. The audit therefore refuses the single verdict "measurement changed categories"; it returns three verdicts plus the compounds named where they occur.

### 1.4 Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. **Perception:** the instrument must already be returning something that fails to resolve (Fig 6 :34-35: "the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there").
2. **Vocabulary:** the society must have terms for what the reading affords (Fig 3 :7: "it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked").
3. **Objective:** it must be able to say what would count as progress in its own terms (Fig 3 :10).
4. **Planning:** a route chosen under incomplete information.
5. **Learn and carry:** name what worked, verify it, carry it (Fig 3 :15).

The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance: Fig 3 :20 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Most historical commentary lands on link 1 (new instrument) or link 5 (new classifications adopted), because those have obvious tests. The break is usually between 2 and 3, where a society can name the new reading and still have no term for what would count as progress. Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

## 3. Findings

### F1 - A new measurement extends representation; it does not rearrange what is already held (decisive for D1)

Fig 6 :26: "Composition explores what the operators reach; instruments extend what can be represented at all."

Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."

The question's ordinary phrasing, "new measurement systems changed the categories," hides two operations that the figures keep apart. Composition rearranges the atoms already in Γ; it never adds one. An instrument extends what Γ can represent at all. So a new measurement does not produce a new category by composing harder inside the old vocabulary. It makes a previously unrepresentable reading available, and only then can a mint be attempted. This is why the answer is not "measurement rearranged our categories" but "measurement extended the representable, and a mint or refusal then became possible."

Fig 6 :29: "inward: is our representation adequate to hold it? The instrument extends access."

**Mode: `specified`.** Not `grounded`: no instrument was built or read here.

**Refuter:** show a new category produced by pure composition from held atoms, with no extension of what can be represented, and with the old instrument still returning the same reading. That would collapse the distinction and falsify this finding.

### F2 - The product is an ordering over a state; the figure forbids a bare state-to-state bond

Fig 13 :20: "ordering, so more and less can be said."

Fig 13 :74: "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."

Fig 13 :75: "Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."

A measurement does not attach a number to a bare thing. It produces a spectrum, a structure, over a state. "Hot" and "cold" become degrees only when hotness is ordered on a scale; a temperature is not "more hot" until a spectrum exists. The thermometer passage in the world sources states the same thing in historical terms: a thermoscope has no scale and the modern concept of temperature had not yet arisen; adding a scale is what makes it a thermometer. The figure governs the shape: a new measurement supplies a structure in the middle, not a new label pasted on a state.

**Mode: `general` for the cross-kind rule, `specified` for the claim about a given instrument.**

**Refuter:** exhibit a stable category system that partitions a continuum without an ordering over any state, or exhibit a category composed state-to-state with no intervening structure and show the framework must admit it. Either breaks the finding.

### F3 - A new category is a mint, and the guards are a product, not a checklist

Fig 5 :21: "the gap has real mass somewhere." Fig 5 :23: "it composes from what we already have." Fig 5 :25: "it is not already in the library."

Formula step 3: "gated by a product; any factor at zero forces inertness."

Fig 5 :43: "A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before."

For a category change to be a mint:
- **SUPPORT** - some slot carries positive mass. The new reading fails to resolve under a held category; it is not merely finer detail.
- **REACHABILITY** - the new category composes from atoms the vocabulary already holds. A completely alien category is not a mint; it needs an import.
- **NOVELTY** - the term is not already in the library. Relabeling an existing category is not a new category.
- **BARGAIN** - stating the new category plus what remains unexplained costs less than the confusion it removes.

This is a strict standard. Most historical cases of "a new measurement changed a category" satisfy support and novelty but fail the bargain or the reach test until a whole instrumentation plus a community of use has arrived. That is why the mint typically happens at a later date than the instrument, and why the lineage, not the instrument alone, is the proof (Fig 13 :127: "The array cannot be checked by inspection, so the record is what makes it verifiable").

**Mode: `specified`.**

**Refuter:** a category admitted and used with no positive-mass slot, or one that does not compose from held atoms yet is claimed as a mint, or one whose statement costs more than the confusion it removes without any compensating settlement.

### F4 - The people domain has a different anchor, and the instrument is not it (decisive for D2)

Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."

Fig 2 :37: "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."

Fig 2 :38: "It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."

This is the single most important difference between the three domains. For a physical state, the instrument can be the anchor because the question is constitutively about the state the instrument ranges against. For a person, the question "is this the right category for this person" is about people, so human judgment is the anchor and the test or census is only an instrument. It can be precise, reliable, and wrong on the axis that matters. This is re-grounding, not a refusal of measurement: a test can supply support for a mint, and a diagnosis can be settled by an outcome, but the instrument's own reading cannot settle whether the category is good for a person.

The IQ history corroborates the shape in a low-overlap field: test scores are described as estimates, with "a concrete measure of intelligence cannot be achieved given the abstract nature of the concept," and the same page records how a score became a category with institutional force and how eugenic programs used it. Fig 2 :33-34: "The anchor must not update. If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1." An instrument whose category is adopted because people change under it is not a still anchor.

**Mode: `specified`.** The anchor claim is figure-derived and triangulated across Fig 2 and Fig 10; the historical application is single-source per world page and is not upgraded.

**Refuter:** show a measurement of people that settles a question constitutively about people without any appeal to human judgment, and where the reading does not move when the classified change their behavior.

### F5 - In the people domain the classification changes contact, and the classified answer back

Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."

Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

A new category of people is not a neutral copy of a pre-existing division. Installing the category introduces an actor into a habitat: clinics, schools, programs, and the people themselves now range against it. Contact changes, so the ground returns different readings, so the category can become self-confirming or self-refuting. The philosopher whose work is cited in the world sources calls this the looping effect of human kinds: classifications of people interact with the phenomena classified. Fig 11 :32: "introduce, never subtract" - the introduction is available and exclusion is not, so a category once installed must be tracked for what it brought with it.

This also gives the honest reason the people domain needs its own loop. In D1, an incorrect reading can be corrected by a better instrument pointed at the same state. In D2, a better instrument may change the state, because the state is a person who has met the category.

**Mode: `specified`.** Triangulated across Fig 11 and Fig 2; the looping phenomenon itself is world-corroborated by Hacking, not figure-derived.

**Refuter:** exhibit a classification of people that changes no contact, produces no behavioral or institutional consequence, and leaves the ground reading identical before and after. Such a case would be a mere relabeling, not a category change in the sense of this audit.

### F6 - In social life, a count can stop being a convention and become a verdict

Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."

Fig 10 :48: "a convention nothing can check is a constant the seat authored, carrying the seat's authority."

Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does."

Fig 10 :26: "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings."

A social statistic is a convention: a shared meaning that lets different agencies coordinate. It becomes corrupt as a verdict the moment the count decides an outcome it was only supposed to describe, because then the reference updates on the frame and coherent drift begins (Fig 2 :34). Channel failure is the other mode: the ground does not decay while the categories, instruments, and reporting rules change, so the reading no longer reaches what it named. The census history corroborates both: racial categories were added and removed across enumerations, self-identification replaced enumerator assignment, and the Census Bureau itself warns that 2000 race data are not directly comparable with earlier censuses. That non-comparability is a channel failure with a non-updating legal ground (the Voting Rights Act compliance requirement) still in place.

**Mode: `specified` on the mechanism, `general` on the convention/verdict distinction.**

**Refuter:** show a count that decides an outcome while remaining a checkable convention with a ground that does not update on it, and with no saturated metric or pooled reading. If that is possible, the distinction drawn here is wrong.

### F7 - The effect is per category slot, never a global average

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."

Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

A measurement system does not change "categories" in general. It changes a particular slot: the 1890 census introduced a new distinction among Asian groups; a new diagnostic threshold changes one boundary; a standardized temperature scale replaces one ordering of hotness. A global claim such as "measurement made society more quantitative" averages away the only signal that says where to look. Fig 1 :20-21: "Support is satisfied when some slot carries positive mass, so a global residual near zero with one live slot is a legal state and not an inert one." So a society can look largely unchanged while one slot is on fire.

**Mode: `general` for the indexing rule, `specified` for any particular historical slot.**

**Refuter:** produce a genuine change in the category system whose residual is identically zero in every slot. By the figure's own definition there is no such change, so this refuter is a structural check rather than an empirical one.

### F8 - The question is a union; one lever leaves the intersections standing

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."

Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

The three domains overlap: a medical thermometer is a physical instrument operating on a person inside an institution. Applying one lever (say, "better instruments") clears the region where only the physical condition holds and leaves the contact and convention intersections standing. The honest output is therefore three verdicts plus named compounds, never an averaged "how measurement changed categories."

**Mode: `general` for the union reading.**

**Refuter:** show a single measurement lever that changes all three domains identically, including the anchor conditions, so that the union collapses to a partition.

### F9 - No figure line returns the specific new categories; this is a declared gap

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."

Fig 13 :135: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"

The figures can derive the shape of a category change (support, mint or refusal, cross-kind structure, per-slot residual, anchor by domain). They cannot derive which categories a future instrument will produce, nor rank historical cases by importance. That is a `gap`, not a failure, and it is of the underivable-in-principle kind for the *names* and the unreachable-at-this-budget kind for a complete historical census. Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

**Mode: `general`, verdict `gap` for the enumeration.**

**Refuter:** derive a specific new category, or a complete list of categories changed by measurement, from the figures alone, with no world import and no instrument. That would falsify the claim that the framework returns a shape and not a name.

### F10 - Comparability across time requires the lineage, not the artefact

Fig 13 :127: "The array cannot be checked by inspection, so the record is what makes it verifiable."

Fig 13 :130: "A term carries the atoms it was built from and the bonds that joined them. That record is not documentation of the term. It is the term."

Fig 12 :107: "Settling compresses the description and not what it cost to establish."

The history of measurement is full of broken time series: the same word names a different category in two enumerations, and the reader of the artefact cannot tell. The figures say the lineage is the term, not a gloss on it. So a category without a recorded provenance (which slot, which instrument, which mint or refusal, which anchor, which date) is not a checkable category, and a cross-time comparison that ignores the lineage is dead reckoning. Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."

**Mode: `general`.**

**Refuter:** exhibit a category whose correct cross-time use can be recovered from the category label alone, with no lineage, and where two different lineages would give the same comparison.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the standing instruction that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The overall shape claim (a separator with three domain-specific verdicts, mint or refusal by the guards, anchor by domain, per-slot residual, no named future categories) is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A new measurement changes a category while leaving the representation unchanged, with no extension of what can be represented (breaks F1).
2. A stable category system partitions a continuum with no ordering structure over a state, or a category composes state-to-state with no intervening structure and must be admitted (breaks F2).
3. A category is admitted with zero support, or fails reachability or novelty, or costs more than the confusion it removes, and is still a legitimate mint (breaks F3).
4. A measurement of people settles a constitutively human question without human judgment as anchor and without moving when the classified change (breaks F4).
5. A classification of people changes no contact and returns an identical ground reading before and after (breaks F5).
6. A count that decides an outcome remains a checkable convention with a non-updating ground and no saturated or pooled reading (breaks F6).
7. A genuine category change has zero residual in every slot (breaks F7).
8. A single lever changes all three domains identically, including their anchors (breaks F8).
9. The figures alone yield a specific future category or a complete historical list (breaks F9).
10. A category's correct cross-time use is recoverable from the label alone (breaks F10).

If any of these fires, the separator is wrong and the relevant finding is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this folder may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Send the separator, not the history. The historical examples are world corroboration, secondary to the figure derivation (Fig 8 :53: "Residual first, frame second").
2. Keep three verdicts separate. Never average D1, D2, and D3 into a single "impact of measurement" (Fig 1 :19, Fig 9 :54).
3. State the anchor in each domain before citing any source. Where the anchor is missing, say so (Fig 2 :29, Fig 10 :47).
4. Record the lineage of every category used: slot, instrument, mint or refusal, anchor, date (Fig 13 :130).
5. Ask a checker what would refute the separator before offering any theory (Fig 10 :52, Fig 9 :27-28).
6. Treat effect sizes, prestige of the source, and number of confirming cases as filters for the budget, never as witnesses for the verdict (Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict.").

## 7. Gaps, strains, and re-census

- **G1, the enumeration gap.** No figure line selects which categories a future measurement will produce, nor ranks historical cases. Reported as `gap` (F9), of both the shape-not-a-name and unreachable-at-this-budget kinds.
- **S1, Fig 6 versus Fig 13.** First pass read them as conflicting: one says the instrument extends what can be represented, the other says composition only rearranges the six. Re-census dissolves it: they govern different operations. No contradiction; no verdict was changed.
- **S2, instrument as anchor versus instrument as instrument.** Dissolved by the re-census in F4: the anchor test is "what is the question constitutively about" (Fig 2 :36). For a physical state the instrument can be the anchor; for a person it cannot.
- **S3, "change" as mint versus refusal versus contact change.** Not a strain but a union of three operations. Kept as three branches with separate refuters.
- **S4, world/figure tension on looping.** Hacking's looping effect is a world claim about human kinds. The figures reach the same shape by a different route (contact change, Fig 11 :40), so they are two witnesses with low overlap, not one evidence pool. The figure verdict does not depend on the world claim; the world claim corroborates it.
- **After re-census: no live figure strains.** One declared gap (G1), four dissolved strains retained visibly above, and one refused conflation recorded in the retraction table.

## 8. Verdict of this audit

The separator survives: a new measurement supplies support and an ordering, and the category change that follows is a mint, a refusal, or a contact change, with the anchor and the currency differing by domain. The effect is indexed per slot, and the verdicts are kept separate. What does not survive is the ordinary single-verdict framing, the idea that the instrument is the anchor for people, and any claim to name the categories a future instrument will produce. Mode `general` on the shape, `specified` per domain, `gap` on the enumeration, not `grounded`, and simulated throughout.
