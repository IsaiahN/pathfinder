# Outreach draft: check the separator before you decide about the theory

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` survives the refuter in section 4. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One separator for aboutness, with a failure condition written in advance

> Hello,
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question in effect terms.** Smoke co-varies with fire; a thermometer reading co-varies with temperature and is *about* it. What is the extra condition that turns the second into a aboutness verdict and leaves the first as mere correlation?
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A separator with three conditions, held per object-slot and never averaged:
>
> 1. **A typed bond, not just co-presence.** The ingredients of a correlation and an aboutness claim can be identical; what differs is the relation between them. A correlation records that two things are both present. An aboutness claim records a directed relation to an object that is not recoverable from the two terms alone.
> 2. **A non-updating ground, with a refusal path.** The object must be something the representation ranges against and that does not move when a frame changes its mind. Where the putative object is fixed by mutual agreement or by preference, the claim is a convention about shared meaning, not a verdict about the world. The representation must be able to be wrong: a vocabulary with no way to mark a claim false can only accumulate.
> 3. **A discriminator.** Several different sources can produce the same reading. Credit for "about O" requires an instrument that would have distinguished O from its rivals; where none exists, the honest report is *unreached*, not a name.
>
> This is a shape, not a list of which tokens are about which objects. The framework returns a kind of thing, never the answer itself, so I am not claiming it names your object for you.
>
> **What would refute it (written before this run):**
> 1. a representation with no non-updating ground contact still passes a ground test in which the putative object did not produce the reading;
> 2. a vocabulary with no refusal operator marks a token as false of an independent object and the ground pays the refusal;
> 3. a symmetric co-occurrence alone carries the typed relation, so correlation and aboutness are one term;
> 4. a discriminator exists and the reading still fixes the object with no contact to either source.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures text extract with line numbers (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Mental Representation", section 6 (Content Determination): https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/mental-representation/
> - Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Intentionality": https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/intentionality/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode (general, specified or grounded) of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
