# Correlation and aboutness: isomers, order and the refusal operator

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The main audit found that correlation and aboutness differ by bond, not by ingredients. Three questions are owed to make that precise: (1) are they **isomers** under Fig 12's definition; (2) what **order** does the aboutness bond have that the correlation bond lacks; (3) what role does the **refusal operator** play, given that it is not a bond? A fourth question follows: does aboutness recurse when a settled representation becomes an atom for the next composition?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held for this sub-derivation: a representation token R, an object O, co-presence of R and O, a typed relation between them, a ground that can settle and refuse, and an instrument that discriminates sources. Bonds held: the seven, including the symmetric `+` and the ordered `→`, `⇒`, `⋛`; plus the unary negation `¬`. No new primitive is available without import. Currency: description bits and settling actions, which are different units.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are correlation and aboutness isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- The two cases can be written over the same atom set `{R, O, co-presence}`:
  - correlation: `R + O` (symmetric conjunction, *"both hold at once"*, Fig 12 :57)
  - aboutness: `R → O` with a ground-range and a refusal path (ordered, directed, not recoverable from the operands)
- Under Fig 12 :92 *"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."* and Fig 12 :94 *"The arrangement is the substance."* the two are **isomers**: same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the data (a correlation coefficient and an aboutness claim can look identical in the readings), and decisive in behaviour (only one can be wrong of O and settled by a ground).
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a symmetric `R + O` can pay a per-slot residual, carry a refusal path and be corrected by a ground without adding any directed relation, then the two are not isomers but one term written twice, and the isomer claim is withdrawn.

### Q2 - What order does aboutness add?

- Fig 12 :59: under a symmetric bond, *"swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No."* Under an ordered bond, Fig 12 :73: *"swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."*
- Operators table :34: *"the only bond a flat chain can express, so every term today is a sequence whether or not it should be"* warns against smuggling in order. So the order must be earned by a test, not assumed.
- The test that earns it here is the ground: the representation ranges *toward* the object and is corrected *by* it, not the reverse (Fig 2 :45 *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*). Swapping R and O would put the ground inside the frame, which Fig 2 :47 calls a ground listed as one of the parties: *"A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame."*
- ⇒ aboutness is ordered by the anchor relation; correlation is symmetric. This is the bond-level version of Fig 12 :46 *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands."*
- **Falsifier:** produce a case in which swapping representation and object leaves the ground correction intact and still pays the residual. Then the order is spurious.

### Q3 - Where does negation enter, given that it is not a bond?

- Fig 12 :140: *"So the operators divide by arity, and this figure describes the binary ones."* Fig 12 :141: *"A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate."*
- Operators table :70: *"AMENDMENT · negation is the one operator here that acts on a single operand."* Operators table :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*
- Aboutness needs a way to write "R is false of O". That is a unary operator on the claim, not a third bond between R and O. Without it, a system can record more co-occurrences but cannot mark one as wrong, so it can never discriminate a representation from a correlation that happens to be right so far.
- ⇒ the refusal condition is licensed by the operator set, and it is precisely the part a correlation-only vocabulary cannot write. Triangulated: Fig 5 :53 (*"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"*), Fig 12 :141, Operators table :58.
- **Falsifier:** show a bond (binary) that writes falsity of an object as well as `¬` does. The figures separate the two by arity, so this would require a re-census of the operator table.

### Q4 - Does aboutness recurse?

- Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"*
- Once a representation is settled by its ground, it can serve as an atom for a representation about that representation (a map of a map, a report of a report). This is the recursion Fig 12 names, not a new primitive and not an import that moves the wall (Fig 6 :21).
- Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* What crosses a scale is the method for checking aboutness, not a recording of one token's object.
- **Falsifier:** show a settled representation that cannot become an atom for a higher-order aboutness claim, or a higher level that requires a new primitive to state one.

### Q5 - Two currencies, which do not add

- Fig 12 :125: *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 12 :126: *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."*
- A correlation can be cheap in bits (one coefficient) while its aboutness is expensive to settle (a whole instrument programme). Conversely, a richly described representation may be settled by one action. Fig 12 :128: *"A long chain of cheap parts is expensive to describe and may cost one action to refute."*
- ⇒ "how much correlation" is a filter for the budget, never a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*).
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a case where description bits and settling actions convert into one another at a fixed rate for this question. Then the two-currency claim is wrong here.

## CONCLUSION

Correlation and aboutness are **isomers** over a shared atom set: same ingredients, different bond, different substance, invisible in the formula and decisive in behaviour. The aboutness bond is **ordered** by the anchor relation; the correlation bond is **symmetric**. The refusal of a claim is a **unary** operator, outside the seven bonds, and is the part a correlation-only vocabulary cannot write. Aboutness **recurses** when a settled representation becomes an atom at the next level, and its two costs, description bits and settling actions, do not add. The live strain is Q1's fixed-atom condition: if a case requires an atom the other lacks, the isomer reading falls to the functional-group reading (Fig 12 :123 *"A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*).
