## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what makes a representation about something rather than merely correlated with it. Option A: treat aboutness as a matter of more or stronger correlation, so the difference is one of degree. Option B: treat aboutness as a union of conditions with different anchors, so correlation is the wrong currency for the verdict. What each leads to: A cannot explain why smoke and thermometer readings differ in kind, and it turns a convention into a verdict by averaging; B returns a checkable separator, keeps per-slot verdicts separate, and leaves the specific object open until an instrument pays.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The unexplained extra between "R correlates with O" and "R is about O". Arity: a relation among representation, object and a settling ground, plus a per-slot residual, so three terms rather than two. Symmetry: correlation is symmetric co-presence; aboutness is ordered and falsifiable. Scale: per object-slot (Fig 1 :19), not a corpus-level average. Fig 9 :57 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
- **Domain 1, the representation as frame.** Actors: atoms, terms, arrangement inside Γ. Currency: composition inside closure(Γ). Anchor: none internal; "No frame escapes its own closure" (Fig 6 :23). Union member, not sufficient alone.
- **Domain 2, the ground that settles the object.** Actors: object, instrument, measurement, outcome. Currency: residual R and settlement. Anchor: whatever settles the question and does not change when a frame thinks (Fig 2 :29). Union member, not sufficient alone.
- **Domain 3, the interpreter and convention frame.** Actors: readers, users, a linguistic community. Currency: agreement and shared meaning. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. This is where derived intentionality and declared meanings live (Fig 10 :49).
- **Order by dependency.** Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :28); settle D2 contact before the D3 convention (Fig 10 :47); refuse a wrong term before minting (formula step 2); only then check the typed bond (D1).

Strain test applied: Fig 2 :40 (agreement disqualifies a verdict) and Fig 2 :41 (agreement is the product of a convention) govern D2 and D3 respectively. Different objects, so no strain after re-census. One live strain remains: whether a missing discriminator is absent in principle or merely unreached at this budget cannot be decided from inside the frame (Fig 6 :27).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

A representation is about an object when three conditions hold per object-slot: a typed bond rather than co-presence, a non-updating ground with a refusal path, and a source-discriminating instrument. Correlation is not a weaker aboutness but the right currency for a convention and the wrong currency for a verdict. Correlation and aboutness are isomers over the same atoms, different bond, different substance. An object fixed by agreement or preference is a convention until a ground can violate it, and an object named from a correlation alone is named from a projection, not settled. The specific object of any given token is a `gap`, reported as unreached, not silence. This is a `specified`, ungrounded result; the shape is `general`.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, three checkable sub-claims and five refuters before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a source instead of a residual, which Fig 2 :42 prices ("Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."). That cost is avoidable by leading with the separator and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The three conditions are separable; a failed one can be withdrawn without disturbing the others, and no primitive has to be removed. What is recorded only grows, so a later reader can locate the correction rather than only feel it.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A discriminator that separates rival sources for a token and a ground that pays one of them would name the object; a refusal in which a system without negation marks a token false and the ground pays it would break condition 2; a symmetric co-occurrence that carries the typed relation would collapse the isomer claim; a figure line naming the object from correlation alone would break the shape-not-a-name limit. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
