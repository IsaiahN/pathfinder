# Expert verification packet - the Tether on aboutness versus correlation

**What this is:** a claim in a form a philosopher of mind, cognitive scientist or semanticist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the three conditions, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A representation is about an object when three conditions hold, held per object-slot and never averaged: (1) a typed bond to the object, not mere co-presence, because the ingredients can be identical and the difference lies in the relation; (2) a non-updating ground anchor with a refusal path, so the representation can be wrong of the object and corrected by it, while an object fixed by agreement or preference is only a convention; (3) a source-discriminating instrument, because several sources can produce one reading and credit requires that the object be fixed by contact rather than declared. Correlation and aboutness are isomers over the same atom set, same ingredients, different bond, different substance. The framework returns that shape and its refuters, not a list of which tokens are about which objects.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable against the figures

- **Statement:** correlation is a symmetric co-presence bond; aboutness is a typed, ordered relation to a ground. The two are isomers under Fig 12's definition.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :46 *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands."* Fig 12 :47 *"A recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself."* Fig 12 :117 *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
- **Refuter:** show a symmetric co-occurrence that settles a per-slot residual and carries the typed relation without any directed bond, so the two are one term. Or exhibit a case requiring an atom the other lacks, which drops the claim to a functional group (Fig 12 :123).

### C2 - philosophy-facing; checkable against the standard literature

- **Statement:** information carriage is not representation. The extra condition must be something like a selected function, an asymmetric dependency or a ground contact, plus a way to mark the claim false.
- **What to check:** SEP *Mental Representation* section 6 says causal-informational relations are insufficient: "Tree trunks, smoke, thermostats and ringing telephones carry information about what they are causally related to, but they do not represent (in the relevant sense) what they carry information about." SEP *Externalism About the Mind* says a notebook sentence is "merely a pattern of shapes against a contrastive background and, in principle, could mean anything" until interpreted. Are these faithful? Does the three-condition separator add anything to the asymmetric-dependency and teleological theories, or merely rename them?
- **Refuter:** show a representation with no refusal operator that is nevertheless marked false of an independent object and the ground pays the refusal; or show that the separator collapses entirely into an existing theory with no remainder, in which case the novelty claim is withdrawn.

### C3 - instrument-facing; the honest stop

- **Statement:** which object a token is about cannot be named from the correlation reading alone. A source-discriminating instrument is owed, and where none exists the case is `unreached`, not silence and not a name.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :32 *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* Fig 13 :134 *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*
- **Refuter:** exhibit an instrument that discriminates two rival sources while the reading still fixes the correct object without contact with either source. That breaks the contact requirement.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a definition of meaning, and not a theory of everything. A separator for this question only.
- Not that correlation is worthless. Fig 2 :41 gives it the legitimate product of shared meaning; Fig 2 :40 disqualifies it only for a verdict about the world.
- Not that an interpreter's "about" assignment is false, only that it is a convention until a ground can violate it (Fig 10 :49).
- Not a per-token object list (Fig 9 :32).
- Not `grounded`. No instrument reading is claimed here.
- Not that this derivation settles the philosophical question. It is a simulation.

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminator, not a further argument. Concretely: (1) an instrument that separates rival sources for a given token and a ground that pays one of them; (2) a refusal test in which the system marks the token false of the object and the ground pays the refusal; (3) an anchor test in which the object's fixing does not move when a frame changes its mind. Standing falsifiers are the five conditions in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 12 :46-47 and :117, Fig 2 :29 and :41, Fig 9 :32, Fig 6 :32 (seven short passages).
2. Check C2 against your own field: is information carriage insufficient for representation, and does the three-condition separator add anything to the asymmetric-dependency or teleological accounts?
3. Name one instrument that would discriminate two rival sources for a token you work with, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
