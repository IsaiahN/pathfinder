# The Tether's answer: what makes a representation about something

**Mode: `specified` on the three conditions, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Smoke co-varies with fire and is not credited as about it; a thermometer reading co-varies with temperature and is. The residual is the extra that turns a correlation into a credit that a ground can pay and refuse. It is a union compound, not one hard question: it needs an internal structure (what the token carries) and an external contact (what settles the object), and neither condition alone suffices (Fig 9 :57 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*). Verdicts are kept per domain and never averaged into a "degree of aboutness".

## 2. The separator

1. **A typed bond, not co-presence.** Correlation records `R + O`, a symmetric co-presence. Aboutness records a directed relation to O that is not recoverable from R and O alone. Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Fig 12 :47: *"A recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself."*
2. **A non-updating anchor, plus a refusal path.** The object must be what the representation ranges against and what does not move when a frame changes its mind. Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* The representation must be able to be wrong: Fig 12 :141 *"A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate."* Where the object is fixed by agreement or preference, the claim is a convention about shared meaning, not a verdict (Fig 2 :41, Fig 10 :47).
3. **A source-discriminating instrument.** Several sources can produce one reading. Credit for "about O" requires an instrument that would have distinguished O from its rivals. Where none exists, the honest report is `unreached`, not a name (Fig 6 :32, Fig 13 :134).

All three are indexed per object-slot (Fig 1 :19), never pooled. The relation between correlation and aboutness is that of **isomers** over the same atom set: same ingredients, different bond, different substance (Fig 12 :117). The refusal is a unary operator, not a third bond (Operators table :58).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a definition of meaning in general, and not a theory of everything. It is a separator for this question.
- Not that correlation is worthless. Fig 2 :41 gives correlation the legitimate product of shared meaning. The claim is that it is the wrong currency for a verdict about the world (Fig 2 :40).
- Not that an interpreter's "about" assignment is false. It is a convention until a ground can violate it, then a candidate verdict (Fig 10 :49).
- Not a list of which tokens are about which objects. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
- Not `grounded`. No instrument reading was taken here.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A discriminator, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened): one green reading, many possible sources; the spectrometer settled it, not an argument. Concretely:

- **Discriminator test:** for a candidate token R and rival sources O1 and O2 that produce the same reading, exhibit an instrument that would separate them and check which source the ground pays. If none can be built and nothing at any resolution is returning something that fails to resolve, the case is `unreached`, not a failure of trying harder (Fig 6 :34 *"Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from"*).
- **Refusal test:** can the system mark R as false of O and have the ground pay the refusal? If not, condition 2 is absent.
- **Anchor test:** does O's fixing move when a frame changes its mind? If it moves, it is not an anchor (Fig 2 :30).
- Standing falsifiers from `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4: groundless credit survives, refusal without negation, symmetric co-occurrence carries the relation, discriminator exists but contact is absent, or a figure line names the object from correlation alone.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The case that defeats the separator is not a proof that aboutness is impossible; it is a report that at this budget no discriminator has been found that the ground would pay (Fig 8 :39 *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*). The six-term set is a visible guess at compression axes, not nature's own list. The answer stops where the instruments can no longer distinguish, and the lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

One live strain is kept visible: whether a missing discriminator is absent in principle or merely unreached at this budget cannot be settled from inside the frame (Fig 6 :27). It requires an instrument, not an argument.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> A representation is about an object when three conditions hold, and a correlation alone meets none of them. First, the token must carry a typed relation to the object, not just co-occur with it: the ingredients can be identical and the difference lies in the bond. Second, the object must be a ground that does not move when a frame changes its mind, and the token must be able to be wrong of it; without a way to mark a claim false, a system can only accumulate. Third, some instrument must be able to discriminate the object from other sources that produce the same reading, otherwise the source is named from a projection and not settled. Correlation is not a weak form of aboutness; it is the right currency for a shared convention and the wrong currency for a verdict about the world. What the framework returns is that shape and its refuters, not a list of which tokens are about which objects. The center stays open until a discriminator pays at the ground.
