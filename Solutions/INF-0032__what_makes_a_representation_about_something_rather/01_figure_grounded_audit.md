## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

The question is not "what is meaning" in the large. It is a separator question. Given a representation R and a candidate object O, R can stand in a reliable co-variation with O and still not be credited as *about* O. The standard examples are tree trunks, smoke, thermostats and ringing telephones: they carry information about what produces them, yet do not represent it (SEP, *Mental Representation*, section 6, opened 2026-11-23).

- **Effect to explain:** the extra that turns a correlation into a verdict someone can refuse. When does "R is about O" get paid by a ground, and when is it only a convention among interpreters who share a code?
- **Arity:** a relation among representation, object and a settling ground, plus a per-slot residual. Not a two-term relation, so a bare correlation coefficient cannot answer it.
- **Symmetry:** correlation is symmetric (co-presence); aboutness is ordered (directed toward an object, and falsifiable). The difference is a bond difference, not an ingredient difference.
- **Scale:** per object-slot, not global. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :28: *"The gap is measured per object, not per step."*
- **What an answer would look like (bound before the run):** a three-condition separator, each condition independently checkable, with a named falsifier. The shape is bound as an ordinary term so the framework can refuse it (formula step "Direction"; Fig 5 :65 *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM."*).

## 3. Findings F1 to F8

### F1 - Correlation is a symmetric co-presence; aboutness is a typed bond (single-source on the figure side, triangulated by the Operators table)

Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."* Fig 12 :46: *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands."* Fig 12 :47: *"A recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself."* Operators table :4: *"The bond carries information the operands do not, so a term that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself."*

A correlation records that R and O are both present, which is the symmetric conjunction bond `+` (*"both hold at once"*, Fig 12 :57). Aboutness records *which* typed relation holds between R and O, directed toward the object and not recoverable from the two terms alone (Fig 12 :72 *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"*). So the difference is in the bond, which is exactly the half the correlation does not record.

**Refuter:** show a symmetric co-occurrence that settles a per-slot residual, can be refused by a non-updating ground, and carries the typed relation without any additional bond. Then correlation and aboutness are one term, and F1 falls.

### F2 - Correlation has valence: convention-grade, disqualifying for a verdict (triangulated, low overlap)

Fig 2 :39: *"Correlation has valence."* Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* Fig 2 :41: *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"* Fig 2 :18: *"one evidence pool"* Fig 2 :20: *"so the agreement is the pool talking,"* Fig 2 :21: *"not two frames confirming each other"*

This is the decisive figure finding. Correlation is not a weak form of aboutness; it is the right currency for a different product. A shared meaning is *made* from correlation (a community agrees what a token means), and that agreement is the product, not a defect. But the same correlation is disqualifying when the claim is a verdict about the world, because agreeing frames that draw on one pool are one frame wearing two names (Fig 8 :49 *"one frame wearing two names"*). So "merely correlated" names a verdict-grade failure, not a magnitude failure: you cannot add more correlation to cross the line, any more than more mirrors add an anchor (Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*).

**Refuter:** exhibit two frames with genuinely low evidence overlap whose agreement about an object, with no anchor and no refusal path, still constitutes a verdict that the ground can pay. Then Fig 2 :40 does not govern this residual and the verdict must be re-censused.

### F3 - Aboutness requires an anchor that does not update (triangulated across Fig 2, Fig 10 and Fig 11)

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :30: *"If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."* Fig 2 :33: *"The anchor must not update."* Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* Fig 2 :46: *"Each frame is corrected by it separately, and what makes the correction sound is that neither frame can move it."* Fig 10 :24: *"the ground does not decay, the channel does"* Fig 11 :39: *"And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*

A representation is about what it ranges against. If the putative object is fixed by the representation's own report, or by the interpreter's preference, the arrangement is Fig 2 panel 1: *"no anchor; each updates on the others"* (Fig 2 :13), converging on *"whoever had the most influence"* (Fig 2 :15). That is a convention driving an outcome, which Fig 10 :47 calls a stopped convention: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*. Aboutness needs something outside the co-variation that can stay put while the representation is corrected.

**Refuter:** show a representation whose object is fixed entirely by mutual update among frames or by a preference, yet whose "about O" survives a ground test that O did not produce the reading. That breaks the non-updating-anchor condition.

### F4 - Aboutness requires the possibility of being wrong, and wrongness requires negation (triangulated across Fig 5, Fig 12, Operators table)

Fig 5 :53: *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"* Fig 5 :56: *"Requires an operator for negation, which the seven bonds do not contain."* Operators table :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"* Fig 12 :141: *"A vocabulary that cannot refuse can only accumulate."* Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."*

A pure correlation can be surprising but not false in the aboutness sense: smoke does not *misrepresent* fire. To credit aboutness you need a standing from which the representation could be refused, which is a unary operator, not another bond (Fig 12 :140 *"So the operators divide by arity, and this figure describes the binary ones."*). This is why the SEP teleological and asymmetric-dependency theories add a selected function or a higher-order dependency: without something like it, information carriage is common but representation is not. The figure-side point is the structural version of that: the vocabulary must be able to write that the claim is false.

**Refuter:** exhibit a system with no negation or refusal operator that can still mark one of its recordings as false of an independent object and have that refusal paid by the ground. Then F4 is refuted and the negation requirement is withdrawn.

### F5 - Aboutness is indexed per slot, not per corpus (single-source on the figure side)

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 1 :28: *"The gap is measured per object, not per step."* Symbols table :39: *"It is the aim, not only the error signal, and it is always a slice"*

A representation is about a particular slot where the live residual sits. A global measure of how much a corpus correlates with the world loses the only signal that says which token is about which object. This is also why "aboutness" cannot be read off a single pooled score, which is exactly the mistake the SEP *Mental Representation* examples expose: a thermometer token is about the temperature at its slot, not about information in general.

**Refuter:** show that a global, pooled correlation can pick out the correct object-slot without any per-slot residual, and that the framework has no per-slot term to refuse. Then F5 is dropped.

### F6 - Declaring a meaning does not ground it (triangulated with F2)

Fig 10 :18: *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"* Fig 10 :19: *"→ install what can be violated"* Fig 10 :49: *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."* Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*

An interpreter can declare "this token is about O", and for many purposes that declaration is a perfectly good convention (Fig 2 :41). It becomes a verdict only when it is installed so the ground can violate it. The SEP *Externalism About the Mind* entry makes the same point from the other side: a sentence in a notebook is *"merely a pattern of shapes against a contrastive background and, in principle, could mean anything"* until it is interpreted, so its intentionality is derived. Derived intentionality is a real and useful product, but it is not the aboutness being asked after here unless it is tethered.

**Refuter:** find a figure line in which a declared mapping constitutes a settled aboutness without a violable ground contact. No such line was found; if one is produced, F6 is retracted.

### F7 - Which source produced a reading needs a discriminator, not an argument (single-source on the figure side, corroborated by an essay with high overlap)

Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 6 :27: *"Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*

The framework essay *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened 2026-11-23) states the mechanism: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"* and *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."* A correlation reading underdetermines its source. So a claim "R is about O" is credited only where a discriminator exists that would have distinguished O from its rivals. Where none exists, the honest report is `unreached`, not a name, and not silence.

**Refuter:** show an instrument that discriminates two sources while the reading nevertheless fixes the correct object without contact with either. That would break the contact requirement and F7 with it.

### F8 - The framework returns a shape, not a name of the object (triangulated)

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* Fig 9 :28: *"any frame you pick will seem to fit"* Fig 9 :30: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"* `FIGURES_README_canonical.md`: the framework says *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*. Fig 13 :134: *"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

So the deliverable is the three-condition separator, not a list of which representations happen to be about which objects. That list is a world task, settled case by case by instruments. Naming a particular object from inside the correlation alone repeats the apophenia Fig 9 :28 names.

**Refuter:** produce a figure line that returns the specific object of a given token from the correlation structure alone. Until then the object field stays open and is reported as a `gap` of the shape-not-a-name kind.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any conclusion, per the seat-map rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim (a union of typed bond, non-updating anchor plus refusal, and a source-discriminating instrument, indexed per slot) is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A representation with no non-updating ground contact, fixed only by mutual update or preference, still passes a ground test in which the putative object did not produce the reading. (breaks F3)
2. A vocabulary with no negation or refusal operator marks a token as false of an independent object and the ground pays the refusal. (breaks F4)
3. A symmetric co-occurrence alone settles a per-slot residual and carries the typed relation, so correlation and aboutness are one term. (breaks F1)
4. A world instrument discriminates two rival sources and the reading still picks the right object with no contact to either. (breaks F7)
5. A figure line is found that names the specific object of a token from its correlation structure alone. (breaks F8)

If any fires, the separator is wrong and is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 7. Recommendation

1. Lead with the separator, not with a theory of meaning (Fig 10 :52 *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*).
2. State the mode on every condition (formula mode rule). No condition is `grounded` here.
3. Keep verdicts per domain separate and never average them into a single "degree of aboutness" (Fig 1 :22, Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*).
4. Treat a declared "about" as a convention until an instrument can violate it (Fig 10 :49).
5. Report which object a given token is about as a `gap`, not a silence and not an invented name (Fig 13 :134).
6. Send the generator, the separator plus its refuter, not a recording of one philosophical example (Fig 4 :25 *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*).
