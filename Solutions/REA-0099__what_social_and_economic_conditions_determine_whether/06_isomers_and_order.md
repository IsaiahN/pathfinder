The primary audit leaves two structural questions owed:

1. The question says "social **and** economic conditions." Which bond does "and" name, and what does that imply for the sign of the effect?
2. Two habitats can contain the same actors and conditions yet arrange them differently (who oversees whom, who holds final authority, which actor is the maintainer). Are they the same case under the figures, or different substances?

## DERIVATION

### Q1. What bond does "and" name?

- Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." So "social and economic" is not a simple conjunction of disjoint sets. It is a union of overlapping condition sets, with a compound at the intersection.
- Operators table :15: conjunction "+" is symmetric, "swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No." So adding "social" to "economic" does not by itself create a new order.
- Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." The arrangement matters more than the inventory.
- Fig 12 :112: "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."
- Operators table :51: "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one."

**Derivation.** Three bonds are needed to write the claim, and they are not interchangeable:

| quantity | bond | figure | reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| social + economic as a compound | `+` and the union rule of Fig 9 | Fig 9 :55-57 | overlapping sets, intersection is a specific compound |
| autonomy up versus dependence up | `⋛` comparison against a ground | Operators :51; Fig 12 :112 | an ordered sign, not a symmetric join |
| change in capability | `−` subtraction | Fig 12 :112 | `Δcapability = after minus before` per slot |

Because the comparison is ordered and the subtraction is per slot, a flat formula listing "conditions + AI" cannot express the answer. It would record the ingredients and not the arrangement, which is Fig 12's flat-formula collapse.

### Q2. Are two differently arranged habitats isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Fig 11 :49-51: valence is relative to the residual; no standing list of good actors.

**Derivation.** Take two organizations with the same inventory: the same workers, the same AI system, the same budget, the same task set and the same regulator. In habitat H1 the AI advises and the worker holds final authority; in habitat H2 the AI executes and the worker monitors. Same atoms, different bond arrangement. Under Fig 12 :117 these are **isomers**, not variants of one case, and their behaviour can differ in sign. The shared substructure (person, system, task, ground) is a functional group in Fig 12 :124's sense: it says what kind of arrangement this is, while the bond says which member and which sign.

This is the structural reason the requested list cannot work. A list records the inventory, which is the shared atoms. The sign lives in the bond, which the list does not record.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two habitats with identical actor and condition inventories but different bond arrangements (authority, oversight, maintainer) never differ in the measured sign of `Δcapability_s`, the isomer claim fails. The inventory-only reading would then stand, and the figure's bond distinction would be governing a different quantity here.

### Q3. What is the dependency order of a diagnostic?

- Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
- Fig 3 :20-21: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."
- Fig 3 :24-27: the middle link, having terms for what a situation affords and being able to say what counts as progress, "has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments." And: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

**Derivation.** A diagnostic of autonomy change must run in Fig 3 order:

1. **Perception:** can the person tell what the AI changed, and tell it apart from what was there before?
2. **Vocabulary:** do they have terms for what it affords, what can be delegated, checked or refused?
3. **Objective:** can they say what would count as progress toward their own good?
4. **Planning:** is there a route to that objective under incomplete information?
5. **Learn and carry:** can they name what worked and carry it to the next task?

The likely break is at link 3, not at links 1 or 2. It has the fewest instruments. A reading taken below the break is not a weak reading; it is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22-23). So the correct order is to build or find the instrument for "what counts as progress for this person" before measuring planning or skill retention.

### Q4. What is the role of scale?

- Fig 4 :20: "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change." The kernel does not.
- Fig 4 :34-35: "A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."

**Derivation.** The AI is the seed; the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes. The loop (perceive, route, mint, accept, settle, promote) is the same at micro and macro scale. So a diagnostic validated on one person can be carried up as a method, never as a recording of that person's result, and a market-level result cannot be played back down to a person. This is the membrane rule, and it is why Fig 4 requires the round-trip gap `R_T` to be measured rather than assumed.

## CONCLUSION

The bond in "social and economic" is a union with a specific compound at the intersection, not a flat list. The sign of the effect is an ordered comparison against a ground, and the change in capability is a per-slot subtraction. Two habitats with the same inventory but different authority and oversight arrangements are isomers under Fig 12 :117, which is the structural reason a fixed-sign list cannot work. A diagnostic must run in Fig 3 dependency order and stop to instrument the middle link before reading anything below it. Scale carries the method, not the result.
