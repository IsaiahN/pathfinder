**Subject:** A checkable shape for the AI and autonomy question, with a refuter written in advance

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output about the question "what social and economic conditions determine whether AI increases human autonomy or dependence?" I would like you to check it against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The question's grammar puts AI in the subject position, as if the technology had a one-place effect on a person. The figure-grounded derivation says that is the wrong unit. Capability is a property of agent and habitat, never of the agent alone; an improvement that does not change a person's contact with their own goals changes nothing, however much it improves. The direction, autonomy up or dependence up, is a relation between a person, a system and a habitat, measured per person, not an average. Because the question asks about people, the ground that settles it is human judgment about each person's own good. Economic and benchmark measures are instruments and filters for the budget, not witnesses for the verdict.
>
> **What the derivation does not deliver.** It does not produce the list of conditions the question seems to ask for. The figures say an actor's valence is relative to the residual, and that no standing list of good actors survives a change of residual. The same AI can increase one person's capability and decrease another's, depending on which goal is measured. The specific list is therefore recorded as `gap`, not filled in with guesses. Social and economic conditions form a union compound, not two independent lists.
>
> **What would refute it.** I wrote these down before sending this:
> 1. Holding a person's habitat and contact fixed, improving the AI alone reliably raises their measured capability to reach their own stated goals.
> 2. A condition is found whose sign is invariant across two different goals for the same person.
> 3. An economic aggregate or a benchmark score settles a person's own account of their autonomy without asking that person.
> 4. The social and economic condition sets are shown disjoint for the population, so one lever clears all cases.
>
> If any holds, I will retract the shape. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would value from you in twenty minutes:** is the per-slot, habitat-relative framing faithful to what you see in your own work, and can you name an instrument that would measure the sign against a person's own judgment? A correction on shape, or a named failure class, is the useful output.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the three sources. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local framework copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The expert verification packet with the three checkable sub-claims: `08_expert_verification_packet.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the framework files. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
