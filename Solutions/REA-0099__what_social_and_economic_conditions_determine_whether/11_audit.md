## 1. Independent domain census

I redid the census from the residual line alone, before reading the primary's findings, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency. The residual line I started from is the question in effect terms: introduce an AI system into a human setting and ask for the rule that predicts the sign of the per-person change in capability to reach that person's own good.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** Arity: two overlapping conditions, social and economic, so a **union compound**, not one hard question. Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Symmetry: ordered, because autonomy up and dependence up are opposite signs of a comparison against a ground, not two names for one state. Scale: micro to macro, related by the membrane rule, since Fig 4 :20 "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."
- **Domain 1, person plus AI tool (micro).** Actors: one person, one AI system, the task set. Currency: actions and outcomes relative to the person's own goals. Anchor: human judgment about that person's own good, which is nameable, so this is a domain. Question type: partition boundary, does the intervention change contact?
- **Domain 2, social habitat (meso).** Actors: workers, managers, peers, teams, norms, organizations. Currency: roles, skills, trust, decision authority. Anchor: per-slot human judgment collected by an instrument; each person ranges against it separately. Question type: one arm of the union.
- **Domain 3, economic habitat (macro).** Actors: firms, workers, consumers, owners, capital. Currency: money, prices, wages, employment, ownership. Anchor: a market settles questions constitutively about prices or allocation; here it is an instrument and can be wrong on axes it does not measure. Question type: other arm of the union.
- **Domain 4, governance / maintainer seat (compound).** Actors: regulators, standards bodies, law, property rights, accountability regimes. Currency: rules, permitted introductions, liability. Anchor: human judgment about people's good, with the seat's interest declared. Question type: the union where a maintainer exists; its own loop.
- **Order by dependency.** D1 first, because the slot whose capability is at stake is upstream of every habitat description. D2 and D3 depend on D1. D4 acts on all three and can only introduce into a habitat it did not author, since Fig 11 :61 "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you." Within D1, refuse the agent-only reading before minting any condition list, and instrument the vocabulary/objective link before reading anything below it.

All four domains have a nameable anchor. No candidate domain was dropped for lacking one.

My census matches the primary's four domains and its union reading. Where the primary derived the domain boundaries from Fig 9's union rule and Fig 11's habitat rule, I derived the same boundaries from the arity of the residual and Fig 11 :61's asymmetry of introduction. Different starting figures, same domain boundaries. That is low overlap between routes within one framework, so the agreement is weak but not circular inside the framework. It is still one evidence pool.

## 4. Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed zero. I ran the round trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 11 :39 to :40 (capability is agent-and-habitat), Fig 11 :49 to :51 (valence relative to the residual), Fig 2 :37 (human judgment as anchor), Fig 9 :55 to :57 (union compound) and Fig 1 :19 (per-slot residual).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand carried in `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `09_watson_answer.md`: capability is a property of person plus habitat; the sign is relative to the residual; the ground is each person's own judgment; social and economic form a union; measure per person, never an average.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the answer form the question asks for, a list of social and economic conditions each with a fixed sign for autonomy or dependence.

**R_T is nonzero.** Several constraints the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down to the requested list:

- **Per-slot indexing** (Fig 1 :19 and :22) becomes a population average, which throws away the live signal that says where to look.
- **Valence relativity** (Fig 11 :49 to :51) becomes fixed signs attached to condition names, which is exactly the standing list the figure forbids.
- **Ground legitimacy** (Fig 2 :37, Fig 9 :60) becomes a market or benchmark reading, substituting an instrument for the anchor.
- **Union compound** (Fig 9 :55 to :57) becomes two independent lists, so the intersection case disappears.
- **Maintainer declaration** (Fig 10 :59, Fig 11 :61) becomes a governance lever with undeclared interest, and the asymmetry of introduction is lost.
- **Benchmark substitution** (Fig 11 :70) becomes a harness result read as a real-habitat capability change.

Conversely, two things survive cleanly: the unit of analysis is a person together with their habitat, so agent-only improvement is not the lever; and the sign is a comparison against that person's own good, not a property of the tool. That is the residue worth sending.

R_T was **not** measured numerically, because no ground reading exists. It is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the folder's shorthand as identical to the figures would set R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with its gap stated, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` reports.
