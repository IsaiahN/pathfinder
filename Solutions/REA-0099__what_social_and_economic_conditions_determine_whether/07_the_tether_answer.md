# The Tether's answer: social and economic conditions, autonomy and dependence

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks which social and economic conditions determine whether AI increases human autonomy or dependence. Restated in effect terms: introduce an AI system into a human setting; for each person the ground returns a changed capability to reach what that person takes to be their own good; `R_s` is the per-slot discrepancy between the predicted effect and the observed effect. The residual is a vector over people, not a scalar. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

It is also a union compound. Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." A "social" lever and an "economic" lever each clear their own region and leave the intersection standing (Fig 9 :56).

## 2. The shape

1. **The direction is not a property of the AI.** Fig 11 :39: "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." So the conditions that decide the sign are habitat conditions, and the unit is person plus system plus habitat.

2. **Conditions have no fixed sign.** Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." The same AI can raise one person's capability and lower another's, depending on which goal is measured. This is why the requested list is not delivered.

3. **The ground is human judgment, per person.** Fig 2 :37: "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people." Economic aggregates, productivity indices and benchmark scores are instruments and filters for the budget, not witnesses for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).

4. **Social and economic form a compound with its own loop.** Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." The compound must be unbundled, then re-composed with the intersection treated as a specific case.

5. **Governance can introduce but not exclude.** Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you." Any maintainer's interest in the outcome must be declared (Fig 10 :59).

6. **Benchmarks are wiring checks.** Fig 11 :70: "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability." A sandbox success cannot settle a claim about a person's real habitat.

7. **The specific list is a `gap`.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." The figures return the shape above and stop. Naming the conditions that decide the sign requires an import from a frame whose closure differs, with provenance and a shadow test, and then a ground reading. Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that AI inherently increases autonomy or inherently increases dependence. Both are relations to a habitat and a residual.
- Not a list of conditions with signs. That is the standing-list error of Fig 11 :51.
- Not that economic growth, efficiency or benchmark performance settles the question. Those are instruments and filters (Fig 2 :37-38, Fig 9 :60).
- Not that governance can remove contact. It can only add to a habitat it did not author (Fig 11 :61).
- Not that this derivation is `grounded`. No instrument has paid for any part of it.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A measurement in a real habitat, not a further argument. Concretely:

1. **The contact test:** hold a person's habitat fixed, change only the AI, and measure whether their capability to reach their own stated goals changes. If agent-only improvement reliably raises it, the central shape fails.
2. **The sign-variance test:** measure two different goals for the same person in the same habitat. If a condition's sign is invariant across them, Fig 11 :49-51 is wrong.
3. **The instrument for the middle link:** build or find a way to state what counts as progress toward a person's own good under AI (Fig 3 :24-27). Without it, readings below the break are readings of nothing.
4. **The union test:** find the population in which social and economic conditions overlap, and test the compound case separately. If the sets are disjoint for the population, the union reading fails.
5. **Standing refuters** from `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §3, written before the conclusion.

## 5. The honest stop

The answer stops at the shape. The condition vocabulary is not held in the figure closure, and the figures cannot name what they do not hold. The world sources in `09_watson_answer.md` are low-overlap candidates from HCI, health informatics, finance and governance, but they are surveys and venue descriptions, not ground readings of autonomy change. They corroborate the shape and they do not fill the gap. *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself." Indiscernibility is not identity: a gap in this derivation is not proof that no answer exists.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not produce a list of social and economic conditions with fixed signs for autonomy and dependence. It says that the question is aimed at the wrong unit: capability is a property of a person together with their habitat, not of the AI alone, so an improvement that leaves a person's contact with their own goals unchanged changes nothing, however good the tool gets. The sign is relative to the residual being measured, so the same AI can help one person and hinder another; there is no standing list of helpful conditions. Because the question is about people, the ground is human judgment about each person's own good, while GDP, productivity and benchmarks are filters for the budget, not witnesses for the verdict. Social and economic conditions overlap and must be handled as a compound, governance can introduce into a habitat but cannot subtract contact, and a sandbox result proves wiring and not capability. What the figures return is the shape any answer must have plus four written refuters. The names of the actual conditions are recorded as `gap` until a ground reading pays for them.
