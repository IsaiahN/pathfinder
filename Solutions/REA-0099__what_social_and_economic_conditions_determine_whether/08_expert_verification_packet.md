# Expert verification packet: AI, autonomy and human dependence

**What this is:** a claim in a form a specialist in AI and autonomy research can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** No measurement of a person's autonomy change is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status of this packet:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The direction of AI's effect on human autonomy is not a property of the AI and is not a fixed-sign property of any social or economic condition. It is a relation between a person, a system and a habitat, measured per person against that person's own judgment of their good. Capability is agent-and-habitat; an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing. Actor valence is relative to the residual, so no standing list of helpful conditions survives a change of the goal being measured. Social and economic conditions overlap and form a union compound with its own loop, not two independent lists. Governance can introduce actors and instruments into a habitat but cannot subtract contact, and its interest must be declared. Benchmarks are substituted habitats and prove wiring, not capability. The figures return this shape and stop; the names of the conditions that decide the sign are a `gap` until a ground reading pays for them.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1. Framework-internal and field-facing: direction lives in contact, not in the tool

- **Statement:** a better AI with a person's contact held fixed does not reliably raise their capability; the habitat terms are what decide the sign.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39 "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." And Fig 11 :40 "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."
- **What to check:** in your own data, is an autonomy gain ever attributable to model quality alone, with task distribution, oversight and verification conditions held constant? If yes, this is a counterexample.
- **Refuter:** a controlled study in which habitat and contact are held fixed and improving the AI alone reliably raises measured capability to reach the person's own stated goals.

### C2. Framework-internal: conditions have no fixed sign

- **Statement:** the same system and the same condition can increase autonomy for one residual and decrease it for another; a fixed-sign list is categorically excluded.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."
- **What to check:** pick a condition you think is autonomy-enhancing (for example user control, or explanation, or training). Does its effect reverse when the measured goal changes from, say, task completion to skill acquisition? If it never reverses across well-separated goals, the claim is in trouble.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a condition whose sign is invariant across two different residuals for the same person, with each ground measured separately.

### C3. Framework-internal: the ground is human judgment; metrics are filters

- **Statement:** for a question about people, the anchor is human judgment about their own good. Economic aggregates and benchmark scores are instruments and budget filters, not witnesses for the verdict.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :37 "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people." Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Fig 11 :70 "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."
- **What to check:** can your evaluation tell a person's own account of their autonomy from a throughput or benchmark reading? Pallikala and Hariprasad 2026 report that current agent benchmarks measure capability and systematically omit respect for human autonomy. If a benchmark is doing the verdict work in your field, this sub-claim predicts that gap.
- **Refuter:** show an aggregate or benchmark score settling a person's own account of their autonomy without any appeal to that person's judgment, with the prediction surviving a change of habitat.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that AI always helps or always harms autonomy.
- Not a list of conditions with signs. That is the standing-list error the packet refuses.
- Not that productivity, GDP or benchmark performance settles the question. They are instruments and filters.
- Not that governance can remove a person's contact with AI. It can add to a habitat, not subtract one.
- Not that the `gap` proves no list exists. Unreachable is a verdict here, not a proof of absence (Fig 8 :39).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing in this packet has been paid for by a ground reading.

## 5. What would settle it

The ground, not agreement. Four tests, all written before the conclusion:

1. **Contact test:** hold habitat fixed, vary only the AI, measure capability against the person's own stated goals.
2. **Sign-variance test:** measure two goals for the same person; check whether any condition's sign reverses.
3. **Middle-link instrument (Fig 3 :24-27):** state what counts as progress toward a person's own good under AI, then read anything downstream of it.
4. **Union test:** isolate the population where social and economic conditions overlap and test that compound separately.

A failure of the shape, a correction on it, or a named failure class is more useful than an endorsement. A later agreement with the framework is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
