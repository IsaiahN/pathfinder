# Expert verification packet: the Tether on adaptation without cost shifting

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ecologist, resilience researcher or environmental-justice
researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion.

**Mode: `specified` on the shape, `general` on the barrier rules, not `grounded`.** No per-slot
measurement of a displaced burden is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground
reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Adaptation is a change in contact, not a change in the community alone. Because R is indexed per
slot, an adaptation that lowers the acting community's burden while the same burden appears in a
less powerful group's habitat has relocated the residual, not resolved it. The habitat must be
enumerated outward until the cascade stops mattering, and a relocated burden is a substitution of
habitats: the receiving group inherits actors it did not choose, and what was left behind and what
was carried in are invisible until they act. Each affected group needs an anchor that the benefiting
institution cannot move, a union must be unbundled before a verdict, and description cost and
ground-settling action cost must be read in separate columns. The framework does not name which
group bears the cost; that distribution is a declared gap owed to a per-slot instrument.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — The relocated burden is a substitution of habitats, not a removal

- **Statement:** moving a burden to a less powerful habitat leaves unchosen actors in place and two
  classes of invisible failure: what the adaptation failed to reproduce in the original habitat and
  what it unintentionally carried into the new one.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :66 *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one
  habitat for another."* Fig 11 :67 *"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal
  required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."* Fig 11 :68 *"what
  you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"*; Fig 11 :69 *"what you unintentionally
  introduced is invisible until it acts"*. Fig 11 :62 *"You can add an actor and you cannot subtract
  the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."*
- **Refuter:** a documented relocation leaving the receiving habitat with the same actor set, all
  goal conditions reproduced, no inherited unchosen actors and no new invisible failure after the
  goal changes.

### C2 — A net benefit score cannot decide whether a cost was shifted

- **Statement:** the residual is per slot, and a single net score is a filter for budget, not a
  witness for the verdict. The chain usually breaks at vocabulary and objective, because the
  receiving habitat has no instrument.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1
  :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 9 :60 *"Use
  filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* Fig
  3 :27 *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
- **Refuter:** a single monotonic score shown to preserve every per-slot residual and predict the
  displaced burden without separate per-slot readings; or a documented case where the break was at
  perception while vocabulary and objective were measured and working.

### C3 — The reading of the receiving group's burden must be an anchor, and the two currencies must stay apart

- **Statement:** a burden measured by the institution that benefits from the adaptation is a frame,
  not an anchor. The reported benefit (description) and the field audit (action) are in different
  units and cannot be added.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its
  answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 10 :93 *"A ground with a reputation to protect is a
  ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."* Fig 10 :60 *"Where a stake
  cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs
  unchallenged."* Fig 12 :125 *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*
- **Refuter:** a measured burden that moves when the benefiting frame changes its account with
  physical conditions held fixed; or a demonstration that description cost and action cost are
  additive in a common unit with no loss of either reading.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a named community, technology or culprit. Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer
  itself."* The distribution is F9's `gap`.
- Not that "exclusion is not available" forbids excluding an actor from a plan. It governs the
  habitat, because contact is a fact that does not consult the manager. The first reading was
  retracted in `01` §5.
- Not that community agreement is a verdict. Fig 7 :35 *"A room's agreement is variation, not a
  verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*
- Not that this is `grounded`. No field measurement was taken here.
- Not that the framework settles a real case. It returns a shape and an owed instrument.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A per-slot burden instrument for the receiving habitat that registers the slot the acting
   community's report omits, with the reading independent of the benefiting institution.
2. A manipulation in which the benefiting institution's account changes while physical conditions
   are held fixed: does the burden reading move? If yes, it was a frame.
3. The isomer test: two programmes sharing the full atom set but differing in the cost-bearing bond
   are measured for per-slot burden distributions.
4. A check of the persisting monitoring part: regenerating catalyst or spent part reported as live
   (Fig 12 :119).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :62, :66-69 and Fig 1 :19, :22. Six short passages.
2. Check C1 against one case you know: was the burden removed or was the habitat substituted, and
   were the unchosen actors and the two invisible failure classes present?
3. Check C2: ask whether the project's success metric is per slot or a net average, and whether the
   receiving habitat has an instrument at all.
4. Check C3: ask who authored the burden reading and whether the reported benefit and the field
   audit are in one accounting or two.
5. Name one instrument that would resolve the per-slot burden, or say why none is possible at any
   resolution.
6. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
