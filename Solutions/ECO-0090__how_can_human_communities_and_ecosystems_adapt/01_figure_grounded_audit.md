## 0. The residual in effect terms

R is the gap between the conditions a community needs to persist and the conditions its habitat
actually supplies (water, soil, flood protection, heat, livelihood). R = |Γ(b,a) − o′| per slot s.
The slots are not just the acting community: they are every actor and group in contact with the
action, including those three steps out.

The failure this question names is **relocation, not resolution**. An action reduces R on the
acting community's slot while the same burden appears on a different slot: a downstream community,
a downwind group, a future generation, a non-human actor, or a habitat the acting community does
not inhabit. The acting community's instrument does not register that slot, so the global R looks
small while one live slot is still positive. Fig 1 :19 and :22 are the governing statement:

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** (Fig 1 :19)
> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** (Fig 1 :22)

A single "net benefit" number is therefore not a verdict about whether costs were shifted. It is an
average that has deleted the only signal that says where to look. What an answer would look like:
a shape, stated now so it can be refused later. It (1) enumerates the habitat to the cascade
horizon, (2) keeps a separate anchor per affected group, (3) treats the problem as a union of
conditions that must be unbundled, (4) refuses to add two different currencies, and (5) transfers
methods rather than recordings. That shape is the ordinary term bound here and in
`06_isomers_and_order.md`.

## 1. Domain census (before any figure line was chosen)

R by arity, symmetry, scale:

- **Arity.** At least three-place: acting community, action, receiving habitat, plus the cascade of
  actors that the receiving habitat contains. More than two systems, so a union rather than a pair.
- **Symmetry.** Broken. The action is ordered (`→` sequence, `⇒` production) and the seat's power
  is asymmetric: introduction is available and exclusion is not (Fig 11 :61-62). The relation
  between actor and burden is not symmetric either: the same actor is a benefactor to one residual
  and a malfactor to another (Fig 11 :50).
- **Scale.** Local habitat to distant, telecoupled systems; cross-scale, so a loss of resilience at
  a larger scale is not the same fact as a loss at a smaller one. The transform between scales is
  lossy (Fig 4 :27-32).

One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor. A domain with no nameable anchor is not admitted.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Community adaptation seat | planners, residents, engineers, managers | reduced local burden, adaptation benefit | measured local conditions, which must not move when the seat wants them to | admitted |
| D2 Habitat and ecosystem | species, conditions (light, water, temperature, season, terrain), relations (pollination, predation, competition, dependency) | survival, reproduction, resource flows | what the ground keeps: "nothing selects; what survives is what the ground kept" (Fig 11 :14-15) | admitted |
| D3 Displaced habitat (the less powerful group) | that group, its habitat actors, its unchosen conditions | the burden that lands there | that group's ground, independently measured, not the acting institution's account | admitted |
| D4 Verification and channel | instruments, per-slot metrics, outside frames | residuals and agreement | the ground(s), never agreement; independence between frames (Fig 2 :31) | admitted |
| X1 "Community resilience" as a property of the community alone | the community | none distinct from D1/D2 | none nameable apart from D1's local reading | not a domain yet; absorbed by D1+D2 |

**Partition or union.** D1, D2 and D3 are a **union compound**, not a partition. The habitat of one
group is the substituted habitat of another, and the overlap (the "spillover" of telecoupling) has
its own loop and its own ground. Dependency order (Fig 3): perception (can we perceive the burden
where it lands?), vocabulary (terms for what a habitat affords and what a cost is), objective (what
counts as adaptation without shifting?), planning (a route under incomplete information), learn and
carry (name what worked, verify, carry). The middle link is where this question usually breaks: a
burden with no instrument is not a burden that does not exist.

**Strain test applied before concluding.** A line that only fits by stretching is reassigned to
another quantity. Two strains were found and are recorded in §6 rather than smoothed:

- Fig 11 :61-62 ("exclusion is not") governs the **habitat**, not the manager's policy toolkit.
  Reading it as "you may never exclude a harmful actor" is a strain and was retracted.
- Fig 2 :40 (correlation disqualifies a verdict) governs **agreement about the world**, not the
  act of deliberating together, which is a convention until it decides an outcome (Fig 2 :41, :43).
  Reading it as "participation is forbidden" is a strain and was narrowed.

## 2. Findings (one verdict per domain, never averaged)

### F1 — Capability is agent-and-habitat, not the community alone (D1+D2, triangulated)

> **"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."** (Fig 11 :39)
> **"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."** (Fig 11 :40)
> **"it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."** (Fig 4 :20)

A community made more capable "in isolation" has not adapted to its ecosystem; it has changed its
own bookkeeping. The only adaptation that counts changes **contact**. Mode `specified`.
**Triangulated** across Fig 11 (habitat capability) and Fig 4 (kernel invariant across scales,
low overlap between the capability claim and the scale-invariance claim).
**Refuter:** an intervention that measurably improves community capacity while habitat contact,
enumerated per slot, is unchanged.

### F2 — Enumerate the habitat to the cascade horizon (D2, triangulated)

> **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."** (Fig 11 :43)
> **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."** (Fig 11 :44)
> **"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."** (Fig 11 :52-53)

The figure's own case is the reason a direct-contact scoping fails. The cost reached the eagles
three steps out. An adaptation assessed only where it is applied has not enumerated its habitat.
Mode `general` for the enumeration rule, `specified` for a given adaptation. **Triangulated** across
Fig 11 :43-44 (enumeration) and Fig 11 :52-53 (cascade), with the Carson 1962 case as the shared
example, so these two lines are not fully independent; the low-overlap partner is Fig 9 :27-28
(*"describe it before you go looking"*), which requires the residual to characterise the habitat
before a frame is chosen.
**Refuter:** a documented third-order burden that lay outside the enumerated habitat and that the
enumeration procedure could not have surfaced.

### F3 — Shifting the cost is substitution, not removal (D3, triangulated)

> **"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."** (Fig 11 :61-62)
> **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."** (Fig 11 :66)
> **"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."** (Fig 11 :67)
> **"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"** (Fig 11 :68)
> **"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"** (Fig 11 :69)

This is the figure line that governs the exact wrong in the question. Moving a burden to a less
powerful group is Fig 11's "isolation": the habitat is substituted, not removed. The new habitat
brings unchosen actors, and both what was left behind and what was carried in are invisible until
they act. Managed ecosystems are "permanent work rather than a solved state" (Fig 11 :63). Mode
`specified`. **Triangulated** across Fig 11 :61-62 (asymmetry) and Fig 11 :66-69 (substitution),
with the world corroboration from telecoupling spillover systems (Liu et al. 2013) being a separate
field and a separate evidence pool.
**Refuter:** a relocation shown to leave the receiving habitat with the same actor set, all goal
conditions reproduced, no inherited unchosen actors, and no new invisible failure once the goal
changes.

### F4 — The ground must not move when the powerful frame speaks (D4, triangulated)

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."** (Fig 2 :29)
> **"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."** (Fig 2 :45)
> **"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."** (Fig 10 :59)
> **"Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged."** (Fig 10 :60)
> **"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."** (Fig 10 :93)

If the burden on the less powerful group is measured by the institution that benefits from the
adaptation, the anchor has become a frame (Fig 2 :33-34) and the drift is coherent, which is why
nobody notices. The reading must be ranged against a ground that the powerful frame cannot move.
Mode `specified`. **Triangulated** across Fig 2 (anchor rule) and Fig 10 (ground maintainer and
stake), with low overlap between triangulation geometry and seat ethics.
**Refuter:** a measured burden that changes when the benefiting frame changes its public account
while physical conditions are held fixed.

### F5 — Unbundle the union; a net index is a filter, not a witness (union, triangulated)

> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** (Fig 9 :55)
> **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."** (Fig 9 :57)
> **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."** (Fig 9 :60)

"Adaptation that helps everyone on average" is a single lever applied to a union. It clears the
region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second
condition still binds. A net score is a filter for where to spend attention, never a witness for
whether a cost was shifted. Mode `general`. **Triangulated** across Fig 9 :55-60 (union and filter)
and Fig 1 :19, :22 (per-slot indexing).
**Refuter:** a single monotonic index shown to preserve every per-slot residual and to predict the
distribution of the displaced burden without a separate per-slot reading.

### F6 — Two currencies do not add (D1+D2, single-source)

> **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."** (Fig 12 :125)
> **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them"** (Fig 12 :126)

An adaptation has a cost in description (the plan, the metric, the reported benefit) and a cost in
actions (the field work that discovers whether the burden actually moved). They are in different
units. A team holding only one accounting will propose arrangements it cannot afford to test, and
the budget, not the claim, will decide how much of the habitat is reached. Mode `general`.
**Single-source** (Fig 12 :125-130), with the world analogue in life-cycle assessment (burden
shifting between impact categories) as corroboration only, not a second figure.
**Refuter:** a demonstrated common unit in which description cost and ground-settling action cost
are additive without loss, such that a single score preserves both readings.

### F7 — Only methods cross up; replays do not adapt (D1, single-source)

> **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."** (Fig 4 :22)
> **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."** (Fig 4 :30)
> **"A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."** (Fig 4 :34-35)

A successful adaptation copied into a different habitat is a recording, not a generator. What
crosses upward is the method for enumerating and checking; what comes down is a head start, never a
replay. The transform is lossy, so the round-trip gap `R_T` must be measured, not assumed zero.
Mode `general`. **Single-source** (Fig 4 :22-35).
**Refuter:** an adaptation transferred unchanged into a different habitat with the identical
per-slot outcome and no round-trip gap.

### F8 — Participation is variation, not a verdict (D4, single-source)

> **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."** (Fig 7 :35)

Community deliberation is necessary for the convention (what counts as adaptation, what is worth
protecting), but a room that agrees is not a measurement. The ground votes through selection
between rooms with low overlap, each ranged against the residual the last one surfaced. Mode
`specified`. **Single-source** (Fig 7 :35-36), corroborated in a different field by Morgan et al.
2024 asking "for whom?" and "for what purpose?".
**Refuter:** a decision justified solely by consensus with no per-slot ground reading from a frame
outside the benefiting institution.

### F9 — The specific displaced burden is a gap (D3+D4, `gap`)

> **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"** (Fig 9 :32)
> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** (Fig 3 :27)
> **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** (Fig 8 :39)
> **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"** (Fig 13 :134)

No figure line names which actor in the receiving habitat bears the cost, or by how much. The
figures give the shape (per-slot, enumerated, substitution-aware, anchored) and stop. Characterising
the missing thing in effect terms is what can travel to a frame that holds it. This is a genuine
`gap` after re-census, not an unanswered rhetorical question and not a place to invent a name. The
instrument owed is a per-slot burden audit that registers the receiving habitat and the cascade
beyond it, independent of the benefiting institution.
**Refuter of the gap verdict:** an instrument, already returning something, that resolves the
displaced burden per slot; at that point this finding becomes `specified` and is tested.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter (written before the run)

The shape claim in §0 is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A relocation of the residual leaves the receiving habitat with the same actor set, all goal
   conditions reproduced, no unchosen inherited actors, and no invisible failure after the goal
   changes (breaks Fig 11 :66-69).
2. A single monotonic score is shown to preserve every per-slot residual and to predict the
   distribution of the displaced burden without a separate per-slot reading (breaks Fig 1 :19,
   :22 and Fig 9 :60).
3. An instrument shows the receiving group's ground unchanged across a habitat substitution while
   the acting institution's account changes (breaks Fig 11 :67-69 and Fig 2 :29).
4. A community-only improvement is shown to change habitat contact per slot with no enumeration of
   the habitat (breaks Fig 11 :39-40).
5. A falsifier written after the run is found to have been shaped by the result. This is recorded
   as a design failure even if the conclusion survives.

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or re-censused. Until one is run, no part of this may be
labelled `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Enumerate the habitat outward until the cascade stops mattering, including distant and spillover
   systems; do not scope to direct contact (F2).
2. Keep a separate anchor for each affected group, independent of the institution that benefits
   (F4). Declare the seat's stake (Fig 10 :60).
3. Unbundle the union before searching; keep per-slot residuals visible and never let a net index
   hand you a verdict (F5).
4. Read description cost and ground-settling action cost in two columns (F6).
5. Transfer the enumeration and checking method, not the successful case, and measure the round-trip
   gap (F7).
6. Deliberate to build the convention, then range each room against the ground separately; do not
   pool agreement as corroboration (F8, Fig 2 :40).
7. Record the gap at F9 as an owed instrument, not a filled name (Fig 9 :32).
