## Round trip gap R_T

The Symbols table defines R_T as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, the gap between what was sent up and what
came back down. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed zero. I ran the round trip
on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :19, :22 (per-slot residual), Fig 11
   :44, :66-69 (enumeration and substitution), Fig 2 :29 and Fig 10 :93 (anchor and stake), Fig 9
   :55-60 (union and filter).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07_the_tether_answer.md` §2, namely: adaptation is
   contact; enumerate the habitat; a moved burden is substitution; keep an anchor per group;
   unbundle; two currencies; transfer methods; the middle link lacks an instrument.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** the concrete project decision, namely: approve programme B, report a
   net benefit score, and treat the receiving group's burden as covered by the same assessment.

**R_T is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **per-slot indexing** of the residual, which the net benefit score collapses (Fig 1 :22);
- the **anchor independence** of the receiving group's reading, which the benefiting institution's
  assessment replaces (Fig 10 :93);
- the **isomer distinction**, which an ingredient-only comparison cannot represent (Fig 12 :117).

Conversely, one thing survives cleanly: enumeration must reach the cascade horizon, and a relocated
burden is substitution rather than removal. That is the residue worth sending. R_T was **not**
measured numerically because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the
enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating the formula as identical to the figure set would set
R_T to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy
condensation with a recorded gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` states.
