# Isomers, bonds and dependency order in community adaptation

**Mode: `specified` on the ecology application, `general` on the bond and order rules.**
This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading
(`05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

Two adaptation programmes can use the same actors, the same technology and the same budget yet
produce different distributions of the environmental cost. The question is what carries the
difference: the ingredients or the arrangement. A second question is which link in the chain
fails when a cost is shifted without anyone noticing.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: community C, habitat H, regulating institution I, technology T, conditions W (water,
soil, heat, season, terrain), relations R (pollination, predation, competition, dependency), and the
receiving habitat H'. Bonds held: the seven, including `+` conjunction, `→` sequence, `⇒`
production, `−` subtraction and `⋛` comparison. No new primitive is available without import.
Anchor: per-slot ground readings in H and H', not the institution's report.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Are two such programmes isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is
  invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :96: **"Two entries can share every ingredient and be inverses of one another."**

Let programme A be written `C + H + T + I + R` under `+` (costs and benefits jointly held in one
habitat) and programme B be `C + T ⇒ H → H'` under `→` then `⇒` (the community acts, the benefit is
produced in H, the cost is passed into H'). Same ingredient list, different bond. Under Fig 12 they
are **isomers**, not variants of one programme. A score that records only the ingredients cannot
tell them apart, and nothing signals that anything was lost. That is exactly the failure the
question describes.
- **Mode:** `specified` for the programme reading; `general` for the isomer rule.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two programmes sharing the full atom set and differing
  only in the bond produce **identical per-slot burden distributions in H and H'**, the isomer claim
  fails and they are the same substance in two notations.

### Q2 — Are they a functional group?

- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says
  what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- Every programme here shares the substructure `actor → habitat → outcome`. That fixes the kind
  (adaptation programme). The qualifier, namely which bond carries the cost, says which member and
  decides whether the cost was shifted. So the weaker claim is: all candidates are in one functional
  group; the just ones are the members whose cost-bearing bond stays inside the enumerated habitat.
- **Falsifier:** a programme that shares the substructure but whose outcome is fixed by a different
  substructure (for example one where the cost is set by an external market rather than by the
  habitat relation) breaks the functional-group reading.

### Q3 — Which link breaks when a cost is shifted?

- Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
- Fig 3 :25-26: the middle link, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what
  would count as progress, has neither obvious failures nor obvious instruments, so it attracts the
  least attention.
- Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**
- Dependency order: perception (is the receiving habitat registered at all?) → vocabulary (is there
  a term for a burden that lands three steps out?) → objective (does "adapted" mean per-slot R
  reduced across H and H'?) → planning → learn and carry.
- When a cost is shifted unnoticed, the break is at vocabulary/objective, not at planning. More
  sophisticated planning above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it. Effort spent on the
  adaptation design does not rescue an uninstrumented receiving habitat.
- **Falsifier:** a documented case where a shifted cost was invisible because the *perception* link
  failed (no sensor at all) while the vocabulary and objective links were measured and working.
  That would move the break and require a new census.

### Q4 — Catalyst and cycle analogues

- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave
  differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."** An independent monitoring body
  that persists across adaptation rounds is a catalyst; a one-off consultation is spent. Treating a
  spent part as regenerating is how an institution reports continuing oversight that no longer
  exists.
- Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it
  closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."** Adaptation changes the habitat, which changes
  the residual, which drives the next adaptation. Whether the loop closes on the same group's burden
  or hands it onward is decided by the sign of the loop; a linear project plan cannot represent this
  at all.
- **Falsifier:** an adaptation programme shown to be genuinely one-shot with no output re-entering
  as input, and no persisting part across uses, would defeat both analogues.

### Q5 — Two currencies, again

- Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**
- Fig 12 :126: **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."**
- The programme's reported benefit is a description cost; the field audit of the receiving habitat
  is an action cost. A budget that counts only the first will keep producing programmes it cannot
  check. This is why the isomer distinction in Q1 stays invisible in ordinary reporting.

## CONCLUSION

The difference between a just adaptation and a cost-shifting one is a **bond**, not an ingredient.
Programmes with the same actors, technology and habitat can be isomers whose ingredient list hides
the decisive difference. They are also members of one functional group whose qualifier is where the
cost-bearing relation terminates. The chain usually breaks at vocabulary/objective, because the
receiving habitat has no instrument; better planning above that break cannot rescue it. Independent
monitoring is a catalyst only if it survives its own use, and adaptation is a cycle whose sign
decides whether the burden returns to the same group or is handed on. The isomer and functional-group
claims are `specified` and carry pre-registered falsifiers; the bond and order rules are `general`.
