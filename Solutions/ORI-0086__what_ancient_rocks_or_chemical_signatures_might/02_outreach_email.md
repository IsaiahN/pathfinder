# Outreach draft: check the shape claim before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the retracted readings in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §9 are respected. In particular, do not present the framework as naming a particular rock or as settling a source. The output must survive the check or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One check on a claim about ancient chemical signatures, before any theory

> Dear [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question of which ancient rocks or chemical signatures might preserve prebiotic chemistry is a union of two conditions, not one question. A medium must have held a trace through billions of years of alteration, and that trace must separate a prebiotic source from an abiotic one. My claim is only about the shape an admissible record must have: a medium that is in contact with the residual, that has retained a source-discriminating trace, and on which an instrument already returning a reading can be sharpened to the needed resolution. It does not name a rock. It does not say that any observed signature is biological.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that a carbon or sulfur isotope ratio by itself names life, that a stromatolite-like lamina is biological, that a mineral assemblage such as greigite or vivianite is a fingerprint rather than a possible abiotic product, or that no trace means the process never happened. A reading does not determine its source. NASA's own wording for the Perseverance sample is "potential biosignature", with the explicit note that abiotic explanations cannot be ruled out. I use "candidate" in the same sense.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before this email:**
> 1. a medium whose reading is identical under a prebiotic and an abiotic pathway at every resolution an existing instrument can reach, with a calibration showing the identity;
> 2. an independent source-discriminating instrument that reverses an attribution previously read as prebiotic;
> 3. a demonstration that alteration removes the discriminating trace in every candidate medium at every accessible resolution (I would report this as `unreachable`, not as absence);
> 4. a single criterion that settles preservation and source discrimination together without a second instrument (then the union split fails).
>
> If any one holds, the claim is refused and I will record it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - The figures as text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - The canonical usage rule (local copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - "Arrival of the Fittest: The Tether Architecture": https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether
> - "The Hard Problem is About Source", on why a reading cannot comment on its source: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/the-hard-problem-is-about-source
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the architecture link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The framework's own record says a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and that outputs could never be verified if they were contrived to fit the formula. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> With thanks for your time,
> [name]
