## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Typing the bond

- Fig 12 :67: "two names, one referent." The Operators test is "substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No." In a spin chain with no horizon, `A/4G` is undefined; in a classical geometry with no state, `S_A` is undefined. Substitution loses something, so `≡` is refused as a general reading. It survives only as a regime-specific binding after renormalization.
- Fig 12 :77: "A produces B; B did not exist before." The geometric entropy was defined historically before the entanglement interpretation and coexists with it, so `⇒` does not type the value relationship. It types the separate dynamical-emergence program (F3 of `01`).
- Fig 12 :87: `⋛` is "a threshold relation between two quantities." Generalized entropy is a *sum*, `A/4G + S_bulk`, not a threshold between two quantities. So `⋛` is refused for the sum itself; it could type a threshold on the generalized entropy (for example a quantum extremal surface selection), which is a different operand level.
- Fig 4 :27: "The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point." This is not one of the seven bonds. It is the cross-scale operator, and it is the correct type for the transport condition.
- **Result:** the union has no single bond. Identity is regime-specific, production is a different compound, comparison is mistyped for the sum, and the cross-scale transform governs the third condition. Triangulated for the refusal of identity (Fig 12 plus Operators); single-source for the transform typing (Fig 4).

### Q2 - What order the union has

- Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"
- Both first-order descriptions are settled in their own domains: the state-relative entropy is settled for lab systems; the horizon entropy is settled as thermodynamics with microscopic counting in supersymmetric cases. Each can therefore serve as an atom at the next level.
- The union is **second order**: `(settled state-relative entropy) mapped to (settled horizon entropy)`, with a lossy transform and a round-trip gap. This is recursion under Fig 12, not a new primitive and not an import that moves the wall (Fig 6 :21 "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom.").
- Fig 12 :30: `→` is sequence, "A then B; B needs A to have happened." The figure's own derivation record warns that order is not introduced at the level of the kinds. Applying `→` here would add an ordering the derivation does not license. Keep the union as a comparison of two settled molecules, not a historical sequence.

### Q3 - Are they isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- As the two quantities are ordinarily written, they share almost no atoms: `S_A` uses a state, a cut and a regulator; `S_BH` uses an area and G. They are **not** isomers on that writing. They share a *functional group*: both are area-law entropies (Fig 12 :124).
- The stronger form: write both over a shared atom set, state, cut/surface, area, coupling and regulator, and let the difference be which atom plays the mediator role. Then they are isomers under Fig 12 :117: same atoms, different arrangement, different behaviour under a changed regulator. This is the claim worth testing, and it needs the shared atom set specified before it can fire.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if either quantity can be written without an atom the other requires (for example an area-law entropy with no regulator, or a horizon entropy with no area), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands instead.

### Q4 - Structural analogues that bear

- **Catalyst** (Fig 12 :119): a part that survives its own use. The renormalized Newton constant plays a catalyst-like role in the bookkeeping: the UV divergence is absorbed into it and it persists. This is an analogue only. The figure's catalyst is a part in a molecule, not a coupling constant.
- **Cycle** (Fig 12 :121): the output re-enters as input. The round trip of Fig 4 is this shape: abstraction up to geometry, concretization back to the state, with the gap measured. It is the strongest structural analogue available for the transport condition.
- **Flat formula collapse** (Fig 12 :14-17): "the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not." Writing "both are area laws" records the ingredient and drops the bond. This is the exact failure the question invites.
- **Two currencies** (Fig 12 :125): "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Bits price the description; actions price finding out whether it holds. The entropy relation is priced in bits (description length) and the source-discriminating instrument in actions (finding out which source). Summing them into one figure is the named error.

## CONCLUSION

The relation is not one bond. The transport condition is a cross-scale transform with a round-trip gap, the identity condition is a regime-specific binding, the production condition belongs to another compound, and `⋛` is mistyped for a generalized-entropy sum. The union is second order under Fig 12's settled-molecule rule. On the isomer question the ordinary writing fails the figure's definition but a shared-atom writing makes it a real, falsifiable claim. This framing rescues the checkable part without minting a seventh term or naming a source the instruments cannot separate.
