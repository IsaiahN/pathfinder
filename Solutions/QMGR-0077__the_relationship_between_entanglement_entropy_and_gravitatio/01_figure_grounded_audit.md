## 0. The question under audit, in effect terms

Two entropy quantities are in play.

- **Entanglement entropy** `S_A = -Tr rho_A log rho_A`: a quantity of a quantum state relative to a bipartition. It changes when the state changes, when the cut moves, and when the UV regulator changes. Its divergence coefficient counts field species.
- **Gravitational entropy** `S_BH = A/4G`: a quantity of a horizon geometry. It changes when the area changes or when Newton's constant is renormalized. It carries no explicit dependence on a quantum state.

They are measured in the same units and both obey an area law in the settings where they are compared. The residual is: **what makes two quantities with the same units and the same scaling one quantity or two?** The options and what each leads to:

| option | bond | what it commits to |
|---|---|---|
| A: they are the same quantity under two names | `≡` identity | substitution with no loss in every setting; Fig 12 :67 "two names, one referent" |
| B: one produces the other | `⇒` production | a dynamical emergence mechanism; Fig 12 :77 "A produces B; B did not exist before" |
| C: they are a cross-scale projection with a gap | Fig 4 transform | a lossy map plus a measured R_T; not an identity |
| D: no figure line governs | gap | an honest unreachable, not a silence |

The sought-for shape is the *bond*, not a name for the microscopic degrees of freedom. This is written down before the run so it can be refused.

## 1. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

### R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity.** Two quantities, so the relation is a binary bond among the seven of Fig 12, or a cross-scale transform. The candidate bonds are constrained by the answer to the symmetry question.
- **Symmetry.** Identity, conjunction and disjunction are symmetric ("swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No."); production, comparison, subtraction and sequence are ordered. The empirical asymmetry is decisive: entanglement entropy exists for a bipartite spin chain with no horizon, and a classical Schwarzschild horizon has an entropy with no quantum state specified. So the general relation is **not symmetric**, which removes `≡`, `+`, and `∥` as general readings.
- **Scale.** `S_A` lives at the quantum state and field stratum. `S_BH` lives at the semiclassical geometric stratum. Fig 10 :68: **"Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between. That is why there is no top rung and no regress: a generator crosses, an instance does not."** This is a cross-scale question, so Fig 4 governs before Fig 12.

### One line per domain

**Domain 1, quantum state and bipartition.**
- Actors: the state, the reduced density matrix, the entangling surface, the UV cutoff.
- Currency: von Neumann entropy in bits or nats; area-law scaling.
- Anchor: the spectrum of the reduced density matrix, obtainable in principle by tomography or a replica construction. *Nameable*: yes, for generic bipartite lab systems.

**Domain 2, horizon geometry.**
- Actors: the horizon, the area operator, Newton's constant, surface gravity, counterterms.
- Currency: `A/4G`; generalized entropy `A/4G + S_bulk`.
- Anchor: horizon area and the first law of black hole thermodynamics, with microscopic counting for supersymmetric cases. *Nameable*: partly. For an astrophysical horizon there is no entropy measurement, only theoretical consistency. This is recorded as a partial anchor, not upgraded to a ground.

**Domain 3, the contact between them.**
- Actors: UV cutoff, replica trick, minimal and quantum extremal surfaces, renormalized G.
- Currency: the map between the two entropies; the generalized entropy functional.
- Anchor: would have to be a source-discriminating instrument that tells an entanglement source from a geometric source for the same area reading. *Nameable*: **no**. There is no such instrument. So this domain is a real compound with actors and currency, but its ground is unreached. Its verdict cannot be `grounded`.

### Partition or union

**Union, not partition.** Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."** "The relationship" bundles at least three conditions: (a) identity, are they one referent; (b) derivation, does one produce the other; (c) transport, how does a quantity cross the membrane. Fig 9 :57: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."** A single lever, for example "both obey an area law," clears one condition and leaves the intersections standing. Each condition gets its own loop and its own verdict; they are never averaged.

### Order by dependency (Fig 3)

Settle each term in its own domain first, then ask identity, then derivation, then transport. Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** The link between "we have terms for our own entropy" and "we can say what would count as progress in relating them" has no instrument. That is where this question sits.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The question is a union compound, not one hard question (decisive)

Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
Fig 9 :57: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

The literature routinely answers three different questions with one sentence: "black hole entropy is entanglement entropy" is an identity claim; "gravity emerges from entanglement" is a production claim; "boundary entanglement equals bulk area" is a transport claim. They have different bonds and different grounds. Refuter: show that a single bond settles all three, so the union is really a partition. Until then, verdicts are kept separate.

### F2 - Identity holds only as a regime-specific binding, not as a general reading

Fig 12 :67: **"two names, one referent."** Operators table :27: *"substitute one for the other. Is anything lost? No."*

Apply the substitution test. In a bipartite spin chain with no horizon, `S_A` is defined and `A/4G` is not. In a classical black hole geometry, `A/4G` is defined without specifying a state. Substitution loses something in both directions, so general identity fails. In the restricted regime of quantum fields near a horizon, with the UV divergence absorbed into the renormalization of G, the two readings coincide. That is a **binding** licensed by a provenanced import from the physics frame, not a general identity and not a derivation from the six-term set. Refuter: produce one setting in which the two quantities are substitutable with no loss in all respects, including regulator dependence and species dependence. This is `specified`, ungrounded.

### F3 - The production reading strains and governs a different compound

Fig 12 :77: **"A produces B; B did not exist before."**

"Entanglement produces gravity" (Jacobson, Verlinde) reads as production. The strain: gravitational entropy did not come into existence when entanglement was identified; the geometric quantity was in the literature first, and both quantities coexist in the semiclassical description. The bond line governs the **existence of the Einstein equation** as an equation of state, which is a different question from the value relationship here. Strain test says: if a line only fits with a strain, it governs another quantity, so re-census. Re-census moves the production claim into its own loop. Verdict for this question: production is **not** the relationship; it is an adjacent, ungrounded program. Refuter: show that `S_BH` is absent in a description where `S_A` is present and appears only after entanglement is added.

### F4 - The cross-scale map is lossy and R_T must be measured

Fig 4 :27: **"The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."**
Fig 4 :30: **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."**
Fig 10 :68: **"Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between."**

Ryu and Takayanagi map boundary entanglement to a bulk area; Engelhardt and Wall map it to a generalized entropy extremized over a surface. Both are cross-scale transforms. Fig 4 requires the round-trip gap `R_T = gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))` to be measured, not assumed zero. The gap is not an error to hide: it is the honest report of what the coarser geometric description cannot hold, including regulator choice and which contributions get called entanglement versus higher-curvature corrections. Refuter: exhibit a round trip from the geometric description back to the state description that preserves every distinguishable, so R_T = 0. None is in hand. Verdict: `specified`, triangulated across Fig 4 and Fig 10 (low overlap between membrane and ground-maintainer).

### F5 - Their agreement may be one evidence pool talking

Fig 2 :31: **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."**
Fig 2 :40: **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**
Fig 8 :50: **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them"**

Both entropies are computed from the same near-horizon field content in the brick-wall and replica constructions. Their numerical agreement is therefore weak corroboration: one pool, not two witnesses. The record of which came first (Bekenstein and Hawking from thermodynamics, entanglement from the state description, the identification afterwards) is the whole provenance issue. Refuter: show a derivation of the area coefficient from a frame that does not draw on the near-horizon quantum field state. Verdict: `general`, triangulated across Fig 2 and Fig 8.

### F6 - The framework already names this case, as a retrodiction

Fig 8 :56: **"the triangulation claim fits four cases already known and has not been tested forward: Wiles and Frey, Montgomery and Dyson, Bekenstein and holography, the relativization barriers in complexity theory."**

The figure text itself lists "Bekenstein and holography" as one of four *known* cases the triangulation claim fits, and says it has not been tested forward. The Arrival of the Fittest citation list tags the Bekenstein entry as "an instance." So the relationship between horizon entropy and holography is a case the framework describes, arrived at independently by others. It is not a result the framework produced. Refuter: a forward test in which the figure set predicts a previously unknown entropy relation that is then confirmed. Until then this is `single-source` for the figure's naming of the case, with world corroboration from the physics literature.

### F7 - The framework returns a shape, not the microscopic name

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**
