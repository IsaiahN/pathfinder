# Isomers and order: union, first and second order, and what the dependency chain forbids

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the union reading, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The literal question bundles four things: (i) is a computational description composable at all, (ii) what is its description cost, (iii) what is its settling cost, and (iv) what is its complexity class. Are these separate domains or one chain? Are a classical and a quantum computational description isomers over a shared atom set? And in what order must the questions be asked? This file derives the order and the isomer claim, each with a falsifier.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: Space, Time, Energy (states) plus Spectrum, Gradient, Network (structures). Bonds held: the seven, plus negation (unary, not a bond). No new primitive without import. The complexity frame (D2) holds circuits, resource bounds, programs and undecidability proofs; it is a differing closure, so a union surplus exists if and only if a residual is described first (Fig 8 :53).

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Union or chain? UNION, with a dependency order inside each branch

Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either".

The four items do not share one anchor. (i) is settled by a derivation in D1; (ii) is a proof about description length; (iii) needs an instrument or a proof of settlement; (iv) is a proof in D2. So this is a union of conditions with an internal order, not a single ladder. A single lever (say, proving a class) clears (iv) and leaves (i) to (iii) standing.

Within each branch, Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." Asking the class before the description exists is a reading taken below the break: "not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing" (Fig 3 :22-23). So the order is: existence of the description first, its prices second, its class third, its settlement last.

**Falsifier, written before this run:** if one instrument or proof settles existence and class together, so that no branch remains, the union claim fails and the single-chain reading stands.

### Q2 - What is first order here?

Fig 13 :74: "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one." A first-order molecule is `state → structure → state`, one crossing.

A computational description of a physical theory at first order reads, for example, `Space - structure - Time` (an ordering or a directed update between two states) or `Energy - structure - Time`. These are the same shapes the exemplar derived for GR and QM. Fig 13 itself licenses more than one first-order term: "A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is."

Note the type caution: a circuit is a sequence (`→`), a bound is a comparison (⋛), and a refusal is negation (¬). The Operators table distinguishes them: "a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one". A computational description is not one operator; it is a molecule with typed bonds, and which bond holds is not recoverable from the atoms (Fig 12 :46).

**Falsifier:** if a computational description can be written as a single operator with no typed bond, the bond analysis fails.

### Q3 - Are a classical and a quantum computational description isomers?

Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 12 :123: "A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

Write both over the full shared atom set {Space, Time, Energy, Spectrum, Gradient, Network}. Classical description: a sequence over a spectrum-like ordering, e.g. `Space - Spectrum - Time`. Quantum description: a production or directed update over a gradient-like structure, e.g. `Energy - Gradient - Time`. Same atoms, different bond and different slot occupancy, different substance: by Fig 12's definition they are **isomers**, conditional on both being written over the full shared set.

As the question actually names them, however, they do not share the full set: a classical lattice update may use no Energy atom, and a quantum circuit may use no Space atom. Then they are members of a **functional group** (Fig 12 :123), the class `state - structure - state`, with different qualifiers. The stronger isomer claim is the one worth testing; the weaker functional-group claim is what the literal wording supports.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if either description requires an atom the other does not even over the full set, or if both reduce to the same molecule under the figure's bond test, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading stands. This is the same fixed-atom condition the validated exemplar records as a live strain.

### Q4 - What order is the union? SECOND ORDER, licensed by settling

Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."

A first-order computational description, settled in its own domain, becomes an atom for the next level. The complexity class question is then a **second-order composition**: `(settled description) ⋛ (resource bound)`. This is legal only because the first-order molecule settled. It is the recursion Fig 12 names, not an import and not a new primitive.

Type caveat, kept open: Operators :48 defines comparison as "a threshold relation between two quantities". At second order the operands are settled molecules now serving as atoms. Whether they count as quantities at that level needs an instrument or a proof, not an argument. This is a strain, not a correction, and `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 7 records it as S1.

**Falsifier:** if a settled molecule cannot serve as an atom, or if ⋛'s operands must be bare quantities at every level, the second-order claim fails and class membership becomes a category error at this level.

### Q5 - What about "the universe is a quantum computer"?

That adjacent question (QMGR-0074) is answered by the same machinery and is not asserted here. A simulated universe proves wiring and not contact: Fig 11 :70: "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability." Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." A successful simulation is a head start for the next search, not evidence of identity.

**Falsifier:** a simulation that changes contact with the same ground, that is, predicts a discriminating observation not used in its construction, would move this from a shape claim to a ground claim.

### Q6 - Are the two currencies isomers or different quantities?

Neither. Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Isomers are same atoms, different bond. Bits and actions are different units, not two arrangements of one set. Fig 12 :128: "Neither figure predicts the other, so both are read or the choice is made blind." The world sources agree: Kolmogorov complexity measures program length and is uncomputable; BQP measures polynomial time under a fixed machine model. A report that gives only one is a partial reading, and calling the pair "complexity" is the flat formula of Fig 12 :6-8.

**Falsifier:** a single measure that prices both without loss would collapse the two-currency claim, and F4 of `01` would be refuted.

## CONCLUSION

The question as posed is a **union of conditions with a dependency order inside each branch**, not a chain and not one number. A computational description is first-order; its complexity class is second-order and licensed by the settling of the first-order molecule. Classical and quantum computational descriptions are isomers over the full shared atom set and members of one functional group as actually named. The two prices of a composition, description bits and settling actions, are neither isomers nor additive: they are different currencies. No figure line returns a class, no ground settles one at this scale, and the fixed-atom condition on the isomer claim is a live, named strain.
