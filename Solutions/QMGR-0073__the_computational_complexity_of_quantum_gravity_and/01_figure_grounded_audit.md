## 0. The residual, in effect terms

The residual is not "how hard is quantum gravity". It is the contact failure between two descriptions and two anchors.

- **Physicists' side:** perturbative gravity diverges; there is no settled, predictive description of the Planck regime; unitarity and definite outcomes do not compose in one frame. The metric and the Hilbert-space state are settled in their own domains, not at their contact.
- **Computation side:** a "computational description" is a request for a term in a language (an algorithm, a circuit, a lattice update, a tensor network), and "computational complexity" is a request for an ordering over resource use (time, space, description length, decidability).
- **What failed to resolve:** whether the first side can be expressed in the second side's language at all, and what the price of that expression would be. Nothing in the question names the slot it wants filled. It asks for a name and a class before it asks what would license either.

Bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula, "Direction"), so it can be refused:

> A computational description of a physical theory is a finite composition over states and structures whose description cost in bits and whose settling cost in actions are both bounded, and whose complexity class is a provenanced binding from a frame whose closure differs.

Two candidate readings of the question, with consequences:

- **A, literal:** spacetime *is* a computation, and quantum gravity has a class (say BQP, or NP-hard, or undecidable). Consequence: a name is assigned to a slot the framework says it cannot fill, a complexity frame is imported with no source gate, and a verdict is stated with no ground.
- **B, figure-derived:** a computational description must satisfy the cross-kind composition rule; complexity has two non-additive prices; a class is a provenanced import and stays `candidate` until a proof or instrument pays for it. Consequence: a checkable shape with written refuters and an open slot.

The audit below derives B and refuses the parts of A that the figures forbid. It does not claim B is true; it claims B is what the figures license.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The question is a union of three conditions, not a single hard question (triangulated)

Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either". Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."

The two halves of the question do not share an anchor. Existence can be settled by a derivation inside D1; class membership can be settled by a proof in D2; the physical claim needs an instrument in D3. A proof of class membership does not establish that spacetime is computational, and a composable description does not give a class. One lever leaves the intersections standing.

**Refuter:** show the two conditions share a single anchor and one instrument, so that settling the class also settles existence, and the union reading collapses.

**Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 9 split plus the anchor test in Fig 2 :29), low overlap between the union rule and the anchor rule.

### F2 - Computation cannot be a seventh primitive (decisive against the literal reading)

Fig 13 :41: "Each is indivisible: no arrangement of the other five yields it." Fig 13 :43: "A continuum was the seventh and was removed for failing exactly this". Fig 13 :44: "A generating set must not contain what its own members produce." Fig 13 :74: "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one." Fig 13 :115: "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."

The continuum is the exact precedent. It was removed because a generating set must not contain what its own members produce. Computation is in the same position: an algorithm is an ordering (spectrum-like), a directed update (gradient-like) and a relation among entities (network-like) composed over states. It is a molecule built from held atoms, not a sixth or seventh kind. So "spacetime has a computational description" cannot be answered by adding computation to the substrate; it can only be answered by showing a composition over the six that predicts the shape of such a description.

Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording." A list of algorithms is not a computational description of a theory; the typed bonds are the claim.

**Refuter:** derive computation as a primitive indivisible from the other five and load-bearing in an established law, or show that removing one of the six and replacing it with computation leaves thermodynamics intact. Either would break Fig 13's minimality claim.

**Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 13 primitive rule plus Fig 12 bond rule; low overlap between set minimality and composition notation).

### F3 - The framework returns a shape, not a name; a class is a binding, not a derivation (with a correction preserved)

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 :135: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it." Fig 13 :134: "It can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands".

So saying "a quantum circuit implements the description" is a **binding** from D2 onto held atoms, which is legitimate provenance, not an invented primitive. ### F4 - "Computational complexity" prices in two currencies that do not add (decisive against the single-number reading)

Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds." Fig 12 :130: "Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it." Fig 5 :45: "This is minimum description length, Rissanen 1978, which is itself a composition of Kolmogorov complexity and Shannon coding put to work on model selection."

The question treats "computational complexity" as if it were one number. Under the figures it is at least two: description length (bits, Kolmogorov or MDL) and settling cost (actions, experiments, proofs). A short algorithm for a theory can be expensive to verify, and a long one can be refuted in one step. Neither figure predicts the other. Fig 12 :128: "Neither figure predicts the other, so both are read or the choice is made blind."

World corroboration at low overlap: algorithmic information theory defines Kolmogorov complexity as "the length of a shortest computer program (in a predetermined programming language) that produces the object as output" and notes it is uncomputable; computational complexity theory defines BQP as "the class of decision problems solvable by a quantum computer in polynomial time, with an error probability of at most 1/3 for all instances." These are different quantities in different units, exactly as Fig 12 says. They corroborate the mechanism shape, not the figure verdict.

**Refuter:** exhibit one measure that prices description bits and settling actions in a single unit without losing information, or prove they are the same quantity. Failing that, any single "complexity of quantum gravity" number is a category error under this framework.

**Status:** `triangulated` (Fig 12 plus Fig 5; low overlap between bond pricing and MDL). World corroboration is genuine but secondary.

### F6 - Undecidability is a shape limit, not an answer (triangulated)

Fig 8 :34: "A frame cannot reach its own metaframe. Godel's second theorem: a consistent system cannot prove its own consistency." Fig 8 :37: "A frame cannot certify its own limit. Chaitin: beyond a fixed constant, no system proves that any object exceeds its own complexity." Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 9 :44: "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."

This is the one place where the question genuinely reaches complexity theory's own barrier results. A computational description of a theory cannot certify, from inside itself, that no shorter description exists; a search for a class that returns nothing is not proof that no class applies. Fig 6 :27: "Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same." Fig 8 :40-41: "Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions."

World sources agree at low overlap: spectral-gap undecidability proves there is "no algorithm to determine whether an arbitrary model is gapped or gapless" and that some questions are "independent of the axioms of mathematics"; algorithmic information theory records that Kolmogorov complexity is uncomputable; computational irreducibility says "the only way to determine the outcome of a process is to go through each step of its computation". These are proofs about the shape of limits, not measurements of spacetime.

**Refuter:** exhibit an instrument or a proof that discriminates underivable-in-principle from unreachable-at-this-budget inside one frame, or show a frame certifying its own limit. That would reverse Fig 8 :37 and is the cleanest available refutation of this finding.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the seat-map discipline, a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim and the residue table are refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Primitive test:** computation is derived as an indivisible seventh primitive that is load-bearing in an established law, or reducing the six to five plus computation preserves thermodynamics. Breaks F2.
2. **Currency test:** description bits and settling actions are shown to be one quantity, or are priced additively in one unit without loss. Breaks F4.
3. **Class derivation test:** a specific complexity class follows from the six-term set alone, with no D2 import, and excludes the alternatives. Breaks F3 and F5.
4. **Import test:** an imported class has a dated source and a shadow test: it explains a residual described before it was chosen. Failure leaves the import unlicensed rather than false, and it is recorded as such.
5. **Instrument test:** a real instrument at the relevant scale returns a discriminating reading, or a mathematical proof settles the exact class rather than a bound. Moves the claim from `candidate` to `grounded` and is the cleanest reversal of F7.
6. **Union test:** the existence and complexity conditions share one anchor and one instrument, so a single lever clears both. Breaks F1.
7. **Limit test:** a frame certifies its own limit, or a search that finds nothing is shown to prove absence. Breaks F6.

None of these was run in this simulated derivation. The refuter exists so a real checker can fire it before seeing the theory.
