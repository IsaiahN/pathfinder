## What the world sources do and do not do

1. **Undecidability is a real barrier, not a rhetorical one.** Cubitt et al. prove a general limit on a physics question. It corroborates Fig 8 :37 ("A frame cannot certify its own limit") at genuinely low overlap. It does **not** tell us whether quantum gravity is undecidable: the spectral-gap theorem is about arbitrary Hamiltonians, and importing it into this question needs a shadow test.
2. **A single complexity number is a frame-internal score.** BQP fixes a machine model and counts polynomial time; Kolmogorov complexity counts description length and is uncomputable. Fig 12 :125-126 says these prices do not add. The two complexity sources corroborate the two-currency shape; they are one mathematical pool, not two independent witnesses for the same claim.
3. **Holographic complexity is a conjecture, not a settlement.** Brown et al. explicitly conjecture a duality and call black holes the fastest computers in nature. It is a binding from the physics frame onto the vocabulary of complexity, not a ground reading at the Planck scale (Fig 2 :29).
4. **Computational irreducibility is the world's version of Fig 8 :39.** "A search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence" and "the only way to determine the outcome is to go through each step" are the same limit read in complexity science. Low overlap, genuine corroboration of the shape, not of the verdict.

No world source conflicts with the figure verdict, so the conflict rule was not triggered. Where a world source could be misread as deriving a class inside physics (Cubitt et al.), the audit re-census assigns it to an adopted import and the verdict is unchanged.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 10 :52** - "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Sending the full architecture before anyone describes a residual sends a recording, not a generator.
- **Fig 10 :42** - "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who has absorbed the framework before the verdict is recorded has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** - "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true before giving them the links.
- **Fig 2 :40** - "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." A send selected for people likely to agree produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Fig 8 :53** - "Residual first, frame second." The venue or person is chosen after the residual is described, never before.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the union split, the shape claim and the six refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and record the verdict; (3) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority; (4) only if asked, the formula as a method with each user's own ground and refuter.

## The two-stage offer

Stage 1 is Fig 10 :52 and Fig 2 :45 ("Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."). Stage 2 is opt-in by construction: a reader who has absorbed the theory before the stage 1 verdict cannot later be pooled as a witness. Stage 3 sends a method, which Fig 4 :22 permits because a generator can be given away without giving away an answer. Each user still needs a ground per problem; the trap essay says "the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."

**Hard precondition:** the offer only works if the stage 1 output survives the six refuter conditions in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4. As written in the original question it does not: it joins existence and class as one question, treats complexity as one number, and asks for a name where the framework returns a shape. The corrected output is in `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md`.
