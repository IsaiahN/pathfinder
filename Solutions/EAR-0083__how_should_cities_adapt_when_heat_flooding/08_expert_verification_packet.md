# Expert verification packet — the Tether on compound urban climate hazards

**What this is:** a claim in a form an urban climate adaptation researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the three sub-claims below; `general` on the structural skeleton. Not `grounded`.** No city was measured. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Cities adapting when heat, flooding, water stress and sea-level rise occur together" is a union compound, not four programmes added together. The intersections are specific compounds, and the losses that matter arrive at the third step out through coupled infrastructure. The city is a habitat to be enumerated by contact, never composed from sectors; adaptation is introduction into a habitat nobody authored, so exclusion is unavailable and the work is permanent. An answer must therefore be a route: a sequence of moves with stated thresholds, a measured subtraction on the compound residual, and recorded provenance for every local technology introduced. The objective on the compound must be instrumented before measures above the break are optimised, and the anchor is realised loss rather than modelled risk or agreement in a planning room. The framework names no seawall, tree species or retreat line.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — A single lever leaves the intersections standing (framework internal plus field-facing)

- **Statement:** the four conditions form a union; a lever that clears one condition does not clear the compound, and a case in two conditions is categorically different from a harder case of either.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55-56 — *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."* Fig 9 :57 — *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."* Also Fig 9 :54 — *"unbundle before you search again."*
- **Field check:** IPCC AR6 WGII Chapter 6 records that observed losses arise from single, compound, cascading and systemic events, and that compound losses arise when one hazard driver interacts with at least one other.
- **Refuter:** a measured city time series in which a single-hazard lever drives the compound residual to zero at every pairwise intersection, with no second condition binding.

### C2 — Contact cascades to the third step, so the habitat must be enumerated (framework internal plus field-facing)

- **Statement:** enumeration must run outward from the residual until the cascade stops mattering, and adaptation is introduction rather than removal, permanent rather than finished.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :43-44 — *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."* Fig 11 :52-53 — *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* Fig 11 :61-63 — *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."*
- **Field check:** IPCC Chapter 6 gives the flood-to-blackout-to-health cascade, and states that current adaptation approaches tend to move risk from one sector or place to others. CCP2 records that hard protection creates long-term path dependency and locks in exposure.
- **Refuter:** a cascade enumeration for a real city that stops at first order with no third-step effect on realised loss, or an adaptation that removes rather than substitutes the habitat.

### C3 — The answer is a route with an instrumented objective (framework internal plus field-facing)

- **Statement:** a plan is a sequence, a goal is a comparison, progress is a subtraction; the dependency chain breaks at the objective unless there is an instrument for the compound.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :111-112 — *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* Fig 3 :27 — *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Fig 6 :32 — *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."*
- **Field check:** CCP2 describes adaptation pathways as near-term low-regret actions and a locally appropriate sequence under uncertain futures. IPCC Chapter 6 records the lack of agreement on metrics and indices for urban adaptation investment, impacts and outcomes.
- **Refuter:** a checkable route on the compound that can be stated without an ordered sequence, a threshold and a measured subtraction, or a demonstrated compound objective with an instrument already returning a reading.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a partition reading: four independent hazard programmes are not an answer.
- Not a resilience score as an anchor. Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
- Not a specific technology, species, wall height or retreat line. Those are local imports with a source gate and shadow test.
- Not a finished state. Fig 7 :6 names *"the limit: no last room."*
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by a city measurement.
- Not corroborated by agreement. Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."*

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading on a named city, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument reads green but *"cannot comment on the source"*; a spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. A per-slot compound-residual time series for each named intersection, extending existing mortality, gauge, outage and loss records. Does it trend down after a sequenced intervention?
2. Does the third-order effect disappear, or merely transfer to another district?
3. Does a stated threshold fire and cause a revisit?
4. Do the seven falsifiers in `01` section 3 survive? In particular: does any single lever clear every intersection, and can a city become competent with no change in contact?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :54-58, Fig 11 :43-44 and :52-53 and :61-64, and Fig 12 :111-112. Five short passages.
2. Check C1 against one city you know: do the intersections of heat, flood, water stress and sea-level rise behave as specific compounds rather than as a sum of single hazards?
3. Name one instrument already returning a reading of the compound, or say why none exists. Do not design a new one from scratch (Fig 6 :32).
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
