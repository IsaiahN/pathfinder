## Wider literature that bears on the claim

1. **Compound and cascading loss.** IPCC Chapter 6 gives the field's own vocabulary: losses arise from single, compound, cascading and systemic events, and compound losses arise when one hazard driver interacts with at least one other. This corroborates the figure reading of a union compound rather than a partition. One assessment programme, so one evidence pool.
2. **Adaptation pathways.** CCP2 describes near-term low-regret actions and a locally appropriate sequence of interventions under uncertain futures, plus the risk that hard protection locks in exposure. This corroborates the figure claim that an answer must be a route with thresholds and reversibility.
3. **Wicked problems.** Rittel and Webber 1973, as recorded on the Wikipedia page, gives no stopping rule and the claim that solving one aspect reveals or creates other problems. This is a genuinely low-overlap field (planning and policy theory) and it predicts the same risk-transfer shape. It is the strongest independent corroboration in this package, though it is a secondary encyclopedia record rather than the primary paper.
4. **Bundle of problems.** The Library of Babel essay states the union rule in the framework author's own words. High overlap, so weak corroboration, but useful as a check that the derivation did not strain the figure line.
5. **Source versus reading.** The Hard Problem is About Source essay says an instrument reads green but cannot comment on the source, and that a better instrument resolved it. Applied here: a falling modelled-risk index does not identify which intervention caused the fall.

**Recommended order in any reply:** (1) the compound claim and its five refuters; (2) ask what the recipient would count as a failure; (3) record that verdict; (4) only then offer the figure text and formula links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.
