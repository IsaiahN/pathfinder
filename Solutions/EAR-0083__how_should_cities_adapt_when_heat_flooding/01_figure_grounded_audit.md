## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

**Do not start with the nouns.** "Heat, flooding, water stress, sea-level rise" is the question's vocabulary, not the residual. Naming four hazards and then looking for four measures is picking frames before describing the gap, which Fig 9 :27 warns against: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."*

The residual in effect terms is this:

> A city must commit adaptation spending now. A lever that clears one condition leaves a positive residual at the intersection with the other conditions, and the loss that matters arrives later and elsewhere: flood water damages power, lost power disables cooling, lost cooling kills; a sea wall holds until a threshold and then transfers risk to the next district. The failure is not four missing measures. It is that the measure is aimed at a slot while the loss lives in the intersections and the third step out.

R by arity, symmetry, scale:

- **Arity.** Four named conditions, but they are not independent operands. They share drivers (a warming atmosphere holds more moisture and raises baseline sea level), and they couple through the city's infrastructure. This is a **union compound**, not a partition of one hazard space (Fig 9 :54-58).
- **Symmetry.** Not a conjunction. A threshold relation dominates: a defence works up to a level and then fails (Fig 12 comparison ⋛, *"a threshold relation between two quantities"*). Order matters: flood then drought is not drought then flood. Under an ordered bond, different orders are different terms.
- **Scale.** First order: one hazard, one sector, one instrument. Second order: intersections, where two conditions bind. Third order: cascades across systems, where the residual that mattered was three steps out (Fig 11 :52-53).

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term (Formula "Direction").** The sought-for shape is written down here before the derivation so it can accrue a residual like anything else:

> An answer is a *route*: (i) an enumeration of the compound cases and their contact cascade, (ii) a sequence of introductions into a habitat the city did not author, each with a stated threshold at which it is revisited, (iii) a measured residual on the compound itself, not four single-hazard indicators, and (iv) a recorded lineage saying which bindings came from local measurement and which came from models. It is revisable, because there is no last room.

This binding is the preference the loop cannot check from inside (Fig 5 :62-66, Formula "Direction"). It is stated so a refuter can point at it.

## 1. Domain census, before choosing any figure line

One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor. No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 Hazard physics and exposure** | heat, pluvial/coastal flood, water stress, sea-level rise, subsidence, urban heat island | energy and water flows, gradients over space and time | measured quantity: temperature, gauge height, salinity, mortality, insurance loss | union of overlapping conditions, not a partition |
| **D2 The urban habitat** | grey infrastructure (drainage, walls, power, transport), nature-based infrastructure (trees, wetlands), social infrastructure (health, community networks), governance and finance | contact, and the capability that comes from contact | realised loss and survival, which do not update on what planners think | habitat enumerated, never composed |
| **D3 Decision and pathway** | city governments, communities, regulators, insurers, finance | sequences of interventions, options kept open | the same ground: observed outcomes, never agreement among planners | dependency chain with a broken middle link |

- **Partition or union?** Union. The four conditions overlap and cascade; a case in two conditions is categorically different from a harder case of either. D2 and D3 are nested in D1's contact field rather than disjoint from it. An overlap would be a compound with its own loop, and that is exactly what each intersection is.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Perception (measure the compound and the cascade), then vocabulary (terms for what an intervention affords at second order), then objective (what counts as progress *on the compound*), then planning (a route under incomplete information), then learn and carry (monitor, name what worked). The chain is *"ordered by dependency, not by importance"* (Fig 3 :20). Effort spent above a break cannot rescue the link below it (Fig 3 :19).

**Anchor test applied.** D1 has an anchor only where there is an instrument: a gauge, a thermometer, a death certificate, a loss ledger. D2 has one in realised loss. D3's anchor is the same realised loss, not a planning consensus. Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* A resilience score agreed by a room of planners moves when the room moves, so it fails the anchor test and cannot be a verdict.

### F1 — This is a union compound, and a single lever leaves the intersections standing (decisive, triangulated)

Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*

Fig 9 :55-56: *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*

Fig 9 :57-58: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*

The question itself bundles four conditions, so it is already a union, not four separate questions and not one bigger question. Fig 9 :59 names the second-order payoff: *"not that the pile is smaller, but that the residue is finally describable."* The practical instruction is to name the intersections (hot and flood; flood then drought; sea-level rise plus flood plus water stress) and give each its own ground and refuter before searching for measures.

World corroboration, lower overlap (planning theory): the Wikipedia entry for *Wicked problem*, opened 2026-11-22, recording Rittel and Webber 1973, says *"Because of complex interdependencies, the effort to solve one aspect of a wicked problem may reveal or create other problems."* Same shape, different evidence pool. The *Library of Babel* essay (Isaiah N, 2026-10-02), opened this run, says: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."* High overlap with the framework author, so weak corroboration, but it is the author's own statement of the union rule.

**Refuter:** a measured city time series in which a single-hazard lever drives the compound residual to zero at every pairwise intersection, with no second condition binding.

### F2 — Contact cascades three steps out, so first-order enumeration is how the loss is missed (triangulated)

Fig 11 :52-53: *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*

A flood plan that enumerates water but not power, cooling and health has scoped to direct contact. The figure's own example is ecological, and the transfer is structural, not a claim that cities are ecosystems. Fig 11 :48: *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."*

World corroboration: IPCC AR6 WGII Chapter 6 (Dodman, Hayward, Pelling et al., 2022), opened this run, states *"Observed losses arise from single, compound, cascading and systemic events (medium evidence, high agreement )."* It gives the exact cascade: *"when flood waters damage energy infrastructure causing blackouts and knock on financial and human impacts."* IPCC WGII Cross-Chapter Paper 2 (Glavovic, Dawson, Chow et al., 2022) adds that *"compound and cascading risks significantly alter C&S risk profiles."* Two IPCC products, but one assessment programme, so one evidence pool, not two witnesses.

**Refuter:** a cascade enumeration for a real city that stops at first order with no third-step effect on realised loss.

### F3 — The city is a habitat: enumerate it, never compose it; introduce, never subtract (triangulated)

Fig 11 :43-44: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."*

Fig 11 :61-62: *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not. You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."*

Fig 11 :63-64: *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."*

Adaptation is therefore a continuing introduction into a habitat the city did not author, not the installation of a finished defence. There is no "remove the hazard" option. Fig 11 :40: *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* A better plan document, with no changed contact, changes nothing.

Retreat is not removal. Fig 11 :66-67: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* *"Move the residual and you must reproduce everything its goal required, and you inherit the new habitat's actors, which you did not choose."* The two silent failure modes are recorded at Fig 11 :68-69: *"what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"* and *"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts."* Planned relocation is a habitat substitution, not a clean exit.

World corroboration: IPCC Chapter 6: *"The most consistent limit for all infrastructure types is in risk transfer. Current adaptation approaches in cities, settlements and key infrastructure tend to move risk from one sector or place to others."* CCP2: *"hard protective measures also create long-term path dependency as they last for decades and attract new development, locking in impact and exposure as C&S grow."* Same shape as exclusion-is-unavailable and permanent work.

**Refuter:** a city that attains durable competence on the compound by improving the plan alone with no change in contact, or an adaptation that removes the habitat rather than substituting one.

### F4 — The answer must be a route: sequence, goal, progress (triangulated)

Fig 12 :111-112: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."*

A list of measures (trees, walls, pumps, reservoirs) names ingredients. It cannot say what follows what, at what threshold a measure is revisited, or how far along the city is. The Operators table defines → as *"A then B; B needs A to have happened"* and ⋛ as *"a threshold relation between two quantities."* A "climate-resilient city" declared by a score is a goal stated as a verdict, which Seat Map :281 names as the output-layer failure: *"an unmeasured quantity is a specification of what to measure and is worth having, it is not a verdict."*

World corroboration: CCP2: *"An adaptation-pathways approach sets out near-term 'low-regret' actions that align with societal goals, facilitates implementation of a locally appropriate sequence of interventions in the face of uncertain climate and development futures, and enables necessary transformation (high confidence)."* This is the sequence/comparison shape in the field's own vocabulary. Knapp, quoted on the *Wicked problem* page: *"shift the goal of action on significant problems from 'solution' to 'intervention'"*, with further actions always needed.

**Refuter:** a checkable route on the compound that can be stated without an ordered sequence, a threshold, and a measured subtraction.

### F5 — The chain breaks at the objective, and the first lever is an instrument (triangulated)

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* Fig 3 :29: *"Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed."*

Most urban adaptation effort lands above the break, where measurements already exist (engineering designs, heat maps, flood models). The compound objective, link 3, has no agreed instrument. IPCC Chapter 6 records the gap verbatim: *"A lack of agreement on metrics and indices to measure urban adaptation investment, impacts and outcomes, reduces the scope for sharing lessons and joined-up action across interconnected sectors and places in the face of compound and systemic risks."*

Fig 6 :32 gives the instruction for building the missing instrument: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* The worse instruments already returning something exist: heat mortality counts, flood gauge records, outage logs, insurance losses, clinic presentations. Extend those; do not commission a designed-from-nothing resilience score.

Fig 5 :48: *"Its own output is never a measure of success. A system can be enthusiastically curious and produce nothing."* A plan count is not progress.

**Refuter:** a demonstrated compound objective with an instrument that returns a reading today, plus evidence the chain reaches planning.

### F6 — The ground is realised loss, not modelled risk and not agreement (triangulated)

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :33-34: *"The anchor must not update. If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."*

Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Here the cost is mortality, stranded assets and locked-in exposure, so the price of a single evidence pool is high. Fig 2 :40: *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."* Low-overlap witnesses for this residual are: utility outage logs (engineering), mortality and clinic data (health), insurance loss ledgers (finance), and resident and Indigenous local knowledge (on-the-ground contact), each ranging separately against realised outcomes.

Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict."* More workshops produce a larger vocabulary, not an anchor.

**Refuter:** an anchor for urban adaptation that changes its answer when a planner, a modeller or a stakeholder room pushes on it.

### F7 — Metric transfer is the named failure, and it is the single-lever failure again

IPCC Chapter 6: *"Current adaptation approaches in cities, settlements and key infrastructure tend to move risk from one sector or place to others."* Fig 11 :61 says exclusion is unavailable, so moved risk is not deleted risk; it reappears in another slot. Fig 9 :55-56 says the second condition still binds. The wicked-problem literature says the same from planning theory: *"Every wicked problem can be considered to be a symptom of another problem."*

This is one finding reached from three low-overlap routes, so it is recorded once rather than counted three times.

**Refuter:** a transferred risk that stays eliminated in the receiving sector and district over a full hazard cycle.

### F8 — Shape, not a name: the framework cannot pick the wall, the tree or the retreat line (single-source for the limit, corroborated at high overlap)

Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* The figures README says the framework reports *"a shape, not a name"* for a missing thing. Fig 13 :134-135: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence, It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

So the derivation returns: union compound, contact cascade to third order, habitat enumerated, route with thresholds, instrument before verdict, ground of realised loss. It does not return "build this seawall", "plant this mangrove", or "retreat to this line". Those are bindings from hydraulics, ecology, local knowledge and finance, and each must be provenanced as a binding. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (opened this run): *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"*, and an instrument reads green but *"cannot comment on the source."* A drop in modelled risk is a reading; which intervention caused it is a source question that needs a discriminating instrument, not an argument.

**Refuter:** a figure line that yields a particular adaptation technology from the six-term substrate alone, without an import from the local frame.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A single lever drives a measured compound residual to zero at every pairwise intersection, with no second condition binding (breaks Fig 9 :55-56).
2. A cascade enumeration for a real city stops at first order and no third-step effect appears in realised loss (breaks Fig 11 :52-53).
3. A city becomes durably competent on the compound by improving the plan alone, with no change in contact (breaks Fig 11 :40).
4. Adaptation is demonstrated to be a solved state requiring no further introduction (breaks Fig 11 :63).
5. A checkable route on the compound is stated without an ordered sequence, a threshold and a measured subtraction (breaks Fig 12 :111-112).
6. An anchor is produced that changes its answer when a planning room pushes on it (breaks Fig 2 :29, :33).
7. A specific adaptation technology is derived from the six-term substrate alone, with no import from the local frame (breaks Fig 9 :32).

If any fires, the corresponding shape claim is withdrawn and the retraction is recorded in section 5 and in `11_audit.md`. Until one is run, nothing here may be labelled `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Rename the problem before solving it: four conditions in a union, not four programmes. Name each intersection as its own compound with its own ground (F1).
2. Enumerate the habitat by reading the world: utilities, health, transport, water, finance, communities, and their second and third contacts (F2, F3).
3. Instrument link 3 first. Extend gauges, mortality records, outage logs and loss ledgers into a compound residual; do not buy a designed-from-scratch index (F5, Fig 6 :32).
4. Write the answer as a sequence with thresholds and reversibility. Fig 9 :41: *"so keep every cut ranked and reversible."* Prefer near-term low-regret moves that keep options open over irreversible hard protection (F4).
5. Treat relocation as habitat substitution: list what must be reproduced and what will be inherited before moving anyone (F3, Fig 11 :66-69).
6. Range separate witnesses against realised loss and never pool agreement as corroboration (F6).
7. Send the residual and the refuter first (`02`, `08`), never the framework first (Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*).
