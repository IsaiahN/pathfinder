# The Tether's answer: how cities adapt when four hazards coincide

**Mode: `specified` on every substantive claim below; `general` on the structural skeleton. Nothing here is `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading of any city.

## 1. The residual it answers

A city must commit adaptation spending now under coupled and deepening uncertainty. The failure is not four missing measures. It is that a lever aimed at one condition clears the region where only one condition holds while the intersections remain, and the loss that matters arrives at the third step out (flood damages power, lost power disables cooling, lost cooling kills). Fig 9 :55-56: *"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*

The question is a **union compound**, not four separate questions and not one bigger question (Fig 9 :54-58). It has its own loop and its own ground.

## 2. The shape

1. **Unbundle before searching.** Name the intersections as specific compounds: hot and flood; flood then drought; sea-level rise plus flood plus water stress. Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."* Do not pick a lead hazard before describing the residual (Fig 8 :53: *"Residual first, frame second."*).

2. **Enumerate the habitat outward, never compose it.** Fig 11 :43-44: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."* Read the list off the world: utilities, health, transport, water, finance, communities, and their second and third contacts. Fig 11 :52-53 warns that first-order enumeration is exactly how the third-step loss is missed.

3. **Adaptation is introduction, not subtraction.** Fig 11 :61-63: *"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."* There is no "remove the hazard" option. Relocation is habitat substitution, not a clean exit (Fig 11 :66-69), so list what must be reproduced and what will be inherited before moving anyone.

4. **The answer is a route, not an ingredient list.** Fig 12 :111-112: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."* *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."* A route states what follows what, the threshold at which each move is revisited, and the measured subtraction on the compound residual. Order matters: flood-then-heat and heat-then-flood are different terms (`06` Q4). The route must be a cycle (monitored and revisable), not a chain, and must record which parts regenerate and which are spent (`06` Q5).

5. **Instrument the objective before optimising the measures.** Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* The chain usually breaks at link 3, the objective on the compound. IPCC Chapter 6 records the missing agreement on metrics and indices for urban adaptation outcomes. Extend instruments already returning something, mortality, gauges, outage logs, loss ledgers (Fig 6 :32: *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."*).

6. **Anchor on realised loss, not modelled risk or agreement.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* Here that cost is mortality and locked-in exposure, so range engineering, health, finance and local-knowledge witnesses separately. Do not pool agreement as corroboration (Fig 2 :40; Fig 7 :35 *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict."*).

7. **The framework names no technology.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Seawalls, mangroves, cooling centres and retreat lines are bindings from hydraulics, ecology and local knowledge, provenanced as such. What the six-term set contributes is the shape of the route, not the choice of atom to introduce.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not: four independent hazard programmes added together. That is the partition reading refused in F1.
- Not: one resilience score settles whether a city is adapted. A score that moves with the room fails the anchor test.
- Not: a specific technology, species, wall height or retreat line. Those are local imports with their own source gate and shadow test.
- Not: adaptation is finished. Fig 11 :63 makes permanent work structural, and Fig 7 :6 names *"the limit: no last room."*
- Not `grounded`. No city's compound residual was computed here.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A named city, a pre-registered compound residual, and instruments that discriminate among sources. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument reading green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it, not an argument. Here:

- **Compound residual:** a per-slot time series for each named intersection, extending existing mortality, gauge, outage and loss records. Does it trend down after a sequenced intervention?
- **Cascade test:** does the third-order effect disappear, or merely move to another district? IPCC Chapter 6 warns that current approaches *"tend to move risk from one sector or place to others."*
- **Threshold test:** does a stated trigger fire and cause a revisit, or is the plan followed past its limit?
- **Standing falsifiers:** the seven in `01` section 3. In particular, a single lever that clears every intersection, or a city that becomes competent with no change in contact.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The six-term set is *"a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes"* (*They're Scanning Books*), so declaring it makes the choice visible, not neutral. Instruments exist that already return readings (SEARCHED and INWARD are open); frames with different closures exist (OUTWARD is open). Neither was run as a measurement in this simulated derivation. The answer stops at the limit of what the available instruments distinguish, and the lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

A further limit specific to this domain: the ground is slow. Cities and climates change on decadal scales, so a verdict that looks settled this year may be below the union horizon next year. That is a reason to keep the route revisable, not a reason to withhold the shape.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not give a city a list of adaptation projects, and it does not claim a finished master plan. It says the four hazards are a union compound, not four programmes added together, so the intersections must be named before any measure is chosen; that the city's contact habitat must be enumerated outward to the third-order effect, because the losses concentrate where one system's failure becomes another's; that adaptation is introduction into a habitat nobody authored, so exclusion is unavailable and the work is permanent; that the answer must be a route with sequence, thresholds and a measured subtraction, not a menu; and that the objective on the compound must be instrumented before the measures above the break are optimised. The framework names no seawall, tree or retreat line. It returns the shape any candidate answer must have, the falsifiers written in advance, and the ground readings that would close it. The route stays revisable until the ground settles it.
