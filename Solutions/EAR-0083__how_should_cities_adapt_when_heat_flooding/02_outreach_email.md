# Outreach draft — check the output first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the compound objective in `01` section 0 has been given a name and a refuter that a practitioner can recognise. A two-stage offer collapses on contact if stage 1 is a slogan.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about compound urban climate risk, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know from practice. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** "cities adapting to heat, flooding, water stress and sea-level rise together" is not four programmes added together. The figures treat it as a union compound. A lever that clears the region where only one condition holds leaves the intersections standing because the second condition still binds, and a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either, it is a specific compound. The losses that matter arrive at the third step out: flood damages power, lost power disables cooling, lost cooling kills.
>
> What follows from that is a **shape**, not a prescription:
> 1. enumerate the contact habitat outward to the third-order effect, reading it off the world rather than composing it from sectors;
> 2. treat adaptation as introduction into a habitat the city did not author, so exclusion is unavailable and the work is permanent, not a solved state;
> 3. write the answer as a **route**: a sequence of moves, a stated threshold at which each is revisited, and a measured subtraction on the compound residual;
> 4. instrument the compound objective before optimising measures above the break, extending instruments already returning something (mortality, gauges, outages, losses);
> 5. range separate witnesses against realised loss, never against agreement in a planning room.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that this tells you which seawall, which tree species, or which retreat line to choose. The figures return a shape, not a name. Those bindings come from hydraulics, ecology, local knowledge and finance, and each is owed its own provenance and ground. I am also not claiming a resilience score settles whether a city is adapted. A score that moves when the room moves is not an anchor.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a single lever drives a measured compound residual to zero at every pairwise intersection, with no second condition binding;
> 2. a cascade enumeration for a real city stops at first order and no third-step effect appears in realised loss;
> 3. a city becomes competent on the compound by improving the plan alone with no change in contact;
> 4. an adaptation is demonstrated to be a solved state requiring no further introduction;
> 5. a checkable route is stated without an ordered sequence, a threshold and a measured subtraction.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape claim is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop and mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - Public IPCC assessment chapter on cities, for the field's own statement of compound and cascading loss: https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg2/chapter/chapter-6/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [name]
