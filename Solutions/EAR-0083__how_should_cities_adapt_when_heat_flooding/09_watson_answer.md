## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the choice a city faces when four climate hazards coincide. Option A: treat the question as four hazard programmes added together, pick a lead hazard, and optimise measures against single-hazard indicators. Option B: treat it as a union compound, enumerate the contact habitat outward to the third-order effect, instrument the objective on the compound, and commit to a sequenced, thresholded, revisable route. What each leads to: A spends the budget on regions where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections and the cascades standing; B yields a checkable shape with falsifiers written in advance and an open slot for every local technology until a ground reading settles it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** A city must commit adaptation spending now while a lever that clears one hazard slot leaves a positive residual at the intersections, and the decisive loss arrives three steps out through coupled infrastructure. Arity: four named conditions, not independent. Symmetry: not a conjunction; thresholds (⋛) and order (→) dominate, so flood then heat is a different term from heat then flood. Scale: first order inside one hazard and sector, second order at intersections, third order at cascades. Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."* Union, not partition.
- **Domain 1, hazard physics and exposure.** Actors: heat, pluvial and coastal flood, water stress, sea-level rise, subsidence, urban heat island. Currency: energy and water flows, gradients over space and time. Anchor: a measured quantity, thermometer, gauge, salinity reading, mortality count, loss ledger. Union of overlapping conditions.
- **Domain 2, the urban habitat.** Actors: grey infrastructure (drainage, walls, power, transport), nature-based infrastructure (trees, wetlands), social infrastructure (health, community networks), governance and finance. Currency: contact and the capability that comes from contact. Anchor: realised loss and survival, which do not update on what planners think. Habitat enumerated, never composed.
- **Domain 3, decision and pathway.** Actors: city governments, communities, regulators, insurers, finance. Currency: sequences of interventions and options kept open. Anchor: the same realised outcomes, never agreement among planners. Dependency chain with a broken middle link.
- **Order by dependency.** Perceive the compound and the cascade (link 1), find terms for what an intervention affords at second order (link 2), state an objective on the compound (link 3), choose a route under incomplete information (link 4), then monitor and carry (link 5). Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the menu does not. The four hazards form a union compound whose intersections are specific compounds, the contact habitat must be enumerated outward to the third-order effect, adaptation is introduction into an unauthored habitat and therefore permanent work, the answer is a sequenced and thresholded route with a measured subtraction, the objective on the compound must be instrumented before measures above the break are optimised, and the anchor is realised loss rather than modelled risk or agreement. No specific technology is derived. This is a `specified`, ungrounded, candidate result. The strongest outside point is low-overlap corroboration from planning theory: solving one aspect of a wicked problem reveals or creates other problems, which is the figure's single-lever failure in another vocabulary.

- **cost if wrong:** high and locatable. IPCC Chapter 6 records that current approaches tend to move risk from one sector or place to others, and CCP2 records that hard protection locks in exposure through path dependency. Choosing the partition reading buys a plan that clears single-hazard slots while the intersections keep killing people. The avoidable part is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a recording instead of a generator, which Fig 2 :42 prices directly.
- **undoable:** partly. The shape itself is undoable by a refutation; no primitive has to be withdrawn and the seven refuters are written in advance. Physical commitments are not equally undoable: hard protection creates path dependency, so the figure's instruction at Fig 9 :41 to keep every cut ranked and reversible favours near-term low-regret moves and an adaptation-pathways sequence over irreversible works. Relocation is a habitat substitution and must be treated as hard to reverse because what was failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A per-slot compound-residual time series on a named city showing a single lever driving every intersection to zero; a cascade enumeration that stops at first order with no third-step effect; a city becoming competent with no change in contact; an adaptation demonstrated to be a solved state; a route with no sequence, threshold or subtraction; an anchor that moves when a planning room pushes on it; a specific technology derived from the six-term set alone. Any one fires the corresponding pre-registered refuter in `01` section 3. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
