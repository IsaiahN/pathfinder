## 1. The residual, in effect terms

A system is prepared in a state that, expressed in some basis, has k components (k ≥ 2). The theory's prediction Γ(b,a) for the outcome slot is a distribution over those components; under unitary evolution the system plus apparatus remains a superposition of terms, one per component. The observation o′ returned by a detector is one definite value. So

```
R = |Γ(b,a) - o′| on the outcome slot s
```

is live: the formalism does not say which single value the slot will carry. Globally R is near zero, because the Born rule matches the relative frequencies of many runs. Both are legal at once.

> Fig 1 :13 "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"
> Symbols table :43 "One object, region or entity being tracked. R is measured per slot, never as a single global number."
> Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

The usual reply, "the Born rule gives the statistics, so the problem is solved," is exactly the aggregation the figure forbids. It averages the live slot into the ensemble and reports the disappearance of the signal as its resolution. Fig 1 :23 "The ground enters as the only metric." The ground here is the single detector reading, not the histogram.

**Stated as an effect, not a cause:** many candidate components are available; one is actualised on each run; the held theory has no term that performs the actualisation. What failed to resolve is the one-slot prediction, not the many-run frequencies.

## 2. Domain census (before any figure line is chosen)

### 2a. R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** many-to-one. k amplitude components map to one registered value. The residual relates the k to the one; it is not a two-body disagreement.
- **Symmetry:** within a chosen basis the components are interchangeable with respect to the question "which outcome" until registration; registration breaks that symmetry. There is a second symmetry over the choice of basis itself, which the apparatus in practice breaks. Both symmetries are broken by the same event, so "what selects one" and "which basis" are not automatically the same question (they are separately named in the literature; see F3).
- **Scale:** the step (one trial, one outcome) and the cycle (many trials, frequencies). The round trip between them is where the aggregation happens (Fig 4 :30).

### 2b. One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor

- **Domain 1, the unitary formalism frame.** Actors: state vector, Hamiltonian, amplitudes, basis, tensor product, Born rule. Currency: unitary evolution and probability. Anchor: the prepared state and the detector reading. A nameable anchor exists, so this is a domain.
- **Domain 2, the detector and record frame.** Actors: apparatus pointer, environment, observer's record. Currency: definite outcomes and relative frequencies. Anchor: the detector click. This is the ground for the physics question because the question is constitutively about definite outcomes (Fig 2 :36).
- **Domain 3, the Tether composition frame.** Actors: the six terms, the seven bonds, negation, Γ, R, the ground. Currency: composition inside closure(Γ), then settlement. Anchor: the figures as metalaws, plus the ground for any measurement.
- **Domain 4, the verification and outreach frame.** Actors: expert readers, interpretations as frames. Currency: agreement and residuals. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run all four domains draw on one evidence pool, so the second seat's independence is zero (declare it; see `11_audit.md`).

No domain without a nameable anchor is admitted. "Consciousness causes collapse" would not be a domain here until a nameable anchor for it is produced; it is recorded as a proposed import, not an admitted domain (F8).

### 2c. Partition or union?

**Union, not partition.** The residual splits into at least three conditions: preferred basis, non-observability of interference, and definite outcomes. Each has its own successful machinery and its own failure. Decoherence addresses the first two and leaves the third standing. Overlap is a compound with its own loop.

> Fig 9 :4 "First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."
> Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
> Fig 9 :57 "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"

### 2d. Order by dependency (Fig 3)

> Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."

1. Describe R in effect terms before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).
2. Hold the detector reading fixed as anchor while interpretations range against it (Fig 2 :45).
3. Settle basis and interference before the outcome question; a lever that clears the first two leaves the compound residue (Fig 9 :54-57).
4. Refuse a false held term before minting a replacement (Formula step 2).
5. Only then offer imports, with provenance and a shadow test (Fig 6, Fig 8).

## 4. Findings

### F1: The residual is per slot, and the ensemble answer aggregates it away (decisive, triangulated)

> Fig 1 :13 "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"
> Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
> Symbols table :43 "One object, region or entity being tracked. R is measured per slot, never as a single global number."

The single-outcome question survives the correctness of Born statistics. A global R near zero is a legal state alongside one live slot. This is not a puzzle about the accuracy of quantum mechanics; it is a puzzle about which quantity is being measured. **Verdict: `specified`, triangulated** across Fig 1 (residual instrumentation) and the Symbols table (notation), low overlap between them. **Refuter:** show that the per-slot residual trends to zero under any resolution-preserving reading of the formalism, so that the ensemble distribution is itself a per-slot prediction rather than an aggregate.

**World check (opened 2026-11-23).** Tomaz, Mattos and Barbatti, *The Quantum Measurement Problem: A Review of Recent Trends*, arXiv:2502.19278v3 (2025), write: "The Born rule accurately predicts the statistical distribution of outcomes over many measurements. Still, it does not explain why a particular result occurs or what selects it in an individual run." That is F1 stated in physics language by an independent source. It corroborates, not decides (Fig 2 :40: one evidence pool is one witness; here the pools are genuinely different, framework versus physics review, so the corroboration is weak but real).

### F2 - The question is a union compound, not one hard question (triangulated)

> Fig 9 :4 "First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."
> Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."

The field already has the split. Tomaz et al. (2025), following Schlosshauer, name three problems: preferred basis, non-observability of interference, and outcomes. Bacciagaluppi, *The Role of Decoherence in Quantum Mechanics*, SEP (first published 2003, substantive revision 2025), gives the related three-aspect Maudlin decomposition: "that measurements have results, that these results obtain with some characteristic probabilities, and that depending on the result of a measurement the state of the system is generally transformed in a characteristic way." Myrvold, *Philosophical Issues in Quantum Theory*, SEP (2016, revision 2022), gives Bell's dilemma and three strategies: deny completeness, modify the dynamics, or hold both.

**Verdict: `specified`, triangulated** across Fig 9 (search topology) and the independent physics sources (different evidence pool). **Refuter:** a result showing the three conditions share one mechanism such that a single lever closes all three, which would make it one question after all. Decoherence is not that lever; see F3.

### F3 - Decoherence clears the wrong two conditions (single-source for the figure, corroborated)

> Fig 8 :53 "Residual first, frame second."
> Fig 9 :27 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"

Bacciagaluppi (SEP, 2003/2025): "while decoherence *does* explain why we do *not* observe superpositions of measurement results, it does *not* explain why we *do* observe measurement results in the first place." Tomaz et al. (2025): "Decoherence does not tell how and why only one of these outcomes is measured."

The figure verdict: a lever that clears the interference and basis conditions and leaves the outcome condition standing has cleared the region where one condition holds and left the compound residue. That is Fig 9 :54-57 exactly. **Verdict: `specified`.** The figure line is single-source for the structural reading; the physics claim is corroborated by two reviews that share a literature pool, so count them as one witness. **Refuter:** a derivation of unique outcomes from decoherence alone, with no added dynamical or interpretive atom.

### F4 - Definite outcomes are unreachable inside the unitary closure (triangulated)

> Fig 6 :21 "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."
> Fig 6 :22 "NOVEL means novel relative to atoms, not to the world. IMPORT adds an atom, and the added atom is what expands the closure."
> Fig 8 :32 "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."

Standard quantum mechanics, taken as a closed frame Γ of unitary evolution plus the Born rule, does not contain a term that maps a superposition to one actual outcome. Fig 6 :21 and Fig 8 :32 say the same thing by two independent arguments, one compositional and one metamathematical. No amount of search inside that closure manufactures the missing atom. Every proposed resolution therefore imports: a collapse dynamics (GRW-type), hidden variables (Bohm-type), branching (Everett-type), or a cut (Copenhagen-type). Myrvold (SEP) lists exactly these strategies.

**Verdict: `specified`, triangulated** across Fig 6 and Fig 8 (low overlap between the two arguments). **Refuter:** a pure derivation of a definite single-run outcome from unitary dynamics and the Born rule with no added or reinterpreted term. If that fires, F4 is false and the import verdict falls with it.

**Strain test, applied and refused.** Could the six-term substrate of Fig 13 govern this instead? A Hilbert-space state is not one of space, time, energy, spectrum, gradient, network. Forcing it there would make Fig 13 :74-75 contradict ordinary quantum mechanics. Per the canonical rule, a reading that makes figures contradict one another or the ground means the domain was misidentified. So Fig 13 is **not** used as the composition rule for this question. It is used only for its meta-level statements about unreachability and shape (F6). This is recorded as a refused strain, not a hidden one.

### F5 - Underivable is not the same as unreachable at this budget

> Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
> Fig 8 :40 "Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions."
> Fig 9 :44 "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."

F4 says the outcome term is underivable from the unitary closure. It does **not** say no frame anywhere holds it, and it does not justify giving up. Fig 8 :54, quoted in the tacit-knowledge essay: "underivable is not unknowable." The three instructions stay separate:

- **SEARCHED:** is it in this closure and unfound? For unitary-only QM the closure is defined narrowly enough that this is answered by F4, but for the broader physics frame it is still open; keep searching.
- **INWARD:** extend the instrument. Here that means a source-discriminating experiment, not a new argument (*The Hard Problem is About Source*, essay 2026-08-28: "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists.").
- **OUTWARD:** import from a frame whose closure differs, with a source gate and a shadow test.

**Verdict: `general`, single-source chain (Fig 8 plus Fig 9).** **Refuter:** a proof that the relevant closure is sealed, which Fig 9 :44 says cannot be done from inside; if someone does it, revise the figure reading.

### F6 - The framework returns a shape, not the name of the mechanism (gap declared)

> Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"
> Fig 13 :135 "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."
> Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"
> Fig 13 :136 "What it can do is narrow. It can say the question is not one question and must be split before it is searched again."

What the figures license here is a **shape** with three constraints:

1. the missing term lies outside the unitary closure (F4);
2. it must close the definite-outcome condition of the compound (F2), after basis and interference are handled;
3. it arrives only as a provenanced import with a shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :11-13), and its cost is charged in two currencies, bits and actions (Fig 12 :125-130).

What the figures do **not** license: choosing among collapse, hidden variables, branching, or a cut. Fig 13 :135 is explicit. So the verdict on the *physical mechanism* is a plain **gap**, not an evasion: "Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence." **Refuter:** an instrument that discriminates among the candidate mechanisms at the relevant scale and settles one. That is an INWARD move and would convert the gap into a grounded result.

### F7 - The residual may owe a refusal, not an explanation, and the sought shape must be bound first

> Fig 5 :53 "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"
> Fig 5 :55 "Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach."
> Fig 5 :62 "A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term."
> Fig 5 :65 "make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM."

Three held terms are enumerable candidates for refusal, each already predicting and each carrying a residual that persists and does not trend down: (i) the quantum state is complete; (ii) evolution is always linear; (iii) successful measurements have unique determinate outcomes. Myrvold (SEP) presents exactly these as the three horns. Fig 5 :58-60 says rank by persistent-and-flat residual, the shrinking test read with the opposite sign.

But Fig 5 :62-65 names a limit: the shape we are looking for, "a mechanism that yields one outcome," is a preference, not a term, so the loop cannot see it. The remedy is a discipline. I bind it as an ordinary term in §5 (pre-registered refuter) before drawing the conclusion. **Verdict: `specified`, single-source.** **Refuter:** show that no held term in the unitary frame carries a persistent flat residual on this question, i.e. that the residual is a mere omission of bookkeeping rather than a false regularity.

### F8 - Interpretations are frames, and their agreement cannot settle the question

> Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."
> Fig 2 :45 "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."
> Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"
> Fig 7 :35 "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict"

The many interpretations are frames with different closures. A survey that counts how many physicists favour one cannot raise its verdict weight; it is a convention, not a triangulation. Tomaz et al. (2025) report that a 2024 World Science Festival panel of three quantum specialists gave three entirely distinct answers, and that Wikipedia lists thirteen classes of interpretations. That is evidence of a live disagreement, not evidence for any one answer. A proposal like "consciousness causes collapse" is a proposed import; without a nameable anchor and a shadow test it is not admitted as a domain (§2b). **Verdict: `general`, triangulated** across Fig 2 and Fig 7 (different collapse analyses). **Refuter:** an experiment on which the frames make different predictions and the ground pays for one.

### F10 - A note on the bond between state and outcome

> Fig 12 :111 "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."

Asking "why one outcome" asks for a route, not an ingredient list. Two bonds are candidates for the relation: ⋛ comparison (a threshold between quantities: "a goal is a comparison") if the question is read as a threshold selecting one amplitude, and ⇒ production ("A produces B; B did not exist before") if the outcome is read as a new definite value. Operators table :48-52 and :36-40. The figures cannot decide which: that depends on whether the outcome is an already-present value revealed or a newly created one, which is an interpretation-level question and therefore a gap under F6. This is written up with a falsifier in `06_isomers_and_order.md`. **Verdict: `specified` on the question type, `gap` on which bond.**

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach: "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device" (seat map, failure genera). Written down now, before §6 and before the WATSON verdict is composed.

The shape claim in F6 and F9 is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Closure refuter.** A derivation, inside unitary quantum mechanics with no added dynamical or interpretive atom, of a unique definite outcome for a single run. This breaks Fig 6 :21 and Fig 8 :32.
2. **Slot refuter.** A resolution-preserving reading under which the per-slot residual trends down while the ensemble statistics change, so F1's live slot is shown to be an artefact of aggregation.
3. **Compound refuter.** A single mechanism shown to close basis, interference and outcomes together, so the union reading of Fig 9 :54-57 is false.
4. **Instrument refuter.** A source-discriminating experiment that names the resolving atom without an imported interpretation, closing the F6 gap at the ground.
5. **Census refuter.** A low-overlap world source from cognition, animal behaviour or mathematics that conflicts with the union-compound or per-slot reading. Conflict sends us back to §2; it does not flip the verdict by itself.

Explicit non-refuters: the number of physicists who agree; the prestige of the person who agrees; a new interpretation that asserts a solution without a checkable prediction; a simulation such as this one agreeing with itself. All four are filters or pooled agreement, never witnesses (Fig 9 :60).

I also bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term, per Fig 5 :65: a mechanism that lies outside the unitary closure and closes the outcome condition, arriving with a provenanced source gate and a shadow test It now accrues a residual like anything else and can be refused by the same tests. This is the discipline step, not a result.
