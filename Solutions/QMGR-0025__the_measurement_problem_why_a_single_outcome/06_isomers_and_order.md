## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Is the problem one question or a compound?

- Fig 9 :4 "First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."
- Tomaz, Mattos and Barbatti (arXiv:2502.19278v3, 2025) follow Schlosshauer in naming three: preferred basis, non-observability of interference, outcomes.
- Bacciagaluppi (SEP, 2003/2025) gives the Maudlin three-aspect version: results, probabilities, state transformation.
- ⇒ The problem is a **union compound**. Decoherence clears interference and (largely) basis; the outcome condition remains. Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if one mechanism is shown to close all three conditions together, the compound claim fails and the problem is one question after all. Triangulated (Fig 9 plus two independent review literatures counted as one witness).

### Q2 - Are the three conditions isomers, or members of a functional group?

- Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- The three conditions do not share the same atom set. Basis selection operates on the apparatus Hamiltonian and the system-environment coupling; interference suppression on the reduced density matrix; outcomes on the actualisation event. By Fig 12's definition they are **not isomers**. They are members of a **functional group**, the recurring structure "many candidate components, one selected result," with different actors filling the slots.
- The stronger isomer reading would hold only if all three could be written over one shared atom set and differed only in arrangement. No source read here licenses that. It is refused as unsupported, not merely unproven.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit one atom set spanning all three conditions with only the bond differing. If found, the isomer claim stands.

### Q3 - What bond joins the superposed state to the definite outcome?

- Fig 12 :111 "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." Asking "why one outcome" asks for a route.
- Operators table :48-52 defines ⋛ comparison as "a threshold relation between two quantities," with the test "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present?" If the outcome is read as a threshold selection among amplitudes, ⋛ is the candidate.
- Operators table :36-40 defines ⇒ production as "A produces B; B did not exist before." If the definite value is read as newly created at measurement, ⇒ is the candidate.
- Fig 5 :53 "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so" is a third possibility: the question may owe a refusal of the held term "the quantum state is complete," not a bond at all.
- **The figures cannot decide among these.** The choice depends on whether the outcome is a revealed pre-existing value or a newly created one, which is interpretation-level and therefore a gap under `01` F6. Named strain, not hidden.
- **Falsifier:** a source-discriminating experiment showing that the definite value was pre-existing (kills ⇒, supports a disclosure reading) or that it was created at the interaction (kills the disclosure reading). Until then the bond type is open. Single-source for each definition, `gap` for the selection.

### Q4 - What order do the conditions run in?

- Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." Each link consumes what the one above produces.
- Fig 9 :27 "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."
- Formula step 2: refuse before mint; a term held false must be refused before a replacement is composed over it.
- Fig 2 :45 "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."
- ⇒ Dependency order: (1) describe R per slot; (2) hold the detector reading as anchor; (3) settle basis and interference; (4) refuse any held term found false; (5) only then import a mechanism with provenance. An import at step 1 would be an answer chosen before the residual was described, which Fig 9 forbids.
- **Falsifier:** a case where the outcome condition must be settled before the basis condition, so the order above is not a dependency order. None found in the sources read.

### Q5 - Do the analogues map by structure or by looks?

- **Perceptual binding.** Velik (Frontiers in Psychology 3, 2012) defines the binding problem as "how information from millions of sensory receptors, processed by millions of neurons working in parallel, can be merged into a unified percept," and notes that each proposed mechanism "can never give a complete explanation." Jasper, Tanabe and Kohn (J Neurosci 39, 2019): "Decisions can be predicted from the responses of small populations, even when choice signals in individual neurons are, on average, barely evident." Structure: many components to one reported choice; currency: spikes and choice; anchor: the reported choice. This maps the *many-to-one* relation and the *aggregate versus single trial* distinction. It does **not** map the physical mechanism (Fig 4 :19-20 "Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change"; relations, not looks).
- **Animal foraging.** Stephens (CABN 8, 2008): "experimental preparations based on binary, mutually exclusive choice are not good models of foraging decisions. Instead, foraging choices have a sequential foreground-background structure, in which one option is in the background of all other options." This corroborates the union/compound reading: the foreground-background structure is a compound, not a binary either-or. Different field, no overlap with physics foundations.
- **Mathematics.** The second incompleteness theorem: a consistent system cannot prove its own consistency (SEP, *Godel's Incompleteness Theorems*, 2013/2025). This is Fig 8 :34's "A frame cannot reach its own metaframe" from a field with no overlap with physics practice. It corroborates F4's underivability claim at the level of form, not content.
- **Falsifier for all three:** show that the analogue depends on a feature absent from the quantum case (e.g. that the neural "one choice" is itself a population state rather than a single actualisation). Then the analogue is dropped and the figure verdict stands on its own.

## CONCLUSION

The measurement problem is a **union compound** of at least three conditions with a functional-group structure, not a single question and not a set of isomers. The bond between state and outcome is a **gap**: ⋛, ⇒ and refusal are each definable, and the figures do not select among them because the selection is interpretation-level. The dependency order is describe, anchor, settle basis/interference, refuse, import. Three low-overlap analogues corroborate the structure without supplying the mechanism. Everything specific about *which* physical mechanism closes the outcome condition remains a declared gap.

This framing rescues what is defensible in the question without pretending to answer it: the shape of any answer is known, the refuters are written in advance, and the instrument that would close the gap is named. A name for the mechanism is not among the things the framework can supply.
