## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The single-outcome question is a live residual on one slot: the theory predicts a distribution over superposition components, the detector returns one definite value, and the accuracy of the many-run statistics does not remove the one-run residual. The question is a union compound of at least three conditions, preferred basis, non-observability of interference, and definite outcomes; decoherence clears the first two and leaves the third. The detector reading is the anchor, and interpretations are frames that range against it. A definite outcome is not composable inside the unitary closure alone, so any resolution imports a primitive from a frame whose closure differs. The framework can characterise the shape that import must have, and cannot name which one is correct. That part is an honest gap.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figure 1 and Figure 9

- **Statement:** the residual is per slot and the question is a union compound. Ensemble accuracy is not a resolution of the single-outcome question.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :13 "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"; Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."; Fig 9 :4 "First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."
- **Physicist-facing check:** is the three-condition split (basis, interference, outcomes) faithful to the current literature? Both reviews cited below use it. Does decoherence clear two and leave one?
- **Refuter:** show that the per-slot residual trends down under a resolution-preserving reading, or that one mechanism closes all three conditions together.

### C2 - physics-facing; checkable against the unitary formalism

- **Statement:** a definite outcome for a single run is not derivable inside the unitary closure without an added or reinterpreted term. Every known resolution imports something: collapse, hidden variables, branching, or a cut.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :21 "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."; Fig 8 :32 "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."
- **Physicist-facing check:** from the measurement problem's standard statement, the final state of system plus apparatus is a superposition of outcome-correlated terms, not a single eigenstate of the reading variable. Is the claim that this remains true after decoherence correct? The SEP entry on decoherence says yes: "it does *not* explain why we *do* observe measurement results in the first place."
- **Refuter:** a derivation of a unique definite outcome from unitary dynamics alone, with no added dynamical or interpretive atom. This is the strongest single refuter; if it fires, C2 and the import verdict fall.

### C3 - framework-internal derivation

- **Statement:** the framework returns a shape, not the name of the mechanism. The outcome is `gap` at the level of physical mechanism, not `grounded` and not silently omitted.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"; Fig 13 :135 "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."; Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence".
- **What to check:** does the packet anywhere assert an interpretation as the framework's answer? It should not. Does it state what a candidate import must look like? It should: outside the unitary closure, closes the outcome condition after basis and interference, arrives with a provenanced source gate and a shadow test.
- **Refuter:** a source-discriminating experiment that names the mechanism at the ground. That would convert the gap into a result. Until then, naming one is the metamerism error described in *The Hard Problem is About Source*: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that collapse, many-worlds, Bohm, Copenhagen or any other interpretation is correct.
- Not that consciousness causes collapse. That is a proposed import with no nameable anchor here, so it is not admitted as a domain.
- Not that the wavefunction is "just information" or that the quantum state is incomplete. The completeness term is a **candidate for refusal**, not a refused term yet.
- Not that decoherence solves the outcome problem. It clears two conditions of the compound.
- Not a derivation of quantum mechanics from the six-term substrate. That reading was refused at the strain test: a Hilbert-space state is not one of the six.
- Not a theory of everything; no final room.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.
