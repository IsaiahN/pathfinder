# Outreach draft: check one output before deciding whether the shape is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** the stage-1 output below must survive scrutiny on its own. It is a shape and a refuter, not a conclusion about yields. If a recipient cannot check it without first accepting the framework, the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about pollinator, pest and soil effects on yield

> Hi [name],
>
> I am writing about a narrow question in pollination and pest ecology: how changes in pollinators, pests and soil organisms affect food production. I have one output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** Yield change should be indexed per slot: crop x region x season x management regime. An average across crops or regions throws away the signal that says where to look. The three organism groups are not three additive inputs. They are actors in a habitat in contact with the yield residual, along with conditions (light, water, temperature, season, terrain) and relations (pollination, predation, competition, dependency). The relations are ordered and non-additive: pest control at an action threshold changes the value of pollination, not just its sum. An organism's sign is relative to the residual, not a property it carries, so "pollinator", "pest" and "soil organism" name roles rather than fixed good or bad kinds. Effects cascade: first-order enumeration misses second and third-order effects, as the DDT-to-eagles case did. Management can add an actor and cannot subtract the habitat, so it is permanent work rather than a solved state. The anchor is the measured harvest. Expert agreement is not the anchor.
>
> **What this is not claiming.** Not a global yield forecast, not a claim that one group matters more than another everywhere, not a euro valuation presented as a verdict, and not a claim that any named organism is universally beneficial. The framework returns a shape, not a name. My own derivation is a simulation checked for internal consistency, not a ground reading, so it cannot settle the question it reports on.
>
> **What would refute it. I wrote these down before sending this:**
> 1. A simple three-variable response surface predicts the per-slot residual across a documented habitat change without enumerating second-order contact, at least as well as a habitat census on held-out slots.
> 2. An organism group has an invariant sign across residuals, crops and regions, established by measurement rather than averaging.
> 3. An additive model with no interaction term predicts the residual at least as well as an interaction model across several crops and regions.
> 4. A one-off introduction stays settled, needs no maintenance and produces no unintended cascade across a full season in more than one habitat.
>
> If any one holds, the corresponding part of the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The verbatim figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figure text with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`, especially Figure 11, The Habitat.
> - IPBES assessment on pollinators, pollination and food production: https://www.ipbes.net/assessment-reports/pollinators
> - Lundin et al. 2013, "When ecosystem services interact: crop pollination benefits depend on the level of pest control": https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3574341/
>
> **If you want to use the method:** the formula is in the figures folder. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Stage sequence

1. **Stage 1.** Send the output and its refuter. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict **before** showing any framework material.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the three links. The figures, not the summary, are the authority.
3. **Stage 3, opt-in only.** Offer the formula as a method. Each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

A stage-2 or stage-3 agreement is never pooled as corroboration. It is a conversation, not a second witness (Fig 7 :13: "agreement compounds, evidence does not").
