# Expert verification packet: the Tether on pollinators, pests, soil organisms and food production

**What this is:** a claim in a form a pollination or pest ecologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the field application, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No harvest reading is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a yield forecast.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Yield change should be read per slot (crop x region x season x management regime), not as a global average. Pollinators, pests and soil organisms are actors in a habitat in contact with the yield residual, alongside conditions (light, water, temperature, season, terrain) and relations (pollination, predation, competition, dependency). Valence is relative to the residual, so these are roles, not standing good or bad kinds. Effects cascade beyond direct contact; pollination and pest control interact non-additively, and soil processes condition both. Management can add an actor and cannot subtract the habitat, so it is permanent work. The anchor is the measured harvest, not an aggregate and not expert agreement. The framework returns this shape, not a magnitude, and the magnitude stays open until the ground settles it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - habitat and valence, checkable by reading Figure 11 and against field experience

- **Statement:** the three groups must be enumerated as habitat actors with relations, not treated as three additive inputs with fixed signs.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :47 "relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency" Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."
- **What to check:** can you name a crop or region in which a usual "benefactor" reverses sign for a different residual (for example a pollinator that competes with a native bee, or a natural enemy that becomes a pest)? If valence were fixed, that would be impossible.
- **Refuter:** an invariant sign per organism group across all residuals, crops and regions, established by measurement rather than averaging.

### C2 - non-additive union and cascade, checkable against the interaction literature

- **Statement:** pollination and pest control form a union with an interaction term, not two additive contributions; the same applies to soil condition, and direct-contact enumeration misses cascades.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 11 :52 "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."
- **World corroboration (secondary, not decisive):** Lundin, Smith, Rundlöf and Bommarco, *When ecosystem services interact: crop pollination benefits depend on the level of pest control*, Proc R Soc B 280:20122243, 2013. Full text read 2026-11-22: "the gain in seed set obtained when simultaneously increasing pollination and pest control outweighed the sum of seed set gains obtained when increasing each service separately." Also: past work "solely attributed" yield instability to pollinator declines "without considering how pest control services have changed in tandem."
- **What to check:** is the interaction real in a crop you know, and is its sign stable? Does a second or third-order effect appear that a direct-contact census would miss?
- **Refuter:** an additive model with no interaction term predicts the per-slot residual at least as well as an interaction model across several crops and regions, on ground readings held out from the fit. A closed-form three-variable surface that predicts held-out slots without enumerating second-order contact also refutes C2.

### C3 - management asymmetry and the anchor, checkable against practice and measurement

- **Statement:** management can introduce actors but cannot subtract the habitat; it is permanent work. Yield is the anchor, so an aggregate yield series does not settle a per-slot service question.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :61 "Introduction is available; exclusion is not." Fig 11 :63 "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state." Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."
- **What to check:** find a one-off introduction (pollinator, parasitoid, soil inoculant) that stayed settled, needed no maintenance and produced no unintended cascade across a full season in more than one habitat. Also check whether a rising aggregate yield series has ever hidden a declining per-slot service in your data.
- **Refuter:** such a maintenance-free, cascade-free introduction exists on measurement; or a ground reading shows aggregate yield declining with no live slot residual hidden by the average.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a global yield forecast, not a percentage, not a ranking of the three organism groups.
- Not that any named organism is universally beneficial or harmful.
- Not that adding pollinators, parasitoids or inoculants always raises yield.
- Not that the euro valuation of pollination (Gallai et al. 2009: 153 billion euros, 9.5 percent of world food-crop production value) settles the effect. It is a filter for budget, not a witness for the verdict.
- Not that expert agreement about the mechanism counts as corroboration. Agreement from a shared evidence pool is one witness (Fig 2 :40).
- Not `grounded`. No harvest measurement was taken in this run.

## 5. What would settle it

A per-slot ground reading, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source." Here:

1. Per-slot yield, pollination service, pest pressure and soil condition measured together over a full season.
2. An interaction test on held-out slots: additive versus interaction model.
3. A threshold test: does a site-specific IPM action threshold fire before a cascade closes, and do early-warning indicators register (Scheffer et al. 2009)?
4. A cascade census before and after each introduction.
5. The refuters in section 2, any one of which withdraws the sub-claim it targets.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :43, :47, :50, :52, :61 and :63, six short passages, in `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`.
2. Check C1: name one real reversal of organism valence across residuals. Two minutes.
3. Check C2 against the Lundin 2013 abstract or full text: is the synergy finding compatible with your crop? Five minutes.
4. Check C3: name a maintenance-free introduction with no cascade, or say why none exists. Five minutes.
5. State which refuter you would fire, or send a failure you know of. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
