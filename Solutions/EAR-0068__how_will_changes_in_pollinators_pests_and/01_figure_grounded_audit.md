## 0. The residual, in effect terms

**Form.** `R = |Γ(b,a) − o′|` per slot `s` (THE_FORMULA.md step 1).

- `Γ`: the production frame's predicted yield for a crop, given inputs, area, weather and management.
- `o′`: the realised harvest, measured as yield, quality or nutrition.
- `s`: crop x region x season x management regime. **R is measured per slot, never as a global total.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :28: "The gap is measured per object, not per step."
- The residual of interest is the part of a yield change the production frame did not predict, and that becomes visible when pollinator, pest or soil-organism populations change.

**What is being decided.** Option A: treat the question as a three-variable input problem, "more or fewer pollinators, pests, soil organisms, therefore more or less food." Option B: treat it as a habitat-and-cascade problem indexed per slot, with actor valence relative to the residual and a maintained channel to the yield ground. A leads to a single averaged coefficient and a lever that clears only single-condition regions. B leads to a census, a cascade map, a union reading, and a checkable refuter.

**Anchor.** Yield is the anchor. Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it." This question is constitutively about food production, so a measured harvest is a legitimate anchor, while expert agreement about mechanisms is not. Fig 11 :73: "The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not." The three organism groups are the **habitat**, not the ground. Yield is the ground.

**Direction bind (formula step, "Direction").** I write down what an answer would look like before the run, so the sought-for shape is an ordinary term that can accrue a residual: a per-slot habitat census and cascade map with actor valence relative to the residual, union conditions unbundled, the seat's asymmetric power stated, and a refuter written in advance. That shape is then refusable by the same test as everything else.

## 1. Domain census, before choosing any figure line

### 1.1 R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity.** A multi-place relation: organism group x habitat condition x relation x slot x time. At least three organism classes and at least three named relations are in contact with the residual at once.
- **Symmetry.** The bonds are ordered and non-symmetric. Pollination precedes fruit set (Fig 12 :72 "A then B; B needs A to have happened"). Production is `⇒`: Fig 12 :76 "A produces B; B did not exist before". Pest suppression is a threshold comparison `⋛`: Fig 12 :87 "a threshold relation between two quantities". There is no symmetric `+` reading of "pollinator plus pest plus soil organism" that preserves the effect.
- **Scale.** Three scales are in play: field or farm, landscape, and regional or global food system. The membrane between them is lossy (Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap."). Contact also has depth: first-order, second-order and third-order effects (Fig 11 :52).

### 1.3 Partition or union?

**Union, not partition.** Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."

The three organism groups overlap. Pollination and pest control interact synergistically in a controlled crop experiment (Lundin, Smith, Rundlöf and Bommarco 2013, read 2026-11-22: "the gain in seed set obtained when simultaneously increasing pollination and pest control outweighed the sum of seed set gains obtained when increasing each service separately"). Soil biota condition both plant nutrient supply and suppression of soil-borne pests and pathogens (Wall, Nielsen and Six 2015; Nielsen, Wall and Six 2015). A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. So the residual must be unbundled into its overlapping conditions before a frame is chosen.

### 1.4 Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. **Perception.** Is a slot residual actually arriving? Per-slot yield deviation, not a global average (Fig 1 :19).
2. **Vocabulary.** Do we have terms for what the organisms afford? Fig 11 :45 to :47: actors, conditions, relations.
3. **Objective.** What counts as progress here, in the slot's own terms? Yield and nutrition, the anchor.
4. **Planning.** Which intervention, at which threshold, in which habitat?
5. **Learn and carry.** Name what worked, verify it against the harvest, carry the method (not a recording) to the next slot.

Dependency order matters because "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not" (Fig 3 :19), and "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at" (Fig 3 :27). Most effort in this field lands at the planning link, where measurements already exist, while the vocabulary link (what a changed organism actually affords in this slot) attracts the fewest instruments.

## 3. Findings F1 to F9

### F1 - This is a habitat question, not a three-variable input question (decisive, structural)

Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."
Fig 11 :44: "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."
Fig 11 :45: "actors: what acts on it, and what it acts on"
Fig 11 :46: "conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"
Fig 11 :47: "relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"
Fig 11 :48: "You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."

The figure's own relation list names **pollination, predation, competition and dependency**. The question's three organism groups are actors in the habitat of the yield residual. The answer shape is therefore a habitat census and cascade map per slot, not a coefficient on three variables. The three groups are things in contact with the residual; they are not independent inputs to a closed production function. The Symbols table defines the habitat the same way: "Everything in contact with the residual: actors, conditions, relations. Enumerated, never composed."

World corroboration (secondary, low overlap with the figure's provenance): the IPBES assessment calls pollination "a regulating ecosystem service that underpins food production" and frames the assessment around "status and trends in pollinators and pollination networks and services, drivers of change, impacts on human well-being." That is a habitat framing, not a single-coefficient framing.

**Mode:** `general` (structural). **Refuter:** a closed-form response surface in three variables that predicts the slot residual across a documented habitat change without enumerating second-order contact, and does so at least as well as a habitat census on held-out slots. If that fires, F1 is wrong and the input model stands.

### F2 - Valence is relative to the residual, and these are relations, not kinds

Fig 11 :49: "valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."
Fig 11 :50: "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."
Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

A honeybee is a benefactor to fruit set and a competitor to some native bees. A seed weevil is a malfactor to seed set and a resource for parasitic wasps. Soil microbes suppress pathogens or are pathogens depending on the residual named. The labels "pollinator", "pest" and "soil organism" name roles in a relation, not a moral or fixed functional class. This is not a philosophical nicety: a standing list of good actors would tell a manager to add the same organism in every slot, which is exactly the failure the figures name.

World corroboration, low-overlap field (animal behaviour): Wikipedia's *Flower constancy* entry (fetched 2026-11-22) states "Constancy is also not a fixed preference; the same individual may exhibit constancy for different flowers on different bouts of foraging." At the level of individual behaviour, the same actor changes role with context, matching the valence rule at a different stratum. Overlap with the ecology pool is low.

**Mode:** `specified` for this field, `general` for the rule. **Refuter:** an invariant sign per organism group across all residuals, crops and regions, established by a ground reading (not by averaging). One counter-case where an actor reverses valence across residuals is enough to refute the standing-kind reading, not the figure.

### F3 - Cascades are third order; first-order enumeration is the characteristic failure

Fig 11 :52: "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."
Fig 11 :54: "Carson, Silent Spring, 1962. The mechanism, biomagnification through the food chain to eggshell thinning, was established over the following decade."

The figure's own case is agricultural and ecological: a pesticide aimed at one pest reached a top predator three steps out, and the mechanism took a decade to establish. In this question the analogous risk is that a change in soil organisms alters nutrient supply and pathogen suppression, which changes plant condition, which changes pollinator visitation and pest pressure, which changes yield. A census that stops at direct contact will miss it.

World corroboration (secondary, same ecology pool as F1): Lundin et al. 2013 write that "Decreasing level and stability on yield in insect-pollinated crops has so far solely been attributed to pollinator declines, without considering how pest control services have changed in tandem." That is the first-order enumeration failure, stated in the_field's own literature. Also Scheffer et al. 2009 (Utrecht record, fetched 2026-11-22), a low-overlap complex-systems source: "Complex dynamical systems, ranging from ecosystems to financial markets and the climate, can have tipping points at which a sudden shift to a contrasting dynamical regime may occur." A cascade can close as a threshold, not trend linearly.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Refuter:** second and third-order contact terms contribute zero to the slot residual once first-order terms are fitted, across at least two independent habitats and a full season, measured at the ground.

### F4 - The union is synergistic, not additive; unbundle before searching again

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"
Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance."

The three groups are not additive contributions to yield. In red clover, "the gain in seed set obtained when simultaneously increasing pollination and pest control outweighed the sum of seed set gains obtained when increasing each service separately" (Lundin et al. 2013). The interaction is a bond, not an extra operand: same crop, same two services, different arrangement, different result. That is Fig 12's isomer point at the level of services. An additive model would clear the single-condition regions and leave the intersection standing.

This is also where the question's "affect" needs a bond type. Pollination `⇒` fruit set (production); pest pressure `−` seed set (subtraction); natural-enemy control `⋛` an economic threshold (comparison); soil nutrient supply `→` plant condition (sequence). A flat `+` formula records the ingredients and loses the arrangement, which is Fig 12's flat-formula collapse.

**Mode:** `specified`, and `grounded` only for the single red-clover experiment, not for the general claim. **Refuter:** an additive model with no interaction term predicts the slot residual at least as well across several crops and regions, on ground readings held out from the fit.

### F5 - The seat can add and cannot subtract; management is permanent work

Fig 11 :58: "Maintained. A seat exists and can act: choose the instruments, distil the impactful actors, introduce what was not there."
Fig 11 :61: "Introduction is available; exclusion is not."
Fig 11 :62: "You can add an actor and you cannot subtract the habitat, because contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you."
Fig 11 :63: "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."
Fig 11 :65: "Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994."

Management is not a one-off correction. You can introduce a pollinator, a parasitoid or a soil inoculant; you cannot subtract the habitat it lands in. Fig 11 :65 continues: "Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated." The failure mode is introduction without a cascade census.

World corroboration (secondary, same management pool): Wikipedia's *Integrated pest management* entry (fetched 2026-11-22) states "IPM programs identify acceptable pest levels or action thresholds based on estimated economic injury, and apply controls if those thresholds are exceeded" and "Thresholds are pest and site specific." That is the comparison bond `⋛` applied per slot, and an acknowledgement that the threshold is not a global constant. IPM's six-step process (sample, identify, learn biology, determine threshold, choose tactics, evaluate) is a dependency chain in miniature, matching Fig 3.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Refuter:** a one-off introduction that remains settled and requires no maintenance, with no unintended cascade, across a full season and more than one habitat. Until such a case is measured, "permanent work" stands.

### F6 - Yield is the anchor, so aggregated statistics can hide a live slot residual

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."
Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."
Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict."

There is an apparent conflict in the world literature. The *Pollinator decline* entry (Wikipedia, fetched 2026-11-22) states: "Despite the dire predictions, the theorised decline in pollinators has had no effect on food production, with yields of both animal-pollinated and non-animal-pollinated crops increasing at the same rate, over the period of supposed pollinator decline." IPBES and Gallai et al. state the opposite kind of concern. Re-census resolves it without flipping a verdict: the two sources report different quantities. Aggregate yield (tonnes per hectare, global) is not the slot residual (fruit set in a specific crop-region-season). If a per-slot service is declining while aggregate yield rises through area, inputs, cultivar replacement or management, the aggregate is a **filter for budget**, not a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).

Gallai et al. 2009 (RePEc/IDEAS abstract, fetched 2026-11-22) report that "The total economic value of pollination worldwide amounted to €153 billion, which represented 9.5% of the value of the world agricultural production used for human food in 2005" and that "This ratio varied considerably among crop categories." The variation across crop categories is exactly the per-slot structure an average erases. A valuation in euros prices the description (bits), not the actions needed to enumerate the habitat (Fig 12 :125).

**Mode:** `specified`. **Refuter:** a ground reading showing that per-slot residuals trend down (toward zero) while the aggregate also declines, with no live slot hidden by the average. Until then, an aggregate yield series does not settle this question.

### F7 - The ground settles it; the channel can be lost

Fig 11 :73: "The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."
Fig 10 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does"
Fig 10 :26: "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings"
Fig 7 :13: "agreement compounds, evidence does not"

A well-known cascade (DDT to eagles) is memorable; a slow decline in soil structure is not. That asymmetry is a channel problem, not a ground problem. The *Availability heuristic* entry (Wikipedia, fetched 2026-11-22) describes "a mental shortcut that relies on immediate examples that come to a given person's mind when evaluating a specific topic, concept, method, or decision" and notes that people rate "newsworthy" events as more likely because they can more readily recall an example. That predicts over-weighting of vivid pest or pollinator collapses and under-weighting of diffuse soil change. The cure is not more agreement among experts (Fig 7 :13) but keeping the anchor reachable: per-slot measurements, early-warning indicators and a public record.

Scheffer et al. 2009 supply an instrument shape, not a further argument: generic early-warning signals "may indicate for a wide class of systems if a critical threshold is approaching." An instrument is "improved from a worse instrument already returning something" (Fig 6 :32), so existing IPM thresholds and pollination-deficit protocols are the proto-instruments to sharpen, not a reason to invent a sensor from a description.

**Mode:** `general` for the channel rule, `specified` for this field. **Refuter:** a documented case in which expert agreement or a dramatic case changed the measured yield relationship without any change in the ground.

### F8 - No last room: the answer is a shape, not a point forecast

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"
Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."
Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"

The figures return the narrowed form of any answer: per-slot, cascade-aware, valence-relative, union-unbundled, seat-maintained and ground-settled. They do not return "yields will fall by X percent" or "pollinators matter more than soil organisms" in the abstract, because those are names and quantities for particular slots, and the framework "cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it" (Fig 13). A search that fails to find a governing figure for a quantitative global forecast is a `gap` for that forecast, not a silence about the shape.

**Mode:** `general`. **Refuter:** a figure line that, after a genuine re-census, governs a specific global yield forecast. I ran that re-census and found none; the claim is therefore recorded as a `gap`, not hidden.

### F9 - Two currencies: bits and actions

Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."
Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."
Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome."

A habitat census is cheap to describe in bits and expensive to settle in actions. A global yield model is cheap to state and may take a whole budget to settle. The pollination valuation literature (Gallai 2009, FAO 2009 guidelines) prices the description in euros; it does not price the field actions of enumerating a habitat. Summing the two into one score is the error Fig 12 names, not the fix. Two organisms in the same functional group (for example, two pollinators) can have opposite effects on the same residual if the bond differs, which is the isomer point again.

**Mode:** `general`. **Refuter:** a single accounting that compares description cost and action cost without loss, or a demonstration that one predicts the other.

## 4. Formula step 7: three questions, kept distinct

When no single line closes the quantitative version of R, ask SEARCHED first, then INWARD, then OUTWARD. They are not interchangeable.

**SEARCHED - is the atom in here and unfound?** Within pollination, pest and soil ecology, the interaction terms and the threshold instruments already exist. Lundin et al. 2013 tested three competing hypotheses (independent, compensatory, synergistic) and found synergy. IPM defines action thresholds per site. So for the qualitative shape, more search is the right answer; import is not owed. Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom." The atom, if any, is already in the closure.

**INWARD - extend the instrument from one already returning something.** Existing instruments returning a signal: pollination-deficit protocols (FAO 2009 guidelines), economic injury levels and action thresholds (IPM), early-warning signals for critical transitions (Scheffer et al. 2009), per-slot yield monitoring. Sharpen in our own terms: make thresholds slot-specific, make the sign of valence explicit, make cascade depth explicit. Fig 6 :32: "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."

**OUTWARD - import a frame whose closure differs.** Three imports were tried, each with a source gate and a shadow test, and each debited against independence:

1. *Complex systems* (Scheffer et al. 2009): source gate, peer-reviewed Nature record with full bibliographic provenance; shadow test, explains why a residual can appear suddenly after slow driver change. Corroborates F3 at a low overlap.
2. *Human cognition* (availability heuristic): source gate, Wikipedia entry with Tversky and Kahneman references, fetched this session; shadow test, explains why vivid cascades are over-weighted and soil change under-weighted. Corroborates F7 at low overlap.
3. *Animal behaviour* (flower constancy): source gate, Wikipedia entry with primary citations, fetched this session; shadow test, explains why actor valence is not fixed. Corroborates F2 at low overlap.

INWARD and OUTWARD were never read against one another. `underivable-in-principle` and `unreachable-at-this-budget` are different facts: the global quantitative forecast is `unreachable at this budget` (more search and better instruments apply), not shown `underivable in principle`.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the standing instruction that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following fires:

1. A closed-form three-variable response surface predicts the slot residual across a documented habitat change without enumerating second-order contact, at least as well as a habitat census on held-out slots (breaks F1 and F3).
2. An invariant sign per organism group is established across all residuals, crops and regions by a ground reading (breaks F2).
3. An additive model with no interaction term predicts the slot residual at least as well as an interaction model across several crops and regions (breaks F4).
4. A one-off introduction remains settled and requires no maintenance, with no unintended cascade, across a full season and more than one habitat (breaks F5).
5. Aggregate yield declines with no live slot residual hidden by the average (breaks F6).
6. Expert agreement or a dramatic case changes the measured yield relationship without any change in the ground (breaks F7).

If any fires, the affected finding is withdrawn and the shape is re-censused. Until a ground reading is taken, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`. Prestige and agreement are not such a reading (Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong.").

## 6. Recommendation

1. Restate the problem per slot (crop x region x season x management) and enumerate the habitat before modeling: actors, conditions, relations (Fig 11 :43 to :48). Do not name a beneficial or harmful kind in advance.
2. Keep verdicts per domain separate, never averaged (Fig 1 :19). A domain verdict is `triangulated` only when two figures of low overlap govern it, otherwise `single-source` or `gap`.
3. Unbundle the union before searching again (Fig 9 :54). Treat pollination, pest control and soil processes as overlapping conditions with their own interaction terms, not three additive inputs.
4. Implement control as a per-slot comparison threshold, not a global constant (Fig 12 :87; IPM action thresholds). Keep the observation channel reachable (Fig 10 :24), and record valence relative to the residual.
5. Count both currencies separately (Fig 12 :125): bits for the description, actions for the enumeration. Do not let a euro valuation hand you a verdict (Fig 9 :60).
6. Lead outreach with the residual and the refuter, not the theory (Fig 10 :52 "Bring a characterised residual, never the source."). Ask what would fail. Do not pool later agreement as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
7. Record the stop honestly. The qualitative shape is `specified`; the global quantitative forecast is a declared `gap` (Fig 13 :134).
