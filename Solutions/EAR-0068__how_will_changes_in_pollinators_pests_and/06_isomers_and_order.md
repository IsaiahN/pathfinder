# Union, bonds and order: the supporting derivation for EAR-0068

**Mode: `specified` on the field application, `general` on the structural claims.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The primary audit leaves two questions owed. First: if pollination, pest control and soil processes are overlapping conditions, what **bond** joins them, and are two crop systems with the same actors isomers or merely members of one functional group? Second: in what **order** does the union have to be addressed across scales, and what analogous structures in other fields corroborate that order?

## DOMAIN CENSUS (supporting derivation)

Atoms held: the habitat actors and conditions from Fig 11 :45-46 (pollinators, pests, soil organisms, crops; light, water, temperature, season, terrain) and the relations pollination, predation, competition, dependency (Fig 11 :47). Bonds held: the seven, plus negation. No new primitive is available without import. The anchor remains the per-slot harvest.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Is this a partition or a union, and does that change the bond?

- Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"
- Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."

A partition would let me fit one coefficient per organism group and add them. A union requires the intersections to have their own loop. Lundin et al. 2013 supply the ground-level test: the joint gain "outweighed the sum of seed set gains obtained when increasing each service separately." So the bond between pollination and pest control is not a flat `+`; it is an interaction. Fig 12's warning applies: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."

**Result:** union, with at least one non-additive interaction term. The answer shape must be unbundled before a single lever is proposed.

### Q2 - Which bond types are actually present, and what does each license?

- Fig 12 :72: sequence, "A then B; B needs A to have happened" (→). Soil nutrient supply → plant condition → flowering is a sequence.
- Fig 12 :76: production, "A produces B; B did not exist before" (⇒). Pollination ⇒ fruit set; the fruit did not exist before the visit.
- Fig 12 :87: comparison, "a threshold relation between two quantities" (⋛). Pest pressure versus economic injury level is a threshold, and IPM makes it site-specific.
- Fig 12 :81: subtraction, "A with B removed or absent" (−). Pest damage is seed set with ovules removed.
- Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use." A pollinator or a soil microbial guild survives the service it performs, so spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses. Nothing in a flat ingredient list records which a term is.
- Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input." Crop residue → soil biota → nutrient supply → crop is a cycle. "The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."

**Result:** at least five distinct bond types govern this residual. A flat formula with only `+` records the ingredients and loses the binding information, which is Fig 12's flat-formula collapse. This is why "pollinator + pest + soil organism" is not an answer.

### Q3 - Are two crop systems with the same actors isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance."
- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome."

Two fields with the same actor set, where pollination ⇒ fruit set is the binding relation in one and pest suppression ⋛ threshold is the binding relation in the other, are **isomers** under Fig 12: same atoms, different bond, different substance. Two fields that share the functional pattern `state → service → yield` but use different pollinator species are members of the same **functional group**: "the group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."

This distinction is checkable, not decorative. It predicts that a service provided in one field cannot be assumed to transfer to another merely because the actor list matches. The world literature agrees at the management level: Lundin et al. note that benefit transfer functions extrapolating a service from one area to another "may give misleading results if interactions between ecosystem services are not taken into account." That is a `single-source` corroboration for the isomer reading from the ecology pool, and it is not independent of F4 in the primary audit.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two systems with identical actor sets but different binding bonds yield the same per-slot residual across a season, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading is all that survives. If two systems with different actor sets but the same binding bond diverge sharply, the functional-group claim also fails.

### Q4 - What order do the conditions come in, across scales?

- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."
- Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
- Fig 4 :22: "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."
- Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap."

Dependency order: perception of the slot residual → vocabulary for what the organisms afford → objective (what counts as yield here) → planning the intervention → learn and carry the method. Crossing from field to region, carry **methods** (how to enumerate a habitat, how to set a per-slot threshold), never the recording of one field's success. Bringing a recording down produces a replay that cannot produce a new result in a new habitat.

The round trip is lossy: a method carried up from field studies and reapplied to a region will not match what was sent (Fig 4 :30). The gap `R_T` must be measured, not assumed zero. For this question, the gap is most visible when a region-level yield model is used as if it were a sum of field-level habitat censuses. It is not.

**Falsifier:** a region-level prediction obtained by carrying a field-level recording up and replaying it down matches ground readings in a new habitat without re-enumeration. If that holds, the membrane claim is wrong.

### Q5 - What do low-overlap analogues corroborate?

- **Mathematics and complex systems.** Scheffer et al. 2009: complex dynamical systems "can have tipping points at which a sudden shift to a contrasting dynamical regime may occur." Corroborates the `⋛` threshold and the non-linear union at a low overlap with ecology. It does not license a particular threshold value here.
- **Human cognition.** The availability heuristic is "a mental shortcut that relies on immediate examples that come to a given person's mind"; people rate newsworthy events as more likely because an example is readily recalled. Corroborates the channel-decay finding (Fig 10 :24) at a low overlap: vivid cascades are attended to, diffuse soil change is not.
- **Animal behaviour.** Flower constancy: "the same individual may exhibit constancy for different flowers on different bouts of foraging." Corroborates valence relativity (Fig 11 :49) at a low overlap.
- **Business and economics.** Gallai et al. 2009 report a total pollination value of 153 billion euros, 9.5 percent of world agricultural production value for food in 2005, with a ratio that "varied considerably among crop categories." Corroborates the per-slot structure in a different currency, but is a price in bits, not a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60; Fig 12 :125).

All four are secondary. None overrules a figure. Agreement among them would still be one pool if they drew on the same evidence, but they were chosen from different fields specifically to keep the overlap low (Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence.").

## CONCLUSION

The residual is a union of overlapping conditions joined by at least five distinct bond types, not a three-term sum. Two systems with the same actors but different binding bonds are isomers; two with the same functional pattern are functional-group members. Order follows dependency (Fig 3) and crosses scales only as methods (Fig 4 :22), with a measured round-trip gap. Low-overlap analogues corroborate the threshold, channel, valence and per-slot shape without deciding the field verdict. Both falsifiers above are written in advance and remain live.
