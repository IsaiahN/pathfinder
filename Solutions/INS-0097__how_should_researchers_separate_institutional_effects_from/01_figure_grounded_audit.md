## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

Let I be an institution (a rule, an enforcement practice, a property-rights regime), Y an outcome (trust, growth, corruption, conflict), and C the historical, economic and cultural conditions that travel with I.

For each slot s (a region, an ethnic group, a period, a firm), the unanalysed residual is:

> R_s = | Γ(b, a_I) − o′_s |

where the prediction Γ uses I as if it were the operative term. The question is what happens to R_s when C is entered. Two outcomes are possible and they are not the same claim:

- **The residual collapses.** The association between I and Y was carried by a background source. This is the common-cause collapse of Fig 2, panel 2.
- **The residual persists and does not trend down.** Something is owed to I itself. Per the formula's "WHICH TERM" rule, a residual that persists and does not trend down is the one a refusal or a new term should be aimed at (formula step 2, not a figure line).

Arity: the observed treatment is a compound of I and C, which co-occur. Symmetry: the observed association I-Y is symmetric in the data, while the causal ordering is not; that mismatch is the warning sign, not a detail. Scale: the effect may be first-order (I touches Y directly) or several steps out; Fig 11 :52 records a residual that "mattered was three steps out" in the DDT case. R is indexed by slot: Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."

An answer would look like a procedure that drives R_s toward zero *for a stated reason* and states what would reverse it. That sought-for shape is bound here as an ordinary term (formula, "Direction"), so it can be refuted rather than merely preferred.

## 3. Findings

### F1: The raw institution-outcome association is a common-cause collapse

**Mode: general. Status: triangulated.**

Fig 2 :18-21: "one evidence pool they never talk, and still agree so the agreement is the pool talking, not two frames confirming each other"

Fig 2 :39: "Correlation has valence."

Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"

When an institution and an outcome are always measured in the same historical, economic and cultural setting, their agreement is the setting speaking through both. The association is not biased because the regression is badly run; it is disqualified as a verdict by construction. Fig 7 :12-13 says the same thing at the scale of a literature: "same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not."

Refuter: exhibit a case in which the I-Y association survives enumeration of every condition in contact with it (F5) and survives a source of variation that is demonstrably independent of those conditions (F4), with an anchor that does not update on the researcher's reading. Until such a case, the raw association has no verdict weight.

Fig 1 :27 adds the reason a fit statistic cannot rescue it: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."

### F2: Institution and conditions form a union, so unbundle before searching

**Mode: general. Status: single-source for the unbundling rule (Fig 9), with Fig 2 as corroboration at higher overlap.**

Fig 9 :4-5: "First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."

Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."

Fig 9 :57-58: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."

A study that "controls for history, economics and culture" applies one lever to a bundle. The lever clears cases where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. What remains after the lever is not a slightly smaller version of the same problem; it is a specific compound with its own loop. This is why a list of control variables cannot be the answer to this question.

Refuter: show that for the case at hand the union is actually a partition, that is, the I effect is additive and invariant across every stratum of C (no interaction, no compound). That is an empirical claim about that case, not a licence to ignore the union generally.

Fig 9 :60 keeps the method honest: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

### F3: Separation requires an import from a closure that differs

**Mode: general. Status: triangulated across Fig 6 and Fig 8, which have low overlap: Fig 6 is about the operator that moves a wall, Fig 8 is about where the imported atom comes from.**

Fig 6 :21: "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."

Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."

Fig 6 :14: "the only operator that moves the wall"

Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."

Inside the observed joint distribution of (I, C), no amount of regression searches a new atom. The only operator that moves the wall is contact with a frame whose closure differs: a randomised rollout, a discontinuity, a natural experiment, a historical shock. These are not being recommended because a textbook says so; they are the figure's import operator cast in the language of this field. Fig 13 :115: "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."

Refuter: derive the separation from inside the same observational distribution with no new variation, and show the result is invariant to the functional form and to unmeasured confounding of the kind Bulbulia (2024) and Byrnes (2025) describe. If that can be done, the import requirement is too strong and is withdrawn for that class of cases.

Fig 8 :53 sets the order: "Residual first, frame second." Describe R in effect terms before choosing the instrument, or any instrument will seem to fit (Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit").

### F4: Independence must be bought and verified, not assumed

**Mode: general. Status: triangulated between Fig 2 (anchor and independence) and the world sources Angrist and Krueger (2001) and AJR (2001), which are a different evidence pool.**

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."

Fig 2 :31: "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."

Fig 2 :32: "Independence is what makes agreement mean something."

Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

An imported source of variation only separates I from C if it does not draw on the same evidence as C. The settler-mortality instrument in Acemoglu, Johnson and Robinson (2001) is an attempt at exactly this: a source outside the contemporaneous institution-growth pair. The authors themselves ran over-identification tests to detect whether the instrument had a direct effect on performance (AJR, 2001, full text). Angrist and Krueger (2001) call transparency and refutability of the identifying assumptions the key feature of the natural-experiment approach.

Anchor test: if the proposed source moves when you push on the historical, economic or cultural conditions, it is not an anchor. Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

Refuter: a balance test, placebo test or over-identification test that fails, showing the source is correlated with C. Independence is a bought quantity with a price; it is not a declaration.

### F5: Enumerate the habitat; never compose it; contact cascades

**Mode: general. Status: single-source for the enumeration rule (Fig 11), corroborated by three low-overlap fields (economic history, evolutionary human science, ecology).**

Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."

Fig 11 :44: "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."

Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."

Fig 11 :52-53: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."

Historical, economic and cultural conditions are not a nuisance-parameter block. They are the habitat of the residual: actors, conditions and relations in contact with it. The list is read off the world, not composed from a theory of what confounders ought to look like. Valence is relative to the residual: the same cultural trait can sustain one institution and corrode another, so there is no standing list of "good controls". Contact cascades, so a first-order enumeration can miss the condition that actually carries the association. Nunn (2014) finds that cultural persistence depends on the institutional environment, an interaction exactly of this form. Bulbulia (2024) and Byrnes (2025) argue from human evolutionary science and ecology that unmeasured common causes must be assumed and planned for.

Refuter: a demonstrated case in which a first-order enumeration exhausts the cascade and the effect is invariant to adding second-order conditions. That would narrow the enumeration rule for that class, not repeal it.

### F6: Effects are indexed by slot; an average hides the signal

**Mode: general. Status: single-source (Fig 1).**

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."

Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

An institutional effect is not one number. In one region the rule may bind; in another the same written rule may be inert because the supporting informal conditions are absent. Reporting a single average treatment effect is the aggregation the figure warns against: the live slot residual disappears into the mean.

Refuter: a case in which the effect is exactly homogeneous across all slots and the policy is uniform across those slots, demonstrated by a pre-registered heterogeneity test rather than assumed. Then aggregation loses nothing for that decision.

This is a filter on how to report, not a witness for the effect's existence (Fig 9 :60). It must never hand a verdict.

### F7: The institution is a molecule, not an atom; culture is partly the bond

**Mode: specified. Status: single-source for the bond reading (Fig 12), with a live strain recorded in section 8.**

Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."

Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."

An official rule set is an ingredient list. The enforcement practice, the informal norm and the sanction are the bonds. Two polities with the same written constitution and different bonds are different substances, which is why a name on a statute book does not identify an institution. Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."

This re-censors the question itself. "Culture" is not always a separate condition alongside the institution; it is often the bond that makes the institution the thing it is. Separating I from C therefore means separating atoms from bonds before it means separating variables. Boyd and Richerson (2003) put the empirical version plainly: legitimate institutions cannot be created just by adopting a constitution, because the surrounding cultural evolution supplies the bonds. Nunn (2014) calls institutions a black box in the macro literature, which is the measurement form of the same point.

Refuter: exhibit a case where the written rule and the informal practice vary independently and each has its own identifiable effect. Then they are two atoms and the bond reading is not needed for that case.

### F8: Transport is habitat substitution; isolation is not removal

**Mode: general. Status: triangulated across Fig 11 and Cartwright (2010), a philosophy-of-science source with low overlap with the figures.**

Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."

Fig 11 :68: "what you failed to reproduce is invisible until the goal fails"

Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

A clean identification inside one population does not by itself tell you what the institution would do elsewhere. A controlled study, or an imported institution, substitutes a habitat: you inherit actors you did not choose, and whatever you failed to reproduce stays invisible until the policy fails. Cartwright (2010) reaches the same conclusion from the external-validity literature: securing internal validity "goes only a very short way" toward predicting what the cause will do in another population, and the required assumptions are heavy.

Refuter: a transplant that produces the identical effect in a habitat where the original accompanying conditions are demonstrably absent, with a full enumeration of what transferred and what did not.

### F9: Unreachable is a verdict, not a silence

**Mode: general. Status: triangulated across Fig 8, Fig 13 and Fig 9.**

Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

Fig 8 :41-42: "Underivable: go outside. Unreachable-at-budget: the term is already in the closure and was not found, so the remedy is more search, not another atom."

Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"

If no exogenous variation exists at this budget, the honest output is "not identified with these data", not a manufactured estimate. The two failure modes issue different instructions and must not be merged. Underivable in principle says go outside and find a different frame. Unreachable at budget says the needed variation may already be in hand and the remedy is more search or better measurement, not another imported atom, which would cost more than starting from primitives.

Refuter: an instrument found later for the same case. That would show the verdict was unreachable-at-budget, not underivable, and would confirm rather than break the distinction.

### F10: Declare the stake; keep the lineage

**Mode: general. Status: triangulated between Fig 10 and Fig 9.**

Fig 10 :59: "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."

Fig 10 :60: "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it."

Fig 9 :51: "evidence bearing on the residual is admissible; evidence bearing only on its maker is a stake-inducing channel, and the verifier is denied it"

Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"

Researchers want institutions to matter, and funders want usable results. That stake is unavoidable but must be declared. A covariate choice, a lag choice or a sample restriction that decides the answer has stopped being a convention and has become an authoring act. Fig 13 :142: "The seed was always the seedline." The record of which residual, which import, which independence test and which refuter is the checkable object.

Refuter: a reader who can reconstruct the estimate from the paper's narrative alone, without the data and the pre-specified design, and obtain the same answer. Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."

### F11: A coefficient is a lossy condensation; measure the round trip

**Mode: general. Status: single-source (Fig 4 and the Symbols table definition of R_T).**

Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."

The symbols table defines R_T as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down. A headline effect size is a condensation of a mechanism, a habitat and a design. Treating the coefficient as if it were the mechanism sets R_T to zero by assumption, which is the dead-reckoning error. The audit in `11_audit.md` enumerates what the condensation in this folder drops.

Refuter: a model in which one scalar estimate is sufficient for every policy prediction in the target population, demonstrated rather than asserted.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

The shape claim of this folder is: separation is possible only through unbundling the union, importing variation from a closure that differs, buying independence against the ground, enumerating the habitat, and reporting per-slot residuals with a declared stake and a refuter.

It is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A design is exhibited in which I and C are fully separated inside one closure, with no import and no new variation, and the effect is invariant across every stratum of C.
2. The raw I-Y association survives every enumerated habitat condition, every placebo and balance test, and a true anchor that does not update on the researcher's reading, so that no residual remains to be explained.
3. A transplanted institution produces the identical effect in a habitat where the original accompanying conditions are demonstrably absent, showing that culture was an atom and not a bond.
4. One scalar estimate predicts outcomes in every target population without further information, so the round-trip gap is zero.

If any fires, the shape claim is withdrawn and the gap is recorded, not explained away. Written before the recommendation below.

## 5. Recommendation: the shape of a defensible separation

1. **State R per slot in effect terms.** What failed to resolve, for which unit, at what scale (Fig 1 :19, Symbols table). Do not begin with a method name.
2. **Measure before diagnosing.** Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." A link below the measurement break cannot be read.
3. **Unbundle the union.** Separate the conditions that can be independently varied from the compound in which I and C bind (Fig 9 :55-58). Expect a specific compound, not a smaller mixture.
4. **Import variation from a closure that differs.** Randomisation, a discontinuity, a natural experiment or a historical shock (Fig 6 :14, :23). Describe R first, frame second (Fig 8 :53).
5. **Buy and test independence.** Balance, placebo, over-identification and sensitivity analyses, priced by the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42). If the source moves with C, it is not an anchor.
6. **Enumerate the habitat outward.** Historical, economic, cultural, geographic and informal relations, then what contacts those (Fig 11 :43-44, :52-53).
7. **Report per-slot residuals, not only an average** (Fig 1 :22).
8. **Test transport explicitly** (Fig 11 :66; Cartwright 2010). Do not infer it from internal validity.
9. **Declare the stake and publish the lineage** (Fig 10 :59-60; Fig 13 :142). A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
10. **If nothing closes R, say unreachable** (Fig 8 :39; Fig 13 :134). A search that finds nothing is not a proof of absence, and it is not a licence to invent an effect.
