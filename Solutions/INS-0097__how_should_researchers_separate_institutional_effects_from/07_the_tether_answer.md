# The Tether's answer: separating institutional effects from accompanying conditions

**Mode: `general` on the shape of a valid separation, `specified` on any claim about a particular institution, dataset or instrument. Not `grounded`.** This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading. No institutional effect was measured here.

## 1. The residual it answers

An institution I and an outcome Y are observed together with historical, economic and cultural conditions C. The thing to explain is the part of Y that the institution term fails to predict, unit by unit, before and after C is entered. If the residual collapses when C enters, the association was carried by a background source. If it persists and does not trend down, something is owed to the institution. The question "how should researchers separate them?" asks for the shape of a procedure that makes this discrimination possible and states what would reverse it.

## 2. The shape

1. **A raw association is not a verdict (`general`).** Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". The institution and the outcome agree partly because the same setting speaks through both. Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." A fit statistic cannot upgrade this to evidence.

2. **The problem is a union, so unbundle it before searching (`general`).** Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." A single lever of control variables clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing. What remains is "a specific compound" (Fig 9 :57-58), not a smaller mixture. There is no general list of covariates that performs this unbundling.

3. **Separation requires an import from a closure that differs (`general`).** Fig 6 :23: "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 8 :32: "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom." Inside the observed joint distribution of I and C, no amount of refinement adds the missing atom. In this field the import is a randomised rollout, a discontinuity, a natural experiment or a historical shock. Fig 8 :53: "Residual first, frame second." Describe the effect before choosing the source, or any source will seem to fit.

4. **Independence is bought and tested, not declared (`general`).** Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." If the proposed source moves when the historical, economic or cultural conditions move, it is not an anchor. Balance, placebo, over-identification and sensitivity analyses are the price, scaled to what a wrong answer would cost.

5. **Enumerate the habitat outward (`general`).** Fig 11 :44: "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering." Conditions are not a nuisance block; they are the habitat of the residual, and the condition that matters can be several steps out. Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." There is no standing list of good controls.

6. **Report per slot, not as one average (`general`).** Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." An institutional effect is a set of slot residuals, because the same written rule can bind in one place and be inert in another.

7. **Transport is a separate claim (`general`).** Fig 11 :66: "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." A clean design in one population substitutes a habitat when it is moved; what was not reproduced stays invisible until the policy fails. Internal validity does not carry the effect across.

8. **The institution is a molecule, and culture is partly the bond (`specified`, one live strain).** Same atoms under a different bond give a different substance (Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."). The written rule is an ingredient list; enforcement and informal norms are bonds. Whether a cultural condition is an atom or a bond is level-relative and needs an instrument, not a preference.

9. **Refuse before minting, declare the stake, keep the lineage (`general`).** A held term that is false owes a refusal, not an explanation (formula step 2). Fig 10 :60: "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it." Fig 13 :142: "The seed was always the seedline." The checkable object is the record of residual, import, independence test and refuter.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any particular institution has a particular effect size. No dataset was read here.
- Not that randomised trials are the only admissible import. Fig 6 :14 names the operator, not a ranking of its instances.
- Not that culture is always a separate confounder. It is often the bond that makes the institution what it is; the level-relative strain in section 2.8 is live.
- Not that a significant coefficient transports. That is a separate claim with a separate test (Fig 11 :66).
- Not that institutional effects are unidentifiable in principle. Fig 8 :41-42 distinguishes underivable from unreachable-at-budget. The verdict for a given case is one of those, not a universal impossibility.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by an outcome.

## 4. What would settle it (the ground)

A ground reading, not more agreement. Concretely:

1. A pre-registered design with a source of variation that is demonstrably independent of H, K, Cu and G, with the independence tests run and reported before the effect is interpreted. This tests clauses 3 and 4.
2. A transport test in a second habitat with the substituted conditions enumerated in advance. This tests clause 7 and the atom-versus-bond strain in clause 8.
3. A source-discriminating instrument, of the kind *The Hard Problem is About Source* describes for metamerism, that distinguishes an institution effect from the background configurations that can produce the same reading. This tests clause 1.
4. A pre-registered interaction test showing the union is a partition for a given case, which narrows clause 2.
5. The standing falsifiers from `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4: full separation inside one closure with no import; an association surviving every enumerated condition and every placebo test; an identical transplanted effect where the original conditions are absent; a single estimate sufficient for every target population.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** This folder can say what a separation must look like. It cannot say whether any particular institution has been validly separated, because no outcome, register or instrument was read here. The verdict is `candidate` and the status is `unreachable at this budget`, not silence and not a universal impossibility. Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence," The framework returns "a kind of thing, never the answer itself" (Fig 9 :32). Indiscernibility is not identity, so the record of the route is what keeps the result checkable.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> This derivation does not estimate an institutional effect and does not claim one is impossible. It says that a raw institution-outcome association cannot be a verdict when the institution and the outcome draw on the same historical, economic and cultural pool; that the question is a union, so the conditions must be unbundled before any method is chosen; that separation needs a source of variation imported from a setting whose closure differs, with independence bought and tested rather than declared; that the habitat of conditions must be enumerated outward because the condition that matters can be several steps away; that effects must be reported unit by unit rather than averaged; and that moving a result to another setting is a separate claim. It also notes that the institution is a molecule whose bonds may be cultural, so culture is sometimes the thing being separated and sometimes the thing doing the separating. What it offers is the shape any defensible answer must have, four refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. The answer stays open until the ground settles it.
