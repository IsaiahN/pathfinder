# Union versus partition, atoms versus bonds: the supporting derivation

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The main audit says the institution-outcome relation is a union compound and that an import is required. Two supporting questions are owed:

1. **Order.** In what sequence must the operations be run, and which reversals invalidate the result?
2. **Structure.** When is a cultural or historical condition a separate atom alongside the institution, and when is it the bond that makes the institution the thing it is?

## DOMAIN CENSUS (supporting)

Atoms held from the social-science frame: a written rule R, an enforcement practice E, a sanction S, an outcome Y, and conditions H (historical), K (economic), Cu (cultural), G (geographic). Bonds held from the figure set: the seven typed relations plus negation. No new primitive is available without import.

Domains, with anchor:

- **D1, measurement.** Actors: units and instruments. Currency: the per-slot residual. Anchor: the measurement register. Order: first.
- **D2, compound resolution.** Actors: I and C as overlapping conditions. Currency: the intersection that survives a single lever. Anchor: the ground for the compound. Order: second.
- **D3, import and independence.** Actors: the source of variation and the background conditions. Currency: independence. Anchor: a source that does not update. Order: third.
- **D4, transport.** Actors: study habitat and target habitat. Currency: what survives substitution. Anchor: the target ground. Order: fourth.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: What is the dependency order, and which reversals are fatal?

- Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."
- Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
- Fig 8 :53: "Residual first, frame second."
- Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed."

The order that follows is:

> describe R per slot → measure the units → enumerate the habitat → unbundle the union → characterise R in effect terms → import variation → test independence → report per slot → test transport → declare the refuter

Two reversals are fatal rather than merely untidy:

- **Naming a method before describing R.** Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Every instrument will appear suitable when the residual has not been described.
- **Reporting an estimate before testing independence.** Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." A coefficient reported before the test is a convention that has decided an outcome (Fig 10 :47: "a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one").
- **Refusing after minting.** Formula step 2 sorts a residual by what is missing or what is wrong and requires a refusal where a held term is false. A design that mints an effect term while still holding the common-cause term has skipped the refusal. This is a formula rule, not a figure quote.

The other reversals merely waste budget. Those are filters, not verdicts (Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict.").

### Q2: Is the union a partition after the lever?

- Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- Fig 9 :57-58: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."

Let the lever be "control for the historical, economic and cultural conditions". If the joint conditions bind (the institution is only effective where Cu holds), the lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing. The residue is a **specific compound**: an institution-in-context, not a smaller institution. This is why the answer to this question cannot be a list of covariates.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if a study shows the institutional effect is additive and invariant across every stratum of H, K, Cu and G, with a pre-registered interaction test and no residual intersection, then the union is a partition for that case and the unbundling step is unnecessary there. The claim is then narrowed, not defended by re-description.

### Q3: Atom or bond? The isomer question for institutions

- Fig 12 :117: "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :103: "Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."
- Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."

Two polities can share the same written rule R, the same enforcement practice E and the same sanction S, yet differ in the bond between them. In one, E follows R because an informal norm makes compliance expected; in the other, E is disconnected from R because the norm does not travel. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The official statute book records the ingredients and not the bond, which is exactly the flat-formula failure Fig 12 names.

At a coarser level the entire institution (R, E, S bonded) becomes one atom: Fig 13 :111: "A settled composition enters the library as a term, and is then an operand like any other." At that level the culture that sustains the bond is a separate condition in the habitat. At a finer level it is the bond itself. Both are true at their levels.

- **Falsifier:** exhibit a case where the written rule and the informal norm vary independently and each has its own separately identified effect. Then they are two atoms at the level under study and the bond reading adds nothing there.

This is the one live strain carried from `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 8. It is named, not hidden. The level at which a study operates has to be declared with an instrument, not chosen by preference.

### Q4: What is the analogue in the identification literature?

The settler-mortality chain in Acemoglu, Johnson and Robinson (2001) is written by the authors as: potential settler mortality → settlements → early institutions → current institutions → current performance. That is a dependency chain of the Fig 3 kind, and its lineage is the thing being tested. A defect in any link (mortality affecting performance through a different route, for example) breaks the whole identification, exactly as a broken middle link in Fig 3 makes everything above it useless. Their over-identification tests are the attempt to range the chain against a ground rather than against its own plausibility.

Nunn and Wantchekon (2011) decompose the slave-trade effect on trust into a cultural-norm channel and an institutional channel. That decomposition is the empirical form of the union reading: the total effect is a compound, and the two channels can be separated only with different strategies. It does not show that culture is an atom in all cases; it shows that a compound can be taken apart when the right sources of variation exist.

These are world-source analogues and corroboration only. They do not decide the figure verdict (Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you.").

### Q5: What follows for the order of a real study?

1. Per-slot description of R (Fig 1 :19).
2. Enumerate H, K, Cu, G and what contacts them (Fig 11 :44).
3. Decide the level of analysis: is Cu a bond or an atom here, and what instrument decides it?
4. Unbundle the union; expect the intersection to survive (Fig 9 :55-58).
5. Characterise R in effect terms, then look for a frame whose closure differs (Fig 6 :23, :30).
6. Test independence and buy it in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42).
7. Report per slot, test transport, declare the stake and the refuter.

## CONCLUSION

The supporting derivation adds two constraints to the main answer. **Order:** describe, enumerate, unbundle, import, test independence, report, transport, in that order; two reversals are fatal. **Structure:** the institution is a molecule whose bonds may be cultural, and whether culture is an atom or a bond is level-relative. Both constraints are checkable, and each has a falsifier written before the run. The union reading is strengthened, not replaced: the residue after the lever is a specific compound.
