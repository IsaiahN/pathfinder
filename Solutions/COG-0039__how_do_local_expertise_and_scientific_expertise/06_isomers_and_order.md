## DERIVATION

### Q1. Are the two frames isomers? CORRECTION TO MY OWN FIRST READING

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- First reading: both frames run the same loop, so they share the same atoms and differ only in arrangement; call them isomers.
- Re-census: the claim "same atoms" in Fig 12 is a claim about the atoms of a composition, and the two expertise frames are not built from a shown shared atom set at this level. What is actually shared is the **loop form**, not a enumerated atom inventory. Under Fig 12's own definition the isomer label does not pass.
- **Corrected verdict:** the two frames are members of the same **functional group** (a corrective loop ranging against a ground, Fig 4 :19-20), with different qualifiers (anchor legitimacy and habitat relation). The isomer claim is an **analogue**, not a derivation.
- **I overstated this earlier** in `01` F3's first draft. Retracted as applied to the two frames; the analogue is retained only as a question worth testing. Triangulated (Fig 12 :117, :124 and Fig 4 :19-20).

### Q2. What is their order in the dependency chain?

- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**
- The chain: perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry.
- Mapped to the question:
  1. **Perception:** the frame can tell what is in front of it and distinguish it from what was there before (a residual arrives).
  2. **Vocabulary:** the frame has terms for what the situation affords (Fig 3 :7). This is where the habitat is enumerated (local: in place; scientific: substituted).
  3. **Objective:** the frame can say what would count as progress in its own terms (Fig 3 :10). This is where "the questions they ask" lives.
  4. **Planning:** a route to that objective under incomplete information.
  5. **Learn and carry:** name what worked and carry it.
- Fig 3 :26: **"The link between them, having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."** Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**
- Reading: the difference between the two frames sits at links 2 to 3, the uninstrumented middle. That is why the question cannot be settled by comparing outputs; the difference is upstream of the output, in what counts as progress. `specified`, triangulated for the chain order (Fig 3 and Fig 5).

### Q3. Is one frame an atom for the other?

- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."**
- Each frame has settled terms (decades of verified local practice; decades of verified scientific result). Each can therefore serve as an atom for a comparison at the next level. Their comparison ⋛ is a **second-order composition**: `(settled local molecule) ⋛ (settled scientific molecule)`. This is legal only because both first-order molecules settled.
- Type caveat, still open: the Operators table defines ⋛ as *"a threshold relation between two quantities."* At second order the operands are molecules-now-atoms; level relativity permits the type only if they count as quantities at that level. This needs an instrument, not an argument. Named, not hidden.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** show that a settled local or scientific molecule cannot serve as an atom for a comparison, or that ⋛'s operands must be bare quantities at every level. Either kills the second-order reading.

### Q4. What is the legitimate analogue, and where does it break?

- The tempting analogue is the one used by the expertise-studies pool: expert versus novice (Chi, Feltovich and Glaser 1981, read 2026-11-22: *"experts initially abstract physics principles to approach and solve a problem representation, whereas novices base their representation and approaches on the problem's literal features"*).
- **Where it works:** it corroborates the structural claim that what a frame takes as the question depends on the structure of its library, which is Fig 5's direction claim at a different stratum.
- **Where it breaks:** a local expert is an expert in their own frame, not a novice. Mapping local expertise onto "novice" and scientific expertise onto "expert" is echo without shadow: a structure found elsewhere given somewhere to live. Fig 9 :28: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"** and Fig 8 :53: **"Residual first, frame second."**
- The legitimate analogue from the world pool is instead the **two-instrument** case: two instruments pointed at the same thing are two frames (Fig 2 :31), and a spectrometer resolving a metamerism is an instrument that discriminates sources (*The Hard Problem is About Source*, read 2026-11-22). That analogue preserves the anchor/contact structure without importing a prestige ranking.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a local/scientific comparison in which the local frame is demonstrably a less-developed instance of the scientific frame on the same evidence and instrument, with no residual left. Then the two-instrument analogue fails and the deficit reading wins.

## CONCLUSION

The two frames are **not isomers** under Fig 12's definition; they are members of one functional group with different qualifiers. Their difference sits at the uninstrumented middle of Fig 3's chain, where a situation is turned into a question. Their comparison is second-order, licensed by Fig 12's settled-molecule rule, with one operand-type caveat still open. The expert/novice analogue is refused as a prestige-loaded echo; the two-instrument analogue is kept. This rescues what was defensible in the intuitive "same machinery, different setup" reading without asserting a shared atom inventory, a seventh primitive, or a ranking of the two frames.
