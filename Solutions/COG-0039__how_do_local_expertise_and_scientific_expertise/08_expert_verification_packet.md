# Expert verification packet: the Tether on local and scientific expertise (COG-0039)

**What this is:** a claim in a form an expertise researcher or ethnoscientist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the frame difference, `general` on the loop shape. Not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed to settle the binding of a specific protocol to a specific figure term. Simulated derivation, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Local and scientific expertise are two expert frames running the same perceive-and-correct loop. They differ in which anchor is legitimate for their question (Fig 2 :36), in whether the habitat is enumerated in place or substituted (Fig 11 :43, :66), and in the direction of the residual they pursue, a preference their mechanisms cannot see until it is written down as an ordinary term (Fig 5 :62-63, :65). They share the loop and a functional group; neither is senior. Agreement between them is corroboration only if reached by separate evidence routes (Fig 2 :31, Fig 8 :50). The framework returns the shape of the difference, not the concrete evidence inventory for a given domain (Fig 9 :32).

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1. The anchor-legitimacy claim; checkable by reading Figure 2

- **Statement:** the legitimacy of an anchor depends on what the question is constitutively about. Human judgment is an anchor for a question about people and an instrument for a question about code, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure. Applied to the two frames, this is the source of the evidence difference, at `specified` mode.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :36, **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."** Fig 2 :37, **"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people."** Fig 2 :37-38, **"It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."**
- **What to check:** is the mapping from the figure's person/code example to local/scientific questions faithful, or is it an analogy presented as a derivation? The claim itself is explicitly labelled `specified`, so the honest check is whether it needs to be downgraded further.
- **Refuter:** exhibit an instrument that settles the axis a local question is constitutively about, without loss and without moving with the community's opinion, so the anchor difference collapses on that axis.

### C2. The habitat-contact claim; checkable against the ethnoscientific literature

- **Statement:** local evidence comes from an enumerated habitat in long contact, including cascades several steps from the immediate observation; scientific evidence is gathered in a substituted habitat, with named blind spots (what was not reproduced, what was unintentionally introduced).
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39, **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."** Fig 11 :43, **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."** Fig 11 :66, **"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."** Fig 11 :68, **"what you failed to reproduce,"** Fig 11 :69, **"what you unintentionally introduced is invisible until it acts"**.
- **What to check:** does this match the documented practice in your field? The opened literature reports the same contact/timescale contrast (Bélisle et al. 2018: *"local people interact with the environment on a daily basis, yearlong, and over the long term"*) and also the limits of local knowledge on some ecosystem processes (a result this packet does not deny). If you can show a local capability that is portable without contact loss, the claim fails.
- **Refuter:** a local capability shown to be portable with no contact loss, or a scientific result whose substituted habitat demonstrably reproduces the target's third-order cascade.

### C3. The residual-direction claim; checkable by a short exercise

- **Statement:** the difference in the questions the two frames ask is a difference in the direction of the residual, held as a preference the mechanism cannot see; writing the sought-for shape as an ordinary term makes it refusable.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :62-63, **"A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term. It is not bound, it predicts nothing, and it accrues no residual, so nothing here can see it."** Fig 5 :65-66, **"The remedy is a discipline rather than a branch: make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."**
- **What to check:** ask a local expert and a scientific expert each to write down what a satisfactory answer to the same practical question would look like before they work it. Do the two descriptions differ in the direction of search in the way claimed, and can each be refused by an observation?
- **Refuter:** show a frame whose question direction is fully determined by its mechanism and ground, leaving no free preference term. Then F3 is false for that frame.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that either kind of expertise is superior, more rational, or more rigorous.
- Not that local expertise is proto-science, or that science is formalised local knowledge (Agrawal 1995 refuses that dichotomy).
- Not that local frames are holistic and scientific frames mechanistic (Ludwig and Poliseli 2018 refuses that divide).
- Not that community consensus or peer prestige settles truth. Fig 1 :27: **"A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."** Fig 7 :35: **"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**
- Not that the two frames are isomers under Fig 12. They are members of one functional group (`06`).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment, and this packet cannot settle COG-0039.
