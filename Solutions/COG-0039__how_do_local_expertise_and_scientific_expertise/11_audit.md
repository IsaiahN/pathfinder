## 2. Independent re-census from the residual line

### 2.1 Arity, symmetry, scale

- **Arity.** The minimal relation is frame Γ, action a, observation o′ plus a ground. The question compares two such relations, so there is a second-order comparison operator. A two-place reading (expert, evidence) loses the action and the prediction and is rejected.
- **Symmetry.** The frames are not substitutable: their anchors and habitats differ. But the relation is not a ranking, because no figure makes one frame the anchor for the other. This seat initially wanted to read the comparison as Fig 12's comparison bond ⋛, which is *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* (Operators table :48-49); it withdrew that on re-census because the two frames are not shown to be quantities. The comparison is left as an unpriced second-order relation.
- **Scale.** The slot is an expert; "expertise" as a ranking exists only at a population scale; the habitat crosses a lossy membrane (Fig 4 :27). So the residual is cross-scale, and any single-scale answer is incomplete.

**Divergence from the primary census, recorded.** The primary admitted five domains, collapsing habitat into one domain D2 (in place versus substituted) and anchor legitimacy into D4. This seat at first proposed splitting the habitat into two domains, C (in place) and D (substituted), because the anchors differ in kind: C is settled by the state of a particular place, D by an instrument designed to be frame-independent. On reflection this seat keeps the split, and records why: *"Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another"* (Fig 11 :66), and the two modes have different failure lists (what was not reproduced versus what was unintentionally introduced, Fig 11 :68-69). The primary kept them together as two readings of one domain; this seat keeps them as two domains. Both arrive at the same verdict because the overlap is a compound (Fig 9 :54). The difference is a real census divergence, not a wording change.

This seat also tested merging B and E, since both involve other frames, and declined: B is about selection between rooms (Fig 7 :35), E is about which wall moves (Fig 6 :23). Different currencies, different anchors.

**Partition or union:** union of contact maintenance (A, C, D) and reach or direction renewal (E, F, with B supplying the differing closure). Their overlap is a compound with its own loop (Fig 9 :54-57).

**Dependency order:** perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, learn and carry (Fig 3 :20). This seat agrees with the primary and adds that the residual line itself forces only perception and vocabulary; objective, planning and carrying are candidate answers introduced by the frame, not forced by the residual.

## 4. Round-trip gap R_T

Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*

- **T_A, going up:** carry the primary's shape to one sentence. Result: *expertise differs by what each frame is willing to be corrected by.*
- **T_E, coming back:** put that sentence into a specific situation. Result: *ask each expert what observation would change their mind, and record it.*

**R_T = gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x)).** The abstraction cannot hold four things:

1. It loses the anchor-legitimacy condition. "Willing to be corrected by" makes community outcome and instrument sound interchangeable; Fig 2 :36 says legitimacy depends on what the question is about. Coming back down, the one-sentence version would let an instrument anchor a question about people without recording the loss.
2. It collapses the habitat split. The abstraction has no place for "what you failed to reproduce" versus "what was unintentionally introduced" (Fig 11 :68-69), so a substituted habitat looks like a cheaper version of the in-place one rather than a different object with a different failure list.
3. It loses the direction limit. "What would change your mind" is an output question; the real difference is the sought-for shape, which is a preference the mechanism cannot see (Fig 5 :62-63). The abstraction makes the problem look easier than it is.
4. It loses the independence condition. Two frames naming the same observation would look like agreement in the one-sentence version, while Fig 2 :40 says shared-pool agreement is disqualifying for a verdict.

The gap is not an error to eliminate; it is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold (Fig 4 :31). This is why the outreach packet sends the generator, not the one-sentence version. The one-paragraph version in `07` is a deliberately lossy artefact and is labelled as such.
