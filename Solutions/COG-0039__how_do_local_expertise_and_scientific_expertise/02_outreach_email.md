# Outreach draft: check one shape claim before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** send only the residual, the shape claim and the refuter in stage 1. Record the recipient's verdict before any framework material is shown. The links in stage 2 are offered only after that verdict is on record.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about local and scientific expertise

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** local expertise and scientific expertise are two expert frames running the same perceive-and-correct loop. They differ in two ways. First, each ranges against a different *legitimate* anchor. The framework's figure says an anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it: human judgment is the right anchor for "is this a good answer for a person," while it is only an instrument for "is this code correct," and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure. So a local expert's question about a particular place and community has that place and community outcome as its legitimate anchor; a scientific expert's question about a frame-independent feature uses an instrument as its anchor, with the instrument's blind spots inherited. Second, the habitat is different: local evidence comes from an enumerated habitat in long contact, including relations several steps out from the immediate observation; scientific evidence is gathered in a deliberately substituted habitat where contact is narrowed, so it carries things that were not reproduced and things that were unintentionally introduced.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that one kind is more rigorous than the other, that local knowledge is merely proto-science, that scientific knowledge is merely a special case of local knowledge, or that the framework can tell you the actual evidence list for a particular fishery or clinic. It returns the shape of the difference, not the inventory. Where the two frames converge, that is corroboration only if they reached the answer by separate routes; if they drew on the same observations, it is one pool speaking twice.
>
> **What would refute the claim:**
> 1. An instrument that settles the very axis a local question is constitutively about, without loss and without moving with the community's opinion, so the anchor difference collapses on that axis.
> 2. A local capability shown to be portable with no contact loss, or a scientific result whose substituted habitat demonstrably reproduces the target's third-order cascade.
> 3. A frame whose questions are fully determined by its mechanism and ground, leaving no direction of search that has to be written down and checked separately.
>
> I wrote these down before writing the claim. If any one holds, I will retract the part it touches. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the three public sources. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figures with line numbers: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in the public repository, branch `general-agent`: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Full architecture (19 min): https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
> - Worked narrative: https://adventuresinml.substack.com/p/how-intelligence-actually-works
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground, write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

[name]

## Two-stage discipline, stated plainly

1. **Stage 1.** Send the claim and the three refuters. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Write down their verdict before showing any framework material.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only.** If they are curious, offer the three links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration: once they have absorbed the framework's interior they are no longer an outside frame (Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*).
3. **Stage 3, optional.** Offer the formula as a reusable method, with the instruction that each user writes a fresh refuter and states a mode.
