# Two senses of one word: within-scale versus cross-scale, and the order of refusal

**Mode: `general` on the structural derivation, `specified` on the world-facing checks.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).

## RESIDUAL

The question uses one word, "information", for two different maps: the invertible dynamics map inside a frame, and the lossy description map across the membrane. Is this just two names for one thing, or two different substances? And in what order must the answer be assembled?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Are the two senses isomers or the same thing twice?

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

Fig 12 :94-96: "The arrangement is the substance." / "Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."

The two senses share the word (the "ingredient" in the record) but not the operator:

- **Within-scale sense:** invertible map. The earlier description is recoverable. This is the dynamics map that unitarity governs. Operators table :29-33: sequence "A then B; B needs A to have happened" and the test "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes." A reversible dynamics map carries the symmetry of an identity-like relation (Operators table :23-27: "two names, one referent" with "subtract one for the other. Is anything lost? No.").
- **Cross-scale sense:** non-invertible map. Fig 4 :28-29: "Going up throws away detail; that is what makes the result reapplicable. But it means the trip is not reversible, and something carried up and brought back down will not match what was sent." The operator here is the lossy transform with a direction, "a filter with a direction" (Fig 4 :21).

These are not two names for one quantity; they are two arrangements of one word under different operators, with different consequences. By Fig 12's definition they are **isomer-like**: same token, different bond, different substance, and the formula (the single word "information") cannot tell them apart. That is exactly why averaging them produces nonsense.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if the within-scale map and the cross-scale transform can be shown to be one operator (same invertibility, same range), the isomer reading fails and the two senses collapse into one. F4 of `01` then fails and the question is one question after all.

### Q2 — What order does the answer require?

Fig 3 :19-21: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." / "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."

Fig 3 :25-27: the middle link, "having terms for what a situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress", has "neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments." / "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

For this question the dependency chain is:

1. **Perceive:** name the two maps in effect terms (recoverability of earlier from later). Link 1 to 2.
2. **Vocabulary and objective:** define "conserved" as invertible, "lost" as non-invertible with a recorded gap. This is the middle link that usually has no instrument, and it is the one this package supplies explicitly in §0 of `01`.
3. **Refuse before mint:** Operators table :53-58: negation "this does not hold" with the test "is the point that it is false, rather than absent? Yes." and "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without negation can only accumulate." The conflation of the two senses must be refused (¬) before any conservation claim is minted. Formula step 2: "REFUTED — a term held is false; what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation."
4. **Settle the substrate:** F3 of `01` (energy is the conserved state; no information primitive).
5. **Measure the cross-scale gap:** F1 of `01`; `R_T` by the round trip (Fig 4 :30).
6. **State the D3 gap:** no figure line governs quantum-gravity dynamics; report `gap`, not a name.

A reading that starts at step 6 (the physics consensus) and works backwards is a reading below the break: "It is a reading of nothing" (Fig 3 :22-23).

### Q3 — What does the round trip preserve, and what does it drop?

Symbols table :75-78: `R_T` is "the gap between what was sent up and what came back down: gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x)). Measures what the coarser description cannot hold."

Apply it to this package's own condensation:

- **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figures, chiefly Fig 4 :27-32 (lossy cross-scale transform, measured gap), Fig 13 :13-14 and :3 (energy conserved, six closed terms), Fig 9 :32 and Fig 13 :134 (shape, not name; unreachable is a verdict).
- **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand "the framework says information is not conserved across scales, energy is what is conserved, and quantum gravity is a gap".
- **Concretisation (T_E):** a reader's natural reading, "information is destroyed in black holes".

**R_T is nonzero.** The shorthand does not survive the trip back down intact: the loss is in the *description transform*, not in dynamics; the conserved state is *energy*, not a generic conserved quantity; the quantum-gravity verdict is a *gap*, not a no. Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." The honest form is a lossy condensation with a measured gap, not an identity between the formula and the figures. This mirrors the round trip recorded in the exemplar's `11_audit.md`.

### Q4 — Does the substrate let us mint an information atom?

Fig 13 :115-117: "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists." / "Six members, and no amount of arranging produces a seventh: a new member would have to be a new kind, and composition only ever rearranges the kinds already held."

Fig 5 :29-31: the three guards, "SUPPORT |R⁺_s| > 0 for some s before the search", "REACHABILITY φ ∈ closure(Γ) during", "NOVELTY φ ∉ atoms(Γ) after", and "Any one of them at zero stops the mint entirely."

For a conserved-information primitive φ:

- **SUPPORT:** no live residual on a slot. The framework's residual here is the bundle in the question, not an unheld conserved quantity. Zero.
- **REACHABILITY:** information is not derivable from the six and seven bonds. Zero.
- **NOVELTY:** novel, yes, but novelty alone cannot pass a product gate (Fig 5 :41 "Any one of them at zero stops the mint entirely.").

No mint. What is owed is not a new term but a refusal of the conflation and a `gap` report for D3.

## CONCLUSION

The two senses of "information" are isomer-like: same token, different operators, different consequences, and the single word hides the difference. The answer must be assembled in dependency order: bind the shape, refuse the conflation, settle the substrate, measure the cross-scale gap, and finally report the physics `gap`. The round trip of this package's own condensation has a nonzero `R_T`, recorded above rather than assumed away. No conserved-information atom can be minted from the six; the conserved state in the substrate is energy.

This rescues what is defensible without inventing a seventh primitive: a structural no for cross-scale conservation, a structural no for an information conserved-primitive, and an honest stop for quantum gravity.
