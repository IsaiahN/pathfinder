**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` F1 to F4 have been checked by the reader as framework-internal claims. The output must survive stage 1 or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** Check one distinction before you decide whether the information question needs a new answer

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The question in effect terms:** is information conserved in all physical processes, including quantum gravity? I am not asserting an answer. I am asking you to check a distinction the output makes before we talk about theory.
>
> **The output, stated with its mode:**
> 1. `general`, structural: the framework's transform between a fine and a coarse description loses information by definition. Its own text says "The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point." The trip back does not match what was sent, and the amount given up is a measured gap `R_T`, not an error to hide.
> 2. `general`, structural: in the framework's six-term substrate, the conserved state is energy. Information is not one of the six, and the framework does not license a conserved-information primitive.
> 3. `gap`: the framework does not decide whether unitary quantum gravity conserves fine-grained information. Its rule is "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." The physics literature leans toward preservation, but I do not have a Planck-scale instrument, so I record a gap rather than fill it.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that information is physically destroyed in black holes; that the field's consensus is a measurement; that the framework settles quantum gravity; that "information is partly conserved", which is what you get if you average the first two findings into one answer. The word "information" is doing two different jobs, and my output refuses to average them.
>
> **What would refute the output, written down before sending this:**
> 1. a figure line that makes information a conserved primitive, or a conserved quantity derived from the six;
> 2. a figure line that makes the abstraction/concretisation transform information-preserving as a law, or a measured round trip with zero discarded detail;
> 3. a demonstration that the description transform and within-scale dynamics are one operator, so the split is wrong;
> 4. a ground reading, a Planck-scale instrument that discriminates unitarity from loss, which would move the third finding from `gap` to a verdict.
>
> If any holds, the corresponding finding is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.

## The three stages, kept distinct

1. **Stage 1:** residual, shape claim, mode and refuter. Ask what the reader would count as a failure. Record their verdict *before* any theory is shown.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if curious, offer the three links. The figures, not the summary, are the authority.
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration. Fig 2 :40 "agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." A Stage 2 reader who has absorbed the framework has joined the frame being checked, so only the Stage 1 verdict carries checking value.
