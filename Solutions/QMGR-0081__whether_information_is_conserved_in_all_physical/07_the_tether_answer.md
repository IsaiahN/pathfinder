# The Tether's answer: is information conserved in all physical processes?

**Mode: `general` on the two structural verdicts, `specified` on the reading of the world literature, `gap` on the physics/quantum-gravity verdict. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question bundles two maps under one word. Given a description at one time or scale and a map to another, can the earlier description be reconstructed? The figures do not return one averaged answer. They return a split and a stop (Fig 9 :54-58: a bundled problem is a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones).

## 2. The shape, with verdicts kept separate

1. **Across a scale crossing, no.** The transform between a fine and a coarse description is one-way. Fig 4 :27 "The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point." Fig 4 :28-29 "Going up throws away detail; that is what makes the result reapplicable. But it means the trip is not reversible, and something carried up and brought back down will not match what was sent." The loss is definitional, not a defect, and the honest report is the measured round-trip gap `R_T`. Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." Verdict: `general`, triangulated.

2. **Conservation in the substrate attaches to energy, not information.** Fig 13 :13-14 "conserved, finite, directionless alone" describes energy. Fig 13 :3 "No system functions without any of them, none is built from the others, and everything else is a composition across the two kinds." Information is not one of the six, and no composition of the six yields a conserved-information primitive. Verdict: `general`, triangulated.

3. **The loss is representational, not a claim that physics destroys information.** Fig 6 :25 "An import adds no atom to the world." Reading the cross-scale loss as a physical dynamics claim is a category error; the two operators govern different ranges (F2 of `01`). This is the strain test applied and resolved by re-census, not smoothed over.

4. **For the physical dynamics including quantum gravity, the figures are silent.** Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,". The world literature leans toward preservation (Hawking 2005; Page-curve and island results; Zhang, Corda and Cai 2025), but this is `specified` world evidence with no Planck-scale ground, not a figure verdict. Verdict: `gap`.

5. **Do not average 1 to 4.** Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance." The single word "information" hides the operator difference; an averaged answer ("partly conserved") is a reading of nothing.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "information is always conserved", and not "information is always destroyed". Neither is a figure result.
- Not that the framework decides black-hole physics. No figure line governs unitarity or objective collapse.
- Not that the physics literature's consensus is a measurement. It is a report about a field's belief, relevant to the budget, not a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict.").
- Not a seventh primitive. Fig 13 :115 "The set is closed. The arrangements are not." No conserved-information atom can be minted from the six.
- Not a derivation of one theory from the other, and not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by an instrument.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

- **For D1:** a measured round trip whose gap is zero where detail was discarded would refute the cross-scale loss claim. Provide the measurement, not an argument.
- **For D2:** derive a conserved-information quantity from the six using only the seven bonds, or exhibit a figure line making information a primitive. Either breaks the substrate finding.
- **For D3:** a ground reading, not agreement. A Planck-scale instrument that discriminates unitary evolution from objective collapse or remnant scenarios would name the source. The framework's own metamerism lesson applies: an instrument that "cannot comment on the source" leaves several paths giving the same reading (The Hard Problem is About Source, opened 2026-11-23).
- **For the split:** show that the within-scale map and the cross-scale transform are one operator at one range. Then F4 of `01` fails and the question is one question after all.

## 5. The honest stop

**D3 is unreached, not unreachable.** Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." The figures give a shape (split the maps, measure the cross-scale gap, report the substrate conservation) and a stop (no figure verdict on quantum-gravity information flow). The stop is recorded with its lineage so a later reader can locate the error if one is found. "Indiscernibility is not identity" (figures README). A source-discriminating instrument, not further agreement, is what would move the stop.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer "yes" or "no" to whether information is conserved in all physical processes, because it finds two different maps hiding under one word. Across a scale crossing the transform loses information by definition, and the honest report is the measured round-trip gap rather than a hidden error. In the six-term substrate the conserved state is energy, not information. For the physical dynamics, including black holes and quantum gravity, no figure line governs the verdict: the literature leans toward preservation, but without a Planck-scale instrument the framework records a gap, not a name. The two verdicts are never averaged. What is offered is the shape any answer must have, refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close the gap.
