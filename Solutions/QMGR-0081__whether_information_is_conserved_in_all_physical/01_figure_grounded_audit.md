## 0. The residual, in effect terms

The question: "Whether information is conserved in all physical processes, including quantum gravity" (`QM & GR Questions.txt` line 103).

Stated as an effect, not a topic: given a map from a description at one time or scale to a description at another, can the earlier description be reconstructed from the later one? Option A says yes for all physical processes. Option B says no for at least some. What each leads to:

- **A (conservation everywhere):** every physical map is invertible; nothing is discarded; no gap needs recording. This would require a conserved-information primitive or a composition of the six that yields one.
- **B (not everywhere):** at least one map discards something; the discarded amount must be reported, not averaged away. Under the figures this is the round-trip gap `R_T` (Symbols table: "the gap between what was sent up and what came back down").

**Bound shape term (written before the run, so it can accrue a residual):** "information conserved" means the map is invertible; "information lost" means the map is non-invertible and a gap must be recorded. This makes the question's own preferred shape refusable (Fig 5 :65-66: "make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else.").

**R by arity, symmetry and scale.**

| property | reading |
|---|---|
| arity | at least two conditions bundled: a within-scale dynamics condition and a cross-scale description condition. **Union compound**, not one hard question (Fig 9 :54-58: a bundled problem is a union of overlapping conditions; a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either). |
| symmetry | conservation is invariance under an invertible map (symmetric/bijective); loss is a non-invertible, directed discard. Two different operator families, so the word "conserved" is being used for two different relations. |
| scale | within one frame (dynamics at a scale) versus crossing the membrane (description at two scales). First order versus round trip. |

## 3. Findings

### F1 — The framework holds a lossy transform between scales as definitional (decisive for D1)

Fig 4 :27: "The transform between scales loses information, and that is the point."

Fig 4 :28-29: "Going up throws away detail; that is what makes the result reapplicable. But it means the trip is not reversible, and something carried up and brought back down will not match what was sent."

Fig 4 :31-32: "The gap is not an error to eliminate. It is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold, and it is the only honest report of how much was given up."

The membrane is "a filter with a direction" (Fig 4 :21: "The membrane is the boundary between the sizes, and the rule about what may cross it. Not a wall: a filter with a direction."). Between a fine and a coarse description the map is one-way: methods go up, head starts come down, and the trip does not match what was sent.

**D1 verdict:** no. Information is not conserved across a scale crossing. The loss is not a defect to be engineered away; it is exactly what makes an abstraction reusable, and the honest report is `R_T`, measured, not assumed. Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."

**Mode:** `general` (structural). **Status:** triangulated (Fig 4, Fig 6, Symbols table, and the independent RG literature in `09_watson_answer.md`, low overlap). **Refuter:** a figure line stating that the abstraction/concretisation transform preserves information as a law (`R_T = 0`), or a measured round trip with zero gap where detail was discarded.

### F2 — The loss is in the representation, not a new physical atom (strain resolved by re-census)

Fig 6 :25: "An import adds no atom to the world."

Fig 6 :26: "The world does not gain primitives; a frame gains access."

A careless reading of F1 says "information is physically destroyed in all processes." That reading strains against the physics literature, which holds that fine-grained information is preserved under unitary dynamics. Re-census: F1 governs the transform between a frame's coarse and fine descriptions (`T_A` / `T_E`), an operator on representations. Unitarity governs the dynamics within one frame, a different operator at a different range. Fig 4 :19-20 separates them: "The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."

So there is **no strain after re-census**: the two claims govern different quantities. The strain arises only if the membrane transform is misidentified as a physical process. That misidentification is an import with no source gate (Fig 8 on provenance), and it adds no atom to the world.

**D1 qualifier:** *the frame loses descriptive information across a scale crossing* is not the same claim as *physical dynamics destroys information*. Keeping them separate is the whole point of the census. **Refuter:** a figure re-census showing the membrane transform and the within-scale dynamics are one operator, not two ranges.

### F3 — Conservation in the substrate attaches to energy, not to information (D2)

Fig 13 :13-14: "energy" / "conserved, finite, directionless alone".

Symbols table :89-91: energy is "Conserved, finite, and directionless on its own." The Symbols table also defines `R_T` as "the gap between what was sent up and what came back down: gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x)). Measures what the coarser description cannot hold."

Fig 13 :3: "No system functions without any of them, none is built from the others, and everything else is a composition across the two kinds."

Fig 13 :115-117: "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists." / "Six members, and no amount of arranging produces a seventh: a new member would have to be a new kind, and composition only ever rearranges the kinds already held."

Information is not one of the six. There is no figure line that attributes conservation to an information primitive. The thermodynamic-law table in Fig 13 makes energy load-bearing for the second law ("finiteness in a finite region is what makes the counting terminate", :67-68) and makes no such assignment to information.

**D2 verdict:** the six-term substrate does not license "information is conserved" as a primitive or as a derived conserved quantity. The conserved state in the set is energy. **Mode:** `general` (structural). **Status:** triangulated (Fig 13 list, Fig 13 minimality table, Symbols table). **Refuter:** derive a conserved-information quantity from the six using only the seven bonds, or exhibit a figure line making information a seventh member.

### F4 — The question is a union compound, not one hard question (governs the method)

Fig 9 :54-58: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." / "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." / "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

The word "information" is doing two jobs: the conserved fine-grained quantity of dynamics (D3) and the discarded descriptive detail of the scale transform (D1). ### F5 — The physics/quantum-gravity verdict is a gap, not a "no" (D3)

No figure line governs whether unitary quantum gravity conserves fine-grained information. The figures are a metatheory of frames, composition and grounds, not a dynamical theory of black holes. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence," and :135: "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." That distinguishes a gap from a refutation: the framework does not decide the physics question, and its silence is reported, not filled.

The world literature (read 2026-11-23, full ledger in `10_source_ledger.csv`) shows a field with an internal theoretical ground and no Planck-scale experimental anchor:

- Hawking (2005): "Elementary quantum gravity interactions do not lose information or quantum coherence."
- Zhang, Corda and Cai (2025): "Our calculation shows that the continuous tunneling black hole radiation process is an entropy-conserving process. Thus, we can say that the black hole radiation process is a unitary process, and information is not lost."
- Wikipedia reference article: "The unitary evolution of a state according to Schrödinger's equation preserves the entropy." and "A process may appear irreversible if one keeps track only of the system's coarse-grained features and not of its microscopic details, as is usually done in thermodynamics."
- Mori and Nagasawa (2026), in a different field (RG / statistical mechanics): "We also introduce the relative entropy of coarse graining to measure discarded UV information, prove its monotonicity along successive coarse grainings."

These are secondary corroboration only. They do not supply the anchor the figures lack, and agreement among them is partly a shared evidence pool (several cite the same Page-curve results). The field's own consensus ("It is now generally believed that information is preserved in black-hole evaporation") is a report about belief, not a ground reading.

**D3 verdict:** `gap` from the figures. **Status:** single-source for the framework's silence (no figure line either way), with world sources leaning toward unitarity but ungrounded at the Planck scale. **Refuter:** a figure line that states a dynamics-level conservation law, or a Planck-scale instrument that discriminates unitarity from information loss. Until one fires, the honest answer is "the figures do not decide this".
