## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134-135: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence. It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel* (read 2026-11-23): *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For QMGR-0032 the shape is: **two claims, one about a decaying channel and one about a claimed change of ground, with the second requiring an import and an instrument that does not yet pay.** The name of a specific collapse model is not part of the shape.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* The README: the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."*
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :51-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*They're Scanning Books When They Should* (carried): *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space."* Also: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."*

For this question: naming Diósi-Penrose is a binding from the physics frame onto a held vocabulary, permitted if provenanced, forbidden as a framework derivation. Naming a decoherence rate is forbidden at figure level because no figure prices one. The six-term substrate is a visible guess, not nature's own list.

## 4. Why "gravity causes collapse" is the source error the README forbids

*The Hard Problem is About Source* (read 2026-11-23) gives the mechanism. A color instrument reads green and *"it cannot comment on the source"*: one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information."* *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy."* It was the **spectrometer**, not an argument, that resolved it. *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."*

Here the reading is a loss of interference visibility. Several sources can produce the same reading: coupling to internal clock degrees of freedom (Pikovski et al. 2015), a graviton bath (Buza and Toroš 2026), stochastic metric fluctuations (Donadi et al. 2026), or a genuine physical collapse (Bassi et al. 2013). Naming "collapse" as the source of the visibility loss is the metamerism error: a source named from a reading where several sources could give the same reading. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument, not a name.

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (read 2026-11-23): *"the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself."* This is why this folder cannot settle the question, and why agreement among readers is not the validator.

## 5. Q2 - What licenses a particular collapse model here?

**Answer: nothing in the framework.** The framework returns a shape. A particular collapse model is licensed by the ground settling a minted term, or by a provenanced import with a shadow test. Until one of those, the name is the searcher's own contribution and must be recorded as such.

**Does the question commit the forbidden error?** The source question asks whether gravity *itself* causes collapse as though a visibility reading could answer it. That is the metamerism error described in §4. The correct form keeps two lines: (1) does the channel decay under gravity, and (2) is the ground of the dynamics modified by gravity. These have different instruments and are not averaged.

## 5b. Correction to my own §3

I initially wrote that "shape, not a name" forbids naming any mechanism in this question. That overstates the rule. The README's phrase governs a **missing** primitive, one not held in `Γ`. The physics frame already holds collapse models and master equations; naming them is a **binding** onto terms external to the framework, not the invention of a new primitive inside it. What remains wrong is calling such a binding a derivation, or claiming it moves the wall (Fig 6 :25: *"An import adds no atom to the world."*). Both claims are recorded so neither is quietly lost.

## 6. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (read): *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."*

A campaign that gathers famous names to agree that gravity collapses the wavefunction is the named collapse, not corroboration. Fig 2 :42: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* The legitimate ask gives the claim a budget to be tried: point an instrument at it.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of the claim and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on.
