**Subject:** Two gravity claims that get bundled, and one thing that would refute the split

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not
> asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree. If the output does not
> survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`).** The question "does gravity induce decoherence,
> and does gravity itself collapse the wavefunction" is two claims in one sentence. The framework
> I am using has a rule: when a split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before
> you search (Fig 9 :54). A second rule says the ground does not decay, the channel does
> (Fig 10 :24). Applied here, decoherence is a claim about the channel: phase information goes
> into degrees of freedom you did not track, so visibility falls and the reduced state
> diagonalises. Collapse is a claim about the ground: the dynamics itself is modified. A
> visibility loss is not a collapse result, and the framework returns a shape here, not a name.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that gravity has been shown to cause collapse, that a particular
> collapse model is the right one, or that any measured decoherence rate follows from the
> framework. No figure prices a rate. I am also not claiming this settles whether gravity is
> quantum: one recent result states that decoherence alone cannot certify that.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a gravity-induced loss of coherence in which no unobserved degree of freedom carries the
>    phase, so the decay is in the ground and not the channel;
> 2. a derivation of a collapse operator from the framework's six terms and seven bonds with no
>    imported stochastic atom;
> 3. a source-discriminating instrument showing that decoherence alone certifies quantised
>    gravity;
> 4. evidence that no existing instrument returns any gravity-correlated coherence signal at
>    any resolution, so the question is unreachable rather than merely unreached.
>
> I wrote these before sending this. If any one holds, I will narrow or retract the claim. A
> one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not
> engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my
> summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract in this solution):
>   `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - The eight-step formula and mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - Published review of collapse models and their experimental tests:
>   https://arxiv.org/abs/1204.4325
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the local file above. Run it on a
> problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result
> *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. The framework's own rule is that a
> loop cannot check the direction of its own question. A reported failure is more useful to me
> than a reported success.
>
> - [name]
