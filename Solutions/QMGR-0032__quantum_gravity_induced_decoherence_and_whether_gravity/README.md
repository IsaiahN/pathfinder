## What this folder is

A derive-first audit of a two-part question. The question joins (A) a claim that gravity induces decoherence and (B) a claim that gravity itself causes wavefunction collapse. The framework's own union rule requires these to be unbundled before either is searched. Part A is a channel claim; part B is a claim about the ground. The figures separate them categorically and do not average their verdicts.

**Boundary.** Nothing outside this folder was modified. Primary sources: the 13 figures, Operators table and Symbols table in `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`, the canonical usage rule in `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`, and `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`. ## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to believe the claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the figures' own words:

1. **A room does not settle a ground question.** Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Decoherence and collapse are decided by matter-wave instruments, not by a show of hands.
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* and Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
3. **Asking a reader to trust before they check spends their independence.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."*

Legally: harvesting personal emails for unsolicited bulk outreach breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and will get a sending domain blacklisted. That is a practical failure on top of the structural one.
