**What this is:** a claim in a form a gravitational-decoherence researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No measurement at the relevant scale is claimed here. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "does gravity induce decoherence, and does gravity itself collapse the wavefunction" is two claims joined by a word. Fig 9 :54 requires a union to be unbundled before it is searched. Decoherence is a claim about a channel: phase information enters degrees of freedom the observer did not track, so the reduced state diagonalises and visibility falls. Collapse is a claim about the ground: the dynamics itself is modified so one outcome is physically selected. Fig 10 :24 says the ground does not decay, the channel does. A collapse term is therefore a new atom, imported from the physics frame with provenance, not a derivation from the framework's six terms. Written over the same reduced-state atoms the two claims are a testable isomer claim: same atoms, different coupling, different substance. The framework returns a shape, not a name; the name comes from an instrument. Instruments already exist, so the question is unreached, not unreachable.

### C2 - physics-facing; checkable by a researcher against the decoherence literature

- **Statement:** the decoherence half is a coupling-to-unobserved-degrees-of-freedom result, not a collapse result, and different sources can give the same visibility reading. Written over the same reduced-state atoms, the two claims are Fig 12 isomers: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- **What to check:** Pikovski et al. 2015 derive decoherence *"even without any external environment"* through internal coupling; Angeli et al. 2026 derive a master equation whose noisy terms suppress coherences between different mass quadrupoles; Buza and Toroš 2026 report the graviton effect *"remains small for realistic parameters"*; Matsui 2026 states *"decoherence alone cannot certify that gravity is quantized"*. Do these support the structural distinction, or does some result make visibility loss and physical collapse the same dynamics?
- **Refuter:** a derivation in which a single coupling both decoheres the reduced state and physically selects one branch without an additional term; or a demonstration that collapse cannot be written in reduced-state form (which kills the isomer claim and leaves the functional-group claim standing).
