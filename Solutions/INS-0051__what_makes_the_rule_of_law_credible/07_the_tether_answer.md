# The Tether's answer: what makes the rule of law credible under political pressure

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A legal system predicts that verdicts settle disputes by law. The observed condition is that enforcers are subject to political pressure. R is positive when rulings track influence rather than evidence. This is a **union compound** of four conditions, not one hard problem. Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Verdicts below are kept per domain and never averaged into a single credibility score.

## 2. One loop per domain

### D1, case-level adjudication: VERDICT (triangulated)

Fig 2 :29 says "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :33 says "The anchor must not update." The facts and the law are the anchor; if a ruling changes when a powerful frame pushes, the reading is not an anchor reading. The failure is named in Fig 2 :14: "they converge, and what they converge on" and continues "is whoever had the most influence". World corroboration from a different field: the Tacit Knowledge essay says "Courts of law rule on evidence, which neither side can move." **Verdict: a non-updating anchor is necessary. No name for the institution is derived.**

### D2, institutional checking arrangement: VERDICT (triangulated for the rule, single-source for the lottery mechanism)

Fig 10 :42 says "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Fig 10 :59 says "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying." Fig 10 :60 says "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged." Fig 10 :80 says "Frames whose independence is renewed by separate experience can be maintained; frames whose correlation is fixed at origin can only be rotated". Fig 10 :93 says "A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."

So the applier is kept blind to the wanted result, interests are declared rather than denied, independence is bought and refreshed, and a court that guards its own brand is disqualified. For the pool, Fig 10 :73 says "A candidate set chosen by a participant is an authored room however well the selection is reasoned, so the pool must be unauthored first and examined second." Fig 10 :76 permits "a lottery". The Sortition article independently reports that allotment was used "to avoid the corrupt practices used by oligarchs to buy their way into office". **Verdict: blind seat plus declared stakes plus unauthored pool plus rotation. The recorded cost is competence, to be bought separately without re-authoring the pool.**

### D4, legitimacy and measurement: VERDICT (triangulated)

Fig 1 :19 says "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 2 :40 says "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Fig 7 :35 says "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."

Credibility is the observable effect of the anchor holding, measured per case and per institution. Public approval is a convention about shared meaning, not a verdict on whether the anchor held. Fig 10 :65 says "Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right: the ground does that, and a wrong ruling stays wrong however clean its record." **Verdict: per-case, source-discriminating measurement; never a pooled trust score; never a list of approvers.**

### D3, the political pressure frame: no separate verdict

D3 has no nameable anchor of its own. It is the frame that presses on the anchor, not a domain with a ground. By the census rule it does not get a verdict. Fig 2 :45 says "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." The political frame must not become a party to the ruling.

## 3. The shape, assembled

1. **The anchor does not update.** Evidence and law settle the case; pressure does not change the answer (D1).
2. **The checker is blind.** The applier gets the case as a characterised residual, never the political source, and cannot reconstruct the wanted verdict (D2, Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source.").
3. **Interests are declared.** An interest in the reading, not the outcome, disqualifies; standing is itself a stake (D2).
4. **The pool is unauthored**, with a lottery as one permitted mechanism, and independence is refreshed by rotation or separate experience (D2).
5. **Credibility is measured per case**, by an instrument that discriminates law from pressure, not by agreement (D4).
6. **Precedent recurses.** A settled ruling becomes an atom for later rulings, transmitting the method, not the recording (Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it.").

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a constitution. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Tenure, sortition, budget firewalls, recusal rules and disclosure are bindings from the world frame, provenanced as such.
- Not that judges must have no stake. Fig 10 :58: "A seat with no stake at all does not exist."
- Not that more law, more training, or more transparency is sufficient. Each is a single lever on a union compound (Fig 9 :55).
- Not that public approval corroborates the rule of law. Correlation has valence (Fig 2 :40 and :41).
- Not that clean provenance makes a ruling correct (Fig 10 :65).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement in this folder settles whether a particular institution has a non-updating anchor.

## 5. What would settle it, and the honest stop

**The ground, not agreement.** Reach for an instrument that discriminates the source of a ruling:

- Per-case data on whether outcomes change when the political composition of the appointing body changes, holding evidence constant.
- Reasoned opinions and selection records that let an outside frame distinguish convergent derivation from adopted import (Fig 8 :50).
- Separate-experience checks: panels whose independence is renewed, compared with panels whose correlation is fixed at origin.
- A per-slot measure, not a national average, because Fig 1 :22 says "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

**Honest stop: unreached, not unreachable.** The four essays are a visible guess at where credibility lives, not nature's own list. This run stops at the limit of what the available instruments distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity" (figures README). The pre-registered refuters in `01` section 4 would move it or kill it. Nothing here is settled.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> Credibility under political pressure is not a property of a judge's virtue, the text of the law, or the number of people who approve of the courts. It is a property of a checking arrangement. The facts and law that settle a case must not change their answer when power pushes on them; if they do, rulings converge on whoever has the most influence. The person applying them must be kept blind to the politically wanted result, must declare any stake in the reading, and must come from a pool that was not hand-picked by a participant. Independence has to be bought and refreshed, because a checker who only reads itself drifts with it. Credibility itself has to be measured per case by an instrument that can tell whether a ruling came from law or from pressure, since a verdict alone cannot disclose its source. What the framework offers is that shape, five refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it. It does not name a constitution, it does not call public approval evidence, and it does not claim to have measured any court.
