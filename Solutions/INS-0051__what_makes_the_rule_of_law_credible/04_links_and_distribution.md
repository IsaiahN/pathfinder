## Wider literature that bears on the claim

1. **Procedural justice and legitimacy.** The procedural justice material gives an independent (social psychology, organisational behaviour) reason to think that the *process* by which a verdict is reached carries credibility, not only the verdict itself. Leventhal's "bias suppression" and Rawls's "independent criterion for a fair or just outcome" are the world-field counterparts of the anchor and blind-checker conditions. This corroborates Fig 2 and Fig 10; it does not derive them.
2. **Constitutional economics and investment.** The judicial independence article's economic-effects section reports that investors have confidence in "a strong and stable judiciary that is insulated from interference". That is a world-field prediction of Fig 2 :42, "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong": the cost of a captured court is priced by markets.
3. **Sortition and unauthored pools.** The sortition material is the clearest low-overlap corroboration of Fig 10 :76, "a lottery". Athens' allotment machines were an explicit anti-capture mechanism. The recorded expertise objection is a genuine design cost and is handled in the census, not hidden.
4. **Source discrimination.** The metamerism example in the Hard Problem essay is the analogue for the central confusion in this question: a ruling that *looks* lawful (a green reading) may have been produced by law or by pressure (different sources). What is owed is an instrument that discriminates sources, not a better argument about the verdict.

No world source conflicts with the figure verdict. The one internal tension (sortition versus expertise) was raised by a source that otherwise corroborates the claim, and is recorded as a cost to buy separately.

## Distribution rules

Figure-grounded reasons for offering, not pushing:

- **Fig 4 :22** "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." Sending the full architecture before anyone has described a residual sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the four-condition shape and its refuter; offer the links after.
- **Fig 10 :42** "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27-28** "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Ask what they would expect to observe if the anchor held before showing the sources.
- **Fig 2 :40** "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." A mail-merge to likely agreeers produces a convention, not corroboration.
- **Recursive Self-Improvement Trap** "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly." A list of famous names saying yes gives the claim a budget, not a verdict.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape and its five refuters; (2) ask what they would count as a failure of the shape; (3) record their verdict; (4) only then the three links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1:** validate the shape against the ground. The recipient checks the four conditions without holding the framework's interior.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in:** if curious, read the theory that produced it. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3:** use the formula recursively on other problems, with each user writing their own refuter and stating their own mode (Fig 4 :22).
