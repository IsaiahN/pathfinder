# Expert verification packet: the Tether on rule of law credibility

**What this is:** a claim in a form a legal scholar, institutional designer or empirically minded lawyer can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.** No measurement of any court is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A legal system's verdicts are credible under political pressure only if four conditions hold together. The thing that settles a case does not change its answer when a powerful frame pushes on it. The applier is not handed the politically wanted result and cannot reconstruct it from the case material. Any stake in which way the case comes out is declared, and an interest in the reading rather than the outcome is disqualifying. The pool of appliers was not hand-picked by a participant, and a per-case record lets an outside reader tell whether the anchor actually moved. This is a shape, not a constitution. The framework does not name tenure, sortition, a budget rule or a country as the answer.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: the anchor must not update (checkable by reading Figures 2 and 10)

- **Statement:** credibility requires a non-updating anchor. A ruling that changes with political pressure is not an anchor reading; the system has converged on whoever had the most influence.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Also Fig 2 :33 "The anchor must not update." And Fig 2 :30 "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."
- **What to check:** is "the anchor must not update" actually a rule of the figures, and does the failure mode under pressure follow from it? Compare with the Tacit Knowledge essay's world-field statement: "Courts of law rule on evidence, which neither side can move."
- **Refuter:** a legal system whose rulings demonstrably track who has the most influence yet still satisfies the anchor test; or a figure re-census showing an anchor may update on a frame and still count as an anchor.

### C2: the checker must be blind, the stake declared, and the pool unauthored (checkable by reading Figure 10, then by inspecting real selection rules)

- **Statement:** the applier receives the case, not the political instruction; an interest in the outcome is unavoidable but an interest in the reading disqualifies; a participant-chosen pool is an authored room, and a lottery is a permitted unauthored mechanism.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Fig 10 :59 "an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying." Fig 10 :60 "Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it. An undeclared interest is modelled anyway and an unnamed model runs unchallenged." Fig 10 :73 "A candidate set chosen by a participant is an authored room however well the selection is reasoned, so the pool must be unauthored first and examined second." Fig 10 :76 "a lottery".
- **What to check:** do these lines say what the packet claims? Then apply to one real institution: can its judges see the wanted result, are interests disclosed, and who composed the pool? The Sortition article is a low-overlap corroboration of the lottery mechanism, including the caveat about expertise.
- **Refuter:** a checker that can reconstruct the wanted verdict yet still discriminates law from influence at maximum independence; or an authored pool whose agreement carries as much information as an unauthored one; or a demonstration that competence cannot be added to a lottery without re-authoring it.

### C3: credibility must be measured per case, and provenance is not correctness (checkable by reading Figures 1, 2, 8 and 10)

- **Statement:** a pooled trust score hides live failures; agreement within a shared evidence pool is a convention, not a verdict; a ruling that agrees with a powerful frame needs a record to distinguish convergence from capture; and a clean record is not the same as a correct ruling.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Fig 8 :50 "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them". Fig 10 :65 "Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right: the ground does that, and a wrong ruling stays wrong however clean its record."
- **What to check:** do the valence rule and the non-aggregation rule apply to credibility measurement as claimed? Is there any instrument in the literature that reads a verdict alone and correctly identifies law versus pressure with no access to reasons or selection records?
- **Refuter:** a pooled measure that preserves every per-slot failure without loss; or an instrument that discriminates the source of a ruling from the verdict text alone.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a theory of a particular constitution, and not "the answer" to institutional design. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."
- Not that judges can have no stake. Fig 10 :58: "A seat with no stake at all does not exist."
- Not that public approval, prestige or a list of eminent signatories corroborates the rule of law. Prestige can give a claim a budget to be tried; only the ground settles it.
- Not that more law, more training or more transparency is sufficient. Each is a single lever on a union compound (Fig 9 :55).
- Not that clean procedure makes a ruling correct (Fig 10 :65).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement of any court is asserted here.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; a spectrometer settled the case. Here:

1. Does the outcome change when the political composition of the appointing body changes, holding evidence constant? That tests whether the anchor updates.
2. Do reasoned opinions and selection records let an outside frame distinguish convergent derivation from adopted import (Fig 8 :50)?
3. Do panels whose independence is renewed by separate experience behave differently from panels whose correlation is fixed at origin (Fig 10 :80)?
4. Is the failure visible per case rather than only in a national average (Fig 1 :19)?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29, :30, :33 and Fig 10 :42, :59, :60, :73, :76, :65. Ten short passages.
2. Check C2 against one institution you know: are its appliers blind to the wanted result, are interests declared, and was the pool authored by a participant?
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish a ruling produced by law from one produced by pressure, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
