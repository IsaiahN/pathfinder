## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical figures README states the limit: the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13's record section: "It can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands... It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."

From *The Library of Babel*: "You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question." And: "The problem you have determines what makes a book relevant to you or not."

So a **shape** is the narrowed form of an answer you can state before you can name it. It constrains; it does not fill. For this question the shape is the four-condition arrangement in `01` section 0. A particular constitution, a particular tenure rule, or a particular country is a name.

**Application:** "the rule of law is credible if X" is a shape only while X is stated in effect terms (anchor does not update, checker cannot reconstruct, etc.). "Country Y has the rule of law" is a name and is outside what the framework returns.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. "With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."
- Fig 6 :30: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms." Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: "You will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place, as there is nowhere else to search and nothing else that can describe the answer better." The paradox: "By the time you learn the EXACT search terms to find the CORRECT book, you won't need to read it anymore, because you have already composed the answer through the process of locating it."

For this question, the search began from the effect named in the question itself: "those who enforce the law are also subject to political pressure." That effect selects the figures about anchors, seats, correlation and drift. Frames were not chosen first and mapped afterwards.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays." The README: the framework "stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and shadow test.

*The Hard Problem is About Source* sharpens this: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." An instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source". It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved the case. "What the Spectrometer Example Reveals: The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."

**Application to INS-0051:** a ruling that looks lawful is a reading, not a source. It may be produced by law or by political pressure. Naming the source from the reading alone is the metamerism error. What is owed is an instrument that discriminates sources: per-case records, reasons, and independent frames that range against the same evidence. Fig 8 :50: "Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them".

## 4. Why a single lever cannot be the answer

The question bundles at least four conditions. Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." *The Library of Babel* gives the same instruction from the search side: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."

So "appoint better judges", "write clearer laws", or "publish approval ratings" are each single levers. Each clears only the region where one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing. The shape is the conjunction, and the conjunction is the honest answer.

## 5. The drift-stoppers as a world-field restatement of the figure shape

*Decoding Tacit Knowledge* records three safeguards that communities build against coherent drift:

1. "A ground that both are corrected by, separately." Frames range against the ground, they do not negotiate with it (Fig 2 :45).
2. "Separate experience." Fig 10 :80: "Frames whose independence is renewed by separate experience can be maintained."
3. "Claims in effect terms." Fig 6 :30: "Cast it in effect terms."

And it names the institution this question is about: "Courts of law rule on evidence, which neither side can move." That is a world-field restatement of Fig 2 :29, not an independent derivation of it. It is recorded because it is a clean effect description anyone can check.

The same essay gives the caution that governs the answer's mode: "Communities and institutions that only check against themselves drift together and don't notice." A legal system that certifies its own credibility is such a community.

## 6. Prestige is not action, and this audit is a simulation

From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*:

> "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly."
> "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."

A campaign that gathers prestigious endorsements for the rule of law is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask is to give a checkable claim a budget to be tried and let the ground settle it.

> "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post. It will often be better than the generic outputs that you will usually get otherwise too. However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."

So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation, checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of the claim and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on. This is stated on every substantive file in the folder.

## 7. Correction to an easy overreach

It would be tempting to say "the framework returns a shape, not a name, so it cannot say anything about institutions." That is an overreach and is recorded as such. The "shape, not a name" limit governs a **missing primitive**, one not held in the library. This question does not require a new primitive. The conditions in `01` are stated in effect terms using documents every legal system already holds (a ruling, evidence, an interest, a selection pool, a record). Naming concrete mechanisms (tenure, sortition, disclosure) is a **binding from the world frame** onto those conditions, provenanced as such by the world sources in `10_source_ledger.csv`. What is forbidden is presenting a binding as a derivation, or claiming the framework selects one constitution as "the answer". Both claims are refused here.
