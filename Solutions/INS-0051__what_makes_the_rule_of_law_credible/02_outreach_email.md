# Outreach draft: check the output before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until F1 to F5 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are stated cleanly and the refuter is visible. The offer collapses on contact if the stage-1 claim cannot survive checking.

---

**Subject:** One checkable claim about judicial credibility, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a legal system's verdicts are credible under political pressure only if four conditions hold together. (1) The thing that settles a case, the evidence and the law, does not change its answer when a powerful frame pushes on it. If it does, the system converges on whoever had the most influence. (2) The person applying it is not handed the politically wanted result and cannot reconstruct it from the case material. (3) Any stake in which way the case comes out is declared, and an interest in the reading rather than the outcome is disqualifying. (4) The pool of appliers was not hand-picked by a participant, and a per-case record lets an outside reader tell whether the anchor actually moved.
>
> This is a **shape**, not a constitution. It does not name tenure, sortition, a particular budget rule or a particular country as the answer. Those are bindings from the world frame and carry no weight until their provenance is shown.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that public approval corroborates the rule of law. Agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool. Approval builds a convention about what a court is, not a verdict on whether the anchor held. I am also not claiming that judges can have no interests, or that clean procedure makes a ruling correct.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a legal system whose rulings demonstrably track who has the most influence, yet still passes the framework's own anchor test;
> 2. credibility produced by agreement alone, with no non-updating anchor and no instrument that distinguishes law from pressure;
> 3. an enforcer with an interest in which reading the ground returns today who is still a sound checker;
> 4. a standing list of "good institutions" that survives a change of residual;
> 5. a pooled national trust score that replaces per-case measurement without losing a live failure.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the claim is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract used for this derivation): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in the Project Pathfinder workspace.
> - Canonical usage rule (figures README, branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - The architecture and the figures: https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]

## Two-stage offer, made explicit

1. **Stage 1:** send the four-condition shape and its refuter. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record their verdict **before** showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the three links. Their later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode. A method may cross up; a recording may not (Fig 4 :22).
