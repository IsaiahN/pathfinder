# The Tether's answer on climate, fire regimes and land management

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the checkable sub-claims, `gap` on the empirical central question. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

We want to know (a) how fire-regime quantities change under a changing climate and (b) how much a land-management seat can move them. The residual is the gap between prediction and observation of those quantities, per region, biome and metric. It is a **union compound**, not one hard question (Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*). Climate and land use overlap in the same cases, and the intersection has its own loop.

## 2. The shape

1. **R is per slot, never a global average.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* A global trend line is not a legal report of this residual. Region, biome and metric must be named.
2. **The fire regime is a field of contact, enumerated outward.** Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."* Fig 11 :46: *"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"*. Temperature, water, season and terrain are named in the figure itself. Contact cascades several steps out (Fig 11 :52), and an actor's valence is relative to the residual, not fixed (Fig 11 :49). The figure's habitat is **not** the ecological niche; that mapping is declined by the source and retracted in `05_what_shape_means.md` section 4.
3. **The climate/land-use union must be unbundled.** Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either (Fig 9 :57).
4. **Management leverage is asymmetric, bounded and permanent work.** Fig 11 :61: *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* Fig 11 :63: *"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."* The seat can add treatments, prescribed and cultural burning, and suppression capacity. It cannot subtract drought or fire weather. Effects are real inside footprints and over finite times, and decay as fuels rebuild.
5. **Management acts downstream of the condition it cannot change.** Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* A treatment-effect estimate without an upstream fire-weather measurement is a reading below the break, which is *"a reading of nothing"* (Fig 3 :23).
6. **The empirical numbers are owed by the ground, not derived from the figures.** No figure line predicts a biome-specific sign or a treatment effect size. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*. This is an honest `gap` on empirical content, not a silence and not a failure of the derivation.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not one global "fire regimes are intensifying" trend. Not a number for any biome.
- Not that the framework's habitat is the ecologists' niche. That mapping is declined.
- Not that land management can cancel climate-driven change. It can introduce, not exclude.
- Not a single lever for the union. The intersection needs its own loop.
- Not `grounded`. No fire measurement was taken; observation products and experiments were read as secondary sources only.
- Not a claim that agreement among reviews settles anything. Reviews sharing underlying studies are one evidence pool (Fig 2 :40).

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A measurement, not a further argument. Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."*

- **Per-slot ground:** fire counts and burned-area products (MODIS, VIIRS, GFED, FIRMS), read per region, biome and metric, with the observation uncertainty stated.
- **Management ground:** before/after/control/impact designs and post-fire severity mapping, with treatment age and fire-weather percentile recorded. Davis et al. 2024 supply a stand-level severity estimate; McKinney et al. 2022 show why the landscape-level estimate is not yet firm.
- **Union ground:** attribution analyses that treat climate and land use as overlapping conditions, not additive terms. Thapa et al. 2026 name this attribution as an ongoing uncertainty.
- **Standing falsifiers:** one global metric that predicts every slot; an action that excludes a habitat condition; treatment effect invariant to fire weather; additive and non-overlapping climate and land-use effects; or a figure line that directly predicts the empirical sign.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The shape stops where the instruments stop distinguishing. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*. The fire-science literature supplies real ground, but it does not supply a figure-derived number, and agreement among reviews is not a second witness. Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* The round-trip gap is enumerated in `11_audit.md`.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not predict a single global trend for fire regimes, and it does not say how much land management can cancel climate change. It says that the residual is indexed by region, biome and metric, so a global average hides the live signal; that the relevant field is enumerated from contact, climate, fuel, ignition, terrain and people, with cascades several steps out; that climate and land use form an overlapping union whose intersections need their own treatment; and that land management can introduce but cannot exclude, acts downstream of fire weather, and is permanent work rather than a solved state. The sign, size and effect numbers are owed by observation and experiment, not by the figures, and this derivation records that gap rather than filling it. What it offers is the shape any answer must have, four refuters written in advance, and the instruments that would close it.
