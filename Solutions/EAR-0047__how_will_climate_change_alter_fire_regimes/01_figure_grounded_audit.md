## 0. The residual in effect terms

The question bundles two asks. Option A: report one global direction for "fire regimes" and treat land management as a lever that offsets it. Option B: treat the residual as per-slot, enumerate the field of contact, unbundle the union of climate and land-use conditions, and report a shape plus a bounded, asymmetric management leverage. What each leads to: A hides live local signals in an average and overstates management; B keeps the residual visible per slot and states what would have to be measured before any number is believed.

**R** is the gap between a prediction of fire-regime behaviour and the observation of it, indexed by slot: region, biome and fire-regime metric (frequency, severity, intensity, seasonality, burned extent, reburn interval). `R = |Γ(b,a) - o'| per slot s`. The ground is the observation, not the model's agreement with itself.

- **Arity.** Multi. The fire regime is a bundle of metrics; the drivers are a bundle of conditions; management is a bundle of actions. It is not one number.
- **Symmetry.** Broken. Climate and fuel conditions are not negotiators, and management is not symmetric with them: introduction is available, exclusion is not (Fig 11 :61).
- **Scale.** Landscape to regional, with cascades several steps out (Fig 11 :52). A global average is the wrong scale for a per-slot signal.

## 3. Findings

### F1  -  R is indexed by slot, not aggregated (general, triangulated)

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**
Fig 1 :13: **"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"**

A single statement such as "fire regimes are intensifying" is not a legal report of R. The signal lives in particular biome-region-metric slots. World corroboration (low overlap with the framework): Thapa et al. 2026 write *"The direction and magnitude of fire regime changes vary considerably by biome type, region, and the interplay of land use and management factors."* Haas et al. 2024 list a highlight: *"Fire size, burnt area and fire intensity are influenced by different factors; current relationships between these aspects of wildfire could become decoupled in an altered climate."* Flannigan et al. 2009 report a general increase in area burned with *"a lot of spatial variability, with some areas of no change or even decreases in area burned and occurrence."*

**Verdict:** triangulated (Fig 1 plus three independent fire-science pools, which do not draw on the framework). **Refuter:** show that one global fire-regime index predicts every per-slot trend without residual loss, or that the per-slot variance is pure measurement noise. Falsification would downgrade F1 to a bookkeeping preference.

### F2  -  A fire regime is a field of contact, enumerated, not composed (general, triangulated)

Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**
Fig 11 :44: **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**
Fig 11 :46: **"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"**
Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
Fig 11 :52: **"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**

The enumeration is read off the world, not invented. Temperature, water, season and terrain are named conditions in the figure itself. Contact cascades: a change in drought or season reaches fuel, then fire behaviour, then vegetation and carbon, several steps out. Valence is relative: the same prescribed fire is a benefactor to a fuel-load residual and a malfactor to a smoke or biodiversity residual.

**Important strain, resolved by re-census.** The source article's own "Three mappings offered and declined" section declines *"The habitat as an ecological niche"*, saying *"The habitat here is defined by contact with a residual, and the residual moves so the same actors constitute a different habitat when the question changes."* So Fig 11 is **not** a licence to borrow the ecologists' habitat concept wholesale. What transfers is the structural rule (enumerate contact, cascade outward, valence relative to R), not the ecological term. Recorded in `05_what_shape_means.md` and in the retraction log below.

World corroboration: Haas et al. 2024 identify *"vegetation properties associated with fuel availability and continuity and climate factors associated with fuel drying as the most important influences on wildfire extent and severity"* and note *"Ignitions, whether anthropogenic or natural, are generally not limiting."* This is an enumeration result, not a composed one.

**Verdict:** triangulated (Fig 11 + independent empirical synthesis). **Refuter:** demonstrate that the relevant contact set is closed under composition from a small fixed list of variables, so enumeration adds nothing, or that ignition is limiting at landscape scale in a way the global synthesis misses.

### F3  -  Management leverage is asymmetric, bounded and permanent work (specified, triangulated)

Fig 11 :61: **"Introduction is available; exclusion is not."**
Fig 11 :63: **"Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."**
Fig 11 :40: **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

The seat can add fuel treatments, prescribed fire, suppression capacity and cultural burning. It cannot subtract drought, fire weather or the habitat. So the honest answer to "how much can land management influence them" is not a single percentage. It is: within the footprint and for a finite time, materially; against the upstream condition, not at all; and only as continuing work.

World corroboration, two independent pools:
- Davis et al. 2024 (meta-analysis): reducing surface and ladder fuels and tree density *"lowers subsequent wildfire severity by, on average, 62-72%"*, while *"Treatment efficacy is expected to decline over time as fuels rebuild"*, with treatments older than 10 years reducing severity by about 28 percent on average, underscoring *"the importance of repeated or 'maintenance' treatments"*.
- McKinney et al. 2022 (systematic review of empirical evidence): *"the state of knowledge based on empirical evidence is at its infancy"* and *"strong conclusions that would inform operational decisions, such as the size, shape, pattern, and type of treatments to deploy, are not possible based on current published information."*

**Verdict:** triangulated (Fig 11 + two independent empirical syntheses). Note the two world sources agree on the direction but differ on confidence: Davis is strong at stand level, McKinney is cautious at landscape level. That is a real scope difference, not a conflict; it is recorded as such. **Refuter:** show a management action that removes a habitat condition (eliminates a fire-weather exposure) rather than only adding an actor, or show a treatment effect that does not decay as fuels rebuild. Either would break the asymmetry/permanence claim.

### F4  -  Management acts downstream; it cannot rescue a broken upstream link (specified, triangulated)

Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**
Fig 3 :22: **"A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can."**
Fig 3 :23: **"It is a reading of nothing."**

The chain here is: observe climate and fuel state, then expect fire behaviour, then evaluate a treatment. If fire weather is the binding condition, spending on a downstream treatment is effort above the break. A treatment-effect estimate taken without the upstream measurement is a reading of nothing.

World corroboration: McKinney et al. 2022 report that at two spatial extents *"fire weather (defined by burn index or energy release) played a more important role in fire severity than the amount of the area treated"*; Jain et al. 2021 state *"Treatment effectiveness was often limited during periods of extreme fire weather, underscoring the need for treatment designs to incorporate the increasing occurrence of extreme burning conditions."*

**Verdict:** triangulated (Fig 3 + two independent reviews). **Refuter:** show a landscape where treatment effect is invariant to fire-weather percentile, or where the upstream measurement is unnecessary for a valid treatment-effect estimate.

### F5  -  The question is a union compound; unbundle before searching (general, triangulated)

Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**
Fig 9 :57: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either"**

Climate change and land use are not two independent piles of cases. Savanna fire trends, for example, are mediated by land use and suppression, so climate alone cannot predict them. The intersection is a categorically different object with its own loop.

World corroboration: Thapa et al. 2026 list as an area of ongoing uncertainty *"attribution of fire regime shifts between climate change versus land use and suppression legacies"*, and state that *"savanna fire frequency trends are mediated strongly by land use change and fire suppression, meaning climate projections alone cannot predict savanna fire trajectories."*

**Verdict:** triangulated (Fig 9 + independent review). **Refuter:** show that climate and land-use effects are additive and non-overlapping across all major biomes, so one lever clears both.

### F6  -  The ground settles; agreement does not (general, triangulated)

Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."**
Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**
Fig 2 :42: **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."**
Fig 2 :40: **"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**

The anchor here is the observation record and the experiment: fire counts, burned-area products, severity mapping, BACI designs. Agreement among reviews is not a second witness when they cite the same underlying studies. The cost of being wrong is high (fire management and carbon policy), so independence should be bought deliberately, by using observation products and experiments that do not draw on the model pool.

**Verdict:** triangulated (Fig 2 + Fig 10 + the IOP review's explicit use of independent MODIS and GFED5 data as a ground check). **Refuter:** show that the relevant quantities are constitutively about model agreement rather than about fire on the ground. This is the Fig 2 :36 anchor-legitimacy test.

### F7  -  The empirical central question is a `gap` for the figures, honestly stated (not a silence)

After a genuine re-census of all 13 figures and both tables, **no figure line predicts the sign or magnitude of climate-driven change in a specific biome's fire frequency, severity or season length, and no figure line yields the numeric effect size of a fuel treatment.** The figures govern the shape of the answer (per slot, enumerated, unbundled, asymmetric, ground-settled) and the order of inquiry. They do not contain a fire-behaviour model.

Fig 8 :32: **"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom."**
Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**
Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"**
Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**

Formula step 7 keeps the three remedies distinct. SEARCHED is not the issue: the figure set has been enumerated and no governing line was found. INWARD would mean extending the instrument (better fire datasets, more experiments), which is exactly what the world sources call for. OUTWARD means importing the empirical numbers from observation with a source gate and a shadow test, debited against independence. Importing a number from a review that shares the same underlying studies is not a second witness (Fig 2 :40).

**Verdict:** `gap` on empirical content, `general` on shape. **Refuter:** produce a figure line that directly predicts a biome-specific sign or a treatment effect size, with a verbatim citation. That would move F7 from gap to triangulated and would be welcome.

### F8  -  Do not let a tier filter hand you a verdict (general, triangulated)

Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**
Fig 1 :23: **"The ground enters as the only metric."**

The importance or tractability score used to schedule this question is a budget filter. It must not be quoted as evidence that the shape is true. Likewise, the number of journals that will accept a submission is a contact-cost estimate, not a witness.

**Verdict:** triangulated (Fig 9 + Fig 1). **Refuter:** a verdict that changes when a filter changes. If it does, the filter was doing witness work.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. One global fire-regime metric predicts every per-slot trend without residual loss (breaks F1 and Fig 1 :19).
2. Land management is shown to exclude a habitat condition, not merely introduce actors, or its effect does not decay with fuel regrowth (breaks F3 and Fig 11 :61, :63).
3. A treatment effect is shown to be invariant to the upstream fire-weather condition (breaks F4 and Fig 3 :19).
4. Climate and land-use effects are shown to be additive and non-overlapping across major biomes (breaks F5 and Fig 9 :55).
5. A figure line is produced that directly governs the empirical sign or magnitude (F7 moves from gap to governed, and this derivation is superseded).

No empirical number in this folder is asserted as settled. Only a ground reading (observation product, field experiment, or BACI design) can move a `specified` sub-claim to `grounded`. Agreement among the sources in `10_source_ledger.csv` that share underlying studies is one witness.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Report R per slot and per metric. Refuse the single global trend line (F1).
2. Enumerate the contact field outward: climate, fuel, ignition, topography, people, and the second-order cascades into vegetation, carbon and smoke (F2). Do not call the figure's habitat an ecological niche; the source explicitly declines that mapping.
3. State management leverage as bounded, asymmetric and permanent: material inside footprints and over finite times, ineffective against the upstream condition, needing maintenance (F3, F4).
4. Unbundle the climate and land-use union before attributing any trend (F5).
5. Import empirical numbers from observation with provenance, never from agreement among reviews that cite the same studies (F6, F7).
6. Send the refuter before the theory (Fig 10 :52, *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source"*).
