**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the strained atom mappings in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §6 are confirmed as retracted. The output must survive checking or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** Check a four-slot claim about category learning before you read the theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A child bets on where a category boundary falls. Its prediction is compared with what the local social ground returns. The gap is measured per slot, not averaged. For the question as asked there are at least four slots: personhood, kinship, ownership, responsibility. Each is itself a compound of overlapping conditions, so the question is a union of loops, not one mechanism. The figures' rule is that aggregating across slots is how the signal saying where to look disappears. So the first output is simply this: do not answer "how children learn categories"; answer it once per slot.
>
> **The shape claim.** Three things follow from the figure set. (1) Two children with identical architecture can land on different boundaries because their histories and their weighting between inherited prior and private experience diverge, and that weighting is learnable. (2) What the culture hands the child is a head start that makes the child's own search cheaper, not a finished boundary copied verbatim; the wall of what the child can reach moves only on contact with a differing frame. (3) Cultural specificity is carried mostly by the arrangement, the bonds between shared atoms, not by a different set of atoms. The same atoms under a different bond are a different substance. The framework returns that shape. It does not name which specific bond fills which slot in which culture; that is a ground question.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** not that any category is innate in a fixed sense, not that the weighting settles the nativism versus empiricism debate, not that the six-term substrate is a list of human conceptual primitives, and not that cross-cultural agreement settles anything. Agreement across frames that share an evidence pool is one witness talking, not corroboration.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim.** I wrote these down before sending this:
> 1. Two children with matched histories, matched weighting and matched habitat still converge on different boundaries on one slot.
> 2. A boundary is transmitted as a verbatim replay that yields the same category in a different habitat with no re-derivation, and the framework permits it.
> 3. Two cultures' category systems use disjoint atom sets with no shared substrate at any level.
> 4. A child's category objective is derivable entirely inside its own frame with no import from a differing closure, and the ground pays for it.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the shape. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula, with line numbers, from the local framework: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` and `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`.
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`.
> - Wider context actually read: Piaget on assimilation and accommodation; Tomasello on cultural learning, shared intentionality and norm enforcement; Vygotsky on scaffolding and internalization; the cognitive-categorization and folk-taxonomy literature on context and cultural variation. Titles and links are in `04_links_and_distribution.md`.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on one slot you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
