## The residual in effect terms

A child's library Γ predicts where a category boundary falls (who counts as kin, what counts as owned, who counts as a person, what counts as responsible). The child bets; the cultural ground returns an observation. R = |Γ(b,a) − o'| per slot s. The four named categories are four slots, not one mechanism. R is indexed by slot and must not be aggregated (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22). What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run: a mechanism that closes R on each slot, that explains why two children with the same architecture can land on different boundaries, and that says which part of the category is shared and which part is local.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the shape claim.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons, in the framework's own words:

1. **A room does not upgrade the label.** Seat Map :283: *"A room does not upgrade the label... Adding people raises the vocabulary and does not add an anchor."* A list of agreeable readers is a convention, not corroboration.
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** Seat Map :163: *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that would make their agreement worthless.
3. **Asking for trust before checking spends independence.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* The first contact carries the residual and its refuter, never the framework's interior.

Legally: unsolicited bulk email to scraped personal addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions. Public opt-in venues avoid that failure and the structural one.
