## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, how children acquire culturally specific categories such as personhood, kinship, ownership and responsibility. Option A: the boundaries are innate and culture only labels them. Option B: the boundaries are read off the world by a domain-general learner with no inherited structure. Option C: the figures' answer, a shape in which the child is a frame composing over a held substrate, the cultural ground supplies imports and binds the sought-for shape as an ordinary term, and specificity lives in a learnable weighting and in the bonds rather than in a different atom set. What each leads to: A and B each erase half the residual (A erases the local variation, B erases the inherited structure); C keeps four separate loops, states five refuters in advance, and stops at the instruments.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

Divergence is carried by history and a learnable weighting between inherited prior and private experience; culture sends head starts, not replays; caregivers bind the sought-for shape so the child can check against it; cultural specificity lives in the bonds over shared atoms, so the same atoms under a different bond give a different substance.
