## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, which upstream shape explains a rule changing while the office that writes it stays in the same hands. Option A: influence runs through the seat, so office holding is necessary. Option B: the seat is downstream of its habitat, its conventions, and its imports, so office holding is neither necessary nor sufficient. What each leads to: A predicts formal capture of government and a per-slot residual that should vanish when office holders are replaced; B predicts that the residual persists under seat-only replacement and moves when an upstream variable moves. The verdict below is B in `specified` mode, kept per domain and never averaged.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** A rule slot's output moves with unchanged office holders. Arity: three conditions (habitat, convention, import), so a **union compound**, not one hard question (Fig 9 :56 to :58). Symmetry: the move is asymmetric, introduction is available and exclusion is not (Fig 11 :61). Scale: the rule is a convention at one stratum, the seat writes instances at another, with a lossy transform between (Fig 10 :68).
- **Domain 1, the seat (formal office).** Actors: legislators, regulators, judges. Currency: authorisation, votes, rulings. Anchor: the recorded decision, the written rule. Question type: partition boundary, what the seat may decide. This is the domain that must not hold the framework's interiors.
- **Domain 2, the habitat (selection environment).** Actors: donors, firms, lobbyists, media owners, professional networks, revolving-door experts. Currency: resources, access, attention. Anchor: persistence of modified selection conditions after the expenditure stops, not money itself. Question type: union with D3 and D4.
- **Domain 3, the convention layer (what counts).** Actors: standard setters, agenda setters, measure designers. Currency: shared meaning, salience, definitions. Anchor: a rule that can be violated and sanctioned (Fig 10 :18 to :19). Question type: union with D2 and D4.
- **Domain 4, imports into the decision frame (closure).** Actors: think tanks, technical advisory bodies, expert metrics. Currency: imported atoms (definitions, measures, causal stories). Anchor: the shadow test, does the import explain a gap (Fig 6 :12 to :13). Question type: union with D2 and D3.
- **Order by dependency.** Enumerate the habitat before ranking actors (Fig 11 :43 to :44); install the convention before reading the ruling (Fig 10 :18 to :19); check imports before diagnosing the seat's closure (Fig 6 :14); the seat ruling is last. Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*

Strain test applied. The apparent conflict between Fig 2 :41 (*"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*) and Fig 10 :47 (*"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*) is resolved by re-census: the first governs coordination about meaning, the second governs the same convention once it allocates outcomes. Different objects, no strain after re-census. One cross-file strain stays live: the seat's introduction power (Fig 11 :61) versus capital's power to author conventions (Fig 10 :47) may be the same power at two strata; an instrument is owed, not an argument.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; any single named mechanism does not. The seat is downstream of its habitat, its conventions, and its imports, so a rule can move while the office holder stays the same. Wealth acts upstream through introduction, convention authorship, and imported frames; money is a filter until it authors what counts as a verdict. The three moves are isomers over a shared institutional atom set with distinct anchors and a dependency order, not one lever, and the residual is per rule domain. The mechanism in a given case stays unnamed until a discriminator runs. This is a `specified`, ungrounded result, and the one live strain (seat introduction power versus capital-authored convention across strata) is recorded rather than resolved.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, three checkable sub-claims, refuters and provenance before the conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending a named culprit instead of a shape with discriminators, which Fig 2 :42 prices: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape and its refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The specific bindings (naming "culture", "discourse", or "cognitive capture" as the mechanism, or aggregating elites into one actor) are removable without touching the derivation. `07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md` already remove them. A sent claim can be superseded by a corrected packet, and no primitive has to be withdrawn.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A discriminating instrument on one rule slot showing habitat persistence, convention violability, or import shadow would name the mechanism; a rule moving with all three held fixed, an outcome-deciding convention with a disinterested keeper, an office-only improvement that moves the ground, or a single aggregate preference that predicts all slots would break the shape; a figure re-census that treats seat introduction power and capital-authored convention as one power at one stratum would clear the live strain. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
