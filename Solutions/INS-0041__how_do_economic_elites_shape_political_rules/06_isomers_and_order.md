## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Isomers, distinct kinds, or three names for one thing?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :117 is about arrangement over a shared atom set. The three upstream mechanisms do draw on a shared institutional atom set: actors, meanings, and imported descriptions all appear in each. Over that full set they are **isomers**: same atoms, different arrangement, different behaviour. A habitat change and a convention change can produce the same rule reading and still be different substances.
- But isomers are still one kind at the level above. **They are not interchangeable at this level**, because each has a different anchor: habitat has persistence of modified selection conditions (Fig 11 :73), convention has violability (Fig 10 :19), import has the shadow test (Fig 6 :12 to :13). Fig 13 :35: *"Space, time and energy are not three readings of one quantity"*; the same independence rule applies here. Three mechanisms that shared one anchor would be fewer mechanisms wearing three names.
- Verdict: **isomers at the arrangement level, distinct at the anchor level.** Triangulated across Fig 12 :117 and Fig 13 :35 to :39. Single-source for the institutional mapping itself.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two of the three mechanisms are found to share both an anchor and a discriminating instrument, the distinction collapses and they must be merged in the next re-census.

### Q2 - Dependency order

- Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."** Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."**
- Fig 11 :43 to :44: enumerate the habitat first, *"then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."* You cannot rank actors before enumeration, because *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual"* (Fig 11 :49).
- Fig 10 :18 to :19: install what can be violated before reading the ruling. *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one → install what can be violated."* A convention that has not been installed as a violable rule cannot yet be blamed for an outcome.
- Fig 6 :14: import is *"the only operator that moves the wall"*, so check the decision frame's closure before diagnosing what the seat could consider.
- Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."** Each of the three has an instrument named in section 3 of `01`: persistence, violability, shadow test. The seat link has the recorded decision. No link is unexamined, so no reading is taken below a break.
- Order: **habitat → convention → import → seat ruling.** This is a dependency order, not a claim that every case must run all four.

### Q3 - Union or partition?

- Fig 9 :54: **"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
- Fig 9 :56 to :57: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."*
- Fig 9 :58: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**
- The three upstream variables overlap. A revolving-door expert is both a habitat actor and an import into the decision frame. A metric funded by an industry association is both a convention and an import. Treating the question as a partition would let one lever appear to work while the intersections remain. Verdict: **union**. This is also why the outreach packet asks for one rule slot at a time.

### Q4 - Catalyst and functional group

- Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**
- Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- A revolving door is a catalyst: the same industry actor is regenerated across successive regulatory cycles, and a records system that counts only appointments will miss the regeneration. "Capture" is a functional group: the presence of an outcome-deciding convention fixes the kind, and the qualifier (tax, licensing, media ownership) says which member. This is useful because it explains why "capture" can be a real structural description without being a single mechanism: it names the group, not the member.
- Fig 12 :120: *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* A captured rule that funds the next round of habitat introduction is a positive cycle; a disclosure rule that makes the next capture attempt visible is a negative one. Whether a given arrangement closes is an empirical question, not a figure verdict.

### Q5 - Is the seat an atom at its own level?

- Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."**
- A legislature or regulator is a settled institution: it has been tested against the ground over time. It therefore serves as an atom at the next level, which is why "the seat" can be treated as a single operand in this derivation. The individual office holder is an instance, not a generator. Fig 10 :68: *"Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between. That is why there is no top rung and no regress: a generator crosses, an instance does not."*
- Consequence: a claim about "the persons in the seat" is a claim about instances; a claim about "the seat" is a claim about the settled institution. Conflating them is a level error. Fig 12 :104 makes the distinction checkable.

## CONCLUSION

The three upstream mechanisms are **isomers over a shared institutional atom set** and **distinct at the anchor level**. They are not three names for one lever, and they are not three unrelated causes. They run in a dependency order (habitat → convention → import → seat ruling), they form a union, and two of their recurring structures are a catalyst (the revolving door) and a functional group (capture). The seat is a settled atom at its own level; its occupants are instances.

This rescues what is defensible in ordinary elite-influence talk without accepting any single named theory. It requires no new primitive, no aggregate influence score, and no claim of `grounded` status. What remains open is which isomer fires in a given rule slot; that needs a discriminator, not an argument (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 4).
