# Expert verification packet - the Tether on elite influence over political rules

**What this is:** a claim in a form a scholar of political economy or institutions can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` - not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A political seat is downstream of three things it did not author: a habitat of actors and conditions, a layer of conventions that decide what counts, and imports into the decision frame. A rule slot can therefore move while the office holder stays the same. Wealth acts upstream, by adding actors and conditions to the habitat, by turning coordinating conventions into outcome-deciding rules, and by importing expert frames that change what the seat can consider. These three moves are isomers over a shared institutional atom set, have distinct anchors and discriminators, and run in a dependency order (habitat → convention → import → ruling). Money is a filter for what gets tried until it is promoted into a verdict about what counts as evidence. The framework does not name which isomer fired in a given case; it supplies the shape, the discriminators, and falsifiers written in advance.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 11 and 10

- **Statement:** the office that writes a rule is downstream of its habitat and of the conventions it applies. Direct control of the office is not necessary for a rule to change.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :61 - *"The seat’s power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."* Also Fig 10 :47 - *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*, and Fig 11 :40 - *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*
- **Refuter:** exhibit a rule that changes with the seat's occupants, the habitat, the conventions, and the imports held fixed across the interval, with the ground measured; or show an office-only improvement that moves the ground and persists. Either fires the falsifier and the downstream claim is withdrawn.

### C2 - institutional-facing; checkable by a scholar against one rule domain

- **Statement:** the three upstream moves are distinct isomers with distinct discriminators, not one lever. Over the shared atom set they are Fig 12 :117, *"same atoms, different bond, different substance"*. The discriminators are habitat persistence, convention violability, and import shadow test (Fig 6 :12 to :13, *"source gate: provenance"*, *"shadow test: explains a gap"*).
- **What to check:** choose one rule (a licensing rule, a tax rule, a disclosure rule). Can you name an instrument that separates a habitat change from a convention change from an import? If all three produce the same observable reading, the metamerism objection from *The Hard Problem is About Source* stands and the three must be merged.
- **Refuter:** two of the three are found to share both an anchor and a discriminating instrument (the distinction collapses, re-census owed), or one aggregate elite preference predicts rule changes across all slots without slot-specific information (breaks the per-slot requirement, Fig 1 :19).

### C3 - framework-internal derivation with a cross-field check

- **Statement:** money is a budget filter until it authors the verdict, at which point the rule that allocates outcomes has stopped being a convention.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :60 - *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* Fig 1 :27 - *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."* Cross-field check from an unrelated literature: Goodhart's law, *"When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure"* (Strathern 1997; Goodhart 1975).
- **Refuter:** a case where expenditure demonstrably changed only what was attempted, never what counted as evidence or what was admissible, across the whole rule domain; or a rule that systematically allocates outcomes while remaining a checkable, violable convention with a keeper who has no interest in its reading (Fig 10 :59).

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that elites are one actor, or that wealth buys office.
- Not that every rule is captured, or that public regulation is always captured. Fig 12 :120 leaves the sign of the loop open.
- Not a conspiracy account. The claim is about upstream conditions and conventions, not hidden intent (Fig 2 :47).
- Not a normative claim that any of this is desirable or undesirable.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by a measurement.
- Not that "culture", "discourse", "ideas", or "cognitive capture" is the mechanism. These are bindings from other frames, provenanced in `10_source_ledger.csv`, carrying zero verdict weight until a shadow test is run.
- Not that the framework is the only account of elite influence. A search that finds nothing is not a proof of absence (Fig 8 :39).

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminating instrument on one rule slot, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer resolved it. Here:

1. **Habitat test:** do new actors or conditions enter the rule's environment before the change, and does the change persist after the spending stops?
2. **Convention test:** was the rule a coordination device that became outcome-deciding, with a keeper who has an interest in its reading?
3. **Import test:** did the decision frame's consider-able options change on contact with a differing closure, with a provenanced source and a shadow test?
4. **Standing falsifiers:** rule moves with all three held fixed; an outcome-deciding convention with a disinterested keeper; an office-only improvement that moves the ground; a single aggregate preference that predicts all slots.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 11 :61, Fig 10 :47, Fig 9 :60 and Fig 12 :117 - four short passages.
2. Check C1 against one rule domain you know: did the office holder actually have to change for the rule to move?
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish habitat persistence from convention violability from import shadow on that rule - or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement, and a later agreement is never pooled as corroboration if we have shared this packet.
