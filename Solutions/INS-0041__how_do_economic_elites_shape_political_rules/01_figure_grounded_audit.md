## 0. The residual in effect terms

R is not "elite power" as one magnitude. R is indexed per rule-domain slot: tax incidence, campaign finance disclosure, regulatory entry, media ownership, professional licensing, and so on. Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

The effect to explain: a slot's rule output changes while the occupants of the office that writes the rule do not change. In symbols, Γ(b,a) returns the rule the seat expects, o′ is the rule the world returns, and the puzzle is which upstream variable moved so that R registered. Option A puts that variable inside the seat (the elite holds the office). Option B puts it upstream of the seat (the habitat, the convention layer, or an import into the decision frame). The shape owed is the set of upstream conditions, not the name of a villain.

**Direction, bound before the run (Formula step "Direction"):** an answer would look like (i) a description of at least one upstream variable that can move a rule slot with the seat fixed, (ii) a discrimination instrument that tells a habitat change from a convention change from an import change, and (iii) a refuter that fires if the rule moves with all three held fixed. Written down here, it is now an ordinary term that can accrue a residual like anything else.

**Question type.** Fig 9 :54: *"when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* The Library of Babel makes the same point in prose: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."* This question is a **union compound**, not one hard lever. Campaign finance, media ownership, professional networks, and regulatory expertise are overlapping conditions, not four spellings of one.

## 2. Domain census (before choosing any figure line)

**R by arity, symmetry, scale.** Arity: at least three conditions (habitat inputs, convention layer, imported frames), so a union compound. Symmetry: the move is asymmetric, introduction is available and exclusion is not (Fig 11 :61). Scale: the rule is a convention at one stratum; the seat writes instances at another, with a lossy transform between (Fig 10 :68, Fig 4 :27).

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | partition or union | order |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1, the seat (formal office)** | legislators, regulators, judges, office holders | authorisation, votes, rulings | the recorded decision, the written rule | partition boundary: what the seat may decide | downstream; last in dependency order |
| **D2, the habitat (selection environment)** | donors, firms, lobbyists, media owners, professional networks, revolving-door experts | resources, access, attention, persistence | what survives contact and changes what gets selected, not the habitat itself | union with D3 and D4 (overlapping conditions) | first: enumerate before ranking actors |
| **D3, the convention layer (what counts)** | standard setters, agenda setters, framers, measure designers | shared meaning, salience, definitions, what is statable | none until installed; Fig 10 :18 to :19 requires installing what can be violated | union with D2 and D4 | second: a convention must be installed before its rulings are diagnostic |
| **D4, imports into the decision frame (closure)** | think tanks, technical advisory bodies, expert metrics, models | imported atoms (definitions, measures, causal stories) | the shadow test: does the import explain a gap? Fig 6 :12 to :13 | union with D2 and D3 | third: the frame's wall moves on contact with a differing closure |

**No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.** D2's anchor is not "money"; money is a currency. D2's anchor is the persistence of modified selection conditions, which is checkable (a rule environment that stayed changed after the spending stopped). D3's anchor is a convention that can be violated, which is checkable (a rule someone can break and be sanctioned for breaking). D4's anchor is the shadow test, which is checkable (an imported measure that predicts a gap the frame could not previously state). D1's anchor is the recorded decision. All four have anchors, so all four are domains.

**Dependency order (Fig 3 :20): *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*** Enumerate the habitat (D2) before ranking which actor matters, because *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual"* (Fig 11 :49). Install the convention (D3) before reading the ruling, because a reading below the break is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22). Check imports (D4) before diagnosing the seat's closure, because only import moves the wall (Fig 6 :14). D1 is last. Effort aimed at D1 alone cannot rescue a break in D2 to D4.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - A rule that allocates outcomes has stopped being a convention (decisive)

Fig 10 :47: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**

Fig 10 :18 to :19: **"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"** and **"→ install what can be violated"**

Fig 2 :43: **"Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."**

A rule that merely coordinates meaning is a convention. The moment it systematically allocates who gets what, it is no longer a convention in the harmless sense; it is an outcome-deciding device wearing convention's clothes. This is why the elite need not hold the seat. They need the convention to be authored or captured upstream, and the seat then applies it. The mechanism is not command, it is pre-authorisation of what counts.

**Refuter:** exhibit a rule that systematically allocates outcomes while remaining a checkable, violable convention with no interest in its reading on the part of any author or keeper. Fig 10 :59: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* If a genuine disinterested keeper exists and the allocation persists, F1 fails.

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 10 :47 and Fig 2 :43 (two figures, low overlap in wording, same source set). Confirmed from an unrelated field by Goodhart's law, *"When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure"*, which is world corroboration only.

### F2 - Introduction is available, exclusion is not

Fig 11 :61 to :62: **"The seat’s power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."**

Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."** Fig 11 :44: **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**

Fig 11 :49: **"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**

Resources buy introduction into the habitat: candidates, lobbyists, think tanks, media outlets, professional networks, experts on secondment. They cannot subtract the public from the habitat, because *"contact is a fact about the world and does not consult you"* (Fig 11 :61 to :62). So the shape is not "elites replace the public"; it is "elites add actors and conditions whose cascade reaches the rule slot". Valence is relative: a media outlet is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so the phrase media ownership names a relation to a slot, not a kind.

**Refuter:** a rule changes with the habitat enumerated and demonstrably unchanged over the relevant interval (no new actor, no changed condition, no altered relation). If the residual still moves, F2 is not the mechanism.

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 11 :61 and Fig 11 :43 plus the habitat enumeration rule. Corroborated in an unrelated field by niche construction: organisms *"modify their own and/or each other’s niche"*, and an evolutionary niche is *"the sum of all natural selection pressures to which the population is exposed"*. World source, not a witness for the verdict.

### F3 - Improving the seat in isolation changes nothing if contact does not change

Fig 11 :39: **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."**

Fig 11 :40: **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

This explains why formal ethics rules, disclosure statutes, and vetting procedures often fail without a change in contact. Fig 3 :19: *"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."* The seat is a link, not the chain. Replacing the office holder while leaving the habitat, convention, and import channels fixed is an agent-only improvement, and it changes nothing the ground can register.

**Refuter:** a rule changes after a seat-only intervention with habitat, convention, and imports held fixed, and the change persists after the intervention ends. That would refute the capability claim (the essay's own stated refuter: an agent-only improvement that moved the ground).

**Mode:** `specified`, single-source for the figure line (Fig 11 :39 to :40), corroborated by regulatory capture's distinction between material and cognitive capture, where changing formal rules does not remove the expert pool drawn from the regulated industry.

### F4 - Imports move the wall of what the decision frame can consider

Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**

Fig 6 :14: **"the only operator that moves the wall"** (import). Fig 6 :25: **"An import adds no atom to the world."**

Fig 8 :51 to :52: **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."**

Think-tank proposals, technical metrics, and expert definitions are imports into the legislative frame. They add no atom to the world; they give the frame access. Regulatory capture's "cognitive capture" is the same move described from another field: the regulator adopts the industry's representation because the expert pool comes from the industry. Crucially, an import that is not provenanced looks identical to an independently derived conclusion, so the record, not the content, decides whether it is an import.

**Refuter:** show the decision frame's closure unchanged (the same set of consider able options and measures before and after) while the rule output moves. If closure is unchanged and the rule still moves, the import mechanism did not fire.

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 6 :23 and Fig 8 :51 to :52 (closure mechanism plus provenance test). World corroboration from regulatory capture and agenda-setting, both opened and recorded in the ledger.

### F5 - Money is a filter until it authors the verdict

Fig 9 :60: **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

Fig 1 :27: **"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."**

From the RSI essay, the same rule in social terms: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* Wealth is legitimately a budget filter: it decides which proposals get tried, which candidates get a platform, which studies get funded. It becomes the captured mechanism when it is promoted to a witness, that is, when it decides what counts as evidence, what is measurable, or which frame is credible. That promotion is exactly F1's convention that decides an outcome.

**Refuter:** a case where expenditure demonstrably changed only what was attempted, never what counted as a verdict or what was admissible as evidence, across the whole rule domain. If such cases dominate, F5 does not explain the residual.

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 9 :60 and Fig 1 :27 (low overlap: budget/verdict split versus frame-internal scoring), with the prestige/action rule from the RSI essay as a secondary corroborator.

### F6 - Venue selection is an upstream lever

Fig 7 :35: **"A room’s agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**

Fig 7 :36: **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."**

Fig 7 :41: **"And the pool it is drawn from must be unauthored."**

Agenda-setting research supplies the cross-field reading: the press *"may not be successful much of the time in telling people what to think, but it is stunningly successful in telling its readers what to think about"*, and *"News sources can also provide definitions of issues, thus determining the terms of future discussion and framing problems in particular ways"*. Which committee hears the bill, which standard body writes the metric, and which venue is treated as authoritative are room selections. They are upstream of the vote.

**Refuter:** the same set of venues, with the same membership and the same salience distribution, produces different rules under otherwise fixed conditions. That would remove room selection as the lever.

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 7 :35 and :36 (selection between rooms) and the agenda-setting source (communication studies, low overlap).

### F7 - Per-slot residual, not one elite with one preference

Fig 1 :19 and Fig 1 :22 as cited in section 0. Fig 13 :115: **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."**

"Economic elites" is a convenience label, not an actor with a single preference function. The residual is registered per rule slot, and different fractions of capital want different rules (trade exposure, regulation, tax). Treating elites as one winning coalition aggregates away the signal, the exact error Fig 1 :22 names. What survives is per-slot: which fraction, in which rule domain, via which upstream channel.

**Refuter:** a single aggregate preference of the top wealth fraction predicts rule changes across all slots with no slot-specific information. If it does, the per-slot requirement is unnecessary and F7 fails.

**Mode:** `general` on the structural requirement, `specified` on the claim that aggregate elite preference loses information. Triangulated across Fig 1 :19 to :22 and Fig 13 :115.

### F8 - Agreement among linked frames is a convention, not corroboration

Fig 2 :40: **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**

Fig 2 :41: **"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"**

Fig 2 :42: **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."**

If three elite-linked think tanks and a trade association all endorse the same rule, that is one evidence pool speaking four times. It is efficient for producing a convention (shared meaning is the product), and worthless as a verdict about what the rule does. The cost of being wrong about a rule that allocates resources is high, so independence should be bought in proportion.

**Refuter:** two genuinely low-overlap frames (different evidence, different closures, no shared authors or funders) converge on the same rule prediction and survive a source-gate check. That would be corroboration at maximum independence (Fig 8 :50) and would defeat the "one pool" reading for that case.

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 2 :40 to :42 and Fig 8 :50.

### F9 - The keeper of the ground is disqualified by a stake in its reading

Fig 10 :93: **"A ground with a reputation to protect is a ground someone benefits from, and its keeper is thereby disqualified."**

Fig 10 :61: **"And standing is a stake that survives the outcome it was earned on."**

Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**

When a regulator's authority, budget, or post-service employment depends on the industry it measures, the ground has become a frame. Fig 10 :59: *"an interest in the outcome is unavoidable, an interest in the reading is disqualifying."* The remedy is not to demand disinterest (no such seat exists) but to declare the stake and arrange refusal. Fig 10 :65: *"Provenance shows a ruling was reached legitimately. It never shows it was right: the ground does that, and a wrong ruling stays wrong however clean its record."*

**Refuter:** a rule-setting body whose keeper has no stake in which value the ground returns, verified from public records, and whose decisions still track the elite-linked frame. That would show the stake is not necessary for this mechanism.

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 10 :93 and :59 to :61 (three low-overlap passages within the same figure) and Fig 2 :29.

### F10 - Same rules, different bonds, different substance (supporting)

Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use."**

Two polities can write identical formal rules and produce different distributions because the informal bonds differ: who may speak, which venue is authoritative, which metric counts. Under Fig 12 they are isomers of the same institutional atom set. A captured regulator is a catalyst, *"a part that survives its own use"*: the same industry actor is regenerated across successive regulatory cycles. This is developed in `06_isomers_and_order.md` with its own falsifier.

**Refuter:** show two cases with identical formal rules and identical informal bonds but different rule outcomes, with the ground measured. That breaks the arrangement claim.

**Mode:** `specified`, single-source for the figure definition (Fig 12), with the isomer test itself unmeasured.

## 3b. Strain test and re-census

**Apparent strain:** Fig 2 :41 says agreement about a shared meaning is *"the product, not a corruption of it"*, while Fig 10 :47 says *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"*. Read as one claim they contradict: is a convention a legitimate product or a captured device?

**Re-census (not a correction):** Fig 2 :41 governs coordination about meaning, where agreement produces the shared language that makes a convention work. Fig 10 :47 governs the same convention once it decides an outcome, where agreement produces allocation rather than coordination. Different objects at different points in the loop, exactly the move the figures README prescribes: *"a reading that makes one contradict another has misidentified its domain, not found a flaw."* No strain remains after re-census. This re-census also narrows outreach: asking elites to agree on a shared description is fine (convention), asking them to ratify a rule is not (verdict).

**Second strain, kept live:** Fig 11 :61 gives the seat asymmetric power of introduction, but F1 and F5 give capital the power to author conventions. Whether these are the same power at two strata or two different powers needs an instrument, not an argument. Recorded as a strain in `11_audit.md`, not hidden.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter

Required before any outreach. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A rule slot changes with the seat's occupants, the enumerated habitat, the convention layer, and the imports into the decision frame all held fixed across the interval, with the ground measured.
2. A rule that systematically allocates outcomes is shown to remain a checkable, violable convention with a keeper who has no interest in its reading (breaks F1).
3. An agent-only or seat-only improvement moves the ground and the change persists (breaks F3 and Fig 11 :40).
4. A single aggregate elite preference predicts rule changes across all slots without slot-specific information (breaks F7).
5. Two genuinely low-overlap frames converge and pass a source-gate check for a given rule prediction (this does not refute the shape, but it removes the "one evidence pool" diagnosis for that case and must be logged as a correction).

If any of 1 to 4 fires, the upstream shape is wrong and the claim is withdrawn. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

### Seven-question recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is this one question?** No. Union compound (Fig 9 :54), so unbundle into habitat, convention, import, and seat.
2. **Does the seat need to change hands?** No. The seat is downstream of the convention (F1) and of the habitat (F2).
3. **Which upstream variable is necessary?** At least one of three; the figure set does not select among them. This is the shape, not a name.
4. **What is money?** A filter for the budget until it authors what counts as a verdict (Fig 9 :60, F5).
5. **Is the elite one actor?** No. Residual per slot (Fig 1 :19), so "elites" is a label for a set of positions, not a preference function.
6. **What moves the decision frame's wall?** Import from a differing closure (Fig 6 :23), provenanced as such (Fig 8 :51).
7. **Settlement?** None. Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* Status `candidate`.

### Updated verdict

The shape survives: economic elites shape rules by acting upstream of the seat, through introduction into a habitat, authoring or capturing conventions, and importing frames that move what the decision system can consider. The seat is downstream and need not change hands. What does not survive is any single named mechanism, any aggregate elite preference, or any claim of `grounded` status. Strains after re-census: one live (seat power versus capital-authored convention across strata), named in `11_audit.md`, none hidden.
