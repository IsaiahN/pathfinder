# Outreach draft - validate the output first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until F1, F2, and F5 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are stated cleanly and the mode is on every sentence. The output must survive the check or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** Check one downstream shape before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question: how can a rule change while the office that writes it stays in the same hands? I have one output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** in the framework's terms, a political seat is downstream of three things it did not author. First, a habitat of actors and conditions that can be added to but not subtracted from (Fig 11 :61, *"Introduction is available; exclusion is not"*). Second, a layer of conventions that decide what counts and what is statable, where *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"* (Fig 10 :47). Third, imports into the decision frame, since *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs"* (Fig 6 :23). The shape claim is that a rule slot can move with the seat fixed if one of those three upstream variables moves. The framework does not say which one dominates in your case. That is the part I am asking you to check.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that economic elites are one actor with one preference, that wealth buys office directly, that every rule is captured, or that this is a conspiracy. The residual is per rule domain, not one aggregate score (Fig 1 :19). Money is a filter for what gets tried, not a witness for what is true, until it is promoted into a convention that decides an outcome (Fig 9 :60). I am also not claiming this derivation is settled by data. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency, and it cannot settle the question it reports on.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. A rule changes with the occupants of the seat, the enumerated habitat, the convention layer, and the imports into the decision frame all held fixed, with the ground measured.
> 2. A rule that systematically allocates outcomes is shown to remain a checkable, violable convention with a keeper who has no interest in its reading.
> 3. An office-only or agent-only improvement moves the ground and the change persists.
> 4. One aggregate elite preference predicts rule changes across all domains with no domain-specific information.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Canonical figures text with line numbers (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Figures README, canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step formula (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the local files. Run it on a rule domain you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success, and later agreement is never pooled as corroboration if we have shared this email thread.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
