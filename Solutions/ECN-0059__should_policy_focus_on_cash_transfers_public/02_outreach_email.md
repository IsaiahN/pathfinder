**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Subject:** Check one claim about anti-poverty policy before you decide whether the frame behind it is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a figure-grounded derivation about whether policy should focus on cash transfers, public services, wage-setting institutions, or asset ownership to reduce poverty. I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output fails, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on the per-domain predictions, not `grounded`):**
>
> 1. The four options are not four points on one lever. They are a union of overlapping conditions. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds. The figure says: "when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again" (Fig 9 :54).
> 2. The signal is per household, not per national average. "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated" (Fig 1 :19). Aggregating across households is how the live signal disappears.
> 3. The anchor has to be the household's realized material condition, not the delivered input. "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks" (Fig 2 :29). Dollars delivered, service units, wage rates and account balances are produced by the instrument frames, and "A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces" (Fig 1 :27).
> 4. An agent-only improvement is insufficient. "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation" (Fig 11 :39). This is not a claim that cash transfers fail. It is a claim that a transfer which changes holdings without changing the conditions that generate the shortfall changes contact only partly.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the figures pick cash, services, wage floors or assets as the winner. They return "a kind of thing, never the answer itself" (Fig 9 :32). The empirical ranking is an open slot that only a measured outcome can close. I am also not claiming that cost-effectiveness settles the question, because "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict" (Fig 9 :60).
>
> **What would refute the shape claim.** I wrote these down before this run:
> 1. poverty conditions are shown to be a disjoint partition, so one lever clears everything without an intersection compound;
> 2. a delivered-input metric is shown to track the material outcome identically in every household slot, with no live residual left;
> 3. an agent-only change is shown to change contact with the ground with no habitat change;
> 4. a figure re-census admits a line that names one of the four instruments as the unique focus.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the shape. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula (canonical text extract, local path in this solution folder): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - World corroboration, read 2026-11-23: J-PAL cash transfers evidence page; Low Pay Commission Report 2025; IZA World of Labour on minimum wages; ASPE report on asset poverty; PNAS study on bandwidth and consumption (full links in `04_links_and_distribution.md`).
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, because a frame cannot check the direction of its own question. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

> — [name]

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Output first, theory second | Fig 10 :52 — "Bring a characterised residual, never the source." Fig 2 :45 — "Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it." |
| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35 — "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." |
| Pre-registered refuter | A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. |
| Mode on every claim | `THE_FORMULA.md` mode rule: "Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which." |
| Instrument ranking withheld | Fig 9 :32 — "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." |
| Cost-effectiveness withheld as verdict | Fig 9 :60 — filters for the budget, witnesses for the verdict. |
| No request for trust or prestige | *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: prestige can fund an action but "cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it." |
| Offer the formula as a method | Fig 4 :22 — "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down." |
| Ask users to state their own ground and refuter | Formula step "Direction": a frame cannot check what it is looking for. |

## Stage order (three stages, opt-in throughout)

1. **Stage 1:** residual, shape claim, refuter. Ask what the recipient would count as a failure. Record the verdict before showing any theory.
2. **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if curious, offer the figure text, canonical README and world sources. Later agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, with the instruction that each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode.

Nothing is sent by this agent. You host and send.
