## Restatement as an effect

The question asks which single instrument a policy should focus on. In effect terms: four candidate levers each move some material conditions of poor households and leave others unresolved. The residual is the gap between a household's actual material condition and what any one lever delivers. An answer would look like a rule that, for a given household and a given binding condition, says which instrument or combination changes the household's contact with the ground, and what would falsify that choice.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the answer.
- No bulk-send plan.

Three reasons in the figures' own words:

1. **A room does not upgrade the label.** Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Agreement among selected responders is a weighted average of influence, not an anchor.
2. **Correlation disqualifies a verdict.** Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool." Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that voids their agreement.
3. **Asking for trust before checking spends independence.** Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."

## Central verdict in one line

The figures return a shape, not a name: the four options are a union of overlapping conditions mis-framed as a partition, the residual is per household slot, the anchor is the material outcome rather than the delivered input, and which instrument wins is a `gap` that only the ground (a measured outcome) can close. "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" (Fig 13 :134).
