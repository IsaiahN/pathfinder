# The Tether's answer: cash, services, wages, or assets?

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the per-domain predictions. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks which single instrument policy should focus on to reduce poverty. In effect terms, each instrument moves some material conditions of a poor household and leaves others unresolved. A household can be short of cash, services, wages and assets at once, and the same instrument moves different households differently. Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." This is a **union compound**, with its own loop and its own ground, not one hard question with four candidate answers.

## 2. The shape

1. **Do not focus on one lever, because the conditions overlap.** Fig 9 :56: "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds." Unbundle the union before searching (Fig 9 :54).
2. **Read the residual per household slot.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." The national average hides which condition binds for whom.
3. **Anchor on the material outcome, not the delivered input.** Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Dollars, service units, wage rates and account balances are produced by the instrument frames; "A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces" (Fig 1 :27).
4. **Agent-only improvement is insufficient.** Fig 11 :39: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation." A transfer that changes holdings without changing wage, service, asset or market conditions changes contact only partly. The figure does not refuse cash; it refuses "cash alone is the whole answer." Cash remains a permitted introduction into the habitat (Fig 11 :61), and there is no solved state (Fig 11 :63).
5. **Combine the instruments as low-overlap frames, selected by residual.** Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." Fig 8 :53: "Residual first, frame second." Agreement among them is not evidence if they share an evidence pool (Fig 2 :40).
6. **The figures return a shape, not a name.** Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Which instrument has the largest effect for a stated binding condition is a `gap`; only a measured outcome closes it. "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence," (Fig 13 :134).
7. **Cost is a filter, not a witness.** Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Keep every cut ranked and reversible (Fig 9 :41).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "cash transfers do not work." The figure verdict is about isolated agent change and about single-lever framing, not about the efficacy of transfers.
- Not "public services, wages or assets are the answer." The same refusal applies to each single focus.
- Not a ranking of the four instruments. That is a `gap`, not a hidden conclusion.
- Not a claim that cost-effectiveness is irrelevant. It filters the budget; it is not the witness.
- Not `grounded`. No program outcome was measured in this run.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

A source-discriminating outcome, not a further argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (as recorded in the exemplar): an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the corresponding tests are:

- For a stated household slot and binding condition, does a measured material outcome separate the effect of a transfer, a service, a wage change and an asset change? An instrument that discriminates those sources would close the `gap`.
- Does a delivered-input metric track the material outcome identically in every slot, with no live slot residual? If yes, F2 and F3 fall.
- Are poverty conditions shown to be a disjoint partition, so one lever clears all of them without an intersection compound? If yes, F1 falls.
- Does an agent-only change move contact with no habitat change? If yes, F4 falls.

These are written before the run in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §5. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.

## 5. The honest stop

**Reached on the shape, unreached on the ranking.** The figure set yields a robust shape (per-slot, unbundled union, ground-anchored, triangulated, reversible) and refuses the question as written. It does not yield a winning instrument. Fig 13 :134 gives the correct stop: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence," and "It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it." What remains outside is a seat's office and a measurement, not more argument. One strain stays live: how "contact" with a habitat is measured in a social policy setting (Fig 11 :61-63), recorded in `01` §6.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not pick cash, public services, wage floors or asset ownership as the one anti-poverty lever. It says the four options are a union of overlapping conditions mis-framed as a partition: a single lever clears the households with one condition and leaves the intersections standing. It says the signal is per household, not per national average, and that the anchor must be the household's realized material outcome rather than the money, service units, wage rates or account balances a program delivers. It says changing a household's holdings in isolation does not change the conditions that produced the shortfall, so cash is a permitted part of a package but not a sufficient answer on its own. What it offers is the shape any answer must have, four falsifiers written in advance, and an open slot for the empirical ranking. The slot stays open until a measured outcome, not an agreement or a cost ratio, closes it.
