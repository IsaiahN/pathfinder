# Expert verification packet — the Tether on anti-poverty policy focus

**What this is:** a claim in a form an anti-poverty policy researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the per-domain predictions. Not `grounded`.** No program outcome was measured. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The four options in the question are not four points on one lever. They are a union of overlapping household conditions mis-framed as a partition. The residual must be read per household slot; the anchor must be the realized material outcome, not the delivered input; an agent-only improvement is insufficient to change contact with the conditions that generate the shortfall. The four instruments are low-overlap frames to be triangulated by residual, selected by measured outcomes and kept reversible. The figures return this shape, not the name of a winning instrument. Which instrument wins for a stated binding condition is an open slot that only a ground reading can close.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — question form; checkable by reading Figures 9 and 1

- **Statement:** the conditions behind the four instruments are a union, not a partition, and the residual is per slot.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :55 — "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :56 — "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds." Fig 1 :19 — "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
- **Refuter:** show that the four conditions are disjoint, so a single lever clears all of them with no intersection compound; or show that an aggregate poverty metric leaves no live slot residual.

### C2 — measurement; checkable by an anti-poverty researcher against evaluation practice

- **Statement:** the anchor is the material outcome; delivered inputs are frame-internal and cannot score the frame that produces them.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 — "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 1 :27 — "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces." Fig 9 :60 — "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."
- **What to check:** in a program you know, is success recorded as money delivered, service units, wage rate or account balance, or as a measured household outcome? Does the input metric ever move while the household condition does not?
- **Refuter:** show a delivered-input metric that tracks the material outcome identically in every slot with no live residual.

### C3 — contact; checkable by a researcher across labour, cash and asset evidence

- **Statement:** changing the agent in isolation does not change contact; instruments must be combined as low-overlap frames, selected by residual.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39 — "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation." Fig 8 :45 — "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." Fig 8 :53 — "Residual first, frame second."
- **What to check:** does a transfer-only or wage-only result leave some binding condition untouched? Do the Low Pay Commission and IZA findings about wage floors being only part of a package match this? Does the J-PAL Cash Plus model embody the union reading?
- **Refuter:** show an agent-only change that moves the ground with no habitat change, or show that one instrument reaches all conditions without a compound residue. This sub-claim does **not** assert that cash transfers fail; that stronger claim was retracted in `01` §6.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that cash transfers, public services, wage floors or assets are ineffective. The claim is about single-lever framing and agent-only change.
- Not a ranking of the four instruments. The ranking is a `gap`, not a hidden conclusion.
- Not that cost-effectiveness is irrelevant. It filters the budget and is not the witness.
- Not that the figures can name a winner. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here was settled by a measured program outcome.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating outcome, not a further argument.

1. For a stated household slot, does a measured material outcome separate the effects of cash, services, wages and assets?
2. Are poverty conditions a disjoint partition or a union with intersections?
3. Does a delivered-input metric track the material outcome with no live slot residual?
4. Does an agent-only change move contact with no habitat change?

A positive result to 1 closes the ranking `gap`. A positive result to 2, 3 or 4 refutes the shape claim.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :54-57 and Fig 1 :19 (five short passages).
2. Check C2 against one evaluation you know: was the anchor the input or the outcome?
3. Check C3 against your field's evidence: does one instrument leave a compound residue standing?
4. Name one instrument that would discriminate the sources of a household outcome, or say why none is available.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
