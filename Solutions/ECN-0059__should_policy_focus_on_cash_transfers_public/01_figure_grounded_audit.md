## 0. The residual in effect terms

The question offers four single focuses: cash transfers, public services, wage-setting institutions, asset ownership. Stated as an effect: each lever changes some material condition of a poor household and leaves other conditions unresolved. The same household can be simultaneously short of cash, short of services, short of wages, and short of assets, and the same lever moves different households differently.

**R (the residual).** The gap between a household's realized material condition and what a chosen single lever delivers. Arity: four candidate conditions, which overlap, so R is a union of conditions rather than a partition of one cause. Symmetry: not symmetric, because the effect of a lever depends on which condition binds (a workless household, a working-poor household, an assetless household are not interchangeable operands). Scale: first order at the household slot, second order at the population aggregate, and aggregating across slots discards the signal about where to look.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run (Formula step "Direction"): a rule that names the binding condition for a household slot, selects the instrument whose closure predicts that condition's shape, anchors on the material outcome, and states what would falsify the selection. This binding is the sought-for shape made refusable rather than an unbound preference.

Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

**Partition or union.** Union. Fig 9 :54: "when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." So the four options are not four points on one lever; they are four conditions that overlap, and the intersections have their own loop.

## 3. Findings F1 to F8

### F1 — The "focus on one lever" question is a union mis-framed as a partition (decisive for the question's form)

Fig 9 :54: "when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :56: "A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds." Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."

The four options are conditions that overlap. A household with no work *and* no assets is not a harder cash-only household; it is a different compound. A single-focus policy clears the region where one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing.

**Mode:** `general` (structural), with the `specified` prediction that a single-instrument focus will leave intersection households unimproved. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 9 :54-57, Fig 1 :19 and :22 (low overlap: condition structure vs per-slot residual structure), and world sources LPC 2026 and Gindling 2024 (labour market / wage-setting evidence). Fig 9 :54-57 governs the form; the world sources are secondary corroboration only.

### F2 — The residual is per household slot, not an aggregate

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

A national poverty rate, or a national average effect of one instrument, is the aggregation the figure warns against. The live signal is which condition binds for which household. J-PAL's framing that questions remain about "how well they work, for whom, and under what conditions" predicts exactly this per-slot requirement.

**Mode:** `specified`. **Status:** triangulated: Fig 1 (signal structure) and Fig 9 :27-28 (describe the residual before choosing a frame), plus J-PAL 2025 (field-specific, independent host).

### F3 — The anchor is the material outcome, not the delivered input

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 2 :30: "If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor." Fig 1 :23: "The ground enters as the only metric." Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."

Dollars delivered, service units, wage rates, and account balances are produced by the instrument frames. Using them as the success metric is a frame scoring itself. The anchor must be the household's realized material condition, which does not move because an agency re-describes it.

**Mode:** `general` for the anchor rule, `specified` for its application to policy metrics. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 2 :29-30, Fig 1 :23/:27, and Fig 9 :60 ("Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict.").

### F4 — Changing the agent in isolation does not change contact

Fig 11 :39: "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation." Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."

This bears specifically on the cash-only reading. A transfer changes the agent's holdings; it need not change the conditions (wage structure, service availability, asset base, markets) that generate the residual. The figure does **not** refuse cash. It refuses the claim that an agent-only improvement is sufficient. Cash can be an introduction into the habitat, which is permitted: Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." And there is no terminal fix: Fig 11 :63: "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."

**Mode:** `specified`, ungrounded. **Status:** single-source for the figure line (Fig 11), corroborated in a different field by Schofield and Venkataramani 2021 (PNAS) on the "double tax" of bandwidth and by Nam, Huang and Sherraden 2008 (ASPE) on assets producing effects "income alone cannot." Corroboration is secondary and does not set the verdict.

### F5 — The four instruments are low-overlap frames; combine by triangulation, not averaging

Fig 8 :45: "Two frames together reach what neither reaches alone." Fig 8 :49: "Independence is one quantity read twice." Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool."

Cash, services, wage floors, and assets draw on different evidence and have different closures. Their union can have a surplus. Their agreement is worth nothing if they are evaluated with one pooled indicator. Fig 2 :42: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 8 :45/:49 and Fig 2 :40/:42. The cost being wrong is high (household welfare), so independence is worth buying.

### F6 — Residual first, frame second

Fig 8 :53: "Residual first, frame second." Fig 9 :27: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."

Choosing the instrument before describing the binding condition is the inversion the figure names. Under that inversion, whichever instrument an agency already prefers will seem to fit the data. The pre-registered refuter and the one-paragraph answer lead with the residual.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** single-source for the figure wording (Fig 8 and Fig 9 are two different figures, but they share the framework's author and evidence pool; this is two statements of one rule, not two independent witnesses).

### F7 — The figures return a shape, not a name; the ranking is a gap

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :115: "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"

The shape any answer must have is: per slot, ground-anchored, union-unbundled, triangulated across low-overlap frames, reversible. Which named instrument has the largest effect on the ground is **not** derivable from the figures. It is a measurement. Mark the empirical ranking `gap` and stop honestly. "A search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence" (Fig 8 :39).

**Mode:** `general` for the shape-not-a-name limit, `gap` for the instrument ranking. **Status:** triangulated across Fig 9, Fig 13 and the canonical figures README.

### F8 — Cost-effectiveness is a filter, the ground is the witness

Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Fig 9 :41: "so keep every cut ranked and reversible"

Evidence about cost per dollar delivered ranks the budget. It is not the witness for whether a household's condition changed. Every cut in the analysis stays ranked and reversible.

**Mode:** `general`. **Status:** single-source (Fig 9), corroborated in field by J-PAL's insistence on randomized evaluation rather than administrative input measures.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

"A falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A household's poverty conditions are shown to be a **partition** (disjoint), so that one lever clears all conditions without an intersection compound (breaks F1).
2. A delivered-input metric (money, service units, wage rate, account balance) is shown to move the material ground outcome identically across every slot, with no live slot residual (breaks F2 and F3).
3. An agent-only change is shown to change contact with the ground with no change in habitat conditions (breaks F4).
4. A genuine figure re-census admits a line that directly names one of the four instruments as the unique recommended focus (breaks F7).
5. A measured outcome, not a model or an agreement, ranks one instrument above the others for a stated binding condition. This does **not** refute the shape; it closes the `gap` and moves the ranking from `gap` to `grounded` for that condition.

If 1 to 4 fires, the shape is withdrawn. Until a ground reading is run, nothing here may be labelled `grounded`.

## 6. Strains and re-census

- **Initial reading, retracted:** Fig 11 :39-40 was at first read as a refusal of cash transfers. Re-census narrows it: the figure refuses agent-only improvement, not transfers. Cash can be an introduction into the habitat (Fig 11 :61), and world sources show real welfare gains from cash (J-PAL, PNAS). The strong reading is withdrawn; the narrow reading stands.
- **Initial reading, narrowed:** "Shape, not a name" was first read as banning the naming of any instrument. Re-census: Fig 9 :32 and Fig 13 :134 govern what is missing or unreachable. All four instruments are already held frames, so the issue is selection by residual, not prohibition on naming. Narrowed, not retracted.
- **Live strain, named:** Fig 11 :61-62 ("Introduction is available; exclusion is not") appears to say a policy can add actors but never remove a condition. Real policy can decommodify a service or delink a benefit. Re-census: the figure governs contact facts in the habitat, which are not authorable; a policy changes an actor's valence (Fig 11 :49), not the fact of contact. This stays a strain because the measurement of "contact" in a social habitat is not an instrument we hold. It is not hidden.
- **Functional group vs union:** Fig 12 :124 describes instruments as members of a functional group (same purpose, different member); Fig 9 :54 describes their conditions as a union. Re-census: Fig 12 governs the structure of an answer term, Fig 9 governs the condition structure of the question. Different objects, no strain.
- **After re-census:** one live strain (contact in a social habitat), two narrowed readings, none hidden.

## 7. Recommendation

1. Restate the question before answering it: not "which one lever?" but "which condition binds for which household, and which combination, selected by residual and anchored on the material outcome, changes contact?" (F1, F6).
2. Report the residual per household slot; keep the headline aggregate as a filter only (F2, F8).
3. Anchor on measured material outcomes; never score a program with the input it produces (F3).
4. Treat cash, services, wage floors and assets as low-overlap frames to be triangulated, each with a stated refuter; do not let agreement among them stand as evidence (F4, F5).
5. Keep every cut ranked and reversible (Fig 9 :41). Record the lineage, the `gap` on the ranking, and what instrument or measurement would close it.
6. Lead outreach with the residual and the refuter, not the framework (Fig 10 :52: "Bring a characterised residual, never the source.").

### Seven-step recursive derivation (summary)

1. **Is the question one question?** No. Union of overlapping conditions (Fig 9 :54-57).
2. **What is the slot?** The household and its binding condition (Fig 1 :19).
3. **What is the anchor?** The material outcome, not the input (Fig 2 :29-30).
4. **Which figure governs the form?** Fig 9 and Fig 1; per-slot, unbundled, ground-anchored.
5. **Do the figures name a lever?** No. Shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32).
6. **Is the ranking reachable?** `gap`: only a measurement on the ground closes it (Fig 13 :134).
7. **What reverses the shape?** A partition result, an input metric that tracks the ground perfectly, an agent-only change that changes contact, or a figure line naming a unique focus.
