## Independent domain census

I redid the census before relying on the primary's conclusions, using the WATSON order: R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the gap between a household's realized material condition and what a single lever delivers. Arity: four conditions that overlap, so a **union compound** (Fig 9 :55-57), not a partition of one cause. Symmetry: not symmetric; the effect of a lever depends on which condition binds, so the operands are not interchangeable. Scale: first order at the household slot, second order at the population aggregate, where the where-to-look signal is lost.
- **Domain 1, household material condition.** Actors: household members, time, health, skills, cash, assets, services. Currency: realized consumption, security, capability. Anchor: the household's actual material outcome. Question type: union of overlapping conditions.
- **Domain 2, instrument frames.** Actors: transfers, services, wage floors, asset accounts, administrators. Currency: delivered inputs, frame-internal. Anchor: none of its own; borrowed from Domain 1. Question type: four low-overlap frames.
- **Domain 3, evaluation.** Actors: researchers, agencies, budgets. Currency: indicators and agreement. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. Independence in this simulated run: zero, because all domains draw on one evidence pool.
- **Domain 4, intersections.** Actors: households in two or more conditions. Currency: compound residual. Anchor: Domain 1's ground. Question type: union compound needing its own loop.

Order by dependency: describe the slot and the binding condition before selecting an instrument; refuse the single-focus framing before minting an answer; anchor on measured outcomes and keep cuts reversible.

My census matches the primary's four domains and its union reading. Where the primary derived from Fig 9 :54-57 and Fig 1, I derived from Fig 9 :57 ("a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either") and Fig 3 :20 (dependency order).
