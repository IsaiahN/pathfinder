## 0. The residual, in effect terms

The question asks which of three properties "matters most": resistance to change, recovery after disturbance, or persistence of ecosystem functions. Stated as an effect, a decision maker must choose which observable to instrument, fund and manage for, when a system meets a disturbance. The three candidates are offered as though they were alternatives on one scale.

- **A, resistance:** how little a variable moves under the perturbation. Measured at the moment of impact. Currency: a magnitude of displacement, a magnitude read as "how little".
- **B, recovery:** how fast the variable returns after displacement. Measured along a return trajectory. Currency: a rate over a sequence.
- **C, persistence of functions:** whether the function keeps occurring through time. Measured over a history or horizon. Currency: a duration, a threshold over time.

**R (arity, symmetry, scale).** Arity: one decision over three non-substitutable predicates. Symmetry: none, A, B and C do not commute and cannot be substituted for one another without changing the question. Scale: A is one slice, B is a trajectory, C is a horizon. The residual is indexed by the function or object slot being tracked, never by a single global "stability" number: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." (Fig 1 :19).

**What an answer would look like (the sought-for shape, bound as an ordinary term before the run).** An answer is acceptable only if it (i) names the slot and the objective, (ii) states which predicate is the live residual for that slot, and (iii) names the anchor that would settle it. A bare claim that "resilience matters most" is not an answer; it is a preference, and the framework cannot see a preference because it is not a bound term (Formula step 2, "Direction").

## 2. Findings

### F1: The question is a bundle of three questions, not one hard question (decisive)

Fig 9 :4-5:

> **"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."**

Fig 9 :54-55:

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."**

The three candidates keep working well on different subsets: resistance on sudden shock, recovery on transient displacement, persistence on chronic pressure. A single search for "which matters most" does not shrink with effort because each instrument measures a different observable and each answer selects a different subset. This is a union compound, not one scalar. Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."

The Library of Babel essay states the same failure in the vocabulary of search: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way." (Isaiah N, 2026-10-02). The question before us is that sentence almost verbatim.

**Verdict for this finding: `gap` on the literal superlative, resolved by splitting.** Triangulated: Fig 9 (union/split rule) has low overlap with the Library of Babel search argument (a different field, search and information theory). The convergence is expected but not circular, since one is a figure line and one is an essay by the same author, so the overlap is declared as high at author level. Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence" governs the honest stop.

**Refuter:** a figure line is produced that ranks resistance, recovery and persistence on one common scale, or an instrument is demonstrated that returns all three as the same observable with no information loss. Either would break the split.

### F2: The residual is per slot, never a global stability number

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."

Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."

Fig 1 :23: "The ground enters as the only metric."

A vegetation community under drought "might conserve biomass but lose biodiversity" (Wikipedia contributors, "Ecological stability", 2026). That is two slots with opposite readings. A single "stability" score averages them and hides the one that matters. This is also why a global answer to "which kind matters most" would be an aggregate over slots, and therefore exactly the move Fig 1 forbids.

**Verdict: the question must be re-indexed per function and per objective before it can be answered.** Triangulated: Fig 1 (per-slot rule) and the ecological case reported in the world literature (low overlap between a framework figure and an ecological observation). Mode `specified` for the per-slot requirement; the ecological example is `grounded` in the cited source but is used here only as illustration, not as this solution's ground.

**Refuter:** show a system in which all tracked functions move together under every disturbance and over every horizon, so that slot indexing makes no difference. That would remove the need for the split at this level, not at all levels.

### F3: The three predicates are in different currencies; ranking them is a unit error

Fig 13 :35: "Space, time and energy are not three readings of one quantity. Neither are spectrum, gradient and network."

Fig 13 :36: "adjacency is not duration, an ordering is not a pathway, a pathway is not a direction."

The figure's point is independence of predicates. Applied here: displacement magnitude is not return rate, and return rate is not duration of function. The same structure in the bond figure:

Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."

Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units"

Fig 12 :130: "Summing them into one figure is the error this names, not the fix for it."

The ecological literature independently reaches the same place. Grimm and Wissel found 70 stability concepts and recommended replacing "ecological stability" with more specific terms such as constancy, resilience and persistence (Wikipedia contributors, "Ecological stability", 2026). A separate page reports that their inventory reduced to three fundamental properties: "staying essentially unchanged", "returning to the reference state...after a temporary disturbance" and "persistence through time of an ecological system" (Wikipedia contributors, "Resistance (ecology)", 2026). Those three map exactly onto A, B and C of the question, and the literature treats them as distinct, not as ranks.

**Verdict: no figure line ranks the three.** Triangulated: Fig 13 (independent predicates) plus Fig 12 (incommensurable currencies), low overlap between the substrate census and the bond-pricing rule. The world sources corroborate but share a broad ecology evidence pool with each other, so they count as one witness, not three.

**Refuter:** a measurement or derivation showing that one of the three is a function of the other two for all systems, so that a common currency exists. Until then, "A matters more than B" is a category error of the kind Fig 12 :130 names.

### F4: Which predicate matters is anchor-relative, not absolute

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."

Fig 2 :36: "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."

Fig 2 :37-38: "Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."

The anchor changes with what the question is constitutively about. If the decision is about surviving an abrupt shock, resistance is the live predicate. If it is about recovery time after a transient, recovery is. If it is about a service being delivered over a management horizon, persistence of function is. There is no anchor-free ranking. Fig 11 :39: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone" and Fig 11 :40: "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Monitoring a property that does not change contact with the failure mode is not management.

**Verdict: relative to a named objective and anchor, the ranking becomes determinate and local; globally it does not exist.** Triangulated: Fig 2 (anchor rule) and Fig 11 (contact rule), low overlap between an alignment figure and a habitat figure. Single-source on the specific mapping from objective to anchor, which is our binding, not a figure line.

**Refuter:** a case in which the same named objective and the same anchor select two different predicates as decisive under the same disturbance, with no change of slot or scale. That would break the anchor-relativity claim.

### F5: Vocabulary is the missing middle link; a reading below the break is a reading of nothing

Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."

Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."

Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"

The dependency order is: terms for the observables, then an objective, then measurement, then the management decision. The ecological literature confirms that link 2 and 3 are exactly where this field is weak: "This overuse of the term has led to controversy over its definition and implementation." (Wikipedia contributors, "Ecological stability", 2026). Choosing which stability to manage for before defining what would count as a failure is a reading taken below the break.

**Verdict: split and define before measuring.** Triangulated: Fig 3 (dependency order) and Fig 9 (describe before looking). Single-source for the claim that this field's middle link is the definitional one, which comes from the world source.

**Refuter:** demonstrate a deployed instrument that discriminates all three predicates without a prior definitional choice. Then the vocabulary link already works and the order is not binding here.

### F6: Persistence as a managed outcome is not the ground (strain named and re-censused)

Fig 11 :63: "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state."

Fig 11 :61: "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."

Fig 11 :24: "the ground does not decay, the channel does"

Strain: if "persistence of ecosystem functions" is treated as the anchor, and persistence is something a manager maintains, then the anchor appears to move when a frame acts on it. Fig 2 :25 says the anchor is "pinned: it does not update on A or B". Re-census resolves this. The ground is what the ecosystem actually does; the maintained function is the objective, an effect description, not the ground. Fig 2 :36-38 licenses an anchor only when the question is constitutively about it, and warns that instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure. So a managed function can be the objective of the decision while the measurement of what the system does remains the anchor. Different objects, no contradiction.

**Verdict: no strain after re-census; the objective/ground distinction must be stated explicitly in any packet.** Single-source for the re-census itself (Fig 2 plus Fig 11 read together).

**Refuter:** a case where the act of measuring the function changes the function irreversibly and no independent reading of the system remains. Then the channel cannot be kept reachable and the anchor reading is compromised.

### F7: Agreement among stability definitions is not a verdict

Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."

The field has 70 stability concepts and a documented terminological dispute. Adding more adherents to one definition produces a convention, not corroboration. In this simulated run every seat draws on the same evidence pool, so agreement between the primary census and the second seat is one pool talking: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool" (Fig 2 :40).

**Verdict: methodological, no ecological ranking.** Triangulated across Fig 2 and Fig 7; still one evidence pool in this simulation.

**Refuter:** a genuinely independent instrument, not another reader of the same literature, that returns a ranking and survives a round trip. Then agreement would carry information.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

The split claim is refuted if any of the following holds.

1. A figure line is found that ranks resistance, recovery and persistence on one scale, or a derivation shows one is a composition of the other two.
2. An instrument is demonstrated that returns all three as the same observable with no loss, so the currencies are in fact one.
3. Across at least two low-overlap systems with the same stated objective, one predicate's failure consistently precedes the other two and the others never precede it, under independent instruments. This would give a domain-specific winner and would force a re-census of F4, even though it would not by itself create a global winner.
4. A world source is opened that reports the field has settled on a single measure of stability that predicts the other two across systems. (None was found in the sources read; the sources read report the opposite.)

If any fires, the split is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, nothing here may be labelled `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Ask "which stability, for which function, under which disturbance, over which horizon, for whose objective?" Do not ask "which matters most?".
2. Keep the three verdicts separate and per slot. Never average them into a stability index (F2).
3. State the objective and the anchor before naming a predicate (F4).
4. Treat the vocabulary split as the first link of work (F5).
5. Send the generator, that is the split, the refuter and the shape claim, not a recording of which stability the author prefers (Fig 4 :22 "Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down.").
