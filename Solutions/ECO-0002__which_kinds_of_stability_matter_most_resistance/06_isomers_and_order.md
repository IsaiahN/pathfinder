# Three predicates as different bonds over shared state variables

**Mode: `specified`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading.

## RESIDUAL

Can the three candidates (resistance to change, recovery after disturbance, persistence of ecosystem functions) be written as different arrangements of the same ecological state variables, and does the figure notion of isomer help decide whether they are one thing or three? A second question: in what order must they be assessed?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms available in the ecology frame: a state variable `x` (biomass, diversity, a function rate), a disturbance `d`, and a horizon `h`. Operators available in the figures: ⋛ comparison, → sequence, − subtraction, and the unary negation. No new primitive is needed. Bonds held: the seven plus negation. Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."

## DERIVATION

### Q1: Can each predicate be written as a distinct bond?

- **Resistance.** Compare `x` before and after `d` at the same slice: a magnitude of difference, thresholded. Bond: ⋛ comparison, "a threshold relation between two quantities" (Operators table). Currency: magnitude.
- **Recovery.** A trajectory from the post-disturbance value back toward the prior value over time. Bonds: − subtraction (distance from reference) and → sequence (order of the return). A spent difference, which is Fig 13 :22 "a difference that can be spent". Currency: rate.
- **Persistence.** Whether `x` stays above a floor across `h`. Bond: ⋛ comparison applied over a duration, plus the time state. Currency: duration.

All three use the same atoms, `x`, `d`, `h`. They differ in which bond is charged and which state is in the slot. Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance."

**Strain, named:** the figures do not contain the words "resistance", "recovery" or "persistence". This three-way mapping is our binding onto figure predicates, not a figure-derived identification. Re-census: Fig 12 governs bonds and what they cost; ecology governs which observable is read. Different objects, so the apparent strain dissolves. The binding remains single-source and ungrounded.

### Q2: Are they isomers?

Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."

- If all three are written over the shared atom set `{x, d, h}`, they are **isomers** in the figure's sense: same atoms, different bond, different substance. Writing "stability = high" without saying which bond is held is the flat formula error of Fig 12 :14-16: "the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not".
- The ecological literature agrees on the three properties but not on their relation: Walker et al. treat resistance as a component of resilience (Wikipedia contributors, "Resistance (ecology)", 2026). That is a **union**, not a partition. Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." So the isomer reading is clean only when the bonds are kept separate; if a definition folds resistance into resilience, the folded term is a compound with its own loop (Fig 9 :57-58: "it stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound").
- Weaker reading: the three share a **functional group** (Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."), namely "a state variable evaluated against a disturbance". The qualifier (comparison, sequence, duration) says which member.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if one of the three can be derived from the other two over the shared atom set without adding a bond or a new state, the isomer claim fails and the three collapse into one predicate with three names. If the figure set is amended to price duration as a bond rather than a state, the mapping in Q1 must be re-censused.

### Q3: What order are they assessed in?

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."

The dependency order for this question is:

1. **Vocabulary.** Terms for the three observables and a refusable definition of each. Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
2. **Slot and objective.** Which function, which disturbance, which horizon, whose objective. Fig 1 :19, Fig 2 :36.
3. **Anchor.** What will settle the reading and cannot move when the frame is pushed. Fig 2 :29.
4. **Measurement.** Only now instrument resistance, recovery and persistence per slot.
5. **Decision.** Only now choose which predicate to manage for.

A ranking attempt at step 5 with steps 1 to 3 missing is a reading below the break. Fig 3 :22-23: "A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can."

### Q4: Why the order is not "importance"

Fig 3 :19: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not." The literature has spent decades improving stability measurement (step 4) while the definitional dispute (step 1) remained open: "This overuse of the term has led to controversy over its definition and implementation." (Wikipedia contributors, "Ecological stability", 2026). That is exactly effort above the break arriving at a step that cannot use it. The remedy is to split the vocabulary first, which is Fig 9 :4-5, not to build a better single stability index.

## CONCLUSION

The three candidates are best read as **isomers over a shared ecological atom set**: same state variables, different bonds, different substances. That reading explains why a single "stability" scalar loses the signal: the arrangement is not recorded, and nothing downstream can tell which bond was held. The assessment order is dependency order, vocabulary then slot and objective then anchor then measurement then decision. Neither result ranks the three. Both are `specified`, ungrounded, and carry a stated falsifier.
