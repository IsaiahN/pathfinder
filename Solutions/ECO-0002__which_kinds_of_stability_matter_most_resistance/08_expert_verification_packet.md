# Expert verification packet: the Tether on kinds of ecological stability

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ecosystem-stability researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the shape, not `grounded`.** No ecosystem measurement is claimed by this run.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "which kind of stability matters most, resistance, recovery, or persistence of function?" has no anchor-free answer. The three candidates are distinct predicates with distinct currencies and distinct slices: resistance is a displacement magnitude at impact, recovery is a rate along a return trajectory, persistence is a duration above a floor. The figures license splitting the question, indexing the residual per function and slot, and selecting the live predicate relative to a named objective and anchor. They do not license a global winner. The specific mapping of the three ecological properties onto figure predicates is our binding, recorded as such, not a figure-derived identification.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 9, 12 and 13

- **Statement:** the three candidates are not three readings of one quantity; no figure line ranks them and a single stability score loses the arrangement.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 13 :35 "Space, time and energy are not three readings of one quantity. Neither are spectrum, gradient and network." Also Fig 12 :125 "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." and Fig 9 :4-5 "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."
- **Refuter:** derive one of the three predicates from the other two, or produce a figure line that ranks them on one scale, or demonstrate an instrument that returns all three as the same observable with no information loss.

### C2: ecology-facing; checkable against the stability literature

- **Statement:** the literature itself treats these as distinct fundamental properties and recommends splitting the vocabulary rather than ranking a single stability. As reported in the reference pages opened for this folder, Grimm and Wissel inventoried 167 definitions, found 70 stability concepts, and proposed replacing "ecological stability" with constancy, resilience and persistence; their inventory reduces to "staying essentially unchanged", "returning to the reference state...after a temporary disturbance" and "persistence through time of an ecological system". Walker et al. treat resistance as a component of resilience, which is an overlap, not a rank.
- **What to check:** is that report faithful to Grimm and Wissel 1997 and Walker et al. 2004? Does any later synthesis establish a single measure that predicts the other two across systems? (None was found in the sources opened here.)
- **Refuter:** a synthesis showing that one predicate's failure consistently predicts the other two across at least two low-overlap systems with the same objective, under independent instruments. That would give a local winner and force C2 to be narrowed.

### C3: framework-internal derivation, with the order claim

- **Statement:** assessment order is dependency order, not importance: vocabulary, then slot and objective, then anchor, then measurement, then decision. A ranking attempted before the first three links is a reading below the break.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it." Also Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." and Fig 2 :36 "An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."
- **Refuter:** demonstrate a deployed instrument that discriminates all three predicates without a prior definitional choice and that fixes the anchor independently of the objective. Then the vocabulary link already works and the order is not binding here.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that one stability kind is universally best, nor that "resilience" subsumes the others as ranks.
- Not that ecology has settled on one stability measure. The sources report a terminological dispute.
- Not which predicate wins for any named ecosystem. No anchor or measurement was supplied.
- Not that persistence of function is the ground. It is an objective; the ground is what the system does. Fig 11 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does".
- Not `grounded`. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It cannot settle the claim it reports on.
- Not that the figures name resistance, recovery or persistence. The predicate mapping is a binding from the ecology frame, provenanced as such.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not agreement.

1. For a named system, function and objective, pre-register the failure event and run independent instruments for all three predicates per slot.
2. Test whether one predicate's failure consistently precedes the other two under low-overlap instruments.
3. Test the unit claim: is there a measurement returning all three as one observable with no loss?
4. Test the isomorphism: can one predicate be derived from the other two over the shared atom set?
