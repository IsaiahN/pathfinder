**Subject:** A claim about three kinds of ecological stability, with its refuter written in advance

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "which kind of stability matters most, resistance, recovery, or persistence of function?" does not have a ground-free answer. The three candidates are different predicates measured in different units, on different slices: resistance is a magnitude at impact, recovery is a rate along a return, persistence is a duration over a horizon. Fig 13 :35: "Space, time and energy are not three readings of one quantity. Neither are spectrum, gradient and network." Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 9 :4-5 says that disagreements which do not shrink with effort are "not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." So the shape claim is: re-index the question by function and slot, name the objective and the anchor, and only then say which predicate is live. A global winner is a `gap`, not a silence.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that one of the three is always best, that ecology has settled on a single stability measure, or that this derivation settles any real ecosystem. The framework returns a shape, not a name. The mapping of resistance to a comparison, recovery to a sequence or spent gradient, and persistence to a duration is my binding onto the figures, not a figure-derived identification, and it is recorded as such.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a derivation showing one of the three is a composition of the other two, or a figure line that ranks them on one scale;
> 2. an instrument that returns all three as the same observable with no information loss;
> 3. across two or more low-overlap systems with the same stated objective, one predicate's failure consistently precedes the other two under independent instruments (this would give a local winner and force the claim to be narrowed).
>
> I wrote these down before writing the conclusion. If any holds, the split is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.

> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
