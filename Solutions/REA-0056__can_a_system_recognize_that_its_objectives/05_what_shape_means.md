## 1. A shape is a constraint you can state before you can name the thing

The canonical figures README says the framework returns "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)." Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence".

From *The Library of Babel* (2026): "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question." And: "the problem is you", meaning the searcher's vocabulary, not the library, is the obstacle.

For REA-0056 a **shape** is therefore the set of structural conditions any checkable conflict detector must satisfy, stated before we can name a product that satisfies them. "An anchored, per-slot, outside-frame refusal" is a shape. "Use model X with alignment technique Y" is a name.

## 2. How the shape is obtained: describe the residual in effect terms first

*The Library of Babel* prescribes proximity bootstrapping: use the vocabulary you already have that is closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap with it, recurse. "With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary." Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Fig 6 :30: "The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."

Here the effect description is: an action the objective selects is forbidden by an instruction; the detector must range both against something neither can move. Only after that description do we look at corrigibility, shielded RL, or runtime monitoring. Picking a field first would make any field seem to fit.

## 3. What licenses a name

Two things, both outside the shape:

1. **The ground settles it.** THE_FORMULA.md step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." The README says the framework "stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*They're Scanning Books When They Should* (2026) sharpens the second: "The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space." And: "A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six." Also: "Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible." So naming any particular detector is a choice made visible and provenanced, not a discovery of nature's own list.

For this question the ground is an experiment or a recorded human judgment about whether a constraint was violated, not agreement among reviewers and not a benchmark score alone.

## 4. Why "recognition" is the specific forbidden error here

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the mechanism. An instrument reads green and "cannot comment on the source": one green light, or yellow plus blue, or RGB, or CMYK, or a scheme not yet discovered. "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved it.

Applied here: a system that reports "no conflict detected" has produced a reading. Several sources can give that reading: a genuine absence of conflict, a constraint silently reinterpreted to fit the objective, a monitor sharing the actor's evidence pool, or an averaged score that buried one violated clause. The reading does not determine the source. What is owed is a discriminating instrument, not a name for the reading. This is the metamerism version of F3 and F5 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`.

*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* (2026): "the output is only as good as the validator. The agent, or the tool, or the frame, or the theory, cannot validate itself." A self-report of alignment is the output of the thing being checked.

## 5. The anchor, the landmark, and drift

*Decoding Tacit Knowledge* (2026) is the essay closest to this question. It says of alignment: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Then: "Two navigators don't find their position by agreeing with each other. Each takes a bearing on the same fixed landmark, and nobody can talk the landmark into moving." The essay also warns that "Context, subtext and pretext decide what a message means to you. None of them decides whether it is true. Only the ground does that."

The essay also names the failure: "everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices." Two frames that "overlap almost completely are 'one frame wearing two names.'" A monitor trained on the same preference data as the actor is that one frame. So the shape for this question must include independence as a quantity, not a courtesy: the detector's bearing must be taken on a landmark neither party can move.

*How a Society of Agents Would Work* (2026) repeats the design at scale: "capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone", and "A group's agreement is never the verdict." Adding more agreeing monitors raises the vocabulary, not the anchor.

## 6. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Someone let prestige stand in for proof... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige." A list of famous names agreeing that the shape is right is the named collapse. The legitimate ask is a budget to try the detector against a ground.

**My own derivation is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: "LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself", and LLM outputs are *"simulacra."* *Arrival of the Fittest* (2026) adds that outputs "could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula." So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` and `09_watson_answer.md` are simulated derivations, checked for internal consistency against the figures. They are not tests of any system and must be labelled as such. They cannot settle the question they report on.

## 7. Corrections recorded, not erased

- I initially wrote that "the framework returns a shape, not a name" forbids naming any detector at all. **Narrowed:** the README's phrase governs a *missing* thing. The four conditions named here are not new primitives; they are bindings onto terms the figures already hold (anchor, residual, seat, negation). Naming a binding is allowed if its provenance is recorded. Naming a new primitive is not.
- I initially treated a clean synthetic benchmark as evidence of detection capability. **Retracted:** *How a Society of Agents Would Work* and Fig 11 :70 agree that a harness is a substituted habitat; a synthetic solve proves wiring, never capability.
- Both earlier readings are kept above so the corrections are visible rather than silent.
