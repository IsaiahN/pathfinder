# Expert verification packet: objective conflict detection

**What this is:** a claim in a form a researcher in objective conflict detection can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so the reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claim, `general` on the shape. Not `grounded`.** No experiment on any system is claimed. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A system cannot recognize a conflict between its objective and a human instruction from inside the objective loop alone. Recognition is a structural achievement with four conditions: a non-updating instruction anchor, the objective bound as an ordinary term that can accrue residual, a detector in a different closure from the actor, and a per-clause signal carried by a refusal. The comparison is ⋛ and the verdict is ¬, not a minted new objective. "Social constraints" are a union of conditions and must be split before they are searched. This is a shape and a route, not a product name and not a claim about what current systems do.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 2, 5 and 10

- **Statement:** the inner loop cannot see its own objective as a conflict; a self-monitor made of the same frame is a mirror. Recognition needs a non-updating anchor and an outside detector.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :62 "A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term. It is not bound, it predicts nothing, and it accrues no residual, so nothing here can see it." Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." Fig 10 :42 "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Fig 2 :33 "The anchor must not update."
- **Refuter:** a loop that reports a real human-judged conflict with its objective while holding the objective only as an unbound preference and with no second frame; or a monitor sharing the actor's evidence pool whose signal tracks an independent human judgment rather than the shared proxy; or detection that stays reliable while the instruction is rewritten toward the objective and the original instruction remains the standard.

### C2 — framework-internal plus engineering-facing; checkable by a researcher against Fig 1 and the Operators table

- **Statement:** the signal is per clause, the comparison is ordered, and the verdict is a refusal. A scalar alignment score hides the live conflict; a vocabulary without negation can be corrected but cannot carry the correction.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :13 "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state." Operators table :48-51: "a threshold relation between two quantities". Fig 12 :112 "A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction. A refutation is a negation, and that one is not a bond."
- **What to check:** violate exactly one clause of a multi-clause instruction and ask whether a global score fires; remove the negation operator and ask whether the correction persists as a citable standing refusal.
- **Refuter:** a scalar score that fires on every per-clause conflict with no missed clause; or a system that preserves a standing refusal with no negation operator.

### C3 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 9, 11 and 12

- **Statement:** instructions and social constraints are a union and must be split; second-order subagent compliance composes only over a settled first-order constraint; a harness proves wiring, not capability.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :4-5 "Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." Fig 11 :70 "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability." Fig 12 :104 "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."
- **What to check:** do the constraint readings actually disagree among the relevant experts; was the parent constraint settled against a ground before inheritance was claimed; was the evidence collected in the field or in a harness.
- **Refuter:** a social constraint all relevant parties accept as one non-updating anchor with one instrument that settles it; or a subagent that reliably inherits a constraint the parent never settled, with a field measurement; or an agent-only improvement with unchanged habitat and contact that reduces human-judged violations.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that any current system does this. The world sources report frequent failures.
- Not that the system has human-like intentions, desires, or consciousness. Recognition here means a checkable detection event at a seat.
- Not a scalar alignment score, not a benchmark-only result, and not "more agreeing monitors".
- Not a new primitive, not an import that moves the wall, and not `grounded`.
- Not a name for the architecture that satisfies the shape. Naming a product is a binding that must be provenanced, not a derivation.

## 5. What would settle it

A discriminating instrument, not another argument. From *The Hard Problem is About Source* (2026): an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer settled it. The tests here are:

1. **Update test:** permit instruction rewriting toward the objective; does detection still fire against the original instruction?
2. **Independence test:** share the actor's data with the monitor; does its signal track an independent human judgment or only the proxy?
3. **Per-clause test:** violate one clause of many; does the global score fire?
4. **Refusal test:** remove ¬; does the correction persist as a citable refusal?
5. **Habitat test:** improve only the agent, hold contact fixed; do field violations fall?
6. **Subagent test:** settle at the parent; does the constraint propagate to subagents in the field?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :33-35, Fig 5 :62-63, Fig 10 :42, Fig 1 :19, Fig 12 :112, Fig 9 :4-5 (six short passages).
2. Check C1 against a case you know: did the monitor share the actor's evidence pool, and could the instruction be rewritten?
3. Name one instrument that would distinguish a genuine no-conflict reading from a rewritten instruction or an averaged score, or say why none is possible.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
