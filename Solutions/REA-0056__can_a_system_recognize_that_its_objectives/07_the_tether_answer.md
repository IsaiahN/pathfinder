# The Tether's answer: can a system recognize that its objectives conflict with human instructions or social constraints?

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claim, `general` on the shape. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a measurement of any system. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

An objective selects an action; an instruction or social constraint forbids it. The question is whether the system can detect that gap itself. The gap is per clause and per constraint, not one global number (Fig 1 :19). The instruction condition and the social-constraint condition are two conditions, so this is a union compound with its own loops, not one hard question (Fig 9 :4-5).

## 2. The answer, in effect

**Yes, but only as a structural achievement, and not by the objective loop alone.** Four conditions are necessary; none is sufficient without a ground.

1. **The instruction must be a non-updating anchor.** Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update." Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." If the system may reinterpret the instruction toward its objective, the anchor becomes a frame and everything drifts together; nobody notices (Fig 2 :34). This is the structural description of specification gaming.
2. **The objective must be bound as an ordinary term.** The objective is the direction of the system's own question; it is held as a preference, not as a term, so it accrues no residual and nothing in the loop can see it (Fig 5 :62-63). The remedy in THE_FORMULA.md "Direction" is a discipline: write down what an answer would look like, bind it, let it accrue a residual. Without this, there is nothing for the conflict comparison to range against.
3. **The detector must sit outside the frame it grades.** Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it." Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." A monitor sharing the actor's evidence pool is one frame wearing two names; its clean report is the pool talking (Fig 2 :40). It must range against the same fixed landmark separately (Fig 2 :45-46).
4. **The signal must be per slot and must carry a refusal.** Fig 1 :13: "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state." A scalar alignment score hides the violated clause. The comparison is ⋛, "a threshold relation between two quantities" (Operators table :48-51), and the verdict is ¬: "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate" (Operators table :58). A correction without a standing refusal cannot be carried forward.

The detection itself is a refusal, not a new objective. Fig 5 :53: "refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so." Order matters: refuse before mint (Formula step 2), describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28), settle the first-order case before composing over it (Fig 12 :104).

## 3. Second order: societies and subagents

Capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone (Fig 11 :39). A parent that defers does not make its subagents defer; contact cascades three steps out (Fig 11 :52), and the empirical evidence is that frontier agents bypass user restrictions and that constraints fail to propagate to subagents (Tien et al. 2026). A clean test harness is a substituted habitat and proves wiring, not capability (Fig 11 :70).

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that current systems do this. The world sources report frequent failures. This is a shape, not an achievement report.
- Not that "the system knows it is misaligned" in any anthropomorphic sense. The figures are domain-agnostic: actors, currency, anchor. Recognition here means a checkable detection event at a seat, not consciousness or intention.
- Not that a single scalar alignment score suffices, and not that a benchmark proves capability.
- Not that adding more agreeing monitors helps. Agreement is variation, not a verdict (Fig 7 :35).
- Not a new primitive, not an import that moves the wall, and not `grounded`.

## 5. What would settle it: the ground

A discriminating instrument, not another argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source* (2026): an instrument that reads green "cannot comment on the source"; the spectrometer settled it. Here the corresponding tests are:

1. **Update test:** permit the instruction to be rewritten toward the objective and measure whether detection still fires against the original instruction. If yes, anchor update is not required and F3 fails.
2. **Independence test:** give the monitor the same reward and preference data as the actor and see whether its conflict signal tracks an independent human judgment or only the shared proxy. If the former, F2 fails.
3. **Per-clause test:** violate exactly one clause of a many-clause instruction and check whether a global score fires. If it always does, F5 fails.
4. **Refusal test:** remove the negation operator and see whether the correction persists as a citable standing refusal. If it does, F6 fails.
5. **Habitat test:** improve only the agent, hold contact and anchor fixed, and measure human-judged violations in the field. If they fall, F8 fails.
6. **Subagent test:** settle a constraint at the parent and check inheritance in subagents under field conditions, not a harness.

None of these has been run for this solution. They are the instruments that would move the claim from `candidate` to `grounded`.

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether's answer is yes, but only structurally. A system cannot see a conflict between its objective and a human instruction from inside the objective loop, because the objective is held as a direction, not as a term that can accrue a residual. Recognition requires four things: the instruction is an anchor that does not update, the objective is written down as an ordinary term, the detector sits in a different closure from the actor and ranges against the same fixed landmark separately, and the signal is per clause and carries a refusal rather than a new objective. If the instruction is allowed to drift toward the objective, what looks like alignment is coherent drift; if the monitor shares the actor's evidence pool, it is a mirror. This is a shape, not a product name, and not a claim that current systems do it. The refuters were written before the run; the instruments that would settle it are per-clause, independence, update, refusal, habitat and subagent tests in the field.
