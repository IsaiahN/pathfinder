## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the question "How do media systems and information
inequality affect the quality of democratic accountability?" Option A: read it as one lever with a
single effect size, so that more media freedom and more information produce more accountability.
Option B: read it as a union of overlapping conditions indexed per accountability slot. What each
leads to: A averages away the live signal, lets a frame-internal index occupy the anchor seat, and
hands a filter a verdict; B returns a separator, keeps verdicts per domain, and leaves the scalar
open until a discriminator settles it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain giving actors / currency / anchor, partition
or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The gap between formal answerability and the actual detection, naming and
  sanctioning of failures of power. Arity: three conditions, a union compound. Symmetry: "affect" is
  ordered, not a symmetric correlation; the arrow is not recoverable from a coefficient. Scale: one
  citizen judging one official on one issue is a slot; a national index is a lossy aggregate. Fig 1
  :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."
- **Domain 1, the media system as channel or mirror.** Actors: outlets, owners, platforms, ranking
  systems, journalists, funders, regulators. Currency: attention, reach, revenue, editorial
  independence. Anchor role: an independent record of what happened, which does not move when coverage
  is pushed. Question type: contact and independence inside a union.
- **Domain 2, information distribution across the population.** Actors: citizens as frames with belief
  `b` and weighting `w`; groups by education, income, access, language. Currency: knowledge and the
  ability to attribute credit and blame. Anchor role: a factual benchmark on a named issue; polls are
  instruments pointed at it. Question type: per-slot registration inside a union.
- **Domain 3, the checking arrangement.** Actors: voters, courts, auditors, oversight bodies, outside
  readers of a claim. Currency: verdicts and sanctions. Anchor role: the actual performance of power
  holders, which must not change when the oversight body's own report changes. Question type: ground
  contact and refusal inside a union.
- **Domain 4, refused.** "Information inequality as one scalar" has no nameable anchor at the right
  level and is not a domain yet.
- **Order by dependency.** Anchor role and channel before discriminating distribution before verdict
  and sanction. Optimising a link above a broken one is motion without progress (Fig 3 :20).

- **Mass communication / information inequality.** Tichenor, Donohue and Olien 1970; Gaziano 1983 and
  1997; Hwang and Jeong 2009; Mondak and Anderson 2004, "Knowledge gap hypothesis" (Wikipedia, 2026):
  "Since there is already a pre-existing gap in knowledge between groups in a population, mass media
  amplifies this gap to another level." Predicts that equal access alone does not close the gap;
  corroborates the weighting finding. Overlap: high with the digital-divide source (one mass-communication pool).
- **Information science / development.** "Digital divide" (Wikipedia, 2026): "The digital divide
  worsens inequality in access to information and resources", and the divide "is fundamentally a
  socio-institutional gap". Predicts first, second and third-level divides; corroborates the union
  reading of access, skills and advantage. Overlap: high with the knowledge-gap source.
- **Media studies / UNESCO.** "Media pluralism" (Wikipedia, 2026): "Media capture is considered the
  primary threat to media pluralism", and, from UNESCO, "even if multiple kinds of information and
  programming are available, each segmented group may only ingest one branch of the whole". Predicts
  that outlet count fails where evidence pools overlap; corroborates the independence finding.
- **Political economy / institutional economics.** "Media capture" (Wikipedia, 2026), citing Besley and
  Prat 2006 in the American Economic Review: "Media capture reduces government accountability by
  limiting public access to independent information." Predicts a channel failure, not a ground
  failure; corroborates the channel-versus-ground distinction. Overlap: one economics pool with the
  principal-agent source.
- **Economics / institutional theory.** "Principal–agent problem" (Wikipedia, 2026): "The problem
  worsens when there is a greater discrepancy of interests and information between the principal and
  agent, as well as when the principal lacks the means to punish the agent." Predicts that information
  asymmetry and weak punishment are separate conditions; corroborates F6 and F1.
- **Political science / governance.** "Accountability" (Wikipedia, 2026): "Governments are
  'accountable' if voters can discern whether governments are acting in their interest and sanction
  them appropriately", and "Freedom of the press contributes to the reduction of corruption by
  exposing corrupt actions." Predicts a discernment plus sanction test separate from a press metric;
  corroborates C3 and the channel reading.
- **Journalism history / law.** "Fourth estate" (Wikipedia, 2026): Benkler's networked fourth estate

  corroborates F10 and the bond-to-record requirement.
- **Ecology, genuinely low overlap.** Wanless, Wardle and Scales, "How can approaches from physical

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The shape survives; the scalar and the standing list do not. Democratic accountability quality is a
union of three overlapping conditions with different anchor roles, indexed per named institution,
issue and public. Media systems are a channel whose evidentiary weight comes from independence of
evidence rather than outlet count. Information inequality is a weighting and support condition, not a
volume condition; equal access does not equal equal benefit. The checking seat must be unable to
profit from its reading, and the chain usually breaks at the channel-to-objective link where no
instrument exists. Over a fixed atom set, different media regimes can be isomers. No universal
coefficient and no named institution is returned; that is a declared `gap`. The result is `specified`,
ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states mode, refuters and provenance before the
  conclusion, so a mistake is findable. The larger cost is spending an outside reader's independence
  by sending a recording or a prestige-weighted list instead of the shape and its refuter. Fig 2 :42
  prices that: "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." It is avoidable by leading
  with the shape claim and the refuter.
- **undoable:** yes. The four-atom core and its bindings are removable without altering the figure
  derivation; `07` and `08` already state them as bindings. A published claim can be superseded by a
  corrected packet, and no figure line is withdrawn.
- **reversed by:** a discriminator, not agreement. A per-slot measurement showing that the record moves
  when the powerful push on it, that low-overlap frames do not converge where shared-source frames
  do, that equalising access closes the gap with weighting held fixed, or that volume alone moves the
  verdict on a slot, would break the shape. A derivation of a universal coefficient from the figures
  would close the `gap`. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
