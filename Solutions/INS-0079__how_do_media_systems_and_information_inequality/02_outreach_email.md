# Outreach draft :  check one output before deciding whether the framing is worth reading

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note for a submission through a public venue in
`03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the live type question in `01` §8 (the anchor role for an
authored record) is stated as role-relative rather than as a property. The stage-1 output must survive
a referee's check or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** One checkable claim about media, information inequality and accountability

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking
> you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does
> not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** "How do media systems and information inequality
> affect the quality of democratic accountability?" does not have one answer. It is a union of three
> overlapping conditions, each with a different thing that settles it:
>
> 1. Is a record of what actually happened reachable through a channel that does not move when
>    owners, platforms or reporters are pushed? The corrupt act does not change when coverage does.
> 2. Can the relevant public discriminate that record, given how information and the benefit from
>    exposure are distributed? Equal access does not equal equal benefit.
> 3. Can a body able to sanction form a verdict without profiting from the reading it returns?
>
> These are not three answers to one question. They are three conditions whose intersections are the
> hard cases, so a lever that clears a region where only one holds leaves the intersections standing.
> Accountability has to be checked per named institution and issue, not as a national score. More
> published information is a budget for more attempts, not evidence that a verdict was reached.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that press freedom is the ground of accountability (it is a channel);
> that a press-freedom index or an approval rating settles anything; that more outlets are necessarily
> more independence; that any specific medium is good or bad regardless of the failure being traced;
> or that there is a universal percentage by which these two variables change accountability. The
> framework returns a shape, not a number.
>
> **What would refute the output** (written down before I sent this):
> 1. the three conditions collapse to one latent variable, measured, so one lever clears every
>    intersection;
> 2. a national score moves while every named slot's channel-to-sanction chain is shown intact;
> 3. a media metric settles a contest while the underlying act is held fixed and coverage pushed;
> 4. equalising access closes the gap with benefit-from-exposure and weighting held fixed;
> 5. a keeper with a protectable stake settles a contest against the subject's own reading, with the
>    stake held constant;
> 6. publication volume alone moves the ground verdict on a slot with the other conditions fixed.
>
> If any of these holds, I will retract or narrow the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is
> more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the links. The figures, not my summary, are the
> authority.
> - Figures and formula, public repository: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical figures README: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - HKS Misinformation Review, open access, where the information-environment structure question is
>   argued empirically: https://misinforeview.hks.harvard.edu/article/how-can-approaches-from-physical-ecology-help-research-and-policy-on-the-information-environment/
>
> **If you want to reuse the method:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on an accountability
> problem you can check against a ground, write down what would refute your result *before* you run
> it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported
> success.
>
> Isaiah
