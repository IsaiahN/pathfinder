# Union structure, dependency order, and the isomer question

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the order rule. Simulated derivation
checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).**

## RESIDUAL

The question "how do media systems and information inequality affect accountability quality?" hides
three asking-orders that give different answers:

1. Is it a **partition** (two independent levers with additive effects) or a **union** of overlapping
   conditions?
2. If it is a union, what is the **dependency order** of the links, and which link actually breaks?
3. Are two media regimes that solve the same failure **isomers**, or only members of a **functional
   group**?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 :  Partition or union?

- Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- Fig 9 :57: "a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- The intersections are non-empty: capture (D1) skews distribution (D2); unequal distribution (D2)
  weakens sanction (D3); a keeper who benefits (D3) corrupts the record D1 reports. A media-only
  lever clears the D1-only region and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :56).
- ⇒ **Union.** Verdict `specified`, triangulated (Fig 9 plus the political-economy and
  mass-communication sources as one corroborating shape, not two witnesses).
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** one lever measured to clear the D1∩D2, D1∩D3 and D2∩D3
  intersections without touching the others.

### Q2 :  Dependency order, and where the chain breaks

- Fig 3 :20: "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
- Ordered chain for one slot: `record (anchor role) → channel → discriminating public → verdict and
  sanction`. Each link consumes what the one above produces.
- Fig 3 :25–27: the middle link, having terms for what a situation affords and being able to say what
  counts as progress, has neither obvious failures nor obvious tests.
- ⇒ A sanction reform (say, a new oversight committee) placed above a broken channel is "motion, no
  progress" (Fig 7 :32). The measurement effort usually lands above the break because that is where
  the instruments already exist (Fig 3 :29–30).
- *Accountability* supplies the operational test at the last link: "Governments are 'accountable' if
  voters can discern whether governments are acting in their interest and sanction them appropriately".
  Most indices measure the first half badly and the second half not at all.
- ⇒ **The chain usually breaks between channel and objective, and that link has no instrument.**
  Verdict `general` on the chain rule, `specified` on this domain, single-source figure side.
- **Falsifier:** a working instrument for the middle link in a named polity, with the chain still
  breaking above it.

### Q3 :  Are two media regimes isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible
  in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :124: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says
  what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Fix the atom set `{independent record, channel with differing closure, discriminating public,
  refusal-capable seat}`. Regime P uses a public-service broadcaster as the channel; regime N uses a
  distributed network of small outlets as the channel (Benkler's "Networked Fourth Estate": "a
  diverse set of actors instead of a small number of major presses"). Same atoms, different mediator,
  different behaviour: **isomers** under Fig 12.
- If instead the regimes differ only in that one has the four-atom core and the other lacks the
  refusal-capable seat, they are not isomers. They are different molecules, and the missing atom is
  an import obligation (Fig 6), not a rearrangement.
- *Fourth estate* also records Benkler's 2011 "Free Irresponsible Press" worry, so diversity of
  actors does not fix the outcome by itself. The bond to the non-updating record is the difference,
  and the bond is the part a flat formula omits (Fig 12 :5).
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a regime can be written with a different atom set and
  still produce the same sanctioned correction, the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group
  claim (same core, different members) stands.
- Verdict `specified`, with the fixed-atom condition named as a live strain and not hidden.

### Q4 :  What order is the union after unbundling?

- Fig 9 :54: "when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- Unbundled order: D1 anchor-role and channel test; D2 distribution and weighting test; D3 refusal and
  sanction test; then the intersections in the order D1∩D2, D1∩D3, D2∩D3, then the triple. Fig 9 :57
  says the residue is "categorically different", so the triple is not a harder D1 case.
- Fig 7 :36: "A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match
  the residual it surfaced." The next check must have low overlap with the previous one; rereading
  the same index under a new name is motion.
- Verdict `general` on the order, `specified` on the application.

### Q5 :  Two currencies, and why a composite index is the wrong object

- Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."
- Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."
- Press-freedom and information indices price **bits**: diversity of outlets, access, literacy scores.
  The accountability verdict prices **actions**: whether a named failure was documented, taken to a
  seat that could refuse, and sanctioned. A composite index sums the two and "will not notice the
  shortfall" (Fig 12 :127).
- Fig 1 :27: "They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."
  An index produced from the same readings it grades is exactly this.
- **Falsifier:** a single measure in one unit that predicts both the description cost and the action
  cost with no residual left over, on held-out slots. Verdict `specified`, triangulated across Fig 1,
  Fig 9 and Fig 12.

## CONCLUSION

The honest structure is a **union of three overlapping conditions with a dependency order**, not a
partition and not an additive scalar. The chain breaks at the channel-to-objective link, which has no
instrument. Over a fixed atom set, a public-service channel and a networked channel that solve the
same failure are isomers: same atoms, different bond, different behaviour. Without the fixed atom set
they are only members of a functional group, and a missing atom is an import obligation, not a
rearrangement. Description cost and action cost are two readings held apart; summing them into one
accountability index is the named error, not the fix.

This rescues what is defensible in the ordinary question (media structure and information
distribution both matter, and matter differently at different links) without inventing a universal
coefficient or a standing list of good outlets. `metric = anchor` and `volume = verdict` remain
unproven bindings; `media regimes are isomers over a fixed four-atom set` is a checkable structural
claim.
