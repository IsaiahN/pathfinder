> **The output (`general` on the shape, `specified` on each domain, not `grounded`):** in a figure-grounded derivation, R is indexed per wealth slot, never averaged (Fig 1 :19, :22). A top-tail wealth residual is a live slot that a population mean erases. When the four candidate causes are censused as conditions rather than answers, they form a **union of overlapping conditions**, not a partition: unequal returns are a compounding cycle over a stock; unequal saving is the flow that feeds that stock; inheritance is a head start crossing a generation membrane; ownership of scarce assets is the closure that decides what can be composed at all. Each has its own anchor. They compound, so a case with inheritance and scarce assets and high returns is a specific compound, not a harder case of one.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that a variance share can tell us which of the four is "mainly" responsible. In the figures, that is a filter for the budget, not a witness for the verdict (Fig 9 :60). A share computed inside one closure cannot score that closure from outside. The framework returns a shape and an instrument that would discriminate which condition is load-bearing in a given case. It does not return a ranking. The ranking slot is a declared `gap`, recorded as unreachable rather than left as silence.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a clean partition, with every intersection among the four conditions empty;
> 2. variation in exactly one condition leaving the top-tail residual unchanged across two independent administrative datasets;
> 3. a verbatim figure line that selects one of the four as the main cause;
> 4. a zero or negative feedback sign in the relevant wealth process, so the top tail does not amplify.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, I retract the union claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
