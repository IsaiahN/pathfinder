# Union, cycle and order: the supporting derivation for ECN-0058

**Mode: `specified` on the structure, `general` on the union/partition rule.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The main audit (`01`, F2) concluded that the four candidate causes form a union of overlapping conditions. Three questions are owed before that conclusion can be used: (1) under Fig 12's definitions, are the four **isomers**, members of one **functional group**, or something else; (2) is the compounding account a **cycle** or a one-shot composition; (3) in what **order** must the conditions be enumerated, and does that order claim more than the figures license?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are the four conditions isomers?

Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
Fig 12 :123: **"A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

As the question writes them, the four candidates are not isomers. "Unequal returns", "unequal saving", "inheritance" and "scarce-asset ownership" do not share an atom set: a return is a rate, saving is a flow, inheritance is a transfer, scarcity is a property of a stock. Under Fig 12 :117 they are not even candidates for isomerism, because isomerism requires identical atoms.

The stronger form is worth stating. Take a fixed atom set `{person, claim, period, cohort}` and vary only the arrangement:

- `person + claim -> (return cycle) -> person` (returns),
- `income - consumption -> claim` (saving, the subtraction bond),
- `cohort -> claim` across the membrane (inheritance, a head start),
- `claim ∈ closure` with fixed supply (scarce ownership, a reachability condition).

Same atoms, different bonds, different wealth trajectories. Under Fig 12 :117 these **are** isomers, and two people with the same ingredients can end at opposite tails of the distribution because one arrangement closes the compounding loop and the other does not. This is the claim worth testing. It is `specified`, not `grounded`, and it is not a claim that any one arrangement is "mainly" responsible.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if any two of the four arrangements are shown to be the same mechanism under two names (an identity bond ≡, Operators table :23-26), the isomer claim fails and the four collapse to three or fewer conditions.

### Q2 - Cycle or one-shot return?

Fig 12 :121: **"The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**
Fig 13 :22: **"a difference that can be spent"**
Fig 13 :31: **"a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos"**

Wealth is spendable, so it has the gradient's directedness. When output re-enters as input, the composition is a cycle. A linear chain cannot close by construction, so a one-shot "return on a fixed starting stock" account cannot generate a persistent top tail on its own; it needs the loop to re-enter. The sign of the loop is the quantity that decides whether the tail amplifies. This is a structural claim. Fagereng et al.'s measured heterogeneity and persistence are a separate, world-facing reading, and the two are not added together (Fig 12 :125: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**).

### Q3 - What order, and what does the order commit to?

Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."**
Fig 3 :22-23: *"A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can. Which is why a reading taken below the break is not a weak reading. It is a reading of nothing."*

Dependency order for this question: closure of scarce claims (what can be composed) -> transfer across the generation membrane (who gets a head start) -> saving and return flows (how the stock grows) -> measurement (which link is load-bearing). This is a **dependency** order: each link consumes what the one above produces. It is not a claim that every rich person passed through all four in that temporal sequence.

**Strain named, not hidden.** Writing the order with → (sequence) would commit to temporal ordering. Fig 13 :75 writes composition with commas: *"It is not space-time but space, a structure, time"*. Here the figures' own dependency order is about diagnosis, not chronology. I therefore write the order with arrows only in the sense of Fig 3 ("ordered by dependency") and explicitly refuse the temporal reading. If a reader requires → as sequence, the claim becomes stronger than the figure supports and is retracted to a comma-list.

### Q4 - Union or partition, restated structurally

Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
Fig 9 :56: **"A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**
Fig 9 :57-58: **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."**

Structurally, the four conditions are four bonds, not four values of one variable. Removing returns leaves a person with a bequest and a scarce claim whose wealth still grows, more slowly. Removing the bequest leaves a person with a claim and a return whose starting stock is smaller. Each single lever clears only the region where one condition binds. The residue is smaller and categorically different. That is why the question's multiple-choice framing must be unbundled before any search for "the main cause".

## CONCLUSION

The four candidates are not isomers as written, but over a fixed atom set they form a genuine isomer claim with a stated falsifier. The returns account is a cycle, not a one-shot composition, and the cycle's sign is the quantity that decides concentration. The conditions must be enumerated in dependency order (closure, transfer, flow, measurement), with the temporal reading of that order explicitly refused. The union reading is structural, not merely rhetorical: each condition is a different bond, and removing one leaves the intersections standing. All of this is `specified`, ungrounded, and checked only for internal consistency against the figures.
