# Outreach draft — check the output first, then decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim and the five refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` sections 3 and 4 are stated in the recipient's own field terms. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.

**Standing:** this draft describes a simulated derivation checked against a framework, not a ground reading. Say that in the first paragraph. Do not let a recipient think a measurement was taken.

---

**Subject:** Check one structural claim about cultural division before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at. This is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against a figure set, not a measurement, and I have no data of my own to show you.
>
> **The residual (general shape, not grounded):** take an unfamiliar culture and ask how it divides time, space, nature and social relationships. The gap is per slot: the divisions inherited from the analyst's own language fail to carve the new culture at the joints insiders use. The gap is not a single "cultural distance" score. The four slots overlap, so one lever moves the intersection it touches and leaves the others standing.
>
> **The claim I want checked:** these four domains are not four extra kinds. A figure set I am testing holds six terms in two kinds, states (space, time, energy) and structures (spectrum, gradient, network), with composition running across kinds and never within one. Time and space are two of those states. Social relationships are the network structure. Nature is a compound over energy plus ordering and directedness. The figures say the six are indivisible and the set is closed while the arrangements over it are not. So what varies across cultures is the typed bonds over a fixed axis set, not the axes themselves. Continuity versus discreteness, which spatial axis is imported to talk about time, where a color boundary falls, and which kin positions are merged are all arrangement choices or composition remainders, not new primitives.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that I can tell you which division a particular culture uses. The framework returns a shape, not a name. A name would have to come from a behavioral measurement, not from more derivation. I am also not claiming that any published effect in this area is settled: one well-known time-metaphor result has a published failure to replicate, and I record that rather than hiding it.
>
> **What would refute the claim, written before I sent this:**
> 1. A division of one of these domains that cannot be composed from the six held terms and is not a compound of them.
> 2. A division shown invariant across all sampled cultures under a behavioral anchor that does not move when people relabel it.
> 3. A division that is a direct bond of two states or two structures with no intervening opposite-kind term.
> 4. A claimed cross-domain mapping shown to be a post hoc analyst gloss with no behavioral correlate once the analyst's narrative is removed.
> 5. Two supposedly independent informant or fieldworker frames shown to draw on one elicitation protocol and one shared narrative.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the affected claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Figures with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`, cite as `Fig N :line`.
> - Canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`.
> - The eight-step loop and mode rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`.
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the figures link carries the generator. Run it on a problem you know and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [your name]
