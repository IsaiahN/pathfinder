## 1. The residual it answers

An analyst meeting an unfamiliar culture has a per-slot gap: the inherited divisions do not carve that culture's time, space, nature and social relationships where insiders carve them. The gap is indexed by slot (Fig 1 :19), never pooled into a cultural distance score. The four slots are a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition (Fig 9 :54-55): time is divided through space, social relations through spatial and hierarchical terms, nature through temporal order. One lever moves its intersection and leaves the others standing.

## 2. The shape

1. **The four domains are windows onto one fixed six-term substrate, not four extra kinds.** Time and space are two of the held states; social relationships are the held network structure; nature is a compound over energy plus the ordering and directedness structures. Fig 13 :35 and :41 make the axes indivisible and non-redundant. No culture contributes a seventh.
2. **What varies is the bond, not the atom.** Fig 12 :5: the bond is the half that an ingredient list omits. Which spatial axis is imported for time, where a color boundary is drawn, which kin positions are merged: these are typed relations over held terms. Continuity and discreteness are not different primes; Fig 13 :124 says they are what a composition leaves behind.
3. **The union is ordered and the verdicts stay separate.** Cross-domain mappings run from a source domain to a target (spatial terms used for time), so Fig 12 :46's ordered bonds apply, not symmetric ones. Per-domain verdicts are never averaged.
4. **A disagreement is settled by behavior that does not move.** Fig 2 :29: the anchor is whatever does not change because a frame thinks differently. For a question about people, human behavior is the legitimate anchor (Fig 2 :37-38), but what people *say* about their own divisions is an instrument that can be wrong on the axis it does not measure. Fig 2 :35: agreement is not a verdict.
5. **The culture-specific name is a gap, not a silence.** Fig 9 :32: the framework returns a kind of thing, never the answer itself. It says variation is bond-level over a fixed set; it does not say that a named people divides time vertically. That name is owed to a behavioral instrument.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that any named culture divides time, space, nature or social relationships in a specific way. The name is a gap (Fig 9 :32).
- Not that a global "cultural cognition style" is a verdict. That is an aggregate over slots (Fig 1 :22) and a filter for the budget, never a witness (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that language determines thought. The world evidence supports a shaping effect and also contains a published failure to replicate; the figure verdict does not depend on either.
- Not that the six-term set is nature's own list. Declaring it makes a choice visible; it does not make it neutral. Another sensorium's minimum set need not be these six (canonical README).
- Not `grounded`. No cross-cultural measurement was taken.

## 5. What would settle it — the ground

A discriminating instrument, not another argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a reading (a color term, a kin term, a self-report) "cannot comment on the source"; an instrument that cannot distinguish sources "demonstrates its own inadequacy." The corresponding questions:

- **Axis test:** does the time division follow the vocabulary's spatial axis under a non-spatial target, and does it disappear when the analyst's narrative is removed? A preregistered replication resolves the published conflict.
- **Frame test:** do absolute-frame speakers code rotated arrays by cardinal bearing rather than by left/right, with compass-checked pointing as the anchor?
- **Foci versus boundary test:** do color foci cluster across languages while boundaries vary locally? That discriminates held atoms from arrangement.
- **Kin-grid test:** which feature dimensions (gender, generation, relative age, lineality) are encoded in each terminology, and do two frames using independent codings agree when mapped to the grid?
- **Standing falsifiers:** a seventh kind; an invariant division under a non-updating anchor; a direct state-state or structure-structure bond; a mapping shown to be a post hoc gloss; two independent frames shown to share one protocol.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The instruments named above exist, but none was run here. Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." The set of six is a visible guess at the compression axes; a name is owed to contact with a ground, not to further derivation. The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable. "Indiscernibility is not identity."

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The figures do not rank cultures and do not name a people's categories. They say that the four domains the question names are windows onto one fixed set of six terms: space, time and energy as states, spectrum, gradient and network as structures, with composition running across the two kinds and never within one. Time and space are already in the set, social relationships are the network structure, and nature is a compound over energy and ordering and directedness. What varies across cultures is therefore the typed bonds over those axes, not the axes themselves: which spatial axis is imported to talk about time, where a color boundary falls, which kin relations are merged. Continuity and discreteness are what a composition leaves behind, not extra primitives. The four slots overlap, so the verdicts stay separate rather than collapsing into a cultural style score, and a disagreement is settled by behavior that does not move when an informant or analyst relabels it. The framework returns that shape and refuses the culture-specific name; the name is owed to a discriminating instrument and is recorded here as a gap. This is a simulated derivation, not a ground reading, and it cannot settle which division any particular culture actually uses.
