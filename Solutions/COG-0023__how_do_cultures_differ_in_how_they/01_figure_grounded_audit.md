## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

The question asks for the axes along which cultures differ when they "divide up" four domains. Restated as an effect: an analyst who meets an unfamiliar culture has a per-slot prediction gap. The categories inherited from the analyst's own language and institutions fail to carve that culture's time, space, nature and social relationships at the joints insiders use. The residual R is the gap between the inherited division and the division that actually governs reasoning and conduct in that community. It is indexed per slot, never pooled into a single "cultural distance" (Fig 1 :19, :22).

- **Arity.** Four named slots in the question: time, space, nature, social relationships. A genuine fifth slot, the overlaps among them, appears as soon as the cases are inspected: time is divided through space in the reported metaphors, social relations through spatial and hierarchical terms, nature through temporal/developmental sequences. So the residual has at least five live slots, not four independent ones.
- **Symmetry.** "Divide up" reads as a symmetric partition operation, but the relations between the four are ordered (space is imported to talk about time; a metaphor maps one domain onto another). The compound therefore contains ordered bonds, so it cannot be treated as a symmetric list (Fig 12 :46, :92).
- **Scale.** Three scales are involved: the individual learner acquiring distinctions, the linguistic community that stabilizes them, and the institution (calendar, taxonomy, kinship law, bureaucratic classification) that fixes them. The kernel is the same at each scale; what changes is actors, currency and anchor (Fig 4 :19-20).

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term (formula step "Direction").** Before searching, write down the sought-for shape so it can accrue a residual: "An answer states (a) which of the divisions are variations over a fixed set of axes rather than new axes, (b) which bonds carry the variation, (c) which anchor would settle a disagreement about a division, and (d) which part is a name the framework cannot supply." This binding is written before the findings so that the preference is refusable by the same test as everything else (Fig 5 :62-66).

## 1. Domain census, before any figure line is chosen

One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor. No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.

| slot | actors | currency (what is exchanged or settled) | anchor (non-updating) | nameable? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 time | speakers, calendars, temporal metaphors, bilinguals | distinctions that change non-linguistic timing and ordering judgments | behavioral priming and ordering tasks that do not move when the informant relabels them | yes |
| D2 space | speakers, wayfinders, gesture | navigation, memory coding, direction accuracy | compass-checked pointing and rotated-array memory tests | yes |
| D3 nature | speakers, folk taxonomies, color lexicons, rare categories | category foci and boundaries, living-kind partitions | color-chip focusing and sorting tasks; expert taxonomy | yes |
| D4 social relationships | speakers, kinship terminologies, descent groups | which relations merge or split, and the obligations that follow | genealogical-grid mapping of terms across languages; observed conduct | yes |
| D5 overlaps | metaphor mappings, hierarchies, developmental sequences | the cross-domain bond itself | discriminating tasks that separate mapping from domain | yes |

**Partition or union.** Union, not partition (Fig 9 :54-55). D1 is partly carried by spatial metaphors, D4 by spatial and hierarchical terms, D3 by temporal/developmental order. A lever that changes only the space slot leaves the social and time intersections standing, because the second condition still binds. Verdicts are therefore kept per domain and never averaged into "culture A is more analytic than culture B."

**Dependency order (Fig 3 :20).** The chain is ordered by dependency, not importance. (1) Perception: can the observer tell that a division exists and differs (Fig 3 :4-5). (2) Vocabulary: are there terms for what the distinction affords (Fig 3 :6-8). (3) Objective: can the observer say what would count as progress, i.e. what observation would confirm that a division is behavioral rather than lexical (Fig 3 :9-11). (4) Probe: a comparison task designed under incomplete information (Fig 3 :12-14). (5) Carry: name what worked, verify it, carry it to the next culture (Fig 3 :15-16). The chain usually breaks at link 3, because having terms for what a situation affords is "a dictionary" treated as "a grammar" (Fig 12 :115). A link with no instrument is not a link that works (Fig 3 :27).

**Strain test applied during the census.** My first pass tried to assign "nature" to a seventh kind. It only fits under a strain, so per the strain rule it governs another quantity. I re-census it as a compound over the held energy state plus the ordering and directedness structures (Fig 13 :19-24, :74). That re-census is preserved in section 7 rather than silently erased.

## 3. Findings F1 to F9

### F1 — The four domains map onto the held six-term set, not onto new kinds (general, triangulated)

Fig 13 names the substrate directly:

> **"Space, time and energy are not three readings of one quantity. Neither are spectrum, gradient and network."** (Fig 13 :35)

> **"cannot: adjacency is not duration, an ordering is not a pathway, a pathway is not a direction."** (Fig 13 :36)

The six are listed as states space ("near, far, between, contained", Fig 13 :10), time ("the ordered succession of differences", Fig 13 :12), energy ("conserved, finite, directionless alone", Fig 13 :14), and structures spectrum ("ordering, so more and less can be said", Fig 13 :20), gradient ("a difference that can be spent", Fig 13 :22), network ("that entities relate, and influence travels", Fig 13 :24).

Mapping the question's four domains onto these:

| question domain | held term(s) | what is not new |
|---|---|---|
| time | time (state) | the ordered succession of differences is already a primitive |
| space | space (state) | near/far/between/contained is already a primitive |
| nature | energy (state) + spectrum (ordering) + gradient (directedness) | a compound of held terms, not a new kind |
| social relationships | network (structure) | "that entities relate, and influence travels" is already a primitive |

The six are indivisible: "Each is indivisible: no arrangement of the other five yields it." (Fig 13 :41). And the set is closed: "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists." (Fig 13 :115). So cross-cultural variation cannot be variation in the kinds themselves. If a purported new division cannot be composed from the six, it is either a compound not yet parsed or a genuine new kind, and the figures say the second would break minimality (Fig 13 :143).

**Mode `general`, triangulated** across Fig 13 (substrate) and Fig 2 :38 (the independence condition applied to the set). World corroboration is secondary: the recurring universals in color foci (Regier et al. 2005) and the near-universal one-dimensional directional time metaphor (Boroditsky 2001) both fit a fixed, non-arbitrary axis set. That is corroboration, not the source of the verdict.

**Strain, named:** mapping "nature" onto energy plus spectrum plus gradient is a *binding* from the folk-biology frame onto held atoms. It is not a derivation of nature from the six. Naming the binding as provenance is required by Fig 8 :50-52, which says convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents and only the record separates them.

### F2 — The variation is in the bonds, not the atoms (specified, triangulated)

Fig 12 states what the ingredient list omits:

> **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."** (Fig 12 :5)

> **"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."** (Fig 12 :46)

> **"The arrangement is the substance."** (Fig 12 :94)

Fig 13 closes the set but leaves the arrangements open (Fig 13 :115-118). It also says that the properties people often treat as different kinds of time are by-products of arrangement:

> **"Smoothness, symmetry, chaos and discreteness are each what a particular composition leaves behind, and none is a seventh member."** (Fig 13 :124)

So "a culture divides time into discrete units" versus "a culture treats time as continuous" is not a difference of primes. It is a difference in what a composition leaves behind. The same goes for "a culture splits cousins" versus "a culture merges them": the network atom is held, the typed relation is what varies. Fig 12 :111 warns that "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one," which is exactly the failure of an inventory-only answer to this question.

**Mode `specified`, triangulated** across Fig 12 (bond definition) and Fig 13 (:115, :124). The world prediction is that differences will concentrate in *relations over* the axes (which axis is imported to talk about time, which relations are merged in a kin term, where a color boundary is drawn) while focal points and axis inventory stay constrained. That is what the sources report.

### F3 — The question is a union of overlapping conditions, not a partition (general, triangulated)

Fig 9 :54-55:

> **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**

> **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**

The evidence that the four are not independent: spatial terms are imported to divide time (Boroditsky 2001), spatial and hierarchical terms organize social relations (Nisbett et al. 2001; kinship typologies), and developmental or seasonal order organizes nature. One lever, such as teaching vertical time metaphors, moves the time/space intersection but leaves the nature and social intersections standing.

**Mode `general`, triangulated** across Fig 9 and the metaphor/mapping world sources. This is the reason the answer is a separator with four domain verdicts, not a single "cultural cognition style" number.

### F4 — The residual is indexed by slot; a culture-level average destroys it (general, single-source on the figure side)

Fig 1 :19 and :22:

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**

> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

A global statement such as "East Asian cultures are holistic and Western cultures analytic" is exactly the aggregate the figure forbids for R. It can be a useful *filter* for where to look (a budget device), never a *witness* for what a culture does at a slot. Fig 9 :60: "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."

**Mode `general`, single-source on the figure side** (Fig 1 alone governs per-slot indexing). It is reinforced by the world result that the same population can group by relation in one task and by category in another (Nisbett et al. 2001), which is a pool-level warning rather than a second figure.

### F5 — The anchor is behavior that does not move when the frame relabels it (specified, triangulated)

Fig 2 :29-30:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."**

Fig 2 :31-32:

> **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."**

And crucially for a question about people, Fig 2 :37-38:

> **"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."**

The question "how do cultures divide X" is constitutively about what people do, so human behavior is the legitimate anchor. But *what a speaker says about their own divisions* is only an instrument: it can be wrong on the axis it does not measure (non-linguistic reasoning). A self-report that changes when the interviewer reframes the question is a frame, not an anchor (Fig 2 :33-34). The non-updating ground is the behavioral task: response time, pointing accuracy, category focusing.

**Mode `specified`, triangulated** across Fig 2 (:29, :37-38) and Fig 10 :52 ("Bring a characterised residual, never the source"). Fig 2 :35: "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you." An informant and analyst who share one narrative are one frame, not two.

### F6 — Independence is the scarce quantity; shared pretext is the failure mode (specified, triangulated)

Fig 8 :48:

> **"How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."**

Fig 13 :38-39 applies the same condition to the axis set:

> **"independence is how little two frames draw on the same evidence"**

> **"makes their agreement mean anything. Six members that overlapped would be fewer members wearing six names."**

The essay *Decoding Tacit Knowledge* (read this session) makes the same point for precisely this subject: culture is "a shared backdrop, Collins's collective tacit knowledge," and "Shared pretext is good for one thing and ruins another. Agreement is the whole point when you're settling what words mean. It's worthless for settling what's true when both sides learned from the same sources." A bilingual informant who shares both the analyst's and the community's narrative is the "one frame wearing two names" case (Fig 8 :48-49). Two fieldworkers using the same elicitation manual and the same translated stimuli are also one pool. This is why the ledger records overlap per source and why the world conflict in F8 matters.

**Mode `specified`, triangulated** across Fig 8, Fig 13 and the tacit-knowledge essay. **Strain handled:** Fig 2 :37 says human judgment is the right anchor for a question about people, while Fig 2 :40 says agreement is disqualifying for a verdict. Re-census dissolves it: *whose* judgment is the anchor is decided by the question's object (behavior about divisions), and *agreement between two frames drawing on one narrative* is what is disqualifying. Different quantities. No figure is overruled.

### F7 — The framework returns a shape; the culture-specific name is a declared gap (general, gap)

Fig 9 :32: "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :134: "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,". The canonical README states the limit as "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)".

The shape this derivation returns:

1. The axes are the fixed, indivisible six. No culture adds a seventh; a new-looking division is a compound over held terms (F1).
2. Cross-cultural variation is carried by typed bonds over those terms (F2).
3. The four named domains are a union with overlaps, so verdicts stay per slot (F3, F4).
4. A disagreement about a division is settled by a non-updating behavioral ground, never by negotiation among informants (F5, F6).

What it cannot return: which axis a particular culture imports for time, which kin relations it merges, where it draws a color boundary. Those are names. They are owed to measurement. Fig 13 :134 is explicit that the framework "cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it." Here the missing thing is not a primitive but a binding, a culture-specific arrangement; the point is the same.

**`gap` declared, of the shape-not-a-name kind, not unreachable in principle.** The instruments exist (priming, pointing, focusing, term mapping). Nobody ran them here. Reported as a gap rather than silence, per Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."

**World conflict recorded, not smoothed.** Boroditsky (2001) reports that Mandarin speakers think about time vertically even when tested in English, and that brief training of English speakers in vertical time talk reproduces the pattern. Chen (2001, PubMed 17070793) found that Chinese speakers actually use horizontal spatial metaphors more often than vertical ones and failed to replicate the effect in four attempts. Per the method, a conflict sends us back to the census and never flips the verdict. Re-census result: the *shape* claim (a spatial axis is imported to divide time; the choice is a bond over held states) survives both papers, since both accept the import and dispute only which axis and whether the behavioral effect holds. The *magnitude and direction* claim is recorded as contested, with the ground (a preregistered, sufficiently powered replication) still owed. This is a genuine low-overlap conflict, and it is honest to say the world evidence here is weaker than a clean corroboration would be.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Per the seat map, "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Seventh-kind failure.** A cultural division of time, space, nature or social relations is demonstrated that cannot be composed from the six held terms and is not a compound of them. This breaks Fig 13 :41 (indivisibility) and Fig 13 :115 (closed set).
2. **Invariance failure.** Under a non-updating behavioral anchor, a division previously claimed variable is shown invariant across all sampled cultures. The "variation is in the bonds" claim then fails for that slot.
3. **Composition failure.** A division is shown to be a direct bond of two states or two structures with no intervening opposite-kind term at first order. This breaks Fig 13 :75.
4. **Mapping failure.** A mapping claimed as cross-domain (for example space imported to talk about time) is shown to be a post hoc analyst gloss with no behavioral correlate once the analyst's narrative is removed. The shape stands; that particular world corroboration falls and is marked `single-source`.
5. **Independence failure.** Two supposedly independent informant or fieldworker frames are shown to draw on one elicitation protocol and one shared narrative. Their agreement is reclassified as one pool talking (Fig 2 :40), not as corroboration.

If any fires, the affected claim is withdrawn and the retraction log below is updated. Until one is run, no part of this is `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Lead with the shape and the refuter, not with any named culture or any claim about a particular people. The claim that survives is structural.
2. Keep the four domain verdicts separate (F3, F4). Do not publish a "cultural cognition index."
3. Treat self-report as an instrument and behavior as the anchor (F5). A division counts only if it shows up where the informant cannot narrate it.
4. State mode on every sentence. A `gap` is a verdict (F7), not an invitation to fill the slot with the analyst's favorite culture.
5. Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong (Fig 2 :42): independent stimulus sets, independent stimulus translation, and at least one frame that did not read this folder.
6. Offer the generator (the residual, the census and the refuter), not a recording of one convenience sample (Fig 4 :22, :25).

## 7. Strains and re-census

- **S1, nature as a seventh kind.** Dissolved by re-census (F1, log). Strain was the signal that it governed a compound.
- **S2, symmetric "divide up" versus ordered metaphor mapping.** Dissolved: the partition reads as symmetric but the actual relations are ordered bonds (Fig 12 :46, :92).
- **S3, human judgment as anchor versus agreement as disqualifying.** Dissolved by separating the object of the judgment from the pool of the agreeing frames (F6).
- **S4 (live), the operand type of a cross-domain mapping.** The figures permit a structure to mediate between two states at first order (Fig 13 :75), and the world literature describes spatial terms being imported into the time domain. Whether the mapping is a bond over held atoms or an import of a new atom is not decidable from the figure text alone. An import would need a source gate and a shadow test (Fig 8 :50-52). Named as a live type question, not hidden.
- **S5 (live), world replication conflict.** Boroditsky 2001 versus Chen 2001 is kept as evidence that the time slot's magnitude claim is unsettled. It does not flip the figure verdict.

After re-census: two live strains (S4, S5), three dissolved strains, none hidden.

## 8. Updated verdict

The shape survives. The four domains are windows onto one fixed six-term substrate: time and space are held states, social relations the held network structure, nature a compound over energy and the ordering and directedness structures. Cultural variation is carried by the typed bonds over that substrate, and continuity, discreteness, axis choice and kin-term merger are all composition remainders or arrangement choices rather than new kinds. Verdicts stay per slot and are never averaged. The anchor is non-updating behavior, not informant agreement inside a shared narrative. The framework returns the shape and refuses the culture-specific name; that name is a `gap` owed to measurement. Mode `general` on the shape, `specified` on the four domain verdicts, `gap` on the name, nothing `grounded`. This folder is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading, and it cannot settle the question it reports on.
