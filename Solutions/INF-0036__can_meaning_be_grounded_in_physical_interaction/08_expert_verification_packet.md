# Expert verification packet - the Tether on grounded meaning

**What this is:** a claim in a form a cognitive scientist, semanticist, animal-behaviour researcher or philosopher of mind can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the structural claim, `specified` on any system-facing claim. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A human-like interpreter cannot be the anchor for what a signal is about for a system, because an anchor is whatever settles a question without changing its answer when a frame changes its mind, and an interpreter is a frame. Physical contact with the world can play that anchor role: it settles what the signal affords and what pays. A signal's content is carried by the typed bond between the signal and its consequences, so the same signal under a different bond carries different content, and no extra interpreter atom is needed to carry it. The apparent regress of interpreters is a level error, dissolved because a settled term becomes an atom at the next level with a different ground. Two caveats stay attached: shared meaning is a real product of convention but does not settle aboutness against the world, and whether a particular system has aboutness is measured by instruments for affordance-terms and objectives, not asserted by this derivation.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - anchor; checkable by reading Fig 2 and the Symbols table

- **Statement:** a frame cannot be the anchor for a question constitutively about contact with the world. A human-like interpreter is a frame. The physical ground can be the anchor. Status: `general`.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 2 :29-30: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."*
  - Fig 2 :36: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."*
  - Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*
  - Symbols table: *"A frame is any bounded system with a library: an agent, a person, a discipline, a model."*
- **What to check:** is there a case where the answer to what a signal is about for a system is fixed by an interpreter's attribution and *does not* move when the interpreter changes its mind? If yes, the anchor claim fails.
- **Refuter:** anchor reversal plus no physical contact measure settling the case (breaks Fig 2 :29-30). Also: a convention whose shared reading settles aboutness with no contact measure and does not move when the community changes (breaks Fig 10 :47).

### C2 - bond carries content; checkable against Fig 12 and a real signalling system

- **Statement:** content is carried by the typed relation between signal and consequence, not by the signal's ingredients and not by a further interpreter. Same signal, different bond, different content. Status: `general` for the rule, `specified` when applied to a given species or code.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
  - Fig 12 :94: *"The arrangement is the substance."*
  - Fig 12 :101: *"A bond that costs nothing is a bond that carries nothing, which is the flat case."*
  - Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- **What to check:** take the honeybee waggle dance (Frontiers 2015; Nature 2005; PNAS 2023). A waggle run's duration is produced by distance; its angle is a comparison against a sun-referenced bearing. The same dance carries two contents by two bonds. Is that a faithful reading of the experiments? Would a reader who only records the ingredients (duration, angle) miss the content? If the bond makes no behavioural difference, the isomer claim fails for this case.
- **Refuter:** two different bonds between the same signal and consequence produce no behavioural difference in a system with the required vocabulary and objective; or a signal carries content with no priced bond to anything it affords or produces, purely by declaration, and still passes C1.

### C3 - level-relativity; checkable by reading Fig 12 and Fig 10

- **Statement:** the regress "who interprets the interpreter" assumes each level needs the same explanation. The figures deny the premise: actors, currency and ground change at each crossing; a settled term becomes an atom for the next level. Status: `general`.
- **Source, verbatim:**
  - Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"*
  - Fig 12 :103: *"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."*
  - Fig 10 :68: *"Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between. That is why there is no top rung and no regress: a generator crosses, an instance does not."*
- **What to check:** is there a level crossing in which actors, currency and ground do not change and the transform is identity, with a human-like interpreter still required? If yes, the no-regress claim fails.
- **Refuter:** such a crossing, or a settled signal-term that cannot become an atom without a fresh human-like interpreter.

## 4. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that every physical interaction has meaning. Affordance-terms and an objective comparison are required and are measured (Fig 3 :7, :10, :27).
- Not that receivers are absent. Bees are receivers and are not human-like.
- Not that convention is corruption. Fig 2 :41: *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"*; Fig 10 :49: *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."*
- Not that a signal's shape alone determines its content (Fig 12 :117).
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken by this agent.
- Not that this derivation settles the question it reports on. It is a simulation checked for internal consistency against the figures (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: output is *"simulacra"*).

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29-30 and :36, and the Symbols table entry for "frame". Decide whether an interpreter can satisfy the anchor definition. (C1)
2. Read Fig 12 :5, :94, :101, :117. Look at one real signalling system you know (bee dance, alarm call, traffic light, molecular signal). Decide whether the bond makes a behavioural difference. (C2)
3. Read Fig 12 :103-104 and Fig 10 :68. Name one level crossing where actors, currency and ground stay the same. If you cannot, say why the regress is or is not dissolved. (C3)
4. Check the convention caveat against your own field: does shared meaning count as a product there, and does it settle anything about the world?
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
