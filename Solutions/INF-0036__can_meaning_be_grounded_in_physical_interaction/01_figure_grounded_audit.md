## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

> Can meaning be grounded in physical interaction without invoking a human-like interpreter?

Restated as an effect, with the two options and what each leads to:

- A signal is in contact with a physical system and its surroundings. Observers disagree about whether the signal *means* anything, and if so what.
- **Option A (grounded in interaction).** The fact of the matter is settled by what the physical contact does: what the signal affords the system, what consequences follow, what pays when the system acts on it. The anchor is the ground. No human-like interpreter is needed.
- **Option B (interpreter required).** The fact is settled only by something that understands the signal. Physical interaction yields correlation and causation but not aboutness. The anchor is the interpreter's reading.
- **What A leads to:** a checkable anchor, a route to test whether a given system has aboutness, and a refusal that can fire.
- **What B leads to:** either an infinite regress of interpreters interpreting interpreters, or a convention among interpreters. Fig 10 :49: *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."* Fig 10 :47: *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."* Both are forbidden as grounds by the figures.

**Binding the sought-for shape as an ordinary term** (Formula "Direction"): an answer to this question would look like (i) a nameable anchor that settles aboutness and does not move when a frame pushes on it, (ii) an account of how a signal carries its content in its arrangement rather than in an interpreter's attribution, and (iii) a stated failure condition. That shape is written down here before the figures are applied, so it can accrue a residual like anything else rather than operating as an invisible preference (Fig 5 :65-66, formula "Direction").

## 1. Domain census, done before choosing any figure line

### R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** the residual is a relation, not a property of the signal. It needs at least four places: signal, system, world or affordance, consequence. Its content fuses two conditions: (a) is there a ground for aboutness at all, and (b) is an interpreter required to supply it. Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* This is a **union compound**, so each condition gets its own loop and its own ground. It is not one hard question and the verdicts must not be averaged.
- **Symmetry:** aboutness is ordered (this signal is about X for this system), and physical contact has direction (affordance, production, consequence). The figures' ordered bonds are the candidates: `→` sequence, `⇒` production, `−` subtraction, `⋛` comparison. The symmetric bonds (`+`, `∥`, `≡`) flatten exactly the difference the question asks about. So R is asymmetric, and any answer that treats "is correlated with" as equivalent to "means" has already changed the subject.
- **Scale:** a settled signal-term becomes an atom for whatever composes over it (Fig 12 :103-104). "Interpreter" is a role at a level, not a prerequisite at every level. Requiring one at every level is the regress the figures resolve by level-relativity.

### One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | nameable anchor? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1, coding / information frame** | signals, codes, channels, entropy, mutual information, decoders sharing a code | bits, compressibility, statistical correlation | none for meaning. A shared code fixes what a decoder recovers, not what the signal is about | **no** for meaning. Not a domain for this residual until an anchor is supplied from outside |
| **D2, physical interaction / ground** | systems in contact, affordances, consequences, instruments, habitat | residual `R = |Γ(b,a) − o'|` per slot, what pays, survival | the ground: settles the question and does not update | **yes** (Fig 2 :29-30) |
| **D3, interpreter / convention** | interpreters, vocabularies, communities, conventions, shared readings | agreement, shared meaning, convention | an interpreter's reading, which moves with the frames | **no** as a ground. A convention can be an anchor for "what do we mean by this word here," not for "is this about the world" |
| **D4, composition / structure** | atoms, bonds, molecules, arrangements, typed relations | two currencies kept apart: bits price the description, actions price finding out whether it holds | the bond carries information the operands do not (Fig 12 :5) | **yes** (Fig 12 :94) |

### Partition or union

D2 and D3 overlap in D4: an interpreter is itself a composed system, and a signal's content is carried by a bond to its consequences. That overlap is a **compound with its own loop**, not a subcase of either. D1 is upstream substrate, not a domain for meaning: its anchor is missing for this residual, and a domain without a nameable anchor is not yet a domain for the question (Fig 2 anchor test, Fig 13 :134-135).

### Dependency order (Fig 3)

1. **Contact / perception** (Fig 3 :4-5): the system can tell what is in front of it and tell it apart from what was there before. No contact, no residual.
2. **Vocabulary** (Fig 3 :7): *"it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked."* This is the middle link that usually breaks (Fig 3 :27).
3. **Objective** (Fig 3 :10): *"it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms."* Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."*
4. **Convention** last: conventions are built over terms that have already been settled; a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one (Fig 10 :47).
5. **Refuse before mint** (Formula step 2): refuse the interpreter-as-anchor before trying to mint any account of meaning.

## 3. Findings

### F1 - The anchor test decides what may ground meaning (decisive for D2; `general`)

Fig 2 :29-30:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome. If it moves when you push on it, it is not an anchor."**

Fig 2 :36:

> **"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."**

Fig 2 :37-38:

> **"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."**

A human-like interpreter is a frame: a bounded system with a library (Symbols table). If "what this signal means" changes when the interpreter changes its mind, the interpreter is not an anchor for that question; it is panel 1 of Fig 2, the mutual-update collapse. The question here is constitutively about what the signal does in contact with the system and world, not about what an interpreter reports (Fig 13 :26: *"it decides what the measurement would mean"*). So the interpreter can be an instrument, and an instrument can be wrong on axes it does not measure. The physical ground is the anchor because it settles and does not update.

**Refuter:** show a case where pushing on a frame (changing the interpreter's attribution) changes the answer to what the signal is about for the system, and no physical contact measure settles it. That makes the interpreter the anchor and breaks Fig 2 :29-30.

**Triangulation:** triangulated, low overlap between Fig 2's anchor definition and Fig 13's structure-decides-meaning clause (two figures, different quantities).

### F2 - Declaring a meaning does not ground it (`general`)

Fig 10 :49:

> **"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."**

Fig 10 :18:

> **"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"**

Fig 10 :47 and Fig 2 :43:

> **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**
> **"Guard, from Figure 10: a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."**

This refuses Option B in D3. An interpreter that assigns a meaning by declaration has produced a convention, not a verdict about the world. The figures do not say conventions are worthless. Fig 2 :41 says the opposite about conventions:

> **"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it"**

So the honest position is two-sided: shared meaning is a real product of agreement (Fig 2 :41), and it does not settle aboutness against the world (Fig 10 :49). Collapsing these two into "interpreters never confer meaning" would break Fig 2 :41. That is the strain test at work, and §5 records the re-census.

**Refuter:** show a convention whose shared reading, with no contact measure, settles what the signal is about in the world, and whose answer does not move when the community changes. That would make convention an anchor and break Fig 10 :47.

**Triangulation:** triangulated (Fig 10 :49 and Fig 2 :41-43, opposite halves of the same distinction read from different figures).

### F3 - The bond carries meaning; adding an interpreter as an extra atom is a free bond (`general`)

Fig 12 (title, :2) is titled *"The Bond: How Composition Carries Meaning."* Fig 12 :5:

> **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**

Fig 12 :94:

> **"The arrangement is the substance."**

Fig 12 :101:

> **"A bond that costs nothing is a bond that carries nothing, which is the flat case."**

Fig 12 :111:

> **"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."**

A signal's aboutness is carried by its typed relation to what it affords and produces, not by a further interpreter hovering over the pair. If you name an interpreter as the thing that confers meaning, you have renamed the bond as an entity while keeping its work at zero cost. Fig 12 :101 prices that move: a bond that carries nothing is the flat case, where the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not.

**Strain test, applied here rather than hidden.** Does "interpreter" fit as a bond? No. A bond is a typed relation between two operands; an interpreter is a bounded system with a library (Symbols table). A line that only fits with a strain governs another quantity. Re-census: Fig 12 governs the *arrangement* that carries content; the interpreter question is a question about an *actor in a frame*, which D3 handles. No figure line was forced.

**Refuter:** show a signal whose content is carried with no priced bond to anything it affords or produces, purely by an interpreter's say-so, and where the claim still survives the anchor test of F1. That breaks Fig 12 :101.

**Triangulation:** triangulated (Fig 12 :5, :94, :101 and the Operators table's bond definition, low overlap between the price argument and the bond definition).

### F4 - Level-relativity dissolves the interpreter regress (`general`)

Fig 12 :103-104:

> **"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."**
> **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"**

Fig 10 :68:

> **"Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between. That is why there is no top rung and no regress: a generator crosses, an instance does not."**

Once a signal-term is settled at one level, it becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. An interpreter is itself a composed system occupying a level. Requiring a human-like interpreter at every level is an *instance* (a playback) pretending to be a generator. The figures forbid the regress not by declaring it impossible in the abstract, but by changing the actors, currency and ground at each crossing, with a lossy transform between.

Fig 4 :22 adds the transfer rule:

> **"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."**

What crosses levels is a reusable method for relating signal to consequence, not a recording of one interpreter's reading.

**Refuter:** show a level at which a settled signal-term cannot serve as an atom for a higher composition without a fresh human-like interpreter, and where the transform is not lossy but identity. That breaks Fig 12 :104 and Fig 10 :68.

**Triangulation:** triangulated (Fig 12 :103-104 and Fig 10 :68, structurally independent statements of level-relativity and no-regress).

### F5 - Aboutness is agent-and-habitat, and the interpreter label is retrospective (`general`)

Fig 11 :39-40:

> **"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone"**
> **"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."**

Fig 11 :36:

> **"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran. The word does no work there."**

Fig 11 :73:

> **"The habitat is not the ground. The habitat is where actors come from and it updates; the ground is what settles, and it does not."**

Adding a human-like interpreter to a system that has no contact with the residual changes nothing; grounding changes contact. The interpreter is a label applied afterwards to a lineage that met its ground, not a precondition for meeting it. Note the habitat/ground distinction: the habitat supplies actors and is enumerated, never composed (Fig 11 :43-44); the ground settles and does not update. A search for meaning must not confuse the two.

**Refuter:** show a system whose aboutness changes when an interpreter is added, with no change in contact and no change in the habitat, and where the ground pays differently. That breaks Fig 11 :40.

**Triangulation:** triangulated (Fig 11 :36, :39-40 and Fig 2 :29-30 anchor test; one figure supplies the contact claim, another supplies the no-update claim).

### F6 - The middle link is measured, not stipulated (`specified`)

Fig 3 :7 and :10 (quoted in §1) give the two links the question actually turns on: terms for what things afford, and the ability to say what counts as progress. Fig 3 :27:

> **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

So the question "does this system have meaning" is not settled by declaring it has an interpreter or lacks one. It is settled by an instrument that can show whether the system has terms for affordances and a comparison that counts as progress. This is where mode becomes `specified`: a structural shape is `general`, but any claim about a given system is a prediction to be checked.

**Refuter:** exhibit an instrument that shows a system has aboutness with no terms for affordances and no objective comparison in its own terms, and where the ground still pays. That breaks Fig 3 :7/:10 and the dependency order in §1.

**Triangulation:** triangulated (Fig 3 :7/:10/:27 and Fig 12 :112 on goal as comparison).

### F7 - Looking for an interpreter and finding none is not a proof of absence (`general`)

Fig 8 :39:

> **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

Fig 9 :44:

> **"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."**

Fig 13 :134-135:

> **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"**
> **"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."**

If you open a system looking for a human-like interpreter and do not find one, that is not evidence there is no meaning. The interpreter-as-precondition may be the wrong shape. Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Searched first (formula step 7), then Inward and Outward kept distinct: an instrument for affordance-terms is Inward; a frame whose closure already predicts aboutness without an interpreter is Outward. Never read one against the other.

**Refuter:** produce a sealed frame in which the absence of an interpreter is proved to the end, so the search really is exhaustive. That breaks Fig 8 :39 and Fig 9 :44.

**Triangulation:** triangulated (Fig 8 :39, Fig 9 :44, Fig 13 :134-135, three separate statements with distinct arguments).

### F8 - Filters for the budget, witnesses for the verdict (`general`)

Fig 9 :60:

> **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

Avenues chosen for how receptive an audience is, a journal's impact factor, a venue's prestige, or how many people can be reached are **contact-cost filters**. They decide what gets tried first. They never enter the semantic verdict. Fig 7 :35 (*"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict"*) and Fig 2 :40 (*"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*) give the mechanism: a list selected for agreement manufactures the correlation that disqualifies agreement as evidence.

**Refuter:** show a case where a contact-cost filter (prestige, receptiveness, reach) changes the answer to what a signal means for a system, with no change in the ground. That breaks Fig 9 :60.

**Triangulation:** triangulated (Fig 9 :60 and Fig 7 :35; one names the rule, one names the failure).

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

The shape claim is: *aboutness can be anchored in physical interaction; the ground settles it; a human-like interpreter is a frame, a retrospective label or a convention, not the anchor; and no extra interpreter atom is required to carry content.*

This shape is refused if any of the following holds:

1. **Anchor reversal.** The answer to what a signal is about for a system is shown to move when a frame pushes on the interpreter's attribution, and no physical contact measure settles it (breaks Fig 2 :29-30).
2. **Contact-free settlement.** Aboutness is settled with no change in contact and no terms for what the signal affords (breaks Fig 11 :40 and Fig 3 :7).
3. **Free content.** A signal carries content with no priced bond to consequences, purely by declaration, and still passes the anchor test (breaks Fig 12 :101).
4. **Exhaustive absence.** A sealed frame is exhibited in which the absence of an interpreter is proved to the end (breaks Fig 8 :39 and Fig 9 :44).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn and the domain census is redone. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`. This refuter was written before the verdict in `07_the_tether_answer.md` and before any outreach text.

### Round-trip gap, qualitative

The condensation used in outreach is: "meaning is anchored in interaction, not in an interpreter." The round trip (Fig 4 :30: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."*) drops three constraints when brought back down:

- the convention caveat (Fig 2 :41), which the shorthand "not in an interpreter" can appear to deny;
- the requirement of vocabulary and objective (Fig 3 :7/:10), without which "interaction" is too coarse to decide anything;
- the habitat/ground distinction (Fig 11 :73), without which any environment is mistaken for an anchor.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Lead with the anchor test (F1) and the convention caveat (F2) together. The claim that interaction grounds meaning is only honest beside the claim that convention produces shared meaning without grounding it.
2. Keep verdicts per domain separate and never average them (§1, F1 to F8).
3. State mode on every sentence. The structural refusal is `general`; any system-facing claim is `specified`; nothing is `grounded`.
4. Send the generator, not the recording. Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* The generator here is the residual, the census and the refuter.
5. Use the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv` as cost filters only (F8), and ask for a refutation rather than agreement.
6. Keep the stop honest. Where no instrument exists to tell whether a system has terms for affordances, report `gap` or `specified, unmeasured` rather than filling the slot with an interpreter. *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"* (Fig 13 :134).
