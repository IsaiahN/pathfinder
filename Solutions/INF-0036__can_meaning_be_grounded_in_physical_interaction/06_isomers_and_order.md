# Bond type, recursion and analogues: the supporting derivation

**Mode: `specified` on the structural application, `general` on the bond and level rules. Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §6).**

## RESIDUAL

The main audit shows that an interpreter is a frame and not an anchor, and that content is carried by a typed relation to consequences. Two supporting questions remain:

1. **Structure:** if content is carried by the bond, what happens when the same signal is bonded differently, and can a "meaning" be duplicated or inverted without changing the signal?
2. **Order:** why does requiring a human-like interpreter at every level look like a regress, and what dissolves it?

## DOMAIN CENSUS (redone here, from the residual line alone)

- Atoms available: a signal token, a system state, a consequence, a world condition. Bonds available: `+`, `∥`, `≡` (symmetric) and `→`, `⇒`, `−`, `⋛` (ordered), plus unary negation `¬` (Operators table). No new primitive is admitted without a source gate.
- **R by arity:** the bond joins two operands, so the relevant operators are binary. The interpreter question is about an actor (a frame), not a two-operand relation, so it lives in a different domain and is not forced into the bond table.
- **R by symmetry:** aboutness is ordered. A signal about a food source at a distance is not the same as the food source about the signal. Symmetric bonds flatten that difference. This is why the candidate atoms must be joined by an ordered bond.
- **R by scale:** a settled signal-term becomes an atom for higher composition (Fig 12 :104). Order is therefore a fact about a level, not about the world.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Can the same signal carry different content with different bonds?

Fig 12 :117:

> **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fig 12 :96:

> **"Two entries can share every ingredient and be inverses of one another."**

Take the bee waggle dance (a real case, not a figure line): a waggle run's **duration** is bonded to distance by `⇒` (production: a longer run is produced by a farther source), and its **angle** is bonded to the sun-relative bearing by `⋛` (comparison against a threshold reference, the vertical comb as gravity reference). Same physical dance, two different bonds, two different contents. Change the bond and you change the substance while the ingredients stay fixed. That is Fig 12's isomer, instantiated outside the framework by an independent field.

**Consequence for INF-0036:** you cannot read "what the signal means" off the signal's shape alone. The bond is the rest of the information (Fig 12 :5). This is exactly the metamerism warning in `05_what_shape_means.md` §4: a reading of the signal does not comment on its source.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if two different bonds between the same signal and the same consequence produce no behavioural difference in a system with the required vocabulary and objective, the isomer claim fails for this domain and only the weaker "same substance, two descriptions" reading survives.

### Q2 - Is the interpreter a bond, a frame, or a state?

The strain test decides this rather than a preference. A bond is a typed relation between two operands (Operators table :4-5: *"The bond carries information the operands do not"*). A state is one of the six substrate terms (Fig 13 :7-14). An interpreter is a bounded system with a library (Symbols table: *"A frame is any bounded system with a library"*). Therefore the interpreter is a **frame**.

This matters because frames are corrected by the ground separately (Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*), and a ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame (Fig 2 :47). Promoting the interpreter to the anchor is exactly Fig 2's collapse 1: the anchor updates on the frames and everything drifts together.

**Falsifier:** exhibit an account in which the interpreter satisfies the two-operand bond test (swapping operands changes nothing or the identified six tests apply) *and* is still the thing that settles the question without moving. That would make the interpreter both a bond and an anchor. No candidate here does; if one is produced, this derivation is wrong.

### Q3 - Why is the interpreter regress a level error?

Fig 12 :103-104:

> **"Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."**
> **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"**

Fig 10 :68:

> **"Different actors, a different currency, a different ground, and a lossy transform between. That is why there is no top rung and no regress: a generator crosses, an instance does not."**

The regress argument assumes that each level needs the same kind of explanation as the one below, so an interpreter must be supplied for the interpreter, and so on. The figures deny the premise: at each crossing the actors, currency and ground change, and the transform is lossy. What crosses is a generator (a method for relating signal to consequence), not an instance (a particular reading by a particular interpreter).

Fig 4 :34-35 supplies the image:

> **"The level a method lands on remakes it. A seed carries no small tree: it carries what will grow one, and the conditions it lands in decide what it becomes."**

So the answer to "who interprets the interpreter" is not another interpreter; it is a different ground at a different level. The requirement of a human-like interpreter at every level is an instance pretending to be a generator.

**Falsifier:** show a level crossing in which the actors, currency and ground do *not* change, and the transform is identity, with a human-like interpreter still needed. That breaks Fig 10 :68 and the no-regress claim.

### Q4 - What is the order of the union of conditions?

The residual is a union compound (Fig 9 :54-58). The dependency order from `01` §1 is:

```
contact (perception) -> terms for affordances (vocabulary) -> objective comparison -> convention
```

A goal is a comparison (Fig 12 :112: *"A plan is a sequence. A goal is a comparison. Progress is a subtraction."*), so the objective link is itself a bond, not a free-floating intention. Conventions come last because they are built over terms that have already been settled: Fig 10 :47 *"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one."*

A signal that ranks `→` then `⋛` is a **route**, a sequence leading to a comparison. A vocabulary with only ingredients can name a state and cannot name a route to one (Fig 12 :111: *"A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one."*). This is why "the signal is correlated with the food" is not the same claim as "the signal is about the food, so act this way."

**Falsifier:** show a system that has the objective comparison before it has terms for what the situation affords, and where the ground still pays. That breaks Fig 3's dependency order and the sequence above.

### Q5 - What does the round-trip gap `R_T` contain here?

Fig 4 :30:

> **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."**

Send up the full derivation (anchor test, bond pricing, level relativity, vocabulary and objective). Bring it down to the one-line outreach claim "meaning is grounded in interaction, not in an interpreter." Three constraints are dropped:

- the convention caveat (Fig 2 :41), which the one-liner can appear to deny;
- the vocabulary/objective requirement (Fig 3 :7, :10), without which "interaction" is too coarse;
- the level-relativity of the interpreter label (Fig 12 :103), which the one-liner turns into an absolute.

`R_T` is nonzero and is recorded as this enumerated set. It is not measured numerically, because no ground reading was taken. `11_audit.md` re-runs this independently.

## CONCLUSION

The same signal under different bonds yields different content (Fig 12 :117), so a reading of the signal is not a reading of its source. The interpreter is a frame, not a bond (Symbols table, Fig 2 :45), so promoting it to the anchor is the mutual-update collapse. The apparent interpreter regress is a level error dissolved by changing actors, currency and ground at each crossing (Fig 12 :104, Fig 10 :68). The dependency order puts contact and vocabulary before objective and convention. Each of these four claims has a falsifier written above.

What survives is a `specified` structural account: content is carried by priced bonds to consequences; the anchor is the ground; the interpreter is a level-relative frame. No new primitive, no import that moves the wall, and no human-like interpreter as a premise.
