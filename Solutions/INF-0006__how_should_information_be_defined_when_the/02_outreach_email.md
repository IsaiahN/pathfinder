**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the shape claim in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §0 and its refuter have been checked by the sender. The output must survive validation or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

**Subject:** One output on defining information when the distribution is unknown

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at. Your field was chosen because your public work touches this exact residual, not because you are likely to agree.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** when the possibilities or their probabilities are unknown, the framework does not return a new formula for information. It returns the shape any acceptable definition must have. Information is bound to a per-slot reduction of the gap between a prediction and an observation, ranged against an anchor that does not move when a frame is pushed. It is never an average over many messages. Any candidate term must pass three guards (something to explain, reachable from what is held, genuinely new), must pay a description-length bargain, must price the bits of the description and the actions needed to test it in separate currencies, and must stay a candidate until a ground settles it. The framework explicitly does not name a single winning definition, because the missing thing here has no nameable anchor yet.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that Kolmogorov complexity is the answer, that a maximum-entropy prior supplies the missing distribution, that credal sets dissolve the question, or that predictive surprise settles it. Those are bindings from other frames onto terms, each needing provenance and a shadow test. Agreement among experts is efficient for a convention and disqualifying for a verdict, so I am not collecting endorsements.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before this message:**
> 1. a receiver's discriminable state changes while the per-slot prediction error stays flat, or the error falls while no receiver state changes;
> 2. a frame derives the missing distribution from inside its own closed library, with no new atom and no peer frame, and the derivation pays against the ground;
> 3. a definition wins by agreement alone, with no anchor that fails to move when pushed, or by a shorter description with no receiver-level residual reduction;
> 4. one definition is shown necessary across physics, biology, cognition and society with a single shared ground, so the problem is one question after all;
> 5. description length and test cost are shown to be the same quantity in the same units.
>
> If any holds, I retract the shape claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **What I would like from you (stage 1, before any theory):** what would you count as a failure of that shape? Would you expect a correct definition of information under unknown probabilities to be per-slot rather than averaged, and anchored outside the assigning frame? I will record your answer before showing you anything else.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the public sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Information (Adriaans and van Benthem, first published 2012, substantive revision 2023): https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/information/
> - Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Imprecise Probabilities (Seamus Bradley, first published 2014, revision 2019): https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/imprecise-probabilities/
> - C. E. Shannon, A Mathematical Theory of Communication, Bell System Technical Journal 27, 1948: https://jontalle.web.engr.illinois.edu/uploads/537.F18/Papers/OLD/Shannon48.pdf
> - Society for Imprecise Probabilities, Theories and Applications (public opt-in mailing list): https://www.sipta.org/
>
> **If you want to use the machinery (stage 3, opt in):** the formula is in the public figures folder. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name / affiliation]
