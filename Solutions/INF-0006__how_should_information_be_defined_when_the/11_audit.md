## Round trip gap `R_T`

The Symbols table defines `R_T` as the gap between what was sent up and what came back down, `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, measuring what the coarser description cannot hold. Fig 4 :30 requires it to be measured rather than assumed. I ran the trip on the condensation this folder actually uses.

1. **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set, chiefly Fig 1 :19 and :27 (per-slot, no self-score), Fig 2 :29 (non-updating anchor), Fig 8 :32 (idempotence), Fig 9 :32 (shape, not a name), Fig 12 :125 (two currencies).
2. **Abstraction (T_A):** the shorthand in `07_the_tether_answer.md`: information is per-slot ground-anchored residual reduction, candidates are gated and provenance-stamped, bits and actions apart, no unique formula.
3. **Concretisation (T_E):** a concrete proposed definition, for example "information is the width of a credal set" or "information is the Kolmogorov complexity of the signal."

**`R_T` is nonzero.** Three things the shorthand holds do not survive the trip back down:

- the **anchor rule** (Fig 2 :29), which a bare credal-width definition drops when the set is chosen by the same analyst it is supposed to check;
- the **two-currency separation** (Fig 12 :125), which a bare bit-count drops when the cost of testing the definition is treated as already paid;
- the **shape-not-a-name limit** (Fig 9 :32), which a concrete definition breaches by presenting one bond as the answer.

One thing survives cleanly: a definition must work at a receiver slot against something that does not update with the frame. That is the residue worth sending. `R_T` was not measured numerically, because no ground reading exists; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set `R_T` to zero and would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names. The honest form is a lossy condensation with a measured gap, which is what `09_watson_answer.md` states.
