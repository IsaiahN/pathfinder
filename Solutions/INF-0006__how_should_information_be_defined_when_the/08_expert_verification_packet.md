# Expert verification packet — defining information when the distribution is unknown

**What this is:** a claim in a form a probability or information researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` for the sub-claims, `general` for the structural rule. Not `grounded`.** No experiment or instrument is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

When the possibilities or their probabilities are unknown, the figures do not name a winning definition of information. They constrain the shape any acceptable definition must have: it is indexed per receiver slot and never averaged; it is ranged against an anchor that does not move when the assigning frame is pushed; it does not score a self-assigned prior with a quantity that prior produced; it prices the description in bits and the test in actions without adding them; and it stays a candidate until a ground pays. The missing distribution is a reachability verdict, not a silence. Search the current closure first, sharpen the receiver's existing error signal second, and import a differing frame only third and with provenance. Shortest-description, credal-set and prediction-error definitions are candidate bindings and budget filters, not a verdict the figures hand down.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 2 and 9

- **Statement:** a definition of information must be per-slot and ground-anchored; agreement among frames is a convention, not a verdict.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 2 :29 *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Fig 2 :40 *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* Fig 9 :60 *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
- **Refuter:** a definition shown to require averaging across slots to work, or a definition whose acceptance is unchanged when every agreeing frame's evidence is replaced by one pool, and which still predicts receiver readings. Either breaks the claim.

### C2 — information-theory facing; checkable against the candidate definitions

- **Statement:** algorithmic complexity, MDL, credal sets and predictive surprise are members of one functional group with different bonds, not variants of one definition and not isomers as written. A definition that scores its own assigned prior is frame-internal.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :124 *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Fig 1 :27 *"They are frame-internal. A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*
- **What to check:** do these candidates share a single atom set? If not, the isomer reading fails and the functional-group reading stands. Does a maximum-entropy prior supply an anchor, or only a number that the same frame assigned?
- **Refuter:** all candidates reduce to one atom set with one bond (differences only of notation), or a self-assigned prior is shown to change contact with a receiver that never saw it. Either breaks C2.

### C3 — framework-internal derivation; checkable by reading Figures 5, 6, 8 and 12

- **Statement:** MDL is a budget filter, not a verdict; missing p is a reachability fact; INWARD and OUTWARD are opposed and come after SEARCHED; bits and actions stay in separate currencies.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :45 *"This is minimum description length, Rissanen 1978, which is itself a composition of Kolmogorov complexity and Shannon coding put to work on model selection."* Fig 6 :32 *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* Fig 8 :32 *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."* Fig 12 :125 *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."*
- **Refuter:** a frame derives p inside its own closure with no new atom and it pays at the ground; or description length and test cost are shown to be the same quantity in the same units at the same level; or a case classified unreachable-at-budget resists all independent further search while an imported atom pays. Any one breaks C3.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that information is Kolmogorov complexity, credal-set width, free energy, or Hartley log-cardinality. Those are candidate bindings with provenance, none settled.
- Not that the question is one problem. It is a union of four conditions with separate loops.
- Not that "unknown probabilities" implies "no measure at all". The uniform-support case has a measure.
- Not that expert agreement settles a definition. Agreement about a convention is the product; agreement about the world from a shared pool is worthless as evidence.
- Not a theory of everything, not a final definition, not a new primitive, and not `grounded`.
- Not a report of a ground reading. This packet is a simulated derivation and cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: a reading *"cannot comment on the source"*; the spectrometer, not a debate, resolved the green case. Here:

1. **Slot test:** does a receiver's discriminable state change while the per-slot prediction error stays flat, or the error fall while no receiver state changes?
2. **Closure test:** can a frame derive p from inside its own closure, with no new atom and no peer frame, and have it pay at the ground?
3. **Anchor test:** does a candidate definition survive replacement of every agreeing frame's evidence with one pool, with no anchor that fails to move when pushed?
4. **Union test:** is one definition necessary across all four domains with a single shared ground?
5. **Currency test:** are description bits and test actions the same quantity in the same units?
