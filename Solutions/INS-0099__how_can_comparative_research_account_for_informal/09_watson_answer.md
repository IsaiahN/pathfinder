## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what comparative research must do so that an operative rule invisible to official measures is accounted for rather than averaged away. Option A: add an informal-institutions variable to the existing cross-country design and report the coefficient. Option B: change the unit of diagnosis to the case-slot, disqualify moving anchors, buy independence between readings, treat formal and informal as a union with its own compound intersections, and report a shape plus a refuter with the rule's identity left open. What each leads to: A produces a filter for the budget that is mistaken for a witness, and it leaves the intersections standing; B produces a checkable separator whose `gap` is honest and whose verdict waits for a source-discriminating observation.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** The failure of an official measure to predict behaviour at a slot where an unwritten rule governs. Arity: three terms (measure, behaviour, operative rule); where all three are read from one report, they collapse to one term and the residual is a mirror. Symmetry: a measure-outcome correlation is symmetric while "account for" is ordered (Fig 2 :39 "Correlation has valence."). Scale: generated at the step, stabilised at the cycle, erased at the country level, carried by the lineage.
- **Domain 1, the measurement frame.** Actors: index builders, coders, expert respondents. Currency: scores, ranks, coded rule counts. Anchor: should be observed behaviour; in much work it is another expert survey, which is a frame and not a ground. Nameable anchor present only after repair, so this domain is provisional.
- **Domain 2, the habitat of the operative rule.** Actors: citizens, front-line officials, brokers, customary authorities, firms, kin. Currency: compliance, sanction, reciprocity, bypass. Anchor: observed behaviour and outcomes. Enumerated from contact, not composed from a catalogue.
- **Domain 3, the comparison design.** Actors: researchers, case selectors, instrument builders, peer readers. Currency: residuals and independent readings. Anchor: the ground, never agreement.
- **Domain 4, the seam (union compound).** Actors: the official who must apply the written rule under an unwritten constraint. Currency: the discrepancy between rule-as-written and rule-as-used. Anchor: an outcome neither side can move. This is a union intersection with its own loop, not a harder case of either condition.
- **Partition or union:** union (Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."). A single lever clears only the region where one condition binds.
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3):** perception of the slot before vocabulary for its affordances; vocabulary and objective before design; design before carry; slots before country comparison.

Strain test applied: Fig 11 valence-relative (actor valence depends on the residual) versus Fig 2 non-updating anchor (the reference settles the question) govern different quantities and do not strain after re-census. The one live strain is the fixed-atom condition on the isomer claim in `06`, named there and not hidden.

## WORLD (secondary sources read)

World sources corroborate; they never overrule the figures. Overlap is declared for each.

- **Comparative politics.** Gretchen Helmke and Steven Levitsky, "Informal Institutions and Comparative Politics: A Research Agenda", *Perspectives on Politics* 2(4), 2004. "Mainstream comparative research on political institutions focuses primarily on formal rules. Yet in many contexts, informal institutions ... shape even more strongly political behavior and outcomes." Predicts that a formal-only measure misses incentives; corroborates the residual in D1 and the union in D4. Overlap: high with the institutional-economics and public-administration sources below; one pool.
- **Institutional economics.** Douglass C. North, "Institutions", *Journal of Economic Perspectives* 5(1), 1991. "Institutions are the humanly devised constraints that structure political, economic, and social interaction. They consist of both informal constraints (sanctions, taboos, customs, traditions, and codes of conduct), and formal rules (constitutions, laws, property rights)." Predicts a two-part vocabulary; corroborates the functional group. Overlap: high with the comparative-politics source, same broad institutional literature.
- **Comparative method.** Barbara Geddes, "How the Cases You Choose Affect the Answers You Get: Selection Bias in Comparative Politics", *Political Analysis* 2, 1990. "The only things that can actually be explained using a sample selected on the dependent variable are differences among the selected cases." Predicts that authored pools mislead; corroborates Fig 10 :73-79. Overlap: moderate, shares the comparative-politics pool.
- **Public administration.** "Street-level bureaucracy" (summary of Lipsky 1969/1980). "Street-level bureaucrats often have some degree of discretion on how they enforce the rules, laws and policies which they are assigned to uphold." Predicts that the seam is located at the front-line office; corroborates D4. Overlap: high with the institutional pool.
- **Measurement / economics.** "Goodhart's law" (Goodhart 1975; Strathern 1997). "when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure"; original form "Any observed statistical regularity will tend to collapse once pressure is placed upon it for control purposes." Predicts that an official score moves under optimisation; corroborates Fig 2 :29-30. Scope limit recorded honestly: an IOP 2018 analysis shows the law fails for a physical feedback sensor, so it is not universal; the figure verdict does not depend on it. Overlap: moderate.
- **Law and society.** "Legal pluralism". "individuals would choose to bring their legal claims under the system that they thought would offer them the best advantage" and "the formal ideology of law as it is written exists alongside the informal ideology of law as it is used." Predicts overlapping rule systems; corroborates the union. Overlap: high with the institutional pool.
- **Measurement science / color.** "Metamerism (color)". "metamerism is a perceived matching of colors with different (nonmatching) spectral power distributions." Genuinely low overlap. Predicts that one reading has many possible sources; corroborates the metamerism mechanism behind the anchor test. This is the strongest independent witness.
- **Human cognition.** "Tacit knowledge" (Polanyi 1966; Collins 2010). "we can know more than we can tell"; tacit knowledge "cannot easily be aggregated" and is "distributed across knowing subjects." Low overlap. Predicts that a codified official measure misses the unwritten practice; corroborates the enumeration instruction.
- **Framework essays.** Eight essays by Isaiah N, 2026-08 to 2026-11, opened directly. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"; *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: "When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world"; *The Library of Babel*: "You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question"; *They're Scanning Books*: "The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space." Predict the shape-and-refuter answer; maximal overlap with the figures, so no independent witness weight.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

Comparative research can account for the overlooked rules only by changing what it measures: keep the residual per case-slot, disqualify any anchor that moves when pushed, obtain low-overlap readings and report their overlap, treat formal and informal as a union with its own compound intersections, enumerate the habitat from contact, make the coding convention violable and the pool unauthored, and provenance every imported concept. What survives structurally is a functional group (a rule with a credible sanction, qualifier stated) and a cycle (compliance re-enters as expectation); a standing list of good practices is structurally refused. The framework returns the shape and the falsifiers written in advance. It does not name the winning practice or return a magnitude: that is a declared `gap` of the unreached kind, closable only by a source-discriminating observation at the slot. Mode `specified` on the separators, `general` on the shape, not `grounded`.

- **cost if wrong:** the separator can send a reform toward a written rule while the operative rule continues to govern the slot, because the measure never registered it. Fig 2 :42 prices the remedy: buy independence in proportion to that cost. The cost of the derivation itself is bounded because every claim carries a refuter and a mode, so an error is findable rather than invisible.
- **undoable:** yes. The slot-level design can be adopted or dropped without withdrawing any term; the `gap` is explicit and no primitive is claimed. A study that followed Option A can be re-described at the slot without changing its data collection.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A slot decomposition showing no positive mass, an anchor that holds under an intervention on beliefs, two readings with documented low overlap that still corroborate, a convention that survives an independently checkable redraw, or a source-discriminating observation that names the practice, would each move the corresponding sub-claim. Agreement among scholars sharing one evidence pool (Fig 2 :40) cannot reverse anything.
