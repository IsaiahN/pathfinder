# Tether outreach - figure-grounded package

**Question:** How can comparative research account for informal rules and practices that official institutional measures overlook?

**Source:** `institutional questions.txt`, line 120 (question 99).

**Section:** 10. Institutional change, adaptation, and research.

**Mode:** `specified` on the per-domain separators and the research-design claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the identity and magnitude of any concrete informal rule, not `grounded`. Every substantive file states that this is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading, so it cannot settle the question it reports on.

**Status:** done.

**Registry status:** pending registry ingest.

**Field for public contact points:** informal rules research (comparative institutional analysis, informal institutions, legal pluralism, street-level implementation, and institutional measurement).

---

## The question restated as an effect

Official institutional measures (legal indices, regulatory scores, codified-rule counts, governance ratings) predict behaviour and outcomes only partially. Where they fail, the failure is not noise: it is concentrated in slots where an unwritten rule, a customary practice, or a front-line routine governs what people actually do. The effect to be accounted for is a **residual indexed per case-slot**: the measure's prediction misses the same kind of site again and again, while the average score across cases looks healthy. What is owed is an account that (a) keeps the residual where it is, (b) enumerates the actually-operative rules from the world rather than from a standing list, (c) ranges at least two low-overlap readings of that slot against a ground that does not update on the researcher's own measure, and (d) refuses to name which informal rule wins until an instrument discriminates among the sources of the same reading. The answer is a **shape and a checking discipline**, not a variable to add to a regression.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with this package.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No standing list of "good" or "bad" informal rules. Fig 11 :51 says *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* Which informal practice helps depends on the residual, not on the practice.

Three figure reasons, quoted verbatim:

1. **Agreement is not corroboration when the readers share a pool.** Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*.
2. **A room cannot upgrade a label.** Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*
3. **Do not hand the checker the interiors.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* and Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."*
