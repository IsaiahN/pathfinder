# Outreach draft - check the separator before you decide about the framework

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the stage-1 output below survives a reader's check. The offer collapses on contact if the separator does not hold.

---

**Subject:** Check one research-design separator before you decide whether the framework is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about comparative institutional research. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** a formal institutional measure and the behaviour it is supposed to predict fail in a structured way. The failure is not spread evenly across cases. It concentrates in slots: the licensing desk, the land registry, the police stop, the customary forum where the written rule is bypassed. A country average can look healthy while one of those slots is still carrying the whole signal. Three things follow from that, and I wrote them down before asking anyone:
>
> 1. Keep the prediction failure indexed per case-slot. Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average (Fig 1 :19, :22).
> 2. Disqualify any "anchor" that moves when you push on it. If the measure is another expert-perception survey, it is a frame, not a ground. Two readings drawn from the same report are one reading (Fig 2 :29-30, :31).
> 3. Treat "formal" and "informal" as a union of overlapping conditions, not two boxes. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, where both bind (Fig 9 :54-58). The interesting object is the intersection: the official rule is weak and the same official actor enforces the informal one.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that any particular informal practice is the answer; that there is a universal percentage of behaviour explained by informal rules; that a checklist of "informal institutions" can be appended to an index and settle the problem. The framework returns a shape, not a name. What survives is a discipline for locating and checking the operative rule, not a catalogue of them. A standing list of good actors does not survive a change of residual (Fig 11 :51).
>
> **What would refute the separator:**
> 1. show that the prediction failure is uniformly distributed across slots, with no slot carrying positive mass, and that a slot-indexed term reduces nothing;
> 2. name a candidate anchor, then change what researchers and respondents believe while holding the outcome fixed, and show the reading does not move; produce a non-updating ground for that slot or say there is none;
> 3. show that two readings whose agreement I treat as corroboration share a source, respondent pool or coding scheme;
> 4. show that the formal and informal patterns partition the cases with zero overlap and one lever clears all of them.
>
> I wrote these before sending this. If any one holds, the corresponding claim is withdrawn and the design is rebuilt. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** here are the sources. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figures and formula, public repository (branch `general-agent`): https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
> - Canonical figures README: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - Worked narrative, "Arrival of the Fittest: The Tether Architecture": https://medium.com/@IsaiahNwukor/arrival-of-the-fittest-the-tether-architecture-1de7edf641ec
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the figures link is enough. Run the separator on a comparison you know well and can check against an outcome. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get: `general` for structure, `specified` for a case, `grounded` only when an outcome pays. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
