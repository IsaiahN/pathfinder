# The Tether's answer: how comparative research can account for the overlooked rule

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain separators, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the identity and magnitude of any concrete informal rule, not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a checking discipline, not a result. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

---

## 1. The residual it answers

A comparative study predicts behaviour from official measures and fails in a structured way: the misses concentrate at particular slots (the licensing desk, the land registry, the police stop, the customary forum) while the country average looks healthy. What the measure cannot represent is the operative rule in contact with that slot, with its sanction and its community. The question asks how to account for it. The answer is not a new variable in the regression. It is a change in the unit of diagnosis, the anchor, the independence of readings, and the status of the coding convention.

## 2. The answer, per domain (verdicts kept separate, never averaged)

**D1, the measurement frame. `specified`, triangulated.**

1. Keep the residual indexed per case-slot. Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."** Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."** A global score near the mean with one live slot is a legal state, not evidence the measure is adequate.
2. Disqualify any anchor that moves when pushed. Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."** Fig 2 :30: **"you push on it, it is not an anchor."** An expert-perception index is a frame. When the score is optimised the score changes while behaviour may not (Fig 2 :47: **"A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame. Which is panel 1, arrived at by a different route."**). Where no non-updating outcome can be named for a slot, say `unreached` for that slot.

**D2, the habitat of the operative rule. `specified`, triangulated.**

3. Enumerate the contacts from the world, not from a standing catalogue. Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."** Fig 11 :48: **"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."** List actors, conditions and relations at the slot, then go outward until the cascade stops mattering (Fig 11 :52-53: the residual that mattered was three steps out).
4. Refuse a standing list of good practices. Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."** Fig 11 :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."** A broker can be a malfactor for a transparency residual and a benefactor for an access residual.
5. What survives structurally is a **functional group** with a stated qualifier: a rule with a credible sanction (Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**). The stronger isomer claim holds only over a fixed atom set (Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**). An informal norm is a **cycle**, not a chain: Fig 12 :121, **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."** Adding a rule to a chain does not repair a failing cycle.

**D3, the comparison design. `specified`, triangulated.**

6. Buy independence explicitly. Fig 2 :31: **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."** Pair an administrative-records reading with a survey or ethnographic reading. If they share a respondent pool, source document or coding scheme, their agreement is a convention, not a witness (Fig 2 :40: **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**).
7. Treat formal and informal as a union and study the intersections. Fig 9 :55-56: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."** The compound is the slot where the written rule is weak and the same official actor enforces the informal one (Fig 9 :57-58: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**).
8. Make the coding convention violable and the case pool unauthored. Fig 10 :16-17: **"the convention does not fix the bug"** and **"it makes the bug statable"**; Fig 10 :19: **"→ install what can be violated"**. Fig 10 :73-74: **"The residual selects the members, but only from what is available. A candidate set chosen by a participant is an authored room however well the selection is reasoned, so the pool must be unauthored first and examined second."** Select by lottery, by all who qualify, by a set assembled before the question existed, or by the enumerated habitat (Fig 10 :76-79).
9. Describe the residual before importing a frame (Fig 8 :53: **"Residual first, frame second."**), and provenance every imported concept. Fig 8 :50-52: **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."**

**D4, the level and the stop. `general` on the shape, `gap` on the name, triangulated for the limit.**

10. Work the dependency chain in order: perception, vocabulary, objective, planning, carry. Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** The missing instrument is at the vocabulary/objective link (Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**), not at the planning link where bigger samples live.
11. Slots that are settled compose into a country-level comparison; the round trip has a nonzero gap that must be measured, not assumed (Fig 4 :30: **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."**).
12. The figures do not name the rule or return a magnitude. Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**; Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"**. That is a `gap`, reported, not filled.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a list of informal institutions to code for. No standing list survives a change of residual (Fig 11 :51).
- Not a universal percentage, not a new index, and not "informal institutions matter."
- Not that any particular practice (blat, guanxi, clientelism, legal pluralism) is the answer. Naming one is a provenanced binding, not a derivation.
- Not that the written rule is irrelevant. Curvature is not denied in physics and the written rule is not denied here; what is denied is that it is the only thing in contact with the slot.
- Not a causal identification. Correlation is symmetric and "account for" is ordered (Fig 2 :39: **"Correlation has valence."**); a pooled association cannot choose the arrow without the ground work in D3.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement at a slot was taken in this derivation.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating observation at the slot, not a further argument. The *Hard Problem is About Source* essay (opened 2026-11-23) states the general form: an instrument that reads a projection *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy"*; the spectrometer, not an argument, resolved the green case. Here the corresponding tests are:

1. **Slot test:** does a slot-indexed residual term reduce the failure where a country-level term does not, and is there positive mass at some slot? Negative answer refutes the unit-of-analysis claim.
2. **Anchor test:** name a candidate outcome, change what researchers and respondents believe while holding the outcome fixed, and check that the reading does not move. If it moves, the candidate is disqualified.
3. **Independence test:** compute the evidence overlap of the two readings. If they share a source, the agreement carries zero verdict weight.
4. **Convention test:** write the coding rule so it can be violated, redraw the case pool by a pre-existing rule, and check the conclusion survives.
5. **Cycle test:** observe whether compliance at the slot re-enters as expectation at the next use (Fig 12 :121). If the practice is one-shot and externally enforced, the cycle claim narrows.

Standing falsifiers from `01` section 4 apply to all of the above.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The library the researcher holds already contains most of the relevant ordinary terms; what is missing is a source-discriminating instrument at the slot and an unauthored case pool. Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** Fig 6 :34-35: the question is whether anything at any resolution is already returning something that fails to resolve, and field observation of "this is what happened at the desk" is such a proto-instrument. So the missing thing is a shape plus a schedule of readings, not a named primitive. Indiscernibility is not identity: two countries with the same official score remain different until an instrument separates their sources, and the lineage of the reading must be kept (Fig 13 :142: **"The seed was always the seedline."**).

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not supply a list of informal institutions and does not claim a universal percentage. It changes what is being measured. The prediction failure of official institutional measures is indexed per case-slot, not averaged into a country score, because a global average is how a live signal disappears. At that slot two things must happen: the anchor must be something that does not move when researchers or respondents change their minds, and two readings of the slot must draw on different evidence, or their agreement is a convention rather than a witness. Formal and informal rules are a union of overlapping conditions, so the interesting object is the intersection where the written rule is weak and the same official actor enforces the unwritten one. An informal norm is a cycle: compliance re-enters as expectation, which is why adding a rule to a formal chain often leaves behaviour unchanged. The framework returns the shape of the operative rule, a credible sanction with a community, and a falsifier written in advance. It does not name which rule wins; that waits for a source-discriminating observation, and is reported as a gap until one exists.
