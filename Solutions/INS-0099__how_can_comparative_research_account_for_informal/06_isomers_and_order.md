## RESIDUAL

The official measure records the written rule; the behaviour follows an operative rule the measure cannot represent. Two questions are owed beyond the main audit: (1) are a formal and an informal institutional arrangement the **same atoms under a different bond** (an isomer claim), and (2) what is the **dependency order** of the derivation, and what carries the loop across cases and across levels. A third question follows from the first: if the atoms are not the same, what weaker structural claim survives?

## DERIVATION

### Q1 - Are a formal and an informal institutional arrangement isomers?

Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**
Fig 12 :46: **"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands. A recipe that names only its ingredients has recorded half of itself."**
Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

A formal rule and an informal rule can be written over the same atom set: an actor who decides, a counterparty who is affected, a sanction that follows non-compliance, a forum in which a dispute is settled, and a written artefact (which may be a form in one case and absent in the other). If the written artefact is required as an atom, the sets differ and the isomer claim fails. If "writtenness" is treated as a bond property (the relation to the artefact, rather than a new atom), the sets match and the difference is the bond: the formal arrangement uses a codified, court-backed relation; the informal arrangement uses a relation enforced outside officially sanctioned channels.

Under Fig 12 :92 (**"Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."**) the two are not permutations of one another under a symmetric bond. Swapping "written" and "unwritten" changes the substance, so the bond is ordered. They are therefore good isomer candidates, with the standing caveat that the atom set must be fixed before the claim is made.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if either arrangement requires an atom the other does not (for example, a court with enforcement power on one side and no equivalent forum on the other), the isomer claim fails and the functional-group reading of Q2 stands instead.

### Q2 - If the atoms differ, what survives? The functional group

Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

The weaker and more robust claim is that both arrangements share a functional group: a rule with a sanction that is credible at the slot. The group fixes the kind of thing (a governance relation that changes behaviour); the qualifier (written/court-backed, or unwritten/peer-enforced) says which member. This survives even when the atom sets differ, because it makes a claim about a substructure, not about the full inventory.

This is the form in which the finding should travel to a comparative audience: do not assert "formal and informal are isomers" as a universal; assert a functional group with a stated qualifier and a stated falsifier. The isomer version is a `specified` claim over a **fixed** atom set, and the fixed-atom condition is a live strain (see Strains below).

### Q3 - The informal norm is a cycle, not a chain

Fig 12 :121: **"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."**

An informal practice persists because compliance by one actor is an input to the next actor's expectation of compliance. The output (observed compliance) re-enters as input (the expectation that makes the sanction credible). A written rule can be enforced by a chain (statute, regulation, court, sanction) that does not need the loop to close at each use. A norm does. That is why an official measure that counts rules on the books misses it: a chain is visible in the artefacts; a cycle is visible only in repeated behaviour.

The sign matters. A positive loop (compliance reinforces expectation, which reinforces compliance) closes and sustains the norm. A negative loop (exposure of the bypass reduces its credibility) unwinds it. A chain cannot close by construction, so a reform that adds a rule to a chain does not repair a failing cycle. This is a structural reason why "add a formal rule" often leaves the operative behaviour unchanged.

**Falsifier:** exhibit an informal practice that persists with no re-entry of compliance into expectation (a one-shot convention enforced externally by a single sanction) and the cycle claim is narrowed to repeated-interaction norms only.

### Q4 - The broker is a catalyst, and spending is invisible

Fig 12 :119: **"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."**

The broker who connects an applicant to the clerk is a catalyst: the broker survives the transaction and is available for the next one. A fee that is paid and consumed is a spent part. A comparative measure that counts transactions without recording which actors persist will treat them alike. The practical instruction is to record, per slot, which actors are regenerating and which are spent. Fig 12 :119 adds *"nothing records which a term is"*, so this has to be observed, not inferred from the category label "broker" or "fee".

**Falsifier:** show that a given actor is spent after one use (it never reappears at any neighbouring slot) and the catalyst assignment is withdrawn for that actor.

### Q5 - Order: two layers and two currencies

Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**

The derivation has two ordered layers.

1. **Within one slot.** Perception of the failure (Fig 3 :4-5) gives a residual; vocabulary for the affordances it offers (Fig 3 :6-8) gives bonds; the objective (Fig 3 :9-11) says what would count as accounting for it; planning (Fig 3 :12-13) designs the reading; learn-and-carry (Fig 3 :14-16) names what worked and verifies it. The middle link (vocabulary plus objective) is the one with no instrument (Fig 3 :27), so it is worked first.
2. **Across scales.** A settled slot-level account becomes an atom for a country-level comparison. Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"**. The comparison is a second-order composition over settled slots, not a direct bond between two country scores. This is the level-relativity the main audit relies on when it refuses to diagnose at the country level.

Fig 4 :30 requires the round trip: **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."** A slot-level account carried up to a country comparison and brought back down will not match what was sent; the gap `R_T` is the measurement of what the coarser country description cannot hold, and is reported rather than zeroed.

Finally, the two currencies do not add. Fig 12 :125-126: **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."** and **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."** A coding scheme that names the operative rule cheaply (few bits) can be very expensive to verify at the ground (many actions). A study that reports only the description cost mistakes the denominator.

**Falsifier:** show that a country-level score, carried up from slot accounts and back down, reproduces the slot residuals with zero gap, and that a single currency prices both describing and verifying the rule. Then the level distinction and the two-currency warning are withdrawn.

## CONCLUSION

The two strongest structural claims are the **functional group** (a rule with a credible sanction, qualifier stated) and the **cycle** (compliance re-enters as expectation). The isomer claim is real but conditional on a fixed atom set; it should be stated with that condition visible. The broker is a catalyst and must be distinguished from spent parts by observation. The dependency order puts the vocabulary/objective link first and the country comparison second, with a measured round-trip gap. All four claims are `specified`, ungrounded candidates.

## Strains

- **Live:** the fixed-atom condition on the isomer claim. If "writtenness" is an atom, the atom sets differ; if it is a bond property, they match. This needs a decision recorded with the atom census, not an argument that hides it.
- **Dissolved by re-census:** Fig 12 :104 (a settled slot becomes an atom for the country level) versus Fig 1 :19 (the residual is indexed per slot). They govern different quantities: composability across levels versus the unit of error measurement. No contradiction.
- **Dissolved:** Fig 12 :121 (cycle) versus Fig 3 :20 (ordered chain). A chain is the degenerate case of a molecule with one bond type; a cycle is a different bond arrangement. Fig 12 :121 says a chain cannot close by construction, which is why formal rule-chains and informal cycles behave differently. Different arrangements, not competing rules.
