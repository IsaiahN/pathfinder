## 0. The claim under audit, and the proposal header

### 0.1 The question in effect terms

> How can comparative research account for informal rules and practices that official institutional measures overlook?

Stated as an effect: a comparative study predicts behaviour from official measures (laws on the books, regulatory indices, codified procedures, governance scores). The prediction fails in a structured way. It fails at the same kinds of site, and it fails while the country-level average looks healthy. The residual is not "institutions matter somewhat." It is: **what actually governs the slot was never in the instrument's library.** The measure reads the written rule; the behaviour follows an unwritten one. Something has to account for the gap without pretending the gap is noise.

### 0.2 What an answer would look like (the sought-for shape bound as an ordinary term)

Per `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction": the loop cannot check the direction of its own question, so write down what an answer would look like and let it accrue a residual. The sought-for answer is bound here as:

> A comparative procedure that (i) keeps the prediction failure indexed per case-slot rather than averaged into a national score; (ii) enumerates the actors, conditions and relations in contact with that slot from the world, not from a standing catalogue of "informal institutions"; (iii) obtains at least two readings of the same slot that do not draw on the same evidence, and ranges both against an outcome that does not move when the researcher's measure changes; (iv) treats formal and informal as a union of overlapping conditions and studies the intersections, not the two boxes; (v) makes its coding convention checkable and violable; (vi) records the provenance of any concept imported from another frame; and (vii) names no specific informal rule as the answer until a source-discriminating instrument exists.

This binding is an ordinary term now. It can be refused by the same test as everything else (see the pre-registered refuter, section 4).

### 0.3 Proposal header (seat reasoning, five lines)

```
DECIDES:    If the residual stays per case-slot and two low-overlap readings are ranged
            against a non-updating outcome -> keep the formal/informal distinction as a
            union compound and study the intersections (do B). If all readings are drawn
```

## 2. Domain census (before any figure line was chosen)

### 2.1 R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity.** The residual relates at least two things: the official reading of a case-slot and the behaviour the reading fails to predict. A third term is already present in the question: the informal practice that governs the behaviour. So R is not a two-variable gap; it is a three-term seam. Where the measure, the behaviour and the practice are all read from the same expert report, all three collapse to one term, and the apparent residual is a mirror (Fig 2 :35).
- **Symmetry.** A correlation between a formal-measure score and an outcome is symmetric, while "account for" is ordered. A pooled association cannot choose the arrow (Fig 2 :39: *"Correlation has valence."*). Two readings of one event are interchangeable only if they draw on the same source; then they are one report, not two frames (Fig 2 :31).
- **Scale.** Four scales are in play: the step (one citizen's or one official's rule-following decision), the cycle (a repeated interaction that sustains a norm), the level (a national institutional index), and the lineage (transmission of a practice across cohorts of officials). The residual is generated at the step, stabilised at the cycle, erased at the level, and carried by the lineage. The measurement error is a scale error as much as a variable error (Fig 4 :27-32).

### 2.2 One line per domain: actors / currency / anchor

**D1, the measurement frame (official institutional measures).** Actors: index builders, coders, expert respondents. Currency: scores, ranks, coded rule counts. Anchor: should be observed behaviour or outcomes. In much comparative work the "anchor" is another expert perception survey, which moves when pushed (Fig 2 :29-30) and is therefore not an anchor. When the ground is listed as one of the agreeing parties, it has become a frame (Fig 2 :47). *No nameable, non-updating anchor means this domain is not yet a domain; it is a candidate that must be repaired before it can be ranged.*

**D2, the habitat of the operative rule.** Actors: citizens, street-level officials, brokers, customary authorities, firms, kinship groups. Currency: compliance, sanction, reciprocity, bypass. Anchor: observed behaviour and outcomes, a court that actually rules, a market that actually clears, a case that actually gets processed. This is enumerated, never composed (Fig 11 :43). The valence of any given informal practice is relative to this residual (Fig 11 :49-51), so a catalogue of "good informal institutions" is not a domain fact.

**D3, the comparison design (research method).** Actors: researchers, case selectors, instrument builders, peer readers. Currency: residuals and independent readings. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. A case pool chosen because the cases succeeded is an authored room (Fig 10 :73-74); an unauthored pool is the requirement (Fig 10 :76-79).

**D4, the seam (formal rule meets informal practice in the same slot).** Actors: the front-line official who must apply the written rule under an unwritten constraint. Currency: the discrepancy between the rule as written and the rule as used. Anchor: an outcome that neither side can move. Fig 10 :13-19 names this: *"two frames, one field, no shared reading"*, and the convention's job is to make the bug statable, not to fix it.

### 2.3 Partition or union?

**Union, not partition.** "Formal" and "informal" are not two clean boxes. Helmke and Levitsky's four patterns (complementary, accommodating, competing, substitutive, opened 2026-11-23) are defined by the relation between the two, and the relationship literature on legal pluralism records systems that overlap in one jurisdiction. Fig 9 :55-56 applies: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."* Adding an "informal institutions" dummy variable is exactly such a single lever. The residue after it is a **specific compound** (Fig 9 :57-58) with its own loop: the case where the written rule and the operative rule diverge *and* the official actor is the one who enforces the informal rule.

### 2.4 Order by dependency (Fig 3)

1. **Perception.** Does any instrument register the informal practice at all? If nothing at any resolution registers it, there is no edge to extend (Fig 6 :34-35), and the honest report is `unreached`, not a fabricated variable.
2. **Vocabulary.** Do we have bonds, not just ingredients? A list of informal practices names what a situation contains and cannot name a route to it (Fig 12 :111-113).
3. **Objective.** Can we say what would count as accounting for it? The target is a residual that stops arriving at the slot, not a larger R-squared.
4. **Planning.** Design the comparison: unauthored case pool, two independent readings, a non-updating anchor, a violable convention.
5. **Learn and carry.** Carry the method up and a head start down, not a recording of one field site (Fig 4 :22-25).

Effort in the literature lands at steps 4 and 5 (bigger N, more variables) because that is where the measurements already exist (Fig 3 :29-30). The break is at steps 2 and 3, where there is no instrument (Fig 3 :27). Most improvement effort therefore cannot rescue the failing link (Fig 3 :19).

## 3. Findings (verbatim quotes before conclusions)

### F1 - The residual is indexed per case-slot, and the national average erases it

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

A comparative index is an aggregate by construction. It is precisely the object Fig 1 forbids as the unit of diagnosis. The live signal in this question is that the *same kinds of slot* fail in different countries: the licensing desk, the land registry, the police stop, the customary court. A global "institutional quality" score near the mean with one live slot is a legal state under Fig 1 :13 and is not evidence that the measure is adequate.

**Refuter:** show that the prediction failure is uniformly distributed across slots, with no slot carrying positive mass, and that adding a slot-indexed term does not reduce the residual anywhere. Then the aggregation is harmless and this finding is withdrawn.

**Mode:** `general` structural, triangulated with Fig 1 :23 (*"The ground enters as the only metric."*) and `Symbols` :38-40, which calls R *"the aim, not only the error signal, and it is always a slice"*.

### F2 - A measure that moves when you push on it is not an anchor

Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."**
Fig 2 :30: **"you push on it, it is not an anchor."**
Fig 2 :34: **"If it does, panel 3 turns into panel 1: the anchor becomes another frame, everything drifts together, and the drift is coherent, which is why nobody notices."**

This is the decisive measurement finding. An expert-perception governance index is generated by frames (respondents) who have read the same international reports. It is a frame, and when a government optimises its score the score changes without behaviour changing (the Goodhart/Campbell analogue, opened 2026-11-23: *"when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure"*). That is the anchor moving. Comparative research that uses such an index as the ground has installed panel 1.

The world source gives the general warning; the figure gives the verdict. The world does not overrule the figure: Fig 2 :47 says **"A ground listed as one of the parties has already become a frame. Which is panel 1, arrived at by a different route."**

**Refuter:** name a candidate anchor for a given slot and show, by an intervention that changes the researchers' and respondents' beliefs but not the outcome, that the reading does not move. If it moves, it is disqualified. If no such candidate exists for the slot, report `unreached` for that slot rather than substituting agreement.

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated (Fig 2 :29-30 + Fig 2 :47 + the Goodhart source, with the source's overlap declared).

### F3 - Independence is how little the two readings draw on the same evidence

Fig 2 :31: **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."**
Fig 2 :32: **"is what makes agreement mean something."**
Fig 2 :40: **"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**

Two area studies that cite the same national expert survey, the same country report and the same legal database are one frame wearing two names (Fig 8 :48-49). Their convergence on "informal institutions matter here" is a convention, not corroboration. The question asks how comparative research can *account for* the overlooked rules; the figure says the accounting has to be bought: Fig 2 :42, **"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."**

Low-overlap pairs for a given slot: an administrative records reading (what the registry actually processed) against an ethnographic or survey reading (what people report they had to do); a court-outcome reading against a business-survey reading. Their agreement is evidence only if they do not share the instrument.

**Refuter:** exhibit two readings whose agreement is used as corroboration and show they share a source, a respondent pool or a coding scheme. Then the corroboration is void and the claim must be restated as a single-source observation.

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated (Fig 2 :31 + Fig 8 :48-49).

### F4 - The chain breaks at vocabulary and objective, not at sample size

Fig 3 :25: **"Perception has obvious failures and obvious tests. Planning has obvious failures and obvious tests. The link between them, having terms for what a"**
Fig 3 :26: **"situation offers and being able to say what would count as progress, has neither, so it attracts the least attention and the fewest instruments."**
Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**
Fig 3 :29-30: **"Before asking whether a system is good, ask which link it currently stops at, and whether the answer was measured or assumed. Most improvement effort in practice lands above the break, because that is where the measurements already exist."**

Comparative institutional research has excellent instruments for perception (surveys, indices) and for planning (regression designs, case selection). It has weak instruments for the middle link: what a local situation *affords* an actor (which rule can be bypassed, which sanction is credible) and what would count as progress in accounting for it. Fig 3 :19: **"Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."** Adding covariates to the regression optimises a link that already works.

**Refuter:** produce a validated instrument that registers the affordance and the progress criterion at the slot, with a reported error rate. Then the break has been repaired and the "nobody has looked at it" diagnosis is retracted for that subfield.

**Mode:** `specified`, single-source for the diagnosis itself (Fig 3), corroborated in the world by the measurement literature without being decided by it.

### F5 - Formal and informal are a union; the intersections are a compound with their own loop

Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
Fig 9 :55-56: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**
Fig 9 :57-58: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."**

The four patterns in the informal-institutions literature (complementary, accommodating, competing, substitutive) are already a union typology. The figure adds the instruction for what to do with it: unbundle and give the intersection its own loop. The intersection here is the slot where the official rule is weak *and* the informal rule is enforced by the same official actor who is supposed to apply the written rule. That is not "informal institutions matter more"; it is a different object.

**Refuter:** show that the four patterns partition the case set with zero overlap and that a single lever clears all of them. Then the union reading is unnecessary and is withdrawn.

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated (Fig 9 :54-58 + the Helmke and Levitsky typology, with overlap declared: the world source is about institutions, the figure about problem structure).

### F6 - Describe the residual before choosing the comparative frame

Fig 9 :27-28: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**
Fig 9 :30-31: **"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"**
Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**

It is tempting to answer this question by importing a ready-made frame: legal pluralism, patrimonialism, street-level bureaucracy, clientelism. Each will seem to fit once named. The figure's order is residual first, frame second (Fig 8 :53: **"Residual first, frame second."**). Describe the effect: at this slot the written rule predicted X, the observed behaviour was Y, and the sanction that enforced Y was not in the official measure. Only then look for a frame whose closure predicts that shape.

The shape returned is a **kind of thing**: an operative rule in contact with the slot, with a sanction and a community, not the name of a particular practice. Naming one is a binding from the frame that supply it, and must be provenanced (see F9).

**Refuter:** exhibit a case where a frame was chosen first and the residual was then described in that frame's vocabulary, and show that no residual remains unexplained from outside that vocabulary. Then the inversion did no harm in that case.

**Mode:** `general` structural, `specified` on the per-slot application, triangulated (Fig 9 :27-28 + Fig 8 :53).

### F7 - Enumerate the habitat; do not use a standing catalogue of good practices

Fig 11 :43: **"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."**
Fig 11 :44: **"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."**
Fig 11 :48: **"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."**
Fig 11 :49: **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**
Fig 11 :51: **"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."**

A checklist of "informal institutions to code for" is a composition: it is assembled from prior theory. The figure requires enumeration from contact with the residual. Concretely, for a failing licensing slot: who acts on it and what it acts on (the clerk, the applicant, the broker, the fee, the queue); conditions (the backlog, the season, the court calendar); relations (who can sanction whom, whose approval is needed twice). Then go one step out: the clerk's supervisor, the newspaper that reports the court, the family network that supplies the broker. Fig 11 :52-53 warns that the residual that mattered can be three steps out: **"Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."**

Valence is residual-relative. A broker who is a malfactor for a transparency residual can be a benefactor for an access residual. So no practice is entered into the ledger as intrinsically good or bad (Fig 11 :50).

**Refuter:** show that a standing catalogue of informal practices predicts the residual as well as a locally enumerated habitat across several residuals, with no change of valence. Then enumeration adds nothing and the claim is narrowed.

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated (Fig 11 :43-51 + Fig 11 :52-54).

### F8 - The coding convention must be violable, and the case pool must be unauthored

Fig 10 :13: **"two frames, one field, no shared reading"**
Fig 10 :16: **"the convention does not fix the bug"**
Fig 10 :17: **"it makes the bug statable"**
Fig 10 :19: **"→ install what can be violated"**
Fig 10 :24: **"the ground does not decay, the channel does"**
Fig 10 :73-74: **"The residual selects the members, but only from what is available. A candidate set chosen by a participant is an authored room however well the selection is reasoned, so the pool must be unauthored first and examined second."**

When the formal measure and the operative practice meet in one slot there are two frames and no shared reading. The researcher's coding scheme is a convention. Its job is to make the disagreement statable, not to decide which rule counts. Fig 10 :18: **"a declared meaning is not a grounded one."** A convention that decides an outcome has stopped being a convention (Fig 10 :47: **"a convention that decides an outcome has stopped being one"**).

Case selection is the other half. Selecting countries because they are "successful informal-institution cases" is selection on the dependent variable; Geddes (1990, opened 2026-11-23) found that *"The only things that can actually be explained using a sample selected on the dependent variable are differences among the selected cases."* Fig 10 :76-79 permits four unauthored mechanisms: **"a lottery / everyone who qualifies / a set assembled before the question existed / or the habitat itself, enumerated rather than composed."**

Channel maintenance is the ongoing cost: saturated metrics and pooled readings sever contact while the ground does not decay (Fig 10 :25-29).

**Refuter:** exhibit a study that selects on the dependent variable or whose coding convention fixes the outcome, and show that its conclusion survives when the pool is redrawn by a pre-existing rule and the convention is made independently checkable. Then the design criticism is withdrawn for that study.

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated (Fig 10 :13-19 + Fig 10 :73-79 + the Geddes result as secondary corroboration).

### F9 - A concept imported from another frame is a binding, not a new primitive

Fig 6 :21: **"MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom."**
Fig 6 :23: **"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."**
Fig 8 :50-52: **"Convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents. Only the record of where each came from separates them, and the surplus lives only where the closures differ."**

To say "this slot is governed by *blat*, or *guanxi*, or *competing legal pluralism*" is to carry a concept from another frame. That is allowed and often necessary, but it is an import with a price: it must carry provenance (where it came from) and pass a shadow test (does it explain a gap that was already described?). Without the record, convergent derivation and borrowed label are indistinguishable in the text. The framework's own rule is that import from a live peer is debited against independence and a wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives (Fig 6 :24).

This is also the correction to an overstatement I considered and refused: the "shape, not a name" limit (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6) governs a **missing** primitive. Most of the concepts here are already in the researcher's library as ordinary terms; naming them is a binding, not the invention of a seventh term. What is forbidden is pretending the binding is a derivation or that it moves the wall.

**Refuter:** show that a named informal practice was derived from the residual alone, with no contact with the frame that supplies the word, and that it adds no outside provenance to the record. Then the import charge is retracted.

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated (Fig 6 :21-24 + Fig 8 :50-52).

### F10 - Inward and outward are two instruments and must not be read against each other

Fig 6 :29: **"inward: is our representation adequate to hold it? The instrument extends access. Fails by refining forever on an axis that cannot carry it. Sharpen in our own terms."**
Fig 6 :30: **"outward: does another frame already hold it? The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."**
Fig 6 :32: **"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."**
Fig 6 :34-35: **"The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve. Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there."**

For the informal rule, the inward track is: can our current coding scheme represent a sanction that is not a legal sanction, a rule that is not written, a forum that is not a court? If the scheme has no slot for "enforced outside officially sanctioned channels," refining the scheme on the same axis will not create one. The outward track is: which other frame already holds that shape (legal anthropology, sociolegal studies, public administration)? Describe the residual in effect terms first, because a description in our own jargon fails to travel (Fig 6 :30).

There is a genuine proto-instrument here: field researchers already return "this is what I saw happen at the desk," which fails to resolve the source but gives an edge to extend. So this is not underivable in principle; it is `unreached` at the present budget for the present case.

**Refuter:** show that the existing instrument already resolves the source of the reading for the slot (it distinguishes which practice produced the behaviour) and that no new frame is needed. Then the outward search is unnecessary.

**Mode:** `specified`, triangulated (Fig 6 :29-35 + Fig 8 :54 "underivable is not unknowable").

### F11 - A chain of agreeing area studies is one long room

Fig 7 :12-13: **"same closure, handed along; agreement compounds, evidence does not"**
Fig 7 :35: **"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."**
Fig 7 :36: **"A chain of mirrors is one long room. The next room must differ from the last, and match the residual it surfaced."**

Comparative research often proceeds by accumulating country studies that use the same instrument and the same vocabulary. Each additional case feels like progress; under Fig 7 it is motion. A room's agreement is variation, and the next room must differ from the last *and* match the residual the last one surfaced. The design implication is a schedule of deliberately different closures, selected against the residual, not a larger sample of the same closure.

**Refuter:** exhibit a multi-case literature whose cases differ in closure (different instruments, different evidence pools) and whose convergence still tracks the residual at the ground. Then the mirror diagnosis does not apply to that literature.

**Mode:** `general`, triangulated (Fig 7 :12-13 + :35-36 + Fig 2 :31).

### F12 - No figure line returns the identity or magnitude of the winning informal rule (gap)

Fig 13 :134: **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"**
Fig 13 :135: **"It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."**
Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**

After the full re-census, no figure line selects *which* informal rule governs a given slot, how much of the outcome variance it accounts for, or what the correct coding categories are for a specific country. The figures return the shape (an operative rule in contact with the slot, with a sanction, a community and a non-updating anchor) and the discipline for checking it. They cannot name the rule, and they do not license a universal percentage. Fig 13 :136-138 is explicit: **"What it can do is narrow. It can say the question is not one question and must be split before it is searched again. It can characterise what is missing in effect terms, which is the description that travels to a frame that may hold it. And it can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands, so something has to be imported before it is asked again."**

This is a `gap` of the **unreachable-at-this-budget / shape-not-a-name** kind, not underivable in principle: a source-discriminating instrument for the slot (F2, F8) would move it. Report it, do not fill it.

**Refuter:** exhibit a figure line that, after re-census, governs the identity of the rule or the numerical share, and show that it does not strain. Until one is produced, the gap stands.

**Mode:** `general`, single-source for the figure limit itself (Fig 13 + Fig 9), corroborated by the world measurement sources.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required by the seat rule that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. The prediction failure is uniformly distributed across case-slots, with no slot carrying positive mass, and a slot-indexed term reduces nothing (refutes F1).
2. A candidate anchor for a slot is shown to move when the researchers' or respondents' beliefs change while the outcome is held fixed, and no non-updating anchor can be named for that slot (refutes F2).
3. Two readings whose agreement was used as corroboration are shown to share a source, respondent pool or coding scheme (refutes F3).
4. A validated instrument registers the affordance and the progress criterion at the slot, with a reported error rate, so the middle link is no longer uninstrumented (refutes F4).
5. The formal/informal patterns form a clean partition with zero overlap and a single lever clears all of them (refutes F5).
