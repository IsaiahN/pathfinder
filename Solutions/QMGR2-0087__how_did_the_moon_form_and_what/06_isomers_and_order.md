# Are the formation variants isomers? Arrangement, order and analogues

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The world literature presents several formation paths that reproduce the same compositional reading: the canonical grazing giant impact, the direct collision, the synestia, the terrestrial magma ocean, the equilibration scenario, and historically capture, fission and co-accretion. Two questions are owed: (1) are these different substances or different arrangements of the same atoms, and (2) what order do the domains of the derivation run in?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held for this derivation are the geochemical and dynamical observables from D1 of `01`: {small iron core, volatile depletion, deep magma ocean / anorthositic crust, Earth-like O and Ti isotopes, high Earth-Moon angular momentum, proto-Earth mantle material, impactor material, debris disk, silicate vapour atmosphere, degree of equilibration}. Bonds held are the seven plus negation, but the ones actually used are `→` (sequence) between domains, `+` (conjunction) among constraints, and `⋛` (comparison) when a threshold is tested. No new primitive is available without import.

## DERIVATION

### Q1. Are the variants isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 13 :121: "Different routes can arrive at the same substance."
- Fig 13 :122: "In chemistry this has been established since Liebig and Wöhler found two substances with the same composition and different properties, 1824 to 1826, and Berzelius named it isomerism in 1830."

All the giant-impact variants use the same atom set listed above. They differ in which bond holds between proto-Earth and impactor material (glancing versus direct), whether a vapour atmosphere equilibrates the reservoirs, whether a transient doughnut-shaped structure intervenes, and which reservoir dominates the debris. By Fig 12's definition they are isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different behaviour. The behaviour is decisive because each variant predicts different isotope mixing fractions and volatiles retention, which is what measurements can test.

- The historical alternatives are a different case. Capture and co-accretion do not share the impact atom set (no debris disk from a single collision, no silicate vapour atmosphere from that event). They are not isomers of the giant-impact family; they are different molecules over a partially different atom set. Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance." Different ingredients give different substances, not isomers.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a candidate path requires an atom absent from the set (the synestia's new transient object state is the standing test case, as is any additional reservoir invoked to explain isotope similarity), the isomer claim fails for that path and the relation becomes composition plus import (Fig 6 :23). The claim is therefore `specified` and testable, not a classification by assertion.

### Q2. What is the order of the derivation?

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."

The dependency chain for this question is:

1. **Perception / reading (D1).** Can we tell what is in front of us and apart from what was there before? The returned samples and spectra do this. Fig 3 :5: "the system can tell what is in front of it, and tell it apart from what was there before."
2. **Vocabulary / affordances.** Do we have terms for what the observables afford? Yes: isotope ratio, volatile depletion, core mass fraction, magma ocean, angular momentum. Fig 3 :7: "it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined, blocked."
3. **Objective / progress.** Can we say what would count as progress here? Yes: a formation frame that reproduces the constraint set, plus an instrument that discriminates among variants. Fig 3 :10: "it can say what would count as progress here, in its own terms."
4. **Planning / source discrimination.** Choose among candidate paths under incomplete information, and pick the measurement that would discriminate. This is the link where the question currently stops for the variant.
5. **Learn and carry.** Name what worked, verify it, carry it to the next problem (Fig 3 :15). New sample returns carry forward as head starts, not replays (Fig 4 :22).

The chain breaks, for the specific variant, between 3 and 4. The objective is statable (reproduce the constraints) but the instrument that selects among the variants is not yet decisive. Fig 3 :27: "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at." A reading taken below that break, a date or an angle asserted without a discriminating instrument, is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22).

### Q3. Which bonds are load-bearing?

- Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
- Fig 12 :46: "A bond is the typed relation between two operands."
- Fig 12 :92: "Symmetry is what decides whether an arrangement is a new thing."

The constraints combine under `+` (conjunction), which is symmetric: the core-mass slot and the volatile slot can be listed in any order and the constraint set is the same (Fig 12 :59: "swap the operands. Does the meaning change? No."). The domains of the derivation combine under `→` (sequence), which is ordered: reading before source before inference (Fig 12 :74: "swap them. Does it become a different thing? Yes."). Confusing these two bonds is the error of writing the question as an unordered list of facts. A fact list can name a state; it cannot name a route (Fig 12 :111: "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one.").

### Q4. Is the early-Earth inference a second-order composition?

- Fig 12 :103: "Composition is recursive, and the level decides what is irreducible."
- Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world."

Each first-order result, the compositional reading and the source ranking, is settled against the ground to the extent the samples allow. Each can therefore serve as an atom for the early-Earth inference at the next level. The inference is second-order and is licensed by Fig 12's settled-molecule rule rather than by any new primitive.

The round trip is lossy. Fig 4 :30: "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked." What is carried up from the reading is a coarse shape; what comes back down as an early-Earth claim omits the impact-model assumptions that generated it. `11_audit.md` records this round trip gap R_T qualitatively.

**Type caveat, still open.** The `⋛` comparison bond is defined as "a threshold relation between two quantities" (Operators table :49). At second order the operands are settled readings now serving as atoms. Whether those count as quantities at that level needs an instrument, not an argument. This is a strain, named rather than hidden, and is carried into `08` and `09`.

### Q5. What would settle which isomer holds?

Fig 12 :125: "A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add." Fig 12 :126: "Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."

Describing a variant is cheap in bits; discriminating it is expensive in actions (new samples, new instruments, new simulations). The two currencies do not add, so a team holding only description cost will propose arrangements it cannot afford to test (Fig 12 :127). The discriminator the world literature itself points to is new samples from the far side and poles (NASA), which is an action-cost item. Agreement among models trained on the same Apollo pool is cheap in both currencies and buys nothing (Fig 2 :40).

## CONCLUSION

The giant-impact variants are **isomers over a fixed atom set**, distinguished by arrangement, not ingredients, with a stated falsifier for any path that needs a new atom. The historical alternatives (capture, fission, co-accretion) are not isomers of that family because they use a partially different atom set. The derivation order is reading, vocabulary, objective, source discrimination, carry; it currently stops between objective and source discrimination for the specific variant, so that slot is `gap`, not silence. The early-Earth inference is second-order and licensed by the settled-molecule rule, with a named type strain on `⋛` and a nonzero round trip gap. Bits and actions are held apart.

This framing rescues what is defensible in the ordinary answer without the part that fails: the five constraint groups and the family shape need no derivation of a specific impactor, no direct sampling of early Earth, and no figure-forced number. "Giant-impact variants are isomers" remains a checkable structural claim; "we know which one happened and when" remains a world measurement with its own provenance.
