**Subject:** Check one output on lunar formation before you decide whether the frame is worth reading

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about lunar origin. I am not asking you to read any theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`):** the question "how did the Moon form and what can its composition tell us about early Earth" is two conditions with different anchors, not one question with one answer. The first is settled by returned samples, remote spectra and lunar laser ranging. The second is settled by independent constraints on the pre-impact Earth. They overlap, so one measurement can clear one slot and leave the other standing.
>
> The figure-grounded shape is this: a high-energy, late, mantle-derived, volatile-depleted, small-iron-core event whose debris re-accretes. That is a *shape*, not the name of a variant. The composition of the Moon constrains it but does not, by inspection, pick out one formation path, because several paths can yield the same compositional reading. A multi-receptor reading cannot recover the full spectrum behind it, and the compositional readouts are the same kind of lossy reading. What the framework returns is the narrowed form of the answer, not the answer itself.
>
> On the second half: the Moon's composition is a *head start* about the pre-impact Earth, not a replay of it. Earth's active geology erased its own formation record; the Moon preserved a slower one. But the Moon is a recording of one occasion and a lossy transform, so it cannot be read as a direct sample of early Earth's bulk state. The FeO and isotope difficulties are exactly why the direct reading fails.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that the figures derive the identity of Theia, an impact angle, a date, or a percentage of proto-Earth material. Those are world measurements and model outputs, not figure results. I am not claiming the giant-impact family is "the one true mechanism" from the framework. It is the favored world frame, and favorability is a filter for what gets tried, not a witness for the verdict. I am not claiming this is grounded. No measurement here was taken by me.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim:**
> 1. a compositional signature shown to be uniquely produced by one formation path, with the exclusivity demonstrated and the measurement made;
> 2. two independent sample sets with genuinely different evidence pools yielding verdicts that do not shrink with effort, which would force a split;
> 3. the Moon's composition matching early Earth under several incompatible early-Earth states with no possible discriminator, which would collapse the head-start reading;
> 4. a figure line that directly governs an impact parameter, which would close the gap I am reporting.
>
> I wrote these before sending this. If any one holds, the shape is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because ..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Local figure text with line numbers: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in the Project Pathfinder folder.
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`.
