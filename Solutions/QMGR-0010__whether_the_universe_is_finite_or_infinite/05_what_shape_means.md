## 1. A shape is a constraint you can state before you can name

The canonical README phrase is "what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)". Fig 9 :32 says "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself", and Fig 13 :133 says "what it returns is a shape rather than a name".

From *The Library of Babel*: "you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."

Applied here, a shape is:

> locally flat; if compact, the fundamental domain is larger than the observed last-scattering radius; if multiply connected, the identifications are undetected at current resolution; boundedness and connectivity are separate slots; the instrument that would close them is named.

That is a constraint set. "The universe is an infinite Euclidean 3-space" and "the universe is a Poincaré dodecahedral space" are names. The first is a shape, the second two are not.

## 2. How the shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*, on vocabulary bootstrapping: "use the existing vocabulary you do know that is closest to the words that you wish to describe and grasp the shape of." Here the closest held vocabulary is Fig 13's "near, far, between, contained" plus the six-term set. You do not start with a topology catalogue.
- *Library of Babel*, on recursion: "With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."
- Fig 9 :27-28: "describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit." Hence the residual in `01` is described before Planck or any catalogue is consulted.
- Fig 9 :30-31: "your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause." The effect is: matched circles would appear or would not; the cause is a global topology that is not observed directly.

## 3. What a shape is not: the bundle case

*The Library of Babel* names this failure exactly: "Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."

This is the essay-level counterpart of Fig 9 :54 ("And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again"). "Finite or infinite, and what its global topology is" is such a bundle: curvature, boundedness and connectivity. A shape for the bundle is a set of three constrained slots, not one adjective.

## 4. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: "the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground." For topology that would be a detection (matched circles, repeated images) or an instrument that discriminates compact from noncompact at the relevant scale.
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23), with source gate and shadow test. Importing the name "3-torus" from the mathematics frame into Γ adds no new atom if flatness and identification are already expressible; it is a binding, not a wall-moving import (Fig 6 :26, "An import adds no atom to the world").

*The Hard Problem is About Source* gives the instrument rule in its own terms: "What if you have metrics that do not discriminate about its inputs?" and "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information." A flat local reading can come from Euclidean space or from a 3-torus. It cannot comment on the source. And: "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists." This is the essay-level counterpart of Fig 6 :27 (depth versus absence) and Fig 8 :39 (null is not absence).

## 5. Narrowing versus naming: the "shape, not a name" limit is about missing primitives

The exemplar records a correction that applies here too. The README's phrase governs a *missing* primitive, one not held in Γ. Flatness, adjacency, and containment are already held (Fig 13 :9-10, :35). Naming "flat 3-manifold" is therefore a binding onto held atoms, not the invention of a seventh term. What remains forbidden is:

- inventing "boundedness" or "infiniteness" as a new primitive (Fig 13 :41-44);
- claiming a null search proves absence (Fig 8 :39);
- claiming the framework names one topology (Fig 13 :133).

I initially read "shape, not a name" as banning all naming in this solution. **Corrected here, visibly:** it bans naming a *missing primitive*, not describing arrangements of held atoms. Both readings are recorded so the narrowing is checkable.

## 6. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: "Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige." A list of famous cosmologists who agree with a topology is the named collapse. So `01` and this file are simulated derivations checked for internal consistency against the figures. They cannot settle the question they report on.

## 7. What would settle it

From *The Hard Problem is About Source*: "It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument." Here that means an instrument that returns a discrimination where matched circles returned a null at increasing resolution: a source-discriminating statistic for global identifications, or an independent probe whose readings are not projections of the same last-scattering surface. Until then the answer stops at the horizon and the lineage is recorded. "Indiscernibility is not identity."
