## DERIVATION

### Q1: Are the candidate spaces isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- World anchor (secondary): the cosmology reference states that a 3-torus "has everywhere zero curvature but is finite in extent, whereas a flat simply connected space is infinite in extent"; the 3-manifold reference states that a 3-torus "is a three-dimensional compact manifold with no boundary" and has "finite volume and no boundary".
- ⇒ Flat Euclidean 3-space and a flat 3-torus hold the same local atoms (zero curvature, local adjacency) and differ only in how those atoms are bonded globally (periodic identification). That is exactly Fig 12's isomer: invisible locally, decisive globally. "Flat 3-manifold" is the functional group; "Euclidean" or "3-torus" is the qualifier.
- Fig 13 :115: **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not."** The catalogue of flat 3-manifolds is an unbounded set of arrangements over a closed generative set.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if a purely local measurement discriminated a compact flat manifold from Euclidean space, the isomer claim fails and flatness would settle boundedness. Equivalently, if compactness were a seventh indivisible primitive, the arrangement reading fails.

The break is between 3 and 4, illustrated only (Fig 3 :18: "Where a given chain stops is a reading, never a property of the chain"). A reading taken below the break is not a weak reading, it is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22-23). So the honest stop is at the instrument, not at a topology verdict.

**Falsifier for the order claim:** if an instrument already returns a discrimination between compact and noncompact flat spaces, link 4 is reached and the chain extends; the stop claim is then withdrawn.

### Q3: Is ⋛ the right bond for the curvature comparison?

- Operators table: ⋛ is **"a threshold relation between two quantities"**; the test is "is it about which is larger, rather than both being present? Yes."
- Measured Ω_k versus the flat reference Ω_k = 0 is exactly a threshold comparison between two quantities, so ⋛ is licensed there. This is a **comparison of numbers**, not of manifolds.
- Applying ⋛ to "compactness versus noncompactness" would be a type error: those are not two quantities at the same level. The world source makes the point indirectly: curvature constraints do not determine topology.
- **Falsifier:** exhibit a definition under which compactness is a quantity comparable by ⋛ to flatness at the same level. Until then the bond is refused.

### Q4: Does the union of the three questions need a new loop?

- Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
- Fig 9 :57-58: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."
- ⇒ Bundledness and connectivity are overlapping conditions. Their intersection (flat plus compact plus multiply connected, for example) is a **specific compound** with its own loop, not a harder flatness question. This is why the three verdicts in `01` are never averaged.
- **Falsifier:** if compactness and connectivity were independent of curvature everywhere, the union would be a partition and unbundling would be optional. A theorem to that effect would simplify this derivation.

## CONCLUSION

The candidate global spaces are Fig 12 **isomers** over the shared local atoms: same local geometry, different global bonding, different boundedness. The correct reading of "flat 3-manifold" is a functional group, with the named manifold as the qualifier. The three questions are ordered by dependency and the chain currently stops at link 4, the instrument, which returns only a null. ⋛ is licensed for the curvature comparison and refused for the topology comparison. The union is a compound with its own loop, so the verdicts stay separate.

This rescues what is defensible in the naive "flat means infinite" claim (flatness is a real, settled local reading) without importing the part that fails (boundedness and connectivity are not settled by it).
