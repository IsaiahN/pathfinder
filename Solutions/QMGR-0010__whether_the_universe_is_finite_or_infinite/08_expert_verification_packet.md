**What this is:** a claim in a form a cosmologist or geometric topologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Finite or infinite, and what the global topology is" is a union of three overlapping conditions, not one question: curvature, boundedness and connectivity. Curvature is settled locally and consistent with flat. Flatness does not decide boundedness, because a flat compact 3-manifold and flat Euclidean 3-space hold the same local geometry and differ only in global arrangement, which is Fig 12's isomer. The matched-circle searches returned nulls with stated limits, and a null search is not a proof of absence: from inside the CMB frame, compact-but-larger-than-the-sky and noncompact look the same. So the honest verdict is **unreached at this budget**, with three separate domain verdicts and the slot left open. The framework returns a shape, not a name.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: union and isomer; checkable by a cosmologist or topologist

- **Statement:** flatness does not determine compactness; the candidate spaces are Fig 12 isomers over the same local atoms.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117, "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 13 :115, "The set is closed. The arrangements are not." Fig 9 :54, "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- **What to check:** does a flat compact 3-manifold really share all local curvature readings with Euclidean 3-space? (The secondary anchor: a 3-torus "has everywhere zero curvature but is finite in extent".) Is the union reading correct, given that positive curvature forces compactness while flatness does not?
- **Refuter:** a purely local measurement that discriminates the two, or a derivation of "boundedness" as a seventh indivisible primitive.

### C2: null result as limit; checkable against the published analyses

- **Statement:** the Planck and WMAP nulls bound topology scale; they do not prove infiniteness or simple connectivity.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 8 :39, "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 6 :27, "Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."
- **What to check:** Planck reports no compact topology below the last-scattering diameter, with `R_i > 0.97 chi_rec` for the cubic torus (99% CL from polarisation alone); Cornish et al. report no matched circles above 25 degrees and exclude topology scales below 24 Gpc for a wide class of models. Are those limits, or do the papers assert absence?
- **Refuter:** show that the CMB frame is a sealed room, that is, that a finite search over its closure can prove a term absent.

### C3: shape, not a name; framework-internal

- **Statement:** the framework cannot return a unique topology from the six-term set; it returns a constrained shape and an instrument requirement.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :32, "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 13 :133, "what it returns is a shape rather than a name". Fig 13 :134, "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence".
- **What to check:** does the derivation in `01` and `06` actually avoid naming a topology, and does it keep the three verdicts separate? Check that the fourth slot (the discriminating instrument) is present.
- **Refuter:** a derivation of a unique topology from the six, or a ground reading that settles one (which would narrow, not merely confirm, this claim).

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that the universe is infinite, finite, simply connected or multiply connected.
- Not that flatness implies infiniteness. That shortcut was retracted in `01` §6.
- Not that the Planck or WMAP nulls prove absence. They are limits with confidence levels.
- Not that space is discrete because "continuum" was removed as a seventh primitive (Fig 13 :43 governs generativity only).
- Not a final room or a theory of everything. Fig 7 :6: "the limit: no last room."
