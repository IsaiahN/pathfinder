## 0. The claim under audit, stated as an effect

The question asks for a verdict on two global properties of space: boundedness (finite or infinite) and connectivity (simply connected or multiply connected, which is what "global topology" asks). It presumes that a local measurement of curvature plus a finite sky can decide them.

Bound as an ordinary term so it can accrue a residual (formula "Direction"): an answer is a four-tuple `(local curvature, boundedness, connectivity, the instrument that decides the last two)`. Mode `specified`. What each option leads to:

- **A, assert a verdict now.** A null matched-circle search is read as "no compact topology", flatness is read as "infinite", and a topology is named. This breaks Fig 8 :39, breaks Fig 9 :32, and converts a budget limit into an absence claim.
- **B, unbundle and narrow.** Three conditions are separated, each gets its own verdict and refuter, the null result is labelled depth-or-absence (undecidable from inside), and the fourth slot is left open with an instrument requirement. This is what the figures license.

The audit below derives B before consulting any world source.

## 2. Domain census (before choosing any conclusion)

### 2.1 R by arity, symmetry and scale (mode `general`)

- **Arity: three, not two.** The question bundles (i) local geometry/curvature, (ii) boundedness, (iii) connectivity. These are three conditions whose intersections matter. This is a **union with overlap**, not a partition: positive curvature forces compactness, while flatness permits both compact and noncompact. Per Fig 9 :54 the union must be unbundled before search.
- **Symmetry.** The matched-circle statistic is symmetric in its two circles (a pair of readings, a conjunction). Model elimination over candidate topologies is ordered and reversible (Fig 9 :40-41, "keep every cut ranked and reversible"). Curvature is a threshold comparison against a flat reference, which is ⋛ ("a threshold relation between two quantities", Operators table), not a symmetric join.
- **Scale.** Two strata: the local sky inside the last-scattering surface, and the global manifold whose topology scale may exceed it. The transform between them is lossy (Fig 4 :30). Treating one as the other is the dead-reckoning error.

### 2.2 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | question type | verdict status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1, observational cosmology | last-scattering surface, CMB photons, Planck and WMAP instruments, Ω_k, matched-circle statistic | angular power spectra, likelihood ratios | the measured sky, which does not move when a model pushes on it | measurement, partition at the horizon | `grounded` in the literature, restated here at `specified` |
| 2, mathematical topology | 3-manifolds, fundamental group, flat compact quotients, injectivity radius, Thurston geometries | proof | theorem | union classification | `general`, proof-grounded |
| 3, framework-internal composition | the six terms, seven bonds, negation, closure(Γ) | composition and settlement | the figures | which arrangements are composable | `general` structural, `specified` when applied here |
| 4, verification and outreach | expert readers, opt-in venues | agreement and residual | the ground, never agreement | checking arrangement | `gap` on settlement in this run |

No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet. Domain 4's anchor is present (the ground) but this simulated run never reaches it, so its settlement verdict is `gap`, honestly reported. All four domains are retained.

### 2.3 Partition or union

Union with overlap (section 2.1). A single lever, the flatness measurement, clears the region where only curvature is at issue and leaves the intersections (flat plus compact, flat plus multiply connected) standing. Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds." So the answer to "is it flat?" is not an answer to "is it finite?".

### 2.4 Dependency order (Fig 3)

1. Describe the residual before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28).
2. Unbundle the union before searching (Fig 9 :54).
3. Settle local geometry before making any global inference (domain 1 before domains 2 and 3).
4. Distinguish underivable-in-principle from unreachable-at-this-budget before importing (Fig 8 :40).
5. Refuse a premature name before minting one (formula step 2).

Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not (Fig 3 :19). Arguing about which named topology is correct while step 2 is undone is effort above the break.

## 3. Findings F1 to F9

### F1: The question is a union, and must be unbundled (decisive, `general`)

Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."** Fig 9 :55: **"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones. A single lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds."**

Consequence: report three separate verdicts: curvature (measured), boundedness (unknown), connectivity (unknown). Never average them into "the universe is finite/infinite".

**Refuter:** if a single local measurement determined both boundedness and connectivity, unbundling would be unnecessary and this finding would be void. A theorem of that form would refute it.

### F2: Same local geometry, different global topology is Fig 12's isomer (decisive, `general`)

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."** Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

A flat 3-torus and flat Euclidean 3-space share every local reading (same curvature atoms) and differ only in global bonding (periodic identification versus none), which is decisive for boundedness. "Flat 3-manifold" is the functional group; the particular manifold is the qualifier. This is the exact framework reason why flatness cannot settle finiteness. World corroboration (secondary): the cosmology reference says a 3-torus "has everywhere zero curvature but is finite in extent, whereas a flat simply connected space is infinite in extent" (Wikipedia, *Shape of the universe*, 2026).

**Refuter:** a purely local measurement that discriminates a 3-torus from Euclidean space would break the isomer claim. Equivalently, if compactness were a seventh primitive rather than an arrangement, this finding fails.

### F3: Finiteness is an arrangement, not a primitive (`general`)

Fig 13 :43: **"A continuum was the seventh and was removed for failing exactly this: it is a spectrum without gaps, or smoothness over space and time."** Fig 13 :115: **"The set is closed. The arrangements are not."** Fig 13 :118: **"So a frame can search without end and still not arrive: infinite effort inside a finite reach."**

"Infinite" and "finite" do not name members of the six. Space is a state whose local predicates are "near, far, between, contained" (Fig 13 :9-10); global boundedness is a composition over those predicates and a structure, not a new atom. Searching a catalogue of topologies at one resolution cannot manufacture a new primitive (Fig 6 :21).

**Important non-claim:** Fig 13 :43 removing "continuum" says a continuum is not *generative*. It does not say space is discrete, and it does not decide any physical Planck-scale question. Read strictly as a claim about the minimum set.

**Refuter:** derive a seventh primitive "boundedness" from the six that is indivisible and load-bearing, or show the six force one global topology. Either breaks this finding.

### F4: A null search is not an absence proof (decisive, `specified`)

Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."** Fig 9 :44: **"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."**

The Planck search found no compact topology at a scale below the last-scattering surface and set `R_i > 0.97 chi_rec` for the cubic torus (Planck Collaboration 2015/2016, arXiv:1502.01593). Cornish et al. found no matched circles above 25 degrees and excluded topology scales below 24 Gpc for a wide class of models (PRL 92, 201302, 2004). These are **limits**, not verdicts of infiniteness. From inside the CMB frame, "compact but larger than our sky" and "not compact" look identical.

**Refuter:** show that the CMB frame is a sealed room, that is, that a finite search over its closure can prove a term absent. Fig 9 :44 says it cannot; a proof otherwise refutes this finding.

### F5: Depth versus genuine absence (decisive, `specified`)

Fig 6 :27: **"Which is why some unreachability is depth and some is genuine absence, and from inside the frame the two look the same."** Fig 8 :40: **"Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions."** Fig 6 :32: **"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."**

There is already a proto-instrument: matched circles return a null at a stated resolution, so there is an edge to extend. What is forbidden is inventing a sensor from a description of what we would like to see (Fig 6 :34-35: "Where nothing is, there is no edge to extend from, and the search for a proto-instrument is a search for one that is already there"). The honest classification is **unreached at this budget**, not "underivable in principle" and not "absent". Importing a new primitive against a budget limit would add an atom that was not needed (Fig 8 :40-43).

**Refuter:** a specified instrument that returns a reading from beyond the last-scattering surface without being improved from an already-returning one would collapse the depth/absence distinction and refute this finding.

### F6: The anchor is the measurement, and the two instruments are genuinely different frames (`specified`)

Fig 2 :29: **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."** Fig 2 :31: **"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."** Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

Planck and WMAP are different instruments aimed at the same sky. Under Fig 2 :31 that counts as independence in hardware, so their agreement is weak triangulation, not a single report read twice. But they share the same physical sky as ground, so their agreement is **one witness about the ground**, not two independent grounds, and their null results still fall under F4. The model catalogue is not an anchor: if a topology "prediction" moved when a theorist pushed on it, it is a frame.

**Refuter:** show that the reported flatness or matched-circle limits change with a model prior rather than with the data, and they cease to be anchors.

### F7: Shape, not a name (`general`)

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**. Fig 13 :133: **"what it returns is a shape rather than a name"**. Fig 5 :33: **"The three guards are a product, not a checklist."**

The framework can return: "the space is locally flat; if compact, its fundamental domain exceeds the observed last-scattering radius; if multiply connected, the identifications are undetected at current resolution." It cannot return "the topology is the Poincaré dodecahedral space" or "the universe is infinite". Such a name would need a ground settlement (formula step 5) or a provenanced import with a shadow test (Fig 6 :23), neither of which is held.

**Refuter:** if the six terms alone yielded a unique topology, or if a candidate were settled by the ground, the shape-only limit would be narrowed or broken. Narrowing by a ground reading is an update, not a refutation of the limit itself.

### F8: No last room (`general`)

Fig 7 :6: **"the limit: no last room"**. Fig 7 :30: **"every room closes some of the gap; no finite number of them ever arrives"**. Fig 4 :33: **"Which is why there is no top and no infinite regress."**

A final, all-settling verdict on global topology would be a terminal room. The figures forbid it. This is a structural limit on the answer form, not an assertion that the question is meaningless. A room (a survey, a catalogue, a theorem) closes part of the gap and is selected by the residual it surfaced, not by agreement (Fig 7 :35).

**Refuter:** a frame with no outside left that settles global topology would break this finding.

### F9: The round trip gap R_T is nonzero (`general`)

Fig 4 :30: **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."**

- **Sent up (x):** the verbatim figure set (Fig 6 :27, Fig 8 :39, Fig 9 :32/:54, Fig 12 :117, Fig 13 :43/:115).
- **Abstracted (T_A):** "flat to 0.4 percent or better, no matched circles; topology remains a shape".
- **Concretised (T_E):** "the universe is infinite and simply connected".

Three constraints do not survive the trip back down: (i) flatness does not imply noncompactness (F2), (ii) a null search is not an absence proof (F4), (iii) the union must be unbundled before verdicts are stated (F1). What survives cleanly: a structure sits between local and global claims, and the set is closed while arrangements are not. R_T was not measured numerically because no ground reading was taken here; it is recorded qualitatively as the enumerated set of dropped constraints. Treating "the formula is the condensed version of the metalaws" as identity would set R_T to zero and commit the dead-reckoning error.

**Refuter:** if "flat means infinite" survived the return trip with no loss, R_T would be zero and this finding fails.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required by the seat discipline: "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device." The shape claim is refuted or narrowed if any of the following holds:

1. **Union refuter.** A single local measurement determines both boundedness and connectivity (breaks F1).
2. **Isomer refuter.** A purely local reading distinguishes a flat compact 3-manifold from Euclidean 3-space, or compactness is derived as a seventh primitive (breaks F2 and F3).
3. **Absence refuter.** The CMB frame is shown to be a sealed room, so a null search proves absence (breaks F4 and F5).
4. **Name refuter.** The six terms alone yield a unique topology, or a ground reading settles one (narrows F7).

None had fired at the time of writing. If any fires, the relevant finding is withdrawn and the verdict re-derived from the census, not softened.
