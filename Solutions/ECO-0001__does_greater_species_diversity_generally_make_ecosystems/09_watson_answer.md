## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the two options. Option A: one aggregate species count maps monotonically to one system property called stability, so adding species generally stabilises ecosystems. Option B: the effect is conditional on which species are present. What each leads to: A licenses a simple lever (act on the count) but discards identity, interaction structure, conditions and the choice of stability metric; B keeps identity but is under-specified until the residual is named. The shape an answer must have is a statement of the form "for residual R, under habitat H, varying quantity Q changes stability property P by sign and magnitude M", with all five slots named.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

Neither option survives as the question is posed. The figure-grounded answer is a third shape: the question is a union compound of an anchor, a cast with its bonds, and a habitat with its conditions and cascades. Species richness is a symmetric aggregate that cannot carry the ordered interaction mechanism or tell you where to act. Valence is relative to the residual, so no species is stabilising in general and no standing list of good species survives a change of metric. Stability belongs to the community and habitat, and the habitat must be enumerated. Within a specified habitat and metric, higher richness often stabilises the aggregate property; change the cast, bonds, conditions or metric and the sign can change. Both are within-condition findings, not the general law the question asks for. The sign inside a named habitat is `unreached`, not `grounded`, and the verdict is `specified`.

- **cost if wrong:** if the union verdict is wrong and a general law holds, management loses a cheap and powerful lever and conservation messaging loses its simplest argument. If the general-rule option is wrongly accepted, effort is spent adding species that do not change contact while the real drivers (interaction structure, conditions, cascades) are left untouched. Fig 6 warns that a wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives. The cost is bounded because the packet states its refuters before its conclusion, so an error is findable rather than invisible.
- **undoable:** yes. The verdict is a decomposition, not a deletion. Naming the anchor and enumerating the habitat refines it without retracting it. If a general monotone law were established under a single anchor, the union reading would narrow to a partition rather than collapse. Nothing has to be withdrawn from the record.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. Specifically: (1) one stability property and perturbation accepted as the anchor across contexts, with the diversity effect keeping its sign when cast, bonds and conditions are held fixed in several habitats; (2) identical stability for communities with the same species set but different interaction networks under all measured perturbations; (3) a stability change when species are added to a system whose conditions, relations and cascades are demonstrably fixed; (4) an invariant species set with the same sign of effect across metrics and perturbations. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`. More agreement from a shared evidence pool is not such a reading.
