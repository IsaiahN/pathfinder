# Expert verification packet — the Tether on diversity and ecosystem stability

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ecologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the ecological verdict, `general` on the structural rules. Not `grounded`.** No measurement was taken in this derivation. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question "does greater species diversity generally make ecosystems more stable, or does it depend on which species are present" is a union compound of three conditions: which stability property and perturbation is the anchor, which species, traits and interaction bonds are present, and the habitat of conditions and relations that carries contact and cascades. Species richness is a symmetric aggregate over species slots and cannot carry the ordered information of the interaction mechanism. An actor's valence is relative to the residual, so no species is stabilising in general. Stability belongs to the community and habitat, not to the count, and two communities with the same species list but different bonds are different substances. Within a specified habitat and metric, higher richness often stabilises the aggregate property; changing the cast, bonds, conditions or metric can change the sign. Neither option alone, as the question is posed, has the right shape.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1, 9 and 11
- **Statement:** the question is a union compound, so it must be split before search; a count is the aggregate that loses the per-slot signal.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :5 *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 11 :43 *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*
- **Refuter:** show that one stability property is the agreed anchor across contexts and that a single monotone diversity effect holds once it is named; or show that the species count carries the interaction information the figures say it drops.

### C2 — ecology-facing; checkable by an ecologist against the empirical and theoretical literature
- **Statement:** valence depends on the residual, and interaction structure changes the outcome at the same species count. The grassland positive result is a within-condition finding, not a general law.
- **What to check:** Allesina and Tang 2012 find *"remarkable differences between predator-prey interactions, which are stabilizing, and mutualistic and competitive interactions, which are destabilizing."* Tilman et al. 2006 find *"greater numbers of plant species led to greater temporal stability of ecosystem annual aboveground plant production"* via portfolio averaging and overyielding. Ives and Carpenter 2007 say *"the concept of stability is multifaceted; different types of stability describing different properties of ecosystems lead to multiple diversity-stability relationships."* Craven et al. 2018 conclude *"a more multicausal relationship than has been previously acknowledged."* Are those characterisations faithful, and do they support a within-condition reading rather than a general rule?
- **Source, verbatim figure line:** Fig 11 :49 *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :51 *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*
- **Refuter:** a fixed set of species whose sign of effect on stability is invariant across metrics, perturbations and habitats; or a demonstration that the diversity effect keeps the same sign once cast, bonds and conditions vary freely.

### C3 — framework-internal derivation with a world-facing check
- **Statement:** same species set, different bonds, different substance; effects reach second and third order through the habitat.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 11 :52 *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."* Fig 11 :65 *"Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994. Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated."*
- **What to check:** Jones, Lawton and Shachak 1994 define engineers as organisms that *"modify, maintain and create habitats"*. Is the cascade claim faithful to how habitat modification actually works in the systems you know?
- **Refuter:** two communities with identical species sets but different interaction networks, shown to have identical stability under every measured perturbation; or evidence that stability effects in the systems considered are confined to direct contact.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that diversity is irrelevant, or that more species never help.
- Not that identity alone decides, or that a fixed list of stabilising species exists.
- Not that no general law exists. Fig 8 :39 *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken and no dataset was analysed here.
- Not a derivation of ecology from the framework. The binding of "capability" to "stability" is a `specified` mapping, recorded as such.
- Not a claim that the empirical diversity-stability literature is wrong. Its positive result is kept as a within-condition finding.
- Not a verdict from agreement. Fig 2 :40 *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*; this simulated audit shares its evidence pool with the primary derivation.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not an argument or a vote. The four tests, in order of dependency:

1. **Anchor test:** name the stability property and perturbation that all parties accept as the anchor.
2. **Fixed-habitat test:** vary S with cast, conditions and bonds held fixed in several habitats; check whether sign and magnitude are stable.
3. **Isomer test:** compare communities with the same species list and different interaction networks under all measured perturbations.
4. **Contact test:** add species where conditions, relations and cascades are demonstrably fixed; see whether stability changes.
