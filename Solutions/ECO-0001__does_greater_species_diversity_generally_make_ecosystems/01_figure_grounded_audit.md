## 0. The question in effect terms

> Does greater species diversity generally make ecosystems more stable, or does its effect depend on which species are present?

Restated as effects, with what each option leads to:

- **Option A, the general rule.** One aggregate number, species richness S, maps monotonically to one system property, "stability". If true, the actionable lever is S: add species and stability rises, whatever their identities, interactions, conditions, or which stability property is measured. Management can act on a count.
- **Option B, the identity rule.** The effect is conditional on which species are present. If true, the actionable lever is the cast and its bonds, not the count. Management must know the species and their interactions before acting.
- **What an answer would look like (bind the sought-for shape as an ordinary term, Formula "Direction"):** a statement of the form "for residual R, under habitat H, varying quantity Q changes stability property P by sign and magnitude M", with R, H, Q, P and M all named. A bare "more is better" or a bare "it depends" does not meet that shape, because neither names a residual.

**The residual R.** The literature disagrees, and the disagreement does not shrink as more species-count studies are added, because different studies hold different things fixed and measure different properties. That is the signature Fig 9 :5 names: *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* R is therefore a **union compound**, not one hard question, and it has its own loop and its own ground (Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*).

## 1. Domain census

Describe R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor. A quantity with no nameable anchor is not yet a domain.

**R by arity / symmetry / scale.** Arity: at least three conditions, namely (i) the stability property and perturbation (the anchor), (ii) the cast of species, traits and interaction bonds, (iii) the habitat of conditions and relations that carries contact and cascades. Symmetry: the predictor S is invariant under permutation of species identities, so it is a symmetric aggregate over slots; the mechanism (who eats whom, who facilitates whom, who engineers the habitat) is ordered and asymmetric. This mismatch is developed in `06_isomers_and_order.md`. Scale: S is one number over the whole community; the signals that decide stability are per species and per interaction, and their effects reach second and third order through the habitat.

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | partition or union |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1, empirical ecosystem-stability frame | species, populations, habitats, perturbations, field experimenters | variance of biomass production, return rate, resistance, resilience, persistence | the measured time series and the controlled experiment | union of sub-conditions (different stability metrics do not share one anchor) |
| D2, interaction and mechanism frame | species pairs, interaction signs and strengths, network topology | eigenvalues, feasibility, persistence probability | the mathematical stability criterion, checked against the community matrix and then against experiment | union with D1 (overlaps through the same data) |
| D3, habitat and valence frame | actors, conditions, relations | contact and cascade | the ground, which settles what persists; the habitat is read off the world, not composed | union with D1 and D2 (contains both as sub-habitats) |
| D4, verification and outreach frame | expert readers, public submission venues | agreement and residuals | the ground, never agreement | separate loop; in this simulated run all seats share one evidence pool |

**Quantity, not a domain:** species richness S. It has actors (species as interchangeable slots) and a currency (a count), but no anchor of its own. It is a number computed inside D1 and D2, not a domain that can settle anything. Treating it as a domain is how the general-rule option gets its apparent authority.

**Partition or union overall.** Union. The three ecological domains overlap through the same empirical systems, and the overlap is a compound with its own loop (Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*). It must be unbundled before search.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** The chain runs: (1) name the residual, that is, which stability property, which perturbation, which habitat; (2) enumerate the habitat, actors then conditions then relations; (3) only then evaluate what varying S does inside that enumerated habitat; (4) verify against the ground. Effort applied at step 3 before steps 1 and 2 arrives at a step that cannot use it (Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*).

**Strain test applied.** The count-versus-per-slot rule (Fig 1) and the variance-of-an-aggregate result in the grassland literature appear to strain against each other. Re-census shows they govern different quantities: Fig 1 governs the search signal (where to aim a probe), while the grassland "portfolio (statistical averaging)" result governs the variance of a system-level time series. Both are kept separately in §2b. No figure line was forced to fit.

## 2. Findings

### F1 — The question is a union compound, so it must be split before it is searched

Fig 9 :4-5: *"are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*
Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*

The apparent contradiction between "more species stabilises" and "it depends which species" is the signature of a bundled question. Different studies measure different properties (temporal variance of production, resistance, resilience, return rate) on different subsets, so each rule keeps working well on its own subset and the disagreement never shrinks. Splitting is the instruction, not averaging the two options.

- **Mode:** `general` (structural rule), applied here as `specified` to this literature.
- **Triangulation status:** `triangulated` across Fig 9 and the Ives and Carpenter finding that stability is multifaceted (low overlap: a metalaw versus a discipline review).
- **Refuter:** show that one stability property is the agreed anchor across contexts and that a single monotone diversity effect holds once it is held fixed. If so, the union reading collapses to a partition.

### F2 — A diversity count is exactly the aggregate Fig 1 says throws away the signal

Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
Fig 1 :28: *"The gap is measured per object, not per step. A system that averages across objects has thrown away the only signal that says where to look."*

Species richness S is a count over object slots. It is symmetric under permutation of species identities and carries no information about which slot to act on. As a description of a community it is fine; as the lever for a general rule it is the quantity Fig 1 disqualifies for steering. This is the structural reason Option A overclaims.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Triangulation status:** `single-source` for the metalaw (Fig 1), with the symmetry derivation in `06_isomers_and_order.md` as an internal second route, not an independent witness.
- **Refuter:** a demonstration that S alone predicts the sign and magnitude of the stability response across habitats, casts and metrics, with identity and interaction structure varying freely. That would mean the aggregate does carry the signal and Fig 1 has been misapplied.

### F3 — Valence is relative to the residual, so "which species" is not a standing list

Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
Fig 11 :50: *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."*
Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."*

This is the sharpest figure statement bearing on the question. A species is not "stabilising" in itself. Its valence depends on which residual is in play, that is, which stability property is being threatened by which perturbation. So Option B is closer to the figures than Option A, but a bare "it depends which species" is still under-specified: it must say which residual the species is being assessed against. A list of "good species" that survives a change of metric or perturbation would contradict Fig 11 :51.

- **Mode:** `general` for the valence rule, `specified` for its application to stability.
- **Triangulation status:** `triangulated` across Fig 11 and Allesina and Tang 2012 (predator-prey stabilizing, mutualistic and competitive destabilizing: different method, different evidence pool).
- **Refuter:** a fixed set of species whose sign of effect on stability is invariant across perturbations, metrics and habitats, shown experimentally.

### F4 — Stability is a property of the community and habitat, never of diversity alone

Fig 11 :39: *"So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."*
Fig 11 :40: *"A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."*

Read with the domain-agnostic rule (Fig 4 :19: *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change"*), the figure says the stability question is not "how diverse is the community" but "what makes the community-and-habitat stable". Adding species in the abstract, without changing contact (conditions, relations, cascades), changes nothing. This is the framework's reason for refusing a lever that acts on S alone.

- **Mode:** `specified`. The mapping of "capability" to "stability" is a binding from the ecology frame onto a figure term, not a derivation. Recorded as such.
- **Triangulation status:** `single-source` for the figure claim, corroborated weakly by Ives and Carpenter 2007 (diversity-stability relationships cannot be understood outside the environmental drivers affecting both) and by Craven et al. 2018 (a more multicausal relationship).
- **Refuter:** an experiment in which species are added while conditions, relations and cascade structure are demonstrably held fixed, and stability changes. That would refute the contact claim as applied here.

### F5 — The habitat must be enumerated: actors, conditions, relations

Fig 11 :43: *"The habitat is enumerated, never composed."*
Fig 11 :44: *"List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."*
Fig 11 :45: *"actors: what acts on it, and what it acts on"*
Fig 11 :46: *"conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain"*
Fig 11 :47: *"relations: pollination, predation, competition, dependency"*
Fig 11 :48: *"You do not invent the list. You read it off the world, and what you cannot perceive or measure yet is the residual."*

A species count composes actors only. It discards conditions and relations, which are two of the three enumerated categories the figure requires. This is the structural reason the diversity number cannot be the whole answer, and it is why the question must be unbundled before search (F1). The list is read off the world, not generated from a theory of what a community ought to contain.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Triangulation status:** `triangulated` across Fig 11 and Jones, Lawton and Shachak 1994, the very source the figure cites at Fig 11 :65 (different evidence pool: a field-biology paper, not a metalaw).
- **Refuter:** a demonstration that stability is fully determined by the actor list with conditions and relations held constant across all tested habitats.

### F6 — Contact cascades, so first-order enumeration misses the effect that matters

Fig 11 :52: *"And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."*
Fig 11 :65: *"Introducing an actor for its cascade is ecosystem engineering, after Jones, Lawton and Shachak, 1994. Cane toads and kudzu are the same move with the cascade unenumerated."*

A count of species present is first order. Stability effects can be second or third order: a predator removed, a grazer released, a habitat engineered, a nutrient pathway changed. The figure's own example is DDT reaching eagles three steps out. Jones et al. define engineers as organisms that *"modify, maintain and create habitats"*, which is a direct world-facing instance of the figure line. A general count rule cannot represent cascade order.

- **Mode:** `general` for the cascade rule, `specified` for the ecology instance.
- **Triangulation status:** `triangulated` across Fig 11 and Jones et al. 1994 (low overlap: the figure imports the example, the paper supplies the mechanism).
- **Refuter:** evidence that stability effects in the systems considered are confined to direct contact, with no measurable second-order pathway.

### F7 — Same species set, different bonds, different substance

Fig 12 :5: *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
Fig 12 :119: *"catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."*

Two communities with identical species lists but different interaction networks are different substances under Fig 12. The species list records the ingredients and not the arrangement. This is the figure-level version of "which species are present and how they are connected", and it is stronger than Option B as usually stated, because identity alone is still only the ingredient list. The catalyst entry adds that a species which survives its own use behaves differently across uses, which is why a one-off count is a poor predictor.

- **Mode:** `general` (structural analogue), `specified` for the ecology claim.
- **Triangulation status:** `single-source` for the figure definition, with Allesina and Tang 2012 as a low-overlap corroboration (interaction type changes stability hierarchy at the same species count).
- **Refuter:** two communities with identical species sets but different interaction networks, shown to have identical stability under all measured perturbations. That kills the isomer reading.

### F8 — "Stability" has no anchor until a property and a perturbation are named

Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."*
Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*
Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*

Different stability properties are different questions with different anchors. A high-variance but rapidly recovering community and a low-variance but slowly recovering community can swap ranks depending on the metric. Until the question names the property and the perturbation, no ground can settle it, and any literature picked afterwards will seem to fit. What the derivation can return is a shape: a decomposed answer, not a single verdict number.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Triangulation status:** `triangulated` across Fig 2, Fig 9 and Ives and Carpenter 2007 (three low-overlap routes: a metalaw, an epistemological figure, and a discipline review).
- **Refuter:** an agreed single operational definition of ecosystem stability that all parties accept as the anchor across contexts.

### F9 — Agreement from a shared pool is not a witness, and a filter is not a verdict

Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*
Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*
Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*

The empirical grassland experiments and the random-matrix theory papers are two genuinely low-overlap frames: different instruments, different evidence, different closures. Their partial agreement that context matters is weak corroboration only, because both are ecology and both inherit parts of the same tradition. The figures decide the verdict, not the number of citations. In this simulated run, all seats draw on the same evidence pool, so the second seat in `11_audit.md` is a mirror with a different name.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Triangulation status:** `single-source` for the metalaw, applied as a discipline rule.
- **Refuter:** not applicable as a claim about the world; this is a rule about what counts as evidence. It is violated if a verdict is asserted on the strength of pooled agreement alone.

### F10 — Unreachable is a verdict, not silence

Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
Fig 9 :44: *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room."*

If no general diversity-stability law is found under a fixed residual, that is not a failure to report. Here the derivation does reach a verdict (the question is a union compound, so a single general law is the wrong shape), so the honest status is `specified`, not `gap`. The absence of a general rule is not being claimed as a proof that none exists; it is the figure-derived reason why searching for one in the pooled frame is the wrong search.

- **Mode:** `general`.
- **Refuter:** a ground reading that establishes the general law under a single named anchor. That would move the verdict, not silence it.

## 2b. Strain test, re-census and a genuine tension in the world literature

**Tension.** Tilman, Reich and Knops 2006 report that *"greater numbers of plant species led to greater temporal stability of ecosystem annual aboveground plant production"* and that temporal stability rose "because of both portfolio (statistical averaging) and overyielding effects". Read naively, this looks like Option A winning and Fig 1 being wrong.

**Re-census.** Fig 1 :22 governs the search signal: what information is available for deciding where to aim the next probe. The grassland result governs a different quantity: the variance of an aggregate time series over time. Statistical averaging is a real mechanism for reducing aggregate variance, and the figures do not deny it. What Fig 1 denies is that the aggregate tells you which slot needs attention. Both statements are kept, with their quantities named. No quote was strained, and no figure was made to contradict the experiment.

**Second tension.** Fig 11 :39 speaks of "capability" and "agent"; this audit maps those onto "stability" and "community". That is a binding, not a figure-stated equivalence, and it is labelled `specified` in F4. The canonical usage rule says a reading that makes figures contradict each other has misidentified its domain; here nothing contradicts, but the mapping is declared rather than hidden.

**What survives after re-census.** (i) The question is a union compound (F1). (ii) S is a symmetric aggregate that cannot steer a probe (F2, F7). (iii) Valence depends on the residual (F3). (iv) Stability belongs to community-and-habitat and the habitat must be enumerated (F4, F5). (v) Effects cascade beyond direct contact (F6). (vi) No anchor, no verdict (F8). Strains after re-census: one declared binding (capability to stability), one declared quantity distinction (search signal versus aggregate variance). None hidden.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per Fig 10's discipline that a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A single stability property and perturbation can be named that all parties accept as "the" anchor for ecosystem stability, and the diversity effect keeps the same sign and comparable magnitude across all enumerated habitats when S is varied with cast, bonds and conditions held fixed. This collapses the union to a partition.
2. Two communities with identical species sets but different interaction networks are shown to have identical stability under every measured perturbation. This kills the isomer reading in F7.
3. Adding species changes stability in a system where conditions, relations and cascade structure are demonstrably fixed and unchanged. This refutes the contact claim in F4 as applied here.
4. An invariant set of species is shown to have the same sign of effect on stability across metrics, perturbations and habitats. This refutes Fig 11 :49-51 as applied here.

If any fires, the union verdict is withdrawn and the corresponding simpler option is reinstated. Until one is run, none of this is `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Do not answer the question as a single yes/no. Answer the decomposed shape: name the stability property, the perturbation and the habitat first.
2. Treat S as a description within a specified habitat, not as a general lever (F2, F5).
3. Ask "which species, in which relations, for which residual", not "which species are good" (F3, F7).
4. Enumerate actors, conditions and relations before comparing diversity levels; include second and third order contact (F5, F6).
5. Use filters such as tier, ground availability and literature size for the budget, and witnesses (experiments, refuters) for the verdict (Fig 9 :60).
6. Send the decomposition and the refuter, not a conclusion (Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."*).
