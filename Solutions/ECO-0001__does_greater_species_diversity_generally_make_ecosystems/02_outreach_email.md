**Subject:** A figure-derived check on the diversity-stability question, and what would refute it

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read any theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "does greater species diversity generally make ecosystems more stable, or does it depend on which species are present" is not one question. It is a union of at least three conditions: (1) which stability property and which perturbation is the anchor, (2) which species, traits and interaction relations are present, and (3) the habitat of conditions and relations that carries contact and cascades. A species count is a symmetric aggregate over species slots, and the framework's rule is that aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average. So the count can describe a community inside a specified habitat, but it cannot be a general lever for stability. A second rule says an actor's valence is relative to the residual, so no species is stabilising in general, and no standing list of good species survives a change of metric or perturbation.
>
> This predicts that within a fixed habitat and a fixed stability metric, more species often stabilises the aggregate property (which is what the grassland experiments find), while changing the cast, the interaction structure, the conditions or the metric can change the sign (which is what the interaction-matrix results find). Both are within-condition results. Neither is the general law the question asks for. That is a shape and a route, not a measurement.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that diversity never helps, that identity alone decides, that a particular species is always stabilising, that this settles the empirical literature, or that the framework returns a number. It returns a decomposition. The specific binding of the framework's "capability is agent-and-habitat" rule onto ecological stability is my mapping, recorded as such, not something the figures derive.
>
> **What would refute it (written before this email):**
> 1. One stability property and perturbation can be named that everyone accepts as the anchor, and the diversity effect keeps the same sign across habitats when the cast, bonds and conditions are held fixed.
> 2. Two communities with identical species sets but different interaction networks always show identical stability under every measured perturbation.
> 3. Adding species changes stability where conditions, relations and cascade structure are demonstrably fixed and unchanged.
>
> If any one holds, I withdraw the decomposition and reinstate the simpler option it displaces. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
