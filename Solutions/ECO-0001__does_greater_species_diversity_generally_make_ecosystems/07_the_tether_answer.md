# The Tether's answer on diversity and ecosystem stability

**Mode: `specified` on the ecological verdict, `general` on the structural rules. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Two well-supported bodies of work appear to disagree: one finds that higher species richness stabilises ecosystem production, the other finds that the sign of the diversity effect depends on interaction type, context and the stability metric. The disagreement does not shrink as each side adds studies, because each is working on a different subset with a different anchor. Under Fig 9 :5 this is *"not one hard question but several well-formed ones"*, a union compound with its own loop and its own ground. The residual is the bundle, not a single number to be averaged.

## 2. The shape

1. **The question must be split before it is searched.** Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* The three conditions are: which stability property and perturbation is the anchor; which species, traits and interaction bonds are present; and the habitat of conditions and relations that carries contact and cascades. Neither "more is better" nor a bare "it depends" fills this shape.
2. **A species count is the aggregate that loses the signal.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* S is symmetric under permutation of species identities; the mechanism is ordered. That is a type mismatch, developed in `06_isomers_and_order.md`. S can describe a community inside a habitat; it cannot steer an intervention.
3. **Valence is relative to the residual.** Fig 11 :49: *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :51: *"No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."* No species is stabilising in general. "Which species" is only answerable after the stability property and perturbation are named.
4. **Stability belongs to the community and habitat.** Fig 11 :39: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40: *"An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves."* Adding species in the abstract, without changing contact, is not a lever. This binding of "capability" to "stability" is mine, recorded as `specified`.
5. **The habitat is enumerated, not composed.** Fig 11 :45-47 lists actors, conditions (light, water, temperature, season, terrain) and relations (pollination, predation, competition, dependency). A count includes only actors. Effects also cascade three steps out (Fig 11 :52), which a first-order list cannot see.
6. **Same species set, different bonds, different substance.** Fig 12 :117: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Identity alone is an ingredient list; the interaction network is the bond.
7. **No anchor, no verdict.** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks."* Stability is multifaceted. Until the property and perturbation are named, no ground can settle the question and any literature chosen afterwards will seem to fit (Fig 9 :27-28).

**Predicted observation.** Within a fixed habitat, a fixed set of conditions and relations, and a fixed stability metric, higher S often increases the temporal stability of the aggregate property, through asynchrony and overyielding. Change the cast, the bond structure, the conditions or the metric, and the sign can change. Both statements are within-condition results. Neither is the general law the question asks for.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that diversity is irrelevant, or that more species never help.
- Not that identity alone decides, or that there is a fixed list of good species.
- Not that no general law exists. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* The claim is about the shape of the question, not an absence proof.
- Not `grounded`. No measurement was taken and no new dataset was analysed.
- Not a derivation of ecology from the framework. The mapping of figure terms onto ecological terms is a declared binding, not a result.
- Not a claim that the grassland experiments are wrong. Their result is kept as a within-condition finding with a named mechanism.

## 4. What would settle it — the ground

A ground reading, not more agreement. Concretely:

- **Anchor test:** name one stability property and perturbation that all parties accept as the anchor, across contexts. Ives and Carpenter 2007 say the concept is multifaceted; if that changes, the union narrows.
- **Fixed-habitat test:** vary S with cast, conditions and interaction structure held fixed, in several habitats, and check whether the sign and magnitude of the effect stay the same. This is the load-bearing test for F1 and F2.
- **Isomer test:** compare communities with identical species lists but different interaction networks under all measured perturbations. Identical stability everywhere kills F7.
- **Contact test:** add species where conditions, relations and cascades are demonstrably fixed. If stability changes, F4 as applied here is refuted.
- **Metric test:** rank the same communities under temporal variance, resistance, resilience and return rate. The ranking is expected to change; if it never does, the valence claim is weakened.

## 5. The honest stop

**Reached in shape, not in fact.** The figures return a decomposition and a set of refuters, not a number. The framework stops where the available instruments stop distinguishing: without a named anchor, the sign of the diversity effect is not a well-posed quantity. Indiscernibility is not identity. The lineage is recorded so the stop is checkable, and nothing here is asserted as settled. Prestige and citation counts are budget filters, not witnesses (Fig 9 :60).

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not answer yes or no to whether more species make ecosystems more stable. It says the question is a bundle of three conditions: which stability property and perturbation you mean, which species and interaction relations are present, and the habitat of conditions and cascades they sit in. A species count is an aggregate over slots, and the framework's rule is that aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average. An actor's valence is relative to the residual, so no species is stabilising in general. Two communities with the same species list but different interaction networks are different substances, and stability effects often sit two or three steps out, beyond what a count can see. So more species often stabilises a named property inside a named habitat, and changing the cast, bonds, conditions or metric can change the sign. What this offers is the shape any answer must have, written refuters, and the tests that would close it. The sign stays open until the ground settles it.
