## QUESTION

Do interest-rate changes reliably control inflation and employment, or do their effects vary substantially across time and economies? Restated as an effect: is R_s,t = |Γ_s,t(b,a) - o'_s,t| for a policy-rate change a and observed inflation and employment o' small and stable across economy slots s and dates t? Option A says the map is stable enough to transport one rule. Option B says its parameters and sign change with habitat and epoch. The figure derivation finds these are overlapping conditions, not exclusive answers. Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*

## VERDICT (from the figures)

**The figure-derived verdict:** the question's either/or is a union of overlapping conditions, so reliability is not a fixed property of the rate change. The loop is invariant while the cast changes (Fig 4 :20); capability belongs to policy plus habitat (Fig 11 :39-40); the same action changes valence with the regime (Fig 11 :49-51); apparent time-variation is often channel failure (Fig 10 :24-28); the residual is per slot (Fig 1 :19, :22); and a transported model carries a measurable round-trip gap (Fig 4 :30). Effects therefore vary substantially across time and economies in the specific sense that the map's parameters and sometimes its sign change with habitat and contact. That variation is the mechanism, not noise around one reliable lever. The verdict is kept separate per domain and never averaged. Mode `specified` for the structure, `general` for indexing and union, `gap` for the coefficient. The economics pool corroborates the shape; the low-overlap fields corroborate the mechanism; none supplies the coefficient.

- **cost if wrong:** if invariance were the truth and this package claimed conditionality, a central bank would over-invest in per-slot re-estimation and in measuring round-trip gaps, and would under-use a transportable rule. The cost is real but bounded and locatable because every sub-claim has a refuter and a provenance trail. The opposite cost, treating a conditional mechanism as universal, is larger: it applies one lever to a union and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :57). Independence bought from outside readers is the scarce resource, and Fig 2 :42 prices it: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."*
- **undoable:** yes. Nothing is deleted if a sub-claim fails. A refutation adds a term that refuses and the reach falls because *"Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach."*, so the correction is computable from the record rather than stipulated. The shape, the five falsifiers and the source ledger are recorded so a later reader can locate the error (Fig 10 :62-64 provenance rule).
- **reversed by:** any of the five pre-registered refuters: (1) invariant sign and magnitude across fixed, flexible-credible and flexible-noncredible regimes with habitat controlled; (2) a pooled estimate with no per-slot residual after stratification; (3) a time shift with the channel demonstrably unchanged and the ground moved; (4) one lever clearing both residues with no categorically different remainder; (5) a per-slot, per-date coefficient settled by a source-discriminating instrument and reported with provenance. A world source with a genuinely different evidence pool showing per-slot responses fully predicted by a single invariant rule would also reverse it. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`.
