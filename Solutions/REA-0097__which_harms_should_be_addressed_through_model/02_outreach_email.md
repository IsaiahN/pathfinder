**Subject:** One checkable claim about which AI harms design can fix

> Hi [name],
>
> I'm [your name]. I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know from working on AI harms and institutions. I am not asking you to read a theory first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "which harms should be addressed through model design, and which require changes to institutions, markets or law" is not a partition question. It is a union of two conditions, and treating it as a list that assigns each harm to one bucket leaves the interesting cases standing. The two conditions are: (1) a change inside the model's closure that changes contact with the outcome that settles the harm; and (2) a change to the habitat of deployers, markets, rules and affected people that changes what that outcome pays. A harm satisfying both is a compound with its own loop and needs two levers, not one.
>
> The sharp form of the claim is: model design composes inside what the system can already express, and composition inside a closed set cannot add a new atom. So design is the right lever for channel failures (a stale percept, a saturated metric, a pooled reading) and for harms whose missing term is already reachable inside the model. It is the wrong lever, on its own, for harms constituted by the surrounding habitat. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves the benchmark.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that this produces a definitive list naming which harms go where. The framework returns a decision procedure and a shape, not a name. I am not claiming that law can subtract a habitat, that a test harness removes a harm, or that agreement among people who already share this field's evidence pool corroborates anything. I am not claiming this is settled by experiment; it is a `specified` prediction with falsifiers written in advance.
>
> **What would refute it:**
> 1. One intervention restores both conditions across all harm types and leaves no categorically different residue.
> 2. A model-only change adds a new primitive to the system's closure and is paid for at the ground with the habitat held fixed.
> 3. A channel independently measured as restored leaves the harm unchanged, or a model-only change reduces a habitat-constituted harm with the habitat unchanged.
> 4. A fixed harm-to-lever assignment survives a documented change of the affected residual and ground.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any one holds, the shape claim is wrong and I will retract it. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK\reference\03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local framework file): `FRAMEWORK\reference\FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
