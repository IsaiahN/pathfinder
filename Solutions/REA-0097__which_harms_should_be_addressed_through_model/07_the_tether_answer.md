# The Tether's answer on model design versus institutions, markets and law

**Mode: `specified` on the lever assignment, `general` on the union and anchor structure. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 6), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

A harm-slot's residual is `R_s = |Γ(b,a) − o′|`, indexed by affected group, application and time, never aggregated (Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."; Fig 1 :22 "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."). The question asks which lever reduces it: a change to the model, which composes inside `closure(Γ)`, or a change to institutions, markets or law, which changes the habitat. The two levers are not symmetric, so the answer is a test, not a table.

## 2. The shape

1. **It is a union, not a partition.** Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." Asking for a two-column harm list is already the wrong shape. The two conditions are C1, a model change that changes contact with the outcome that settles the harm, and C2, a habitat change that changes what that outcome pays.

2. **Model design is the right lever when the change composes inside the closure and changes contact.** Fig 6 :21 "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Its cleanest case is the channel: Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does", with Fig 10 :26 "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings". A stale percept or a saturated metric is fixed by changing the instrument, not by changing the ground. If the needed term is already held but unfound, SEARCHED comes first (Fig 6 :49 "more search at the same reach: is it in here and unfound? The closure does not change. Same atoms, same wall, more of the space actually visited.").

3. **Institutions, markets and law are the right lever when the harm is constituted by habitat actors, conditions or relations.** Fig 11 :39 "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40 "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 11 :61 "Introduction is available; exclusion is not." A rule, a price or a duty introduces an actor; it does not subtract the habitat, so governance is permanent work (Fig 11 :63 "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state.").

4. **A harm in both conditions is a compound with its own loop and two levers.** It is not a harder case of either; it is categorically different (Fig 9 :57). One lever clears the region where only one condition holds and leaves the intersection standing.

5. **The anchor decides the assignment, not the label.** Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." Name the outcome first; if it moves when a design frame pushes on it, it was an instrument. Fig 2 :35 "Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."

6. **A fixed harm list cannot survive.** Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :51 "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual." The lever for a named harm changes when the residual and ground change.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not a definitive assignment of current named harms to columns. The framework returns Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself".
- Not that model design is useless. Design governs mint, re-fit, SEARCHED and channel repair; it just cannot add the atom the frame does not hold (Fig 6 :21, Fig 6 :49).
- Not that a law subtracts a habitat. Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another." and Fig 11 :70 "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."
- Not that field consensus settles the split. Fig 2 :40 "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool".
- Not that a severity ranking is the verdict. Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."
- Not grounded. No per-slot ground reading in this folder pays for the assignment.

## 4. What would settle it: the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. From the essay *The Hard Problem is About Source*, fetched 2026-11-24: an instrument that detects green reports only what it measured and cannot comment on the source; the spectrometer resolved it, not an argument.

- **Lever test:** for the same named harm, compare a model-only change with the habitat held fixed against a habitat-only change with the model held fixed, and measure the per-slot outcome prospectively. If only one moves the outcome, that lever is assigned for that slot.
- **Anchor test:** check whether the nominated outcome moves when a design frame pushes on it. If it does, it is an instrument, not an anchor (Fig 2 :29).
- **Channel test:** restore a stale percept, un-saturate a metric and disaggregate a pooled reading, then see whether the harm moves. If not, the harm is not a channel failure (Fig 10 :24, Fig 10 :26).
- **Standing falsifiers** from `01` section 5: a single lever restoring both conditions; a model-only change adding a primitive at the ground; a model-only change reducing a habitat-constituted harm; a fixed assignment invariant across a change of residual; an institutional removal of an actor with no substitution.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The missing thing is a per-slot instrument that discriminates design-origin from habitat-origin. It is characterized in effect terms, not named. The canonical rule says the framework gives the shape of a missing thing rather than its name, and stops where instruments can no longer distinguish, recording the lineage. Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,".

The harm vocabulary itself is a visible choice, not nature's list. The 21-risk taxonomy in Weidinger et al. is a useful map of what people look for, not a ground partition (Fig 9 :60). What this folder supplies is the decision procedure and its falsifiers. The names remain open.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not publish a table assigning AI harms to model design or to law. It says the question has the wrong shape: the answer is a union of two conditions, not a partition. Design is the right lever when a change composes inside the model's closure and changes contact with the outcome that settles the harm, which is most clearly true for broken measurement channels and for missing terms the model could already reach. Institutions, markets and law are the right lever when the harm is constituted by the surrounding habitat, because an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing however much it improves the benchmark. A harm in both conditions is a categorically different compound needing two levers. The assignment is relative to the harm-slot and its ground, so no standing harm list survives a change of residual. What is offered is a three-way decision procedure, falsifiers written in advance, and the tests that would close it. The outcome, not agreement in the field, decides.
