## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The question which harms should be addressed through model design and which require institutions, markets or law has the wrong shape. It is a union of two conditions, not a partition. A harm-slot is assigned to design when a change composes inside the model's closure and changes contact with the outcome that settles that harm; its cleanest case is a broken measurement channel or a missing term the model could already reach. It is assigned to institutions, markets or law when the harm is constituted by habitat actors, conditions or relations, because an improvement that does not change contact changes nothing however much it improves the benchmark. A harm in both conditions is a categorically different compound needing two levers and two instruments. The assignment is relative to the harm-slot and its ground, so no standing harm-to-lever list survives a change of residual. What is offered is a three-way decision procedure with falsifiers written in advance, and the tests that would close it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1: the split is a union, not a partition

- **Statement:** unbundling yields two conditions, C1 (design changes contact with the outcome that settles the harm) and C2 (habitat changes what that outcome pays). A harm in both is a compound with its own loop, not a harder case of either.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones." Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- **What to check:** can a reader name a single lever that reduces both conditions at the ground without leaving a categorically different residue? If yes, the union claim is false.
- **Refuter:** one intervention that restores both conditions across all harm types, with no categorically different residue left over. Secondary refuter: a harm-to-lever table whose membership is invariant across a documented change of residual and ground.

### C2: design composes inside the closure and cannot add the missing atom

- **Statement:** a model change is a mint or re-fit inside what the system can already express. If the needed term is already held but unfound, the remedy is more search, not a new atom. Where design is the right lever, its cleanest case is the measurement channel: a stale percept, a saturated metric or a pooled reading.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :21 "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Fig 6 :49 "more search at the same reach: is it in here and unfound? The closure does not change. Same atoms, same wall, more of the space actually visited." Fig 10 :24 "the ground does not decay, the channel does". Fig 10 :26 "stale percepts, dropped transients, saturated metrics, pooled readings".
- **What to check:** is the named harm a channel failure or a missing term already inside the closure? A benchmark that moves when a team optimises for it is an instrument, not an anchor (Fig 2 :29).
- **Refuter:** a model-only change that demonstrably adds a new primitive to the system's closure and is paid for at the ground with the habitat held fixed. This breaks idempotence (Fig 8 :32). A model-only change that merely finds an atom already in the closure does not refute C2; that is SEARCHED. Secondary refuter: a channel independently measured as restored that leaves the harm unchanged.

### C3: habitat-constituted harms need the habitat lever, by introduction not exclusion

- **Statement:** a harm produced by the arrangement of model and deployment is not a property of the model considered alone. Law, markets and institutions introduce actors, rights, prices and duties; they do not subtract the habitat, so moving a system into a test harness proves wiring and not the absence of harm.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :39 "So the answerable question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone." Fig 11 :40 "A system cannot be made more capable by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." Fig 11 :61 "Introduction is available; exclusion is not." Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."
- **What to check:** is there a controlled case where a model-only change reduces a habitat-constituted harm, measured prospectively per slot, with the habitat demonstrably unchanged?
- **Refuter:** that controlled case, if it survives, breaks C3. Secondary refuter: an institutional intervention that removes an actor from a habitat with no substitution, no unchosen inherited actors, and a ground reading that pays.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a definitive assignment of current named harms to columns. The framework returns a kind of thing, never the answer itself (Fig 9 :32).
- Not that model design is useless. Design governs mint, re-fit, SEARCHED and channel repair.
- Not that a law subtracts a habitat, or that a test harness removes a harm. Fig 11 :70 "A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."
- Not that agreement in the AI harms field settles the split. Most of the field shares one evidence pool (Fig 2 :40).
- Not that a severity ranking is the verdict. Severity budgets the search; the per-slot residual is the witness (Fig 9 :60).
- Not that this is `grounded`. No per-slot ground reading in this folder pays for the lever assignment.

## 5. What would settle it

- **Lever test:** for the same named harm, compare a model-only change with the habitat held fixed against a habitat-only change with the model held fixed, and measure the per-slot outcome prospectively.
- **Anchor test:** name the outcome that settles the harm, then check whether it moves when a design frame pushes on it. If it moves, it was an instrument (Fig 2 :29).
- **Channel test:** restore the percept, un-saturate the metric and disaggregate the reading, then see whether the harm moves (Fig 10 :24, Fig 10 :26).
- **Source-discriminating instrument:** from the essay *The Hard Problem is About Source*, a reading of green cannot comment on the source; the spectrometer resolved it. Here the instrument must say whether a harm's origin is the model or the habitat, per slot.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 9 :54 to :57 and Fig 6 :21 and :49, four short passages, and say whether the union claim survives.
2. Check C2 against a harm you know: is it a channel failure, a term already inside the closure, or neither?
