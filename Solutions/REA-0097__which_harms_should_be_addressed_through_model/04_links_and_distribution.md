## Wider literature that bears on the question

1. **The field already distinguishes mitigation types by point of origin.** Weidinger et al. locate privacy leaks in training data (a design-origin harm) but also list social and public policy interventions and participatory institutions among mitigations, and warn that a risk's point of origin is not a perfect guide to its mitigation. This corroborates the union reading rather than a two-column partition. It is one evidence pool with the rest of the AI ethics literature (Fig 2 :40).
2. **The design literature is about accidents, not all harms.** Amodei et al. scope their five problems to unintended behaviour from poor design. They do not claim to cover distribution of power, labour effects or the erosion of rights. Treating their list as the complete "design side" would be a category error; the figures' own SEARCHED rule (Fig 6 :49) asks whether the term is already in the closure before importing another frame.
3. **Principles do not settle implementation.** Jobin et al. found convergence on words and divergence on implementation. That is the figure's distinction between a convention about shared meaning (Fig 2 :41) and a verdict about the world (Fig 2 :40).
4. **Measurement corruption is a separate failure from ground failure.** Strathern and Hardin come from anthropology and ecology, not AI. Both are low overlap, so they are useful witnesses for mechanism shape. Hardin's "no technical solution" was found to conflict with F2 as written and was returned to the census, not used to flip the verdict (see `01` F11).
5. **The rigidity-trap literature corroborates what a stuck closure costs.** MathisonSlee et al. describe a system that becomes *"highly connected, rigid, and inflexible"* with diminished capacity for novelty. That is Fig 6 :21 and Fig 7 :36 observed in social-ecological systems, with no shared evidence pool with AI safety.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 4 :22**: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Sending the full framework before anyone has described a harm-slot sends a recording, not a generator. Lead with the shape claim and its refuter; offer links after.
- **Fig 10 :42**: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* Seat 4 must not hold the interiors. A recipient who has absorbed the framework before being asked for a verdict has joined the frame being checked.
- **Fig 9 :27**: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*. Ask what they would expect to see if the shape claim were true, before giving them the sources.
- **Fig 2 :40**: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Do not select recipients for likely agreement. Most of the field shares the same benchmarks and incident reports.
- **Fig 2 :42**: *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* One low-overlap outside frame is worth more than ten more papers from the same pool.
- **Seat Map :171**: *"an interest in the reading is disqualifying. Where a stake cannot be removed, declare it."* A developer assessing whether design alone fixes their own harm must declare the stake.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the shape claim and the union test; (2) ask what they would count as a failure and which outcome they would treat as the anchor; (3) record their verdict; (4) only then the links, with the note that the figures, not the summary, are the authority.

## The two-stage offer

1. **Stage 1 before stage 2.** Send the residual in effect terms and the pre-registered refuter. Fig 10 :52: *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Fig 2 :45: *"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."* The recipient checks the claim without holding the framework's interior.
2. **Stage 2 is opt-in by construction.** Once a recipient has absorbed the theory, their agreement stops being evidence. Record the stage-1 verdict before any theory is shown, and never pool later agreement as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
3. **Stage 3 sends a method, which is permitted.** Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* The formula is a generator, so giving it away does not give away an answer. Each user must name their own ground and write their own refuter (Formula, "Direction").
4. **Stage 3 still needs a ground per harm-slot.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"the catch is that the output is only as good as the validator."* A benchmark that moves when optimised is not an anchor.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 output survives. It is currently a `specified` shape with its falsifiers stated. If any falsifier fires, the packet in `08` must be corrected before it is sent.
