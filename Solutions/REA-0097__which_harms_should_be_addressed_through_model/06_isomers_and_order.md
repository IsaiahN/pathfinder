# Union, bond types and order: the supporting derivation for REA-0097

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6).

## RESIDUAL

The question asks for a partition of harms into two columns. Two derivations are owed before any lever can be assigned: (1) is the structure a partition or a union, and what bond type does each lever use; and (2) two harms that share a label, are they the same harm under the figure's definition of structure? A falsifier is stated at the end of each derivation and a combined falsifier is stated in the conclusion.

The residual stays per harm-slot `s`. It is never a single global harm score (Fig 1 :19, Fig 1 :22). The derivation asks which change reduces `R_s = |Γ(b,a) − o′|`: a change to the model library `Γ`, or a change to the habitat of deployers, markets, rules and affected people.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

- **Atoms held:** the harm-slot `s`, the model library `Γ`, the habitat actors and conditions, the ground outcome `o′`, and the benchmark or instrument.
- **Operators held:** the seven bonds (conjunction, disjunction, identity, sequence, production, subtraction, comparison) plus negation as a unary operator.
- **Arity and symmetry:** the lever question is a comparison, not a symmetric join. Swapping design and habitat changes the thing being compared. Fig 6 :14 calls import "the only operator that moves the wall", so the two levers are not interchangeable.
- **Scale:** one agent's design loop, the population habitat, and the lossy channel between them (Fig 4 :20, Fig 4 :22). The round trip gap `R_T` is a measurement, not an assumption (Fig 4 :30).
- **No new primitive is available** without an import from a frame whose closure differs (Fig 6 :23). Every conclusion below is a rearrangement of what is already held.

## DERIVATION

### Q1: partition or union?

- Fig 9 :54 "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."
- Fig 9 :55 "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."
- Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."
- The two conditions overlap. C1 is a model change that changes contact with the outcome that settles the harm. C2 is a habitat change that changes what that outcome pays. The measurement channel is inside the design frame and reaches into the habitat; affected people are in the habitat and are the ground. So the split is a union, not a partition.
- The question as asked expects a two-column harm list. That shape is already wrong. A single lever clears the region where only C1 or only C2 holds and leaves the intersection standing, because the second condition still binds.
- **Mode:** `general` for the union structure, `specified` for AI harms. **Status:** triangulated, Fig 9 union rule and the *Library of Babel* bundle passage, low topical overlap between leave/arrive and search/format.
- **Falsifier:** one intervention that restores both conditions across all harm types and leaves no categorically different residue. That would collapse the union into a partition.

### Q2: what bond type does each lever use?

- **Design is a re-fit or a mint inside the closure.** Fig 6 :21 "MINT composes inside the closure and can never add an atom." Also Fig 5 :13 "mis-attached: refit what you have, do not invent" and Fig 5 :14 "mechanism broken: a new term is owed".
- **Habitat change is an import.** Fig 6 :14 "the only operator that moves the wall" and Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." A rule, a price or a liability is an atom the model's frame did not author.
- **There is a third operator that is not a design or habitat lever: refusal.** Fig 12 :134 "AMENDMENT · a bond is a binary operator, and this figure defines the bonds. It does not define the unary ones." The Operators table, line 58, states "a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate". A law that says a practice does not hold applies negation to a claim. It does not apply subtraction (the absence of an operand).
- This matters because Fig 11 :61 "Introduction is available; exclusion is not." A seat can introduce an actor and cannot subtract the habitat. So the claim that a harmful practice was removed from the deployment is not a literal subtraction of the habitat. What is available is a refusal of a claim plus the introduction of a new arrangement. Fig 11 :66 "Isolation is not removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."
- **Conclusion for Q2:** the lever vocabulary needs at least three operators, not two: mint inside closure, import from a differing closure, and negation as refusal. Any scheme with only a model fix and a regulation collapses a unary operator into a bond and will misuse subtraction for exclusion. Mode `general`. Triangulated across Fig 6, Fig 11 and the Operators table, three distinct roles with low overlap.
- **Falsifier:** a lever pair in which habitat change is shown to be composition inside the same closure as the model change, or a case where exclusion literally removes an actor from a habitat with no substitution.

### Q3: are two harms that share a label isomers?

- Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance."
- Take two harms both labelled discrimination. Harm A: a model reproduces stereotypes because the training corpus overrepresents them. Harm B: the same model is used by a hiring system with no audit duty, so a group is denied interviews. Over the atom set (corpus, model, deployer, affected group, benchmark), A and B share the atoms and differ in which bond carries the harm, corpus to model versus deployer to affected group. Under Fig 12 they are isomers, not variants of one harm. The label is invisible to the difference and decisive in the behaviour.
- The weaker reading: if A and B do not share the full atom set, they are members of the same functional group (Fig 12 :124 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member.") and the shared kind is material harm to a group, not discrimination.
- A harm taxonomy that lists labels without bonds cannot tell the two apart. That is why a benchmark reading cannot comment on the source (see `05` section 3). Mode `specified` for AI harms, `general` for the isomer rule. Single-source for the figure definition (Fig 12), corroborated at a different stratum by Weidinger et al. 2022, which distinguishes representational from allocational harm and locates them at different points of origin.
- **Falsifier:** a label-only taxonomy that prospectively predicts, per named slot, which lever reduces the ground outcome without recording the bond. If one works, Fig 12's arrangement claim does not govern harm-slots and Q3 is retracted.

### Q4: what order do the levers run in?

- Fig 3 :20 "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."
- Fig 3 :27 "A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."
- Formula step 2 routes the residual before minting, and a REFUTED term is a refusal, not an explanation. The order is: describe the residual per slot, check whether a held term is false and refuse it if so, ask SEARCHED before OUTWARD (Fig 6 :49), then choose the lever.
- Applied order: (1) perception, is the metric still returning something for this group; (2) vocabulary, do we have terms for what design and habitat each afford here; (3) objective, what counts as harm reduction in our own terms; (4) planning, which lever in which order; (5) learn and carry. The middle link is where the assignment of harms gets stuck, because it has no instrument (Fig 3 :27).
- Fig 12 :111 "A vocabulary without bonds can name a state and cannot name a route to one." Naming a harm is not naming a route to its reduction.
- **Falsifier for the ordering:** a documented case where the habitat lever had to be applied before the channel was measured and the harm was reduced at the ground, with the channel independently shown to have been dead all along. That breaks the dependency order in Q4, though not the union claim in Q1.

## CONCLUSION

The question is a union, not a partition. The lever vocabulary has three operators (mint inside closure, import from a differing closure, negation as refusal), not two, and exclusion is not available as a habitat subtraction. Harms sharing a label are isomers over a shared atom set when their bonds differ, so a label-only taxonomy cannot decide the lever. The levers run in dependency order, with refusal before mint and SEARCHED before import. No new primitive is imported by this derivation.

**Combined falsifier:** a single documented intervention that (a) assigns every named harm to one of two columns, (b) predicts the lever prospectively per slot without a bond record, and (c) survives a measured change of residual and ground. If that exists, Q1 to Q4 fail together. Until it is run and fails to fire, the result stays `specified` and ungrounded.
