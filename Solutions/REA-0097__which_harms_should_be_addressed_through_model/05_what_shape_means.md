## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical usage rule states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"*. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"So the system can report that it lacks something rather than producing an answer anyway. Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"*.

From *The Library of Babel*, fetched this session: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

So a **shape** is the form of an answer you can state before you can name the specific instance. It constrains; it does not fill. For REA-0097 the shape is a three-way decision procedure with tests (design, habitat, compound), not a table that assigns named harms to columns. `design / habitat / compound` is a shape. `"toxicity goes to design and labour displacement goes to law"` is a name.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary already closest to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause"*. The effect version of this question is not "which harms are technical", it is "which lever reduces this slot residual at the ground".
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*, fetched this session: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."*
- *Library of Babel* on bundles: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"* This is the direct warrant for F1 in `01`: the question arrives as a list-shaped question and must be unbundled before a lever is chosen.

For this question the vocabulary bootstrap is deliberately kept in effect terms. "Model design" is not a primitive; it names a family of moves that compose inside a model's `closure(Γ)`. "Institutions, markets, law" is not a primitive either; it names habitat actors, conditions and relations that a seat can act on (Fig 11 :37, :46-47). The shape is the relation between these families and the ground, not a list of harms.

## 3. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays."* The README says the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."*
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

*The Hard Problem is About Source*, fetched this session, gives the mechanism. An instrument that detects green *"is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source."* And *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"*. A benchmark reading that says "this model is biased" does not say whether the source is the training corpus, the decoding rule, the downstream hiring system or the absent audit. It was the spectrometer, not an argument, that resolved the colour case: *"It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."*

The same error is the standing temptation in REA-0097. A severity ranking, an incident count or a benchmark gap is a projection with lossy information about the source. Naming the lever from that reading is the metamerism error. What is owed is a source-discriminating instrument, characterized in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` but not held here.

## 4. Why a definitive harm list is the specific forbidden error

The question asks for a partition. Three separate figures refuse it:

- Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones."*
- Fig 9 :57: *"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."*
- Fig 11 :49: *"An actor’s valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*

A harm's lever is likewise relative to the residual and the ground. A fixed list cannot survive a change of deployment context. Weidinger et al. record one instance of this in the field's own terms: *"mitigation approaches to toxic speech can inadvertently lead to lower LM performance for some social groups."* The same mitigation changes valence when the residual changes.

Fig 9 :60 adds the methodological rule: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* A ranked harm list is a budget instrument. It is not the verdict.

## 5. The shape this solution returns

The answer is a decision procedure with three outputs and a test for each:

1. **Design.** The lever composes inside `closure(Γ)` and changes contact with the outcome that settles the harm. Includes channel repairs (Fig 10 :24-29) and SEARCHED cases where the needed atom is held but unfound (Fig 6 :49).
2. **Habitat (institutions, markets, law).** The harm is constituted by actors, conditions or relations outside the model's closure; only a seat acting on the habitat can introduce what is owed (Fig 11 :37, :61).
3. **Compound.** Both conditions hold; the case is categorically different from either and needs two levers with separate instruments (Fig 9 :57).

What is NOT in the shape: a named harm, a priority ranking, a claim that any specific current intervention works, and any `grounded` verdict. Names require the discriminating instrument or a provenanced import with a shadow test.

## 6. Two consequences for this folder

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*, fetched this session: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly."* and *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."* A list of prominent researchers asked to endorse the harm-lever split is the named collapse, not corroboration (Fig 2 :40). The legitimate ask is the opposite: give the claim a budget to be tried and let the outcome settle it.

**My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading.** Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post."* and *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* So `01` is a simulated derivation, checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of the lever assignment and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on.

And from *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"Communities and institutions that only check against themselves drift together and don't notice"*. That is the exact structural risk in asking the AI harms community to validate a claim about whether the AI harms community can validate it. The check has to come from an outcome neither side can move, with at least one frame whose closure differs (Fig 10 :80, Fig 2 :35).
