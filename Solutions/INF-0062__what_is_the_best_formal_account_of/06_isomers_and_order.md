# Are the candidate accounts isomers? Structure, order and analogues

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the ordering rules. Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading.**

## RESIDUAL

Two questions are owed after `01`. First, are PAC, Bayesian inference, MDL and active learning interchangeable spellings of one account, or different substances? Second, what order does the loop impose on the operations that combine them? A wrong answer to the first makes us rank incomparable things; a wrong answer to the second makes us settle before we have perceived.

## CENSUS

Atoms held: object slots, residual R, support (positive mass in some slot), prediction, observation, a candidate term, its statement cost, its leftover residual, the ground, and the budget. Bonds held: the seven, plus negation (a refusal is a negation, not a bond). No new primitive is available without an import, which must carry provenance and a shadow test (Fig 6 :11-14).

## Q1 - Are the four accounts isomers?

Fig 12 :117: **"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**

Written in their own vocabularies, the four accounts do **not** share an atom set. PAC's atoms are hypothesis class, VC dimension, error and confidence. Bayesian inference's atoms are prior, likelihood, posterior and marginal likelihood. MDL's atoms are a code, model cost and data-given-model cost. Active learning's atoms are a pool, a query and an oracle. By Fig 12's definition they are not isomers as written; they are members of a **functional group**, the substructure "account that updates from observations," with different atoms in the slots. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member.

The stronger claim is available if each is rewritten over the shared atom set `{slots, residual, support, cost, ground, budget}` and they differ only in which bond connects which atoms. Then they are isomers: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance, with the difference *"invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* That is the checkable structural claim, and it is `specified`, not measured.

**Falsifier, written before the run:** if any one of the four can be written without an atom another requires (for example, an account of learning under uneven data with no slot decomposition and no per-slot support), the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group reading stands. This is the same test shape the exemplar used for GR and QM, applied to a different domain.

## Q2 - What order does the loop impose?

Formula step 2 sorts R by what is missing and names a fifth kind by what is wrong: a refusal is owed before a mint when a held term keeps producing a residual that *"PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN."* So:

1. **Perceive and route first.** Can a slot be registered at all? Fig 3 :27: *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."*
2. **Refuse before mint.** An account that only accumulates cannot carry a correction. Formula: *"a refusing term pays the same bargain. What it removes is not a term but a term's standing to be composed over."* Applied here: a pooled loss that keeps failing on one slot must be refused, not tuned.
3. **Support before search.** Fig 5 :21: *"the gap has real mass somewhere."* Formula step 3 puts SUPPORT before the search as one factor of a product.
4. **Settle before carry.** Fig 5 :32: **"then check it against the ground, then carry it up"**. Fig 12 :104 (prefix): **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it."** A candidate account that has not paid against a ground cannot become an atom in a wider theory of learning.
5. **Describe the residual before the frame.** Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."*

**Falsifier for the order claim:** a case where a term must be minted before the refusal of the failing held term, and the resulting account still settles against the ground without the refusal being added later. That breaks the prescribed order in Formula step 2.

## Q3 - Sparse, noisy and uneven: partition or union?

Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**
Fig 9 :57 (substring): **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

The three adjectives are overlapping conditions. Sparse and uneven overlap when the few observations that arrive are concentrated in one region. Noisy and sparse overlap when the noise dominates the small sample. A single lever (say, more data) clears the region where only sparsity binds and leaves the intersections standing, because the second condition still binds (Fig 9 :55). The residue after unbundling is *"smaller and also categorically different"* (Fig 9 :57), so each intersection gets its own loop and its own refuter. This is why `01` separates D1 to D4 instead of reporting one average verdict.

## Q4 - Analogues that carry, and one that does not

- **Active learning as introduction.** Fig 11 :61: **"The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not."** Choosing which point to label adds an actor to the habitat. It cannot subtract the habitat, so a query strategy that ignores contact will still be surprised by the distribution.
- **Benchmark as substituted habitat.** Fig 11 :70: **"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."** A sparse-data benchmark constructed to test an account proves that the wiring runs; it does not prove the account handles sparse data in the wild.
- **Analogue that does not carry:** "noise" in the physical sense of a thermal bath is not the same atom as "noise" in a dataset. Fig 6 :30 requires the description to travel in effect terms. Both are *"what the coarser description cannot hold"* (Fig 4 :31), but importing thermodynamics into the learning question would add an atom that was not needed and would cost more than starting from primitives (Fig 6 :52).

## CONCLUSION

The four accounts are functional-group members as written, and a real isomer claim only after each is rewritten over the shared atom set. The order the loop imposes is perceive, refuse, support, settle, carry, with describe-the-residual first. Sparse, noisy and uneven are a union to be unbundled, not one hard question. Active learning and the benchmark are habitat facts: one introduces, the other substitutes. Each claim has a falsifier above. The isomer claim is `specified`; the ordering rules are `general`.
