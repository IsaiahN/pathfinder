# Expert verification packet: the Tether on inflation initial conditions

**What this is:** a claim in a form a cosmologist can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the closure and union structure, `specified` on the shape of the missing item, not `grounded`.** No measurement at the past boundary is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

**Status of this packet:** a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figure text. It cannot settle the question it reports on.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

The inflationary frame can predict the CMB from an already prepared patch but cannot derive that patch from inside its own closure. What is owed is a boundary condition imported from a frame whose closure differs, with provenance and a shadow test. The question is a union of at least three overlapping conditions, a smooth patch, a field value that lands on the flat potential, and a regular past boundary, so no single lever clears it. The candidate imports (no-boundary, tunneling, kinetic dominance, bounce and measure) stand as isomers over a shared atom set, same ingredients arranged differently with different observable behaviour. The framework returns the shape any answer must have, not a name, so the slot stays open until a source-discriminating observation closes it.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1. Framework-internal and physics-facing: the prepared patch is not derivable from inside inflation

- **Statement:** no frame derives an atom it does not hold. Inflation's library contains the inflaton, potential, expansion and perturbations; it does not contain its own past boundary. More model building inside that closure cannot add the missing item.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Fig 8 :32 "Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means." Fig 9 :19 "the witness is always imported".
- **What to check:** is there a checkable derivation inside inflationary effective field theory that produces the smooth, displaced patch from its own atoms? Does the past-incompleteness argument apply to the model class in question, or is that class an evasion? Is the proposed import actually adding an atom, or just renaming the patch?
- **Refuter:** exhibit such an internal derivation, or show the import adds no atom and still closes the residual. If either holds, C1 falls.

### C2. Structural: the question is a union, and the candidates are isomers

- **Statement:** a smooth patch, a correct field value and a regular past boundary are overlapping conditions, not one hard question. Clearing one leaves the intersections standing. Written over the shared atoms {inflaton, potential, Planck density, de Sitter vacuum, measure, decoherence, time orientation}, the candidate boundary conditions differ in bond (sequence, production, threshold comparison) and therefore in behaviour.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." Fig 3 :19 "Optimising a link that already works cannot rescue one that does not."
- **What to check:** can any of the three conditions be derived from another, collapsing the union? Does any candidate require an atom none of the others holds? Do two candidates reduce to the same term under a symmetric bond?
- **Refuter:** a proof that the conditions form a partition, or that a candidate needs a private atom, or that two candidates are one term. The first kills the union claim, the second kills the isomer claim and leaves the functional-group claim, the third reduces the candidate count.

### C3. Epistemic: the answer is a shape, not a name, and the boundary frame has no settled anchor

- **Statement:** the figures license the shape (an imported boundary condition with provenance, shadow test and a discriminating observation). They do not select among no-boundary, tunneling, kinetic dominance, bounce or measure. The inflationary frame has an observational anchor, the past-boundary frame does not.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself". Fig 2 :29 "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks." Fig 9 :44 "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room. So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."
- **What to check:** does any cited proposal come with a source gate and a shadow test? Is there an observation that discriminates it from the others? If not, it is a binding, not a derivation.
- **Refuter:** a figure-licensed derivation naming one candidate, or a ground-visible observation selecting one and excluding the rest. Either fills the slot and downgrades C3 at that level.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that inflation occurred or did not occur. The question is conditional.
- Not that a singular beginning is proved. Past incompleteness and a singularity are different claims, and BGV is a world corroboration only.
- Not that fine-tuning is established. Finn 2020 and Carrasco, Kallosh and Linde 2015 disagree because their measures differ.
- Not that the CMB reads the source. It reads a projection; several sources give the same reading.
- Not that an existing proposal is the answer. All named proposals are bindings from world frames.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating observation, not a further argument:

1. A finite-onset power spectrum signature with the predicted decaying oscillations.
2. A specific non-thermal initial-state signature in the bispectrum or tensor sector.
3. A bounce or contracting-phase signature that distinguishes it from a no-boundary weight.
4. A measure-independent typicality result that survives a change of model class.

Standing falsifiers, written before the run: an internal derivation of the patch; a discriminating observation; a reviewed past-completeness result; a failure of idempotent closure; a proof that the three conditions form a partition.
