# Outreach draft: check the comparison before deciding about the mechanism

**Use:** one to one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until F1 to F5 in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` are stated cleanly in the recipient's own vocabulary. The shape must survive a first reading or the two-stage offer collapses on contact.

---

**Subject:** What would tell you which of two equally fitting models has the right mechanism?

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific question about model comparison that I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the shape does not survive your check, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The residual (`general` for the shape, `specified` for any particular pair of models):** suppose two models with different mechanisms both reproduce the same observed pattern. Their fit residuals are near zero on that pattern. The unresolved quantity is not fit. It is source: which mechanism would have generated the pattern, and what would each model return in a regime the pattern does not cover? The observed pattern cannot answer that, because it is exactly where the two models are indiscernible. In the framework's terms, it is a projection asking to name its own source.
>
> **What follows from that, in effect terms:** the two models are metamers at the current instrument. They are isomers if they can be written over a shared set of ingredients arranged differently, or members of one functional group if they only share a substructure. A fit score prices the description; it does not price the actions needed to find out which mechanism holds. So a ranking by AIC, BIC or cross-validation is a filter for what to try first, not a verdict about mechanism.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that a simpler model is the true model; that one of the two is already ruled out; that the mechanism is unobservable in principle; or that the framework can name the winner. The framework returns a shape, not a name. Which mechanism is correct stays open until a ground settles it.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written down before this email:**
> 1. a figure-level rule that names a mechanism class from a pattern-only comparison;
> 2. a documented case where more observations of the same pattern, with no new intervention, instrument or frame, separate two mechanisms on a ground where the mechanism is known;
> 3. a global fit score that returns the discriminating signal without per-slot decomposition, validated on a known ground;
> 4. two frames drawing on exactly the same evidence that discriminate mechanism at maximum independence;
> 5. a single lever that clears both fit and source identity with no categorically different residue.
>
> If any of these holds, I will withdraw or narrow the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figures text with line numbers (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - The canonical usage rule (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Public figures repository: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/tree/general-agent/figures
>
> **If you want to reuse the machinery:** the formula is in the figures link. Run it on a comparison you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. The framework's own rule is that a loop cannot check the direction of its own question, and its author warns that outputs *"could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula."* A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
