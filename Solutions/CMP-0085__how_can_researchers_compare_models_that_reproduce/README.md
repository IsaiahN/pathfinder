## The question restated as an effect

The loop defines `R = |Γ(b,a) - o'|` per slot `s` (Formula step 1; Symbols table, `R`). Here:

- **b** is the forcing, the initial and boundary conditions, the input the models are given.
- **a** is what the researcher does: observe, intervene, perturb, hold a variable fixed.
- **Γ_i** is model i's library, that is its mechanism, its atoms and the bonds between them.
- **Γ_i(b,a)** is what model i predicts.
- **o'** is the observed pattern the models reproduce.

The phenomenon is not fit. It is that two or more models with different Γ return `R_i(s) ≈ 0` on the same observed pattern. They are indiscernible at the instrument that produced that pattern. The residual that remains is a **source residual**: which mechanism actually generated the pattern? It lives on a slot the current instrument cannot read. In the Symbols table's words, it is *"always a slice: what cannot be perceived yet is residual and simply cannot be read."*

- Option A, "rank the models by how well they fit the observed pattern and pick the best": leads to a filter for the budget, not a witness for the verdict. Agreement on the pattern is one evidence pool (Fig 2 :40).
- Option B, "find an instrument on which the mechanisms return different readings": leads to a source-discriminating measurement, a union split, and a verdict that stays per domain and is never averaged.

**What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run** (Formula, "Direction"): a shape stating (i) the condition under which two models are metamers at the current instrument, (ii) the effect description of a measurement that would separate them, and (iii) an instrument that would discriminate a failure of the fit condition from a failure of the source condition. Until bound, that shape predicts nothing and no check in the loop can see it. It is now bound, so the refuter in `01` section 5 can fire on it.

The verdict recorded in `09_watson_answer.md` is Option B in `general` mode, with the specific isomer or metamer reading in `specified` mode, kept per domain and never averaged. Which mechanism is actually correct is a declared `gap`, not a hidden answer.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree.
- No bulk-send plan.
- No claim that this derivation is `grounded`, no claim that it settles which mechanism is right, and no claim that the framework is the only true account of model comparison.

Four reasons, three from the figures and one practical:

1. **Agreement on the pattern is not a verdict.** Fig 2 :40: *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Two models fitted by the same team to the same data are one witness wearing two names.
2. **A room is not an anchor.** Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."* Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."*
3. **The verifier must not hold the interiors.** Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."* The first contact therefore carries the residual and its refuter, never the framework.
4. Legally, unsolicited bulk email to scraped addresses breaches GDPR and CAN-SPAM style rules in many jurisdictions and gets the sending domain blacklisted. Public opt-in routes avoid that failure as well as the structural one.
