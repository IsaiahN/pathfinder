# Expert verification packet - comparing models that fit the same pattern

**What this is:** a claim in a form a modeller, statistician or complex-systems researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the isomer reading. Not `grounded`.** No discriminating measurement is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

Two models that reproduce the same observed pattern are metamers at the current instrument: their fit is not the residual, because fit is zero; the residual is on the source, which mechanism would generate the pattern and what each model would return elsewhere. If the two models can be written over a shared atom set, they stand as isomers under the figures: same atoms, different arrangement, different substance. If they share only a substructure, they are members of one functional group. A score such as AIC, BIC or cross-validation prices the description, not the actions needed to identify the mechanism, so it is a filter for the budget and never a witness for the verdict. What is owed is a source-discriminating bearing, indexed per slot and answerable to a measurement that does not move when the researchers argue. The framework returns the shape of that bearing, not the name of the winning model.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 - framework internal; checkable by reading Figures 12 and 2

- **Statement:** same pattern plus different mechanism is a relation about arrangement, not ingredients; agreement on the pattern is one evidence pool and cannot be a verdict.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :117 - *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :14 - *"the ingredients are recorded and the arrangement is not"*. Fig 2 :40 - *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"*. Fig 2 :31 - *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence."*
- **What to check:** does your pair of models share a full atom set (isomer) or only a substructure (functional group, Fig 12 :124)? Are the two fits computed on the same sample and scored with the same rule?
- **Refuter:** the two models share neither a full atom set nor a functional substructure, so neither reading holds; or two frames drawing on exactly the same evidence discriminate mechanism at maximum independence on a ground where the mechanism is known.

### C2 - statistics facing; checkable against the comparison literature

- **Statement:** model-selection criteria are filters for the budget, not witnesses for mechanism identity; they price bits, while the discriminating intervention prices actions.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 9 :60 - *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."* Fig 12 :125 - *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 12 :126 - *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds."* Fig 5 :69 - *"settling is necessary for a term to be composed over, and it is not sufficient."*
- **What to check:** can you name a criterion whose value changes with the mechanism while the sampled pattern is held fixed, validated on a ground where mechanisms are known? If not, the filter reading stands. The statistics source used in the derivation says the selected model may be *"the lucky winner among a few close competitors"*, fine for prediction and *"severely unreliable and misleading"* for interpretation.
- **Refuter:** such a validated criterion exists and is in routine use. Then C2 is narrowed, though Fig 9 :60 still forbids letting it hand down the verdict.

### C3 - method facing; checkable against your own practice

- **Statement:** the next useful step is a source-discriminating bearing, found by SEARCHED, then INWARD, then OUTWARD as opposed tracks; not more observations of the same pattern by default.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 6 :23 - *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 6 :32 - *"An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."* Fig 8 :39 - *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 9 :27 - *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"*.
- **What to check:** is there a variable, tail, transient or scale where the models already diverge, that is, an edge to extend from? If nothing anywhere returns a failing resolution, the honest report is a gap, not a permanent impossibility.
- **Refuter:** a documented case where more observations of the same pattern, with no new intervention, instrument or differing frame, separated two mechanisms on a known ground. Then the "bearing owed" claim is false for that case and C3 is narrowed.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that a simpler model is the true model. Simplicity is a filter, not a witness.
- Not that one of the two models has been refuted, or that indiscernibility is identity.
- Not that the mechanism is unobservable in principle. A below-horizon case is a report that nobody has reached it, not a proof of absence.
- Not that more data of the same kind is useless. It is SEARCHED, the cheapest branch, and it is asked first.
- Not `grounded`. No discriminating measurement was taken in this derivation.
- Not that the figures name a winning mechanism. They return the shape of the discriminator.

## 5. What would settle it

1. A per-slot divergence analysis: do the models separate on a tail, a transient, or an intervention response that the aggregated score hides?
2. A designed intervention: do the two mechanisms predict different readings under a clamp or step, and does the ground return one of them?
3. An anchor check: does the discriminating reading stay fixed when the modelers change their priors or their preferred mechanism?
4. A provenance check: were the two fits derived independently from genuinely different evidence, or is one an adopted import of the other?

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 12 :14, :117, :124 and Fig 2 :31, :40 - five short passages.
2. Say whether your two models are isomers, a functional group, or neither. One line is enough.
3. Name one observation or intervention that would make them disagree, and say whether any current instrument already returns a failing resolution there.
4. Name the criterion you currently use to choose between them, and say whether it is a filter for the budget or a witness for the mechanism.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
