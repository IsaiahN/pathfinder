# The Tether's answer on comparing models that fit the same pattern

**Mode: `general` on the structural answer, `specified` on the isomer reading. Not `grounded`.** This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 8), not a ground reading.

## 1. The residual it answers

Two models with different mechanisms reproduce the same observed pattern. Both have `R_i(s) ≈ 0` on that pattern, so fit is not the residual. The residual is on the source: which mechanism would have generated the pattern, and what would each model return in a regime the pattern does not cover? It lives on a slot the current instrument cannot read. Fig 1 :13 "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state" and Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."

This is a **union compound**, not one hard problem: condition C1 is pattern fit, condition C2 is source identity. Fig 9 :57 "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." A lever on C1 leaves C2 standing.

## 2. The shape

1. **The pattern is a projection, not a source.** It cannot name the mechanism that produced it, because it is exactly where the two models are indiscernible. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: *"the instrument is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source."* The two models are **metamers** at the current instrument.
2. **The difference is in the arrangement.** Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour." If both models are written over a shared atom set, they are isomers. If they share only a substructure, they are members of one functional group (Fig 12 :124). Fig 12 :94 "The arrangement is the substance."
3. **A fit ranking is a filter, not a verdict.** Fig 9 :60 "Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict." Fig 5 :69 "settling is necessary for a term to be composed over, and it is not sufficient." Scores price bits; the discriminator prices actions, and the two do not add (Fig 12 :125-126).
4. **The discriminator is a bearing, not more of the same data.** Fig 6 :23 "No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on contact with a frame whose closure differs." Ask SEARCHED first, then INWARD (extend an instrument already returning a failing resolution), then OUTWARD (find a frame whose closure predicts the effect description). Fig 6 :32 "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."
5. **Independence is bought, never assumed.** Fig 2 :31 "Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence." Fig 2 :42 "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." Two models fitted to the same sample are one witness wearing two names.
6. **The framework does not name the winner.** Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself." Fig 13 :133 "what it returns is a shape rather than a name." Which mechanism is right stays a declared `gap` until a ground settles it.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that a simpler model is the true model. Simplicity is a filter for the budget.
- Not that one of the two models has been refuted. Indiscernibility is not identity.
- Not that the mechanism is unobservable in principle. Fig 8 :39 "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." A below-horizon case is a report, not a verdict.
- Not that more data of the same kind breaks the tie. That is SEARCHED, and it only visits more of the same closure.
- Not `grounded`. No discriminating measurement was taken.
- Not a claim that the figures select a mechanism class. They return the shape of the discriminator.

## 4. What would settle it - the ground

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. The essays give the template: the spectrometer separated green sources; isotope ratios separated mined from lab-grown diamonds. Both were instruments, not debates.

The restated questions:

- **Slot test:** at which variable, tail, transient or regime do the models quietly diverge? Index it per slot, never average (Fig 1 :22).
- **Intervention test:** what would each model return if that variable were clamped, stepped or perturbed? Write the prediction down before running it (Formula, "Direction").
- **Anchor test:** does the resulting reading move when the modelers argue? If it does, it is not an anchor (Fig 2 :29-30).
- **Convergence test:** do two frames drawing on genuinely different evidence arrive at the same mechanism by separate derivation? If so, record the provenance; the content looks identical either way (Fig 8 :51).
- **Standing falsifiers:** the six in `01` section 5, in particular a case where more of the same pattern separates mechanisms on a known ground, or a global score that returns the discriminating signal without per-slot decomposition.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The shape narrows what the discriminator must do, but no figure line names a winning mechanism. Fig 13 :134 "Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence". Fig 9 :44 "Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room." The answer stops at the limit of the available instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *They're Scanning Books* adds the limit on our own categories: *"A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six."*

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The short answer is that fitting the same pattern does not make two models comparable on that pattern, because the pattern is exactly where they are indiscernible. They are metamers at the current instrument. If they are written over the same ingredients arranged differently they are isomers, the difference being in the arrangement rather than the formula; if they share only a substructure they are members of one functional group. A fit score prices the description, not the actions needed to find out which mechanism is right, so it is a filter for what to try first and never a verdict. What is owed is a source-discriminating bearing: a per-slot divergence, an intervention with divergent predictions, or a low-overlap frame whose closure predicts a different shape. That bearing must be answerable to a measurement that does not move when the researchers argue. The framework returns the shape of that bearing, not the name of the winning mechanism. Which model is right stays open until a ground settles it.
