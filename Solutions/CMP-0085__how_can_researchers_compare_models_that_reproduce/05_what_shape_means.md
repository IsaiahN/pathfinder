## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The figures README states the limit: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13: *"It can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands... It cannot say which primitive is missing, because naming it would mean already holding it."*

From *The Library of Babel*: *"You can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."*

For CMP-0085 a **shape** is the narrowed form of a comparison you can state before you can name the winning mechanism. It constrains: the discriminator must separate sources, must be answerable to a ground that does not move when the modelers argue, and must be indexed per slot rather than averaged. It does not fill: it does not say "choose model A".

Two contrasts make this concrete in *How Intelligence Actually Works*:

> "The projection of green can't tell you whether it was RGB, CMYK or something else that composed it."
> "The frame and the photograph are indistinguishable as artifacts, and they carry different warrants. One was taken; one was extracted. And nothing in the image records which."

A shape here is the statement that the artifact carries two different warrants and that provenance or a better instrument is owed. It is not the name of the source.

## 2. How a shape is obtained

- *Library of Babel*: use the vocabulary you already have that is *closest* to the thing, look for recurring new terms that overlap or correlate with it, recurse. *"With each new term gained, you are able to narrow the space by definition and deepen your vocabulary."*
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* Fig 9 :30-31: *"your words are borrowed too, so describe the effect rather than the cause."*
- *Decoding Tacit Knowledge*: *"When contact isn't possible, the best you can send is a plain description of what you'd expect to see, because anyone can check that against the world."*
- *Library of Babel* on bundling: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one?"* This is the union of `01` F7.
- *Library of Babel* on stopping: *"you will know that you are done because the finish line will also be the starting place."* The paradox is that by then you have *"composed the answer through the process of locating it."*

Applied here: describe the divergence in the models' behaviour under a hypothetical intervention, in terms a researcher in a different field could check, before deciding which field to import from. That is the effect description of S2 in `01`.

## 3. The source rule: the projection cannot name its own cause

*The Hard Problem is About Source* is the essay that governs this question most directly:

> "What if you have metrics that do not discriminate about its inputs?"
> "If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information"
> "the instrument is speaking only about what it measured, it cannot comment on the source."
> "An instrument that can't distinguish these cases demonstrates its own inadequacy, it cannot claim anything substantial about what is being measured or what causes it."
> "The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."
> "It was proven wrong not by argument, but by the arrival of a better instrument."

The model-comparison analogue is exact. Two models reproducing one pattern are two sources of one green reading. Asking which is right by examining the green is a category error. The spectrometer resolved the colour case, not an argument about colour. What is owed here is the equivalent of a spectrometer: an instrument that returns differently for the two mechanisms.

The same essay distinguishes two cases that must not be merged:

- **Traces exist.** Mined versus lab-grown diamonds were separated when isotope ratios became readable. The instrument improved and the case closed.
- **No trace exists.** The green projection under the original reading has no recoverable source in the projection itself. Only provenance from outside the projection can decide.

*How Intelligence Actually Works* names the boundary condition: *"the answer doesn't exist until the question is contacted."* And the caution: *"The recursion converges toward indiscernibility, and indiscernibility is not identity."* A model that is indiscernible from its rival at the current instrument is not thereby the same mechanism, nor is the rival refuted.

## 4. What licenses a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* The README: the framework *"stops at the limit of what the available instruments can distinguish, and records the lineage so the stop is honest."*
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test. *They're Scanning Books* sharpens it: *"The real import is not a new term in the existing space. It is a new dimension of the space."*

The same essay adds the honesty condition on declared primitives: *"A minimum viable set of six terms is a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes; another sensorium's minimum set need not be those six."* And: *"Declaring the primitives does not make them neutral. It makes the choice visible."* So the shape we report is a visible choice of what counts as a discriminator, not nature's own list.

## 5. Q — does the framework tell us which mechanism is right?

**No.** Nothing in the figure set names a mechanism class as the winner of a pattern-only comparison. The framework's own limit is Fig 9 :32 (*"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*) and Fig 13 :133 (*"what it returns is a shape rather than a name."*). A particular mechanism is licensed only by an instrument settling it, or by a provenanced import with a shadow test. Until one of those, the name is the researcher's own contribution and must be recorded as such.

This is not an evasion. The canonical README says what the framework *can* do: state what is reachable from what you hold, what is unreachable and therefore owed, and what a missing thing would have to look like. Here the missing thing is a discriminating instrument, characterized in effect terms but not held.

## 6. Correction to my own §3

An early reading of §3 concluded that the framework forbids naming any mechanism whatsoever. That overstates it. The README's phrase is *"what a **missing** thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* The subject is something not held in Γ. If both models are already expressible in the comparison frame, naming one as *the candidate under test* is a binding from the domain frame onto a held atom, not the invention of a new primitive. What remains forbidden is claiming the framework itself selected that candidate, or that the binding moves the wall (Fig 6: *"IMPORT adds an atom"*, and this adds none).

Both the source rule and the narrowed form are now recorded so neither is quietly lost. The source rule still bites: the pattern cannot discriminate the sources, and a name read off the pattern is a projection error.

## 7. Two consequences for outreach

**Prestige is not action.** From *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly... Someone let prestige stand in for proof... Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it. The Ground Maintainer must be blind to prestige."* A campaign that gathers well-known names to agree that one model is right is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask gives the comparison a budget to be tried and lets a ground, an instrument, an intervention, settle it.

**Difference is the resource.** From *How a Society of Agents Would Work*: *"A group is only worth having while its members stay different."* Ten reviewers who all fitted the same models to the same sample are one reader with ten names. Fig 2's independence rule is the arithmetic version of the same claim.

## 8. My own audit is a simulation, not a ground reading

Same essay: *"LLMs can quickly simulate an approximate answer/output with the formula found in the architecture post. It will often be better than the generic outputs that you will usually get otherwise too... However, the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture or working the formula yourself."* The architecture article adds that outputs *"could never be verified if the solutions presented were not contrived to fit the formula."*

So `01` is a simulated derivation, checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is **not** a test of the model-comparison claim and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on. Its value is the shape, the refuter written in advance, and the record of what would reverse it.
