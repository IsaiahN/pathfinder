**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover letter attached to a submission through a public venue in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Subject:** Check one shape claim about ocean heat and carbon transport before we talk theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific shape claim I would like you to check against what you already know about ocean circulation. I am not asking you to read any framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the shape does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`, and explicitly a simulated derivation, not a ground reading):**
>
> 1. Heat, nutrients and carbon are not one transported stock. They have different currencies and different anchors, so this is a union of overlapping conditions with its own loop each, not one question with one answer. A weaker overturning does not clear all three with one lever.
> 2. Heat transport is a flow: energy carried by a gradient over a state. A gradient over space and a gradient over time are different terms. That is why a basin-specific, depth-specific, time-window-specific result is not the same reading as a global mean heat content trend. The global average is the aggregate that hides the subpolar cooling and the Indo-Pacific warming.
> 3. The nutrient and carbon consequences are contact cascades, not first-order transport. The change in the current is first-order; the residual that matters for productivity, community structure, export and fisheries is several steps out, and its sign flips with the region and the question.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** I am not giving you a number, a basin-specific forecast, or a date for a tipping event. The figures I work from return a shape, not a name and not a magnitude. No measurement was taken for this note, so I am calling those slots a `gap`, not an answer. I am also not claiming that model-ensemble agreement is corroboration of any magnitude. Models and reanalyses largely share the same observations and ancestry.
>
> **What would refute the claim, written before this note was sent:**
> 1. Heat, nutrients and carbon share one currency and one anchor, so one lever clears all three.
> 2. A heat transport term is exhibited in which energy is not bonded to a gradient over a state, or a gradient over space and one over time are the same term.
> 3. The nutrient and carbon response is strictly first-order in the current anomaly, with no cascade and no sign flip across regions.
> 4. An assessment frame is exhibited whose reported value changes when a model prior changes.
>
> If any holds, I withdraw the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** I will send three public sources. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Ocean heat transport and the Southern Ocean's mediator role: https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/2752-5295/aeb326
> - Ocean heat content, reanalysis spread and RAPID comparison: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41612-024-00860-6
> - Fine-scale currents and biogeochemical cycling: https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-marine-020723-020531

> **If you want to reuse the method:** the formula is public in the figures link I will send at that stage. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get (general, specified or grounded). A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## What not to add

- Do not add a preferred institution, a famous author, or "the leading expert thinks..." Prestige can fund an action; it cannot settle one (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*).
- Do not add a number to make the email feel conclusive. That converts a `gap` into a verdict in `general` mode.
- Do not send to a list selected for likely agreement. Fig 2 :40 again: shared evidence makes the agreement worthless as a verdict.
- Do not attach the framework PDF to stage 1. Fig 10 :42: "A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."
