## 0. The claim under audit

> Changing ocean currents will redistribute heat, nutrients and carbon in some determinate direction and magnitude.

The task is to derive what the figures license before answering. The question is an empirical forecast about a real physical system. The figures are a domain-agnostic barrier theorem. They cannot contain a measurement of the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation. What they can do, and what this audit does, is fix the shape any answer must have, name the anchor that could settle it, and write the refusal conditions before the conclusion. The specific direction and magnitude is reported as a `gap`, not as a silence and not as a number invented to fill the slot.

---

## 1. The residual in effect terms

**R** is the gap between what a held picture of ocean transport predicts about where heat, nutrients and carbon end up, and what the observing system actually reads under a changed circulation.

- **Arity.** At least four: one carrier (the circulation) and three transported quantities (heat, nutrients, carbon). The three do not share a currency, so this is a **union compound** (Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."). Heat is energy moved by a gradient; nutrients are matter consumed and regenerated by biology; carbon is matter moved by both a solubility pump and a biological pump and can cross the air-sea boundary. One lever cannot clear all three (Fig 9 :55: "A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate ones.").
- **Symmetry.** Broken. Heat transport is approximately conservative within a basin budget; nutrient and carbon transport has sources, sinks and biological transformation. The three cannot be permuted. The comparison bond is a threshold relation, not a symmetric join.
- **Scale.** Basin to global; seasonal to centennial; surface to abyssal; fine-scale fronts (1 to 100 km) upscaling into basin budgets. A single global mean is the wrong object (Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average.").
- **Per-slot reading.** Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :13: "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state". Slots here are basin x depth x season for heat, region x nutrient x trophic level for nutrients, and pump x depth x basin for carbon.
- **Anchor.** Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." In this field the anchors are mooring arrays (RAPID, OSNAP, SAMBA), Argo and BGC-Argo profiles, altimetry and tracer inventories. They do not update on what a model concludes (Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update.").

## 4. Findings

### F1  -  The question is a union compound, not one transport problem (governing)

Fig 9 :54: "And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again." Fig 9 :57: "What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either." And Fig 9 :4-5: "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."

A single lever (a weaker overturning) does not clear the intersections. Heat follows a gradient and is nearly conservative within a basin budget; nutrients and carbon are transformed by biology and chemistry and have different sinks. The union is corroborated from a low-overlap world field: Lévy et al. 2024 (Annual Review of Marine Science) state that fine-scale currents are "actively involved in the transport and transformation of biogeochemical tracers in the ocean" while "their overall impact on large-scale biogeochemical cycling on the timescale of years remains poorly understood due to the multiscale nature of the problem." That is a different currency and a different instrument pool from the heat-transport literature.

**Verdict:** `specified` (structural), triangulated across Fig 9 and Fig 13 :74 (composition across kinds) with low overlap, plus a world corroborator from biogeochemistry.
**Refuter:** show that heat, nutrients and carbon share one currency and one anchor so that a single lever clears all three, or that the three are a partition with an assignable shared fraction of zero.

### F2  -  Heat transport is a legal flow; the gradient is bonded to a state (decisive for the shape)

Fig 13 :76: "Energy and a gradient compose a flow. A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is." Fig 13 :74: "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."

Ocean heat transport is exactly this composition: heat (the state Energy) carried down a density and temperature contrast (the structure Gradient) over space (the state Space). A meridional gradient over space and a seasonal contrast over time are different terms, so a decadal basin-mean heat transport and a seasonal heat flux are not variants of one reading. The world literature reaches the same distinction in its own terms: Li and Liu 2026 decompose meridional ocean heat transport into components "due to ocean circulation change" and "due to ocean temperature change", and report that "inter-basin heat transports, especially those across 33° S, are much larger in magnitude than OHU/loss in individual basins". The arrangement, not just the ingredients, decides the result.

**Verdict:** `specified` (composition legality), triangulated across Fig 13 :74/:76 and Fig 12 :5 (the bond carries information the operands do not), with low overlap between the substrate rule and the bond pricing rule.
**Refuter:** exhibit a heat transport term in which the gradient is not bonded to a state, or show that a flow is a direct state-state bond under the six-term set.

### F3  -  Measure per slot; a global mean hides the live signal

Fig 1 :19: "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated." Fig 1 :22: "Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average." Fig 1 :13: "a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state".

The world result is a direct instance. Li and Liu 2026 report that a weakened AMOC "moves heat from the subpolar North Atlantic to the lower-latitude Atlantic and the South Atlantic" and later spreads it into the Indo-Pacific, with "the Southern Ocean's 'mediator' role in redistributing rather than storing heat". Liu et al. 2024 (npj Climate and Atmospheric Science) find that "The spatial OHC trend patterns captured by reanalyses are consistent with each other, but sensitive to the selected time period." A global mean heat content trend is the average that erases the subpolar cooling and the Indo-Pacific warming.

**Verdict:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 1 (residual indexing) and Fig 2 :29 (the anchor is a measurement at a place), with the world sources as secondary corroboration.
**Refuter:** show that the quantity of interest is the global integral alone and that regional sign reversals carry no information for the decision.

### F4  -  Nutrient and carbon effects are cascades; the habitat is enumerated, never composed

Fig 11 :43: "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :46: "conditions: light, water, temperature, season, terrain". Fig 11 :52: "And contact cascades. Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."

A current change is first-order contact; the residual that matters for fisheries and carbon budgets is three steps out, in primary production, community composition and export. Carbon Brief 2026 (Keating) states that the circulation is "an even larger network of ocean currents that continuously moves water, nutrients and carbon around the world" and that "The AMOC does not only transport heat. It also plays a role in the transportation of nutrients that support marine ecosystems, as well as supporting the carbon cycle by transporting carbon-rich surface waters to the deep ocean." The systematic review by Defrance et al. 2026 reports that "A weakened AMOC reorganises the physical supply of nutrients, oxygen, and carbon to the ocean interior" and that "The clearest signal is a fall in marine primary production, concentrated in the North Atlantic."

**Verdict:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 11 (contact and cascade) and Fig 13 :32 ("a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos"), with the world sources as low-overlap secondary corroboration.
**Refuter:** show that the nutrient and carbon response is strictly first-order in the current anomaly, with no trophic or depth cascade and no residual three steps out.

### F5  -  Valence is relative to the residual; the same current is benefactor and malfactor

Fig 11 :49: "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :51: "No standing list of good actors survives a change of residual."

A weaker AMOC cools the subpolar North Atlantic (a loss for some fisheries, a gain for others) while warming the Indo-Pacific and increasing ocean heat uptake efficiency. Li and Liu 2026 report that the weakened AMOC "leads to OHU but surface cooling in terms of global average, hence significantly promotes OHU efficiency." There is no standing list of "good" or "bad" circulation changes; the sign flips with the residual and the slot.

**Verdict:** `specified`, single-source on the figure side for the valence rule (Fig 11) but with independent world corroboration from a different basin and currency; kept `single-source` on the framework claim itself, not upgraded.
**Refuter:** exhibit a circulation change whose valence is invariant across all slots and all residuals.

### F6  -  Model agreement is one witness, not corroboration

Fig 2 :40: "disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool". Fig 7 :35: "A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes." Fig 8 :48: "How much two frames draw on the same evidence decides both whether their agreement means anything and whether their union has any surplus."

CMIP-class models, ocean reanalyses and assessment reports draw on the same Argo and mooring observations and share model ancestry. Their agreement is efficient for a convention (a common vocabulary of "AMOC weakening") and disqualifying for a verdict about magnitudes. The IPCC itself, as quoted by Carbon Brief 2026, has "high confidence" in future weakening as a qualitative feature but "low confidence" in "quantitative projections" of AMOC decline in the 21st century. Liu et al. 2024 report substantial spread and period sensitivity among reanalyses. Met Office scientist Laura Jackson, quoted by the Met Office 2024 and Carbon Brief 2026, says: "The AMOC is not a specific thing - it is the impact of lots of different currents. It is affected by processes happening at small scales, like mixing and eddies which affect how the heat and salt are distributed. We can't resolve many of these with climate models."

**Verdict:** `specified`, triangulated across three figures with low overlap (anchor/independence, room chain, union surplus).
**Refuter:** show that two model families used independent instruments and independent data assimilation, and that their agreement therefore survives the effective-sample-size correction.

### F7  -  The anchor is the observing system, and it does not update on model opinion

Fig 2 :29: "An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome." Fig 2 :33: "The anchor must not update." Fig 6 :32: "An instrument is not built from a description. It is improved from a worse instrument already returning something."

The RAPID array, OSNAP, Argo and BGC-Argo are anchors. They return readings; they do not read model conclusions. Moat (National Oceanography Centre, 2026) reports a weakening trend of "-0.7±0.6 Sv / decade" over 20 years and says "it is crucial to continue observing the AMOC through to at least 2034 to ascertain its trend and confirm if it is natural long-term variability or anthropogenic in nature." That is an instrument requirement, not an argument. The observing system was improved from ship-based snapshot sections to moorings to floats, each built on a residual the previous one produced.

**Verdict:** `specified`, triangulated across Fig 2 and Fig 6 (anchor definition versus instrument descent), with NOC and Met Office as world corroboration.
**Refuter:** exhibit an observing system whose reported value changes when a model's prior changes.

### F8  -  No last forecast: the gap is a verdict, not a silence

Fig 7 :6: "the limit: no last room". Fig 7 :31: "no finite number of them ever arrives". Fig 8 :39: "So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence." Fig 13 :134: "It can say the answer is out of reach with the library as it stands, so something has to be imported before it is asked again."

The figures license the shape, the refuters and the instrument specification. They do not license a numerical redistribution. The absence of a figure line that returns a transport number is not a silence to be filled with a plausible forecast; it is a `gap` of the unreachable-at-this-budget kind. The world literature agrees about the limit in its own vocabulary: Boers, quoted by Carbon Brief 2026, says "So, early warning in terms of a prediction? No way. It just doesn't work." Rahmstorf, in the same source, says "we don't have 100 years of accurate AMOC data."

**Verdict:** `general` on the structural limit, `gap` on the number, triangulated across Fig 7, Fig 8 and Fig 13 with low overlap.
**Refuter:** produce a figure line that returns a basin-specific heat, nutrient or carbon transport magnitude, or a ground reading that settles it (in which case the mode moves to `grounded` and this finding is superseded).

### F9  -  Smooth change and threshold change are different objects

Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." The overturning is a cycle whose output (deep water formation) re-enters as input (salt and heat transport), with the salt-advection feedback as the sign. Fig 9 :4-5 splits disagreements that do not shrink with effort. Carbon Brief 2026 documents an active debate about whether the AMOC is bistable and whether a threshold is near, with different definitions of "collapse" and different timing estimates. That debate is not one hard question with two answers; it is a union of a smooth-trend question and a regime-change question with different instruments.

**Verdict:** `specified` on the categorical distinction, `gap` on timing, single-source on the figure side (Fig 12 cycle rule) with world corroboration.
**Refuter:** show that the threshold case is continuous with the trend case, so one instrument and one loop cover both.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the standing instruction that "a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument". The shape claim is refused if any of the following holds:

1. **Union refuter.** Heat, nutrients and carbon are shown to share one currency and one anchor, so a single lever clears all three and the union unbundling is unnecessary.
2. **Flow refuter.** A legal heat-transport composition is exhibited in which Energy is not bonded to a Gradient over a state, or a gradient over space and one over time are shown to be the same term.
3. **Slot refuter.** A decision is exhibited for which the global heat content mean is sufficient and regional sign reversals carry no information.
4. **Cascade refuter.** The nutrient and carbon response is shown to be strictly first-order in the current anomaly, with no trophic, depth or habitat cascade.
5. **Anchor refuter.** An assessment frame is exhibited whose reported value changes when a model prior changes, making the frame itself the anchor.
6. **Gap refuter.** A figure line is produced that returns a basin-specific transport magnitude, or a ground reading settles one (which moves that slot to `grounded`).
7. **Kind refuter.** The threshold case is shown to be continuous with the smooth trend case, so no separate loop is owed.

If any fires, the corresponding finding is withdrawn and the domain census is redone. Until a ground reading fires, no part of this folder may be labelled `grounded`.

2. Name the anchor explicitly: moorings, Argo and BGC-Argo, not model consensus (F6, F7).
3. Report verdicts per domain and never average them into a single "redistribution" number (F1, F10).

6. Keep the threshold case in its own loop (F9).

## 8. Strains and re-census

- **S1, live but narrowed: the gradient atom versus the oceanographic gradient.** Fig 13 :22 calls a gradient "a difference that can be spent". Ocean heat transport is carried by temperature and salinity contrasts plus wind-driven gyres, not by a single scalar. Re-census: the framework's gradient is a structure bonded to a state; the world literature decomposes the same flow into circulation-driven and temperature-driven components. Different objects, no contradiction. The mapping is a binding, not a new primitive.
- **S2, evidence for the union, not a flipped verdict: world sources disagree.** Liu et al. 2024 report results that "do not support previous studies suggesting that MOC changes are reducing Earth's mean surface warming", while Li and Liu 2026 report that a weakened AMOC "significantly promotes OHU efficiency" and redistributes heat to the Indo-Pacific. Returned to the census: they govern different windows, basins and definitions of AMOC, and their disagreement is evidence that this is a union with period-sensitive slots, not one question with a yes/no answer. The verdict was not flipped.
- **S3, dissolved by re-census: per-slot versus basin-integrated budgets.** A basin integral aggregates over point slots, which seems to conflict with Fig 1 :19. Fig 12 :104 resolves it: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it." A basin is a slot at the next level. Irreducibility is level-relative.
- **S4, live and named: cycle sign at threshold.** Fig 12 :121 says the sign of the loop decides whether it closes. Whether the overturning's salt-advection feedback currently has the closing sign is a world measurement question, not a figure question. Kept open with a falsifier in `06_isomers_and_order.md`.

**After re-census: two live strains, both named (S1 narrowed, S4 open), one dissolved (S3), one kept as evidence (S2). None hidden.**
