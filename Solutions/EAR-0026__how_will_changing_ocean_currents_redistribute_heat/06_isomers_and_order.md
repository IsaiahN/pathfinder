# Flows, cycles and kinds of change in ocean redistribution

**Mode: `specified` on the structural distinctions, `gap` on any timing or magnitude. Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (`05_what_shape_means.md` §7).**

## RESIDUAL

The question bundles three transports and a carrier into one verb, "redistribute". Three questions are owed: (1) what kind of composition is a heat transport, (2) what kind of composition is the overturning that carries it, and (3) is a threshold crossing the same kind of object as a smooth trend, or a different one with its own loop?

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held: Space, Time, Energy (states) and Gradient, Network, Spectrum (structures). Bonds held: the seven plus negation. No new primitive is available without import. The ocean frame supplies bindings: a current is a flow over a state, a contrast is a gradient, an ecosystem is a network, a size or age ordering is a spectrum. These are bindings onto held atoms, not new primitives (see `05` §6).

### Q2  -  What kind of composition is heat transport?

- Fig 13 :74: "Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."
- A first-order flow molecule is `state → structure → state`, one crossing. Heat transport reads `Space → Gradient → Energy` when written as a transport of thermal energy through space down a contrast, or `Energy → Gradient → Space` when written as the warming of a region. The order matters because the bond is ordered.
- Fig 13 :76 makes the decisive point: "A gradient over space and one over time are different terms, because which state it is bonded to is part of what it is." So a meridional heat transport and a seasonal heat flux are different molecules, not spellings of one.
- World check, not a figure line: Li and Liu 2026 decompose the transport into a circulation-driven part and a temperature-driven part, and find that "inter-basin heat transports, especially those across 33° S, are much larger in magnitude than OHU/loss in individual basins". Two bonded gradients, different terms. Mode `specified`, unmeasured here; triangulated for legality, single-source for the physics.

### Q3  -  What kind of composition is the overturning?

- Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."
- The overturning is a cycle, not a chain: sinking water sets up the density contrast that drives the return flow, which sets up the conditions for further sinking. The salt-advection feedback is the sign of the loop. Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use." Salt is regenerated and re-enters; the loop can close.
- Carbon Brief 2026 documents the two-regime ("bistable") proposal from Stommel's 1961 model onward and the live debate about whether the present-day system is in such a regime. A cycle with a sign is a different object from a linear trend, and needs its own check.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if the overturning's feedback is demonstrably sign-negative in the present regime so that it cannot close, the cycle claim fails and the system is a forced chain. `gap` on the sign, which is a world measurement.

### Q4  -  Are heat, nutrient and carbon transport isomers?

- Fig 12 :117: "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- Written over the full shared atom set `{Space, Time, Energy, Gradient, Network, Spectrum}`, heat transport, nutrient supply and carbon export share the flow skeleton `state → structure → state` but differ in which state and which structure occupy the slots: heat bonds Energy to Gradient; nutrients bond a spatial contrast to a Network of trophic relations; carbon bonds a solubility Gradient to a biological Network and a Spectrum of particle sizes. Same skeleton, different arrangement, different behaviour. They are **isomers** under Fig 12's definition.
- The weaker claim is safer and also true: they are members of one **functional group** (the flow skeleton), with different qualifiers. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if any of the three can be written without an atom another requires (for example nutrient supply with no Network, or heat transport with no Gradient), the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim stands.

### Q5  -  What order is the union?

- Fig 12 :104: "A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it." Ocean heat content trends and BGC-Argo export estimates are settled against the ground, so each can serve as an atom at the next level.
- Their comparison is **second-order**: `(settled heat budget) ⋛ (settled nutrient/carbon budget)`. Operators table defines comparison as "a threshold relation between two quantities". At second order the operands are settled molecules now serving as atoms. Level-relativity permits the type; whether they count as quantities at that level needs an instrument, not an argument.
- Triangulated for the recursion (Fig 12 + Fig 4 :19-20, "The kernel is the part that does not change with size"), strained-for-now on the operand type. Named, not hidden.

### Q6  -  Smooth change versus threshold change

- Fig 9 :4-5: "are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search." Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule works well on a different subset, are a union to be split.
- The AMOC literature has exactly this shape: a smooth weakening ("very likely" over the 21st century according to the IPCC as quoted by Carbon Brief 2026) and a possible regime transition (bistability, timing debated, definitions of collapse different). One instrument does not cover both.
- Fig 13 :32: "a remainder compounding through a chain that amplifies is chaos, and the detail a crossing discards reappears above it as noise." Internal variability is not an error to be averaged away; it is the remainder that compounders must track.
- **Falsifier:** if the threshold case is shown to be continuous with the trend case, so one loop and one instrument cover both, the split is unnecessary and this finding is withdrawn.

## CONCLUSION

Heat transport is a first-order cross-kind flow; the overturning is a cycle whose sign is a world measurement; heat, nutrient and carbon transports are isomers over the shared atom set, or at minimum members of one functional group; their comparison is second-order and permitted by level-relativity with an operand-type strain still open; and smooth weakening and threshold crossing are different objects with different instruments. This framing rescues the structural content of the question without inventing a seventh primitive, importing an unproven atom, or returning a number the figures do not contain.
