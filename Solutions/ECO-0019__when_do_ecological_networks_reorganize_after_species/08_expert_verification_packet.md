# Expert verification packet — the Tether on post-extinction network function

**What this is:** a claim in a form an ecological-network researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` — not `grounded`.** No measurement at a ground is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"Reorganize" and "lose function" are different quantities and the question is a union of conditions, so it must be split. The residual is per function slot: a function is maintained when a substitute composition is reachable among the remaining actors and the new bonds lower the measured gap in that slot; it is lost when no such composition exists and the reach for that slot has fallen until an import arrives. Which actor matters is relative to the function at stake, so the dangerous loss pattern is selective removal of highly connected actors, not random removal. Contact must be enumerated outward until the cascade stops mattering, because the residual that matters is often several steps out. A network metric is a filter for where to look, not a witness for what happened. The witness is the per-slot functional measurement. In an unmaintained habitat nothing selects for function; in a managed habitat introduction is available and exclusion is not, so management is permanent work.

- **Refuter:** a single global network metric that predicts every function slot with no live slot residual, or a case where all post-loss change is accounted for by direct partners only.

### C2 — ecology-facing; checkable against network-extinction studies

- **Statement:** which species matters is relative to the function at stake; functional maintenance comes from reachable redundancy, and rewiring can change function without changing species counts. Resilience to random loss does not imply resilience to selective loss of connectors.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :49 "An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries." Fig 11 :50 "The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds." Fig 13 :115 "The set is closed. The arrangements are not. That gap is why import exists."
- **What to check:** Memmott, Waser and Price 2004 reported tolerance to pollinator loss deriving from "redundancy in pollinators per plant and from nested topology of the networks", with declines "no worse than linear" under preferential removal of the most-linked pollinators. Solé and Montoya 2001 found networks "very robust against random removals but can be extremely fragile when selective attacks are used". Rojas et al. 2025 found that "plant species that sustain network cohesiveness differ from those that determine the nutritional characteristics of bird diets" and that "rewiring can affect birds' nutrition, which may carry some costs." Do those findings hold in the system you know?
- **Refuter:** removing the most-connected actor being no worse than random removal across all measured slots; or a rewiring that restores contact in every measured slot; or a function loss with unchanged bonds.

### C3 — framework-internal derivation about recovery

- **Statement:** recovery of a role that did not exist before is `⇒` production, not `→` sequence; a managed system can introduce but cannot subtract the fact of contact, so it is permanent work. A rewiring that does not lower the leftover residual is not success.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 11 :61 "The seat's power is asymmetric. Introduction is available; exclusion is not." Fig 11 :63 "Which is why a managed ecosystem is permanent work rather than a solved state." Fig 11 :40 "An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing, however much it improves." `THE_FORMULA.md` step 3: "accept iff cost(φ) + left(R,φ) < cost(R)".
- **Refuter:** a managed system that restores function by subtracting an actor, or that stays solved after a one-off intervention without further work.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not one unconditional "when" covering all functions, habitats and loss patterns.
- Not a universal keystone list, and not that highly connected species are intrinsically important.
- Not that reorganization implies safety, or that a species count or connectivity score predicts function.
- Not that the framework's "habitat" is ecology's "niche". That mapping is declined in the source essay, and this packet uses "habitat" only as the figure's field of contact with the residual.
- Not a numeric threshold. Lever et al. 2014 report threshold and hysteresis in pollinator communities; that is single-source world corroboration, not a figure-derived constant.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

A per-slot functional assay before and after a specified loss, with contact enumerated outward until the cascade stops mattering. The discriminating test is whether a substitute composition is reachable and whether the leftover residual falls after re-wiring. A threshold claim needs repeated pressure levels and reversal, not a single before/after. A single global metric with no live slot residual would refute C1; insensitivity of function to connector removal would refute C2; subtraction-based or permanently solved restoration would refute C3.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, Fig 9 :4 and :60, Fig 11 :44 and :49. Five short passages.
2. Check C2 against their own system: does the most-linked species matter more than a random one, for the function they actually measure?
3. Name one post-loss case where a network metric stayed flat while a function changed, or vice versa. That is the metamerism test.
4. Say which slot they would measure first and how far out they would enumerate contact.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
