# Isomers, catalysts and order in post-loss networks

## RESIDUAL

Two questions the main audit leaves open: (1) are two networks with the same species list but different interactions the "same" network for function purposes, and (2) what kind of order does a loss-and-recovery sequence have? A third supporting question: what does the figure call a part that survives its own use, since that is the shape of a redundant or irreplaceable species.

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Are two webs with the same species list different substances?

- Fig 12 :117 "isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."
- Fig 12 :95 "Two entries with an identical ingredient set are not variants of one thing. They are different things whose notation cannot tell them apart, and the difference is carried entirely by the bond."
- ⇒ Two ecological networks with the same species list but different interaction wiring are **isomers** under the figure's own definition. A species inventory alone cannot tell them apart, and the difference is decisive in behaviour (function). This is the structural reason a species count is not a function predictor.
- **Falsifier:** two webs with the same atoms and different bonds that are indistinguishable in every measured function slot. If such a case is found, the isomer claim fails for ecology and bond-level description is unnecessary.

### Q2 — Are two webs with different species lists ever the same kind of thing functionally?

- Fig 12 :123 "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."
- ⇒ Webs with different species but the same functional substructure belong to one functional group. The species names are qualifiers; the kind is fixed by the substructure. This is the structural reading of redundancy: replacement is possible because the group, not the individual, fixes the outcome.
- World corroboration, low overlap: Memmott et al. 2004 attribute tolerance to "redundancy in pollinators per plant and from nested topology of the networks".
- **Falsifier:** a replacement species in the same functional group that does not restore the function. That breaks the functional-group reading as applied here.

### Q3 — What is the shape of a species that matters beyond its own abundance?

- Fig 12 :119 "catalyst. A part that survives its own use. Spent and regenerating parts behave differently across uses, and nothing records which a term is."
- ⇒ A pollinator, a disperser or an ecosystem engineer is catalyst-shaped: it acts without being consumed by the interaction. The figure's warning is the load-bearing part: "nothing records which a term is", so a species list cannot tell you which actors are spent and which regenerate. That is exactly what Rojas et al. 2025 found: network-centrality importance and nutritional importance are different things. A census of species does not record the distinction.
- **Falsifier:** a demonstrated census field that reliably separates catalyst-shaped from spent actors from the species list alone, across functions and contexts.

### Q4 — What order does loss have?

- Loss is `−` subtraction: "A with B removed or absent" (Fig 12 :80). The second operand's absence is the point. This is not negation: a species' absence is not the refusal of a claim.
- Recovery by an actor that was not there is `⇒` production: "A produces B; B did not exist before" (Fig 12 :76). A restoration sequence is `→` sequence: "A then B; B needs A to have happened" (Fig 12 :72). These are different bonds and must not be confused. A plan is a sequence; a new function is a production; a loss is a subtraction.
- Cascade order is **enumeration**, not composition. Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed." Fig 11 :44 "List everything in contact with the residual, then what is in contact with those, and outward until the cascade stops mattering."
- Fig 11 :52 "Scoping the habitat to direct contact is how DDT reached the eagles: first-order enumeration, second-order effect, and the residual that mattered was three steps out."
- ⇒ The order of a cascade is read off the world, not derived from the network's vocabulary. A first-order assessment is not a weaker version of a third-order one; it is a reading of something else.
- **Falsifier:** a cascade whose order is derivable from interaction types alone without enumerating contact outward.

### Q5 — What does the round trip drop when the figure set is condensed into an ecological prediction?

- The Symbols table defines `R_T` as `gap(x, (T_E ∘ T_A)(x))`, "the gap between what was sent up and what came back down", measuring "what the coarser description cannot hold". Fig 4 :30 "So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."
- **Sent up (x):** Fig 11 enumeration and valence relativity, Fig 1 per-slot residual, Fig 12 isomer and catalyst, Fig 13 closed set / open arrangements.
- **Abstraction (T_A):** "reorganize if a substitute is reachable, lose function if it is not".
- **Concretisation (T_E):** a prediction about a particular web after a particular extinction.
- **Dropped across the trip:** (i) per-slot indexing, so an aggregate metric can hide a live slot; (ii) cascade depth, so a third-order effect is reported as a first-order one; (iii) the catalyst/spent distinction, so a redundant-looking actor is treated as interchangeable; (iv) the union structure of the question, so two conditions are reported as one "when".
- `R_T` is **nonzero** and is recorded qualitatively as this enumerated set. It was not measured numerically, because no ground reading was taken in this folder. Treating the shorthand as identical to the figure set would set `R_T` to zero and commit the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.

## CONCLUSION

Two webs with the same species list are isomers: same atoms, different bond, different function (Q1). Webs with different species can be one functional group, which is the structural reading of redundancy (Q2). Some actors are catalyst-shaped and a species list does not record which (Q3). Loss is subtraction, recovery of a new role is production, a restoration plan is a sequence, and a cascade is enumerated outward rather than composed (Q4). Condensing all of this into a single "when" drops four constraints, so the round-trip gap is nonzero (Q5). The order claim and the isomer claim need no new primitive; they are rearrangements of what the figure set already holds.
