**Subject:** One checkable claim about post-extinction function loss, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against work you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified`, not `grounded`):** ecological networks do not reorganize or lose function as a whole. The residual is per function slot, not one global health number. After a species loss, a function is maintained when a substitute composition is reachable among the remaining actors, and is lost when no remaining composition delivers it. Rewiring is a mint, and it pays only if it lowers the leftover gap; a re-wire that does not restore measured function changes nothing, however much the topology changes. The habitat must be enumerated outward until the cascade stops mattering, because the residual that matters is often several steps out. Relative to this, connectivity and nestedness are filters for where to look, not witnesses for the verdict.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that there is one unconditional "when" that covers all functions and habitats. The question as posed is a union of overlapping conditions and I split it rather than searching it as one thing. I am not claiming a threshold number, a universal keystone list, or that highly connected species are intrinsically important. Whether a species matters is relative to the function at stake. I am not claiming that a managed intervention solves the system permanently: introduction is available, exclusion is not, and a managed ecosystem is permanent work.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim** (written before I ran this):
> 1. a single global network metric that predicts every function slot with no live slot residual left;
> 2. a rewiring that restores contact in every measured slot, or a function loss with unchanged bonds;
> 3. a function that recovers with no substitute composition in the remaining habitat and no import;
> 4. removing the most-connected actor being no worse than random removal across all slots;
> 5. all post-loss function change being explained by direct partners only;
> 6. a managed system restoring function by subtraction, or staying solved without further work.
>
> I wrote these down before sending this. If any holds, I will narrow or withdraw the claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the derivation is below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them, and the world evidence is secondary.
> - The full audit, including the retraction log: `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`
> - The one-page check: `08_expert_verification_packet.md`
> - The synthesized answer and its honest stop: `07_the_tether_answer.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** it is a method, not a conclusion. Run it on a system you know and can measure. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and label the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
