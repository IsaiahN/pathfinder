# The Tether's answer on early-universe gravitational-wave background signatures

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the source name and the discriminating instrument. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section 7), not a ground reading. No measurement was taken.

## 1. The residual it answers

A stochastic gravitational-wave background is detected in the nanohertz band, with a Hellings and Downs correlation that certifies a gravitational origin. Its source is not identified. NANOGrav's own abstract says the signal is *"consistent with astrophysical expectations for a signal from a population of supermassive black-hole binaries, although more exotic cosmological and astrophysical sources cannot be excluded."* Wikipedia states the same limit: *"The sources of this gravitational-wave background cannot be identified without further observations and analyses."*

The residual is therefore not "is there a background" but "what in the background's form would let a source be named." It is a union compound with several conditions (Fig 9 :54-58) and it is indexed by frequency and sky slot (Fig 1 :19).

## 2. The shape, per domain, kept separate

**D1, detector and ground (`grounded` for detection, `specified` for attribution).** The anchor is the measured inter-pulsar correlation, which does not move when a theorist changes models (Fig 2 :29). The background exists in the nHz band. Ground interferometers constrain the Hz band; LISA will open the mHz band. The domains have different anchors, so their verdicts are never averaged into one "the GWB signature."

**D2, source models (`specified`, ungrounded).** Inflation, phase transitions, cosmic strings and SMBH binary populations are imports from a different closure. They produce the same kind of thing, a stochastic strain field, with different statistical properties (Fig 12 :117). Over the shared atom set they are isomers; as currently written they are a functional group (Fig 12 :123). None is selected by the figures.

**D3, inference (`general` on the bargain, `specified` on the per-model fit).** Model choice is an MDL bargain (Fig 5 :42-44). NANOGrav's Bayes factors depend on spectral modeling choices, which is the leftover the bargain prices. The sought shape must be bound as an ordinary term, because a preference the loop cannot see would otherwise be read off any fitted excess (Fig 5 :62-66, Fig 9 :27-28).

**D4, verification (`general`).** Four PTA consortia using different telescopes and analysis methods is closer to independent instruments than to one report read twice (Fig 2 :31-32). Pooling into the IPTA raises sensitivity and can reduce witness independence; the two quantities are read separately. Agreement is never pooled as corroboration (Fig 2 :40).

## 3. What the answer says

1. **A signature is a per-slot deviation, not an existence claim.** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Aggregating across frequency or sky is how a band-local feature disappears (Fig 1 :22). The bound term from the README is a deviation of spectral or statistical form that a source-discriminating observation would settle.
2. **The sources form a union compound.** Astrophysical and cosmological conditions overlap in the same band. A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either (Fig 9 :57), so one lever does not clear the intersections.
3. **The figures return the shape and refuse the name.** Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."* Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence."* Naming a source requires the ground to settle a term or a provenanced import with a shadow test (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52). Neither exists for the observed background.
4. **Instrument condition.** An instrument that reads a spectrum cannot comment on its source. *The Hard Problem is About Source*: an instrument reads green and *"cannot comment on the source"*; several paths create the same behavior; *"the answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* Fig 6 :33-35 says the sharper instrument grows from a reading that already fails to resolve, which is exactly the nHz result. Which observation first discriminates is a `gap`.
5. **Non-detection is not absence.** Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* A limit in one band is not a statement about all bands.
6. **Agreement is not a verdict.** Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Prestige can fund the check and cannot settle it.

## 4. What it explicitly does not say

- Not that the observed nHz background is primordial. That is a source attribution without a discriminating reading.
- Not a spectral index, an amplitude, a source mass or a phase-transition temperature. Those are numbers the ground must return.
- Not which instrument will close the `gap`. That is owed, not discovered.
- Not a derivation of one source model from another, and no new primitive.
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by an observation this derivation did not make.
- Not that the four PTA results are four independent witnesses. They are independent only to the degree that they do not draw on one data product and pipeline.

## 5. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating observation, not a further argument. The relevant questions:

- Does anisotropy mapping resolve individual hotspots, or do the apparent hotspots track regions with sparse pulsar coverage? SciAm reports both possibilities and the need for more data.
- Does the spectral form across nHz, mHz and Hz bands follow one power law, a broken law, a bump or a plateau? See Christensen's review for the band-by-band framing.
- Do non-Gaussianity and polarization states differ between candidate sources? Romano and Cornish require searches to allow them.
- Does a counterpart observation in photons or neutrinos accompany any resolved source? That would be an import with a source gate and a shadow test.

Standing falsifiers from `01` section 5: per-slot signature vanishing in every bin; indistinguishable statistical properties for two candidates at all resolutions; a correlation that moves with the analysis pipeline; agreement sourced from one pipeline; an existing discriminator that names the source; a band limit converted into global absence by argument.

## 6. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable in principle.** The source-discriminating instrument is owed. The six-term set makes the choice visible and does not make it neutral: the categories of "astrophysical" and "cosmological" are a visible guess at how to compress the residual. Another instrument could split the background differently. The answer stops at the limit of what the available instruments distinguish and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. *"Indiscernibility is not identity."*

## 7. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not name the source of the gravitational-wave background, and it does not claim the observed nanohertz signal is primordial. It says that a signature must be read as a per-frequency-slot deviation of the background's form, because a broadband average is how a band-local feature vanishes; that astrophysical and cosmological sources form a union of overlapping conditions rather than a list of rival answers; that over a shared atom set those sources are isomers, same stochastic field arranged differently, while as currently written they are a functional group whose member is not yet selected; and that the framework returns the shape of the discriminator, not the name of the source. An instrument that reads a spectrum cannot comment on its source, so a source-discriminating observation is owed and the name stays open until the ground pays. What the package offers is that shape, falsifiers written in advance, and the instrument condition. The source stays unreached rather than silent.
