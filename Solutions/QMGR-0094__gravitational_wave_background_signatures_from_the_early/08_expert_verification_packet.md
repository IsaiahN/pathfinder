**What this is:** a claim in a form a gravitational-wave researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the source name and the discriminating instrument. Not `grounded`.** No measurement at a source-discriminating resolution is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result. ## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A signature of the early universe in the gravitational-wave background cannot be the bare existence of a background, because a background is detected and its source is not. The signature is a deviation of the background's spectral or statistical form, indexed by frequency bin or sky pixel, that survives independent instruments and that a source-discriminating observation would settle. Astrophysical and cosmological sources form a union of overlapping conditions; over a shared atom set they are isomers, same stochastic strain field arranged differently, while as currently written they are a functional group whose member is not selected. The framework returns the shape of the discriminator, not the name of the source. The instrument that would name the source is owed, not discovered.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 : framework internal and observation facing: per-slot residual

- **Statement:** a GWB signature must be read per frequency or sky slot. A broadband amplitude and spectral index are a compressed description that can discard a band-local or angular feature.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Symbols table :43: *"One object, region or entity being tracked. R is measured per slot, never as a single global number."*
- **What to check:** does any proposed primordial signature in the literature survive only as a broadband fit, or does it have a defined frequency or angular structure? Romano and Cornish's likelihood-level treatment is the relevant comparison.
- **Refuter:** exhibit a signature visible only in a broadband aggregate and vanishing in every frequency bin and sky pixel at achievable resolution. Then C1 fails.

### C2 : union and isomer structure

- **Statement:** candidate sources form a union compound, not a partition. Over the shared atom set they are Fig 12 :117 isomers: *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* As currently written they are a functional group, Fig 12 :123: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* The union split is Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- **What to check:** are the distinguishing properties of SMBH, inflationary, phase-transition and cosmic-string backgrounds arrangements of one strain field (spectral slope, bump, anisotropy, non-Gaussianity, polarization), or do they require different observables altogether? Wikipedia's GWB article and the NANOGrav caveat are the public record.
- **Refuter:** show one mechanism that accounts for all bands and all statistical properties with no residual left (kills the union), or show two candidates statistically indistinguishable at every achievable resolution (kills the isomer reading and leaves the functional group).

### C3 : ground, independence, and no proof of absence

- **Statement:** the anchor for detection is the measured correlation, not a model or a consensus. Detectors can corroborate only if they do not share one evidence pool. A limit in one band is not a global absence result.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Fig 2 :31-32: *"Independence, between two frames, is how little they draw on the same evidence. Two people reading the same report are not two frames; two instruments pointed at the same thing are."* Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 6 :34: *"The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve."*
- **What to check:** does the reported correlation move under a change of pipeline, noise model or prior? Do the PTA results rest on independent instruments and analyses, or on one shared product? Is any band limit being presented as a statement about all bands?
- **Refuter:** a correlation that moves with the analysis frame; agreement that comes from one pipeline; or an argument that turns a band limit into global absence by reasoning alone.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not that the observed nHz background is primordial. Detection and source attribution have different anchors.
- Not a spectral index, amplitude, mass, temperature or source name.
- Not which observation will first discriminate the source. That is an owed instrument, recorded as `gap`.
- Not a theory of everything, not a final answer, and not a new primitive.
- Not `grounded`. This packet was not produced by a measurement.
- Not that four PTA teams are four independent witnesses. Independence is a quantity to check, not a label to assume.

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating observation, not a further argument.

1. Anisotropy mapping: do hotspots resolve into individual sources, or do they track sparse pulsar coverage? SciAm reports the latter as a live concern.
2. Multi-band spectral shape: one power law across nHz, mHz and Hz, or a broken law, bump or plateau?
3. Non-Gaussianity and polarization statistics: do they differ between candidate sources as Romano and Cornish's search space requires?
4. A multi-messenger counterpart: an import with an explicit source gate and shadow test.

If any of these names the source, the `gap` closes and the packet's claim is narrowed accordingly.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19, :22; Fig 9 :54; Fig 12 :117, :123; Fig 2 :29, :31-32; Fig 8 :39. Eight short passages.
2. Check C2 against the candidate source models: is the isomer reading over a shared atom set fair, or are the observables genuinely different in kind?
3. Name one observation that would discriminate an SMBH-binary background from a primordial one at current or near-term resolution, or say why none exists.
4. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
