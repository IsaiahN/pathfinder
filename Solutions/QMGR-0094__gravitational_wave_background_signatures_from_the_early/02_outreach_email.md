**Subject:** One checkable claim about the gravitational-wave background, before any theory

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read a framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with anything. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`specified` on the per-domain claims, `general` on the structural shape, `gap` on the source name):** a signature of the early universe in the gravitational-wave background cannot be the bare existence of a background, because a background is detected and its source is not. The signature has to be a deviation of the background's spectral or statistical form that is indexed by frequency or sky slot, that survives independent instruments, and that a source-discriminating observation would settle. The source, whether supermassive black-hole binaries, inflation, a phase transition or cosmic strings, stays open. The figures return a shape, not a name.
>
> **Why the slots matter:** aggregating a stochastic signal into one amplitude and one spectral index is how a feature living in one band or one angular pattern disappears into an average. Fig 1 :19 says the residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated. Here the slot is a frequency bin or a sky pixel.
>
> **Why this is a union:** astrophysical and cosmological sources can both populate the nanohertz band, with different statistical properties. A case in two conditions is not a harder case of either, so there is no single "the GWB signature" to average toward.
>
> **What would refute the shape claim, written before I sent this:**
>
> 1. a signature that is visible only in a broadband average and vanishes in every frequency bin and every sky pixel at achievable resolution;
> 2. two candidate sources whose statistical properties are indistinguishable at every achievable resolution;
> 3. a reported correlation that moves when the analysis pipeline, noise model or prior changes;
> 4. apparent agreement among detectors that comes from one shared data product and one pipeline rather than from instruments pointed at the same ground;
> 5. a source-discriminating measurement already in hand that names the source (this would close the gap, and I would withdraw my claim to it);
> 6. a non-detection in one band turned into a claim of global absence by argument alone.
>
> I wrote these down before sending. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Figure text extract with line numbers (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Verification packet with the checkable sub-claims: `08_expert_verification_packet.md`

>
