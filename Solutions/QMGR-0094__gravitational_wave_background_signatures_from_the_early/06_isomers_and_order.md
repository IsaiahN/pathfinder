# Isomers, union structure and detector order in the GWB question

**Mode: `specified` on the structural claims, `general` on the bond and order rules, `gap` on which source occupies a slot.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 7).

## RESIDUAL

The observed nanohertz background is registered; its source is not. The question owes three derivations: (1) is the candidate set a partition or a union; (2) are candidate sources isomers or a functional group under Fig 12; (3) in what order do instruments and refutations have to arrive for a signature to be settled.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held in the detector frame: a stochastic strain field, frequency and sky as slots, an angular correlation curve (Hellings and Downs), a spectral form, and the statistical descriptors anisotropy, non-Gaussianity and polarization. Bonds held: the seven plus negation; the relevant ones here are `+` (coexistence of sources), `⋛` (comparison of quantities), `→` (sequence of observations) and `¬` (refusal of a source attribution). No new primitive is available without import. Candidate sources live in a different closure and enter only as provenanced imports with a shadow test.

## Q1 : Partition or union?

Fig 9 :4-5: **"First: is there a fact here? Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."**

Fig 9 :54: **"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."**

Fig 9 :57: **"What remains is smaller and it is also categorically different: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either."**

The background can be produced by astrophysical populations (SMBH binaries, unresolved compact binaries) and by cosmological processes (inflation, phase transitions, cosmic strings, preheating). These conditions overlap in the same band. NANOGrav reports a signal *"consistent with astrophysical expectations ... although more exotic cosmological and astrophysical sources cannot be excluded."* Wikipedia divides GWB sources into cosmological and astrophysical categories and says each produces *"a background with different statistical properties."*

**Result:** union, not partition. A single lever such as fitting a power law clears the region where one condition holds and leaves the intersections standing (Fig 9 :55-58). The correct move is unbundling by statistical property, not a longer search for one answer. **Falsifier:** exhibit a single mechanism that accounts for all bands and all statistical properties with no residual left. Then the union collapses to a partition and this result is withdrawn.

## Q2 : Are the sources isomers?

Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Fig 12 :94: **"The arrangement is the substance."**

Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**

Romano and Cornish require a unified search that allows *"anisotropy, non-Gaussianity, and non-standard polarization states"* across ground, space and PTA instruments. Those are different arrangements of the same strain field, and the different source classes predict different mixtures of them. Over the shared atom set, the sources are isomers: same underlying stochastic field, different bond, different substance. The difference is decisive in behaviour and invisible in a broadband formula, which is precisely why a fitted amplitude and index cannot name the source.

**But the strong claim has a condition.** It holds when both candidates are written over the full shared atom set. Current practice writes an SMBH background and a primordial background with different atoms and assumptions, so as written they share only the functional group. Fig 12 :123: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."** A stochastic background in a band fixes the kind; the spectral slope, bump, anisotropy, non-Gaussianity or polarization fixes the member.

**Result:** isomer over a fixed shared atom set (`specified`), functional group as currently written (`specified`), no name for the actual source (`gap`). **Falsifier, written before the run:** if two candidates produce statistically indistinguishable backgrounds at every achievable resolution, they are one term under Fig 12 and the isomer reading fails.

## Q3 : What is the order of the atom set itself?

Fig 13 :74: **"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."**

Fig 13 :75: **"Two states never bond directly. It is not space-time but space, a structure, time; and two structures join only through a state."**

Fig 13 :20: **"ordering, so more and less can be said"**

Fig 13 :22: **"a difference that can be spent"**

A first pass wanted to identify Fig 13's "spectrum" with the measured Ω_gw(f). That was a strain. Fig 13's spectrum is a primitive structure, an ordering that permits more and less to be said. The measured GWB spectrum is a composition built over the held atoms: energy (a state) read through an ordering (spectrum) and a spatial or temporal gradient, then registered by an instrument. Re-census assigns the primitive to the six-term set and the measurement to the composition. A second temptation, reading the figure's "gradient" as the gravitational-wave strain h, is refused for the same reason: a binding from a physics frame needs provenance and a shadow test (Fig 8 :50-52).

**Result:** no new primitive is required for the shape claim; the observed spectrum is a composition over the six, not a seventh term. **Falsifier:** derive the GWB spectrum from the other five primitives alone with no instrument or import, or exhibit a seventh primitive that the six cannot compose. Either would break the reading.

## Q4 : In what order must instruments and refutations arrive?

Fig 3 :20: **"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."**

Fig 3 :27: **"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."**

Fig 6 :33: **"Nobody specified a telescope from an account of Mars. A wandering point in the sky was already a reading, and every sharper instrument was built on the residual the last one produced."**

Fig 6 :34: **"The question is not whether a sensor could exist. It is whether anything, at any resolution, is already returning something that fails to resolve."**

The dependency order for this question:

1. **Perception.** A correlated signal registers in PTA data. This has happened; the correlation is the anchor (Fig 2 :29).
2. **Vocabulary.** Terms for the background's affordances exist: spectral index, Hellings and Downs curve, anisotropy, non-Gaussianity, polarization. This link has an instrument in Romano and Cornish's likelihood-level treatment.
3. **Objective.** What would count as progress: a source-discriminating deviation, not another detection. This is the link that currently breaks, because the discriminating observable is not yet measured (Fig 3 :27).
4. **Planning.** Which band and which instrument: longer PTA baselines for anisotropy, multi-band spectral coverage, LISA for the millihertz gap.
5. **Learn and carry.** Record the per-instrument readings so the pooling of data (IPTA) does not erase witness independence (Fig 2 :31-32).

Refusal comes before minting. Formula step 2 sorts a gap: REFUTED means a term held is false, and what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation. So a claim that a particular source is present must be refused before a new source term is minted. Operators table :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate."*

**Falsifier:** show that a source attribution can be settled by fitting a broader model to the same data without a new observation. That would dissolve the instrument condition and move the objective link below the break.

## Q5 : What does the round trip preserve?

Fig 4 :30: **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."**

Fig 12 :104: **"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a fact about a level and never about the world"**

A detected background, once settled in D1, becomes an atom for the next level: it can be used as an operand for source comparisons. But the compression from a full strain field to an amplitude and index is lossy. Bringing the compressed description back to a source drops anisotropy, non-Gaussianity, polarization, band-to-band coherence and individual hotspots. Romano and Cornish's requirement to allow those degrees of freedom is the evidence that the gap is real. R_T is recorded qualitatively in `11_audit.md`; it is not zero, and treating it as zero would be the dead-reckoning error Fig 4 :30 names.

**Falsifier:** a compression that preserves the source-discriminating information exactly. Then R_T is zero for that discriminator and this result is narrowed.

## CONCLUSION

The candidate sources form a union, not a partition. Over a shared atom set they are isomers; as currently written they are a functional group whose member is not yet selected. No new primitive is needed to state the shape. The chain breaks at the objective link, because no current observation discriminates the source; instruments grow from the nHz reading that already fails to resolve, and refusal of a prematurely named source precedes any mint. Two strains remain live and are not smoothed over: independence versus pooling (S3) and isomer versus functional group (S4). The falsifiers above are written before the run and are repeated in `08_expert_verification_packet.md` and `09_watson_answer.md`.
