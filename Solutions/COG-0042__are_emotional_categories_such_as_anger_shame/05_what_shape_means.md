## 1. A shape is a constraint, not a filler

The canonical rule: the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)."* Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*

For COG-0042 the shape is **not** a list of universal emotions and **not** a claim that all emotion is culturally made. It is:

> A shared affective capacity exists, and what varies across societies is the bonding of that capacity to situations, causes, selves and social relations, plus the value and frequency of the displays that result. Capacity is shared; category and deployment are locally bonded. Neither half is a name for a specific emotion word.

That is a constraint on any acceptable answer. An answer that says "anger is universal" has skipped the bonding layer. An answer that says "emotion is entirely constructed" has skipped the capacity layer. Both fail the shape.

From *The Library of Babel*, opened this session: *"you can cut your search short if the set of books returned is small and descriptive enough that it provides a general shape of the answer that satisfies your question."* The narrowed set here is the four-row verdict table in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, not a single sentence.

## 2. How the shape was obtained

- The essay's method is proximity and recursion: *"With each new term gained, you narrow the space and deepen your vocabulary."* Applied here, the initial vocabulary was "shared versus variable"; the new terms acquired were capacity, bond, isomer, anchor, convention and per-slot residual. Each narrowed the search.
- Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."* The residual was described in effect terms before any world field was searched. Otherwise any of psychology, linguistics or neuroscience would have seemed to fit.
- Fig 6 :30: *"The description travels. Fails by being sharpened into terms only we can read. Cast it in effect terms."* So the outreach in `02_outreach_email.md` does not use the framework's glyphs in stage 1.
- *The Library of Babel* on questions that refuse to resolve: *"Perhaps your problem was a bundle or conflation of several problems masquerading as one? You will need to search all of those problems individually to find their answers and then combine them in a satisfactory way."* This is the essay-level statement of Fig 9 :4-5 and it is exactly the move made in F1.

## 3. What would license a name

Only two things, both outside the shape itself:

1. **The ground settles it.** Formula step 5: *"the ground settles it. Verified only by what pays. A gate is not the ground."* For this question that means structured first-person report across societies, triangulated with behaviour in described contexts (Fig 2 :36-38).
2. **A provenanced import from a frame whose closure differs** (Fig 6 :23, Fig 8 :50-52), with a source gate and a shadow test.

So naming a specific universal anger module, or a specific set of affective primitives, is a binding from a world frame onto an atom. It is legitimate only with provenance and a shadow test, and it is not a derivation from the figures.

## 4. Why the recognition instrument cannot name the source

*The Hard Problem is About Source*, opened this session, gives the mechanism in a different field: *"If several paths create the same behavior, then behavior is a projection with lossy information (it cannot define consciousness in full)."* The colour example shows one reading with many possible sources; an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source."* The resolution was not an argument but a better instrument: *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."*

Applied here: a forced-choice "this face is angry" reading is one projection with several possible sources. The source might be a shared discrete category, a shared valence-arousal position, a local concept supplied by the task, or a situated-action inference. The reading does not discriminate among them. Gendron 2014 (Emotion) is the empirical instance: unconstrained sorting in a remote culture does not reproduce the forced-choice pattern. By the essay's rule, the instrument that cannot distinguish these cases *"demonstrates its own inadequacy"* on exactly the axis this question asks about.

This is also why F2 refuses to let facial coding stand in for first-person report. The essay's warning is about projection and source; the figures' version is Fig 2 :37-38, *"instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."*

## 5. Prestige is not action, and agreement is not a ground

From the exemplar's mapping of the Recursive Self-Improvement Trap (local file, phrase not re-opened this session): prestige can fund an action and give it a budget to be tried, but it cannot settle an action; only the ground can settle it. A campaign that gathers famous emotion researchers to agree with the shape claim is the named collapse, not corroboration. The legitimate ask gives the claim a budget to be tried and lets a ground vote.

Fig 2 :41 adds the friendlier half: *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."* In emotion research, agreement on what a word means is the product of a shared language. It only becomes a corruption when it is presented as a measurement of shared experience.

## 6. My own derivation is a simulation, not a ground reading

Same essay as section 5: LLM output can simulate an approximate answer, while the truly testable output would have to be derived from the real architecture or worked by hand. So `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures. It is not a test of the claim and must be labelled as such. It cannot settle anything it reports on.

## 7. Correction to my own framing

I initially wanted to write "the framework returns a shape, not a name, so we may not name any affective atoms at all." That is too strong. The canonical phrase governs a **missing** thing, a primitive not held in the library. It does not forbid naming atoms that a world frame already supplies, provided the binding is provenanced. What remains forbidden is claiming that the figures themselves deliver a universal list of anger, shame and grief, or claiming that an instrument reading identifies the source. Both claims are retracted here and recorded in the retraction log in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`.
