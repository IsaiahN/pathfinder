## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

The question presents a binary:

- **Option A (shared):** anger, shame and grief are categories with the same experiential content everywhere, so a category learned in one society maps one to one onto the same felt state in another.
- **Option B (variable):** the categories are carved differently, felt differently and deployed differently, so the mapping is many to one and partly incommensurable.

What each leads to. A leads to the prediction that a translated emotion word or a posed expression elicits the same state in any perceiver, so a difference is a measurement error. B leads to the prediction that categories are local constructions, so agreement on a label tells us about a convention, not about a shared inner state.

**The residual R** is the gap, per society and per category slot, between what a shared-category prediction projects and what that society's reports, concepts and practices return. R is not a single global number:

- Fig 1 :19 *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."*
- Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*

So a mean cross-cultural recognition rate, or a mean number of shared emotion words, is precisely the operation the figures forbid for steering. It throws away the slots where the signal lives.

**R by arity, symmetry and scale (required before any figure line is chosen):**

- **Arity:** at least two conditions must hold at once. There is a shared affective capacity (pleasure and displeasure, arousal) and a local conceptual and normative layer that carves it. R is therefore a **union compound** under Fig 9 :54-55, not one hard question. Fig 9 :4-5 is decisive: *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* The universalist rule works well on the capacity subset, the relativist rule works well on the categorization subset. Neither shrinks under more effort on the other's subset.
- **Symmetry:** asymmetric. The shared capacity is a necessary but not sufficient condition for a particular category. Several different local arrangements can produce the same outward reading (anger from shame in one society, not in another), so the mapping is many to one. This is a metamerism-shaped residual: one reading, multiple possible sources.
- **Scale:** individual experience, face-to-face interaction, society-wide norms, language-family semantics. The same loop runs at each level; only the actors, currency and anchor change (Fig 4 :19-20).

Strain test applied at the outset: a single figure line that only fits by ignoring one of these strata governs a different quantity, so it is sent back to census rather than used as a verdict. This happens twice below (F4 and F6) and is recorded, not smoothed.

**Anchor test, quoted before any conclusion.** Fig 2 :29-30: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Fig 2 :36: *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* Fig 2 :37-38: *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."*

This is the pivot. The question asks what people *experience*. By the figures' own rule the anchor is structured human judgment, not a facial-action coder or an fMRI map. Those are instruments. They can be wrong on the very axis at issue, the felt quality of anger or shame, and Fig 2 :37 says so in general form. Any verdict that substitutes a recognition rate for a first-person report has changed the anchor to a convenient instrument, which is panel 1 drift under another name.

**Order by dependency (Fig 3).** D1 settles before D2, because a category must attach to something felt. D2 settles before D3, because an expression is interpreted through a category. D4 runs last and cannot manufacture evidence: *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* (Fig 2 :35). Describe R before choosing a frame (Fig 9 :27-28), refuse before mint (Formula step 2), and never let D4's agreement substitute for D1 through D3 anchors.

**Partition or union.** The split lands on a union, not a partition (Fig 9 :54-58: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*). The compound is: shared capacity, locally bonded categories, locally valued expressions. It needs its own loop. It is not a harder version of either the universalist or the relativist question.

## 3. Findings

### F1 The binary is a union compound, and must be unbundled before it is searched

Fig 9 :4-5: *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* Fig 9 :54-55: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."* Fig 9 :57-58: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."*

The universalist rule fits D1. The relativist rule fits D2 and D3. Running the binary as one question is the blend failure, not a deep paradox. The answer is a structured both, with a separate verdict per domain and no averaging.

**Refuter:** show that one and the same mechanism governs capacity, category and expression at one level with no dependency break. If a single rule predicted all three subsets and its residual trended down as effort rose, the union verdict fails.

### F2 The anchor is first-person experience, not the recognition instrument

Quoted in section 1: Fig 2 :29-30, :36, :37-38. Applied here, a forced-choice label task, a face-action coder and a scanner are instruments for a question about other things; they are the right anchor only for questions constitutively about faces or BOLD signal. For a question about what anger *feels like*, they are axes the instrument does not measure. The world literature agrees in a different vocabulary: the experience of emotion cannot be read off behaviour or physiology, and *"To know what emotion feels like, it is necessary to ask people what they experience"* (Barrett, Mesquita, Ochsner and Gross, 2007, Annual Review of Psychology).

**Refuter:** exhibit a society in which first-person report is systematically unavailable or meaningless for emotion, and where a non-report instrument is constitutively the thing asked about. Then Fig 2 :36 would not license the report anchor here.

### F3 Per-slot indexing forbids the single cross-society average

Fig 1 :19 and :22 quoted in section 0. A study that reports one recognition percentage across cultures, or one count of shared lexical labels across languages, has performed the forbidden aggregation. The live signal is which slot (which category, which society, which context) breaks. This is also why a meta-analysis that pools effect sizes cannot by itself answer the question, even when it is well run.

**Refuter:** show that the between-society residual on each category slot is zero after correcting for method, so the aggregate and the per-slot reading coincide. Then indexing adds nothing.

### F4 Agreement about a label is a convention, not a verdict about experience

Fig 2 :39 *"Correlation has valence."* Fig 2 :40 *"disqualifying for a verdict: agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* and Fig 2 :41 *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."*

This is the sharpest figure-grounded point in the whole question. When speakers of two languages both say the word translated as *anger*, their agreement is agreement about a shared meaning. That is a convention working exactly as designed. It is not two independent measurements of one inner state. Treating it as corroboration of shared experience is the panel 2 collapse: two frames, one evidence pool, agreeing because the pool is talking. Conversely, a failed translation is not by itself evidence of different experience; it may be a convention with a different boundary.

**Refuter:** exhibit a case where two communities with demonstrably separate evidence pools arrive at the same category boundaries and the same first-person reports without shared words or contact. That would be convergent derivation, maximum independence, and would count as corroboration rather than convention.

### F5 Same atoms, different bond: categories are isomers over a shared affective substrate

Fig 12 :46-47: *"A bond is the typed relation between two operands. There are seven, they are not interchangeable, and which one holds is not recoverable from the operands."* Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."*

If the shared atoms are valence and arousal, then a category is the *bonding* of core affect to a situation, a cause, a self and a social relation. Different societies can bond the same atoms differently. Anger-bonded-to-shame is a different substance from shame-bonded-to-self, and which one a culture builds is invisible in the ingredient list. Where two societies share the bond type but fill the slots with different atoms, the weaker **functional-group** reading applies: the group says what kind, the qualifier says which member.

**Strain named, not hidden:** Fig 12's atoms and bonds are defined over the framework's own symbol set, and Fig 13 :74-75 governs physical states and structures. Mapping valence to spectrum (ordering, more and less) is a natural fit; mapping arousal to gradient (a difference that can be spent) is a cross-level analogy with a real round-trip gap. Fig 4 :30 requires the gap to be measured, not assumed zero: *"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."* The isomer verdict is therefore `specified` and the cross-level mapping is a strain recorded in `11_audit.md`.

**Refuter:** show that category differences disappear once valence and arousal are held fixed, or that a category can be fully specified by its atoms with no bond information. Either kills the isomer claim and leaves only the shared-substrate verdict.

### F6 Naming a category does not ground it, and the six are not the words

Fig 10 :18 *"a declared meaning is not a grounded one"* and Fig 10 :49 *"Declaring a meaning does not ground it."* Wierzbicka (1999, Cambridge University Press, chapter 7) calls the English categories false universals when they are absolutized: *"false universals are a major obstacle in our search for true universals; and in searching for the latter we must, first of all, debunk the former."*

So the shared core does not license a list of universal English emotion words. Fig 13 :35-36: *"Space, time and energy are not three readings of one quantity. Neither are spectrum, gradient and network."* By the same independence condition, six shared primitives are not six names for one feeling, and a translated word is not a shared primitive. The framework returns a shape (a shared substrate with locally bonded categories), not a universal lexicon.

**Strain named:** Fig 13's six-term set is a substrate claim about physical states and structures. Using it as the template for affective primitives is an analogy across levels, permitted by the framework's domain-agnostic recursion but carrying a round-trip gap. It is not used here to name which affective atoms exist; that is owed to the ground. The claim used here is only that distinct frames are not redundant, which the figure states generally.

**Refuter:** produce a language in which the proposed shared atoms are not distinguishable at all, even in first-person report, and where behaviour and context cannot recover them. That would break the shared-substrate verdict, not just the English lexicon.

### F7 Expression is the same atom under a different social bond

Fig 11 :49 *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* Fig 11 :50 *"The same actor is a benefactor to one residual and a malfactor to another, so mutualism and parasitism are relations rather than kinds."*

Applied here, a scowl, a shame display or a grief display is not intrinsically angry or shameful or grief-stricken. Its social meaning is a relation to a local norm. The same display can be a respectful restraint in one setting and a coldness in another. Fig 4 :19-20 says the kernel is invariant and *"Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* The world data fit: Cowen and colleagues (2021, Nature) find context-expression associations *"70% preserved across 12 world regions"* while regions *"varied in how frequently different facial expressions were produced as a function of which contexts were most salient."* The mapping is preserved; the deployment varies. That is exactly the union verdict, observed.

**Refuter:** show that a display has the same social meaning and the same frequency in every society after context is held fixed. Then D3 collapses into D1.

### F8 Why more of the same studies cannot settle it

Fig 8 :32: *"Closure is idempotent. Composing inside a closed set never leaves it, so no search, however long, adds an atom. This one is definitional: it is what closure means."* Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 7 :35: *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*

Adding a hundred forced-choice recognition studies inside the same method adds vocabulary and no anchor. The way forward is a different room: unconstrained sorting, daily diary, colexification across languages, neural pattern analysis, each ranged separately against first-person report. Fig 9 :60: *"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."*

**Refuter:** show that a single new instrument, by itself and without a change of method family, resolves all three domains. Then the multi-room prescription is unnecessary.

## 5. Pre-registered refuter

Written before the conclusion, per the exemplar's discipline: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument.

The union verdict is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A single mechanism is shown to govern capacity, category and expression at one level, with a residual that trends down as effort increases across all three subsets.
2. The between-society residual on every category slot is zero after method correction, so Fig 1 :19 indexing adds nothing.
3. Some society is found in which first-person report is constitutively the wrong anchor for emotion and a non-report instrument is the thing asked about.
4. Two communities with no shared words, no contact and demonstrably separate evidence pools converge on the same category boundaries and the same reports, which would upgrade agreement from convention to corroboration and change the D2 verdict.
5. Category differences vanish with valence and arousal held fixed, which kills F5 and leaves only a shared-substrate answer.
6. A display has identical meaning and identical frequency in every society with context held fixed, which kills F7.

If any fires, the relevant row is withdrawn, not softened. Until one is run, nothing here is `grounded`.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Refuse the binary in its present form. Return a union verdict with per-domain rows, not an average (F1, F3).
2. State the anchor before the method: structured first-person report, with instruments ranged against it rather than substituted for it (F2).
3. Treat translated labels as conventions until provenance shows independent convergence (F4).
4. Describe the shape (shared substrate, locally bonded categories, locally valued displays), not a universal list of anger, shame and grief (F5, F6).
5. Field the next study as a selection between low-overlap rooms, not as another instance of the same forced-choice room (F8).
6. Offer the refuter first and the framework only on request (Fig 10 :52).
