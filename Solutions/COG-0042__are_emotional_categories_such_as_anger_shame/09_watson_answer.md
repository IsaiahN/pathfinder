## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the binary: anger, shame and grief are experienced similarly across societies (Option A), or they vary in important ways (Option B). What each leads to: A predicts a one-to-one mapping from a category word or a posed display to the same felt state everywhere, so a difference is method error; B predicts local construction, so agreement on a label tells us about a convention rather than a shared inner state. A third form is licensed by the figures before either is chosen: the residual may be a union compound that has to be unbundled, with a separate verdict per stratum. The anchor is stated in advance: because the question is about what people experience, first-person report settles it and other measures are instruments ranged against it.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor, partition or union, and order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** Gap between a shared-category prediction and what a society's reports, concepts and practices return, indexed per society and per category slot. Arity: at least two conditions, a shared capacity and a local bonding layer, so a **union compound** under Fig 9 :54-55. Symmetry: asymmetric, since the capacity is necessary but not sufficient and the mapping is many to one. Scale: individual, interactional, societal, language-family. Fig 9 :4-5 rules out treating it as one hard question: each rule works well on a different subset.
- **Domain 1, affective substrate.** Actors: bodies, value circuits, caregivers and infants. Currency: hedonic valence and arousal. Anchor: the capacity itself, reported in first person and stable under relabelling. Question type: candidate partition over societies. Verdict: shared.
- **Domain 2, categories and language.** Actors: speakers, translators, concepts and colexification networks. Currency: category boundaries and prototypes. Anchor: first-person report about what people feel (Fig 2 :36-38). Question type: union with D1 and D3. Verdict: varies importantly.
- **Domain 3, expression and norms.** Actors: interactants, display rules, honour and harmony norms. Currency: expression frequency and social function. Anchor: behaviour in a described context, triangulated with report. Question type: union with D2. Verdict: varies importantly, with a preserved context mapping.
- **Domain 4, verification and outreach.** Actors: expert checkers and instrument builders. Currency: agreement and residual. Anchor: the ground, never agreement. In this simulated run every domain draws on one evidence pool, so D4 independence is zero and its agreement is not a witness.
- **Order by dependency.** Settle D1 before D2 because a category binds affect; settle D2 before D3 because a display is a category made public. Describe the residual before choosing a world frame (Fig 9 :27-28), refuse before mint (Formula step 2), and never let a filter hand you a verdict (Fig 9 :60).

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The binary fails. The figure-governed answer is a union with four separate rows: a shared affective capacity (triangulated); locally bonded categories that vary importantly (triangulated); displays with a preserved context mapping and variable local meaning and frequency (single-source at figure level, supported by one large world dataset); and translated-label agreement that is a convention rather than corroboration (single-source at figure level). The anchor is structured first-person report, with behavioural and neural instruments ranged against it. The framework returns the shape (shared capacity, locally bonded categories, locally valued displays) and no universal lexicon. Every row remains `candidate` because this run took no ground reading and the derivation is simulated.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The packet states the anchor, the mode and six refuters before the conclusion, so an error can be found rather than invisible. The avoidable cost is treating a convention as a verdict (Fig 2 :40) or averaging away a live slot (Fig 1 :22), which would send the next study to the wrong instrument. Fig 2 :42 says "Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong." The way to buy it is low-overlap rooms, not a larger agreeing sample.
- **undoable:** yes. The unbundling and the per-domain verdicts are compositional claims. No primitive is withdrawn if one is wrong; the affected row is re-censused and the record keeps both the claim and its refusal. A sent packet can be superseded by a corrected one.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A per-society slot test with zero residual after method correction would break F3 and the union verdict; a synthesis test in which category differences vanish with valence and arousal fixed would kill the isomer claim; a society in which first-person report is constitutively the wrong anchor would break C3; convergence of two no-contact communities on the same categories would upgrade agreement from convention to corroboration and change D2. Prestige and sample size are not such instruments.
