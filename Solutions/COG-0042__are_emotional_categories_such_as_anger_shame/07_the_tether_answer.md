# The Tether's answer on anger, shame and grief across societies

**Mode: `specified` on the domain split and on each per-domain verdict; `general` on the union structure. Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` §6), not a ground reading. No measurement was taken.

## 1. The residual it answers

The question asks for one binary: shared or variable. The residual is the gap, per society and per category slot, between a shared-category prediction and what that society's reports, concepts and practices return. Fig 1 :19: *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 9 :4-5: *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."*

The universalist rule works well on the capacity subset and the relativist rule works well on the categorization subset. Neither shrinks under more effort on the other's subset. So the residual is a union compound (Fig 9 :54-55), not one hard question, and the binary is a presentation artifact rather than a property of the world.

## 2. The shape, with a verdict per domain

1. **A shared affective capacity, not a shared lexicon.** Pleasure, displeasure and arousal are present across societies and early in life; the capacity is a real shared atom. Fig 4 :19-20: *"The kernel is the part that does not change with size: the same loop and the same rule for naming what worked, whether one agent is running it or a thousand. Only the actors, the currency and the anchor change."* What changes is the cast, not the loop. Verdict: `triangulated`, shared.
2. **Categories are locally bonded, so they vary importantly.** Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* A category is core affect bonded to a situation, cause, self and audience. Different societies build different bonds over the same atoms. Where they share the bond type but fill different slots, Fig 12 :124 applies: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."* Verdict: `triangulated`, varies.
3. **Agreement on a label is a convention, not a measurement of shared feeling.** Fig 2 :39 *"Correlation has valence."* Fig 2 :41 *"efficient for a convention: agreement about a shared meaning is the product, not a corruption of it."* Mutual intelligibility is a feature of language. It is not two independent instruments measuring one inner state. Verdict: `single-source` at figure level (Fig 2 :40), with direct world corroboration from the sorting studies.
4. **Displays keep a preserved mapping and vary in deployment.** Fig 11 :49 *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."* The same display has different social meaning under different norms. Cowen 2021 finds context-expression associations 70 percent preserved across 12 world regions while frequency varies by region, which is the same two-level result observed. Verdict: `single-source` at figure level, corroborated by one large world dataset.
5. **The anchor for the whole question is first-person report.** Fig 2 :36 *"An anchor is legitimate when the question is constitutively about it."* Fig 2 :37-38: *"Human judgment is the right anchor for is this a good answer for a person, because the question is about people. It is only an instrument for is this code correct, and instruments can be wrong on axes they do not measure."* A recognition rate or a scanner is ranged against the report, never substituted for it.

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not "emotions are universal" in the sense of a shared English lexicon. The framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"*).
- Not "emotion is entirely cultural." There is a shared capacity that the categories bind to.
- Not that a posed face, a scanner or a forced-choice label reads off what a person feels. Those are instruments on axes they may not measure.
- Not that cross-cultural difference is a defect or that cross-cultural agreement is proof. Both are observations with different figure statuses.
- Not `grounded`. This simulated run did not take the ground reading.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

A source-discriminating design, not a further argument. The essay on source shows the pattern: an instrument that reads green *"cannot comment on the source"*, and *"The answer is unreached until a better instrument exists."* Here the discriminating tests are:

- **Slot test:** per-society, per-category first-person reports with method correction. Does the between-society residual on any slot remain nonzero? If none does, F3 fires.
- **Synthesis test:** hold valence and arousal fixed and vary the situation and audience. Do category reports still differ? If not, the isomer claim fails (F5).
- **Anchor test:** compare unconstrained sorting, constrained forced choice and first-person report in the same sample. If constrained tasks manufacture the "universal" pattern that free tasks do not, the instrument is wrong on the measured axis (F2).
- **Convention test:** find two communities with no contact and no shared words whose category boundaries and reports converge. That would upgrade agreement from convention to corroboration (F4).
- **Display test:** hold context fixed and compare display meaning and frequency across societies. If both are invariant, F7 fires.

Standing falsifiers from `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §5 apply to all of the above.

## 5. The honest stop

**Unreached, not unreachable.** The first-person data needed to move any row to `grounded` exist in principle and partly in the literature, but they are not synthesized in this run. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* What is recorded is the shape, the per-domain verdicts, the strains and the refuters, so a later reader can locate the error rather than only feel it. Indiscernibility is not identity.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether refuses the "shared or variable" binary. It says there is a shared affective capacity, pleasure, displeasure and arousal, and that societies differ in how they bond that capacity to situations, causes, selves and audiences, so anger, shame and grief are better read as locally bonded arrangements over shared atoms than as either universal modules or pure inventions. Agreement on a translated word is a convention about shared meaning, not two independent measurements of one feeling, and a facial expression or a scanner is an instrument that can be wrong on the very axis this question asks about, what the feeling is like for the person. The right anchor is therefore structured first-person report, with other measures ranged against it. What this offers is the shape any answer must have, the refuters written in advance, and the tests that would close it. Every row stays `candidate` until a ground settles it.
