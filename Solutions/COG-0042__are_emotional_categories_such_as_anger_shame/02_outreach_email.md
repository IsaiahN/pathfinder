**Subject:** One claim about cross-cultural emotion to check, and a refuter written in advance

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one narrow claim I would like you to check against what you already know. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the claim does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The claim (`specified`, not `grounded`):** the question "are anger, shame and grief shared or variable?" is not one binary. It is a union of at least three things at different levels: a shared capacity for pleasure, displeasure and arousal; locally bonded categories that carve that capacity into anger, shame and grief; and locally valued displays whose social meaning depends on context. The shared part and the varying part do not live at the same level, so a single "shared" or "variable" answer loses the signal. The framework's rule here is that disagreements which do not shrink with effort, where each rule works well on a different subset, are several well-formed questions rather than one hard one.
>
> **The anchor I am using:** because the question is about what people experience, the settling thing is structured first-person report, with facial coding, behaviour and neural data treated as instruments ranged against it rather than as substitutes for it. Agreement on a translated label is a convention about shared meaning, not two independent measurements of one inner state.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that there is a universal English lexicon of anger, shame and grief; that a recognition percentage measures what a person feels; that a scowl means anger everywhere; or that this derivation is a ground reading. It is a simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, and it cannot settle the question it reports on.
>
> **What would refute it, written before this message:**
> 1. One mechanism is shown to govern capacity, category and expression at one level, with the residual trending down across all three.
> 2. The between-society residual on every category slot is zero after method correction, so separating the slots buys nothing.
> 3. Some society is found where first-person report is constitutively the wrong anchor for emotion.
> 4. Category differences vanish once valence and arousal are held fixed, or a display has the same meaning and frequency in every society with context held fixed.
>
> If any one holds, the relevant part of the claim is withdrawn. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the figures and the worked derivation are below. The figures, not my summary, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule (local copy): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The eight-step loop: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground. Write down what would refute your result before you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
