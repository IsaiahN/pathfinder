# Isomers, order and analogues for landscape trade-offs

**Mode: `specified` on the isomer claim, `general` on the order, not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures; see `05_what_shape_means.md` section 6.

## RESIDUAL

The question asks how trade-offs emerge "across landscapes". Two things are owed that the main audit does not settle: (1) what is the difference between two landscapes with the same amounts of each land use but different spatial arrangement, and (2) in what order must the derivation run so that a value judgment cannot enter before the measurement.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

Atoms held for this supporting derivation: the land-use classes actually present in the landscape (imported from the world and enumerated, not composed by the six-term substrate), the four service slots, and the seven bonds plus negation. No seventh substrate member is available without import. Bonds needed here: subtraction (a parcel in one use is absent from another), sequence (enumerate, then measure, then compare), comparison (which service is larger under an action), and negation (refuse the aggregate winner).

## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Composition versus configuration is the bond, not the ingredient list

- Fig 12 :5: "A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."
- Fig 12 :94: "The arrangement is the substance."
- The landscape-ecology source (opened 2026-11-23) distinguishes "composition" (number of patch types and relative abundance) from "structure", which is "determined by the composition, the configuration, and the proportion of different patches across the landscape".
- ⇒ Two landscapes with the same composition and different configuration have the same atoms and a different bond. By Fig 12 :117 "Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour" they are **isomers**.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** in a controlled comparison holding composition and total area fixed, a change in configuration produces no change in any of the four service slots or in any pairwise coupling. If that holds, the isomer claim fails and configuration is a mere parameter of the same substance.

### Q2 — Land sharing and land sparing are the clearest isomer pair

- The Conservation agriculture page (opened 2026-11-23, citing Green et al. 2005 and Fischer et al. 2014) describes wildlife-friendly farming, or land sharing, which "allows for the conservation of biodiversity while also allowing for production of agricultural products" by leaving scattered trees and patches inside farmed land, with "much lower yields, but also an increase in biodiversity given time". Land sparing instead maximises yield on farmed land and sets other land aside.
- Same land, same total area, same land-use classes, different bond (interspersed versus separated). This is Fig 12 :117 applied to a real management choice.
- **Strain, named:** if sparing introduces a land-use class that sharing does not (for example an intensive high-yield class), the atom sets differ and the pair is no longer isomeric; it becomes composition plus import under Fig 6 :23. The isomer claim is therefore conditional on a fixed atom set and is recorded as `specified`, not `general`. This strain stays live in `11_audit.md`.

### Q3 — Order of the loop

Fig 3 :20: "The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance." The dependency order for this question is:

1. **Enumerate the habitat** (Fig 11 :43 "The habitat is enumerated, never composed.") before choosing any model, so the actor list is read off the world.
2. **Index the residual per slot** (Fig 1 :19 "The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated.") before comparing slots.
3. **Measure each slot with its own instrument** (Fig 2 :29 on anchors) before comparing signs.
4. **Detect pairwise sign relations** per place and per year, using comparison, not a symmetric join.
5. **Run the cross-scale round trip** and measure the gap (Fig 4 :30) before extrapolating.
6. **Import a differing frame** for any slot the closure cannot hold (Fig 8 :53 "Residual first, frame second."), with provenance and a shadow test.
7. **Refuse before mint** where a value claim tries to assign a winner (Fig 13 :135 on naming; `THE_FORMULA.md` step 2 on refusal).
8. **Report the frontier**, not the scalar (Fig 9 :32 "you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself").

A step whose input never arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can (Fig 3 :22). If step 2 is skipped and services are aggregated, steps 3 to 8 read nothing. This is why the valuation question is last, not first.

### Q4 — Functional groups, catalysts and cycles

- **Functional group.** Fig 12 :123: "functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member." The four "regulating services" (carbon, water purification, flood control, disease regulation) form a functional group in the ecosystem-service classification; the qualifier names which member. Group membership does not make their units commensurable, which is F1.
- **Catalyst.** Fig 12 :119: "catalyst. A part that survives its own use." Pollinator habitat and soil biota are catalysts: they act on several services without being consumed by the service they support, and a management action that destroys them changes several slots at once. A spent part (a one-off fertilizer application) behaves differently across uses, and nothing in an ingredient list records which a term is.
- **Cycle.** Fig 12 :121: "cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction." Nutrient cycling and the return of organic matter under conservation agriculture are cycles; whether the loop is reinforcing (soil built, more water held, more carbon) or degrading (soil mined, more runoff, less carbon) depends on the sign. A chain model of "action to yield" cannot close this loop by construction.

### Q5 — Why no seventh service can be composed

Fig 13 :115: "The set is closed. The arrangements are not." Fig 13 :116: "Six members, and no amount of arranging produces a seventh". Here the analogous claim is about land-use classes and service slots: rearranging the four services and the existing land uses can produce unbounded configurations without producing a new service currency. A fifth service that is not a composition of the four (for example a cultural value incommensurable with all of them) would have to be imported with provenance, not derived by re-arranging the four. This is the structural reason the aggregate index cannot be fixed by more composing.

## CONCLUSION

Over a fixed atom set, land sharing and land sparing are isomers under Fig 12 :117: same land uses, different bond, different service bundle. The derivation must run in the order enumerate, index, measure, compare, check scale, import, refuse, report; an index inserted early makes every downstream step a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22). Nutrient and pollinator structures re-enter as catalyst, cycle and functional-group analogues, each named with its own falsifier. The isomer claim is conditional on fixed atoms, and that condition is a live strain, not a hidden one.
