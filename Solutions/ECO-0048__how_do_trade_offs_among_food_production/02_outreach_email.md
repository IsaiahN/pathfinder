**Subject:** A four-slot check for landscape trade-offs, with the failure conditions written first

> Hi [name],
>
> I have one specific output I would like you to check against what you already know about land-use trade-offs. I am not asking you to read the framework first, and I am not asking you to agree with it. If the output does not survive, there is nothing else to look at.
>
> **The output (`general` on the structure, `specified` on any named place, not `grounded`):** food production, carbon storage, water quality and biodiversity are four tracked slots, each in its own unit. The framework's rule is that a residual is indexed by slot, not aggregated. So a landscape "trade-off" is not one number: it is a sign relation between two slots under one action, at one place, at one scale. Three things follow, and each is checkable.
>
> 1. The sign is relative to the slot you are tracking. The same action (fertilizer, set-aside, afforestation) is a benefactor to one service and a malfactor to another. There is no standing list of good land uses.
> 2. A parcel is exclusive: land in crop at a moment is not land in perennial grass at that moment. On one parcel the bond is subtraction, not conjunction.
> 3. The sign can change with scale. A food versus water-quality trade-off measured in a field need not appear at the watershed, and reducing it locally may not reduce it at the watershed.
>
> **What I am not claiming:** that there is a single best aggregate landscape, that a monetary index settles the comparison, or that the framework names which land use wins. It returns a shape and a set of failure conditions, not a winner. Six different valuation methods exist and none of them is an instrument that does not move when the analyst's weights move.
>
> **What would refute the output.** I wrote these down before sending this:
> 1. A single non-updating measure preserves every decision-relevant slot signal and is settled by the ground.
> 2. Some land use or action is a benefactor to all four services at every measured place, scale and season.
> 3. With composition held fixed, landscape configuration changes no service and no coupling.
> 4. A scaling rule preserves both sign and magnitude of every pairwise relation from field to watershed.
> 5. All measured couplings in a landscape are first order, with no third-order cascade.
>
> If any one holds, I retract the corresponding claim. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figures, not my summary of them, are the authority.
> - Verbatim figure text with line numbers (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical figures README (local): `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - The open-access study that most directly bears on this residual: Qiu et al. 2018, Environmental Research Letters, https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-9326/aabb87
>
> **If you want to use the machinery:** the formula is in the figures folder. Run it on a landscape you know and can check against measurements. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> Thanks,
> [name]
