# Outreach draft — what would count as a failure of a favoured origin-of-life model?

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send.

**Hard precondition:** send this only after the pre-registered refuter in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` §4 has been checked by someone outside this folder. The offer collapses on contact if the refuter is missing.

---

**Subject:** What finding would make you drop a model you currently favour?

> Hi [name],
>
> I am asking one narrow question before showing you anything else, and I am not asking you to agree with a theory or to trust a framework. If the answer does not survive your check, there is nothing further to look at.
>
> **The question, stated as an effect.** Take a leading origin-of-life model you know well. What observation, instrument reading, or measurement of the rock record would make you stop using it, or cut it back substantially? I am interested in the properties such a finding would have, not in naming a specific result.
>
> **The shape I get, and its mode (`specified`, not `grounded`).** A finding has to have four properties. (1) It is a ground reading from something that does not change its answer because of what model adherents think: an instrument, an experiment, a measurement. (2) It lands on a named slot and carries positive residual mass there, rather than being a global impression. (3) Its residual persists and does not trend down under the model's best re-fitting, and it reads the arrangement of the ingredients that distinguishes this model from its rivals, not merely the list of ingredients they share. (4) Refusing the failing term costs less than continuing to hold it, and the vocabulary you are using has a way to say "this term is false" and not only "this term is incomplete."
>
> **What this is not saying.** It is not saying a failed lab synthesis refutes a model. A search that finds nothing is not a proof of absence, and a prediction that is hard to reach at the current budget is a different fact from one that is underivable in principle. It is not saying that a poll of researchers, a shift in fashion, or the authority of a famous reviewer settles anything. Agreement is a convention, not an anchor. It is also not naming which finding will do the job. The framework I use returns a shape, not a name, so I am not going to tell you which isotope ratio or molecule it will be.
>
> **Pre-registered refuter.** I wrote these down before doing this work. I withdraw the shape if:
> 1. a model is rightly abandoned on agreement or prestige alone, with no ground reading moving, and that is later confirmed by independent ground;
> 2. a model is rightly abandoned solely because a predicted product was not made in the lab, with no positive incompatible observation, and that survives a later increase in search budget;
> 3. a single unreplicated observation legitimately causes abandonment in this field and is later ground-confirmed;
> 4. the leading models turn out to share no underlying primitive set, so no finding can discriminate them by arrangement;
> 5. a legitimate abandonment occurs in a vocabulary with no way to state that a term is false;
> 6. a specific finding can be derived from the figures alone that settles a named origin-of-life model.
>
> **What I would like from you (stage 1).** Tell me which of the four properties you would reject, or name one finding you think satisfies all four for a model you know. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed. Please record your answer before reading anything below.
>
> **Only if you are curious, and only after stage 1 (stage 2).** The source material is below. The figures are the authority, not my summary of them.
> - Local figure text extract: `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md` in this project
> - Canonical usage rule: `FRAMEWORK/reference/FIGURES_README_canonical.md`
> - Wider background on theory change in abiogenesis: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA_world and https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/popper/
>
> **If you want to reuse the method (stage 3, opt-in).** The eight-step loop is in the figures folder of this project. Run it on a problem you know well and can check against a ground, and write down what would refute your result *before* you run it. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.
>
> — [your name]

## Two rules for sending

1. Record the stage-1 answer **before** sending any links. A later agreement is never pooled as corroboration, because the reader then holds the framing (Fig 2 :40).
2. If a reader returns a refutation that fires one of the six, copy it into `11_audit.md` with provenance and re-evaluate it. A failure is the useful outcome.
