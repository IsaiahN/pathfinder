## 0. The residual, stated in effect terms

Question 90 asks: *"What finding would cause researchers to abandon or substantially revise a leading origin-of-life model?"*

Restated as an effect, with no theory name chosen first:

> R = |Gamma(b,a) - o'| on object slot s, where Gamma is a leading origin-of-life model M, b is the community's inherited belief about prebiotic chemistry, a is an intervention (experiment, observation, measurement of the rock record), and o' is what the ground returns. The question asks for the properties of the finding f = o' that make this residual (i) positive on a named slot, (ii) persistent and not trending down, and (iii) cheap enough to refuse a bound term of M so that the reach falls rather than the library merely growing.

"Abandon" and "substantially revise" are **not one outcome**. The route in Formula step 2 separates them:

- `BROKEN · rebinding` -> re-fit what you have, do not mint. This is **revision**.
- `BROKEN · mechanism` -> a mint is owed. This is **replacement of an explanation**.
- `REFUTED` -> *"a term held is false; what is owed is a refusal, not an explanation"* (Formula step 2). This is **abandonment**.

Fig 5 :53: **"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"**. So the question is a **union** of three route bins, not a single hard question (Fig 9: a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either). It must be unbundled before a finding is described, or any finding will seem to fit.

**What an answer would look like**, written down and bound before the run (Formula "Direction"): a shape with four properties, P1 to P4, each refusable. That binding is made in §3 and used in `07` and `09`. It is a shape, not the name of a finding.

## 1. Domain census (before any figure line is chosen)

### R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity:** three relata. A model M, a finding f, and a community decision D. On inspection the decision D is not an independent relatum: it is the route assignment of R once f has ranged against the ground. So R is really two relata (M and f) plus a routing rule.
- **Symmetry:** not symmetric. A refutation is a negation (¬), a unary operator: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"* (Operators table :58). f bears on M one way. The sequence (persistence first, refusal second, mint third) is ordered (Fig 12 sequence bond `->`). Swapping the order gives a different thing.
- **Scale:** three scales in one chain. (1) bench and field scale, where o' is produced; (2) model scale, where a term of M is held or refused; (3) community scale, where a verdict is recorded. The membrane (Fig 4) is the boundary between them: only methods cross up, only head starts come down. A finding is a ground reading that must cross up without being turned into a recording.

### One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | partition or union |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Ground and instrument | experiments, instruments, rock and isotope record, field sites, meteorites | observations o', measurements, residual mass on slot s | the ground: *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks"* (Fig 2 :29) | partition (what is readable at all; Fig 6 :34-35) |
| D2 Model closure | competing models (RNA-first, metabolism-first, lipid-first, coevolution), their bound predicting terms, bonds, description length | residual R, cost of a term, reach of the closure | the ground; models range against it and do not negotiate (Fig 2 :45) | union (three route bins in Formula step 2) |
| D3 Verification and community | researchers, reviewers, editors, funders, public venues | verdicts, agreement, prestige, residuals | must be the ground, never agreement (Fig 2 :35, :40) | partition: a verdict is either anchored or it is a convention |

**No nameable anchor means it is not a domain yet.** D1 has an instrument, D2 has a ground, D3 has the ground as its legitimate anchor and agreement as its forbidden one. All three qualify. D3 would cease to qualify as a checking domain if its anchor were replaced by consensus, because a frame that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it (Fig 10 :42).

### Dependency order (Fig 3)

1. **Perception:** can the community tell the finding from what was there before? *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* (Fig 3 :27). If no instrument returns anything on the slot, there is no edge to extend.
2. **Vocabulary:** does the model frame have a term for what the finding affords: re-fit, mint, or refuse? Without negation (¬) it can re-fit and mint but cannot carry the correction (Operators table :58).
3. **Objective:** what would count as progress? The shrink test: stating the refusal plus leftover costs less than the current confusion (Fig 5 :43-44).
4. **Planning:** design the discriminating experiment, described in effect terms before the frame is chosen (Fig 9 :27-28).
5. **Learn and carry:** record the finding, the route, the date, and the refused term in a lineage that later readers can check.

A claim about step 4 or 5 measured while step 1 is unbuilt is a reading below the break: "a reading of nothing" (Fig 3). This is why counting publications that abandon a model is not evidence about whether the model was refuted.

## 3. Findings

### F1 — A finding is a ground reading, not an internal score (governs D1, D3)

Fig 2 :29:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."**

Fig 2 :45: **"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."**

Fig 1 :23: *"The ground enters as the only metric."*

A finding qualifies only if it is produced by something that does not move when a model's adherents push on it: an instrument, a measurement of the rock record, a synthesis result, a reproducible observation. If the "finding" is a reinterpretation of existing data that appears only after a community decides to favour a different model, it is a frame-internal quantity and cannot settle anything. Fig 1 :27: *"A frame cannot score itself with a quantity it also produces."*

**Refuter:** show a case where a community abandoned a model with no ground reading moving, purely by reweighting existing commitments, and where that abandonment was later confirmed by an independent ground reading. That would show the anchor is unnecessary here and break this finding.

### F2 — A finding must carry positive residual mass on a named slot, not an average (governs D1, D2)

Fig 1 :19: **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**
Fig 1 :22: **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**
Fig 5 :21: **"the gap has real mass somewhere"**

"Origin of life" is not one slot. The residual attaches to a specific object: a chirality, a nucleotide yield, a lipid vesicle's reproduction rate, a mineral surface's catalytic selectivity, an isotope ratio in a specific rock formation. A finding that lives nowhere, such as "the field no longer finds the model convincing," has no slot and therefore no live mass.

World corroboration (secondary): the abiogenesis literature is explicit that *"The transition of non-life to life has not been observed experimentally"* (Wikipedia, Abiogenesis, 2026) and that *"A functional protocell, which must exhibit Darwinian evolution, has not yet been achieved in a laboratory setting"* (same). Those are statements about named slots, not about the field's mood.

**Refuter:** exhibit a theory change in this field where no single slot carried positive residual mass and the model was still abandoned on the ground. If that is common, the per-slot requirement is wrong for this domain.

### F3 — The residual must persist and not trend down (governs D2; the sharpest discriminator)

Fig 5 :58: **"The candidates are ENUMERABLE rather than intuited: every term currently bound and generating a prediction is a candidate for being the wrong one."**
Fig 5 :59: **"a term whose residual PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN is the one to point the refusal at"**

This is the operational content of "cause." A single anomalous result is not enough to justify abandonment. The candidate term to refuse is the one that *keeps being wrong and is not improving*. This triangulates with Popper's methodological caution (SEP, 2026): *"a single conflicting or counter-instance is never sufficient methodologically for falsification, and that scientific theories are often retained even though much of the available evidence conflicts with them"*, and with Kuhn's account (SEP, 2026): *"It is only the accumulation of particularly troublesome anomalies that poses a serious problem for the existing disciplinary matrix."*

So the finding is not a one-off. It is a **pattern of results** on one slot whose residual refuses to shrink under the model's best re-fitting. That is why the question is hard: the answer is a shape of a time series, not a single measurement.

**Refuter:** show that in this field a single, unrepeated, un-replicated observation has legitimately caused abandonment and was later confirmed. That would falsify the persistence requirement.

### F4 — Absence is not the same as refutation: budget versus absence (governs D1, D2)

Fig 8 :39: **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**
Fig 8 :39-40: **"Underivable-in-principle and unreachable-at-this-budget are different facts, and they issue different instructions."**

This is the **most important negative result in the audit**, and it is the one most often missed in origin-of-life discourse. A laboratory that fails to synthesise a nucleotide, a vesicle, or a self-replicating polymer has not thereby refuted the model that predicts it. Fig 8 :42-43: *"Unreachable-at-budget: the term is already in the closure and was not found, so the remedy is more search, not another atom."* Importing a new mechanism to explain a budget shortfall is priced above starting from primitives (Fig 6 :24: *"A wrong atom costs more than starting from primitives."*).

The world literature agrees in effect. The RNA-world entry reports that *"even proponents agree that there is still not conclusive evidence to completely falsify other paradigms and hypotheses"* (Wikipedia, RNA world, 2026), and Sutherland himself described the strict RNA world as a *"restrictive, hypothetical arrangement"* (quoted there). Neither statement licenses treating a failed synthesis as a killing blow.

**Refuter:** produce a case in which a model was rightly abandoned solely because a predicted product was not made in the lab, with no positive incompatible observation and no persistent residual, and where the abandonment survived a later increase in search budget. That would falsify the distinction.

### F5 — A discriminating finding must read the bond, not the ingredient list (governs D2)

Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**
Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**

Leading origin-of-life models are **isomers** in the figure's sense: they are assembled from overlapping prebiotic ingredients (nucleotides, amino acids, lipids, mineral surfaces, energy gradients) but arrange them differently and so predict different behaviour. RNA-first puts information storage before catalysis; metabolism-first puts a geochemical gradient and catalytic network first; lipid-first puts a boundary first. A finding that merely lists ingredients present on early Earth does not discriminate among them. A finding that reads the **arrangement**, for example which molecular species must exist *before* heredity can start, does.

This is derived in full in `06_isomers_and_order.md`, with a falsifier. Fig 13 :74 constrains the raw materials: **"Everything else is a composition across the two kinds, never within one."** The set of primitive kinds is closed; the arrangements over it are not (Fig 13 :118: *"infinite effort inside a finite reach"*). So model variety comes from arrangement, and so must the discriminating finding.

**Refuter:** show that the leading origin-of-life models do not share an underlying atom set in the relevant sense (each requires primitives the others cannot express). Then the isomer reading fails and the finding would have to be a different kind of thing.

### F6 — A refusal must be payable, and the vocabulary must contain negation (governs D2, D3)

Fig 5 :42: **"The acceptance test is a bargain, not a threshold."**
Fig 5 :43-44: **"A new term is worth having when stating it, plus what remains unexplained after it, costs less than what was unexplained before."**
Operators table :58: *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*

A finding causes abandonment only if refusing a term pays: stating the refusal, plus what is still unexplained after it, must cost less than holding the term. If the community keeps the model by adding auxiliary assumptions whose total description length grows faster than the residual they remove, that is the conventionalist twist Popper names (SEP: Marxists *"saved from falsification by the addition of ad hoc hypotheses"*), and the framework prices it as a bad bargain rather than a victory.

Structurally, this requires the operator ¬. A vocabulary with only re-fit (mis-attached) and mint (mechanism broken) can revise forever and cannot abandon. "Substantially revise" lives in that re-fit/mint vocabulary; "abandon" requires ¬.

**Refuter:** demonstrate a legitimate abandonment in a vocabulary that has no way to state "this term is false," only ways to state "this term is incomplete." That would show negation is not required for this phenomenon.

### F7 — The community verdict must be anchored, and first contact must not spend independence (governs D3)

Fig 2 :35: **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**
Fig 2 :40: **"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**
Fig 10 :42: **"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it."**
Fig 10 :52: **"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."**

The question asks what would cause *researchers* to abandon a model. That is a community-level event, and it has two possible anchors: the ground, or the agreement of the researchers themselves. Only the first is a verdict. If a critical mass of influential researchers simply stops citing a model, that is panel 1 of Fig 2 (coherent drift), *"agreement among frames is a weighted average of influence"*, not a ground reading. Prestige can fund a program to be tried; it cannot settle it (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: *"Prestige can fund an action. It can give an action a budget to be tried. But it cannot settle an action. Only the ground can settle it."*).

The outreach consequence: ask for the finding and its refuter before showing this framework. Once a reader holds the framework's interiors, their agreement is no longer evidence.

**Refuter:** show a case where authorities who never range against the ground nevertheless produced a reliable, later-ground-confirmed abandonment. That would collapse the distinction between a verdict and a convention for D3.

### F8 — The sought-for shape must be bound as an ordinary term before the run (method finding)

Fig 5 :62: **"A frame searching for an answer of a particular shape holds that shape as a PREFERENCE rather than as a term."**
Fig 5 :65-66: **"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM. Write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."**

This is why the question itself is dangerous if answered casually. "A finding that would cause abandonment" is a shape held as a preference. A frame cannot check the direction of its own question (Formula "Direction"). The remedy is not a cleverer search; it is the discipline of writing the shape down and making it refusable. §4 does that. The pre-registered refuter is a design instrument, not decorative honesty.

**Refuter:** show a derivation of this shape that did not write the shape down in advance and still survived an independent check. That would show the discipline is dispensable here.

### F9 — The framework returns the shape of the finding, not its name (honest limit, governs all domains)

Fig 9 :32: **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**
Fig 9 :27-28: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**

No figure line names an isotope ratio, a molecule, or a specific experiment that would settle an origin-of-life model. The canonical usage rule says the framework returns *"what a missing thing would have to look like (a shape, not a name)"* (FIGURES_README_canonical.md). So the honest output of this audit is a set of properties f must have (P1 to P4 below), not a prediction of which finding will appear. If a specific finding is named as *the* killer, it is an import from the abiogenesis frame, provenanced as such, and carries no figure authority.

**Refuter:** derive, from the figure set alone, the name of a specific finding that settles a named origin-of-life model. Since the figures are domain-agnostic, any such derivation would break the shape-not-a-name rule and would be recorded as a strain, not silently accepted.

### The bound shape (P1 to P4), written before the verdict

| property | effect statement | figure |
|---|---|---|
| P1 Anchored | f is a ground reading from an instrument or record that does not update on model commitment | Fig 2 :29 |
| P2 Local and positive | f lands on a named slot s and carries R⁺_s > 0, not a global average | Fig 1 :19; Fig 5 :21 |
| P3 Persistent and discriminating | f is a pattern whose residual does not trend down, and it reads the arrangement (bond) that distinguishes the models | Fig 5 :59; Fig 12 :117 |
| P4 Payable as a refusal | refusing the bound term costs less than holding it, and the community vocabulary contains ¬ | Fig 5 :43-44; Operators :58 |

A finding with all four properties is, in this derivation, sufficient to cause abandonment. A finding with P1 and P2 but not P3 is an anomaly or a budget shortfall, not a refutation. A finding with P1 to P3 but not P4 changes the science without changing what the community can say.

## 4. Pre-registered refuter (written before the conclusion)

Required before any outreach. Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."*

The shape claim (P1 to P4) is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. **Anchor refuter.** A community abandons a leading origin-of-life model on agreement or prestige alone, with no ground reading moving, and the abandonment is later confirmed by independent ground. That makes the anchor unnecessary (breaks P1, Fig 2 :29, :35).
2. **Budget refuter.** A model is rightly abandoned solely because a predicted product was not made in the lab, with no positive incompatible observation, and the abandonment survives a later increase in search budget. That collapses the underivable/unreachable distinction (breaks P3, Fig 8 :39-40).
3. **Persistence refuter.** A single unreplicated observation legitimately causes abandonment in this field and is later ground-confirmed. That removes the persistence requirement (breaks P3, Fig 5 :59).
4. **Isomer refuter.** The leading models are shown to share no underlying primitive set in the relevant sense, so no finding can discriminate them by arrangement. That breaks the bond/discriminator reading (breaks P3, Fig 12 :117).
5. **Negation refuter.** A legitimate abandonment occurs in a vocabulary with no way to state that a term is false, only that it is incomplete. That makes ¬ dispensable (breaks P4, Operators :58).
6. **Shape refuter.** A specific finding is derived from the figures alone that settles a named origin-of-life model. That breaks the shape-not-a-name limit (Fig 9 :32).

If any fires, the corresponding property is withdrawn and the answer in `07` is revised. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Unbundle the question before answering it. "Abandon" is a refusal (¬); "substantially revise" is a re-fit or a mint. They route differently (Formula step 2, Fig 5 :53).
2. Describe f in effect terms (P1 to P4) before naming RNA-first, metabolism-first, or any other model. Fig 9 :27-28: *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."*
3. Treat failed syntheses as budget information until shown otherwise. Fig 8 :39: *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."*
4. Ask the public venues in `03` what *they* would count as a discriminating finding, and record the answer before showing them any of this framework (Fig 10 :52).
5. Send the generator, not the recording. Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Fig 4 :25: *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."*

## 7. Verdict of the audit

The figure set does govern this question, and it governs it **without naming a finding**. The answer is a shape with four properties: anchored, local and positive, persistent and discriminating, and payable as a refusal in a vocabulary that contains negation. A failed synthesis is not a refutation; agreement is not an anchor; a single anomaly is not a cause. The question's two words, abandon and revise, are two different route bins and are kept separate.

Status: `specified`, ungrounded, candidate. Triangulation: P1 and P2 are triangulated across Fig 2 and Fig 1 (low overlap, anchor rule versus per-slot residual rule). P3 is triangulated across Fig 5 and Fig 12 (shrink test versus bond information), with world corroboration from Popper and Kuhn (separate field). P4 is single-source for the figure statement (Operators :58) with the MDL bargain from Fig 5 as a nearby but distinct line. P0, the union reading, is single-source from Formula step 2. The shape-not-a-name limit is triangulated across Fig 9 and the canonical figures README. Strains after re-census: none live.
