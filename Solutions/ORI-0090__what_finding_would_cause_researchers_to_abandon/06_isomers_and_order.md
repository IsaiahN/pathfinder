# Are the leading origin-of-life models isomers, and in what order does a finding act?

**Mode: `specified`.** This is a simulated derivation checked against the figures, not a ground reading (see `05_what_shape_means.md` §7).

## RESIDUAL

Question 90 asks for a finding that causes **abandonment or substantial revision**. Those are two different operations in the framework. Two questions are owed: (1) can the leading models be compared as isomers over a shared set, so that a finding can discriminate them; and (2) what is the order of operations from finding to abandonment, and does abandonment delete anything?

Atoms held (from Fig 13 and the abiogenesis literature): states space, time, energy; structures spectrum, gradient, network. Prebiotic instances: nucleotides, amino acids, lipids, mineral surfaces, redox and thermal gradients, wet-dry cycles, UV and impact energy (Wikipedia, Abiogenesis, 2026). ## DERIVATION

### Q1 — Are RNA-first, metabolism-first and lipid-first isomers?

- Fig 12 :5: **"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."**
- Fig 12 :117: **"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."**
- Fig 12 :124: **"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says what kind; the qualifier says which member."**
- The abiogenesis entry says the settings "are often in competition with one another as there are many views of prebiotic compound availability, geophysical setting, and early life characteristics" (Wikipedia, 2026). Different settings and orderings over overlapping ingredients, which is the isomer pattern.
- **As isomers:** if all three are written over the shared prebiotic atom set, differing only in which structure mediates which transition (information before catalysis, gradient before heredity, boundary before both), they are Fig 12 :117 isomers. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. A discriminating finding then reads the bond, not the ingredient list.
- **As functional-group members:** if each model is written as its own smaller set of atoms, they share a **functional group** in Fig 12 :124's sense: each is a `state -> structure -> state` molecule with a different occupant in the structure slot. The weaker claim is still useful: it says what kind of thing each model is, and a finding can still rule out an occupant.
- **Falsifier, written before the run:** if any leading model requires a primitive that the others cannot express even after re-description, the isomer claim fails and only the functional-group claim survives. If no model can be written as `state -> structure -> state` at all, both fail and the bonding analysis is wrong for this domain.
- Triangulated for the figure definition (Fig 12 :5, :117, :124); single-source and unmeasured for the chemistry-facing mapping of each model onto an atom set.

### Q2 — What is the order from finding to abandonment?

Formula step 2 sorts the residual before anything is minted: TRANSFERRED (nothing owed), NOVEL (extend perception), BROKEN rebinding (re-fit, do not mint), BROKEN mechanism (a mint is owed), REFUTED (a refusal is owed). Formula step 3 then charges both a term and a refusal the same bargain.

So the order is:

1. **Perceive the finding on a slot** (Fig 3 :5, :27). No instrument, no edge to extend.
2. **Route it** (Formula step 2). Is it novel, mis-attached, mechanism-broken, or refuted? This is the step that decides "revise" versus "abandon."
3. **Re-fit if mis-attached** (BROKEN rebinding). This is *"substantially revise"*: keep the term, correct its attachments.
4. **Mint if mechanism-broken** (BROKEN mechanism). A new term is owed; still revision, deeper.
5. **Refuse if refuted** (Fig 5 :53: *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"*). This is *"abandon"*: a ¬ term is minted.
6. **Settle against the ground** (Formula step 5). *"A gate is not the ground."*

Swapping 3 and 5 gives a different thing: re-fitting a term that should be refused is the conventionalist twist Popper names (SEP, 2026: Marxism was *"saved from falsification by the addition of ad hoc hypotheses"*). Fig 5 :59 gives the discriminator: point the refusal at the term whose residual *"PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN"*.

### Q3 — Does abandonment delete the model?

No. Fig 5 :55: **"What it removes is not a term but a term's standing to be composed over. Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach."** And Fig 6 :42-43: *"A refutation adds a term that refuses; it removes nothing, and the reach falls because something was added rather than because anything was deleted."*

This matters for origin-of-life practice. An abandoned model stays in the record as a term with provenance; what it loses is the standing to be used as a premise in further models. Its failure becomes a refused atom that later work must route around. The library grows while the reach shrinks. Counting the number of models in the literature therefore cannot measure progress: *"a library that grows is not a reach that grows"* (Fig 5 :72).

### Q4 — What is the order of description versus frame choice?

Fig 9 :27-28: **"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit"**. The finding must be described in effect terms (P1 to P4 in `01` §3) before RNA-first or metabolism-first is chosen as the frame whose closure predicts its shape. If the frame is chosen first, the description will be bent to fit whichever model the searcher already favours. This is the shadow/echo inversion named in the exemplar's `06`: echo without shadow is apophenia.

## CONCLUSION

The leading origin-of-life models are best treated as **isomers over a shared prebiotic atom set** (strong claim, falsified if any requires a primitive the others cannot express), or at minimum as members of one **functional group** `state -> structure -> state` (weaker claim). A finding discriminates them by the bond, not the ingredient list.

"Substantially revise" routes to re-fit or mint and keeps the term. "Abandon" routes to REFUTED and mints a ¬ term. Abandonment does not delete the model from the record; it removes the model's standing to be composed over, so the reach falls while the library grows. The order is perceive, route, re-fit or mint or refuse, settle. Describe the finding before naming a rival frame.
