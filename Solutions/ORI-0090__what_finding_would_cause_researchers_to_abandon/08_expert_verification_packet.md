# Expert verification packet — what finding would make researchers abandon or revise an origin-of-life model?

**What this is:** a claim in a form an origin-of-life researcher or philosopher of science can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `specified` on the four properties, `general` on the structural shape, not `grounded`.** No measurement at any prebiotic slot is claimed. This is a shape and a route, not a result.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

A finding causes abandonment or substantial revision of a leading origin-of-life model only if it has four properties: it is an anchor reading that does not update on model commitment; it carries positive residual mass on a named slot; its residual persists under re-fitting and it reads the arrangement that distinguishes competing models, not their shared ingredients; and refusing the failing term is a cheaper description than keeping it, in a vocabulary with negation. "Abandon" and "revise" are two different routes: revise is re-fit or mint, abandon is a refusal (¬) that removes a term's standing to be composed over without deleting the term from the record. A failed lab synthesis is not a refutation, and a consensus is not an anchor. The framework returns this shape and withholds the name of the finding.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — an anchor and a slot, not an impression (checkable against Fig 2 and Fig 1)

- **Statement:** a decisive finding must be a ground reading on a named object slot; field-level impressions and reinterpretations of existing data do not qualify.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 2 :29 — *"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."* Fig 1 :19 — *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 5 :21 — *"the gap has real mass somewhere"*
- **What to check:** is the purported finding tied to a specific observation (a yield, a ratio, a vesicle behaviour, a rock signature) produced by something that does not move when the model's adherents reinterpret it?
- **Refuter:** a documented case in which a model was rightly abandoned on agreement or prestige alone, with no ground reading moving, later confirmed by independent ground. Also: a case where abandonment followed a global impression with no named slot.

### C2 — persistence and bond-discrimination (checkable against Fig 5 and Fig 12)

- **Statement:** the anomalous residual must persist and not trend down under re-fitting, and the finding must distinguish models by arrangement rather than by shared ingredients.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :59 — *"a term whose residual PERSISTS AND DOES NOT TREND DOWN is the one to point the refusal at"*. Fig 12 :117 — *"Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."* Fig 12 :5 — *"A set of ingredients is not one. The bond is the rest of the information, and it is the half nothing was recording."*
- **What to check:** does the anomaly stay flat across labs and settings rather than shrinking as methods improve? Does the finding tell you which *ordering* of steps occurred (for example, whether heredity had to precede catalysis), not merely which molecules were present?
- **Refuter:** a single unreplicated observation that legitimately caused abandonment in this field and was later ground-confirmed; or a demonstration that the leading models share no underlying primitive set, so no finding can discriminate them by arrangement.

### C3 — a payable refusal, not a deletion (checkable against Fig 5, Operators table, Fig 8)

- **Statement:** abandonment mints a ¬ term that is cheaper than the held term and removes that term's standing to be composed over; it does not delete the model from the record. A failed synthesis is not an abandonment.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 5 :53 — *"refuted: a term we hold is wrong; a term is owed that says so"*. Fig 5 :55 — *"What it removes is not a term but a term's standing to be composed over. Nothing leaves the library. What falls is the reach."* Fig 8 :39 — *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Operators table :58 — *"a refutation is a negation, which is why a vocabulary without it can only accumulate"*
- **What to check:** after a model is abandoned, is it still in the record with provenance but no longer used as a premise? Was there a positive incompatible observation, or only an unsuccessful attempt? Does the community's vocabulary have a way to state that a term is false?
- **Refuter:** a legitimate abandonment in a vocabulary with only "incomplete" and no "false"; or a case where a model was rightly abandoned solely because a predicted product was not made in the lab, with no positive incompatible observation, and that survived a later increase in search budget.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not the name of any finding, isotope ratio, molecule, or experiment that will settle a named model. The framework returns a shape, not a name (Fig 9 :32).
- Not that a failed synthesis refutes a model (Fig 8 :39-40).
- Not that consensus, fashion, citation counts, or a famous reviewer's verdict causes legitimate abandonment (Fig 2 :35, :40; prestige rule).
- Not that "abandon" and "revise" are one event. They route differently in Formula step 2.
- Not that abandonment deletes the model from the record (Fig 5 :55).
- Not `grounded`. Nothing here is asserted as settled by experiment.

## 5. What would settle it

A ground reading, not more agreement:

1. **Slot discriminator:** an instrument that reads the arrangement (which species must precede heredity) rather than only the ingredient list.
2. **Persistence test:** an anomalous result that stays flat on its slot under the model's best re-fitting across independent groups.
3. **Budget test:** a demonstration that a missing step is underivable in principle rather than unreachable at this budget (Fig 8 :40-43).
4. **Standing test:** a new lineage composing over the record that the refused model cannot enter, so its lost standing is observable rather than asserted.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 2 :29, Fig 1 :19, Fig 5 :59 and Fig 12 :117. Four short passages.
2. Name one finding from your own subfield that satisfies all four properties for a model you know. Or name the property it fails.
3. Say whether any current origin-of-life anomaly you know is persistent-and-flat rather than shrinking with better technique. That is the empirical crux of C2.
4. Decide whether your own vocabulary can state "this term is false" (C3). If it cannot, the framework predicts you can revise but not abandon.
5. Send the refutation. A failure is more useful than an endorsement. Record your answer before reading `01`, `07` or `09`.
