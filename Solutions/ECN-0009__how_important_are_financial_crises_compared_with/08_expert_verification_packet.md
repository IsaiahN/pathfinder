# Expert verification packet: the Tether on crisis importance versus ordinary fluctuations

**What this is:** a claim in a form a macroeconomist or financial-stability researcher can check. Mode, derivation and refuters are stated before the conclusion, so a reader knows what to look for and what would settle it.

**Mode: `general` on the shape, `specified` on the union finding, `gap` on the numeric weight. Not `grounded`.** No measurement is claimed. Simulated derivation checked for internal consistency against the figures, not a ground reading.

## 1. The claim, in one paragraph

"How important are financial crises compared with ordinary changes in spending and production" is malformed as a single comparison. The two classes are separate slots and must not be averaged. The disagreement is a union compound, so it must be split into an ordinary-fluctuation loop, a crisis loop and a specific overlap with its own signed cycle. Frequency and consequence are two currencies that do not add, so no scalar ranking is licensed until a currency is declared. The numeric weight is a gap owed to a ground reading, not a consensus estimate. Importance ranks research budgets, not truth.

## 2. Three checkable sub-claims

### C1 — framework-internal; checkable by reading Figures 1 and 9

- **Statement:** a single importance score over the two classes is not a legal output of the framework. The residual is per slot, and a dispute where each rule works on a different subset is several questions, not one.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 1 :19, *"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."* Fig 1 :22, *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Fig 9 :4-5, *"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."* Fig 9 :54, *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you search again."*
- **Refuter:** show that aggregating across the two classes preserves the live crisis signal rather than erasing it; or show that one generating process with one distribution reproduces both the frequency of ordinary fluctuations and the depth and persistence of crisis losses with no residual that persists and does not trend down.

### C2 — economics-facing; checkable against the empirical literature

- **Statement:** crisis recessions and ordinary recessions are different enough to require two loops and a compound, not one tail. The overlap is a signed cycle: low volatility raises leverage and fragility, which raises crisis probability.
- **What to check:** do crisis recessions have different depth and persistence than ordinary recessions conditional on the same shock? The world sources point one way: Cerra and Saxena report that *"the large output loss from financial crises and some types of political crises is highly persistent"*; Jordà, Schularick and Taylor report that *"recessions associated with crises lead to deeper recessions and stronger turnarounds in imbalances than during normal recessions"*; the OFR volatility paradox says *"low volatility leads investors to behave in ways that make the financial system more fragile and prone to crisis"*. Check whether those results survive a different conditioning set and a different dataset.
- **Figure line governing the form:** Fig 12 :121, *"cycle. The output re-enters as input. The sign of the loop decides whether it closes, and a chain cannot close by construction."* Fig 11 :49, *"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."*
- **Refuter:** show crisis and non-crisis recessions have statistically indistinguishable depth and persistence once conditioned on the same shock; or exhibit a case where the two conditions neither overlap nor form a compound.

### C3 — framework-internal derivation with an empirical tail

- **Statement:** frequency and consequence are two currencies that do not add, so a scalar importance ranking is a goal statement until a currency is declared. The numeric weight is a `gap`.
- **Source, verbatim:** Fig 12 :125, *"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."* Fig 12 :126-127, *"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them and will not notice the shortfall."* Fig 8 :39, *"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."* Fig 13 :134, *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"*
- **Refuter:** declare a currency that prices both frequency and consequence without loss, so the two classes collapse to one scalar; or produce a ground reading with a stable numeric weight across anchors that holds out of sample.

## 3. What is explicitly not claimed

- Not a theory of financial crises, and not a new model of the business cycle.
- Not that crises are simply the fat tail of ordinary fluctuations. That is a world hypothesis, not a figure verdict.
- Not that ordinary fluctuations are unimportant. Frequency is a filter, not a verdict.
- Not that one general equilibrium model ranks the two classes.
- Not that the six-term substrate names a "crisis atom" or an "importance atom". No seventh primitive was invented.
- Not `grounded`. There is no measurement and no stable numeric weight in this packet.
- Not a claim about whom to believe. Prestige is not a witness: *The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap* says *"Prestige must never be allowed to validate an action directly."*

## 5. What would settle it

A source-discriminating instrument, not a further argument. Concretely:

1. A test of whether one distribution reproduces both the frequency of ordinary changes and the persistence of crisis losses.
2. A depth-and-persistence comparison of crisis and non-crisis recessions under a conditioning set chosen before the run.
3. A declared common currency for frequency and consequence, or a demonstration that none exists.
4. A measured round-trip gap R_T across the firm-to-systemic step, rather than an assumed zero.

## 6. What an expert can check in twenty minutes

1. Read Fig 1 :19 and :22, Fig 9 :4-5 and :54, and Fig 12 :121 and :125. Six short passages.
2. Check C2 against your own knowledge: are crisis recessions genuinely deeper and more persistent conditional on the same shock, or is that an artefact of crisis dating?
3. State whether frequency and consequence can be priced in one currency without loss. A one-line "yes, and here is the unit" would refute C3.
4. Send the refutation, not an endorsement. A failure is more useful than agreement.
