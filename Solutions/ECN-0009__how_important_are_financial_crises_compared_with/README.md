**Question:** How important are financial crises compared with ordinary changes in spending and production?

**Status:** done

## What is being decided, in effect terms

The question asks for a comparison of "importance" between two classes of event in a macroeconomy: rare financial crises, and ordinary changes in spending and production. Option A treats crises as extreme draws from one ordinary process, so importance is proportional to frequency and one model plus one lever suffice. Option B treats crises as a distinct regime with its own generator and disproportionate, persistent consequences, so importance is disproportionate to frequency and two loops plus two levers are required. Option C holds that the comparison is ill posed until a currency is declared that prices both classes, so the honest answer is two verdicts and a gap, never one scalar.

What an answer would look like, bound as an ordinary term before the run: a shape statement of the form "two classes, one union compound, per-slot verdicts, no aggregate importance number without a declared currency, numeric weight owed to the ground." This is the Direction discipline of `THE_FORMULA.md`: write down what an answer would look like so the sought-for shape can accrue a residual and be refused.

## What is deliberately not here

- No personal emails of named individuals.
- No ranking of people by how likely they are to agree with the verdict.
- No bulk-send plan.

The reason is figure-grounded. Selecting recipients for receptiveness manufactures the correlation that disqualifies their agreement as a verdict (Seat Map :163), and asking them to trust the framework before they check the output spends the only independence they contribute (Fig 10 :42; Fig 2 :35). A failure is more useful than an endorsement.
