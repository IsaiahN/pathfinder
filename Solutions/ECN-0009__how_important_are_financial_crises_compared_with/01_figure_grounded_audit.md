## 0. The claim under audit

The question is: *"How important are financial crises compared with ordinary changes in spending and production?"* It is a comparative importance question, which is a trap in effect terms because "importance" is not a slot and has no declared currency. The derive-first rule is to state the residual as an effect before choosing any figure line.

### 0.1 The residual in effect terms

R is the mismatch between what a single importance ranking predicts about the macroeconomy and what the data actually show about two different classes of event. Stated as an effect: we need to decide which class claims the explanatory and policy budget, and whether the two classes are commensurable on one scale at all.

- Option A: crises are extreme draws from the same ordinary process; importance is proportional to frequency; ordinary changes dominate; one model, one distribution, one lever.
- Option B: crises are a distinct regime with a different generator and disproportionate, persistent consequences; importance is disproportionate to frequency; two loops, two levers.
- Option C: the comparison is ill posed until a currency pricing both classes is declared; the honest output is two verdicts and a gap, never one scalar.

The sought-for shape is bound before the run, as `THE_FORMULA.md` "Direction" requires: *"make the sought-for shape an ORDINARY TERM - write down what an answer would look like, bind it, and let it accrue a residual like anything else."* Bound shape: two classes, one union compound, per-slot verdicts, no aggregate importance number without a declared currency, numeric weight owed to the ground.

### 0.2 Domain census: R by arity, symmetry and scale

- **Arity.** Two classes are being compared. The comparison is binary and ordered, not a symmetric joining of interchangeable operands. The figures' ordered comparison bond ⋛ is *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* (Operators table :48-49), not a symmetric conjunction.
- **Symmetry.** The two classes are asymmetric under a swap. Crises are rare, large, discrete and systemic; ordinary changes are frequent, small, continuous and diffuse. Swapping them changes the claim completely. This is the signature of an ordered bond, not a symmetric one (Fig 12 :92-93 on symmetric versus ordered bonds).
- **Scale.** Crises operate at a systemic, national or global scale; ordinary changes at household, firm and sectoral scale. The question is therefore a cross-scale question, which brings in the membrane and the round trip (Fig 4; Fig 10 :68).

### 0.3 One line per domain: actors, currency, anchor

| domain | actors | currency | anchor | nameable anchor? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Real activity, ordinary spending and production | households, firms, consumers, producers | spending, output, income, real and nominal quantities | national accounts, business-cycle dating | yes: measured output |
| D2 Financial crises | banks, borrowers, lenders, asset markets, regulators | credit, leverage, asset prices, balance sheets, spreads | crisis-event datasets, credit spreads, bank failures, output collapses | yes: dated systemic episodes |
| D3 Policy and verification | central banks, fiscal authorities, regulators | policy tools and realised outcomes | recovery, welfare, measured outcome | yes: the ground of policy |
| D4 Measurement and modelling | economists, datasets, models | variance explained, forecast error | out-of-sample prediction against measured output | yes: held-out data |

A domain without a nameable anchor is not a domain yet. All four have one, so all four qualify. None of the four is a "financial-crisis atom" invented from the six-term substrate; that would be a strain and is refused below.

### 0.4 Partition or union

The two classes form a **union compound**, not a partition of one distribution. World evidence gives the reason: credit growth precedes crises (Schularick and Taylor), crisis recessions are deeper and their imbalances unwind more strongly than in normal recessions (Jordà, Schularick and Taylor), and output losses after financial crises are persistent with no significant rebound (Cerra and Saxena). Two different rules work well on two different subsets. Fig 9 :4-5 names that exact condition:

> **"Disagreements that do not shrink with effort, where each rule keeps working well on a different subset, are not one hard question but several well-formed ones. Split it rather than search."**

So this is not one hard question with a hidden scalar answer. It is a bundled union that must be unbundled before it is searched (Fig 9 :54). The intersection of the two conditions, ordinary fragility that becomes a crisis only under credit stress, is a specific compound, not a harder case of either (Fig 9 :57-58).

### 0.5 Dependency order (Fig 3)

Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."* The order here is: perceive each class separately (perception), acquire terms for what each class affords (vocabulary), state what would count as progress for each (objective), then plan a comparative claim (planning), then carry it (learn and carry). A comparative importance number computed before the per-class residuals exist is a reading below the break, which Fig 3 :22-23 says is *"a reading of nothing."*

## 2. Findings

### F1 — The question invites an aggregate the figures forbid (decisive for form)

Fig 1 :19:

> **"The residual is indexed by object slot, not aggregated."**

Fig 1 :22:

> **"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."**

The slots here are the two classes and, within each class, the individual episode and sector. "How important, compared with" asks for one number over both. That is exactly the aggregation the figure forbids. The honest form is a per-slot residual for ordinary changes and a per-slot residual for crises, kept separate and never averaged into a national importance score. Fig 1 :13 adds that *"a global R near zero with one live slot residual is a legal state"*, so even if aggregate variance looks well explained, a live crisis slot can be carrying the whole signal.

**Triangulation:** Fig 1 (agent perception) plus the per-slot rule in the essay *How a Society of Agents Would Work* (a different field-facing use of the same rule), low overlap between an agent-loop context and a multi-agent design context. Mark `triangulated` for form. **Mode:** `general`.

### F2 — This is a union compound that must be split (decisive for structure)

Fig 9 :4-5 and Fig 9 :54 are quoted in section 0.4. Fig 9 :57-58:

> **"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and becomes a specific compound."**

The economics dispute does not shrink with effort because each rule works on a different subset. That is Fig 9's single-question test failing, so the remedy is a split, not a longer search. Verdict: D1 and D2 keep separate loops; their intersection is a compound with its own loop.

**Triangulation:** Fig 9 (search and leave) plus three independent empirical findings from different data programs, Schularick and Taylor (credit predicts crises), Jordà, Schularick and Taylor (crisis recessions differ from normal recessions) and Cerra and Saxena (crisis losses persistent). Low overlap between a methodological figure and long-run macro-financial datasets. Mark `triangulated` for structure. **Mode:** `specified`.

### F3 — Two currencies do not add, so no single scalar is licensed

Fig 12 :125:

> **"A composition costs in two currencies, and they do not add."**

Fig 12 :126-127:

> **"Bits price the description. Actions price finding out whether it holds. They are in different units, so a system holding one accounting cannot compare them and will not notice the shortfall."**

Frequency is one currency (ordinary changes are common), consequence and persistence are another (crises are rare but deep and long). A system holding one accounting cannot compare them. The Operators table :51 says *"a goal is a comparison, which is why a vocabulary without it cannot state one"*, so declaring "crises matter more" is stating a goal, not reporting a measurement, unless the currency is declared.

**Strain test.** Reading ⋛ as "just compare the two magnitudes and rank them" fits only with a strain, because ⋛ is *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* and the two classes are not yet two quantities in one unit. Strain means the line governs another quantity. Re-census: the comparison that is licensed is a threshold relation between two per-slot residuals after a currency has been declared, not a direct crisis-versus-ordinary magnitude. The strain is named, not hidden.

**Verdict:** `single-source` for the figure definition of two currencies, corroborated in form by the world finding that depth and frequency are different axes; no figure line supplies the conversion. **Mode:** `general`.

### F4 — The anchor is the outcome, not agreement

Fig 2 :29-30:

> **"An anchor is whatever settles the question and does not change its answer because of what any frame thinks. A measurement, an experiment, a counter, a market, a patient outcome."**

Fig 2 :35:

> **"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."**

Fig 2 :40:

> **"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"**

For this question the nameable anchors are national accounts, crisis-dating datasets, credit spreads, and outcome studies that measure the depth and persistence of losses. Expert agreement about "importance" is not an anchor because it moves when the experts think differently. The three long-run data programs cited above overlap in authors and datasets, so they are one pool for the parts they share; their agreement is not three witnesses.

**Verdict:** `triangulated` for the anchor rule (Fig 2 plus Fig 7 :35, *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms is where the ground votes."*). **Mode:** `general`.

### F5 — The numeric "how important" is a gap, and that is a verdict

Fig 8 :39:

> **"So a search that finds nothing is never a proof of absence."**

Fig 9 :32:

> **"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"**

Fig 13 :134:

> **"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence"**

No figure line returns "financial crises are N times as important as ordinary changes." The figures return a shape: split the union, keep per-slot residuals, declare the currency, and do not mistake a filter for a witness. The numeric weight is unreached at this budget rather than underivable in principle, because the field has nameable anchors. It is reported as `gap`, not as silence. This agent took no measurement.

**Verdict:** `gap` on the quantity, `triangulated` that the quantity is not derivable from the figures (Fig 8 :39, Fig 9 :32, Fig 13 :134, three low-overlap lines). **Mode:** `general`.

### F6 — Importance is a budget filter, never a verdict

Fig 9 :60:

> **"Use filters for the budget and witnesses for the verdict. Never let a filter hand you a verdict."**

An importance ranking is useful for deciding which question gets tried first and how much budget to spend on it. It is disqualifying as evidence for the truth of a claim about the economy. Seat Map :163: *"High correlation is disqualifying for a verdict and efficient for a convention."* A lead list selected for people likely to agree with a pre-chosen importance ranking manufactures exactly the correlation that makes their agreement worthless.

**Verdict:** `triangulated` (Fig 9 :60 plus Seat Map :163 plus Fig 2 :40, three distinct rules). **Mode:** `general`.

### F7 — The cross-scale step carries a round-trip gap R_T

Fig 4 :30:

> **"So take the round trip and measure the gap. Without it you are navigating by dead reckoning: confident about a position nobody checked."**

Ordinary changes at the firm and household scale are carried up to a systemic claim about crises, then read back down to policy. Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* A recording of one crisis replayed as a forecast of the next is the failure the membrane names. The gap is not an error to eliminate; it is the measurement of what the coarser description cannot hold. **R_T is nonzero** and is enumerated qualitatively in `11_audit.md`.

**Verdict:** `triangulated` (Fig 4 plus the Symbols table definition of R_T plus the world volatility-paradox result that low measured volatility coincides with building fragility). **Mode:** `general`.

### F8 — Crisis valence is relative to the residual, not a property of crises

Fig 11 :49:

> **"An actor's valence is relative to the residual, not a property it carries."**

The OFR volatility paradox (opened 2026-11-23) is the world-side witness: *"the possibility that low volatility leads investors to behave in ways that make the financial system more fragile and prone to crisis."* Low ordinary volatility is a benefactor for a short-horizon performance residual and a malfactor for a systemic-fragility residual. The same actor is not good or bad in itself. Perrow's *Normal Accidents* (opened 2026-11-23) says complexity makes failures inevitable in tightly coupled systems, which is a low-overlap engineering analogue for why the crisis regime cannot simply be subtracted from the ordinary one (Fig 11 :61: introduction is available, exclusion is not).

**Verdict:** `triangulated` for the valence rule (Fig 11 plus the sociotechnical analogue). **Mode:** `specified`.

## 3. Pre-registered refuter

Required before the conclusion, per Seat Map :287: *"a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument, not only an honesty device."* Written before the findings were drawn up.

The shape claim is refuted if any of the following holds:

1. A single generating process with a single distribution reproduces both the frequency of ordinary fluctuations and the depth and persistence of crisis output losses, with no residual that persists and does not trend down (breaks F2, Fig 9 :4-5).
2. A measurement shows crisis and non-crisis recessions have statistically indistinguishable depth and persistence once conditioned on the same shock (breaks the world corroboration of F2; includes the Cerra and Saxena and Jordà et al. results).
3. A declared common currency prices both frequency and consequence so that the two classes collapse to one scalar without loss (breaks F3, Fig 12 :125-127).
4. A framework reading shows that aggregating across slots preserves, rather than erases, the live crisis signal (breaks F1, Fig 1 :19, :22).
5. A case is found where the two conditions do not overlap and do not form a compound, so the union claim is false (breaks Fig 9 :54-58).
6. A ground reading returns a numeric importance ranking that is stable across anchors and holds out of sample (closes the F5 gap).

If any fires, the shape is withdrawn or narrowed. Until one is run, no part of this may be labelled `grounded`.

## 4. Recommendation

1. Do not answer with one importance number. Answer with two per-class verdicts and a declared currency (F1, F3).
2. Split the union before searching: ordinary-fluctuation loop, crisis loop, and the overlap compound (F2).
3. Record the numeric weight as `gap`, with the instruments that would close it, rather than as silence or as a consensus estimate (F5).
4. Measure the cross-scale round trip instead of assuming the firm-level story scales to the systemic one (F7).
5. Use importance only as a budget filter. Send the residual, the shape and the refuter to the public venues in `03_public_contact_points.csv`, and ask what would count as a failure (F6).

### SEARCHED / INWARD / OUTWARD kept distinct

- **SEARCHED:** no figure line returns the numeric importance weight. The closure does not change. The remedy is not a new atom.
- **INWARD:** sharpen the credit-growth and output-persistence instruments already returning readings; the long-run datasets are the worse instruments from which better ones are built (Fig 6 :32).
- **OUTWARD:** characterise R in effect terms, then find a frame whose closure predicts its shape. The volatility paradox, normal accidents and fat-tail frames are candidates. Each is debited against independence and none hands over the verdict (Fig 9 :27-28).

### Updated verdict

The shape survives and the quantity does not. Two classes, one union compound, per-slot verdicts, no aggregate importance number without a declared currency, numeric weight owed to the ground. All verdicts are stated with mode and are kept separate. After re-census, one strain remains live (the operand type of ⋛ before a currency is declared), and it is recorded rather than smoothed. This is a `specified` and ungrounded result.
