## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, what the Tether says about the comparative importance of rare financial crises and ordinary changes in spending and production. Option A: crises are extreme draws from one ordinary process, so importance is proportional to frequency and one model plus one lever suffice. Option B: crises are a distinct regime with a different generator and disproportionate persistent consequences, so two loops plus two levers are required. Option C: the comparison is ill posed until a currency pricing both classes is declared, so the honest output is two verdicts and a gap. What each leads to: A and B hand over a scalar that the figures disallow and invite an importance ranking to be used as a witness; C hands over a checkable shape, written refuters, and a numeric weight owed to the ground.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale, then one line per domain with actors, currency and anchor; partition or union; order by dependency.

- **R (arity / symmetry / scale).** The residual is the mismatch between a single importance ranking and the two-class structure of the data. Arity: two classes plus an overlap compound. Symmetry: asymmetric, since crises are rare, large and discrete while ordinary changes are frequent, small and continuous; the comparison is ordered, not a symmetric join. Scale: firm and household scale for ordinary changes, systemic and national scale for crises, so a cross-scale transform is implicated (Fig 4).
- **Domain 1, real activity.** Actors: households, firms, consumers, producers. Currency: spending, output, income. Anchor: national accounts and business-cycle dating. Nameable anchor, so a domain.
- **Domain 2, financial crises.** Actors: banks, borrowers, lenders, asset markets, regulators. Currency: credit, leverage, asset prices, balance sheets, spreads. Anchor: dated crisis episodes, credit spreads and measured output collapses. Nameable anchor, so a domain.
- **Domain 3, policy and verification.** Actors: central banks, fiscal authorities, regulators. Currency: policy tools and realised outcomes. Anchor: recovery and welfare outcomes. Nameable anchor, so a domain.
- **Domain 4, measurement and modelling.** Actors: economists, datasets, models. Currency: variance explained and forecast error. Anchor: out-of-sample prediction against measured output. Nameable anchor, so a domain.
- **Partition or union.** Union, not partition. Two different rules work on two different subsets, and the disagreement does not shrink with effort (Fig 9 :4-5). The intersection is a specific compound with a signed cycle (Fig 9 :57-58; Fig 12 :121).
- **Order by dependency.** Name the slots, obtain per-slot residuals, declare the currency, then apply the comparison, then report the numeric weight as a gap (Fig 3 :20; Fig 1 :19).

Strain test applied: reading the comparison bond ⋛ as a direct ranking of crisis magnitude against ordinary magnitude fits only with a strain, because ⋛ is *"a threshold relation between two quantities"* and the two classes are not yet quantities in one unit. Re-census puts ⋛ at the per-slot level after a currency is declared. The strain is named, not hidden.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

`general` on shape, `specified` on union, `gap` on number, not `grounded`. The question is malformed as a single comparison. Keep two per-class verdicts and a specific overlap compound. Frequency and consequence are two currencies that do not add, so no scalar ranking is licensed until a currency is declared. The numeric importance weight is unreached at this budget, not underivable in principle, and is reported plainly as `gap`. Importance is a budget filter, never a witness. The anchor is measured outcome, not agreement or prestige. Verdicts per domain kept separate and never averaged: D1 real activity `triangulated` for the per-slot form; D2 crisis regime `triangulated` for the union finding, with the generator details `gap`; D3 policy `triangulated` for the anchor rule; D4 measurement `triangulated` for the gap and the round trip; the numeric ranking `gap (ground owed)`.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded and locatable. The shape claims are mode-labelled and carry six pre-registered refuters, so an error is findable rather than invisible. The real cost is handing an importance score to a funder or a policy audience and letting a filter become a witness, which Fig 9 :60 forbids. That cost is avoidable by leading with the shape claim and its refuter and by reporting the number as a gap. A second cost is spending the independence of outside readers by sending them the architecture before they have checked the output (Fig 10 :42).
- **undoable:** yes. The union split, the per-slot rule, the two-currency restriction and the gap are removable or narrowable without touching the world sources. A ground reading can close the number; a declared currency can clear the type strain; a single-process result can collapse the union. No primitive has to be withdrawn. A shape that has been sent can be superseded by a corrected one.
- **reversed by:** a ground reading, not agreement. A measurement showing that one distribution reproduces both the frequency of ordinary changes and the persistence of crisis losses would break the union finding. A depth-and-persistence comparison under a preselected conditioning set that finds no difference would break C2. A declared common currency for frequency and consequence would clear the two-currency restriction. A stable out-of-sample numeric ranking across anchors would close the gap. A round-trip measurement showing that the firm-level story survives the systemic step unchanged would set R_T near zero. Until one of these fires, the verdict stands as candidate.
