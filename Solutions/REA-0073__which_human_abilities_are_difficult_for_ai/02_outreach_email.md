# Outreach draft: check one separator before you decide about the list

**Use:** one-to-one, or as a cover note attached to a submission through the public venues in
`03_public_contact_points.csv`. **Not** for bulk send. No personal emails are used anywhere.

**Hard precondition:** do not send until the refuters in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md` section 4 are
read and the ability names are kept labelled as world bindings. If the separator collapses under
the first refuter, there is nothing else to look at.

---

**Subject:** Which human abilities resist a body-less AI? Check the separator, not the list

> Hi [name],
>
> I am working on a narrow question: which human abilities are hard for AI specifically because they
> depend on a body, on development, or on social relationships. I am not asking you to agree with a
> list, and I am not asking you to read a theory first. I have one separator I would like you to
> try to break. If it breaks, the list is worthless.
>
> **The separator (`specified`, not `grounded`):** an ability is hard for the stated reason when its
> competence is a property of the agent and its habitat, of a developmental lineage that met the
> ground, or of a relation to another frame whose closure differs, rather than a property of the
> agent's corpus. The figures put it this way: *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat, never
> of the agent alone"* (Fig 11 :39). They add that *"A test harness is a substituted habitat. Which is
> why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability"* (Fig 11 :70). So a benchmark score is
> not the ground here.
>
> **What follows from it, and what does not.** Three domains keep separate verdicts and are never
> averaged: physical contact and affordances; staged development that carries methods rather than
> recordings; and relations to other frames, which need independence rather than more agreement.
> Some abilities are compounds of all three, such as learning a manipulation skill by watching a
> demonstrator's gaze and posture. The names I would attach to those domains come from published
> work in embodied cognition, developmental robotics and social cognition. I am not claiming the
> framework generated those names. It returns a criterion and a stop, not an inventory.
>
> **What would refute it:** (1) a body-less, undeveloped, socially isolated system reaches the same
> ground on an ability I classified as embodiment-dependent; (2) a single training run reaches the
> developed competence without a staged trajectory or a changed habitat; (3) one isolated frame
> tracks a mental state genuinely different from its own, where self-projection does not count; or
> (4) the three conditions prove additive for a compound ability rather than forming a new loop. I
> wrote these down before writing the list. A one-line "this fails because..." is more useful to me
> than agreement. If you would rather not engage, no reply is needed.
>
> **If it survives, and only then:** the sources are below. The figure text, not my summary of it,
> is the authority.
> - Figures with line numbers (local framework extract): `FRAMEWORK/reference/03-FIGURES_TEXT.md`
> - Canonical usage rule: https://github.com/IsaiahN/tether/blob/general-agent/figures/README.md
> - The eight-step loop and the mode rule: `FRAMEWORK/figures/THE_FORMULA.md`
> - Embodied cognition, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/embodied-cognition
> - Sensorimotor contingencies as a drive of development: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neurorobotics/articles/10.3389/fnbot.2019.00098/full
>
> **If you want to use the method:** run it on a problem you know well and can check against a
> ground. Write down what would refute your result *before* you run it, and state the mode of what
> you get. A reported failure is more useful to me than a reported success.

## Why this version is ordered this way

| the move | figure-grounded reason |
|---|---|
| Separator first, list second | Fig 10 :52 *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Fig 9 :27-28: describe the residual before choosing a frame, or any frame will seem to fit. |
| Do not ask for agreement | Fig 2 :35 *"Alignment is triangulation, not negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* |
| Three verdicts, never averaged | Fig 1 :22 *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* |
| Refuters written in advance | Seat rule: a falsifier written before the run is a design instrument. Fig 2 :42 *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* |
| Mode stated on the claim | `THE_FORMULA.md`: *"Mode. general · specified · grounded. State which."* |
| Ability names labelled as bindings | Fig 8 :50-52: convergent derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents; only the record separates them. |
| The inventory reported as a gap | Fig 13 :134 *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not a silence,"* and Fig 9 :32 *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself"* |
| Links only at the end | Fig 4 :22 *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* A method can be given away; a recording sent first turns a checker into a believer (Fig 10 :42). |
| No request for trust or prestige | Fig 2 :40 *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence pool"* |
| Offer the formula as a method | Fig 4 :22 again, plus formula "Direction": a frame cannot check the direction of its own question, so each user writes their own refuter. |

Note on tone: this email never asks the recipient to endorse the framework, never implies that
agreeing with it would help, and never sends a personal address. The only ask is a refutation of a
separator. That is what makes a reply usable as a check rather than as a vote (Fig 7 :35).
