# The Tether's answer: which human abilities are hard for AI

**Mode: `specified` on the criterion and the per-domain verdicts, `general` on the structural shape.
Not `grounded`.** Simulated derivation checked against the figures (`05_what_shape_means.md` section
6), not a ground reading. No experiment was run.

## 1. The residual it answers

A corpus-trained system with no body, no developmental trajectory and no other frame in its habitat
fails to register residuals that a human registers. The question asks which abilities are hard *for
that reason*. Under Fig 9 :55, *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not
a list of separate ones"*, this is a union compound with three single-condition regions and their
intersections, not one hard problem. It has its own loop and its own ground.

## 2. The answer

**The criterion (figure-derived).** An ability is in the hard set iff its competence is a property of
agent-and-habitat contact, of a developmental lineage that met the ground, or of a relation to frames
with differing closures, rather than of the agent's library alone. Fig 11 :39: *"So the answerable
question is not what intelligence is, but what makes a system capable. And capability is a property
of agent-and-habitat, never of the agent alone."* Fig 11 :40: *"A system cannot be made more capable
by improving the agent in isolation. An improvement that does not change contact changes nothing,
however much it improves."*

**Why a benchmark score cannot settle it.** Fig 11 :70: *"A test harness is a substituted habitat.
Which is why a synthetic solve proves wiring and never capability."* Fig 11 :66: *"Isolation is not
removal of the habitat. It is substitution of one habitat for another."* A text benchmark and a
physics simulator are substituted habitats. They can prove wiring; they cannot show the contact.

**The three domains, kept separate and never averaged:**

1. **Embodiment.** Fig 3 :7: *"it has terms for what things afford: what can be moved, opened, joined,
   blocked"*. The affordance vocabulary is the middle link of the dependency chain, and Fig 3 :27:
   *"A link with no instrument is not a link that works. It is a link nobody has looked at."* The
   Symbols table adds the narrowest line in the set: the frame transform `F` maps allocentric to
   egocentric at one scale and *"Cannot be run without a pose."* Hard abilities here: reaching,
   grasping, walking, body-part differentiation, affordance perception, tactile and proprioceptive
   reasoning, pose-dependent spatial perspective, and grounded basic concepts such as up, front and
   push. These names are bindings from the embodied-cognition literature, not figure derivations.
2. **Development.** Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Fig 4 :25:
   *"A recording carried upward looks like knowledge and is a description of one occasion."* Fig 11
   :36: *"Where nothing maintains anything, intelligence is a label applied afterwards to a lineage
   that met its ground. Nothing was intelligent; selection ran."* Hard abilities here: staged motor
   skill from babbling to hand-eye reaching, retention and reactivation of sensorimotor
   contingencies, generalization across contexts, and intuitive physical reasoning built by acting.
   A single training run is a recording, not a trajectory.
3. **Social relationships.** Fig 6 :23: *"No frame escapes its own closure. The bound moves only on
   contact with a frame whose closure differs."* Fig 2 :31-32: *"Independence, between two frames, is
   how little they draw on the same evidence."* Fig 2 :35: *"Alignment is triangulation, not
   negotiation. A mirror cannot correct you."* Hard abilities here: mentalizing and theory of mind,
   perspective taking, self-other distinction, empathy, emotion perception, social norms and social
   knowledge, selective learning from a more skilled model, imitation, teaching and cumulative
   tradition. A self-projection is a mirror and does not count.
4. **The compounds.** Joint attention, reading intention from gaze and posture, learning a
   manipulation skill from a demonstrator, and coordination in a dyad bind all three conditions.
   Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and
   becomes a specific compound."* Each compound needs its own loop.

**Order, not a flat list.** Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance."*
Embodiment supplies perception and affordances, development carries methods across stages, and
social relations compose over developed, embodied frames. A social deficit measured in a body-less,
undeveloped system is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22-23).

## 3. What it explicitly does not say

- Not an exhaustive nameable list. Fig 9 :32: *"you get a kind of thing, never the answer itself."*
  The inventory is a `gap`, reported as a verdict (Fig 13 :134: *"Unreachable is a verdict here, not
  a silence,"*), not filled by the framework.
- Not a claim that AI cannot ever display these behaviours. A fluent behaviour in a harness proves
  wiring; it is not the same competence, and it is not nothing.
- Not a ranking of abilities, and no averaging of the three domains. Fig 1 :22: *"Aggregating across
  slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."*
- Not `grounded`. No hardware transfer test, developmental trajectory or second-frame test was run.
- Not a claim that text contains no human cognition. It contains outputs; *"outputs do not contain
  their generator"* (*They're Scanning Books*, world source, secondary).
- Not a verdict about any particular model or product. No model was tested here.

## 4. What would settle it, the ground

The four pre-registered refuters in `01` section 4 are the instruments to run:

1. **Contact:** demonstrate an embodiment-dependent ability in a body-less agent against the human
   ability's ground, not a text proxy (Fig 11 :40).
2. **Lineage:** reach the developed competence in one training run with no staged trajectory and no
   change in habitat.
3. **Other frame:** track a mental state genuinely different from the system's own, in a habitat with
   no second closure. Self-projection fails this test by Fig 2 :35.
4. **Pose:** run the allocentric/egocentric transform with no pose, or show the Symbols table line
   governs a different quantity.

A fifth test would close the gap rather than the verdict: find a figure line that enumerates
abilities, or derive the list from the six-term set. Until one of these fires, the answer stays
`candidate`.

## 5. The honest stop

**Criterion reached, inventory unreached.** The separator is figure-derived and triangulated: Fig 11
(habitat), Fig 4 (methods across scales) and Fig 6 (differing closures) are three figures with low
overlap on different objects. The ability names are world-frame bindings with provenance. The
six-term set is *"a human's careful guess at reality's compression axes"* (*They're Scanning Books*),
so declaring it makes a choice visible, not neutral. Another sensorium's minimum set need not be
these six. Fig 9 :44: *"Only a sealed room can be searched to the end, and no frame is a sealed room.
So searching and finding nothing proves nothing."* The answer stops at the limit of the available
instruments and records the lineage so the stop is checkable. Indiscernibility is not identity.

## 6. One-paragraph version for outreach

> The Tether does not provide a list of abilities that AI can never master. It provides the separator
> that decides which human abilities are hard for a specific reason: their competence lives in
> contact with a body and habitat, in a developmental lineage that met the ground, or in relations to
> other frames with differing closures, rather than in the model's library. Physical skills,
> sensorimotor concepts and pose-dependent spatial reasoning sit in the first region; staged motor
> and intuitive-physical competence in the second; mentalizing, perspective taking, empathy, social
> knowledge and cumulative tradition in the third; and joint attention or learning from a
> demonstrator in the intersections of all three. A benchmark score cannot decide membership,
> because a harness is a substituted habitat and proves wiring, never capability. The ability names
> come from published cognitive-science and robotics work and are recorded as bindings, not
> derivations. What is offered is the criterion, the dependency order, four refuters written in
> advance, and an honest gap where the figures decline to name an inventory. The ground has not
> settled it.
