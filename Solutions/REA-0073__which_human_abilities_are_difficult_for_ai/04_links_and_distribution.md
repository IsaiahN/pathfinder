## What the wider literature adds, and what it does not

1. **It supplies names, not the criterion.** The three world fields above name reaching, body
   knowledge, sensorimotor contingencies, mentalizing, empathy and social learning. The criterion
   that selects them comes from Fig 11 :39, Fig 4 :22 and Fig 6 :23. Per Fig 8 :50-52, convergent
   derivation and adopted import look identical in the contents, so the record of which came first
   is the only thing that separates them. Keep that record.
2. **The sim-to-real survey is a second reading of the harness claim.** Fig 11 :70 calls a test
   harness a substituted habitat; the survey measures the same effect as the sim-to-real gap. Two
   fields, one effect, which is corroboration, not a second witness for the figure verdict.
3. **The social sources are not evidence that AI lacks these abilities.** They describe human and
   primate competence. A claim about a model would need the ground tests in `01` section 4. Saying
   otherwise would turn a description of humans into a verdict about machines by import, with no
   source gate.

## Distribution: offer, do not push

Figure-grounded reasons:

- **Fig 10 :52** *"Bring a characterised residual, never the source."* Send the separator and its
  refuter first. Sending the whole framework before a check turns a potential outside frame into a
  participant in the frame being checked (Fig 10 :42: *"A verifier that can reconstruct the claim
  cannot verify it."*).
- **Fig 9 :27-28** *"describe it before you go looking, or any frame you pick will seem to fit."*
  Ask what a checker would expect to see if the separator were true before giving them the reading
  list.
- **Fig 1 :22** *"Aggregating across slots is how a live signal disappears into an average."* Do not
  present one blended "AI difficulty score". Enumerate the domains and keep the verdicts apart.
- **Fig 2 :40** *"agreement about the world is worthless when the agreeing frames share an evidence
  pool"* and **Fig 7 :35** *"A room's agreement is variation, not a verdict. Selection between rooms
  is where the ground votes."* A list of people chosen for likely agreement would manufacture the
  disqualifying correlation. No such list exists here.
- **Fig 2 :42** *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* One-to-one contact
  with a public submission venue is enough; a mass send buys spelling, not independence.

**Recommended order in a reply:** (1) the separator and its four refuters; (2) ask what they would
count as a failure and on what ground; (3) only then, the eight links, with the note that the
figure text, not this summary, is the authority.

## The two-stage offer

- **Stage 1:** the residual in effect terms, the criterion, the three separate domains, and the
  pre-registered refuters. No theory, no links, no request for agreement. Fig 10 :52 and Fig 2 :45
  (*"Frames range against the ground. They do not negotiate with it."*) govern this order.
- **Stage 2, opt-in only:** if they are curious, offer the links above. Their verdict on stage 1
  must be recorded before any framework material is shown; later agreement is never pooled as
  corroboration (Fig 2 :40).
- **Stage 3:** offer the formula as a method to reuse, permitted by Fig 4 :22 *"Only methods cross
  up. Only head starts come down."* Each user writes their own refuter and states their own mode,
  because formula "Direction" says a frame cannot check the direction of its own question.

**Hard precondition:** stages 1 to 3 only work if the stage-1 separator survives. If a refuter in
`01` section 4 fires, repair the separator before sending anything.

## Legal and practical note

Public submission portals and opt-in workshop/journal routes are used instead of personal addresses.
That avoids the GDPR or CAN-SPAM style failure that applies to unsolicited bulk email in many
jurisdictions, and it avoids the structural failure Fig 2 :40 names. Nothing in this folder is sent
by this agent. Hosting and sending the packet is yours to do; prefer an opt-in download page that
lets a recipient decline contact.
