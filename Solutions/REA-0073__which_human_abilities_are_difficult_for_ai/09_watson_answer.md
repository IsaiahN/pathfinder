# WATSON answer: which human abilities are difficult for AI because of embodiment, development, or social relationships

**Mode: `specified`, not `grounded`.**

**Status: SIMULATED DERIVATION, not a ground reading.** This WATSON run synthesizes material already
in `01_figure_grounded_audit.md`, `05_what_shape_means.md`, `06_isomers_and_order.md`,
`07_the_tether_answer.md` and `08_expert_verification_packet.md`. It is checked for internal
consistency against the figures. By its own rule (*The Recursive Self-Improvement Trap*: LLM output
is *"simulacra"* and *"the truly testable output would need to be derived from the real architecture
or working the formula yourself"*) it cannot settle the question it reports on. No experiment was
run.

## QUESTION

Decide, in effect terms, which human abilities are hard for AI specifically because they depend on a
body, on a developmental trajectory, or on social relationships. Option A: produce an exhaustive
named list and attribute it to the framework. Option B: derive the criterion that decides membership,
keep the three domains separate, label the names as world-frame bindings, and report the inventory
as a gap. What each leads to: A asserts an enumeration no figure line licenses and invites a verdict
about models that were never tested; B sends a checkable separator, four refuters, and an honest
stop at the limit of the instruments. B is the figure-supported option.

## DOMAIN CENSUS

R by arity, symmetry and scale; one line per domain with actors / currency / anchor; partition or
union; order by dependency.

- **R (the residual).** A corpus-trained, body-less, socially isolated system fails to register
  residuals a human registers. Arity: three conditions binding, so a **union compound**, not one hard
  question (Fig 9 :55 *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of
  separate ones"*). Symmetry: not symmetric; a body supplies pose and contact, development supplies
  a carried method, other frames supply a differing closure, and the order matters. Scale: step,
  cycle, level, lineage.
- **Domain 1, embodiment.** Actors: body, habitat conditions, affordances, poses. Currency:
  sensorimotor residuals and contact. Anchor: physical measurement. Nameable, so this is a domain.
- **Domain 2, development.** Actors: the agent over time, the habitat, the maintainer that introduces
  actors. Currency: methods carried across stages and head starts sent down. Anchor: the ground for
  what the developed ability achieves. Nameable.
- **Domain 3, social relations.** Actors: two or more frames, each with history and closure.
  Currency: agreement, residuals, imports from a differing closure. Anchor: the ground, never
  agreement. Nameable.
- **Domain 4, the intersections.** Actors: embodied, developed frames in relation. Currency:
  residuals that only appear when two or three conditions bind, for example joint attention.
  Anchor: the ground, enumerated outward through the habitat (Fig 11 :43-44). Nameable.
- **Union, not partition.** The intersections are specific compounds with their own loops
  (Fig 9 :57 *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and
  becomes a specific compound."*).
- **Order by dependency (Fig 3).** Perception and affordance vocabulary first, then development and
  carried methods, then social relations over developed embodied frames. A social reading in a
  body-less, undeveloped system is a reading of nothing.

Strain test applied: Fig 3 governs links inside one agent; Fig 11 governs contact between agent and
habitat; Symbols table `F` governs within-scale frame transforms and needs a pose, while Fig 4
`T_A`/`T_E` run between scales. Different objects, so no strain after re-census. The one open item is
the absence of a figure line that enumerates abilities (F7 in `01`), reported as a gap.

## VERDICT (from the figures)

The criterion survives; the inventory is a reported gap. An ability is hard for the stated reason iff
its competence lives in agent-and-habitat contact, in a lineage that met the ground, or in a relation
to frames with differing closures, rather than in the library. The three conditions are ordered by
dependency and form a union with compound abilities at the intersections. Embodiment, development
and social relations keep separate verdicts: a system may pass one and fail another, and averaging
them throws away the signal. Physical skills and pose-dependent spatial reasoning, staged
sensorimotor development, and mentalizing, empathy and cumulative tradition are the three main
regions; joint attention and learning from a demonstrator are compounds. Ability names are world
bindings with provenance. This is `specified`, ungrounded, candidate.

- **cost if wrong:** bounded but real. Misclassifying an ability sends checking effort to the wrong contact and can spend the independence of outside readers by sending a recording instead of a generator. Fig 2 :42 *"Buy independence in proportion to the cost of being wrong."* The cost is avoidable by leading with the criterion and its refuters and by labelling every name as a binding.
- **undoable:** yes. The names can be relabelled or removed without touching the criterion, the dependency order or the union census. No primitive has to be withdrawn and no result has to be retracted to correct a binding. A sent packet can be superseded by a corrected one.
- **reversed by:** a ground test, not agreement. A body-less agent passing the contact test, a single-run system passing the lineage test, an isolated frame passing the other-frame test, or the frame transform run without a pose would break the matching domain verdict. A figure line that enumerates abilities would close the inventory gap. Until one fires, the verdict stands as `candidate`. Prestige, a leaderboard rank or a list of agreeable readers is not such an instrument.
