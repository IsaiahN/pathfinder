## DERIVATION

### Q1 - What order do the three conditions have?

- Fig 3 :20: *"The chain is ordered by dependency, not by importance. Each link consumes what the one
  above produces, so effort spent above the break arrives at a step that cannot use it."*
- Fig 3 :5 gives perception as link 1: *"the system can tell what is in front of it, and tell it apart
  from what was there before"*. Fig 3 :7 gives link 2: *"it has terms for what things afford: what can
  be moved, opened, joined, blocked"*.
- Fig 4 :22: *"Only methods cross up. Only head starts come down."* Development consumes perception
  and produces methods; it cannot precede the contact that supplies the residuals it carries.
- Fig 12 :104: *"A settled molecule becomes an atom for whatever composes over it. So irreducible is a
  fact about a level and never about the world, and there is no bottom"*. A social ability composes
  over agents that are already developed and embodied.
- ⇒ Order: **embodiment, then development, then social relations.** A social deficit read in a
  body-less, undeveloped system is a reading of nothing (Fig 3 :22-23: *"A step whose input never
  arrives cannot be diagnosed, only its predecessor can"*).
- **Falsifier:** a social ability that is reachable without any developed, embodied frame, measured
  against a ground that does not reduce to text similarity. If found, the order is wrong and social
  relations are upstream, not downstream.

### Q2 - Partition or union?

- Fig 9 :54: *"And when the split lands on a union rather than a partition, unbundle before you
  search again."*
- Fig 9 :55: *"A bundled problem is usually a union of overlapping conditions, not a list of separate
  ones."*
- Fig 9 :57: *"a case in two conditions is not a harder case of either. It stops being a mixture and
  becomes a specific compound."*
- ⇒ The three conditions are a **union**, and the intersections (joint attention, imitation of a
  skilled model, reading intention from gaze and posture) are specific compounds with their own
  loops. A single lever that clears the single-condition region leaves the intersections standing
  (Fig 9 :56).
- **Falsifier:** show that the conditions are independent and additive for a named ability. Then a
  partition is the right structure and the compound loop is unnecessary.

### Q3 - Are the three conditions isomers, or functional-group variants?

- Fig 12 :117: *"isomer. Same atoms, different bond, different substance. The difference is invisible
  in the formula and decisive in the behaviour."*
- Fig 12 :124: *"functional group. A substructure whose presence fixes the outcome. The group says
  what kind; the qualifier says which member."*
- As the conditions are written in `01`, they share the **criterion** (competence lives in contact,
  not in the library) but have different actors, currencies and anchors. By Fig 12's definition they
  are **not isomers**: they do not share the same atom set. They are members of a **functional
  group** whose shared substructure is *"capability is a property of agent-and-habitat"* (Fig 11 :39),
  with the qualifier naming which contact is at issue.
- The stronger form: write every ability over the full condition set `{body, lineage, other frames}`
  and let only the binding condition differ. Then the abilities **are** isomers, same atoms arranged
  differently. That is the checkable structural claim worth taking to a checker.
- **Falsifier:** if an ability requires a condition the others cannot even express, so the difference
  is a missing atom rather than a different bond, the isomer claim fails and the functional-group
  reading stands.

### Q4 - What is the order of search inside the union?

- Fig 9 :43: *"The verdict tells you to leave. The lookup tells you where to go. Elimination is what
  you do after you arrive."*
- Fig 6 :49-50: SEARCHED first, then INWARD, then OUTWARD, kept distinct. Fig 6 :53: *"searchers
  drawing on the same evidence are one searcher wearing many names."*
- ⇒ Run three loops in dependency order: first the single-condition regions (embodiment alone,
  development alone, social relation alone), then each intersection. Do not let a result from one
  region stand in for another; that would be a filter handing over a verdict (Fig 9 :60).
- **Falsifier:** a single loop over the bundled question that gives the same verdict for all three
  regions against the ground. Then unbundling added nothing.

### Q5 - Analogues, tested for relations rather than looks

Living-system test (seat-reasoning move 3): map actors / currency / anchor, then ask whether the
analogue changes a decision. A look-alike that changes no decision is dropped.

| analogue | actors | currency | anchor | what it decides |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Seed and conditions, Fig 4 :34 | seed, soil, season | growth carried across a crossing | the ground for what grows | whether a corpus can substitute for a developmental trajectory. It cannot: the seed carries what will grow one, not a small tree. |
| Robot in a harness, Fig 11 :70 | robot, simulator, designer | sim-to-real transfer | hardware measurement | whether a benchmark solve counts as capability. It counts as wiring. |
| Apprentice and master, Decoding Tacit Knowledge | learner, expert, correction | two-way correction and rebuilt pretext | the craft's ground | whether text alone can transfer somatic skill. It cannot; contact does the work. |
| Whale and tit traditions, Whiten and van de Waal 2018 | demonstrator, learner, group | socially transmitted information | locally adaptive behaviour | whether social ability needs a second frame with expertise. It does. |
| Instrument and source, The Hard Problem is About Source | spectrometer, projection, source | discriminating reading | the source, not the projection | whether fluent mind-reading text settles social competence. It does not. |

The first, third and fourth are living systems with a seat or a lineage; the second and fifth are
instruments. None is chosen for resemblance alone. The table changes one decision: it tells us to
grade the ability at the contact, not at the output.

## CONCLUSION

The three conditions are ordered by dependency (body, then development, then social relation), form a
**union** with specific compound abilities at the intersections, and are **functional-group
variants** as written, with a real isomer claim available if every ability is expressed over the
full condition set. None of this adds a primitive or moves a wall. It is the structure the residual
already required.
